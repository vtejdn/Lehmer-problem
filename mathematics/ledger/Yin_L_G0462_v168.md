# Uniform degree and measure bounds for the reciprocal cubic seed and its power substitutions

<a id="degree"></a>
## A degree fraction for every positive exponent and every substitution

Put \(g(Y)=Y^3-Y-1\), \(b(Y)=1-Y^2-Y^3\), \(A_n(X)=g(X^n)+X b(X^n)\), and \(m=\log M\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
For every pair of positive integers \(n,q\), each monic irreducible nontorsion factor \(R\) of \(-A_n(X^q)\) satisfies
\[
 \deg R\ge\frac{(3n+1)q}{34000},\qquad
 m(R)\ge \log\!\left(1+\frac{0.31}{720\cdot34000}\right).             \tag{1}
\]
There are at most \(34000\) nontorsion factors, counted with multiplicity.
The complete degree is \((3n+1)q\), and every irreducible factor is a unit polynomial because both end coefficients of \(-A_n\) equal one.

For the base case \(q=1\), the [fixed two-variable specialization theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v168.md#statement) already gives a positive factor gap with \(F(X,Y)=g(Y)+X b(Y)\).
The [two-sided factor-height comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v158.md#factor-height) then gives \(\deg P\gg n\) for every nontorsion factor \(P\) at odd \(n\).
In particular both alternatives concerning the base degrees left in the [square-root tower analysis](Yin_L_G0462_v164.md#scope) are settled.
There is no sequence of sublinear base degrees, and the largest nontorsion degree is at least a fixed positive fraction of \(n\) for every odd \(n\ge3\).
Such a factor exists by the [exact cyclotomic part](Yin_L_G0462_v158.md#cyclotomic).
The explicit estimate (1) comes from the [separated-block capacity argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#application) and a norm estimate for the small exponents.

<a id="large"></a>
## Separated blocks and the large exponents

Writing \(x=X\), the polynomial \(A_n(x^q)\) has blocks
\[
 x^{3nq}B(x)+C(x),\qquad B(x)=1-x^q,\qquad
 C(x)=x^q-1-x^{nq}-x^{(2n+1)q}.
\]
Their coefficient one-norms are at most \(L=4\).
All zeros of \(B\) lie on the unit circle.
In the notation of the separated-block result, the leading block starts at \(N=3nq\), its width is \(b_0=q\), and the gap is \(G=N-\deg C=(n-1)q\).
For \(n\ge9\) one has \(G-2b_0=(n-3)q\ge(3n+1)q/5\), so the degree hypothesis holds with \(\delta=1/5\) and the complete degree exceeds \(4/\delta\).

Use the intermediate estimate \(\deg R\ge k\lambda^2 D/(384\log T)\) in [the capacity proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#application), with \(k\ge1\) its dyadic rotation parameter and \(T=2L^{10k}\).
Here \(j=\lfloor(n-6)q/2\rfloor\), \(D=j+3q\), and \(\lambda D=j+3q/2\).
If \(e\in\{0,1\}\) is the parity of \(nq\), then
\[
 \lambda^2D=\frac{((n-3)q-e)^2}{2(nq-e)}
           \ge\frac{q(n-4)^2}{2n},\qquad
 \log T=(1+20k)\log2\le21k\log2.
\]
It follows that
\[
                         \deg R\ge\frac{q(n-4)^2}{16128\,n\log2}.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
For \(n\ge1000\), one has \(n-4\ge0.996n\), \(3n+1\le3.001n\), and \(16128\cdot 3.001\cdot0.694/(0.996)^2<34000\).
Since \(\log2<0.694\), (2) proves the degree bound in (1) in this range.

<a id="small"></a>
## The norm of a root covers all smaller exponents and arbitrary substitutions

For a root \(\gamma\) of \(R\) put \(\alpha=\gamma^q\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(H=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and \(t=[H:K]\).
Write \(P\) for the monic minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\).
All these roots are units and \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
For \(n\ge2\) the six nonzero coefficients of \(A_n\) have modulus one, and hence \(m(P)\le m(A_n)\le\log\sqrt6<1\) by Jensen and Parseval.
When \(n=1\), \(A_1=-(x^4+1)\) and all its power substitutions are cyclotomic.

The unit \(\eta=N_{H/K}\gamma\) satisfies \(\eta^q=\alpha^t\), whence \(h(\eta)=(t/q)h(\alpha)>0\) for the absolute logarithmic height.
Its own degree \(d_\eta\) satisfies \(2\le d_\eta\le d\).
Using \(d_\eta h(\eta)=d_\eta t\,m(P)/(qd)\) and [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), we obtain
\[
 \frac{\deg R}{q}=\frac{dt}{q}>
 \frac{2d^2}{d_\eta\,m(P)(\log(3d_\eta))^3}
 \ge \frac{2d}{(\log(3d))^3}\ge\frac{2e^3}{81}>\frac13.
\]
In the last estimate \(e\) is the base of natural logarithms, and the positive-variable function \(v/(\log(3v))^3\) has its minimum on \(v\ge2\) at \(v=e^3/3\).
For \(n<1000\), we have \(3n+1\le2998\), so this estimate proves the remaining degree bound.

There are at most six terms in \(A_n(X^q)\) and its degree is \((3n+1)q\).
[Dobrowolski's divisor bound, Lemma 1](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible), applied to \(R\) and this multiple, gives the measure bound in (1) by \(6!=720\).
Adding the degrees of the nontorsion factors with multiplicity gives the factor-count assertion.

<a id="square-roots"></a>
## Exact square-root lifts at odd base exponents

If \(n\) is odd and \(q=2^k\), the [nonsquareness of both signs](Yin_L_G0462_v164.md#all-towers) gives a stronger identity for every nontorsion irreducible base factor \(P\).
The polynomial \(P(X^q)\) is irreducible, has degree \(q\deg P\), and has exactly the same measure as \(P\).
Consequently the nontorsion factor count with multiplicity is exactly the base count.
When \(n\equiv1\pmod8\), the remaining cyclotomic factor is the single simple polynomial \(\Phi_8(X^{2^k})=\Phi_{2^{k+3}}(X)\).
For every other odd \(n\), there are no cyclotomic factors.
Thus at odd \(n\) and dyadic \(q\) the count of all irreducible factors with multiplicity is at most \(34001\).
For arbitrary \(q\), (1) bounds the count of positive-measure factors, and no uniform count of all cyclotomic factors is possible even at \(n=1\).
For example \(X^{4q}+1\) contains \(\Phi_{8d}(X)\) for every divisor \(d\mid q\) when \(q\) is odd.

## The moving rational map with fixed Lehmer target

The [moving-map construction](Yin_L_G0450_v5.md#moving-map-actual-inputs) fixes the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial \(P_L(Z)=\sum_{j=0}^{10}c_j Z^j\).
At odd \(n\ge3\) it uses \(G_n=X^n-X-1\), \(H_n=1-X^{n-1}-X^n\), and \(F_n=\sum_j c_j H_n^j G_n^{10-j}\).
Set \(G(X,Y)=Y-X-1\), \(H(X,Y)=1-X^{-1}Y-Y\), and take the one fixed integral Laurent polynomial
\[
                     \mathcal F(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{10}c_j H(X,Y)^jG(X,Y)^{10-j}.
\]
Then \(\mathcal F(X,X^n)=F_n\), and each \(F_n\) is monic of degree \(10n\) with no cyclotomic factor by the cited construction.
In particular \(\mathcal F\ne0\) and each specialized polynomial is nonzero.
The [fixed-specialization divisor gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v168.md#statement) therefore supplies a constant \(c_{\mathcal F}>0\) with \(m(S)\ge c_{\mathcal F}\) for every irreducible factor \(S\) of every such \(F_n\).

For a root \(\beta\) of \(S\), [the fixed-field height bound (M9)](Yin_L_G0450_v5.md#moving-map-integral-mobius-height) gives \(h(\beta)\le C/(n-2)\) for one fixed \(C>0\) specified there.
Hence
\[
       \deg S\ge\frac{c_{\mathcal F}}C(n-2)\ge\frac{c_{\mathcal F}}{3C}n,
       \qquad
       \#\{\text{irreducible factors of }F_n\text{ with multiplicity}\}
                  \le \frac{30C}{c_{\mathcal F}}.
\]
This excludes the sublinear factors in [criterion (M14)](Yin_L_G0450_v5.md#moving-map-sublinear-factor-criterion), including the corresponding relative factors over the fixed degree-ten field through the exact absolute degree \(10[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q(\lambda)]\).
The argument gives a degree proportion and bounded factor count throughout this family.
Irreducibility of the individual \(F_n\) is a further question.
