### 2. Exact norm and a uniform whole-space limit

Multiplication in the actual basis 1,tau,tau^2, using tau^3=a*tau^2+(a+3)*tau+1, gives

    N_a(u,v,w)=Norm(u*tau^2+v*tau+w)=a^2 p+a L+R,
    p=u*w*(u-v+w),
    L=-u^2*v+4u^2*w+u*v^2-3u*v*w+2u*w^2-v^2*w+v*w^2,
    R=u^3-3u^2*v+9u^2*w-3u*v*w+6u*w^2+v^3-3v^2*w+w^3. (3)

One direct verification is to form the three columns obtained by reducing V(x), xV(x), x^2V(x) modulo Q_a and take their 3-by-3 determinant.
J64 performs that operation and independently compares its result with the polynomial resultant Res(Q_a,V).
Formula (3) is a polynomial identity for all coefficients, not a numerical interpolation.

Define

    K_a=max_(E=1) |N_a|,
    kappa=max_(E=1) |u*w*(u-v+w)|.                          (4)

The maxima exist by compactness of the positive-definite energy ellipsoid.
Equivalently these are the suprema of |N_a|/E^(3/2) and |p|/E^(3/2) over nonzero vectors.
For K_a, restricting that supremum to primitive integral triples does not change it: rational directions are dense, clearing a common denominator and then primitive content leaves the homogeneous ratio unchanged.
This statement is about a supremum; it does not assert that an integer maximizer exists.

On E=1, the three quantities |u|, |v|, |u+w| are at most 1/sqrt(2), so |w|<=sqrt(2).
Every degree-three monomial in (3) has absolute value less than 3.
The sums of the absolute coefficients of L and R are 13 and 27.
Thus, simultaneously for every direction,

    |N_a/a^2-p| <= 39/a+81/a^2 <=120/a,
    |K_a/a^2-kappa| <=120/a.                               (5)

In particular this limit permits coefficients to depend arbitrarily on a; it is not a limit obtained by first fixing an integer vector.
Since p(1,1,1)=1, kappa>0.
Equations (1)--(5) give the exact supremum identity and its asymptotic

    sup_(primitive integral z) B_a(z)
       =(1/6)log[(6a-1)(2a+3)]+(1/3)log K_a,
    sup B_a=log a+(1/6)log12+(1/3)log kappa+o(1).           (6)

The supremum includes every nonzero element of the actual order, not only a unit subgroup, three primitive content classes, or elements of one fixed field norm.
All signed norms are squared only in the full sextic resultant.
A negative trace norm has not been replaced by a positive trace norm before that step.

### 3. Exact global optimization and its integer-height boundary

The limiting maximum is nonzero, so every maximizing vector has u*w*(u-v+w) nonzero.
Use the projective chart u=1 and set

    K=1+v^2+(1+w)^2, p=w*(1-v+w),
    kappa^2=max p^2/(8K^3).

Every nonzero global maximum on the ellipsoid appears as an interior stationary point of this chart.
Differentiating, and cancelling only the nonzero p and w, gives

    f=-2v^2+3vw+3v+w^2+2w+2=0,
    g=-v^3+2v^2w+v^2+2vw^2+vw-2v-w^3-w^2+3w+2=0.       (7)

For completeness, g-(v/2-w/4+1/4)f is

    (3/4)[(3w^2-5)v-w^3-w^2+4w+2],

and the determinant defining Res_v(f,g) expands to

    9(2w^2+4w+3)(2w^4-8w^2+w+6).                         (8)

The first factor is positive on the real line.
Put J(w)=2w^4-8w^2+w+6.
A root of J cannot satisfy 3w^2=5: substitution in J would force w=16/9, inconsistent with w^2=5/3.
Thus the common v is unique.
Reduction modulo J gives

    v=v_*(w)=-2w^3-2w^2+5w+4.                            (9)

Conversely, substitution of (9) into f and g has zero remainder modulo J, so every real root of J gives the stationary point in (7).
The following disjoint rational intervals each have a sign change of J at their endpoints:

| Root | Rational interval for w | Exact conclusion for p^2/(8K^3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| w_1 | (-1840012,-1840011)/10^6 | 1016/10000 < value < 1017/10000 |
| w_2 | (-892923,-892922)/10^6 | 0 < value < 1/50 |
| w_3 | (1163265,1163266)/10^6 | 0 < value < 1/50 |
| w_4 | (1569668,1569669)/10^6 | 0 < value < 1/50 |

Degree four and the four sign changes prove that these are all real roots, with exactly one in each interval and no multiplicity.
The last column is a rational interval certificate: evaluate (9) by interval Horner arithmetic on the indicated w interval; form p=w(1-v+w) and K=1+v^2+(1+w)^2; square the numerator and divide by the positive interval 8K^3.
J64 retains every resulting rational endpoint.
This calculation uses neither floating-point root ordering nor a plot.
The strict separation proves

    kappa=|w_1(1-v_*(w_1)+w_1)| /
                     [2(1+v_*(w_1)^2+(1+w_1)^2)]^(3/2),
    0.1016 < kappa^2 < 0.1017.                            (10)

There are no omitted zero-product maximizers, since those have value zero.
There are no omitted projective points at infinity: u=0 also has product zero.
The maximum direction is therefore unique up to nonzero scalar.
The numerical values w_1 approximately -1.8400116, v_*(w_1) approximately 0.4879004 and kappa^2 approximately 0.1016306 are only convenient descriptions of (10).

The rational-root test for J lists only +/-1, +/-2, +/-3, +/-6, +/-1/2 and +/-3/2; direct substitution excludes all of them.
Hence no rational direction attains the limiting maximum.
This has an arithmetic cost consequence.
Suppose a_j tends to infinity and primitive integer vectors z_j satisfy

    |N_(a_j)(z_j)|/[a_j^2 E(z_j)^(3/2)] -> kappa.          (11)

Uniform estimate (5) forces their projective directions to the direction (1,v_*(w_1),w_1).
Bounded primitive height would leave only finitely many directions, none of which attains (10).
Thus their primitive heights tend to infinity: any bounded-height subsequence gives the same contradiction.
This is necessary for exact asymptotic saturation, rather than for every positive improvement.

Actual integer approximants exist.
Round the three entries of M(1,v_*(w_1),w_1) to nearest integers, divide by their gcd, and let M tend to infinity.
Primitive normalization does not change direction.
These are actual order elements and, by (5), can be combined with any a_j tending to infinity to achieve (11).
An equality at an irrational direction has not been assigned to a nonexistent fractional unit of the number field.

For these approximants the complete auxiliary Mahler cost has a particularly explicit form.
At the limiting direction, w_1<0 and

    4-2v_*(w_1)+w_1>0,   4+2v_*(w_1)+w_1>0,

as follows already from the rational intervals above.
Therefore x^2+v_*(w_1)x+w_1 has one root in (-2,0) and one in (0,2).
The same strict signs hold for every sufficiently close approximating direction.
All six roots of its actual C_z lie on the unit circle.

    M(C_z)=|u| -> infinity.                               (12)

The last divergence follows because bounded u, together with convergent bounded v/u,w/u, would bound the primitive height.
The complete circle-root cost here is the leading coefficient, not zero.
This gives an explicit boundary for saturating the nonlinear continuous capacity by primitive integer auxiliaries.

### 4. A fixed integral direction exceeding the original total measure

    P_(a,q)(t)=P_a(t^q) is irreducible of degree 6q.
