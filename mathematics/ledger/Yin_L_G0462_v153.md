# A finite test for the two-class cofactor bound

<a id="test"></a>
## An explicit prime and measure threshold

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), with \(d=\deg P\) and \(h=\log M(P)\).
Suppose a prime \(p\ge24\) satisfies \([\mathbb Q(\alpha^p):\mathbb Q]=d\).
Set \(D=p(16d+1)\).
If
\[
                         p h\ \ge\ 32\log(12D),                            \tag{1}
\]
then for any two prescribed residue classes modulo \(p\),
\[
                   h/3\ \le\ \min_F m(R)/p\ \le\ 2h .                    \tag{2}
\]
Here \(F\) runs over the integer multiples of \(P^2\) of degree at most \(D\) occupying exactly the two classes, with coprime rows in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
The product \(R\) contains the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(F/P^2\) other than \(x\), including multiplicities, with \(m=\log M\).
This gives an explicit sufficient condition for the [two-class comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) at every degree admitting a nontorsion unit.

A test independent of the coefficients is
\[
             p\ \ge\ 16(\log(3d))^3\log\bigl(12p(16d+1)\bigr).
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(d\ge2\), and [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(h>2/(\log(3d))^3\).
The degree condition is automatic if \(d<p-1\), by the [norm criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#power).
For example, every prime \(2^{14}<p\le2^{15}\) works for every degree-ten nontorsion unit.
Here \(\log30<7/2\), and \(12D\le12\cdot161\cdot2^{15}=63\,307\,776<20^6<e^{18}\).
Thus \(16(\log30)^3\log(12D)<16(7/2)^3\cdot18=12\,348<2^{14}\).

<a id="constants"></a>
## The constants in the determinant and interpolation estimates

For any integer polynomial \(F\) on two distinct nonempty rows with degree at most \(D\), use the [rotated determinant](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#rotation) and its integer base polynomial \(W\).
Choose two rotations \(F_1,F_2\) with Fourier minor of absolute value at least one, and put \(E=x\,d/dx\).
After factoring out \(F_1F_2\), the remaining determinant is \(EF_2/F_2-EF_1/F_1\).
On the unit circle its absolute value is bounded by \(S_1+S_2\), where \(S_j(x)\) is the sum of \(|x-z|^{-1}\) over all roots of \(F_j\) with multiplicities.
For every complex \(z\), normalized circle measure gives \(\int |x-z|^{-1/2}\,d\mu(x)\le2\).
For \(1/2\le|z|\le2\), rotate \(z\) to the positive real number \(\rho=|z|\) and use \(|e^{it}-\rho|\ge\sqrt{2}\,|\sin(t/2)|\) and \(\sin u\ge2u/\pi\) on \([0,\pi/2]\), with symmetry on the other half.
The integral of \((\sin u)^{-1/2}\) on \([0,\pi]\) divided by \(\pi\) is then at most two.
The other radii give a pointwise bound of \(\sqrt2\).
Square-root subadditivity gives \(\int (S_1+S_2)^{1/2}\,d\mu\le4D\).
Jensen's inequality for the logarithm therefore gives
\[
                            m(W)\le2m(F)+2\log(4D).                       \tag{4}
\]
Rotations and substitution of \(x^p\) preserve logarithmic Mahler measure, and the finitely many zeros and poles on the circle have integrable logarithms.

Let \(\beta=\alpha^p\) and let \(Q\) be its monic minimal polynomial, so \(m(Q)=p h\).
With two rows and \(P^t\mid F\), the [multiplicity bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#determinant) gives \(Q^{t+c-1}\mid W\) for \(t\ge1\), where \(c\) is the common vanishing order of the rows at \(\beta\).
For a single row \(x^rU(x^p)\), the map \(x\mapsto x^p\) is unramified at the nonzero \(\alpha\), so \(Q^t\mid U\) and \(m(F)\ge t p h\).

Apply the [box estimate for divided derivatives](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#jets) at \(t=3\), with \(b=16d,\ n=2(b+1)\) and \(D=p(b+1)\).
It gives a nonzero \(F_0\) on the allowed classes divisible by \(P^3\), of degree at most \(D\), with
\[
 m(F_0)\le
 \frac{3D h+3d\log(6n)+3d\log(1+D)}{n-3d}+\log(2n)
 \le \frac{48}{29}p h+\frac{35}{29}\log(12D).
\]
Here \(D/n=p/2\), \(n>32d\), and each of the three logarithms is at most \(\log(12D)\).
Under (1) this gives \(m(F_0)/(ph)\le1571/928<7/4\).
The single-row estimate would give \(m(F_0)\ge3p h\), so both rows occur.
By (4), \(2+c\le2m(F_0)/(ph)+2\log(4D)/(ph)<7/2+1/16<4\).
Thus \(c\le1\).
Divide both rows by their primitive common polynomial gcd and remove integer content.
The resulting integral polynomial still occupies both rows and contains \(P^2\), since the removed factor has order exactly \(c\) at \(\alpha\).
Its measure and degree cannot increase.
This proves the upper bound in (2).

For any witness in (2) remove scalar content, which leaves \(R\) unchanged and gives \(m(F)=2h+m(R)\).
Use (4) and \(Q\mid W\) to obtain
\[
          \frac{m(R)}p\ge
          \left(\frac12-\frac1{32}-\frac2p\right)h
          \ge \frac{37}{96}h>\frac h3 .
\]
A minimizing \(R\) exists because primitive integer polynomials of bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure have bounded coefficients.

<a id="degree"></a>
## The degree needed for a double root

Keep degree preservation and the two prescribed classes, and allow any prime \(p\).
Without a constraint on \(m(R)\), the least degree \(D_{\min}\) of a two-class multiple of \(P^2\) with coprime rows satisfies
\[
                        pd/2\ \le\ D_{\min}<p(d+1).                     \tag{5}
\]
For any two-row multiple \(F=x^rU(x^p)+x^sV(x^p)\), \(0\le r<s<p\), the base determinant is
\[
               W=(s-r)UV+pX(UV'-U'V).
\]
The coefficient at degree \(\deg U+\deg V\) is the leading coefficient of \(UV\) multiplied by \(s-r+p(\deg V-\deg U)\).
The multiplier is nonzero since it is congruent to \(s-r\) modulo \(p\).
Hence \(d\le\deg W=\deg U+\deg V\le2\deg F/p\), by \(Q\mid W\).
The lower bound also applies before removing any row gcd.
A single nonzero row would require \(Q^2\mid U\), giving \(\deg F\ge2pd\).

For the upper bound, take \(\deg U,\deg V\le d\).
The conditions \(F(\alpha)=F'(\alpha)=0\) impose at most \(2d\) rational linear equations on \(2d+2\) rational coefficients.
A nonzero solution gives an integral multiple after clearing denominators.
Both rows occur by the preceding single-row bound, since a row polynomial here has degree at most \(d\).
If \(Q\) divided both rows they would be constant multiples of \(Q\), say \(U=uQ,\ V=vQ\).
The factor \(Q(x^p)\) has a simple zero at \(\alpha\), so the double zero would require \(\alpha^{s-r}=-u/v\in\mathbb Q\).
Since both \(\alpha^{s-r}\) and its inverse are algebraic integers, this rational value would be \(\pm1\), contradicting nontorsion.
The common row gcd is therefore nonzero at \(\beta\).
Dividing both rows by its primitive integral representative preserves integrality by Gauss's lemma, and the resulting two-row polynomial still has a double zero at \(\alpha\).
The resulting degree is at most \(pd+s<p(d+1)\), proving (5).
This linear construction gives no coefficient estimate for the two-row solution.
The larger cap \(p(16d+1)\) in (2) allows the box argument to bound the extra measure as well.
