# Precision nine excludes both trace factors of the quartic section at 893

<a id="example"></a>
## A strict gain over the signed pole test modulo three

For \(b=893\), let \(H=1+X^3-X^{12}+X^b(X^{12}+X^9-1)\).
Its [complete cyclotomic part](Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#simple) is \((X+1)\Phi_{10}(X)\).
The remaining monic reciprocal polynomial \(R\) of degree 900 is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed, write \(R=X^{450}T(X+X^{-1})\).
The monic trace \(T\) has two distinct irreducible factors modulo 3, of degrees 199 and 251.
Both proper nonempty subsets pass the signed pole test modulo 3.
Their unique monic factor lifts modulo 9 have signed residues 7 and 4 respectively, which excludes both subsets.

For the sign convention, put \(W=C_{12}+C_9-C_3-3\), where \(C_j(X+X^{-1})=X^j+X^{-j}\).
Every monic rational factor \(h\) of \(T\) is integral, and \(X^{\deg h}h(X+X^{-1})\) is a monic reciprocal divisor of \(H/(X+1)\).
Since \(b\) is odd, the [real pole sign](Yin_L_G0462_v211.md#sign) and [trace resultant](Yin_L_G0462_v211.md#trace) force
\[
                   (-1)^{\deg h}\operatorname{Res}(W,h)=+1.
\]
Monicity preserves the degrees under reduction, and squarefree reduction makes the lift of any chosen subset and its complement unique modulo 9.
The two computed residues therefore imply irreducibility of \(T\).
The [quartic phase count](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#phase) gives 899 simple circle roots of \(H\).
After the five torsion roots are removed, \(R\) still has a circle root with trace \(y\in(-2,2)\).
At this real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(y)\), \(y^2-4<0\).
Thus \(X^2-yX+1\) is irreducible over the degree-450 trace field, proving the assertion for \(R\).

## Two Frobenius gcds and one Hensel step

The following certificate uses ascending coefficient order.
Construct \(T=R_{450}+\sum_{j=1}^{450}R_{450+j}C_j\), where \(R_i=[X^i]R\), \(C_0=2,\ C_1=Y,\ C_{j+1}=YC_j-C_{j-1}\).
Write \(\bar T=T\bmod3\).
Starting from \(z=Y\), replace \(z\) by \(z^3\bmod\bar T\) on successive steps.
The monic gcds \(\gcd(\bar T,z-Y)\) at steps \(1,199,251\) have degrees \(0,199,251\), respectively.
Call the last two gcds \(f,g\).
They satisfy \(fg=\bar T\).
Since 199 and 251 are primes, the irreducible factors of either gcd can have only degree one or the indicated prime degree, and the first gcd excludes degree one.
This certifies both irreducibilities and their coprimality.

Take \(F_0,G_0\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) to be the coefficient lifts of \(f,g\) in \(\{0,1,2\}\).
Compute in \(\mathbb F_3[Y]\)
\[
 H_1=\left((T-F_0G_0)/3\right)\bmod3,\qquad
 s=(H_1g^{-1})\bmod f,\qquad t=(H_1-sg)/f .
\]
Here \(g^{-1}\) is the inverse modulo \(f\) and the last division is exact.
One has \(\deg s<199,\ \deg t<251\).
Lifting \(s,t\) by the same coefficient convention gives \(F=F_0+3s,\ G=G_0+3t\), with \(FG\equiv T\bmod9\).
Conversely any monic pair of lifts must solve \(sg+tf=H_1\) with these degree bounds, which proves uniqueness.

Divide each lift by the monic \(W\) modulo 9.
The table gives the remainder coefficients in order \(Y^0,\ldots,Y^{11}\) and the signed resultant.

| Lift | Remainder coefficients modulo 9 | Signed residue modulo 9 |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| \(F\) of degree 199 | \(1,4,5,4,8,3,5,4,1,8,7,5\) | 7 |
| \(G\) of degree 251 | \(0,3,7,8,6,3,8,5,3,0,4,8\) | 4 |

For direct verification, \(W\bmod9=Y^{12}+6Y^{10}+Y^9+5Y^6+6Y^4+5Y^3+3Y+8\).
For each displayed remainder \(r\), form the twelve columns of \(rY^j\bmod W\), \(0\le j<12\), in the ascending power basis modulo 9.
Their determinant is \(\operatorname{Res}(W,r)\), equal to \(\operatorname{Res}(W,F)\) or \(\operatorname{Res}(W,G)\) modulo 9 by monic division.
Both lift degrees are odd, so negate this determinant for the last column.
Its entries both reduce to \(+1\) modulo 3 and multiply to \(+1\) modulo 9.
This example certifies the gain in the signed pole test for this section at the single prime 3.
