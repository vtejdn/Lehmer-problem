# Denominators of reciprocal Hermite interpolation and the cost of a centered derivative

<a id="local-denominator"></a>
## An exact local denominator away from the power index

Let \(P\) be a reciprocal nontorsion unit minimal polynomial of degree \(d\), let \(p\) be a degree-preserving odd prime, and put \(\beta=\alpha^p\) for a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\).
Write \(Q\) for the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\), \(O'=\mathbb Z[\beta]\), \(I=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:O']\), and \(h=\log M(P)\).
Fix \(1\le r<p\), \(N=2d\), \(K=pN+r\).
The [Hermite direction](Yin_L_G0462_v182.md#construction) is the unique rational \(T\) of degree \(<2d\) satisfying
\[
             T(\beta)=1-\alpha^K,\qquad
             \beta T'(\beta)=\frac{K}{2p}(1-\alpha^K).
\]
For a prime \(\ell\nmid 2pI\), put \(O'_\ell=O'\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\).
The power of \(\ell\) in the least positive common coefficient denominator of \(T\) is precisely the additive order of the class
\[
                      K(1+\alpha^K)\quad\text{in}\quad
                      O'_\ell/Q'(\beta)O'_\ell .                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular, outside \(2pI\) only primes of the discriminant of \(Q\) can occur.

Let \(B\) be the unique rational representative of degree \(<2d\) such that \(B(x^p)\equiv1-x^K\bmod P^2\).
The [index on two multiplicity layers](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#layers) is \(p^d I^4\), so this pullback is integral over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) at every \(\ell\nmid pI\).
Evaluating and differentiating at \(\alpha\) gives \(B(\beta)=1-\alpha^K\) and \(p\beta B'(\beta)=-K\alpha^K\).
Thus \(T=B+QC\) with \(\deg C<d\) and
\[
                    C(\beta)=\frac{K(1+\alpha^K)}{2p\beta Q'(\beta)} .
\]
Since \(Q\) is monic, \(\ell^jT\) has integral coefficients locally exactly when \(\ell^j C\) does.
The power basis of \(O'_\ell\) identifies the latter condition with \(\ell^j C(\beta)\in O'_\ell\).
Now \(O'_\ell=\mathbb Z_\ell[\alpha]\) and \(2p\beta\) is a unit, proving (1), including order one for the zero class.

<a id="salem"></a>
## A factor of three in a quartic Salem direction for every prime \(p\ge5\)

Take
\[
       P=x^4-x^3-x^2-x+1=x^2 f(x+x^{-1}),\qquad f(t)=t^2-t-3 .
\]
The two roots of \(f\) are \((1\pm\sqrt{13})/2\), one above two and the other in \((-2,2)\).
The quadratic \(x^2-tx+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13})\) since \(t^2-4\) is negative at the latter real embedding.
Consequently \(P\) is a Salem minimal polynomial.
Every positive power of its unique exterior root preserves degree because that root's power occurs only once in the multiset of conjugate powers, in which each distinct value occurs equally often.

Modulo three the two traces \(0,1\) are distinct simple roots of \(f\).
The original root residues above them are \(i,-i\) with \(i^2=-1\), and a double residue \(-1\), respectively.
For every prime \(p\ge5\), powering bijects these three distinct residues, and for the two roots with residue \(-1\) the quotient \((a^p-b^p)/(a-b)\) reduces to \(p(-1)^{p-1}\ne0\).
The [pair product for the power index](../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) therefore gives \(3\nmid I\).
Use \(r=2\) when \(p\equiv1\bmod3\), and \(r=4\) when \(p\equiv2\bmod3\).
Then \(K=8p+r\) is even and prime to three.

Over \(\mathbb Z_3\) Hensel lifting gives the two trace factors and separates the original order into the corresponding quadratic algebras, since their residue polynomials in \(x\) are coprime.
On the factor with trace \(t\equiv1\bmod3\), \(v_3(t+2)=1\) because \(f(-2)=3\) and the other trace does not reduce to \(-2\).
Hence \(v_3(t^2-4)=1\).
Writing \(a\) for a root in this factor, \(a-a^{-1}\) is a square root of \(t^2-4\) and generates the same order as \(a=(t+(a-a^{-1}))/2\).
This is the full ring of integers of the ramified quadratic extension because the two terms of \(u+v\sqrt{t^2-4}\) for \(u,v\in\mathbb Q_3\) have different valuation classes modulo one whenever nonzero, so integrality requires \(u,v\in\mathbb Z_3\).
The difference \(a^p-a^{-p}\) has valuation \(1/2\), since the geometric-sum quotient relative to \(a-a^{-1}\) is a unit.
The other powered roots have distinct residues from this pair.
Thus \(v_3(Q'(a^p))=1/2\) here, while \(K(1+a^K)\) is a unit since \(a\equiv-1\) and \(K\) is even.
On the factor over trace zero the powered roots are distinct from all other powered residues, so \(Q'\) is a unit there.
By (1) the coefficient denominator of \(T\) consequently has three-adic valuation exactly one for every one of these directions.

## Primitive integral polynomials on the rational line

Write \(g=Q^2\) and \(T=(u/v)T_0\), where \(T_0\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is primitive, \(u,v>0\) are coprime, and the sign is absorbed in \(T_0\).
For a nonzero rational \(\epsilon\), reduce \(\epsilon u/v=t/c\) to lowest terms with \(c>0\).
Before normalization the row is \(g+\epsilon T\).
Its primitive integral representative is
\[
                              A=cg+tT_0 .
\]
Indeed its leading coefficient is \(c\), and every common coefficient divisor must divide \(tT_0\), so primitivity follows from \((c,t)=1\).
If \(A(0)\ne0\), the row with diagonal endpoints one is \(A/A(0)\), whose exact common coefficient denominator is \(|A(0)|=|c+tT_0(0)|\).
The same denominator applies to the entire diagonal because the residue classes are disjoint.
For fixed \(\ell\), taking \(t=\ell\) and increasingly large \(c\equiv1\bmod\ell\) makes \(A(0)\) a unit at \(\ell\) even if (1) is nontrivial.
The [coprimality exceptions](Yin_L_G0462_v182.md#construction) exclude only finitely many members.

With \(P,p,r\) fixed, nonzero rational \(\epsilon\to0\) forces \(c\to\infty\) and \(A(0)/c\to1\).
The diagonal \(F_{\rm norm}\) normalized to have both endpoints one tends coefficientwise to \(Q(x^p)^2(1+x^r)\), with fixed degree and leading coefficient one.
Its logarithmic measure thus tends to \(2ph\) by continuity of roots.
For the primitive integral diagonal \(F_{\rm int}=A(0)F_{\rm norm}\), the noncyclotomic cofactor \(R\) after division by \(P^2\) consequently satisfies
\[
                   m(R)=(2p-2)h+\log|A(0)|+o(1).                       \tag{2}
\]
This diverging cost applies to approximation along the line for a fixed input and prime.
The error in (2) has no asserted uniformity over changing inputs.

<a id="freedom"></a>
## Which reciprocal double-root witnesses have this derivative

For any positive-shift [reciprocal witness](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row), write
\[
 F(x)=A(x^p)+\sigma x^{S}A^*(x^p)=A(x^p)+\sigma x^{J}A(x^{-p}),
 \quad A^*(X)=X^nA(1/X),\quad J=pn+S,\quad S>0,\ (p,S)=1 .
\]
Here \(A\in\mathbb Q[X]\), \(n=\deg A\), \(A(0)\ne0\), and \(\sigma=\pm1\).
On the field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), let \(\iota\) send \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\) and write \(K^+\) for its fixed field.
There is such an involution by reciprocity and because \(\alpha^{-1}\) generates the same field.
Since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion, the two denominators in
\[
 b=\frac{A(\beta)}{1-\sigma\alpha^J},\qquad
 z=\frac{\beta A'(\beta)-J A(\beta)/(2p)}{1+\sigma\alpha^J}             \tag{3}
\]
are nonzero.
Then \(P^2\mid F\) exactly when \(b,z\in K^+\).
Indeed the value condition is \(A(\beta)=-\sigma\alpha^J\iota A(\beta)\).
Under that condition, the Euler derivative condition is equivalent to \(Y=\sigma\alpha^J\iota Y\) for \(Y=\beta A'(\beta)-J A(\beta)/(2p)\), by differentiating the two summands of \(F\).
These are exactly the two invariances in (3).
For fixed \(J,\sigma\), arbitrary \(b,z\in K^+\) specify values and first derivatives in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and hence a unique rational class \(A\bmod Q^2\) by Hermite interpolation.
Imposing the chosen degree, row coprimality and integrality can restrict those classes.
The specific direction \(T\) has \((b,z)=(1,0)\) for \(J=K,\ \sigma=1\), so the local denominator (1) pertains to this choice of values and derivatives.

## A uniform lower bound on the whole subfamily \(z=0\)

Suppose \(A\) is integral in (3) and \(z=0\).
Then \(Q\) divides the nonzero integral polynomial
\[
                         W(X)=2pX A'(X)-J A(X).
\]
Indeed \(W(\beta)=0\) and \(W(0)=-J A(0)\ne0\).
Writing \(d\sigma_0\) for circle probability and \(C_0=\sup_z\int |x-z|^{-1/2}d\sigma_0(x)<\infty\), the [circle logarithmic derivative estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#rotation) gives
\[
                    I(F)=m(A)\ \ge\
           ph-\log(J+2p)-2\log(1+C_0n).                                \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(|W/A|\le(J+2p)(1+\sum_{A(y)=0}|x-y|^{-1})\) on the circle away from the zeros.
The log integral of the parenthesis is at most \(2\log(1+C_0n)\) by subadditivity of the square root and Jensen's inequality.
Since \(Q\) is monic integral, \(m(W)\ge m(Q)=ph\), giving (4).

For primitive integral coprime-row witnesses of this subfamily, the [diagonal comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) then gives \(m(R)/(ph)\ge1-o(1)\) throughout the [fourth-logarithmic window and cap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#finite), uniformly in the bounded-degree span \(J\le p(16d+1)\) as \(d\to\infty\).
In fact all the logarithmic errors in (4) and the comparison are \(O(\log(2+J)+\log p)\), which is \(o(ph)\) in this range, and removing \(P^2\) costs only \(2h\).
Thus for every fixed \(\delta>0\), a reciprocal double-root witness in this window and cap with \(m(R)/(ph)\le1-\delta\) must have \(z\ne0\) once \(d\) is sufficiently large.
The lower bound on \(m(R)/p\) in this subfamily is proportional to \(h\), so shrinking \(h\) leaves an absolute gap open.
