# Inert primes of exact prime order for Salem numbers

<a id="inert-prime-orders"></a>
## A density-one-half parity among prime orders

Fix a Salem number \(\alpha>1\) with minimal polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(D=2n\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and put \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) for \(\beta=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\).
Then \([K:F]=2\) and \([F:\mathbb Q]=n\).
Set \(M_D=4\prod_{3\le r\le D}r\), with the product over odd primes.
Fix a multiple \(L\) of \(M_D\) and a reduced class \(a\bmod L\) with \(a\equiv-1\bmod M_D\).
For every odd prime \(p\) in this progression put
\[
 S_p=N_{F/\mathbb Q}\big(\alpha^{-(p-1)/2}\Phi_p(\alpha)\big),
 \qquad b_p=\sum_{\ell\equiv-1\bmod p}v_\ell(|S_p|),
\]
where the sum runs over rational primes.
Then
\[
   \lim_{x\to\infty}
   \frac{\#\{p\le x:\ p\text{ prime},\ p\equiv a\bmod L,\ b_p\text{ odd}\}}
        {\#\{p\le x:\ p\text{ prime},\ p\equiv a\bmod L\}}
        =\frac12.                                                     \tag{1}
\]
For each sufficiently large \(p\) counted in the numerator there is a rational prime \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod p\) of odd valuation in \(|S_p|\).
Above it is a degree-one prime \(\mathfrak q\) of \(F\), inert in \(K/F\), at which the trace factor in \(S_p\) has odd valuation.
The reduction of \(\alpha\) at the unique prime above \(\mathfrak q\) has exact order \(p\).
Even values of \(b_p\) can also admit negative-class primes.

<a id="trace-sign"></a>
## The signed trace factor modulo its order

Write \(m=(p-1)/2\).
The inversion-invariant Laurent polynomial \(z^{-m}\Phi_p(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^m(z^j+z^{-j})\) is \(\Psi_p(z+z^{-1})\) for a monic \(\Psi_p\in\mathbb Z[Y]\), by the recurrence for \(z^j+z^{-j}\).
In particular \(S_p=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\Psi_p(\beta))\) is a nonzero integer, and \(|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\Phi_p(\alpha))|=|S_p|^2\).
In characteristic \(p\), \(\Phi_p(z)=(z-1)^{p-1}\) and \(z^{-1}(z-1)^2=z+z^{-1}-2\).
Since substitution \(Y=z+z^{-1}\) is injective on \(\mathbb F_p[Y]\), this gives \(\Psi_p(Y)\equiv(Y-2)^m\bmod p\).
Put \(A=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\beta-2)\ne0\).
Taking norms, using their integral determinant representation, and applying Euler's criterion gives
\[
       S_p\equiv A^{(p-1)/2}\equiv
       \left(\frac A p\right)\pmod p\qquad(p\nmid 2A).                \tag{2}
\]
By quadratic reciprocity and its supplements this Legendre symbol is constant on each reduced class modulo \(8|A|\).
Indeed the class fixes \(p\bmod8\) and its residues at every odd prime dividing \(A\).

At the real embedding from \(\alpha>1\) the trace factor is positive.
At the other \(s=n-1\ge1\) embeddings choose the roots \(u_j=e^{2\pi i\theta_j}\) with \(0<\theta_j<1/2\).
The corresponding factor is \(\sin(\pi p\theta_j)/\sin(\pi\theta_j)\) by the finite geometric sum.
The denominators are positive and the numerators are nonzero, hence
\[
                 \operatorname{sgn}S_p
                    =\prod_{j=1}^s\operatorname{sgn}\sin(\pi p\theta_j).
                                                                        \tag{3}
\]

<a id="prime-angles"></a>
## Prime multiples of the Salem angles in a fixed progression

On every fixed reduced progression \(c\bmod B\) the vectors \((p\theta_j/2)_{j=1}^s\) are equidistributed in \((\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^s\) as \(p\) runs over its primes.
To prove this, fix \(h\in\mathbb Z^s\setminus\{0\}\), and impose the class condition by the finite additive Fourier expansion of \(1_{p\equiv c\bmod B}\).
Each resulting phase is \(e(p\gamma)\) with \(e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)\) and \(\gamma=\sum_j h_j\theta_j/2+t/B\), \(0\le t<B\).
The number \(\xi=e(\gamma)\) is algebraic and nontorsion.
Indeed \(\xi^2\) is a root of unity times \(\prod_j u_j^{h_j}\), which is nontorsion by the [independence of the Salem conjugate pairs](Yin_L_G0462_v85.md#archimedean).
Algebraicity of \(\xi^2\) also gives that of \(\xi\).
The [Baker–Wüstholz estimate for distance to one](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle), applied to the fixed \(\xi\), gives \(\|q\gamma\|\gg_\gamma q^{-C_\gamma}\) for some \(C_\gamma\ge1\) and all \(q\ge1\), using \(|1-\xi^q|\le2\pi\|q\gamma\|\).
Here \(\|x\|\) is distance to the nearest integer.

Given a large integer \(X\), Dirichlet approximation supplies coprime \(v,q\), \(1\le q\le R=\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\), with \(|q\gamma-v|\le 1/R\).
Thus \(q\gg_\gamma R^{1/C_\gamma}\) and \(|\gamma-v/q|\le q^{-2}\).
By [Vau77, Theorem 1, (2), printed p. 136, with \(H=1\)](../audits/literature/Vau77/Yin_U001.md#prime-exponential-sum),
\[
 \left|\sum_{j\le X}\Lambda(j)e(j\gamma)\right|
   \ll \log^7(2X)\big(X q^{-1/2}+X^{4/5}+(Xq)^{1/2}\big)
   \ll_\gamma X^{1-\delta_\gamma}
\]
for some \(\delta_\gamma>0\).
Prime powers of exponent at least two contribute \(O(\sqrt X\log^2(2X))\), by summing over their \(O(\log X)\) possible exponents.
Partial summation to remove the weight \(\log p\) consequently gives \(\sum_{p\le X}e(p\gamma)=o_\gamma(X/\log X)\).
Summing the fixed \(B\) Fourier terms gives the same little-oh bound on the selected class.
The [prime number theorem for fixed progressions](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#fixed-progression-pnt) gives \(\pi(X;B,c)\sim\pi(X)/\varphi(B)\), and the [Chebyshev lower bound](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units) gives \(\pi(X)\gg X/\log X\).
The Fourier criterion for equidistribution now applies.
In particular each of the \(2^s\) sign patterns in (3) occupies a box of Haar measure \(2^{-s}\), up to its boundary of measure zero.
The sign of \(S_p\) is therefore positive and negative with equal relative natural densities on this progression.

## Recovering an inert prime of odd valuation

For \(p\nmid 2A\), (2) excludes \(p\) from \(|S_p|\).
Any rational prime \(\ell\) dividing \(|S_p|\) has a prime of \(F\) above it where \(\Psi_p(\beta)\) vanishes.
At a prime of \(K\) above that prime, \(\Phi_p(\alpha)\) vanishes, so the reduction of \(\alpha\) has exact order \(p\) since \(\ell\ne p\).
Then \(p\mid\ell^f-1\) for a residue degree \(f\le D\).
Since \(p\equiv-1\bmod M_D\), one has \(\gcd(f,p-1)\mid2\), so \(\ell\equiv\pm1\bmod p\) by cyclicity of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\), as in the [prime-order construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v4.md#orders).
Thus for large \(p\) all such \(\ell\) avoid the fixed discriminants of the minimal polynomials of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), since \(\ell\ge p-1\).
At a contributor with \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod p\), Frobenius sends \(\bar\alpha\) to \(\bar\alpha^{-1}\ne\bar\alpha\) and fixes \(\bar\beta\).
At good reduction the residue fields are generated by the respective reductions of the primitive elements, since the characteristic does not divide either power-basis index.
Thus the contributing prime of \(F\) has residue degree one, its extension to \(K\) has degree two and is inert, and its integral valuation there is preserved.
The prime contributes to \(\alpha^p-1\) with the same valuation as to \(\Psi_p(\beta)\), since \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha-1\) are units at this prime.

Rational factorization now gives \(|S_p|\equiv(-1)^{b_p}\pmod p\).
By (2), this residue is \(\operatorname{sgn}(S_p)(A/p)\).
Split the given class \(a\bmod L\) into reduced classes modulo \(\operatorname{lcm}(L,8|A|)\), omitting only the finitely many primes dividing the latter modulus.
In each refined class \((A/p)\) is fixed and (3) gives either sign with relative density one half.
Recombining the finitely many classes proves (1).
When \(b_p\) is odd at least one \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod p\) has odd valuation in \(|S_p|\).
For that \(\ell\) every contributing trace-field prime has degree one, so some such prime occurs with odd integral valuation.

For an example with \(b_p\) even and two negative-class primes, take \(\beta=2+\sqrt{13}\) and the larger root \(\alpha\) of \(z^2-\beta z+1=0\).
The other trace \(2-\sqrt{13}\) lies in \((-2,2)\), so the relative quadratic has negative discriminant at that real embedding and is irreducible over \(F\).
Thus \(\alpha\) is Salem of degree four.
For \(p=11\equiv-1\bmod12\), summing \(1+\sum_{j=1}^5(\alpha^j+\alpha^{-j})\) with the trace recurrence and \(\beta^2=4\beta+9\) gives \(\Psi_{11}(\beta)=1279+796\beta\).
Its norm is \(1279^2+4\cdot1279\cdot796-9\cdot796^2=5633=43\cdot131\), with both primes congruent to \(-1\bmod11\).

## Forcing all contributing primes above a fixed multiple of the order

Given any fixed \(H\ge1\), choose a fixed integer \(Y>\max(H+1,D,3)\), put \(M=4\prod_{3\le r\le Y+1}r\) over odd primes, and take a prime \(t>Y+1\).
Use the reduced class defined by \(p\equiv-1\bmod M\) and \(2p\equiv-1\bmod t\).
For large \(p\) in this progression, neither \(ip-1\) nor \(ip+1\) can be prime for even \(2\le i\le Y\).
An odd prime divisor of \(i+1\) divides the former, one of \(i-1\) divides the latter when \(i>2\), and \(t\mid 2p+1\).
Each of these fixed divisors is smaller than the candidate for large \(p\).
Odd \(i\) give even numbers greater than two.
This is the [fixed-modulus use of the prime-order sieve](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v4.md#orders), so every contributing \(\ell\) exceeds \(Hp\).
On this progression (1) still gives density one half for odd \(b_p\), yielding infinitely many such pairs \(p,\ell\).
The modulus here depends on \(\alpha\) and \(H\) and is fixed as \(p\to\infty\).
The density estimate supplies no uniformity when that modulus grows with the order.
