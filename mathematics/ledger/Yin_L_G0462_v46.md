# Endpoint values of integral trace transforms and a rank-one perturbation

<a id="ordered-values"></a>
## A one-sided gap for the endpoint transform

Let \(S(x)=\int(x-y)^{-1}\,d\lambda(y)\) be the transform of a finite positive measure on traces \(y=v+v^{-1}\) of roots of unity, with \(2\) excluded.
Suppose the Laurent series of \(S\) at infinity has integer coefficients.
The possible values \(s=S(2)\) contain no infinite strictly decreasing sequence.
Thus above every real \(a\) there is an interval \((a,a+\epsilon_a)\) containing no such value.
Neither the mass of \(\lambda\) nor the number of its poles needs a separate bound.

Indeed, suppose \(s_n\) decreases strictly and take a fixed integer \(k>2s_1\).
Lift each \(\lambda_n\) to a symmetric circle measure \(\widetilde\lambda_n\) by equal splitting over each trace, allowing the single point at \(-1\).
The total mass is integral, and \(2\int v^l\,d\widetilde\lambda_n\) is integral for \(l\ge1\) by the recurrence for \(v^l+v^{-l}\).
Form the positive measure
\[
      \nu_n=\frac{2\widetilde\lambda_n}{|1-v|^2}+
                           (k-2s_n)\delta_1 .
\]
It has total mass \(k\) and Fourier coefficients \(k-\int |1+\cdots+v^{l-1}|^2\,d\widetilde\lambda_n\) at positive \(l\), which are integers by expansion.
Its mass at 1 increases strictly, contradicting the [finite torsion atom theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v6.md#statement).
The interval assertion follows by choosing successively smaller values above \(a\) if there is no gap.

<a id="negative-endpoint"></a>
## A separate obstruction at the negative endpoint

Let \(C/B\) be the [positive trace transform](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) of a positive-constant Pisot polynomial, with constant \(q\), branch index \(j\), and positive numerator content \(t\).
There is an absolute \(K\) such that
\[
       -\frac{C(-2)}{B(-2)}\le t
            \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q-1\le Kt.                        \tag{1}
\]
There is no bound on \(j\) in (1).
In particular any sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\) eventually satisfies \(-C(-2)/B(-2)>t\).

Put \(V=C/t\), which is integral, and \(r=V/B\).
The monic polynomial \(B+V\) is the denominator of the transformed fraction \(S=r/(1+r)\).
The roots of \(B+V\) are the solutions of \(r=-1\), since \(V,B\) are coprime.
There is one such root to the left of the least pole and one strictly between every two successive poles.
This follows from the positive residues of \(r\) and its negative derivative on each real gap.
If \(r(-2)\ge-1\), all these zeros are at least \(-2\), since the least pole exceeds \(-2\).
If \(q-1>t\), then \(r(2)=-(q-1)/t<-1\) places the last zero below 2, using the fact that only one pole of \(r\) lies above 2.
The same statements hold with one pole, when the sole zero is to its left.
Every zero of \(B+V\) is therefore a torsion trace, by the monic circle lift and Kronecker's theorem.
The residues of \(S=1-1/(1+r)\) are positive, its constant term at infinity is zero, and it has integral Laurent coefficients since \(S=V/(B+V)\) has monic denominator.
Its endpoint value is
\[
                 S(2)=1+\frac{t}{q-1-t}.
\]
The [gap above one](#ordered-values) gives \(q-1\le(1+1/\epsilon_1)t\).
Taking \(K=1+1/\epsilon_1\) covers also \(q-1\le t\).
The [exact derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(j/(q-1)\to0\) on a near-integer sequence, so \((q-1)/t\to\infty\) since \(t\le j\).

In the [inverse simplex](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#endpoints), write \(c=j/t\).
Here \(F(-2)=-4-j/(q-1)+4E<0\) and \(C(-2)/B(-2)=j/F(-2)\).
Thus for all \(q-1>Kt\) the additional lower bound is
\[
                E>1-\frac c4+\frac{j}{4(q-1)} .
\]
For the surviving \(j=2,c=2\) case it discards the entire lower half of the range \(0<E<E_*\) asymptotically.
The odd-degree equality face already satisfies this asymptotic lower bound.
