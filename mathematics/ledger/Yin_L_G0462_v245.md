# Computing the finite coefficient minimum by reduced lattice bases

<a id="minimum"></a>
## An exact algorithm with the row degree as parameter

Let \(M_p(b)\) denote the [finite coefficient minimum](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) with row degree at most \(b\ge1\), for an odd prime \(p\).
There is a computable function \(f\) such that a deterministic algorithm returns \(M_p(b)\) in at most \(f(b)(p+1)^4\) bit operations with classical arithmetic.
It can also return a minimizing pair whenever a genuine pair attains the answer.
The bound is polynomial in the numerical value of \(p\), and its exponent is absolute even when \(b\) is supplied as a parameter.
The initial candidate \(2^p\) itself has \(p+1\) bits.

Suppose a pair \(U,V\) of cost at most \(2^p\) qualifies with a repeated factor \(P\).
For a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), coprimality gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The minimum \(Q\) of \(\alpha^p\) has \(Q^2\mid Y^b(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)\ne0\), and \(p\,m(P)\le\log\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), by the [single-prime lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
Thus \(e=\deg P=\deg Q\le b\), \(m(P)\le\log2\), and \(|[X^i]P|\le2\binom ei\) by the elementary symmetric formulas.
Enumerate all monic reciprocal irreducible unit polynomials in these finite coefficient boxes and discard cyclotomic polynomials.
This preprocessing depends only on \(b\).
For example, reducibility can be tested by enumerating monic integral proper factors whose coefficients have absolute value at most \(2^e\|P\|_1\), by Gauss's lemma and the same root bound for each factor.
One can test divisibility of \(X^n-1\) by each irreducible candidate for \(n\le2b^2\), since \(\varphi(n)^2\ge n/2\) by the prime-power formula.
Candidates above the measure cutoff can stay in the list because every accepted pair witnesses the original conditions.

Given an integer threshold \(1\le T\le2^p\), consider each listed \(P\) and both exact row degrees \(u,v\in\{0,\ldots,b\}\).
Use variables \(x\) for the coefficients of \(U,V\) up to these degrees.
Impose the vanishing of the coefficients of
\[
             U(X^p)+XV(X^p) \pmod{P(X)^2}.
\]
These are integral linear equations, since \(P^2\) is monic.
Computing successive powers of \(X\) through \(pb+1\) in this quotient takes \(O_b(p+1)\) matrix-vector products with a fixed integral matrix whose entries are bounded in terms of \(b\).
The resulting coefficients have \(O_b(p+1)\) bits by submultiplicativity of a matrix norm.
The inequalities \(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1\le T\) have a linear description by all sign vectors of the respective dimensions.
They and the linear equations define a bounded rational polytope \(K\) of dimension and constraint count bounded in terms of \(b\).

Put \(d_t(x)=[Y^t]\bigl(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\bigr)\) for \(-b\le t\le b\), with \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\), and form
\[
 W(x)=([Y^u]U)([Y^v]V)\operatorname{Res}_{u,v}(U,V)
                          \sum_{t=-b}^{b}d_t(x)^2 .
\]
Use the resultant for the indicated degrees, with the empty determinant equal to one for two nonzero constants.
The leading factors ensure that \(u,v\) are the actual degrees and both rows are nonzero.
The remaining factors enforce coprimality over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\) and unequal reflected squares.
Thus a pair of these degrees qualifies for \(P\) exactly when an integral point in \(K\) has \(W(x)\ne0\).
The degree of \(W\) is at most \(2b+6\) and its coefficients depend only on \(b,u,v\).

## Finding a lattice point off a polynomial zero set

Consider a bounded rational polytope in \(\mathbb R^k\) given by at most \(m\) rational linear inequalities, and a rational polynomial \(W\) of degree at most \(\delta\) in dense representation.
Write \(L\ge1\) for the maximum bit length of a numerator or denominator in these data.
We give a search taking at most \(F(k,m,\delta)(L+1)^3\) bit operations for a computable function \(F\).
The maximal-volume simplex and the recursion on parallel lattice hyperplanes use the geometry of [Lenstra's fixed-dimensional integer programming algorithm, §1 and §2, Remark (c)](../audits/literature/Len83/readme.md#geometry).

Enumerate vertices by solving all nonsingular \(k\)-tuples of active constraints and checking feasibility, writing an equation as two inequalities.
Every nonempty bounded polytope has a vertex, and the normals active at any vertex span the ambient space.
From the vertices compute the affine hull by rational elimination.
If its dimension is smaller than \(k\), express its integer points as \(x=x_0+Mz\), \(z\in\mathbb Z^{k'}\), or declare it disjoint from \(\mathbb Z^k\).
For this step clear denominators in each affine equation and use extended gcd column operations to send its integral coefficient row to \((g,0,\ldots,0)\).
An inconsistent zero row or a right side indivisible by \(g>0\) certifies emptiness.
Otherwise remove tautologies or fix the first coordinate, and substitute in the remaining equations, keeping the integral affine parametrization given by the unimodular column operations.
Substitute the resulting map in the inequalities and polynomial.
In dimension zero evaluate \(W\) at the feasible point.

In full positive dimension choose a simplex of vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_k\) of largest absolute determinant and put \(B=(v_i-v_0)_{1\le i\le k}\) with these differences as columns.
Under \(y=B^{-1}(x-v_0)\) the image \(K'\) contains the standard simplex and lies in \([-1,1]^k\).
Indeed replacing the \(i\)-th column by the difference of another vertex and \(v_0\) bounds the absolute value of the \(i\)-th transformed coordinate by one, and the bound extends by convexity.
The transformed integer points lie in \(\tau+B^{-1}\mathbb Z^k\), where \(\tau=-B^{-1}v_0\).
Obtain a [reduced basis](../audits/literature/LLL82/Yin_U001.md#reduction) \(w_1,\ldots,w_k\) of the linear lattice by clearing a common denominator, applying LLL and undoing the scaling.
Write \(G=\|w_k^*\|_2\) for the length of the last Gram–Schmidt vector.
The reduced basis inequality gives \(\|w_i\|_2\le2^kG\) for every \(i\).

The cube of sup-norm radius \(\rho=1/(2k(k+1))\) about \(a=(1/(k+1),\ldots,1/(k+1))\) lies in the standard simplex.
If \(G\le\rho/(k2^k(\delta+1))\), round the coordinates of \(a-\tau\) in the basis \(w_i\) to integers \(n_i\).
All the points
\[
           \tau+\sum_{i=1}^k(n_i+j_i)w_i,\qquad 0\le j_i\le\delta,\quad j_i\in\mathbb Z,
\]
lie in this cube by the triangle inequality.
Map them back to integral \(x\) and test \(W\).
If all tests give zero then the polynomial vanishes identically on this affine coordinate space, by the univariate root bound applied successively to the grid directions.

When \(G>\rho/(k2^k(\delta+1))\), each candidate lattice point has integral last basis coordinate
\[
                 t=\langle y-\tau,w_k^*\rangle/G^2 .
\]
Its real range over \(K'\) has length at most \(2\sqrt{k}/G\), since the diameter of the containing cube is \(2\sqrt{k}\).
Compute the endpoints of this interval at the vertices and enumerate the integers it contains.
There are at most a computable function of \(k,\delta\) such integers.
For each \(t\), substitute \(x=\sum_{i<k}(Bw_i)n_i+(Bw_k)t\) and recurse in the integral variables \(n_i\).
The columns \(Bw_i\) form a unimodular matrix, so this covers exactly the required slices of the original lattice.
The case distinction uses the square \(G^2\) and rational arithmetic.

## Controlling the sizes through the recursion

For fixed \(k,m,\delta\), a vertex, an affine hull equation, the matrix \(B\), and its inverse all have bit length \(O_{k,m,\delta}(L+1)\) per entry by determinant expansion.
The number of vertices and simplices considered depends only on \(k,m\).
In the affine hull parametrization the elementary two-column gcd transformation for entries \(a,b\) can use Bézout entries of magnitude at most \(1+\max(|a|,|b|)\), along with \(a/g,b/g\).
Zero entries are handled by permutations or skipped.
After each such transformation the bit lengths increase by at most a dimension-dependent constant factor, including the translation by a fixed integral coordinate.
Only a dimension-dependent number of these transformations are needed after choosing independent affine equations.
Both the integral parametrization and all inequalities obtained by substitution therefore have \(O_{k,m,\delta}(L+1)\)-bit data.

Clearing the entries of \(B^{-1}\) uses at most \(k^2\) denominators.
The integral input basis to LLL has squared norms with \(O_{k,m,\delta}(L+1)\) bits.
The [integer complexity bound for LLL](../audits/literature/LLL82/Yin_U001.md#complexity) gives \(O_{k,m,\delta}((L+1)^3)\) bit operations and \(O_{k,m,\delta}(L+1)\)-bit operands.
The scaled-back basis, its Gram–Schmidt data, vertex layer coordinates, the integer layer values and the entries of \(Bw_i\) have the same linear bit bound by rational arithmetic in fixed dimension.
This also bounds the integers obtained by rounding in the small-\(G\) case.
Substitution into a degree-\(\delta\) polynomial forms products of at most \(\delta\) affine terms for each original monomial and sums a parameter-bounded number of terms.
The resulting coefficients likewise use \(O_{k,m,\delta}(L+1)\) bits.

Apart from LLL and extended gcd, each node takes a parameter-bounded number of rational arithmetic operations.
Ordinary extended Euclid on \(n\)-bit integers uses \(O(n)\) divisions with \(O(n)\)-bit operands and Bézout coefficients, so the classical \(O((n+1)^3)\) bit bound suffices also for rational reduction.
At most \(k\) dimension decreases occur on each branch, the number of inequalities stays bounded in terms of the original parameters, and the number of children at each slice depends only on those parameters.
The successive linear bit bounds thus compose to \(O_{k,m,\delta}(L+1)\), and summing the cubic node bounds proves the claimed \(F(k,m,\delta)(L+1)^3\).

For the coefficient problem all these parameters are bounded computably by \(b\) and \(L=O_b(p+1)\).
The seed preprocessing depends only on \(b\), and the modular matrix columns themselves can be formed in \(O_b((p+1)^3)\) bit operations by the successive products above.
Binary search on \([1,2^p]\) uses \(O(p+1)\) threshold queries, accepting the terminal value also as the initial candidate.
A final search without that convention returns a witness if a genuine minimizing pair exists at the terminal value.
This proves the stated bound for the exact finite minimum.
