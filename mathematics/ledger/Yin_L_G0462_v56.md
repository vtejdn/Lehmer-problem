# The positive sign at every fixed Chebyshev offset

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded positive-constant inputs

Fix integers \(d,s\ge1\) and \(\eta>0\).
On the positive-constant Pisot branch \(j=2\), write \(h=\deg C\) for the degree of the [trace numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) and \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^hC(z+z^{-1})\).
If \(\Pi\) has at most \(s\) nonzero terms and agrees with \(A=2+z^d\) through degree \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\), then its constant parameter \(q\) is bounded in terms of \(d,s,\eta\).
Consequently the Pisot gap \(\theta-q\) has a uniform positive lower bound here by the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
This includes \(C(x)=2U_h(x/2)+U_{h-d}(x/2)\) for every fixed \(d\), since its lift is \(2+z^d-z^{2h+2-d}(1+2z^d)\) and \(h\ge d\).
The [negative-sign theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#statement) already handles the positive sign for odd \(d\).
The following proof allows even \(d\) as well.

<a id="slopes"></a>
## The phase grid for each primitive offset

Suppose \(q\to\infty\).
The [sparse numerator and coefficient argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#harmonics) applies with \(A=2+z^d\).
Indeed \(A\) has constant two, is irreducible by Eisenstein at two, and satisfies \(|A|\ge1\) on the circle.
The integral limiting reciprocal \(A/K\) and the integer rational limit argument from the [fixed-offset classification](Yin_L_G0462_v55.md#bracket) therefore give \(K=A U/V\), for coprime squarefree signed cyclotomic products \(U,V\) normalized at zero.
Here \(h\to\infty\) and the bounded cutoffs in the sparse numerator argument give \(n/h\to0\) for the first index \(n\) at which the lifted integer denominator differs from \(V/U\).
Those cutoffs use the bound on \(\Pi\), the common linear prefix, bounded circle suprema of the fixed factors, and \(|A|\ge1\).
The continuous density \(\rho=2\Re K\) away from atoms is a nonzero nonnegative Laurent polynomial.
Put \(g=\gcd(d,\deg V-\deg U-d)\), \(a=d/g,\ b=(\deg V-\deg U-d)/g\) and \(\epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\in\{1,-1\}\).
Reciprocity gives \(\rho=(U/V)P(z^g)\), where
\[
                  P(w)=(2+w^a)+\epsilon w^b(2w^a+1).
\]
Every odd-order circle zero of \(P\) must be torsion, because \(U,V\) have only torsion roots and the circle zeros of \(\rho\) have even order.
Also \(M(\rho)=M(P)\) for multiplicative Mahler measure.

For odd \(a\), \(P(-w)=(2-w^a)+\delta w^b(2w^a-1)\) with \(\delta=\epsilon(-1)^{b+1}\).
The odd-order zero condition therefore imposes the [same primitive slope table](Yin_L_G0462_v55.md#fixed) for \((a,b,\delta)\), since that calculation uses only this condition.
The [negative-sign measure evaluations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#brackets) give \(M(P)<2\sqrt2\) except at \(a=1\) with \(b=-1,\delta=1\) or \(b=0,\delta=-1\).
In the first exception \(P\) has no torsion roots, so \(U=V=1\).
In the second \(P(w)=3(1+w)\) and \(\deg V-\deg U=d\).
Even multiplicities in \(\rho\) and squarefreeness give \(UV=1+z^d\), hence \(U=1,\ V=1+z^d\).
This identifies the two exceptional \(K\) directly in the original variable for all \(g\).

For even \(a\), \(b\) is odd and replacing \(w\) by \(-w\) interchanges the two signs of \(\epsilon\).
Parameterize the \(a\) sectors by \(w=\exp(i((2r+1)\pi+t)/a)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\), so \(w^a=-e^{it}\).
The zero equation is
\[
           w^b\,\frac{2e^{it}-1}{2-e^{it}}=\epsilon .
\]
At a torsion zero \(e^{it}\) must be \(1,-1\), or a primitive sixth root by the [torsion argument](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#phase) applied to \(e^{it}\) and \((2-e^{it})/(2e^{it}-1)=\epsilon w^b\).
Take \(f(t)=bt/a+\arg((2e^{it}-1)/(2-e^{it}))\) with its continuous argument starting at zero.
The levels of \(af(t)/(2\pi)\) giving zeros on the \(a\) sectors range over \(\mathbb Z+1/2\).
Indeed the sector offset is \(rb+b/2\) in those units, the target phase is a multiple of \(a\) or a translate by \(a/2\), \(a/2\) is integral, and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).

Putting \(j_0=a+b\), the five phase values at \(0,\pi/3,\pi,5\pi/3,2\pi\) remain
\[
             0,\qquad (a+j_0)/6,\qquad j_0/2,\qquad
                      (5j_0-a)/6,\qquad j_0 .
\]
As in the [four-arc test](Yin_L_G0462_v55.md#bracket), consecutive values cannot strictly straddle a half-integer, since an odd crossing in the intervening open arc would be nontorsion.
The four steps have lengths at most one, giving \(|j_0|\le4,\ |a+j_0|\le6,\ |2j_0-a|\le6\), hence \(a\le6\).
Equality forces \(j_0=0\), impossible by coprimality, so \(a=2\) or \(4\).
For either choice the odd \(j_0\) with \(|j_0|\le4\) are \(-3,-1,1,3\).
Testing the four open intervals against \(\mathbb Z+1/2\) leaves \(j_0=-1,1\) for \(a=2\) and \(j_0=-1\) for \(a=4\).

For the plus choice \(\epsilon=1\), the resulting factorizations after multiplication by \(w^{-b}\) are as follows.

| \(a,b\) | \(w^{-b}P(w)\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(2,-3\) | \((w+1)(w^4-w^3+3w^2-w+1)\) |
| \(2,-1\) | \((w+1)(w^2+w+1)\) |
| \(4,-5\) | \((w+1)(w^2+w+1) w^3(X^3-2X^2-X+3)\), \(X=w+w^{-1}\) |

The quartic in the first row is the [computed quartic of measure below \(5/2\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#brackets) under \(w\mapsto-w\).
The second row has measure one.
In the third, the cubic has no rational root and discriminant \(-31\), so its real root in \((-2,-1)\) is simple and has nonreal conjugates.
It cannot be a torsion trace, since all conjugates of a torsion trace are real.
Its inverse pair therefore gives simple nontorsion zeros of \(P\), excluding this row.
Thus every allowed even primitive offset has \(M(P)<2\sqrt2\).

## Finite shapes and specialization for the positive sign

The moving harmonic rows of the [sparse numerator argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#harmonics) now have the form
\[
    H_k=(1+z^d/2)\sigma c_k\in\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}],\qquad
    |H_k|\le D=|1+z^d/2|\sigma,\qquad H_1\ne0 .
\]
Here \(\sigma=2\Re(2/K)\) denotes the continuous circle density, \(c_k\) are conditional probability moments, and \(M(D)=M(\rho)/2\).
The coefficient argument uses the indicated two-term multiplier with constant one and stays within the common prefix for every fixed harmonic and offset.
When \(M(P)<2\sqrt2\), the [finite-shape lemma](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes) furnishes finitely many \(H_k\) up to monomial shifts.

In the two exceptional cases put \(w=z^d\), \(X=w+w^{-1}\).
For \(K=2+z^d\), \(D^2=(4+X)^2/(5+2X)\) has mean \(7/2\), is bounded by four, and equals four only at \(w=\pm1\), by changing \(w\) to \(-w\) in the [exceptional envelope](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#exceptions).
The same fourth-moment argument leaves only unit monomials and unit binomials, with the exponent difference of a binomial at most \(2d\) by counting alignment points.
Here \(M(D)=(2+\sqrt3)/2<2\).
For \(K=(2+z^d)/(1+z^d)\), \(D=3|1+z^d|^2/|2+z^d|\).
Every \(H_k\) is divisible by \((1+z^d)^2\) by quadratic vanishing at its simple circle roots.
The integer Laurent quotient \(P_0\) has squared circle norm bounded by the mean of \(9/|2+z^d|^2\), which is three.
Equality would make \((2+z^d)P_0\) a constant-modulus monomial, impossible at a root of \(2+z^d\).
The quotient thus has at most two unit terms.
A unit binomial with exponent difference other than \(d\) gives mean squared modulus ten for \((2+z^d)P_0\) by orthogonality, exceeding the pointwise bound nine.
Again the shapes are finite, and \(M(D)=3/2<2\).

The [prime specialization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) applies to each finite list: the phase ratio for a representative \(L\) is now
\[
                   \frac{L(z)(2+z^{-d})}{L(z^{-1})(2+z^d)} .
\]
At any root \(\alpha\) of \(2+z^d\), the numerator is nonzero since \(M(L)<2\), \(2+z^d\) is irreducible, and \(2+\alpha^{-d}=3/2\).
The ratio therefore has a pole of modulus \(2^{1/d}>1\), excluding the rational squaring identity by iteration.
All remaining steps of the specialization use only that nonidentity, integer Laurent rows, conditional positivity at the regular circle points, and \(M(D)<2\).
They give the same contradiction.
