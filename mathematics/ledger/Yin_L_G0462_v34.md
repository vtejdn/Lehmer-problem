# Angular distribution of the interior conjugates of a Pisot root near its positive constant

<a id="geometry"></a>
## A uniform estimate for the original conjugate directions

Let \(f\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta\), with \(q=f(0)\ge2\), \(\delta=\theta-q>0\), and \(n=\deg f-1\).
Write \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n\) for its interior roots and \(v_i=\beta_i/|\beta_i|\) for their directions.
For \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le k\le n\), we have the absolute bound
\[
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n v_i^k\right|
 \ll \sqrt{\frac{k}{n}\bigl(\log(2n)+\log(2+\delta)\bigr)}
      +\frac{k}{n}\bigl(1+\log(\theta/q)\bigr).                       \tag{1}
\]
In particular these directions become uniformly distributed on the circle whenever \(n\to\infty\) and \(\log(2+\delta)=o(n)\), with no bound on \(\log q/n\).
Thus every fixed arc contains its normalized length times \(n\), up to \(o(n)\), of the interior conjugate directions of any near-integer sequence with \(\delta\to0\).
All these conjugates also have moduli tending uniformly to one, since \(\sum_i-\log|\beta_i|=\log(\theta/q)\).
The main researcher proposed studying the root geometry of Boyd's construction, and the proof contributor derived the circle crossing estimate, angular transfer and totally real bound below.

<a id="crossings"></a>
## Counting roots of the associate on a circular arc

Set \(g=z^{n+1}f(1/z)\), \(D=zf+g\) and \(T=zf/g\).
The [positive delay-one construction](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) gives exactly one exterior root \(\sigma>1\) and one interior root \(\sigma^{-1}\) of \(D\), both simple, with \(\sigma\le2+\tfrac92\delta\).
Its other \(n\) roots \(w_j\), counted with multiplicities, lie on the unit circle, where \(T\) has modulus one and \(g\) never vanishes.
The winding number of \(T\) there is \(n\), since \(zf\) has \(n+1\) interior zeros and \(g\) has one.
The \(w_j\) are precisely the preimages of \(-1\) on the circle.

Take a continuous lift \(\Psi(x)\) of \(\arg T(e^{ix})\) over a full circuit, starting away from those preimages.
Each fiber point contributes \(+1\) or \(-1\) for an upward or downward crossing of a level in \(\pi+2\pi\mathbb Z\), and zero for a tangency.
The sum is the winding number, by the total change of the integer part of \((\Psi-\pi)/(2\pi)\) between the two endpoints.
At each point its contribution is bounded above by its multiplicity in \(D\).
The multiplicities sum to \(n\), so each preimage is simple and each crossing is upward.
Consequently on an arc with endpoints off the fiber its number of \(w_j\) differs from the argument increment divided by \(2\pi\) by at most one.
The same bound holds on half-open arcs by moving both endpoints consistently and taking limits.

For \(|a|<1\) denote the Poisson kernel by \(P_a(x)=(1-|a|^2)/|e^{ix}-a|^2\).
Its mean for \(dx/(2\pi)\) is one and its \(k\)-th moment against \(e^{ikx}\) is \(a^k\) for \(k\ge1\), by the geometric series.
Logarithmic differentiation of \((z-a)/(1-\bar a z)\) at \(z=e^{ix}\) gives \(P_a(x)\) as the derivative of its argument with respect to \(x\).
Pairing complex conjugates in the denominator of \(f/g\) gives
\[
                    \Psi'(x)=1-P_{1/\theta}(x)+\sum_i P_{\beta_i}(x).
\]
Indeed the exterior factor \((z-\theta)/(1-\theta z)\) is the inverse of the Blaschke factor for \(1/\theta\).
The measure \(\sum_j\delta_{w_j}-\Psi'(x)dx/(2\pi)\) has total mass zero and its mass on any arc has absolute value at most one.
Integrating by parts against \(e^{ikx}\) with its cumulative mass based off the fiber yields
\[
          \left|\sum_j w_j^k-\left(\sum_i\beta_i^k-\theta^{-k}\right)\right|
            \le 2\pi k.                                                \tag{2}
\]
The use of angles of the original roots costs at most \(\sum_i(1-|\beta_i|^k)\le k\log(\theta/q)\) in this sum.

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## Discriminant of the full associate

Simplicity makes \(\operatorname{disc}(D)\) a nonzero integer even when \(D\) has several irreducible factors.
Apply the [discriminant and regularized logarithm calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0390_v1.md#actual-powered-salem-discriminant-energy) to its complete root set.
Indeed, separating \(\sigma,\sigma^{-1}\) from the ordered neutral pairs in the discriminant and using \(|\sigma-\sigma^{-1}|\le\sigma\), \(|w_j-\sigma|\le2\sigma\), \(|w_j-\sigma^{-1}|\le2\) gives
\[
     \sum_{i\ne j}\log\frac1{|w_i-w_j|}
             \le 2(n+1)\log\sigma+4n\log2 .
\]
For \(t=1-1/n\), put \(S_k=\sum_j w_j^k\).
The absolutely convergent logarithmic series, together with \(|1-tu|^2\ge t|1-u|^2\) for \(|u|=1\), gives
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{t^k}{k}|S_k|^2
 =\sum_{i,j}\log\frac{1}{|1-tw_i\bar w_j|}
 \le n\log n+2(n+1)\log\sigma+4n\log2-\tfrac12 n(n-1)\log t .
\]
The right side is \(O(n[\log(2n)+\log(2+\delta)])\), since \(-\log t\le1/(n-1)\).
For \(k\le n\) we have \(t^k\ge1/4\).
Bounding the individual term and using (2) proves (1).
Under its growth conditions every fixed nonconstant Fourier moment tends to zero, giving convergence to normalized arc length by trigonometric approximation.
If \(\delta\to0\), then \(n\to\infty\) automatically, since the \(\sigma>1\) tend to one and bounded degree monic \(D\) with bounded roots have only finitely many possible integer coefficient vectors.

<a id="real-conjugates"></a>
## A sharp gap for totally real seeds

Suppose \(u\ge1\) of the interior roots are real and \(v=n-u\) are nonreal.
The two integers \(f(1),f(-1)\) are nonzero, hence
\[
           1\le |f(1)f(-1)|
              \le(\theta^2-1)\,2^v
                      \left(\frac{2\log(\theta/q)}{u}\right)^u.       \tag{3}
\]
Each nonreal inner root contributes at most two to the product of \(|1-\beta_i^2|\).
For the real inner roots the positive factors \(1-\beta_i^2\le-2\log|\beta_i|\) have sum at most \(2\log(\theta/q)\), so the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives (3).

If all conjugates of \(\theta\) are real, then \(\theta-q>1\).
Indeed, for \(n\ge3\) and \(\delta\le1\), (3) would imply
\[
                1<(q+1)^2\left(\frac{2}{nq}\right)^n
                          \le\frac9{n^n}<1 ,
\]
where \(\log(1+\delta/q)\le\delta/q\) and \(q\ge2\).
For \(n=2\) write \(f=z^3-a z^2+bz+q\).
The two interior roots have opposite signs by their product \(-q/\theta\).
We have \(f(1),f(-1)\le-1\) by the root signs and integrality, so \(2(q-a)=f(1)+f(-1)\le-2\).
If \(a\ge q+2\), the two interior roots have sum strictly less than one, and the trace gives \(\theta>q+1\).
Otherwise \(a=q+1\) and \(f(1)=f(-1)=-1\), which forces \(b=-1\).
Then \(f(q+1)=-1\), again giving \(\theta>q+1\).
For \(n=1\) the quadratic has one negative coefficient, so the [dominant-coefficient bound and its quadratic examples](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#dominant-coefficient) give the strict inequality and show the infimum in the totally real class is exactly one.
The angular and real-root restrictions leave open the existence of near-integer sequences with unrestricted nonreal conjugates.
