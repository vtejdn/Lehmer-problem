# A squared Salem rate for the coefficient cost at quartic width

<a id="width"></a>
## The initial candidate is eventually the exact minimum at every width up to four

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a quartic Salem number \(\rho>1\).
Its remaining conjugates are \(\rho^{-1},\eta,\eta^{-1}\), with \(|\eta|=1\).
Suppose \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) have degree at most four, \(UU^\iota\ne VV^\iota\), where \(U^\iota(Y)=U(1/Y)\), and \(P(x)^2\mid U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\).
With \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), for all sufficiently large positive integers \(p\) every such pair satisfies
\[
                    \log H\ \ge\ 2p\log\rho-o_P(p).                    \tag{1}
\]
The error depends on the fixed seed and is uniform in the coefficient lists.
No row coprimality is needed for this inequality.

Consequently the [finite minimum of C408](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) is exactly \(M_{p,b}=2^p\) for **every sufficiently large odd prime** and every \(1\le b\le4\).
Indeed the [width and trace classification](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#short) already settles \(b\le3\) for every prime and reduces width four below or at the cutoff to two quartic seeds up to change of sign.
Their positive exterior traces are \((1+\sqrt{13})/2\) and \(1+\sqrt2\), both greater than \(3/\sqrt2=\sqrt2+1/\sqrt2\).
Thus \(\rho^2>2\) for both, and the two sign changes have the same cost at odd \(p\).
There is eventually no qualifying pair even at the cutoff.
The [bounded calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/readme.md#census) covers all odd primes up to \(1009\).
The proof of (1) gives no numerical onset linking these ranges.

Use formal reversal \(A^*(Y)=Y^4 A(1/Y)\).
The two polynomials \(A=U+V^*\) and \(A=U-V^*\) have \(\|A\|_1\le2H\) and yield, respectively, the plus and minus graphs
\[
                   P(x)^2\mid A(x^p)+\varepsilon x A^*(x^p).
\]
Indeed reversal about \(4p+1\) preserves the double factor.
Both polynomials are nonzero by the unequal moduli; they are the two components in the [minimal-width decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines).
Write \(A(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^4 a_iY^i\).
We first bound the integer \(a_4-a_0\) from below when it is nonzero and then show that at least one of these two rows has nonzero difference.

<a id="collisions"></a>
## Divisibility at collisions of the two inverse pairs

Write \(y=\rho^p,\ v=\eta^p,\ S_1=y+y^{-1},\ S_2=v+v^{-1}\), and take the fixed splitting field \(L\) of \(P\).
At a finite prime ideal \(\mathfrak l\) of \(L\) let \(\nu=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}\), with \(\nu(0)=+\infty\).
Set
\[
 r_\pm=\nu(y-v^{\pm1}),\qquad t=\nu(y-y^{-1}),\qquad
 t'=\nu(v-v^{-1}),\qquad c_\pm=\nu(\rho-\eta^{\pm1}).
\]
All are finite nonnegative integers since the bases are units, the two inverse pairs are distinct, \(\rho>1\), and \(\eta\) is nontorsion.
In particular \(r_++r_-=\nu(S_1-S_2)\).

For either eigenrow set \(D=\varepsilon A^*\).
For conjugates \(x=\rho,\eta,\eta^{-1}\), with \(w=x^p\), the value and Euler derivative equations are
\[
         A(w)+xD(w)=0,\qquad
         pw A'(w)+x\big(pwD'(w)+D(w)\big)=0.
\]
Compare each equation at \(x=\rho\) and \(x=\eta^{\pm1}\) modulo \(\mathfrak l^{r_\pm}\), replacing \(w\) by \(y\) in the second evaluation.
Subtraction shows that \(D(y)\) and \(pyD'(y)+D(y)\) have valuation at least \(r_\pm-c_\pm\).
The same lower bound follows for \(A(y),pA'(y),pD'(y)\) from the equations and the fact that \(y\) is a unit.
This comparison requires no unramifiedness and no inversion of \(p\).

Put \(f(Y)=Y^{-2}A(Y)\), \(\delta=Y-Y^{-1}\), \(S=Y+Y^{-1}\), and \(\mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\).
The Laurent identity
\[
        f-f^\iota=\delta O(S),\qquad
        O(S)=a_3-a_1+(a_4-a_0)S
\]
uses exactly the width-four support.
Since \(f^\iota=\varepsilon Y^{-2}D\), both \(p(f-f^\iota)\) and \(p\mathcal E(f-f^\iota)\) at \(y\) have valuation at least \(r_\pm-c_\pm\).
The combination \(\delta\mathcal E(f-f^\iota)-S(f-f^\iota)=\delta^3(a_4-a_0)\) therefore gives
\[
              r_\pm\le\nu\!\left(p(a_4-a_0)\right)+c_\pm+3t .
\]
One has \(\min(r_+,r_-)\le t\), since closeness of \(y\) to both \(v\) and \(v^{-1}\) to precision \(m\) implies \(y^2\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak l^m\).
Also \(\min(r_\pm,t)\le t'\), by comparing the squares of \(y\) and \(v^{\pm1}\).
Bounding the larger \(r_\pm\) by the displayed inequality and the smaller by \(t\) gives
\[
 r_++r_-\le\nu\!\left(p(a_4-a_0)\right)+c_++c_-+8\min(t,t').         \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the initial error \(4t\) can be replaced by \(4\min(t,r_++r_-)\) because the remaining terms are nonnegative.
Then \(\min(t,r_++r_-)\le\min(t,r_+)+\min(t,r_-)\le2\min(t,t')\).

The units \(\rho,\eta\) are multiplicatively independent by taking complex absolute values in a proposed relation and then using nontorsion of \(\eta\).
Their \(2p\)-th powers remain independent and give distinct pairs as \(p\) increases.
The [Corvaja–Zannier bound for common unit differences](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd), with all archimedean places of this fixed \(L\), gives
\[
  \frac{1}{[L:\mathbb Q]}\sum_{\mathfrak l}\min(t,t')\log N\mathfrak l=o_P(p).
\]
Here \(t,t'\) are exactly the valuations of \(\rho^{2p}-1,\eta^{2p}-1\), and the absolute logarithmic heights of the two powers are \(O_P(p)\).
This is the [fixed-seed overlap estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#overlap) applied to the two different inverse pairs.
The summed \(c_\pm\) are bounded by the fixed norms of \(\rho-\eta^{\pm1}\).
The conjugates of \(S_1-S_2\) are among \(\pm(S_1-S_2)\), since Galois permutes the original two inverse pairs and hence their powered traces.
Its normalized log norm is therefore \(\log|S_1-S_2|=p\log\rho+O_P(1)\).
Summing (2) with the same weights gives, whenever \(a_4-a_0\ne0\),
\[
                         \log|a_4-a_0|\ge p\log\rho-o_P(p),             \tag{3}
\]
after absorbing the factor \(p\).

<a id="edges"></a>
## The two signs cannot both have equal edge coefficients

Write \(x_1=\rho,\ x_2=\eta,\ t_i=x_i+x_i^{-1},\ d_i=x_i-x_i^{-1},\
\delta_i=x_i^p-x_i^{-p}\), and \(S_i=x_i^p+x_i^{-p}\) as above.
In addition to \(O\), write \(f+f^\iota=E(S)\) with \(E\) of degree at most two.
For sign \(\varepsilon\), dividing the graph by \(x^{2p}(1+\varepsilon x)/2\) gives the local expression \(E(S)-B_\varepsilon O(S)\), where, for \(t=x+x^{-1}\),
\[
       S=x^p+x^{-p},\quad \delta=x^p-x^{-p},\quad
       B_\varepsilon=H_\varepsilon(t)\delta,\qquad
       H_\varepsilon(t)=\frac{t-2\varepsilon}{x-x^{-1}} .
\]
This follows from \((1-\varepsilon x)/(1+\varepsilon x)=-(t-2\varepsilon)/(x-x^{-1})\).
At each \(x_i\) both \(t\) and \(S\) are valid local coordinates since \(d_i\delta_i\ne0\).
The graph's double vanishing gives equality of the two values \(E=B_\varepsilon O\) and of their first \(S\)-derivatives at both points.

If \(a_4=a_0\), then \(O\) is constant.
That constant cannot be zero for a nonzero eigenrow, since \(E\) would have double zeros at the two distinct \(S_i\) and hence vanish identically.
For a quadratic polynomial the sum of its two derivatives equals twice its secant slope.
Dividing by this nonzero constant gives the necessary equality
\[
  \frac{d B_\varepsilon}{dS}(x_1)+\frac{d B_\varepsilon}{dS}(x_2)
        =2\frac{B_\varepsilon(x_1)-B_\varepsilon(x_2)}{S_1-S_2}.       \tag{4}
\]
Choose the local inverse branch \(x=x(t)\) at each fixed \(x_i\).
The identities \(dS/dt=p\delta/(x-x^{-1})\) and \(d\delta/dt=pS/(x-x^{-1})\) give
\[
 \frac{d B_\varepsilon}{dS}(x_i)
      =H_\varepsilon(t_i)S_i/\delta_i+
                         d_i H_\varepsilon'(t_i)/p .
\]
The two derivative constants are fixed at each sign.
Since \(|S_2|,|\delta_2|\le2\), \(S_1\asymp_P\rho^p\) and \(S_1/\delta_1=1+O_P(\rho^{-2p})\), the secant slope on the right of (4), before its factor two, is \(H_\varepsilon(t_1)+O_P(\rho^{-p})\).
Thus equal edges require
\[
          H_\varepsilon(t_2)S_2/\delta_2
                     =H_\varepsilon(t_1)+O_P(1/p).
\]
The scalar \(S_2/\delta_2\) is the same for the two signs, even if its absolute value is large.
The vectors \((H_+(t_i),H_-(t_i))=((t_i-2)/d_i,(t_i+2)/d_i)\) for \(i=1,2\) have nonzero determinant \(4(t_1-t_2)/(d_1d_2)\).
Elimination of that common scalar shows that both equalities are impossible for all sufficiently large \(p\).
At least one nonzero eigenrow has \(a_4\ne a_0\), as required in (3).

Finally divide the two root equations at \(\rho\) by \(y^4\) and subtract \(4p\) times the value equation from the derivative equation.
The only remaining degree-four coefficient in the latter is \(\varepsilon\rho a_0\), giving \(|a_0|\ll_P p\|A\|_1/y\) by summing the lower terms.
The value equation gives \(|a_4+\varepsilon\rho a_0|\ll_P\|A\|_1/y\), hence \(|a_4-a_0|\ll_P pH/\rho^p\).
Combining this with (3) for the sign with nonzero edge difference proves (1).
At width five the reversed rows have different formal degree, and double vanishing at a collided inverse pair permits a further linear multiplier of the squared reciprocal quadratic.
The argument supplies no corresponding exclusion at width five beyond the existing finite calculation.
