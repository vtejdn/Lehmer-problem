# Small Mahler measures in extensions of a primitive cubic root field

<a id="spectrum"></a>
## An exact spectrum under a bound on the relative degree

Take a nontorsion root \(\alpha\) of the [primitive cubic section](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v384.md#statement) with coprime exponents \(a,b\).
Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and \(m(x)=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q]h(x)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure of a nonzero algebraic number.
For each fixed \(B>0\) there is a \(c_B>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large primitive directions and integers \(1\le q\le c_Bd/(\log(3d))^{212}\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\{m(\beta):\beta\ne0,\ [K(\beta):K]\le q,\ 0<m(\beta)\le B\}\\
 &\qquad=(0,B]\ \cap\!
  \left(\{j m(\alpha):j\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\}
        \ \cup\ \{m(\gamma):\gamma\ne0,\ [\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\le q\}\right).
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
All fields and roots here lie in one algebraic closure.
Thus any independent bounded-measure output under this relative-degree cap has its full absolute degree already within the cap.
The statement allows arbitrary equations over the original root field, arbitrary signatures and nonintegral outputs.

Let \(\delta(q)\) be the least strictly positive log measure of any algebraic number of degree at most \(q\).
It exists: the rational number two supplies a candidate, and the elementary bounds on the coefficients of a primitive minimal polynomial give finiteness in bounded degree and measure.
Taking \(B=\log6\) in (1), the least strictly positive output measure in all extensions generated by one element of relative degree at most \(q\) is
\[
                            \min\{m(\alpha),\delta(q)\}.             \tag{2}
\]
In this formulation one may select any element of such an extension, since its own relative degree is at most the degree of the extension.
The same statement holds for arbitrary towers whose total degree over \(K\) is at most \(q\).
In particular a fixed relative-degree budget cannot improve on an input measure that has fallen below the fixed number \(\delta(q)\).

<a id="small-fields"></a>
## Linear disjointness and independent bounded-measure outputs

For sufficiently large primitive directions the degrees \(d\) tend to infinity uniformly over the nontorsion factors by the [primitive factor degree bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement).
The polynomial has coefficient one-norm at most six and unit endpoints, whence \(m(\alpha)\le\log 6\).
Put \(n=d/2\).
In the rational splitting field of the irreducible polynomial of \(\alpha\), the group contains the transitive group \(W=V\rtimes A_n\) of [even signed permutations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v384.md#statement).
Take \(n\ge7\).
For any number field \(L\) of degree \(l<n\), its normal closure over \(\mathbb Q\) has group of order at most \(l!<n!/2\).
The intersection with the cubic factor's splitting field gives a quotient of that order at most, so it kills the simple natural \(A_n\) in \(W\).
The [normal generation by alternating permutations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v394.md#conjugation) shows that it kills \(W\).
The polynomial defining \(\alpha\) therefore remains irreducible over the normal closure of \(L\), and in particular
\[
                  [KL:K]=l\qquad(l<n).                               \tag{3}
\]

Now take a positive-measure \(\beta\) multiplicatively independent of \(\alpha\), and set \(l=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), \(e=[K(\beta):K]\).
Their joint degree is \(de\).
The [rank-two height product of Amoroso and David](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
         l\le C\,e\, m(\alpha)m(\beta)(\log(3de))^{212}             \tag{4}
\]
with an absolute \(C\), by using \(h(\alpha)=m(\alpha)/d\) and \(h(\beta)=m(\beta)/l\).
Choose \(c_B\le1\) sufficiently small that \(C c_B(\log6)B 2^{212}<1/2\).
If \(m(\beta)\le B\) and \(e\le q\) in (1), then \(e\le d\) and (4) gives \(l<d/2\).
Applying (3) to \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) gives \(l=e\).
In the high-slope range, when \(\beta\) belongs to the [solvable closure of the totally real field](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v394.md#conjugation), the same equality holds at every absolute degree and without a measure bound.

## Positive integer multiples on the multiplicative line of the input

Distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\) have nontorsion quotient by the [primitive cubic root argument](Yin_L_G0462_v177.md#independence), hence \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^u)=K\) for all integers \(u\ne0\).
The [cubic root-field intersection](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#statement) gives \(K\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}=\mathbb Q\) when \(d>12\), so \(\mu(K)=\{1,-1\}\).
The [complete in-field power test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v388.md#statement) excludes both signed squares and all odd prime powers for \(\alpha\), the sign for odd powers being absorbed in the root.
Consequently the class of \(\alpha\) in the free abelian unit group modulo torsion is primitive: divisibility there by any prime would give one of those forbidden powers in \(K\).
For every nontorsion \(\beta\) multiplicatively dependent on \(\alpha\), the [norm and integer measure-ratio result](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v336.md#norm) now gives \(m(\beta)\in m(\alpha)\mathbb Z_{>0}\), including external torsion twists and arbitrary fractional powers.
All positive integer multiples actually occur in \(K\) itself by taking \(\alpha^j\) and using preservation of degree.
Finally, every number of absolute degree at most \(q\) has relative degree at most \(q\) over \(K\).
Together with the independent case this proves both inclusions in (1).
