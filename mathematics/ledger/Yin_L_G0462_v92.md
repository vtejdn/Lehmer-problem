# A short frequency relation for every eight-term trace lift on branch two

<a id="relation"></a>
## The sum of the lower coefficient magnitudes

Fix a positive branch index \(j\) and a positive integer \(r\).
Write an actual positive-constant Pisot trace lift with \(r\) nonconstant lower terms as
\[
 \Pi(z)=A(z)-z^N A(z^{-1}),\qquad
 A(z)=j+\sum_{\nu=1}^r a_\nu z^{d_\nu},\qquad
 1\le d_1<\cdots<d_r\le h,\qquad N=2h+2 ,
\]
where the \(a_\nu\) are nonzero integers.
There is a finite \(B(j,r)\) such that whenever \(\sum_\nu |a_\nu|>j\), some nonzero \((k_1,\ldots,k_r,k_0)\in\mathbb Z^{r+1}\) satisfies
\[
       \max_\nu |k_\nu|\le B(j,r),\qquad
       \sum_{\nu=1}^r k_\nu d_\nu+k_0 N=0,                          \tag{1}
\]
where the maximum includes \(k_0\).
This holds for each actual input, with no near-integer limit or upper bound on its degree.
The [six-term reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) previously obtained the unit-coefficient conclusion for two independent lower offsets on an escaping second-branch sequence.

By the [trace zero locations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace), all zeros of \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^hC(z+z^{-1})\) are on the unit circle, and its multiplicative Mahler measure is \(j\).
Its \(2r+2\) distinct terms therefore have coefficient magnitudes at most \(2^{2r}j\) by the [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound).
If (1) failed for every bound, we could extract a sequence of exponent tuples whose shortest nonzero integer relation tends to infinity, with one fixed tuple of coefficients by finiteness.
Consider the fixed Laurent polynomial
\[
 F(\mathbf x,y)=P(\mathbf x)-yP(\mathbf x^{-1}),\qquad
 P(\mathbf x)=j+\sum_{\nu=1}^r a_\nu x_\nu .
\]
The substitutions with exponent vectors \((d_1,\ldots,d_r,N)\) give precisely the actual lifts.
After clearing Laurent monomials, [Lawton's specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) gives \(M(F)=j\).
On the torus the two coefficients in \(y\) have the same modulus, so integration in \(y\) by Jensen gives \(M(F)=M(P)\).

Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) and use normalized Haar measure \(d\mathbf x\).
Simultaneous rotation of all \(x_\nu\) by \(u\) preserves their joint Haar measure.
Integration first over \(u\) therefore gives
\[
        m(P)=\int_{\mathbb T^r}
             \log\max\!\left(j,\left|\sum_\nu a_\nu x_\nu\right|\right)
             \,d\mathbf x .
\]
The continuous modulus inside the maximum attains \(\sum_\nu |a_\nu|\) by alignment of the phases.
Thus \(M(P)=j\) exactly when \(\sum_\nu|a_\nu|\le j\), since a strict excess at one point persists on an open set.
This contradicts the chosen coefficient tuple and proves (1).

## Consequences for the three lower offsets

On branch \(j=2\), an eight-term lift has \(r=3\) and \(\sum |a_\nu|\ge3\), so (1) holds with the single absolute bound \(B(2,3)\).
For any fixed \(h\) the possible triples \((d_1,d_2,d_3)\) belong to a union of a bounded number of affine planes, each with a fixed integer normal from (1) and constant term \(-k_0(2h+2)\).
At least one of the three lower coefficients of the relation is nonzero because \(N>0\).
Solving for its offset after fixing the other two shows that at most \(O(h^2)\) ordered triples can occur, with an absolute implied constant.
If \(3B(2,3)d_3<N\), every relation supplied by (1) has \(k_0=0\), since \(\left|\sum_\nu k_\nu d_\nu\right|<N\).
In particular, along a sequence with \(d_3=o(h)\), the lower offsets alone eventually satisfy one of finitely many homogeneous integer relations.
For fixed \(j,r\) the same reasoning gives \(O_{j,r}(h^{r-1})\) possible increasing offset tuples when the coefficient sum exceeds \(j\).

The coefficient threshold is sharp for the circle-root property used in this argument.
If \(\sum|a_\nu|\le j\), then for any offsets as above and \(0<t<1\), the polynomial \(A_t=j+t\sum_\nu a_\nu z^{d_\nu}\) is zero-free on the closed disk.
The function \(z^N A_t(z^{-1})/A_t(z)\) is analytic there, vanishes at zero and has modulus one on the circle.
The maximum principle and anti-reciprocity then put every zero of \(A_t(z)-z^NA_t(z^{-1})\) on the circle.
Passing to \(t=1\) preserves the degree and leading coefficient and gives the same conclusion for \(\Pi\).
This converse concerns the location of the lift's zeros alone and imposes no realization as a Pisot trace lift.
