# The cofactor cost of a constant trace value with zero derivative remainder

<a id="cost"></a>
## A lower bound for adjacent residue classes

Let \(P\) be a reciprocal nontorsion unit minimal polynomial of degree \(d\), with root \(\alpha\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(h=m(P)\).
Let \(p\ge5\) be a prime preserving the degree of \(\beta=\alpha^p\), whose minimal polynomial is \(Q\), so \(m(Q)=ph\).
Consider a primitive integral polynomial
\[
 F(x)=A(x^p)+\sigma x^{J} A(x^{-p}),\qquad
 J=pn+S=2k+1,\quad n=\deg A,\quad A(0)\ne0,\quad \sigma=\pm1,
\]
with \(P^2\mid F\), \(S>0\), and \(A\) coprime to its reversal.
Suppose the [odd-span trace value and remainder](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd) have \(u=b\in\mathbb Z\) and \(D=0\).
Then \(b\ne0\) by coprimality, and the centered derivative parameter is \(v=-b\).
Suppose \(S\equiv e\bmod p\) for \(e\in\{1,-1\}\).
Put
\[
 \ell=(J-e)/(2p),\quad B(X)=X^{-\ell}A(X),\quad
 E=X\,d/dX,\quad T=B-e p E B,\quad T^\iota(X)=T(X^{-1}).
\]
The integer \(\ell\) is nonnegative since \(J\) and \(p\) are odd.
Let \(R_F\) be the product of the noncyclotomic factors of \(F/P^2\) with multiplicities.
If \(T\ne T^\iota\), then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m(A)&\ge ph-L,\qquad
 L=\log\!\big(2(1+p(\ell+1))\big)+2\log(1+C_0 n),\\
 m(R_F)&\ge(1-4/p)(ph-L)-2h-\frac2p\log(C_1 J).                  \tag{1}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(C_0=\sup_z\int_{\mathbb T}|X-z|^{-1/2}d\mu(X)\) and \(C_1=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-X|)^{1/2}d\mu(X)\) are finite absolute constants for normalized circle measure.
The condition \(T\ne T^\iota\) holds automatically for \(S>1\).
At \(S=1\) it also holds if \(d\ge4\) and \(p^2\nmid b\).

Indeed the two input values are \(A(\beta)=b\alpha^k(1-\sigma\alpha)\) and \((2p E A-JA)(\beta)=-b\alpha^k(1+\sigma\alpha)\).
When \(e=1\), these become \(B(\beta)=b(1-\sigma\alpha)\), \(pEB(\beta)=-\sigma b\alpha\), and \(T(\beta)=b\).
When \(e=-1\), they become \(B(\beta)=b(\alpha^{-1}-\sigma)\), \(pEB(\beta)=-b\alpha^{-1}\), and \(T(\beta)=-\sigma b\).
As each \(T(\beta)\) is rational, the automorphism inverting \(\alpha\) gives \(T(\beta^{-1})=T(\beta)\).
Hence \(Q\mid T-T^\iota\) in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\), by monicity after clearing a monomial.
If the difference is nonzero, \(ph\le m(T-T^\iota)\le\log2+m(T)\), using \(|T^\iota|=|T|\) on the circle.
There \(|T/B|\le 1+p\ell+p\sum_{A(z)=0}|X-z|^{-1}\) away from the row roots, counted with multiplicities.
The [logarithmic derivative estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#rotation) bounds the log integral of one plus this sum of inverse distances by \(2\log(1+C_0n)\).
Since \(m(B)=m(A)\), this proves the first inequality in (1).
The [diagonal comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) gives \((p-4)m(A)\le p\,m(F)+2\log(C_1J)\).
Primitivity gives \(m(R_F)=m(F)-2h\), proving the second inequality.

The coefficient multiplier of \(X^{j-\ell}\) in \(T\) is \(1-ep(j-\ell)\), nonzero for every integer \(j\).
Its extreme exponents are therefore \(-\ell,n-\ell\), so symmetry requires \(n=2\ell\), or \(S=e\).
Positivity of \(S\) leaves only \(S=e=1\).

<a id="symmetry"></a>
## The divisibility required by exact symmetry

Suppose \(S=1\) and \(T=T^\iota\), so \(n=2\ell\).
Write \(C=(B+B^\iota)/2\).
Since \((EB)^\iota=-E(B^\iota)\), the identity \(T=T^\iota\) gives \(B=C+pEC\), hence \(T=C-p^2E^2C\).
At \(\beta\), inversion of the row value gives \(C(\beta)=b(1-\sigma q/2)\), where \(q=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\).
Using \(T(\beta)=b\) gives the exact equality
\[
                    p^2(E^2(2C))(\beta)=-\sigma b q .                \tag{2}
\]
Since \(2C\) is an integral Laurent polynomial and \(\beta\) is a unit, \(bq\in p^2\mathbb Z[\beta]\subset p^2\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
For \(d\ge4\), \(q\) is a member of the power basis of \(\mathbb Z[q]\), which is a direct summand of \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]=\mathbb Z[q]\oplus\alpha\mathbb Z[q]\).
Its coordinate in \(bq\) is \(b\), so (2) forces \(p^2\mid b\).

<a id="pencil"></a>
## The prescribed integral pencil at good and bad primes

In the [zero-remainder pencil](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#vanishing), \(n=2d,\ S=1\), and \(A_M=M Q^2+T_0\) with fixed \(\deg T_0<2d\).
The positive constant \(b\) is the least integer representing \(bx\) in the powered suborder modulo \(P^2\).
At \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\), the [good-reduction order calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v162.md#support-prime) gives \(v_p(b)=1\) at multiplicity two.
Thus (1) applies to **every** admissible member at such a prime when \(d\ge4\), including those with nonzero centered derivative parameter \(z\).
At any degree-preserving \(p\ge5\), there is at most one exceptional \(M\).
Indeed at \(S=1,\ \ell=d\) the two extreme coefficients of \(T\) are \((1+pd)(M+T_0(0))\) and \((1-pd)M\).
Equality would require
\[
                        2pd M=-(1+pd)T_0(0).
\]
All other admissible integers \(M\) satisfy (1) even at a prime dividing the discriminant.
For fixed \(P,p,\sigma,T_0\) and \(|M|\to\infty\), \(F_M/M\) converges coefficientwise to \(Q(x^p)^2(1+\sigma x)\) of the same degree.
Continuity of the measure and primitivity therefore give the more precise fixed-input cost \(m(R_{F_M})=\log|M|+(2p-2)h+o(1)\).

Throughout the [fourth-logarithmic window and degree cap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#finite), \(p h/\log(2+J)\to\infty\) uniformly as \(d\to\infty\).
Consequently each of the cases covered by (1) has \(m(R_F)/(ph)\ge1-o(1)\), uniformly in the input and row.
This extends the [coefficient-one lower bound for \(z=0\)](Yin_L_G0462_v186.md#cofactor) to the indicated constant-trace witnesses with \(z\ne0\).
The lower bound scales with \(h\); an absolute positive rate for shrinking inputs and the cost of a possible symmetric exceptional member at a bad prime remain open.
