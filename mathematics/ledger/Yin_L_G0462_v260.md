# A lower bound on the height of the residual quadratic one row degree above a seed

<a id="quotient"></a>
## The residual quadratic and its two exceptional shapes

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum of degree \(e=2s\) with inverse-pair representatives multiplicatively independent modulo torsion.
Write \(h=m(P)\), let \(\rho\) be the largest modulus of its roots, and let \(F(P)\) be its [minimum-width coefficient rate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v414.md#statement).
Fix \(w=e+1\).
Take any integral rows \(U,V\) of degrees at most \(w\), coprime in \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), such that \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) and \(D=UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), where \(A^\iota(Y)=A(1/Y)\).
Put \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
The powered minimum \(Q=\min(x^p)\), for \(P(x)=0\), is monic reciprocal of degree \(e\).
The [reflected double divisibility](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) gives
\[
              Y^wD=Q^2R,\qquad R=cY^2+dY+c\in\mathbb Z[Y],\quad R\ne0 .
\]
Indeed \(Y^wD\) has formal reciprocal degree \(2w\), and dividing by the monic reciprocal \(Q^2\) leaves formal reciprocal degree two.
If \(c\ne0\), let \(q\) be the primitive part of \(R\), up to sign, and write \(R=kq\) with a nonzero integer \(k\).
For a root \(t\) of \(q\), both the rationally split case and the irreducible case give \(m(q)=2h_{\rm W}(t)\), where \(h_{\rm W}\) denotes absolute logarithmic Weil height.
For an irreducible quadratic this is the height formula for its primitive minimum.
In the split case its roots are \(t,t^{-1}\), the two primitive linear factors have the same measure \(h_{\rm W}(t)\), and measures add.

Every nonzero integral graph pair of width at most \(e\) with double factor \(P\) costs at least \(\exp(pF(P)-o_P(p))\), including pairs with a common factor or with zero reflected determinant.
Indeed at least one of its two integral signed components is nonzero, is an integer multiple of its primitive signed generator and has one-norm at most twice the cost.
Both primitive generators have the stated rate by the [signed line calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#rate).
Throughout, all error terms are uniform over the rows and their residuals at the fixed seed.

Two shapes permit a direct reduction to width \(e\).
Put \(A_w^*(Y)=Y^wA(1/Y)\), \(B=U+V_w^*\), \(C=U-V_w^*\), and write \(S_+=(B,B_w^*)\), \(S_-=(C,-C_w^*)\).
Each signed pair retains the double factor by reversal, and their polynomial determinant is \(-2Y^wD\ne0\).
If \(R(\xi)=0\) for \(\xi\in\{1,-1\}\), then \(D(\xi)=B(\xi)C(\xi)=0\).
One signed pair is divisible in both coordinates by \(Y-\xi\), since reversing preserves that factor.
Its substituted factor \(X^p-\xi\) is coprime to \(P\).
Division retains double divisibility, gives a nonzero pair of width at most \(e\) and costs at most \(2wH\) by synthetic division at \(\xi\).
Consequently \(\log H\ge pF(P)-o_P(p)\) for this shape, which includes both possible repeated quadratic roots.

If \(c=0\), the residual is \(dY\) with \(d\ne0\), and \(u_wu_0-v_wv_0=0\).
The leading coefficient vectors of \((U,V)\) and \((V_w^*,U_w^*)\) are then linearly dependent.
For each graph its two leading coefficients have size \(O_P(pH/\rho^p)\).
To see this, evaluate at a root \(x\) of modulus \(\rho\), set \(y=x^p\) and subtract \(pw\) times the graph's value from its Euler derivative.
After division by \(y^w\), only \(xv_w\) remains among the leading terms and all lower terms total \(O_P(pH/|y|)\).
The value equation then also bounds \(u_w\), and reversal gives the same bounds at the other end.
If all four leading entries vanish, the original pair already has width at most \(e\).
Otherwise a nonzero column of these two leading vectors supplies integer multipliers, by interchanging its entries and negating one, that cancel both top coefficients with magnitude \(O_P(pH/\rho^p)\).
The two original graph pairs are independent by \(D\ne0\), so this combination is nonzero, retains \(P^2\), and has cost at most \(O_P(pH^2/\rho^p)\).
In either case
\[
                  \log H\ge \tfrac12\big(F(P)+\log\rho\big)p-o_P(p),
\]
using \(F(P)\ge\log\rho\) for the case with all top entries zero.

<a id="descent"></a>
## Descent at a nonendpoint root

Suppose \(c\ne0\) and \(q\) has neither endpoint as a root.
At every sufficiently large \(p\) the residual must satisfy
\[
 pF(P)\ \le\ 2\log H+(w-1)h_{\rm W}(t)+o_P(p)
           = 2\log H+\tfrac{w-1}{2}m(q)+o_P(p).                     \tag{1}
\]
First suppose \(q\) is irreducible and \(q\ne\pm Q\).
Neither \(B(t)\) nor \(C(t)\) vanishes.
For if the primitive reciprocal \(q\) divided a nonzero signed first coordinate, it would divide both signed coordinates.
Its substitution is coprime to \(P\), since \(q\) has no common root with \(Q\).
Division would leave a nonzero signed graph of width \(w-2=e-1\) with the same double factor.
The width-\((e-1)\) signed kernel is zero for both signs at every sufficiently large \(p\).
Indeed the corresponding square signed matrix has full rank at the formal parameter \(N=0\) and independent generic nodes, since then the polynomial would vanish at \(e\) distinct nodes.
Its nonzero determinant stays nonzero on the actual large powers by independence and the [local recurrence estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch).

Put \(J=1+Y,\ K=Y-1\).
The determinant at \(t\) gives \(B(t)/C(t)=-B(t^{-1})/C(t^{-1})\).
Also \(J(t^{-1})/K(t^{-1})=-J(t)/K(t)\).
Since \(t\mapsto t^{-1}\) is the nontrivial conjugation in the quadratic field, \(J(t)B(t)/(K(t)C(t))\) is a nonzero rational number.
Take relatively prime integers \(m,n\) with \(mJ(t)B(t)+nK(t)C(t)=0\).
Their projective height satisfies
\[
 \log\max(|m|,|n|)
   =h_{\rm W}\big([K(t)C(t):J(t)B(t)]\big)
   \le \log(4H)+(w+1)h_{\rm W}(t).
\]
Here both evaluated polynomials have degree at most \(w+1\), integer coefficients and one-norm at most \(4H\).
At any finite place their local maximum is bounded by \(\max(1,|t|)^{w+1}\), and each archimedean bound has the additional factor \(4H\) before normalization.

The pair \(mJ S_+ +nK S_-\) is nonzero by the determinant and is plus signed at width \(w+1\), since \(J_1^*=J\) and \(K_1^*=-K\).
Vanishing of its first coordinate at \(t\) gives divisibility by \(q\) in both coordinates by irreducibility and reciprocity.
Gauss's lemma gives integral quotients and \(q(X^p)\) is coprime to \(P\).
The resulting pair has width at most \(w-1=e\) and retains \(P^2\).
For any nonzero integral numerator \(E\) with integral quotient by \(q\) of degree at most \(e\), coefficient bounds and multiplicativity give
\[
            \log\|E/q\|_1\le e\log2+m(E)-m(q)
                           \le e\log2+\log\|E\|_1-m(q).
\]
Thus division saves \(m(q)=2h_{\rm W}(t)\) from the logarithmic cost, including for nonmonic \(q\).
The numerator costs at most \(8H\max(|m|,|n|)\), so the width-\(e\) bound proves (1).

Now let \(t=u/v\) be a rational root in lowest terms with \(v>0\).
The two signed graph pairs are dependent on evaluation at \(t\).
Some row of the two-by-two coordinate matrix there is nonzero, since vanishing of all four entries would force both \(U(t)=V(t)=0\).
Use that row to choose primitive integers \(m,n\) annihilating both evaluated coordinates.
An evaluated entry multiplied by \(v^w\) has magnitude at most \(2H\max(|u|,v)^w\).
This gives \(\log\max(|m|,|n|)\le\log(2H)+w h_{\rm W}(t)\), also if one multiplier is zero.
The constant combination \(m S_+ +n S_-\) is nonzero and is divisible in both entries by the primitive linear polynomial \(vY-u\).
Its substitution is coprime to \(P\), since the powered minimum has degree \(e\ge2\).
Integral division reduces to width \(e\) and saves \(m(vY-u)=h_{\rm W}(t)\) by the same coefficient estimate.
This proves exactly the same loss \((w-1)h_{\rm W}(t)\) in (1) for two rational inverse roots.

If \(q=\pm Q\), necessarily \(e=2\).
Division by \(q(X^p)\) can then remove a copy of \(P\).
In this quadratic case \(F(P)=2h\), and the individual inequality \(\log H\ge ph\) already implies (1).
A reciprocal noncyclotomic quadratic has multiplicative measure at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2>2\), so its genuine pairs never meet the full cutoff.

## Height and endpoint constraints for the width-nine octic

For \(P=X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1\) write \(h=\log\rho\).
Here \(F(P)=8h\), so the endpoint and degree-drop shapes have lower rates \(8h\) and \(9h/2\), respectively.
Both exceed \(\log2\) since \(\rho>32/25\) and \((32/25)^9>4\). [Octic comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v252.md#specialization)
For every sequence meeting \(H\le2^p\) as \(p\to\infty\), the residual is therefore genuinely quadratic with distinct nonendpoint roots, and (1) gives
\[
          m(q)\ \ge\ \left(2h-\tfrac12\log2\right)p-o_P(p).
\]
This coefficient is strictly positive since \((32/25)^4>2\).
In particular a residual with sublinear primitive logarithmic measure cannot occur along a passing sequence, regardless of its integer content.

On the circle \(|D|\le H^2\).
Since \(m(Q)=ph\), the total measure budget is
\[
    \log|k|+m(q)\le 2\log H-2ph .
\]
Combining it with (1) gives the lower rate \(2e h/(e+2)\) for a Salem seed at \(w=e+1\) in the nonendpoint case, and a smaller upper budget for \(\log|k|\) when the primitive quadratic has the required linear growth.
For the octic the combined lower rate is \(8h/5\), below the already established \(21h/8\). [Single-row estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v255.md#bounds)

There is also a condition joint in the two [endpoint congruences](Yin_L_G0462_v258.md#congruences).
Write the two signed pairs uniquely as
\[
 S_+=(a_+J R_+ + b_+K R_-)/I_+,\qquad
 S_-=(a_-J R_- + b_-K R_+)/I_- ,
\]
using the primitive width-\(e\) graph pairs \(R_\pm\), with \(b_\varepsilon\equiv c_\varepsilon a_\varepsilon\bmod I_\varepsilon\) in that construction.
Their determinant shows that \(q\) is the primitive part, up to sign, of \(A J^2-B' K^2\), where \(A=a_+a_-\), \(B'=b_+b_-\).
At the octic cutoff and large \(p\) neither product vanishes, by the endpoint exclusion.
Divide \(A,B'\) by their common positive gcd to obtain coprime integers \(A',B''\).
The polynomial then has outer coefficients \(A'-B''\) and middle coefficient \(2(A'+B'')\).
Its content divides four, since it divides \(4A'\) and \(4B''\).
Equivalence of the maximum coefficient norm with Mahler measure at degree two gives
\[
         m(q)=\log\max(|A'|,|B''|)+O(1)
              =h_{\rm W}\big([a_+a_-:b_+b_-]\big)+O(1).
\]
Thus the two congruence solutions coming from any passing octic pair must have a product ratio of height at least \((2h-\tfrac12\log2)p-o_P(p)\).
The endpoint estimates bound each \(\max(|a_\varepsilon|,|b_\varepsilon|)\) by \(H\exp(-ph+o_P(p))\), yielding only \(2(\log2-h)p+o_P(p)\) as an upper bound on that height.
This upper rate exceeds the new lower rate, since \(\rho<9/7\) and \((9/7)^8<2^5\) give \(4h<\tfrac52\log2\).
Hence these joint necessary conditions still leave the width-nine cutoff open.
