# Neutral residues of a varying Pisot return at the square constant

<a id="branches"></a>
## The possible size of the integer branch

Let \(f\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta\), of even degree \(d=2m\ge4\) with constant \(q=f(0)\ge2\).
Write \(\delta=\theta-q,\ f^*(z)=z^d f(1/z)\) and \(j=[z]\frac{f}{f^*}-q^2+1\), where \([z]\) extracts the linear Taylor coefficient at zero.
The full return \(P=(zf+f^*)/(z+1)\) has a simple exterior root \(\sigma>1\), its reciprocal and \(d-2\) simple circle roots distinct from the endpoints \(1,-1\). [Exterior pair](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) · [Simplicity](Yin_L_G0462_v34.md#crossings)
Put \(x=z+z^{-1}\), \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(u=T-2\), \(h=m-1\) and \(P(z)=z^m Q(x)\).
Write \(Q=(x-T)H\), where \(H(x)=\prod_{i=1}^h(x-x_i)\) and the \(x_i\) are the neutral traces in \((-2,2)\).
The minimal factor of \(\sigma\) has degree \(D\le d\) and Mahler measure \(\sigma\), including the reciprocal quadratic alternative. [Factorization of the full return](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#infimum)

Use the integral inverse polynomial \(R\) in \(f=P+(z-1)z^{m-1}R(x)\).
Its leading coefficient is \(1-q\), and its neutral residues \(b_i=-R(x_i)/H'(x_i)>0\) satisfy
\[
 \frac{R(x)}{H(x)}=1-q-\sum_i\frac{b_i}{x-x_i},\qquad
 \sum_i\frac{b_i}{2+x_i}<q+T/2,\qquad
 \delta=\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}u+\sum_i\frac{b_i}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x_i}.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
These identities apply to every actual inverse here.
Antisymmetry gives integral \(R\), and the simple-root displacement and the positive signs of \(f(-1)\) and the starting quadratic at \(-1\) give the necessary strict inequalities for a reducible full return as well. [Inverse simplex](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) · [Root displacement and excess](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return) · [Necessity for a simple reducible return](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v374.md#lower)
The rational function \(1-q-(x-2)R(x)/Q(x)\) has expansion \(j/x+O(x^{-2})\), by the [branch trace identity](Yin_L_G0462_v135.md#minimum).
Indeed the coefficient at \(x^{-1}\) in \(-(x-2)R(x)/Q(x)\) is \((q-1)u+\sum_i b_i\), by (1).
Consequently
\[
 s:=j-(q-1)u=\sum_i b_i>0,\qquad
 c_i:=\operatorname{Res}_{x=x_i}\left(1-q-\frac{(x-2)R(x)}{Q(x)}\right)
     =b_i\,\frac{2-x_i}{T-x_i}>0.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Here the \(c_i\) are the actual neutral branch residues; \(s\) is the sum of the inverse residues \(b_i\).

Since \(u=2(\cosh(\log\sigma)-1)\ge(\log\sigma)^2\), [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives the finite exclusions
\[
         \delta>u>\frac{4}{\log^6(3D)},\qquad
         j>(q-1)u>\frac{4(q-1)}{\log^6(3D)}.                           \tag{3}
\]
In particular any proposed branch forces \(D>\tfrac13\exp((4(q-1)/j)^{1/6})\).
Voutier's main bound gives \(u>\tfrac1{16}(\log\log D/\log D)^6\) for \(D\ge4\).
The ratio \(\log\log D/\log D\) decreases for \(D\ge16\), and (3) gives an absolute positive bound for \(2\le D<16\).
Thus for all sufficiently large even \(d\) at \(q=d^2\),
\[
 \delta>\tfrac1{16}\left(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d}\right)^6,\qquad
 j>\tfrac1{16}(d^2-1)\left(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d}\right)^6.
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
In particular any fixed branch, and even \(j=O(d^{2-\varepsilon})\) for fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), is impossible along these degrees.
The [square-constant minima](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v370.md#statement) satisfy \(d^2\eta_d\to+\infty\), improving the [first-coefficient bound](Yin_L_G0462_v159.md#square).
This application of Voutier still permits \(\eta_d\to0\).

<a id="resultant"></a>
## A resultant bound for all neutral residues

Let \(\Delta=\prod_{i<j}|x_j-x_i|\), with the empty product equal to one.
The monic integral polynomial \(Q\) and the integral \(R\) are coprime: \(R(x_i)\ne0\), while \(R(T)/H(T)<0\) by (1).
Their resultant is a nonzero integer, so
\[
            \prod_i b_i=\frac{|\operatorname{Res}(Q,R)|}{|R(T)|\Delta^2}
                     \ge\frac1{|R(T)|\Delta^2}.                       \tag{5}
\]
This calculation uses the full trace polynomial, regardless of the distribution of its irreducible factors.
Write \(H_i=H/(x-x_i)\).
Equation (1) gives \(R(T)=(1-q)H(T)-\sum_i b_i H_i(T)\), and \(s<4q+2T\) since \(2+x_i<4\).
Bounding \(H(T)\) by \((T+2)^h\) and \(H_i(T)\) by \((T+2)^{h-1}\) gives \(|R(T)|\le(2q+1)(T+2)^h\).
Evaluate the monic polynomials \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{h-1}\) at the \(h\) nodes, where \(C_n(2\cos t)=2\cos(nt)\).
The absolute determinant is \(\Delta\), unchanged from the monomials by the unit triangular change of basis.
Column norms are at most \(\sqrt h,2\sqrt h,\ldots,2\sqrt h\), so \(\Delta^2\le4^{h-1}h^h\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and (5) therefore give, in every degree,
\[
               s\ge\frac{4^{-1+1/h}}{(T+2)(2q+1)^{1/h}}.             \tag{6}
\]

<a id="limits"></a>
## The determinant constant on a growing return

Consider a sequence with \(d\to\infty,\ \log q=o(d)\), and bounded \(\sigma\).
Pass to a subsequence with \(\sigma\to\sigma_*\ge1\), and put \(T_*=\sigma_*+\sigma_*^{-1}\).
The counting probabilities \(h^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{x_i}\) converge to the arcsine law \(\rho(dx)=dx/(\pi\sqrt{4-x^2})\) on \([-2,2]\).
Indeed the [discriminant estimate for the full associate](Yin_L_G0462_v34.md#discriminant) gives \(O(n\log(2n))\) for \(\sum_{k\ge1}(1-1/n)^k|\sum_j w_j^k|^2/k\), where \(n=d-1\) and the \(w_j\) are all neutral roots of \(zf+f^*\).
The displayed estimate there uses only bounded \(\sigma\) in bounding the contribution of the two exterior and interior real points.
Each fixed nonzero normalized Fourier coefficient tends to zero, so the counting probabilities on the circle converge to Haar probability.
Deleting the single root \(-1\) and mapping the remaining conjugate pairs to \(x=z+z^{-1}\) gives the assertion for the full set of traces.

For the row indexed by \(x_i=2\cos t_i\), \(0<t_i<\pi\), the squared norm of the same evaluation matrix is
\[
        1+4\sum_{n=1}^{h-1}\cos^2(nt_i)
              =2h-1+2\sum_{n=1}^{h-1}\cos(2nt_i).
\]
The geometric-series bound makes this \(2h+O_\varepsilon(1)\) when \(\varepsilon\le t_i\le\pi-\varepsilon\), and it is at most \(4h\) everywhere.
The fraction of exceptional rows tends to \(2\varepsilon/\pi\).
Apply Hadamard's inequality by rows, take the \(h\)-th root of the squared determinant and then let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
This proves \(\limsup\Delta^{2/h}/h\le2\).

For any \(M>0\) truncate \(\log(T-x)\) from below by \(-M\).
The resulting functions converge uniformly on \([-2,2]\) to the corresponding continuous truncation at \(T_*\).
The \(h^{-1}\)-normalized sums change by \(O_M(1/h)\) on omitting any one summand, uniformly in its index, since \(T\) stays bounded.
Thus both \(h^{-1}\log H(T)\) and \(h^{-1}\log\max_i H_i(T)\) have limsup at most \(\int\log(T_*-x)\,d\rho(x)=\log\sigma_*\), by letting \(M\to\infty\).
The integral follows from \(T_*-2\cos t=\sigma_*|1-\sigma_*^{-1}e^{it}|^2\) and Jensen's formula, including the integrable endpoint logarithm at \(\sigma_*=1\).
The bound \(s<4q+2T\) in the expression for \(R(T)\) now implies \(\limsup |R(T)|^{1/h}\le\sigma_*\).
This also covers \(T\downarrow2\) without dividing by \(T-2\).

From (5), and from (2) for the second product, we obtain
\[
 \liminf h\Big(\prod_i b_i\Big)^{1/h}\ge\frac{1}{2\sigma_*},\qquad
 \liminf h\Big(\prod_i c_i\Big)^{1/h}\ge\frac{1}{2\sigma_*^2}.        \tag{7}
\]
Indeed \(\prod_i c_i/\prod_i b_i=H(2)/H(T)\), with
\(H(2)=|Q(2)|/(T-2)\ge1/(T-2)\) since the return has no trace at two.
Bounded \(T\) gives \(\liminf H(2)^{1/h}\ge1\).
In particular every genuinely shrinking sequence at the square constant satisfies \(\liminf [j-(q-1)(T-2)]\ge1/2\).
Since \(\theta=q+O(1)\) for bounded \(\sigma\) by the [two-point bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#two-points), (1) also yields, when \(q\to\infty\),
\[
          \liminf q[\delta-(T-2)]\ \ge\ T_*-2+\frac1{2\sigma_*}.      \tag{8}
\]
Here one bounds every inverse denominator in (1) from below by \(1/(\theta+\theta^{-1}+2)\).
For a specified grid with a fixed Salem factor, the [factor-specific resultant](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v374.md#lower) gives the larger additive constant \(1/2\) at \(\sigma_*>1\).
Estimate (8) allows the core, the degree of the core and every cyclotomic factor to vary.

<a id="angular"></a>
## Angular restrictions beyond the total mass

Take \(v_i=b_i,\ c_*=1/(2\sigma_*)\), or take \(v_i=c_i,\ c_*=1/(2\sigma_*^2)\).
For each strictly positive continuous \(w\) on \([-2,2]\), apply the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to the numbers \(w(x_i)v_i\).
Equation (7) and the arcsine limit give
\[
             \liminf\sum_i w(x_i)v_i
                 \ \ge\ c_*\,\exp\left(\int\log w\,d\rho\right).      \tag{9}
\]
If the masses \(\sum_i v_i\) stay bounded, take any weak limit \(\nu\) of the finite measures \(\sum_i v_i\delta_{x_i}\) on this subsequence.
Its mass \(S=\nu([-2,2])\) satisfies \(S\ge c_*\).
For a Borel set \(E\) with \(p=\rho(E)\in(0,1)\), equation (9) gives
\[
       \left(\frac{\nu(E)}{p}\right)^p
       \left(\frac{S-\nu(E)}{1-p}\right)^{1-p}\ \ge\ c_* .           \tag{10}
\]
To see this, bounded test functions ranging in a fixed positive interval can be approximated in \(L^1(\rho+\nu)\) by continuous functions in the same interval, by regularity and truncation.
The logarithms converge in \(L^1(\rho)\) as well.
Test (9) with the value \(t>0\) on \(E\) and one elsewhere, giving \(t\nu(E)+S-\nu(E)\ge c_*t^p\).
Taking \(t\to\infty\) or \(t\to0\) excludes zero mass on either part.
Minimizing the left side divided by \(t^p\) gives (10).
The same test when \(p=1\) gives \(\nu(E)\ge c_*\).
Consequently \(\rho\ll\nu\), so a purely atomic limit is impossible.
If \(S=c_*\), testing \(w=1+\varepsilon v\) for any continuous real \(v\) and differentiating from both signs of \(\varepsilon\) at zero gives \(\int v\,d\nu=c_*\int v\,d\rho\).
Thus attaining the total bound forces the limit \(\nu=c_*\rho\).
Bounded total mass is required only for the weak-limit assertions and separately for the chosen residue family.
The estimates constrain the excess above the actual exterior trace and its angular distribution; they leave open whether these exterior traces can tend to two.
