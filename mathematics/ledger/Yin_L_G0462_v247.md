# Excluding a vanishing outer determinant coefficient at quartic seed degree and row width five

<a id="five"></a>
## A restriction on the first remaining width

Fix a quartic Salem number \(\rho\) with minimum \(P\) and write \(y=\rho^p\).
Let \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) have degrees at most five, with \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) and \(D=UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), where \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\).
Write \(u_i,v_i\) for the coefficients, using zeros up to the declared width, and \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
If the coefficient \(u_5u_0-v_5v_0\) of \(Y^5\) in \(D\) vanishes, then for every sufficiently large integer \(p\)
\[
                          \log H\ \ge\ \tfrac32 p\log\rho-o_P(p).        \tag{1}
\]
Both [quartic seeds eligible at width five](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#short) satisfy \(\rho^{3/2}>2\).
Indeed \(2^{2/3}<8/5\), and \((8/5)+(5/8)=89/40<(1+\sqrt{13})/2<1+\sqrt2\).
Thus for all sufficiently large odd primes any genuine pair in the full width-five box must have \(u_5u_0-v_5v_0\ne0\).
The nonzero case remains open.

<a id="three"></a>
## No double-root graph of width three survives at a fixed quartic Salem seed

For every sufficiently large positive integer \(p\) there is no nonzero pair of rational polynomials of degrees at most three whose graph has double factor \(P\).
To see this, reverse the graph about \(3p+1\) and take its sum and difference with its reversal.
A nonzero pair gives at least one nonzero polynomial \(A\) of degree at most three for which \(P^2\mid A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A^*(X^p)\), where \(A^*(Y)=Y^3A(1/Y)\) and \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
For \(S=Y+Y^{-1}\) write
\[
       A+A^*=Y(Y+1)E(S),\qquad A-A^*=Y(Y-1)O(S),
\]
with \(E,O\in\mathbb Q[S]\) linear or constant, by pairing opposite coefficients.
At \(x_1=\rho\) and at either unit-circle conjugate \(x_2=\eta\) put \(z_i=x_i^p,\ S_i=z_i+z_i^{-1}\).
The two traces \(S_i\) are conjugates of degree two, as \(S_1>2\) and \(|S_2|<2\).
Near each node use \(S=x^p+x^{-p}\) as local coordinate and set
\[
          k=\frac{\varepsilon x-1}{\varepsilon x+1},\qquad
          j=\frac{x^p-1}{x^p+1},\qquad B=kj .
\]
All denominators are nonzero since \(\rho>1\) and \(\eta\) is nontorsion.
After division by a nonvanishing local factor the graph becomes \(E(S)-B O(S)\).
The polynomial \(O\) cannot vanish identically since then \(E\) would also vanish at its two distinct nodes.
It has no zero at either quadratic trace, so the fractional linear or constant function \(E/O\) must agree with \(B\) in both value and first derivative at both nodes.
Every such rational function satisfies
\[
           B'_1 B'_2=\left(\frac{B_1-B_2}{S_1-S_2}\right)^2,           \tag{2}
\]
as follows by expanding the determinant of its two linear polynomials.
The primes here denote ordinary derivatives with respect to \(S\).

Since \(S=2(1+j^2)/(1-j^2)\), \(x^p-x^{-p}=4j/(1-j^2)\), and \(dk/d\log x=(1-k^2)/2\), the chain rule gives
\[
                 B'=k\,\frac{(1-j^2)^2}{8j}
                      +\frac{(1-k^2)(1-j^2)}{8p}.
\]
At \(x_1\), \(j=(y-1)/(y+1)\) and \(k\) is fixed with \(k^2\ne1\).
The first term is \(O_P(y^{-2})\) and the second has magnitude comparable to \(1/(py)\), whence \(|B'_1|\gg_P1/(py)\).
At \(x_2\) write \(k=i d,\ j=i t\) with fixed real \(d\ne0\) and real \(t\ne0\) depending on \(p\).
The ratio of the absolute second term to the absolute first is at most \((1+d^2)/(2p|d|)\) because \(|t|/(1+t^2)\le1/2\).
Therefore \(|B'_2|\gg_P(1+t^2)^2/|t|\gg_P 1+t^2\).
Finally \(|B_1-B_2|^2\ll_P1+t^2\) and \(|S_1-S_2|\gg_P y\).
Equation (2) would imply \(1/(py)\ll_P1/y^2\), which fails for every sufficiently large \(p\).
The estimates apply to both signs, including powers with \(z_2\) arbitrarily close to either endpoint.

<a id="outer"></a>
## Reducing width by eliminating the two leading terms

Let \(R=(U,V)\) and let \(J=(V^*,U^*)\) with formal reversal now of width five.
Both graphs contain \(P^2\) by reversal.
At \(X=\rho\), divide the graph by \(y^5\) to see that \(|u_5+\rho v_5|\ll_P H/y\).
Subtract \(5p\) times its value equation from its Euler derivative equation and divide by \(y^5\).
The remaining top term is \(\rho v_5\) and the lower terms have absolute sum \(O_P(pH/y)\).
Applying the same argument to \(J\) bounds all four coefficients \(u_5,v_5,v_0,u_0\) in absolute value by \(O_P(pH/y)\).

By the outer-coefficient hypothesis the top vectors \((u_5,v_5)\) and \((v_0,u_0)\) are linearly dependent.
If both vanish the original pair already has width at most four and the [width-four estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v246.md#width) is stronger than (1).
Otherwise a primitive nonzero integer combination \(aR+bJ\) cancels both leading terms with \(\max(|a|,|b|)\ll_P pH/y\).
Indeed the two coordinates of a nonzero column of the top-coefficient matrix, interchanged and with one sign changed, give such a combination before division by their greatest common divisor.
The condition \(D\ne0\) makes \(R,J\) linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\), because their determinant is \(UU^*-VV^*=Y^5D\).
If \(a=\pm b\) their combination is an eigenvector of width-five reversal, so its vanished leading coefficients also force both constant coefficients to vanish.
After division of both rows by \(Y\) one would have a nonzero width-three graph with double factor \(P\), contradicting the [width-three exclusion](#three).
Thus \(a^2\ne b^2\).

For this combination the reflected determinant is \((a^2-b^2)D\), since the two mixed terms cancel identically.
The resulting width-four pair still contains \(P^2\) and has coefficient cost at most \(O_P(pH^2/y)\).
Applying the [squared Salem rate](Yin_L_G0462_v246.md#width) gives \(2\log H\ge3p\log\rho-o_P(p)\), as claimed.
