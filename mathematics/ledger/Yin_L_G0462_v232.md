# Checking coprimality and comparing the two measures over the seed

<a id="gcd"></a>
## A common divisor of bounded degree before any perturbation

Let \(n\ge2,\ 0<u<n,\ (u,n)=1\), and let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z[T]\) satisfy \(a(0)\ne0,\ \deg a=B\ge1,\ 0\le\deg b<B\).
Suppose there is no \(t\ne0\) with \(a(t)=b(t)=a(t^{-1})=b(t^{-1})=0\).
For example, it suffices for \(a(T)\) and \(T^{B-1}b(1/T)\) to be coprime, the seed condition in the [four-class construction](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#seed).
Form
\[
 A(Y)=a(Y^n)+Y^u b(Y^n),\quad A^*(Y)=Y^{nB} A(1/Y),\qquad
 H(T)=T^B\{a(T)a(T^{-1})-b(T)b(T^{-1})\}.
\]
The polynomial \(H\) has degree \(2B\) and nonzero constant term by the two endpoints of \(a\) and the smaller degree of \(b\).
The monic rational gcd of \(A,A^*\) has degree at most \(2B\), independently of \(n\).

Indeed at a nonzero common root \(y\) put \(t=y^n,\ w=y^u\).
The two equations are \(a(t)+wb(t)=0\) and \(w a(t^{-1})+b(t^{-1})=0\).
At least one of these equations determines \(w\in\mathbb Q(t)\) uniquely by the hypothesis on the four values.
Since \((u,n)=1\), \(t,w\) then determine \(y\) uniquely by Bézout and \(\mathbb Q(y)=\mathbb Q(t)\).
The Laurent identities for \(A,A^*\) give
\[
 H(t)=t^B a(t^{-1})A(Y)-Y^u b(t)A^*(Y),\qquad t=Y^n .
\]
The multiplicity of \(y\) in the gcd is at most the multiplicity of \(t\) in \(H\), since the power map is unramified at \(y\).
Distinct common roots have distinct \(t\), and zero is not a root.
Summing multiplicities proves the bound.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## When the common divisor consists entirely of cyclotomic factors

Write \(L=\log(\|a\|_1^2+\|b\|_1^2)\) for the logarithmic coefficient bound.
If
\[
                      n>L\,(\log(6B))^3,                              \tag{1}
\]
every common root is a root of unity.
In fact the monic rational gcd is then exactly the cyclotomic part of \(A\), with every multiplicity.
To see this, the primitive integral minimal polynomial of \(t\) divides \(H\) in \(\mathbb Z[T]\) by Gauss's lemma, so its logarithmic Mahler measure is at most \(m(H)\le L\).
The degrees of \(t\) and \(y\) agree and the absolute logarithmic height of \(t=y^n\) is \(n\) times the height of \(y\).
The logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive minimal polynomial of \(y\) is therefore at most \(L/n\).
At degree \(2\le v\le2B\), [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives a lower bound \(2/(\log(3v))^3\) for a nontorsion number.
At degree one a nonzero rational nontorsion number has measure at least \(\log2\).
Both possibilities contradict (1), using \(B\ge1\).
Conversely any cyclotomic factor of \(A\) also divides its reversal to the same multiplicity.

The full list can be determined over the seed.
Every possible order \(m\) of \(y\) has \(\varphi(m)\le2B\), so \(m\le8B^2\) by the [elementary totient bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#count).
When \(n\) is a prime exceeding \(8B^2\), \(t=y^n\) has the same order \(m\) and \(y^u=t^j\), where \(j\equiv u n^{-1}\bmod m\) (take \(j=0\) for \(m=1\)).
Thus \(\Phi_m\) divides \(A\) precisely when it divides \(a(T)+T^j b(T)\).
Its multiplicity is the first \(k\ge0\) for which divisibility by \(\Phi_m\) fails for
\[
          (n\mathcal E)^k a(T)+T^j(u+n\mathcal E)^k b(T),
                 \qquad \mathcal E=T\,d/dT .                           \tag{2}
\]
This follows by evaluating the \(k\)-th Euler derivative of \(A\) at \(y\).
At a nonzero root its consecutive vanishing orders give the ordinary multiplicity.
One need only test through \(k=2B\) and one may start from the cyclotomic factors of \(H\), since \(H(t)=0\) is necessary.

For the [high-degree representatives](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#statement) with seed degree \(e\le\log(3d)\), a bounded positive seed measure \(h\), \(B=4e\), and \(L=O(ph+\log(2dp))\), both (1) and \(n>8B^2\) hold uniformly at all the fourth-logarithmic primes of sufficiently large \(d=ne\).
This applies also to a candidate four-class row before enforcing full coprimality, provided its seed coefficients satisfy the displayed hypotheses.
Removing the resulting monic gcd from both rows of \(A(x^p)\pm x A^*(x^p)\) removes a product of cyclotomic polynomials after substitution, preserves every nontorsion factor and its multiplicity, and hence keeps the logarithmic Mahler measure.
The first row retains both endpoints and loses at most \(2B\) degrees.
Dividing can alter its sign under reversal, its support and its prescribed trace jets.
The [two-jet construction](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#coprime) establishes those additional requirements on its own unstripped rows.

## The measure of the lifted witness differs little from its seed measure

For a nonzero integral \(G\) of degree \(s\) with nonzero constant coefficient, put \(G^*(T)=T^sG(1/T)\) and
\[
              F(x)=G(x^n)+\sigma xG^*(x^n),\qquad \sigma=\pm1 .
\]
For every prime \(n>4\), the actual measures satisfy
\[
  -\frac{4m(G)+2\log(C(ns+1))}{n}
       \ \le\ m(F)-m(G)\ \le\ \frac{\log2}{n},                        \tag{3}
\]
with the universal \(C=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\,d\mu(z)\).
The upper bound follows by multiplying the diagonal values over \(x^n=T\) on the circle, as in the [seed upper estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#seed).
For the lower bound let \(J\) be a primitive integral gcd of \(G,G^*\), which is reciprocal up to sign since both endpoints are nonzero.
Dividing by \(J(x^n)\) leaves a pair of coprime rows that are reversals up to sign.
The [single-graph estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) gives the lower bound with \(m(G)-m(J)\) in the error and with the new span at most \(ns+1\).
Adding back \(m(J)\) to the two measures and using \(m(J)\ge0\) gives (3).
Scalar content may be removed and restored in the same way.

For either [four-class witness and its seed](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#seed) one has \(s=4ep,\ m(G)=O(ph)\), and \(P_0^2\mid G,\ P_0(x^n)^2\mid F\).
Both polynomials are primitive because the two seed rows occupy distinct classes modulo \(p\), and the two lifted parts occupy distinct classes modulo \(n\).
Their noncyclotomic cofactor measures differ by \(m(F)-m(G)\) since the same \(2h\) is removed from each.
Here \(\log(ns+1)=o(ph)\), so this difference is \(O(ph/n)\) uniformly in the representative and the window prime.
The [integral rotation determinant](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v404.md#fourth) consequently gives the lower bound \((1-o(1))ph\) separately at both the original seed and the lifted input, for each of the two witnesses.
Thus a cofactor lower bound larger by a fixed positive multiple of \(p\) would have to be present already at the seed.
The comparison does not supply that lower bound.
