## 2. High-degree core, finite-component obstruction and exact low attachments

For a vertex v in H, inequality(5) gives

    deg_(G[H])(v)>=deg_G(v)-1>=2.                     (6)

Every non-isolated component has an H vertex by L419.

Following edges without immediately reversing, or taking a longest simple path, produces a simple cycle.
The graph is bipartite, so that cycle has even length at least4.
Therefore

    every non-isolated finite union component contains
    a simple cycle entirely in H.                     (7)

In particular every finite acyclic union component is exactly an isolated edge.
This excludes branching finite trees as well as L419's previously excluded paths, stars and whole-cycle components.
A cycle lying inside a larger branching component is not excluded by L419 and is not excluded here.
An infinite component need not contain a cycle merely because its high-degree core has minimum degree2.

The low-degree attachments have a precise finite combinatorial form.
Since G[L] is a matching and isolated vertices, a maximal path through L contains at most two consecutive low vertices.
A low singleton has either one H neighbor (a leaf) or two H neighbors (a two-edge bridge).
A low pair has either no H neighbor (the isolated-edge component), one H neighbor (a two-edge pendant path), or two H neighbors (a three-edge bridge).
These cases exhaust the possibilities, since each low vertex has total degree1 or2.
Equation(5) further says each H vertex is incident to at most one such low attachment.
Thus each non-isolated component is obtained from its possibly disconnected induced H graph by those specified pendant paths and bridges, while every vertex of that induced H graph still has at least two H neighbors.
This is a structural decomposition only; bridges can join different H components, and finite H components or cycles are not automatically connected to zero.
