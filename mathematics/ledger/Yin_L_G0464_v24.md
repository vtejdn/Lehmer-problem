# Sharp real matching lengths for the zero profile at a fixed terminal block

<a id="sharp-prefix"></a>
## The extremal ratio

In the [full odd-grid domain](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#original-domain), take \(x=0\), \(p=2n+1\), a strictly negative terminal block of size \(\kappa\ge1\), and a strictly positive initial block.
Let \(L\le n+1\) be a matching index for the fixed reference \(P_{*,0}=(1+z^2)^2/((1-z)^2(1+z))\), with the constant term at index zero.
For \(L\ge5\) put \(D\) equal to the largest odd integer with \(2D\le L+1\).
Then
\[
             (2\kappa+1)(L-2)\le (2\kappa+1)\,2D\le p .          \tag{1}
\]
The strict residue signs alone give (1).
For every fixed \(\kappa\), the largest possible limiting ratio \(L/p\) is exactly \(1/(2\kappa+1)\), even for rational coefficients with the strict left endpoint and complete Mahler measure greater than one.
Writing \(d=2\kappa+1\), the examples below use all sufficiently large odd multiples \(p=dq\) with \(q\) odd and have
\[
 L=q-2,\qquad 1<M(P)\le\exp\!\big(1/(100d^2p)\big).
 \tag{2}
\]
The coefficients have common denominator dividing \(10^6(d-1)d^5p^2\).
These are rational approximations to the [unrestricted integer problem](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
In that same strict chamber, an integral sequence with \(1<M(P)\to1\) must instead satisfy [\(\log L/\log p\to0\) for its longest five-profile prefix](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#prefix-cut).
On 2026-11-16 the construction contributor for Yin developed the bound and rational realization for the fixed zero profile.
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v58.md#fixed-block-prefix-review) checks the upper bound, exact matching length, denominator, signs and complete root count.

<a id="quadrature-bound"></a>
## A single quadrature node bounds the separator

Write \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\), \(B_n(2\cos\theta)=\sin(p\theta/2)/\sin(\theta/2)\), and \(\rho_j=Q(b_j)/B_n'(b_j)\), where \(Q=tB_n+C\) and \(\deg C<n\).
The [prefix moment identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-cut-compatibility) gives
\[
  \sum_j \rho_j f(b_j)=\langle t^2 f(t)\rangle
     \quad(\deg f\le L-2),\qquad
  \langle g\rangle=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi g(2\cos\theta)\,d\theta .
 \tag{3}
\]
The \(D\) equal-weight arcsine quadrature nodes \(2\cos((2i-1)\pi/(2D))\), \(1\le i\le D\), integrate every polynomial of degree at most \(2D-1\) exactly.
This follows by averaging the nonconstant cosines through frequency \(2D-1\) over the midpoint grid, as in the [two-node test](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#prefix-cut).
Since \(D\ge3\) is odd, zero is one of the nodes and is distinct from the leftmost node \(v=-2\cos(\pi/(2D))\).
The cardinal polynomial \(R\) taking value one at \(v\) and zero at every other node has degree \(D-1\) and vanishes at zero.
Put \(U(t)=R(t)/t\), of degree \(D-2\).
For every \(a\) strictly between \(b_{n-\kappa+1}\) and \(b_{n-\kappa}\), equation (3) applies to \((t-a)U(t)^2\), of degree \(2D-3\le L-2\).
Its sum is strictly positive since \((b_j-a)\rho_j>0\) at all nodes and \(U\) has degree less than \(n\).
The integral equals \(\langle(t-a)R^2\rangle=(v-a)/D\) by exact quadrature.
Letting \(a\) increase to the upper end of the separator interval gives \(b_{n-\kappa}\le v\).
Since \(b_{n-\kappa}=-2\cos((2\kappa+1)\pi/p)\), monotonicity gives the second inequality of (1), and \(2D\ge L-2\) gives the first.

<a id="periodic-weights"></a>
## Periodic weights and the first nonintegral coordinate

Fix \(d=2\kappa+1\), let \(q\) range over sufficiently large odd integers, and set \(p=dq\).
Write \(\theta_j=2\pi j/p\) on the full grid \(0\le j<p\) and \(b_j=2\cos\theta_j\).
Choose \(\tau\) to be the nearest integer to \((d-1)\pi/2\), so that each of its distances from \((d-2)\pi/2\) and \(d\pi/2\) exceeds one.
Put \(\eta=\tau^2/p^2\), and define the even weights
\[
 \phi_j=
 \begin{cases}
  \dfrac d2(1+\eta)(1-\cos\theta_j),&d\mid j,\\[3pt]
  \dfrac d{2(d-1)}\big((1+\cos\theta_j)-\eta(1-\cos\theta_j)\big),&d\nmid j .
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(\phi_0=0\).
With
\[
 T_j=d\mathbf1_{d\mid j}-1
      =2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\kappa}\cos(\ell q\theta_j),\quad
 A=1-\frac{d(1-\eta)}{2(d-1)},\quad B=\frac{d(1+\eta)}{2(d-1)},
\]
equation (4) reads \(\phi_j=1+T_j(A-B\cos\theta_j)\).
The lowest positive frequency of \(\phi-1\) is \(q-1\), with Laurent coefficient \(-B/2\), and the largest is \(\kappa q+1<p/2\) for \(q>2\).

On the half grid let \(\omega_j=2\phi_j/p\), prescribe residues \(\rho_j^*=b_j^2\omega_j\), and define a monic trace by
\[
             \frac{Q_*(t)}{B_n(t)}
                    =t+\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\rho_j^*}{t-b_j}.
 \tag{5}
\]
In the [original affine coordinates](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-coordinates) the coefficients are \(a_i=\sum_j\rho_j^* E_i(b_j)\) for \(0\le i<n\), by the [discrete orthogonality](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-cut-compatibility).
Evenness and \(\phi_0=0\) make these the full-grid means of \(\phi_j b_j^2 E_i(b_j)\).
The Laurent polynomial \(b^2E_i(b)\), with \(b=z+z^{-1}\), has extreme powers \(\pm(i+2)\) of coefficient one.
For \(i\le q-4\) its product with \(\phi-1\) has zero mean, because its frequencies have magnitude at most \(q-2\) and cannot cancel those of \(\phi-1\) modulo \(p\).
For \(i=q-3\) precisely the two extreme powers cancel the frequencies \(\mp(q-1)\), changing the mean by \(-B\).
The mean without \(\phi-1\) is \(\langle t^2E_i(t)\rangle\) in this range.
Consequently the first changed coordinate is \(a_{q-3}=4-B\), since \(q-3\) is positive and even for large \(q\).
Here \(0<B<1\) for large \(q\).
The affine prefix equivalence therefore gives the exact last matching index \(q-2\).
All \(a_i\) have denominator dividing \(4(d-1)p^2\), since this integer clears the Laurent coefficients of \(\phi\), and a full-grid mean sums the Laurent coefficients at multiples of \(p\).
The integral basis relation \(Q_*=tB_n+\sum_i a_i B_{n-i-1}\) transfers this common denominator to \(Q_*\).

<a id="strict-cut"></a>
## Strict signs and the endpoint with room for a constant perturbation

Let \(u_j=\pi-\theta_j\) for \(1\le j\le n\).
The terminal \(\kappa\) indices have residues \(1,\ldots,\kappa\) modulo \(d\), because \(n=d(q-1)/2+\kappa\).
At a node with \(d\nmid j\), the sign of \(\phi_j\) is that of \(w_j-\tau\), where \(w_j=p\tan(u_j/2)>0\).
On the terminal block \(w_j\le p\tan((d-2)\pi/(2p))\), whose right side tends to \((d-2)\pi/2\), so \(w_j\le\tau-1/2\) eventually.
At every preceding node \(w_j\ge d\pi/2>\tau+1\).
The nonzero indices divisible by \(d\) always have positive weight.
Thus (5) has the required cut.

For all sufficiently large \(p\) the half grid satisfies
\[
                       |b_j^2\phi_j|\ge\frac1{2p^2}.               \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(|b_j|\ge2\sin(\pi/(2p))\ge2/p\).
For \(|b_j|\le1\), equation (4) gives \(\phi_j\ge1/8\) since \(|\cos\theta_j|\le1/2\) and \(\eta\to0\).
For \(|b_j|>1\), it suffices to bound \(|\phi_j|\).
If \(d\mid j\), the lower bound follows from \(1-\cos\theta_j\ge2\sin^2(\pi/p)\ge8/p^2\).
If \(d\nmid j\) and \(\theta_j\le\pi/2\), (4) gives \(\phi_j\ge1/4\) eventually.
At the other nodes with \(d\nmid j\), one has \(1-\cos\theta_j\ge1\) and
\[
   |\phi_j|=\frac{d(1-\cos\theta_j)}{2(d-1)p^2}
                          |w_j^2-\tau^2|\ge\frac1{2p^2},
\]
since \(|w_j-\tau|\ge1/2\) and \(\tau\ge3\).
This proves (6).
In particular an additive trace constant of magnitude less than \(1/(4p^2)\) preserves the strict cut, using \(|1/B_n'(b_j)|=4\sin\theta_j\sin(\theta_j/2)/p\le4/p\).

The endpoint of (5) is \(-2-\sum_j\rho_j^*/(2+b_j)\).
On its negative block (4) gives \(|\phi_j|\le2\tau^2/p^2\), and \(2+b_j\ge4\sin^2(\pi/(2p))\ge4/p^2\).
The total increase above \(-2\) from the negative terms is consequently at most \(4\kappa\tau^2/p\), while all positive terms decrease the endpoint.
For fixed \(\kappa\) the endpoint ratio is below \(-1\) eventually and remains negative under the indicated perturbations since \(|B_n(-2)|=1\).

<a id="nonreal-pair"></a>
## A nonreal pair at the same matching length

By the frequency gap and \(\phi_0=0\) we have \(\sum_j\omega_j=1\), \(\sum_j b_j\omega_j=0\) on the half grid.
Dividing \(b_j^2\) by \(t-b_j\) in (5) gives \(Q_*=t^2 S(t)\), where
\[
             S(t)=B_n(t)D_p(t),\qquad
             D_p(t)=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\omega_j}{t-b_j}.
\]
For an odd integer \(v\) let \(F_0(v)\) be the full-grid mean of \(1/(z+z^{-1})\) on the \(v\)-th roots of unity, where the denominator never vanishes.
Inserting \(z^k\) in the numerator defines \(F_k(v)\), with \(F_{-k}=F_k\), \(F_1=1/2\), and \(F_{k-1}+F_{k+1}=0\) for \(1\le k<v\).
The identities follow by multiplying by \(z+z^{-1}\) and taking the grid mean.
By \(F_v=F_0\), induction on the odd indices gives \(F_0(v)=(-1)^{(v-1)/2}/2\).
Now \(-D_p(0)\) is the full-grid mean of \(\phi_j/b_j\).
In this mean the term \(T_j\cos\theta_j/b_j=T_j/2\) averages to zero, and the class \(d\mid j\) runs over the full \(q\)-grid.
It follows that
\[
            -D_p(0)=(1-A)F_0(p)+A F_0(q).
\]
If the two signs agree the absolute value is \(1/2\).
If they differ it is \(|1-2A|/2=|1-d\eta|/(2(d-1))\).
In both cases \(|D_p(0)|\ge1/(4(d-1))\) eventually.

Take \(R=1/(100d^2p)\).
For \(|t|\le R\), one has \(|t-b_j|\ge |b_j|/2\).
Also \(\sum_j b_j^{-2}\le p^2\), since \(|b_j|\ge8|j-p/4|/p\) by the sine chord bound.
In detail, the two nearest squared inverse index distances contribute at most \(16+16/9\), and the remaining sum is at most \(2\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-2}\le4\).
As \(|\phi_j|\le2d\) for large \(p\), it follows that \(|D_p(t)-D_p(0)|\le8dp|t|\le 0.08/d\).
For the factor \(B_n=E_n+E_{n-1}\), write \(E_k(t)\) as the upper left entry of the \(k\)-th power of the matrix with rows \((t,-1)\), \((1,0)\).
At zero this matrix is orthogonal, and telescoping the powers bounds \(|E_k(t)-E_k(0)|\le k|t|\exp(k|t|)\).
Hence \(|B_n(t)-B_n(0)|\le2pR\exp(pR)<1/10\) on the disk, where \(B_n(0)=\pm1\).
These bounds give \(|S(t)|>1/(16d)\) throughout the disk.

Set \(\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}(S(0))/(10^6(d-1)d^5p^2)\) and \(Q=Q_*+\epsilon\).
The bounds at the poles and endpoint prove the required strict inequalities for \(Q\).
The integer \(10^6(d-1)d^5p^2\) clears its coefficients since it is a multiple of \(4(d-1)p^2\).
The constant perturbation changes only \(a_{n-1}\), leaving the first changed coordinate \(a_{q-3}\) intact.
On \(|t|=R\) one has \(|t^2S(t)|>R^2/(16d)>|\epsilon|\), so [Rouché's theorem on a disk](../audits/literature/Sha23/Yin_U001.md#rouche-disk) gives exactly two zeros of \(Q\) in the disk, counted with multiplicity.
On the real diameter \(S\) has constant sign \(\operatorname{sgn}(S(0))\), and the chosen sign of \(\epsilon\) excludes any real zero there.
The [same-sign gaps and the endpoint interval](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#original-domain) contain \(n-1\) distinct real trace roots in \((-2,2)\).
The disk pair therefore accounts for every other trace root.
For a trace root of modulus at most \(R\), each root of \(z^2-tz+1\) has modulus at most \((R+\sqrt{4+R^2})/2\le e^{R/2}\).
Each nonreal trace contributes exactly one root outside the unit circle, because its lifted roots are reciprocal and unit-circle roots have real trace.
Thus the complete reciprocal polynomial \(P=z^{n+1}Q(z+z^{-1})\) has the strict measure bound (2).
