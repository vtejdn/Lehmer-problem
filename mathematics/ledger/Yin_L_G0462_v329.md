# What the cross-disciplinary tests detect in the Lehmer problem

2026-11-25.

<a id="core"></a>
## The same orbit at every place

For an irreducible integer input of measure below two, the leading and nonzero constant coefficients must have absolute value one after removing a possible zero root.
The question is therefore already present for a single algebraic unit.
If its conjugates are \(\alpha_i\), their positive radial logarithms sum to \(m=\sum_i\log^+|\alpha_i|\); the negative radial logarithms have total absolute value \(m\).
Kronecker's theorem settles the equality case.
As the degree grows, the project seeks a positive lower bound for this **sum**, or actual units with positive sums tending to zero.
A real Salem unit concentrates the positive sum in one conjugate.
It remains an essential test for any proposed uniform inequality, and the [bounded-orbit reduction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v119.md#statement) explains why it appears even for products of polynomial factors.
The [adjacent-record criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#statement) is equivalent to unrestricted gap failure.
The [integral trace-pole construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) would supply counterexamples if it occurred for unbounded heights \(q\); the reverse reduction remains open.

<a id="one-prime"></a>
## What survives releasing one finite place

The capacity tests compare the conjugates to the logarithmic capacity of the compact sets formed by the segments to their powers. [Van Ittersum, Ringeling and Zudilin](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972721000654)
The [one-prime capacity construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v169.md#statement) gives an entire irreducible rational conjugate set with every powered test satisfied and a zero prefix of order the square root of the degree.
The [volume calculation on the trace pole](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v171.md#volume), using root-to-coefficient coordinates related to [DiPippo and Howe](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9803097), explains why the mean over lattice translates can be small while the specified translate still calls for an arithmetic argument.
At the genuine pole \(q=2\), the rational cubic \(X^3+4X^2+(6X-1)/4\) realizes the root pattern; its reciprocal lift has primitive measure \(4\lambda\) for its exterior root \(1<\lambda<3/2\). [Exact cubic comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v171.md#cubic)

The [joint construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement) meets the exact pole, the strict root conditions, all powered capacity inequalities, a growing zero prefix and any specified compatible congruence away from one chosen prime on the **same** polynomial.
The external archimedean factor \(\lambda\) tends to one; the primitive integer measure is \(\ell^a\lambda\).
The [degree requirement](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v173.md#statement) explains why imposing the pole and growing exact moments cannot maintain the earlier square-root-in-degree scale at \(\log\lambda\asymp 1/q\) and \(J\asymp q\).
This comparison singles out simultaneous integrality as a property the successful argument must exploit.
It supplies neither a principle that rounds these rational examples to units nor a proof that no integral examples exist.

<a id="support"></a>
## A finite-place tool that excludes genuine candidates

The exact value \(q^{2D}F((q+1)/q)=(2q+1)^2\) forces unusually good local approximation when a reciprocal coefficient is followed by a run of zeros.
The odd member of \(q,q+1\) is a dyadic unit, as is the partial sum evaluated at the two consecutive integers after the even powers are factored off.
Applying Yu's estimate to these two local units bounds the position of the next nonzero coefficient; the coprimality of consecutive integers ensures that the multiplicative difference never vanishes. [Yu, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-89-4-337-378) · [Bounds for the first nonzero moment](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v174.md#statement)
Iteration and the archimedean coefficient estimate make the [bounded-support part of the trace-pole construction effectively finite](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#statement), even when heights, degrees, coefficient sizes and separating grids all vary.
In particular any sequence on this branch giving a counterexample needs at least a constant times \(q^{1/3}\) nonzero coefficients in its reciprocal lift.
A remaining question is whether several partial evaluations can be compared at once on dense supports.
For example, at consecutive occupied positions the available inequality bounds \(j+1\) by a growing multiple of \(j\); it permits a coefficient at every position.

<a id="realizations"></a>
## Costs in graph and geometric representations

A Salem trace \(t=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\) is an adjacency eigenvalue of a tree by [Salez's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.4423).
Fragmentation at a repeated eigenvalue, as in [Parter–Wiener](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479801393320), shows that if \(t\downarrow2\), the complementary log Mahler measure **per copy** of the Salem minimum diverges in every such tree. [Tree cost](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v168.md#statement)
Thus the gap for the whole graph polynomial does not directly transfer to a designated factor, even when that factor occurs with arbitrarily large multiplicity.

Rigidity of short geodesics in one arithmetic orbifold suggests representing multiple Salem eigenvalues in the same space. [Frączyk and Pham](https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/8886)
The exact [split-place count and translation lengths](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v170.md#statement) show the cost: distinct marked trace fields force additional noncompact real factors, and using two different trace conjugates of one Salem number also increases both product lengths.
The length in the single marked factor remains \(2\log\lambda\).
Both geometric constructions therefore distinguish the distinguished spectral value from the size of the containing representation.
