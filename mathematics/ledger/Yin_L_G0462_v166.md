# Counting collisions above primes of the original discriminant

<a id="large"></a>
## A loss paid by the original discriminant

Let \(P,\alpha,d,p,I_p,U,V,k,H\) satisfy the [double-root hypotheses](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#statement).
At every auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne p\) with \(2\ell\ge d+1\), including primes at which \(P\) has repeated roots modulo \(\ell\),
\[
          v_\ell I_p\ \le\ 4v_\ell H+\tfrac12v_\ell|\operatorname{disc}P| .       \tag{1}
\]
In the proof a smaller threshold suffices if the multiplicities of the original residues in the powered disks are known.

Use the [full collision tree](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#index) in a splitting field, with \(v(\ell)=1\).
On a mixed disk \(D\) let \(m\) count its marks \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\), and let \(M\) be the largest number of them with the same original residue \(\bar\alpha_i\).
Write \(W_D\) for the number of unordered pairs with equal original residues and \(J_D=\binom m2-W_D\).
The mixed-disk labels \(U(\beta_i)/V(\beta_i)\bmod\ell\) identify exactly these original residues since \(p\nmid k\).
For each pair with different labels take a nonconstant disk on its path, using the [tuned pencil proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#paths).
That step only uses the difference of the two unit residues, and it allows rational depths and ramified centers.
Among the resulting finitely many disks retain the maximal ones.
They are disjoint, and all uncovered marks have the same label, so at least \(m-M\) marks are covered.
For a finite common generic pencil write \(n_D\) for its number of roots in \(D\), with multiplicity.

If every maximal selected disk has separable nonconstant reduction, the [critical polynomial](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#jets) counts every mark in it, including marks of the same label.
Adding the separate estimates \(2n_B-2\ge m_B\) gives \(2n_D\ge m-M+2\).
If any such disk has inseparable reduction, \(n_D\ge\ell\).
Thus the same inequality holds whenever \(2\ell\ge m-M+2\), in particular under the threshold in (1).
For \(M=1\), \(J_D\le 2n_D^2\).
For \(M\ge2\), \(m\le2n_D+M-2\) yields
\[
       \binom m2\le m^2/2\le4n_D^2+(M-2)^2\le4n_D^2+2W_D .
\]
It follows that \(J_D\le4n_D^2+W_D\) on every mixed disk, and this also holds when \(J_D=0\).

The sum of the depth increments times \(J_D\) is \(v_\ell I_p\), and the sum with \(n_D^2\) is at most \(v_\ell H\) by the [nested intersection estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#length).
For a pair with the same original residue the geometric sum for the \(p\)-th power has nonzero reduction \(p\bar\alpha_i^{p-1}\).
Its total shared depth is \(v(\beta_i-\beta_j)=v(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)\).
Pairs with different original residues contribute zero to the original discriminant valuation.
Consequently the sum with \(W_D\) over the entire tree is exactly \(\tfrac12 v_\ell|\operatorname{disc}P|\), proving (1).
More generally, one may retain just the disks satisfying the numerical threshold \(2\ell\ge m-M+2\), together with any other disks known to admit the stated separable covering.
The sum of their weighted \(J_D\) obeys the same right side.

## Which part of the index remains outside the comparison

Let \(I^\dagger_p\) denote the divisor of \(I_p\) supported on auxiliary primes satisfying either \(\ell\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\) or \(2\ell\ge d+1\).
At good primes the [uniform factor \(2/9\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#statement) already applies in every characteristic.
At the remaining primes in \(I^\dagger_p\) use (1).
Summing nonnegative local valuations and logarithms gives
\[
        \log H\ \ge\ \tfrac29\log I^\dagger_p-\tfrac18\log|\operatorname{disc}P| .
\]
Writing \(h=m(P)\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure, Hadamard's inequality on the Vandermonde rows gives
\[
             \log|\operatorname{disc}P|\le d\log d+2(d-1)h .
\]
Indeed the row for \(\alpha_i\) has norm at most \(\sqrt d\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^{d-1}\), and the squared determinant is the absolute discriminant.
At an admissible \(p\) of the [fourth-logarithmic window](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) use its primitive adjacent-row witness and the [resultant-to-measure estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v366.md#statement).
The substitution of the positive lower bound when available, or zero otherwise, gives
\[
 (2p+2)h\ \ge\
 \frac{1-4/p}{32d+1}
 \left[\tfrac29\log I^\dagger_p-\tfrac18\big(d\log d+2(d-1)h\big)\right]
 -\frac2p\log\!\big(Cp(16d+1)\big),
\]
where \(C\) is the circle integral in that estimate.
Moving the term proportional to \(h\) to the left increases its coefficient by a bounded amount.
Thus if \(d\to\infty\) with \(\liminf \log I^\dagger_p/(pd)\ge\kappa>0\), then \(\liminf h\ge\kappa/288\).
Here \(p\gg\log d\), so the original discriminant cost contributes zero after normalization.
The only excluded prime support consists of \(p\) itself and the discriminant primes with \(2\ell<d+1\).
The [Salem example at three](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) places the entire index on the latter support for prescribed primes in the window.
An input-dependent selection giving positive \(\kappa\), or another source of cofactor measure where this fails, is still needed.
