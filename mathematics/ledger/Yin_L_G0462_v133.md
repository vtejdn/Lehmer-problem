# Sampling polynomial weights at the circle conjugates of a Salem number

<a id="energy"></a>
## Fourier energy of the neutral conjugates

Let \(1<\sigma\le2\) be a Salem number of degree \(d\), let \(\nu\) be uniform probability on its \(d-2\) conjugates on the unit circle, and let \(m\) be arclength probability.
Set \(\eta=\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\) and \(N=d^4\).
Use the Fourier convention \(\widehat\nu(k)=\int e^{-ik\theta}\,d\nu(\theta)\).
All implicit constants below are absolute.
The [discriminant estimate (1)](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#energy) with \(Y=d\), \(t=1-(2d)^{-6}\), and the minimal polynomial of \(\sigma\) gives
\[
  \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{t^k}{k}
    \left|(d-2)\widehat\nu(k)+2\sigma^{-k}\right|^2
       \ll d\log(2d).
\]
Indeed the two positive real roots both have reflected value \(\sigma^{-1}\), conjugation makes the neutral Fourier sum real, and the Mahler measure in logarithmic units is \(\log\sigma\le\log2\).
The nonzero integer discriminant contributes a nonpositive logarithmic term.
For \(1\le k\le N\), \(t^k\) is bounded away from zero.
Removing the two real terms costs \(O(\log(2N))\) in the unnormalized energy, by the squared triangle inequality and \(\sigma^{-k}\le1\).
Consequently
\[
              \sum_{1\le |k|\le N}\frac{|\widehat\nu(k)|^2}{|k|}
                     \ll\eta^2 .                                           \tag{1}
\]

<a id="arcs"></a>
## Counts on shrinking arcs and at the trace endpoints

For any arc \(I\) of normalized length \(\ell=m(I)\),
\[
      |\nu(I)-\ell|\ll\eta\sqrt{\log(2+\ell/\eta)}.                       \tag{2}
\]
In particular the supremum over arcs is \(O(\log(2d)/\sqrt d)\), every angular gap between consecutive neutral conjugates is \(O(\eta)\), and \(\nu(I)\sim\ell\) uniformly as \(\ell/\eta\to\infty\).

It suffices to take large \(d\) so that \(h=\eta<1/8\).
In circle coordinates of period one, expand or contract \(I\) by \(h\) at each endpoint and convolve the resulting indicator with uniform probability on \([-h,h]\).
These two continuous functions majorize and minorize the original indicator for any choice of endpoints.
Use the constant one for an expansion filling the circle, and zero for an empty contraction.
Their means differ from \(\ell\) by at most \(2h\).
For either function \(F\), direct integration of the interval indicators gives
\[
  |\widehat F(k)|\ll\min\{\ell+2h,\,1/|k|,\,1/(h k^2)\}\qquad(k\ne0).
\]
Splitting at \(1/(\ell+2h)\) and \(1/h\) gives
\(\sum_{k\ne0}|k|\,|\widehat F(k)|^2\ll 1+\log(2+\ell/h)\).
Its absolutely summable Fourier tail past \(N\) has sum \(O(1/(hN))\).
Cauchy–Schwarz in (1) up to \(N\), followed by \(|\widehat\nu(k)|\le1\) in the tail, bounds \(|\nu(F)-m(F)|\) and proves (2).
An empty arc satisfies \(\ell/\eta\ll\sqrt{\log(2+\ell/\eta)}\), giving the gap bound.

Write \(n=(d-2)/2\) and let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) be the interior traces, one for each neutral conjugate pair.
For \(0\le u\le4\), put \(\ell_u=\pi^{-1}\arccos(1-u/2)\).
Counting the two symmetric phases of each trace gives
\[
 \#\{j:2-x_j\le u\}=n\ell_u+
             O\!\left(d\eta\sqrt{\log(2+\ell_u/\eta)}\right).             \tag{3}
\]
The same formula holds for \(\#\{j:x_j+2\le u\}\) by centering the arc at angle \(\pi\).
As \(u\to0\), \(\ell_u\sim\sqrt u/\pi\), so both endpoint counts are asymptotic to \(n\sqrt u/\pi\) whenever \(u/\eta^2\to\infty\).

## Signed polynomials and their absolute values

Let \(U\) be a real trigonometric polynomial of degree \(1\le K\le d\), with coefficients of arbitrary size, and write \(\|U\|_p\) for its norm with respect to \(m\).
For each of \(g=U,\ |U|,\ U_-=\max(-U,0)\) we have
\[
                  |\nu(g)-m(g)|\ll K\eta\|U\|_1 .                        \tag{4}
\]
Each \(g\) is absolutely continuous with \(|g|\le|U|\), \(|g'|\le|U'|\) almost everywhere in radian coordinates.
Parseval and the degree bound give
\[
 \sum_{k\ne0}|k|\,|\widehat g(k)|^2
    \le\|g\|_2\|g'\|_2
    \le K\|U\|_2^2
    \le K(2K+1)\|U\|_1^2 .
\]
The last inequality uses \(|\widehat U(k)|\le\|U\|_1\).
The Fourier series of \(g\) is absolutely convergent since its derivative lies in \(L^2\), and it equals \(g\) by continuity.
Its tail past \(N\) has absolute sum \(O(N^{-1/2}\|g'\|_2)\), by Cauchy–Schwarz with \(\sum_{k>N}k^{-2}\le1/N\).
As \(\|g'\|_2\le K\sqrt{2K+1}\|U\|_1\) and \(N=d^4\), this tail fits the right side of (4).
Pairing the remaining frequencies with (1) proves (4).
The case \(K=0\) gives exact sampling.

Suppose now that \(U\) is nonnegative at every neutral conjugate.
Then \(\nu(U)=\nu(|U|)\), so (4) yields \(\nu(U)=(1+O(K\eta))\|U\|_1\).
The largest gap also bounds the negative part quadratically,
\[
                         m(U_-)\ll (K\eta)^2\|U\|_1.                      \tag{5}
\]
Each connected component \(J\) of \(\{U<0\}\) lies between consecutive neutral nodes, so its radian length is \(O(\eta)\).
It has endpoint values zero and an interior critical point.
Integration first from the critical point for \(U'\), then from an endpoint for \(U\), gives \(\int_J|U|\le |J|^2\int_J|U''|\).
Summing these bounds gives (5) because \(\|U''\|_1\le K^2\|U\|_1\).
Indeed the [Riesz interpolation identity](../audits/literature/QZ19/Yin_U001.md#qz19-u001) expresses \(U'(\theta)\) as a linear combination of translates of \(U(\theta)\) with absolute coefficient sum \(K\).
Taking the \(L^1\) norm twice yields the stated derivative bound.

In particular when \(K\eta=o(1)\), a bound on the empirical mean of a node-nonnegative \(U\) controls its full \(L^1\) norm, even when the degree grows and the signs between nodes are unknown.
For \(U(e^{i\theta})=W(2\cos\theta)\) this uses the ordinary average over the \(n\) interior traces, since each occurs twice in \(\nu\).
The exterior value \(W(\sigma+\sigma^{-1})\) enters a trace sum separately.
