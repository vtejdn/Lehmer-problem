# Factorization of the cubic graph along primitive directions and at torsion translates

<a id="schinzel"></a>
## Schinzel's conjecture predicts a single nontorsion factor

Let \(g(U)=U^3-U-1\), \(B(U)=1-U^2-U^3\), \(F(U,V)=g(U)+VB(U)\), and \(A_{a,b}(X)=F(X^a,X^b)\) for coprime positive integers \(a,b\).
Assume [Schinzel's rational factorization conjecture](../audits/literature/AS18/Yin_U001.md#as18-u001) for this one Laurent polynomial \(F\).
Then for all sufficiently large \(\max(a,b)\), removing the cyclotomic factors from \(A_{a,b}\) leaves one irreducible factor over \(\mathbb Q\) with multiplicity one.

For every positive \(m\), the diagonal pullback \(F(U^m,V^m)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb C\).
Indeed \(g(U^m)\) has a simple nonzero zero \(u\) at which \(B(U^m)\ne0\), since \(g\) is separable, \(g(0)\ne0\) and \(g,B\) are coprime.
As a polynomial in \(V\), the pullback is Eisenstein at \(U-u\) and primitive over \(\mathbb C[U]\).
It remains irreducible in the Laurent ring since neither coordinate divides it. [Power pullbacks of the graph](Yin_L_G0462_v177.md#independence)

For a nonsingular \(2\times2\) integral matrix \(M\), compose its monomial map with the integral matrix \(\operatorname{sgn}(\det M)\operatorname{adj}(M)\).
The composite is the diagonal power map with \(m=|\det M|\).
A nonunit Laurent polynomial has at least two support points, which remain distinct under every nonsingular monomial substitution.
Hence any factorization of \(F(\mathbf X^M)\) into nonunits would factor the diagonal pullback.
This proves absolute irreducibility for every isogeny pullback, including \(F\) itself.
Its rank-two support also excludes a cyclotomic Laurent polynomial, whose support is contained in an affine line.

Conjecture 2.1 of Amoroso and Sombra provides finitely many exceptional nonzero vectors \(\mathbf c\) and finitely many nonsingular matrices \(M\).
For each \(\mathbf c\), its orthogonal line in \(\mathbb Z^2\) contains only the two opposite primitive generators as primitive points.
Thus all sufficiently large primitive directions avoid these exceptional lines.
In the other alternative, write \((a,b)^{\mathsf T}=M\mathbf v\).
The sole irreducible factor of \(F(\mathbf X^M)\) is \(F(\mathbf X^M)\) itself up to a Laurent unit, and its specialization along \(\mathbf v\) is \(A_{a,b}\) up to a nonzero rational scalar and a monomial.
The conjecture therefore permits at most one irreducible factor of the specialization after all cyclotomic factors are removed, counted with multiplicity.
This specialization is nonzero and has degree \(3a+b\) and nonzero constant term.
Its [cyclotomic part has degree at most twelve](Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#cyclotomic), so the remaining factor is a nonunit for \(3a+b>12\).

In the [escaping exterior partition](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition), the conjectural conclusion gives \(k_P=a\) and \(m(P)=\log\theta+O(\exp(-cb/a))\), where \(\theta>1\) satisfies \(g(\theta)=0\).
Infinitely many distinct primitive directions with at least two nontorsion rational factors would therefore disprove this instance of Schinzel's conjecture, even if their exterior proportions stayed bounded away from zero.
In particular a sequence with \(b/a\to\infty\) and \(k_P=o(a)\) would disprove it.

<a id="bertini"></a>
## Theorem hypotheses in this specialization

The graph satisfies \(U^3V F(U^{-1},V^{-1})=F(U,V)\), placing it outside Schinzel's non-self-inversive case.
Amoroso and Sombra's Theorem 2.4 works over \(\mathbb C(z)\) and requires a nonconstant input in their sense.
Viewed over that field, our \(F\) lies entirely in the constant part removed by the theorem.
Their bivariate theorem retains a free variable, and specialization of that variable to \(1\) need not preserve irreducibility.
Likewise the graph is a curve in a two-dimensional torus, whereas the toric Bertini theorem concerns the inverse images of subtori under a dominant cover of that torus.
The [large-gap factorization theorems](Yin_L_G0450_v2.md#delayed-factorization-source-boundary) control the nonreciprocal part, whereas [the cubic factors of positive measure are eventually reciprocal](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#pairs).
None of these applications bounds the size of an individual part unconditionally for \(b/a\to\infty\).

## Rational irreducibility of cyclotomic products at a fixed direction

Now fix the direction \((a,b)\) and let the multiplier \(\zeta\) vary over roots of unity.
Put \(D=3a+b\), \(F_\zeta(X)=g(X^a)+\zeta X^b B(X^a)\), and \(K=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
For all but finitely many \(\zeta\), \(F_\zeta\) is irreducible in \(K[X]\).
Consequently for every sufficiently large order \(m\) the polynomial
\[
     \prod_{\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)=m} F_\zeta(X)
\]
is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), of degree \(D\varphi(m)\).

Consider the rational map \(X\mapsto z=-g(X^a)/(X^b B(X^a))\) on the projective line.
The two polynomials are coprime and have maximum degree \(D\), so the map has degree \(D\).
Restrict it over \(\mathbb G_m\), excluding its zeros and poles.
The pullback by \(z=w^q\), for every \(q\ge1\), has equation \(g(X^a)+w^q X^b B(X^a)=0\).
At any zero of \(g(X^a)\) the coefficient of \(w^q\) is nonzero and the constant term has a simple zero.
Eisenstein and primitivity again give absolute irreducibility, hence the pullback condition in [Zannier’s Theorem 2.1](../audits/literature/Zan10/Yin_U001.md#zan10-u001).
Proper torsion cosets in a one-dimensional torus are points.
The theorem over the cyclotomic closure \(K\) thus gives \([K(\alpha):K]=D\) for every root of \(F_\zeta\) outside a finite set of multipliers.
The leading coefficient of \(F_\zeta\) is \(-\zeta\), so this proves irreducibility in \(K[X]\).

At a root \(\alpha\), the rational map recovers \(\zeta\) from \(\alpha\), giving \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Irreducibility over \(K\) implies irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), so \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=D\varphi(m)\).
The product over all primitive \(m\)-th roots has rational integer coefficients by Galois invariance and integrality, and this is its degree.
It is therefore irreducible up to its leading sign.

The exceptional set depends on the fixed direction.
For each \(\zeta^a=1\) there exists \(\omega^a=1\) with \(\omega^b=\zeta\), and then \(F_\zeta(X)=A_{a,b}(\omega X)\).
Hence reducibility of \(A_{a,b}\) over \(K\) makes all these \(a\) multipliers exceptional.
The large-order rational product above gives no factor-count conclusion for the untwisted polynomial.
