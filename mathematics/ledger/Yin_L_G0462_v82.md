# A Salem family with trivial primitive resultants at every dyadic order up to the degree ceiling

<a id="dyadic"></a>
## Simultaneous equalities

For every sufficiently large power of two \(J\) there is a Salem minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(J-360\le d\le J-2\) with the following properties.
Its exterior root \(\alpha\) generates the same field as each positive power, and \(M(P)=\alpha<\theta_0\) tends to the plastic number \(\theta_0\) as \(J\to\infty\).
Write \(D_u(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_u)|\) and \(C_u(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^u-1)|\).
Then \(J=2^{\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}\), and
\[
  D_{2^b}(P)=1\quad(2\le b\le\log_2 J),\qquad
  C_{2^b}(P)=C_2(P)\ \mid\ (J-10)(J-2)/4\quad(1\le b\le\log_2 J).
\]
Thus both normalized logarithms at the prescribed ceiling tend to zero while the logarithmic Mahler measure tends to \(\log\theta_0>0\).
The same holds for all dyadic orders between \(d^\kappa\) and the ceiling, uniformly for any fixed \(0<\kappa<1\).
This tests the arithmetic target in [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) on inputs that already satisfy preservation of degree under all powers.

## The degree of the Salem factor

Take \(f=z^3-z-1,\ f^*=1-z^2-z^3,\ N=J-3\), and \(H=z^Nf-f^*\).
This sign belongs to the [Salem construction from a Pisot seed](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#smy15-u002).
Here one can see the factor directly.
The real cubic \(f\) has just one real root \(\theta_0>1\), and its two conjugates have modulus \(\theta_0^{-1/2}<1\).
On the unit circle \(|f^*|=|f|>0\), so for \(0<t<1\) the polynomial \(z^Nf-t f^*\) has exactly one exterior zero by Rouché's theorem.
Passing to \(t=1\) leaves at most one exterior zero, counting multiplicity.
At every fixed \(1<u<\theta_0\) one has \(H(u)<0\) eventually, while \(H(\theta_0)=-f^*(\theta_0)>0\).
Hence the unique exterior zero \(\alpha\) tends to \(\theta_0\) from below.
Since \(H\) is antireciprocal, its only interior zero is \(1/\alpha\).
The monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of \(\alpha\) has unit constant term, so its roots must include \(1/\alpha\) to balance their product.
All other irreducible factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
For every \(r\ge1\) all embeddings above the identity on \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^r)\) send \(\alpha\) to a root of modulus \(\alpha\).
Uniqueness in that modulus gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^r)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).

The [rank-two argument for the six-term support](../claims/Yin_C_G0270_v1.md#rank-two-cyclotomic-bound) applies with the signs of the second column reversed.
Indeed, partition a vanishing sum at a root of unity of order \(m\) into minimal rational relations.
The within-block differences of the six exponent vectors \((1,0),(1,1),(1,3),(0,0),(0,2),(0,3)\), evaluated against \((N,1)\), generate a lattice in the two formal coordinates before evaluation.
If the lattice had rank one and the blocks stayed in columns, both \(f\) and \(f^*\) would vanish at a root of unity.
If its direction crossed the columns, the three pairs would translate \(\{0,2,3\}\) to \(\{0,1,3\}\), whose coordinate sums differ by one.
Neither is possible.
For a single block the differences span \(\mathbb Z^2\), so the [relative order bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0262_v2.md#minimal-vanishing-order) gives \(m\mid30\).
For multiple blocks the largest size is at most four, and the full-rank lattice index divides the determinant of two independent differences, of magnitude at most six.
The same bound gives \(m\mid6s\) for some \(1\le s\le6\), since the second coordinate is evaluated at one.
The sum of totients over the union of these divisor sets is 72 by the [order table and sparse multiplicity argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0270_v1.md#uniform-scaled-plastic-repair).
Every nonzero root of a six-term polynomial has multiplicity at most five by the Vandermonde matrix of its exponents.
Thus the cyclotomic part has degree at most 360.
The roots \(1,-1\) both occur, giving the asserted bounds on \(d\), which eventually exceeds \(J/2\) and two.
The exterior pair and the remaining conjugates of modulus one now give the claimed Salem property.

## The exact resultant on the dyadic grid

At \(z^J=1\) the identity \(z^3H=f-z^3 f^*=(z+1)(z^5-1)\) leaves just the two zeros \(1,-1\) on this grid.
Let \(R=H/(z^2-1)\).
At \(1,-1\), respectively, the derivatives of \(H\) are \(10-J,2-J\), giving \(R(1)=(10-J)/2,\ R(-1)=(J-2)/2\).
At every other grid point \(R(z)=z^{-3}(z^5-1)/(z-1)\).
The fifth-power map permutes the primitive roots of each order \(2^b\) with \(2\le b\le\log_2 J\), so \(D_{2^b}(R)=1\).
The factor \(P\) divides \(R\) in \(\mathbb Z[z]\), hence also \(D_{2^b}(P)=1\).
Multiplying over orders gives \(C_{2^b}(P)=C_2(P)\) for every \(b\ge1\) up to the ceiling, and \(C_2(P)\mid C_2(R)=|(10-J)(J-2)|/4\).
