# Harmonic extinction at every irrational interior coefficient ratio

<a id="profile"></a>
## The integer series is rational even when the ratio is irrational

Fix an irrational real \(r\in(0,1)\).
Suppose \(J\) is the Herglotz transform of a symmetric circle probability and \(B=(1-rw)J\) has integer Taylor coefficients.
Then \(B\) belongs to the [eight-entry list](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#prefix), subject to the restrictions on \(r\) in the [interior-ratio table](Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#measure).
Write \(M_l=2\int v^l\,d\mu(v)\), with \(M_0=2\), and \(B_l=[w^l]B\).
The moments of \(2|1-rv|^2\mu\) at \(l\ge2\) are
\[
          (1+r^2)M_l-r(M_{l-1}+M_{l+1})=B_l-rB_{l+1}.
\]
They take finitely many values since the integers \(B_l\) are bounded.
By symmetry the full Fourier–Stieltjes sequence has finite range.
For each value, polynomial interpolation on this finite range expresses its indicator as a polynomial in the sequence.
Products of Fourier–Stieltjes transforms are transforms of convolutions and constants are transforms of point masses, so each indicator is an integer Fourier–Stieltjes transform.
The [locally compact integer transform theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) makes each indicator periodic outside a finite set of integers.
Consequently \(B_l-rB_{l+1}\) is eventually periodic, and so is \(B_l\), by iterating forward with the summable weights \(r^a\).
Thus \(B=V/U\) in lowest terms with \(U\) squarefree signed cyclotomic of constant one and \(V\) integral of constant one.

The polynomial \(V\) has no root in the open disk since \(1-rw\) is zero-free there and \(\Re J>0\).
Its reciprocal polynomial is monic integral and has all roots in the closed unit disk, so \(V\) is a signed cyclotomic product by Kronecker's theorem.
The circle zeros of \(V\) are simple by the Herglotz lower bound \(\Re J(t\xi)\ge(1-t)/(1+t)\) as \(t\uparrow1\).
Set \(K=1/J=(1-rw)U/V\).
Its continuous density away from atoms is \(\rho=K(w)+K(1/w)\).
This expression is regular across circle poles after the simple Herglotz kernels of their atoms are removed, since each kernel plus its circle conjugate is zero away from the pole.
In particular it has no poles on the circle and it is nonnegative there.
It is not identically zero, since \(K(1/r)=0\) and \(K(r)>0\).

<a id="conjugacy"></a>
## Full Galois orbits of the odd-order circle zeros

Write \(b=\deg V-\deg U-1\) and \(\delta=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\).
The bracket in \(\rho=(U/V)P\) is
\[
             P(w)=(1+\delta w^{b+1})-r(w+\delta w^b).
\]
The zeros of \(UV\) are exactly the odd-multiplicity circle zeros of \(P\), since the nonzero density has even multiplicity at every circle zero, while \(U,V\) are coprime and squarefree.
These zeros form full rational conjugacy classes since \(UV\) has rational coefficients.
If \(\xi\) is one of them and \(\xi+\delta\xi^b\ne0\), then
\[
                    r=\frac{1+\delta\xi^{b+1}}{\xi+\delta\xi^b}.
\]
For each conjugate of \(\xi\), the denominator stays nonzero, the bracket vanishes, and the same displayed quotient has the same value \(r\).
It follows that every automorphism of the cyclotomic field fixes \(r\), contrary to irrationality.
Thus both \(\xi+\delta\xi^b\) and \(1+\delta\xi^{b+1}\) vanish, which entails \(\xi^2=1\).
This restricts the odd crossings to the two endpoints even if some other zeros of \(P\) are themselves torsion.
The [winding and factor calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#measure) requires only that odd crossings and the roots of \(UV\) lie at the endpoints.
The winding number gives \(|b+1|\le2\), removes the same two inadmissible signs, and all remaining factorizations in that calculation are real polynomial identities in \(r\).
The same two range exclusions are forced by simple nonendpoint circle roots of their quadratic factors.
Their allocation to \(U,V\) by degree, parity and positivity gives the listed eight possibilities.

<a id="extinction"></a>
## A different specialization at a prime root of unity

Let \(\sigma=2\Re J\) away from atoms and suppose a common family of conditional probability moments \(c_k(w)\), \(k\ge1\), gives integer Laurent polynomials \(H_k=(1-rw)\sigma c_k\).
Put \(D=|1-rw|\sigma\).
Then \(M(D)=M(\rho)\), and the [envelope bounds](Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#envelope), computed for the normalized bracket, hold with the same real formulas.
Thus \(M(D)<2\) and the nonzero \(H_k\) lie in finitely many shapes up to monomial shifts.
We claim that there are no nonzero rows.

Choose an odd prime \(p\) with \(r\notin\mathbb Q(\xi)\) for \(\xi=e^{2\pi i/p}\), also large enough that none of the finite shapes vanishes at \(\xi\) and \(\sigma(\xi)\) is positive and regular.
An irrational \(r\) belongs to at most one prime cyclotomic field.
Indeed for distinct primes \(p,l\), the compositum has degree \(\varphi(pl)=(p-1)(l-1)\), so the intersection has degree one.
For a nonzero row let \(h=H_k(\xi)\in\mathbb Q(\xi)^\times\).
If \(c_k(\xi)\) were real, then
\[
                       h-\bar h=r(h\bar\xi-\bar h\xi).
\]
If the right parenthesis is nonzero, this places \(r\) in \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\).
If it vanishes, \(h=\bar h\ne0\) would force \(\bar\xi=\xi\), again impossible.
At this prime every nonzero moment is nonreal.

The continuous representatives of the moments are positive definite at \(\xi\), by extension of their finite moment matrices at every regular point with \(\sigma>0\).
Thus they are the moments of one probability there.
Its full two-sided moment sequence takes finitely many values, since the shapes are finite, shifts evaluate in \(\mu_p\), the divisor \((1-r\xi)\sigma(\xi)\) is fixed and the negative moments are the conjugates.
The interpolation argument in the first section makes this sequence eventually periodic.
As in the [progression-average argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion), for a period \(T\) its eventual value on positive multiples of \(T\) is the mass on \(\mu_T\), necessarily zero since every nonzero value is nonreal.
Consequently all progression averages vanish, and the specialized sequence is eventually zero.
No shape vanishes at \(\xi\), so the rows themselves are eventually zero.
If any were nonzero, the greatest nonzero index \(k\) would give \(|c_k|\le1/2\) by positivity after pushing the conditional measures by the \(k\)-th power.
Then \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(D)/2<1\), a contradiction.
The entire argument applies to \(1+rw\) by \(w\mapsto-w\).

## Ratios approaching the irrational limit

If \(r_i\to r\), signed negative masses tend to zero, and \((1-r_iw)J_i\) are integral on prefixes of lengths tending to infinity, any projected limiting probability fits the classification above by convergence at each fixed index.
In fact the [first discrepancy comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#comparison) gives the same uniform positive-fraction prefix agreement near this \(r\).
Before the first discrepancy the error in the \(J_i\) coefficients is \(O(|r_i-r|/(1-r_i))\), which tends to zero since \(r<1\).
The atom matching, positive conditional moments and nonzero first row would contradict the extinction just proved, exactly as in that comparison.
The profile, atomic support and extinction assertions alone suffice to test the farther windows of an actual denominator.
