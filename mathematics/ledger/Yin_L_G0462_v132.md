# A delay-one inverse preserving the number of exterior roots

<a id="inverse"></a>
## Integral inverses for arbitrary exterior trace configurations

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) and Mahler measure greater than one.
Write \(P(z)=z^m Q(x)\) with \(x=z+z^{-1}\), and factor \(Q=GH\) over the reals into monic polynomials, placing in \(H\) exactly the roots in \((-2,2)\).
The roots of \(Q\) are simple, and neither endpoint is a root by irreducibility.
Every root of \(G\) lifts to one interior and one exterior root of \(P\), so \(r=\deg G\ge1\) is exactly the number of exterior roots, counted with multiplicity.
Write \(h=m-r\).

For every sufficiently large integer \(|C|\) with \(C G(2)<0\), there is a monic \(f_C\in\mathbb Z[z]\) of degree \(2m\) satisfying
\[
         zf_C+f_C^*=(z+1)P,\qquad f_C^*(z)=z^{2m}f_C(1/z).
\]
It has no circle root and exactly \(r\) exterior roots.
Dividing those roots by \(|C|^{1/r}\) gives in the limit the \(r\) distinct roots of \(u^r=\operatorname{sgn}(C)\).
The constant term is one if \(r\ge2\), and is \(1+C\) if \(r=1\).
Both the threshold and the convergence assertions here are for fixed \(P\).
For \(r=1\) and a positive exterior root the [previous Pisot inverse](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#statement) also gives a simplex and finer precision in the large root.

To construct \(f_C\), put \(s=\operatorname{sgn}G(2)\), which is the sign of \(G\) on \([-2,2]\).
Choose an integer polynomial \(p_C=C H+E_C\) by rounding the coefficients below degree \(h\) of \(C H-s A H'\) to nearest integers and retaining leading coefficient \(C\).
The real constant \(A>0\) can be chosen depending only on \(Q\) so that at every zero \(x_j\) of \(H\),
\[
                         E_C(x_j)/Q'(x_j)<0.
\]
Indeed \(E_C=-s A H'\) plus a polynomial of degree below \(h\) with coefficients of absolute value at most \(1/2\).
At these finitely many simple roots, \(Q'=GH'\) and \(G\) has sign \(s\).
The coefficients of \(E_C\) stay bounded independently of \(C\), and \(\deg E_C<h\).
For \(h=0\) take \(p_C=C\) and \(E_C=0\).
Use the [polynomial form of the inverse](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex)
\[
                  f_C=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}p_C(x).
\]
The subtracted term is integral and antisymmetric in formal degree \(2m-1\), which proves the return identity.
Its lowest exponent is \(r-1\), with coefficient \(-C\), giving the stated constant terms.

<a id="exterior"></a>
## The direction in which the circle roots move

Replace \(p_C\) by \(C H+tE_C\) for \(0\le t\le1\) and write \(f_t\) for the corresponding real polynomial.
At \(t=0\) it factors as \(z^h H(x)\) times
\[
          z^rG(x)-C z^{r-1}(z-1).
\]
After division by \(-C\) this last factor converges on bounded sets to \(z^{r-1}(z-1)\).
There are \(r-1\) roots tending to zero and one real root \(1+G(2)/C+O_P(C^{-2})\), by expansion at the simple zero one.
The latter is strictly inside because \(C G(2)<0\).
Under \(z=|C|^{1/r}u\), division by \(|C|^2\) gives the limiting polynomial \(u^r(u^r-\operatorname{sgn}(C))\), giving all \(r\) exterior roots and their distinct scaled limits.
The bounded perturbation \((z-1)z^{m-1}t E_C\) has degree at most \(2m-r-1\), so the same scaled exterior roots persist for \(f_t\), upon normalizing the full scaled polynomial by \(|C|^{2m/r}\).

At a circle lift \(\zeta\) of a zero \(x_j\) of \(H\), write \(z(t)\) for the simple root with \(z(0)=\zeta\).
Differentiating \(H(x)(G(x)-C(1-z^{-1}))=t(1-z^{-1})E_C(x)\) gives
\[
 \frac{z'(0)}{\zeta}
     =\frac{E_C(x_j)}
      {H'(x_j)(\zeta+1)\big(G(x_j)-C(1-\zeta^{-1})\big)} .
\]
The real part of \((\zeta+1)(G(x_j)-C(1-\zeta^{-1}))\) is \((1+\Re\zeta)G(x_j)\).
Hence \(\Re(z'(0)/\zeta)\) has the sign of \(E_C(x_j)/Q'(x_j)\), and is strictly negative.
All these roots move into the disk.
If \(f_t\) has a circle root at \(t>0\), its real coefficients and the return identity force that point to be \(-1\) or a circle zero of \(P\).
At a circle zero of \(P\) the correction is nonzero since \(p_t(x_j)=tE_C(x_j)\ne0\) and \(\zeta\ne1\).
At \(-1\) the bracket \(Q(-2)-2(C H(-2)+t E_C(-2))\) is nonzero uniformly in \(t\) for large \(|C|\), since \(H(-2)\ne0\).
Thus no root crosses back, proving the claimed count at \(t=1\).

This count is the smallest possible for a circle-root-free inverse.
Indeed on the circle \(|f^*|=|zf|\), so for \(0<t<1\) the polynomial \(zf+t f^*\) has as many exterior roots as \(f\), by Rouché and equality of degrees.
Every exterior root of its limit \((z+1)P\) persists with multiplicity in the approximants.
The constructed inverse attains this lower bound even if exterior roots occur at both ends of the real axis or in several nonreal pairs.

<a id="recover"></a>
## Recovering all exterior traces from the large roots

Write \(L_C\) for the monic polynomial of the \(r\) exterior roots of \(f_C\), and \(I_C=f_C/L_C\).
Then coefficientwise
\[
 L_C(z)+C\longrightarrow
      \operatorname{quo}\big(z^rG(z+z^{-1}),\ z^{r-1}(z-1)\big),
\]
where \(\operatorname{quo}\) is the polynomial quotient in Euclidean division.
Indeed \(f_C/(-C)\) converges on bounded sets to the monic polynomial \(D_0=z^hH(x)z^{r-1}(z-1)\) of degree \(2m-r\).
Root continuity on disjoint disks around its distinct roots, counting multiplicities, gives \(I_C\to D_0\).
In the identity \(f_C=P-C D_0-(z-1)z^{m-1}E_C(x)\), the last term has degree below \(\deg I_C\), and \(I_C-D_0\) does too.
Thus \(L_C+C=\operatorname{quo}(P,I_C)\) exactly.
Division by a monic polynomial is continuous in its coefficients at fixed degree, and the common monic factor \(z^h H(x)\) cancels in computing \(\operatorname{quo}(P,D_0)\).
The displayed limiting polynomial determines \(G\) uniquely.
Indeed for any nonzero difference \(J\) of two monic degree-\(r\) candidates, \(z^rJ(z+z^{-1})\) has degree \(r+\deg J\ge r\) and cannot have zero quotient on division by \(z^{r-1}(z-1)\).

<a id="two-real"></a>
## Two real exterior traces at the same end

Suppose \(r=2\) and both exterior traces \(T_1,T_2\) are greater than two, or both are less than minus two.
Then \(G(2)>0\), so \(C=-N\) and the two exterior roots \(\eta_N,\bar\eta_N\) of the unit seed are nonreal by their scaled limits \(\pm i\).
Every nonconstant monic integral factor needs an exterior root, since its nonzero integer constant excludes a product of roots of modulus less than one.
Real coefficients place both simple exterior roots in the same factor, proving irreducibility.
For \(S=T_1+T_2,\ U=T_1T_2\), the limiting quotient is \(z^2+(1-S)z+U-S+3\).
Thus \(2\Re\eta_N\to S-1\) and \(|\eta_N|^2-N\to U-S+3\).
For a sequence of such targets with Mahler measures tending to one, both real exterior traces tend to two at the positive end and to minus two at the negative end.
Choosing \(N\) separately for each target to make the errors tend to zero gives respective limits \((3,3)\) and \((-5,11)\) for the pair \((2\Re\eta_N,|\eta_N|^2-N)\).

<a id="pair"></a>
## One nonreal exterior conjugate pair

Suppose precisely two traces of \(P\) lie off \([-2,2]\), a nonreal conjugate pair \(w,\bar w\).
Here \(r=2\), \(G=(x-w)(x-\bar w)\), and \(C=-N\) with \(N\) any sufficiently large positive integer.
The two exterior roots \(\eta_N,\bar\eta_N\) of \(f_{-N}\) are nonreal by their scaled limits \(\pm i\), and all its other roots are strictly inside.
The same factor argument proves that \(f_{-N}\) is irreducible.
Thus the full return uses the minimal polynomial of an algebraic unit of the same degree as \(P\).

Euclidean division for \(r=2\) gives the limiting quotient
\[
          z^2+(1-2\Re w)z+|w|^2-2\Re w+3 .
\]
Consequently \(2\Re\eta_N\to2\Re w-1\) and \(|\eta_N|^2-N\to|w-1|^2+2\).
For example \(Q=x^2+1\) gives \(P=z^4+3z^2+1\) and \(f_{-N}=z^4+(N+3)z^2-Nz+1\).
Here \(P\) is irreducible since a root \(i(1+\sqrt5)/2\) generates \(\sqrt5\) by squaring and then generates \(i\).

Along any sequence of such targets with \(M(P)\to1\), choose the integer \(N\to\infty\) separately for each target to make the two errors tend to zero.
In fact if \(\lambda\) is the lift of \(w\) outside the disk then \(M(P)=|\lambda|^2\), so \(w=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\) approaches the real interval \([-2,2]\).
The resulting minimal polynomials of units therefore have \(\operatorname{dist}(2\Re\eta_N,[-5,3])\to0\) and
\[
       4\big(|\eta_N|^2-N-2\big)-(2\Re\eta_N-1)^2\longrightarrow0 .
\]
This necessary condition uses the same full reciprocal return for each target.
The construction supplies no uniform threshold in the target degree and the two scalar limits by themselves do not classify arbitrary unit seeds.
For general \(r\) the exterior-root count and trace recovery hold for the full monic seed, whose irreducibility is not needed in those assertions.
