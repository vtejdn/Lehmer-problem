# Inert primes of Salem units with a prescribed part of the residue order

<a id="supply"></a>
## Avoiding any finite set of primes

Let \(\alpha>1\) be any Salem number, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
Fix an integer \(m\ge3\), a finite set \(S\) of rational primes, and a real \(H\ge2\).
There is a prime \(\ell\notin S\) and a prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of \(F\) above it, inert in \(K/F\), such that
\[
 N\mathfrak q\equiv-1\bmod m,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak q\mathcal O_K}(\alpha)=mt .
\]
Here \(t>1\) is prime to \(m\) and every prime factor of \(t\) exceeds \(H\).
The construction allows any prescribed finite set of rational primes to be avoided in \(t\) as well.
For \(m=2^k,\ k\ge2\), the rational prime itself satisfies \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod 2^k\), and the absolute residue degree of \(\mathfrak q\) is odd.
Thus every Salem number has infinitely many distinct such inert rational characteristics for every prescribed exact two-part of the order from four onward.
This assertion allows the odd part of the residue order to grow.
The [dyadic Salem family](Yin_L_G0462_v82.md#dyadic) can have no odd prime of the bare order \(2^k\) throughout a growing list of \(k\).

<a id="avoidance"></a>
## An integral quotient with a prescribed sign at finite places

Put \(A(z)=z^{-\varphi(m)/2}\Phi_m(z)\), an inversion-invariant integral Laurent polynomial since \(m\ge3\).
The real algebraic integer \(c=A(\alpha)\) is nonzero.
Enlarge \(S\) to include all primes dividing \(2m|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(c)|\) and put \(b=m\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell\).
Choose a positive integer \(L\) divisible by \(m\), by every prime at most \(H\), by all additional primes to be excluded from \(t\), and by the order of \(\alpha\) in \((\mathcal O_K/bc\mathcal O_K)^\times\).
This order is finite since \(\alpha\) is an algebraic unit.
For positive \(r\equiv1\bmod L\) the quotient
\[
                     E_r=A(\alpha^r)/c
\]
belongs to \(1+b\mathcal O_F\).
Indeed the congruence \(\alpha^r\equiv\alpha\bmod bc\mathcal O_K\) also holds for the inverses, and division by \(c\) gives \(E_r-1\in b\mathcal O_K\).
Invariance under inversion and \(\mathcal O_K\cap F=\mathcal O_F\) give the claim.
Thus the signed norm of \(E_r\) is \(1\bmod b\).

Suppose this signed norm is negative.
The positive absolute norm is then \(-1\bmod m\), and is coprime to every prime in the enlarged \(S\).
If \(\mathfrak q\mid E_r\) then \(c\) is a unit there and the reduction of \(\alpha^r\) at any prime above \(\mathfrak q\) has exact order \(m\), by separability of the \(m\)-th roots away from the characteristic.
Write \(q=N\mathfrak q\).
The roots \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) of the relative quadratic reduce to distinct elements, since their \(r\)-th powers already have order \(m>2\).
The quadratic therefore splits over the local field or generates its unramified quadratic extension according as Frobenius fixes or interchanges the two roots.
In the first case \(q\equiv1\bmod m\), and in the second \(q\equiv-1\bmod m\), by applying Frobenius to \(\alpha^r\).
Since the product of all \(q^{v_{\mathfrak q}(E_r)}\) is \(-1\bmod m\), at least one inert prime occurs with odd valuation.
The order \(j\) of \(\bar\alpha\) there satisfies \(j/\gcd(j,r)=m\), hence \(j=mt\) with \(t\mid r\) and \(\gcd(t,m)=1\).
If \(t=1\) the prime divides \(c\), contrary to its exclusion.
The congruences on \(r\) give all the claimed prime exclusions for \(t\).
When \(m=2^k\) with \(k\ge2\), \(q=\ell^f\equiv-1\bmod4\) forces \(f\) odd.
An odd power map is an automorphism of \((\mathbb Z/2^k\mathbb Z)^\times\) and fixes \(-1\), giving \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod 2^k\).
In fact some such rational prime has odd valuation in \(|N(E_r)|\), since modulo \(2^k\) every prime-ideal norm is a sign, and the negative contributions have odd residue degree at rational primes of class \(-1\).

## The negative norm occurs with positive density among the multipliers

Choose one root \(u_i=e^{2\pi i\theta_i}\) from each of the \(s=[F:\mathbb Q]-1\ge1\) unit-circle conjugate pairs of the Salem number.
No nonzero integer vector \((a_i)\) has \(\prod_i u_i^{a_i}\) a root of unity.
Indeed for each index \(j\) an automorphism of the normal closure sends \(u_j\) to \(\alpha\), and then only its inverse goes to \(\alpha^{-1}\).
Every other \(u_i\) in the product maps to a root of modulus one, so taking absolute values gives \(\alpha^{a_j}=1\).
Thus along \(r=1+Ln\) the vectors \((r\theta_i)_i\) are uniformly distributed modulo one.
For each nonconstant Fourier character this follows by summing a geometric progression with ratio different from one, and trigonometric approximation gives the assertion on sets with boundary of measure zero.

The exterior embedding contributes \(A(\alpha^r)>0\), since \(\Phi_m\) is monic with all roots on the unit circle in conjugate pairs.
Each circle embedding contributes the continuous real function \(A(e^{2\pi i r\theta_i})\).
Its zeros on the circle are simple because the primitive roots are simple, so each of its two signs occurs on an open arc.
The product over the \(s\) independent coordinates takes each sign on a set of positive Haar measure with boundary of measure zero.
Consequently \(N_{F/\mathbb Q}(E_r)\) is negative for a positive relative density of the chosen progression, irrespective of the fixed sign of \(N(c)\).
For \(m=2^k\) the function is \(2\cos(2\pi (m/4)x)\) on the parameter \(x\), and the relative density is exactly one half.
Enlarging \(S\) after any finite list of discoveries gives infinitely many distinct rational primes.
Neither the size of the progression modulus \(L\) nor the least multiplier obtained here is bounded uniformly in the Salem degree.
The argument yields no exponential lower bound on the product of inert primes in the degree window of the [unrestricted gap criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#statement).
