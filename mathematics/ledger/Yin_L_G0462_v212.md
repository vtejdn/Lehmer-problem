# All torsion points of the quartic graph and its cyclotomic sections

<a id="orders"></a>
## The cyclotomic part for every positive direction

Put \(g(U)=U^4-U-1\), \(B(U)=1-U^3-U^4\), \(f=-g/B\), and \(F_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) for positive integers \(a,b\).
The complete list of pairs of roots of unity on \(g(U)+YB(U)=0\) is given by the following table, with **every** primitive \(U\) of the indicated order included.

| Order of \(U\) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 7 | 10 | 18 | 24 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(Y=f(U)\) | \(-1\) | \(-1\) | \(-U^2\) | \(1\) | \(-U^4\) | \(U\) | \(U^3\) | \(U^5\) |

The eight orbits contain 30 ordered torsion points in total.
For \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), the cyclotomic part of \(F_{a,b}\) is the product of precisely the following factors whose conditions hold, each once.

| Factor | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| \(\Phi_2\) | \(b\) odd |
| \(\Phi_4\) | \(a\) odd and \(4\mid b\) |
| \(\Phi_6\) | \(\gcd(a,6)=2\) and \(b\equiv 2a+3\pmod6\) |
| \(\Phi_{10}\) | \(\gcd(a,10)=1\) and \(b\equiv a\pmod{10}\) |
| \(\Phi_{14}\) | \(\gcd(a,14)=2\) and \(b\equiv4a+7\pmod{14}\) |
| \(\Phi_{18}\) | \(\gcd(a,18)=1\) and \(b\equiv3a\pmod{18}\) |
| \(\Phi_{24}\) | \(\gcd(a,24)=1\) and \(b\equiv5a\pmod{24}\) |

Indeed for a primitive \(M\)-th root \(\xi\) the two powers \(U=\xi^a,\ Y=\xi^b\) generate the cyclic group of order \(M\).
Thus \(M=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}U,\operatorname{ord}Y)\) and \(\operatorname{ord}U=M/\gcd(M,a)\).
Applying these two identities and \(Y=f(U)\) in each column gives the displayed necessary and sufficient congruences.
For \(c=\gcd(a,b)>1\), use the table for \((a/c,b/c)\) and replace each \(\Phi_M\) there by the product of all \(\Phi_r\) with \(r/\gcd(r,c)=M\).
This follows from \(F_{a,b}(X)=F_{a/c,b/c}(X^c)\) and the order of \(\xi^c\), including simplicity since \(X\mapsto X^c\) is unramified at nonzero points.

The cyclotomic degree in a primitive direction is at most 13, attained at \((a,b)=(1,101)\).
For \(a\) even only orders \(2,6,14\) are possible, of total degree at most 9.
For \(a\) odd and \(b\) even only order 4 is possible.
With \(a,b\) both odd the possibilities are \(2,10,18,24\), and the last two require respectively \(3\mid b\) and \(3\nmid b\).

## Sign and square transforms exclude all other torsion points

The polynomials \(g,B\) have no common zero, since their sum is \(-U-U^3\) and none of \(0,\pm i\) is a zero of \(g\).
Also \(B(U)=U^4g(1/U)\), so both are nonzero on the unit circle.
Apply the [sign and square argument used for the cubic graph](Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#cyclotomic) to a generator \(\zeta\) of the group generated by \(U,Y\), of order \(M\).
If \(M\) is odd, \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2\) gives another graph point.
If \(M\) is twice an odd number, \(\zeta\mapsto-\zeta^2\) is an automorphism since \(2+M/2\) is prime to \(M\).
In both cases \((eU^2,\delta Y^2)\) is a graph point for some \(e,\delta\in\{\pm1\}\).
If \(4\mid M\), the automorphism \(\zeta\mapsto-\zeta\) changes the sign of at least one coordinate since the two coordinates generate the group.
It must change the sign of \(U\), because fixing \(U\) fixes \(Y=f(U)\).
Thus in this case \(f(-U)=\delta f(U)\) for some sign.

Define \(H_{e,\delta}(X)=-g(eX^2)B(X)^2-\delta g(X)^2B(eX^2)\) and \(S_\delta(X)=g(-X)B(X)-\delta g(X)B(-X)\).
The preceding argument makes \(U\) a zero of at least one of these six polynomials.
Write \(z=X+X^{-1}\) and
\[
 R(z)=z^7-6z^5+9z^3-4z-1,\qquad
 T(z)=z^7+z^6-6z^5-6z^4+7z^3+8z^2+4z+1 .
\]
Multiplication gives the following identities, with each \(\Phi_j\) evaluated at \(X\).
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 (e,\delta)& H_{e,\delta}/(-2)\\ \hline
 (+,+)&X^8R(z)\\
 (+,-)&\Phi_1^3\Phi_2\Phi_4\Phi_3^2\Phi_7\\
 (-,+)&X^8T(z)\\
 (-,-)&\Phi_1\Phi_2^3\Phi_4\Phi_{18}\Phi_{10}
\end{array}
\qquad
 S_+/(-2)=X\Phi_1\Phi_2\Phi_4^2,\qquad S_-/(-2)=\Phi_{24}.
\]
For an exact check on the two residual factors, \(R\) reduces modulo 2 to \(z^7+z^3+1\), and \(T\) modulo 3 to \(z^7+z^6+z^3-z^2+z+1\).
Each reduction takes the value 1 at every element of its prime field.
Modulo the respective reduction, successive \(p\)-th powering from \(z\) gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
   p & z^{p^6} & z^{p^7}\\ \hline
   2 & z^4+z^2 & z\\
   3 & z^6-z^4-z & z
 \end{array}
\]
Hence each divides \(z^{p^7}-z\), whose irreducible factors have degrees dividing 7.
The absence of a linear factor proves irreducibility in degree seven, and therefore over \(\mathbb Q\) for both monic polynomials.
Since \(R(2)=-1\), \(R\) has a real root above two.
Since \(T(-2)=1\), \(T\) has a real root below minus two.
All conjugates of a root-of-unity trace are in \([-2,2]\), so neither irreducible factor can vanish at such a trace.
Only the eight listed orders remain.
For each order \(j\), substituting its tabulated monomial \(Y\) in \(g(X)+YB(X)\) gives zero modulo \(\Phi_j(X)\), verifying existence and the unique second coordinate in every column.

## Simplicity and the section with first exponent three

At a repeated torsion root \(\xi\), logarithmic differentiation of the section equation at \(U=\xi^a\) would give \(U f'(U)/f(U)=b/a\).
If \(U\) has order \(j\ge3\), the same rational equality holds at its conjugate \(\exp(2\pi i/j)\).
The corresponding trace \(t=2\cos(2\pi/j)\) is at least \(-1\).
The [quartic phase derivative](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#phase) is \(-(t^3+2)/|g(U)|^2\), strictly negative there, contradicting \(b/a>0\).
It is negative at \(U=1\) as well.
At \(U=-1\) it equals 6, while \(b=6a\) forces \(Y=U^6=1\) and the graph requires \(Y=-1\).
This proves simplicity at every positive direction.

In particular, for \(a=3\) and every \(b\ge1\) prime to 3, only \(\Phi_2,\Phi_4,\Phi_{10}\) can occur, respectively for odd \(b\), \(4\mid b\), and \(b\equiv3\bmod10\).
Thus the three [J175 census congruences](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0175/readme.md#census) hold for every such \(b\).
The nontorsion part has exact degree
\[
 b+12-\mathbf1_{2\nmid b}-2\mathbf1_{4\mid b}
                    -4\mathbf1_{b\equiv3\bmod10}\ \ge\ b+7 .
\]
Its possible splitting beyond the census remains the [Salem-factor question](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#quartic).
