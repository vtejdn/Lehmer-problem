# The number of terms in two independent rows at large exponents for a fixed seed

2026-11-24T18:57 UTC.

<a id="support"></a>
## Independent coefficient columns for all sufficiently large exponents

Fix a nonzero algebraic number \(\alpha\), a nonnegative integer \(B\), and an integer \(r\ge1\).
Write \(P\) for its rational minimal polynomial, \(d=\deg P\), and \(e=\min_{u\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\alpha^u):\mathbb Q]\).
For integer \(n\ge2\) consider the \(2(B+1)\) rational coefficient columns of
\[
      (A,C)\ \longmapsto\ A(X^n)+XC(X^n)\pmod {P(X)^r},
      \qquad \deg A,\deg C\le B .
\]
The positions in the two rows can be chosen independently.
For all sufficiently large \(n\), every subset of at most \(re\) columns is independent.
Consequently every nonzero graph divisible by \(P^r\) has at least \(re+1\) terms.
The threshold for this assertion may depend on \(\alpha,B,r\).

If every positive power of \(\alpha\) generates \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), then \(e=d\).
In this case, whenever \(2(B+1)>rd\), each prescribed subset of \(rd+1\) columns supports exactly one rational relation up to scaling for every sufficiently large \(n\).
It uses all those columns and can be made integral by scaling.
Indeed the target \(\mathbb Q[X]/(P^r)\) has dimension \(rd\), and every \(rd\) columns of the subset are independent.
These relations assert divisibility alone, without a coefficient-height, row-coprimality or reflected-determinant conclusion.

The [signed-row independence theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v446.md#statement) treats a single coefficient shared by two reflected terms at double contact, including moving widths.
Here each monomial supplies its own coefficient and arbitrary contact orders are allowed at a fixed width.
The proof uses the [maximal-base method for signed rows](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v446.md#determinant) and the [number-field recurrence theorem](../audits/literature/Lec53/Yin_U001.md#torsion-classes).

## Confluent determinants when a repeated level straddles two nodes

Among the conjugates of \(\alpha\) there are \(e\) classes modulo multiplication by roots of unity.
Indeed powering by a common multiple of all orders of torsion quotients among them identifies exactly the conjugates in each such class, while every power keeps different classes distinct.
Choose representatives from these classes.
Modulo torsion the subgroup generated by all conjugates is a finite-rank free abelian group.
To obtain a homomorphism to \(\mathbb Z\) taking different values on the representatives, choose an integer vector in any lattice basis outside the finitely many hyperplanes perpendicular to their differences.

Choose \(k\le re\) distinct columns labelled \((j,\epsilon)\), where \(0\le j\le B\), \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\), and \(k\ge1\).
Use \(\ell=\lceil k/r\rceil\) representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_\ell\), ordered by increasing homomorphism value.
Give the first \(\ell-1\) nodes \(r\) rows each and the last \(k-r(\ell-1)\) rows.
At a node \(x_h\) of order \(t_h\), use the Euler derivatives of orders \(0,\ldots,t_h-1\).
Their entries in column \((j,\epsilon)\) are
\[
                   (nj+\epsilon)^a x_h^\epsilon (x_h^j)^n,\qquad 0\le a<t_h .
\]
Nonvanishing of this square minor implies independence, since a rational relation modulo \(P^r\) would annihilate all the selected rows.

Its determinant is an exponential polynomial in \(n\), whose bases are obtained by assigning the column levels \(j\) to the node slots and multiplying the assigned powers \(x_h^j\).
The largest homomorphism value of a base requires the sorted levels to occur in increasing order of the nodes, by the strict rearrangement inequality whenever both comparisons are strict.
Thus at node \(h\) the multiset of levels is the corresponding consecutive group of \(t_h\) terms of the sorted list.
Every maximizing allocation gives exactly the same base, since permutations inside a node and exchanges of equal levels do not change the product.
Every other base has smaller homomorphism value and therefore its ratio to the maximizing base cannot be torsion.

We compute the leading coefficient of the polynomial multiplying this maximizing base.
Sort the columns initially by \((j,\epsilon)\).
Each level occurs at most twice, and its two columns then have offsets 0 and 1.
Call a boundary split if a repeated level has one slot in each of groups \(h,h+1\).
For each such boundary there are two allocations of its offsets, independent of all other splits.
Once the allocations are fixed, summing over permutations inside each node gives an ordinary Vandermonde determinant in \(nj+\epsilon\), times the product of its factors \(x_h^\epsilon\).
Within a node each pair of unequal levels \(j<j'\) contributes \(n(j'-j)+\epsilon'-\epsilon\).
Each pair with equal levels is wholly inside that node and contributes \(1\) in sorted order.
This holds also for a single slot, whose Vandermonde product is empty.

The degree in \(n\) is the total number of within-node pairs with unequal levels and is independent of all boundary exchanges.
The corresponding Vandermonde leading constant is the same positive integer \(L=\prod(j'-j)\) over these pairs in every allocation.
All columns away from split boundaries contribute a common nonzero factor \(U\) from the offsets, a product of powers of the \(x_h\).
Swapping the offsets at a split boundary changes the permutation sign and replaces the factor \(x_{h+1}\) by \(x_h\).
Hence, up to a common sign, the sum of the leading coefficients is
\[
                     L U\prod_{\text{split boundaries }h}(x_{h+1}-x_h)\ne0.
\]
This includes an incompletely used last node, even if it contributes just one slot.
The polynomial coefficient of the maximizing base is thus nonzero.

On any arithmetic progression, other bases can coincide or cancel, but none can coincide with the new power of the maximizing base.
Its polynomial coefficient remains nonzero after linear substitution for \(n\).
An identically zero sequence \(\sum_z Q_z(t)z^t\) with distinct nonzero bases has all \(Q_z=0\), as follows by applying the shift differences annihilating all other summands.
The zero set of our minor contains no infinite arithmetic progression.
The [Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem](../audits/literature/Lec53/Yin_U001.md#torsion-classes) then implies it is finite.
There are only finitely many column subsets for fixed \(B\), proving the stated eventual independence.

## Fixed-seed and varying-degree quantifiers

Suppose a fixed nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) admits nonzero graphs of width at most \(B\) divisible by \(P^r\) with at most \(s\) terms at infinitely many increasing integers \(n\).
Then \(re+1\le s\).
Choose a positive integer \(u\) realizing \(e\).
The relative norm \(v=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q(\alpha^u)}(\alpha)\) is a nontorsion unit, since \(v^u=(\alpha^u)^{d/e}\).
The absolute-height identity gives \(m(\alpha)=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^u):\mathbb Q(v)]m(v)\).
Thus the [power-field norm and house bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0903_v2.md#interpolation) give
\[
                         m(\alpha)\ge\frac{\log2}{4e}
                                     \ge\frac{r\log2}{4(s-1)}.
\]
In particular, for any sequence of nontorsion unit seeds with measures tending to zero and finite widths \(B_j\), the least support allowed for their respective far tails tends to infinity.
At each seed the assertion applies to every sufficiently large exponent, including every sufficiently large prime, regardless of whether any admissible graphs exist there.
For double contacts on seeds with \(e=d\), the lower bound \(2d+1\) occupies a fraction tending to one of all \(2(B+1)\) slots when \(B=d+o(d)\) and \(d\to\infty\).

The [prime-schedule construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v3.md#statement) uses a common repeated factor at each selected degree throughout its prime tail.
The present conclusion applies at that factor's own eventual threshold.
It does not bound the exceptional exponents uniformly as the degree or the seed changes, so does not exclude a fixed term count at a specified prime comparable to the degree.
