# Resultant bounds for integral multiples and the exact cyclotomic minimum

<a id="resultant"></a>
## A necessary lower bound on the coefficient budget

For monic nonconstant \(Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\) and an integer \(D\ge\deg Q\), let \(C_D(Q)\) be the minimum of \(\max_i\|H_i\|_1\) over nonempty finite families of nonzero integral multiples of \(Q\) with ordinary degrees at most \(D\) and monic gcd exactly \(Q\) in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
Here \(\|H\|_1\) is the sum of the absolute coefficients.
Let \(C_\infty(Q)\) be the corresponding minimum over all degrees.
Both minima are attained since the positive integral costs form nonempty sets containing the cost of the single polynomial \(Q\).
The cap \(D\) measures ordinary polynomial degree, including any initial power of \(X\).

For a monic irreducible \(S\in\mathbb Z[X]\) coprime to \(Q\), of positive degree \(e\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(S)\), one has
\[
 \log C_D(Q)\ \ge\
 \frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(Q,S)|-D\,m(S)}{e}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, in every eligible family some \(H=QU\) is coprime to \(S\), with \(U\in\mathbb Z[X]\) by monicity of \(Q\).
The nonzero integer \(\operatorname{Res}(U,S)\) has absolute value at least one.
For every root \(\gamma\) of \(S\) one has \(|H(\gamma)|\le\|H\|_1\max(1,|\gamma|)^D\).
Multiplication over the \(e\) conjugates and multiplicativity of resultants give
\[
 |\operatorname{Res}(Q,S)|\le|\operatorname{Res}(H,S)|
                    \le \|H\|_1^e\exp(Dm(S)).
\]
In particular a cyclotomic test polynomial \(S\) also gives (1) with \(C_\infty\) and zero degree cost.

For a monic irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial \(P\) put \(A_n(P)=\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_n)|/\varphi(n)\) for \(n\ge1\).
Suppose powering a root \(\alpha\) by a prime \(p\) preserves its degree, and put \(Q=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\alpha^p)\).
Then, simultaneously for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
 \log C_\infty(Q)\ \ge\
 \begin{cases}
 A_n(P)+(p-1)A_{pn}(P),&p\nmid n,\\
 p A_{pn}(P),&p\mid n .
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{2}
\]
All powered conjugates occur once, so \(|\operatorname{Res}(Q,\Phi_n)|=|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_n(X^p))|\).
If \(p\nmid n\), the roots of \(\Phi_n(X^p)\) have orders \(n,pn\), and both primitive orders occur in full.
If \(p\mid n\), they have order \(pn\) alone.
The polynomials are monic with simple roots, giving (2) by (1) and the respective identities \(\varphi(pn)=(p-1)\varphi(n)\) and \(\varphi(pn)=p\varphi(n)\).
Hence the supremum of the right side over \(n\) must be \(o(p)\) whenever the [small-family sufficient condition for the two rows](Yin_L_G0462_v227.md#rows) holds with coefficient budget \(B=o(p)\).
This is a necessary condition for that particular construction on actual nontorsion inputs.

<a id="cyclotomic"></a>
## The exact optimal norm at every conductor

For every \(N\ge2\), let \(q\) be the largest prime divisor of \(N\).
Then
\[
                           C_\infty(\Phi_N)=q.                       \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the [cyclotomic resultant formula](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#cyclotomic-clearing) gives
\(|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_N,\Phi_{N/q})|=q^{\varphi(N/q)}\), yielding the lower bound by (1).
For each prime \(j\mid N\) take
\[
 T_j(X)=\frac{X^N-1}{X^{N/j}-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}X^{kN/j}.
\]
Its coefficient norm is \(j\).
A cyclotomic factor \(\Phi_t\) of \(X^N-1\) occurs in \(T_j\) exactly when \(v_j(t)=v_j(N)\).
These conditions for all primes \(j\mid N\) leave exactly \(t=N\), and \(X^N-1\) is squarefree.
Thus the family has the required gcd for arbitrary prime-power exponents in \(N\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## The least degree cap attaining the optimal norm

Let \(D_0(N)\) be the least \(D\) with \(C_D(\Phi_N)=q\).
Then
\[
 D_0(N)=
 \begin{cases}
 (q-1)N/q,&N\text{ odd},\\
 (q-1)N/(2q),&N\text{ even with an odd prime divisor},\\
 N/2,&N\text{ a power of two}.
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{4}
\]
To prove necessity in the first two cases put \(m=N/q\), and choose from any optimal family an \(H\) coprime to \(\Phi_m\).
Every value \(|H(\zeta)|\) at a primitive \(m\)-th root is at most \(q\), and the product is at least \(q^{\varphi(m)}\) by the same resultant.
Thus \(\|H\|_1=q\) and \(|H(\zeta)|=q\) for each such \(\zeta\).
Equality in the triangle inequality forces all the nonzero terms \(h_k\zeta^k\) to have the same argument.
The coefficients are real, so \(\zeta^{k-l}\in\{1,-1\}\) for every two occupied indices, giving \(m\mid2(k-l)\).
Writing \(w=m/\gcd(m,2)\) and \(c\) for the smallest occupied exponent, we obtain \(H=X^c A(X^w)\) with \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\) nonzero.
At a primitive \(N\)-th root the argument of \(A\) is a primitive \(q\)-th root for odd \(N\), or a primitive \(2q\)-th root for even \(N\) with \(q\) odd.
The vanishing of \(H\) gives \(\deg A\ge q-1\) in both cases, so \(\deg H\ge w(q-1)\).
For \(N\) a power of two every multiple already has degree at least \(\varphi(N)=N/2\), and \(\Phi_N=X^{N/2}+1\) itself attains (3).

For odd \(N\), the family \(T_j\) attains the stated degree cap since \(\deg T_j=N-N/j\) increases with \(j\).
For even \(N\) with an odd prime divisor let \(M\) be the product of its distinct odd prime divisors and put \(k=N/(2M)\).
Then \(\Phi_M(-X^k)=\Phi_N(X)\).
Indeed \(-x^k\) has primitive odd order \(M\) exactly when \(x^k\) has order \(2M\).
Every prime divisor of \(k\) divides \(2M\), so the latter condition forces \(x\) to have order \(2Mk=N\), by taking prime valuations in the formula for the order of a power.
All the roots are simple and the polynomial is monic since \(\varphi(M)\) is even.
Apply \(X\mapsto-X^k\) to the odd-conductor family at \(M\).
Substitution preserves its gcd after composition, since a Bézout identity for the quotients by \(\Phi_M\) stays an identity over \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
The coefficient norms are unchanged and the largest degree is \(k(M-M/q)=(q-1)N/(2q)\).
This proves (4).

At the [large-fluctuation conductors](Yin_L_G0462_v225.md#window), all prime divisors are odd, \(N/\varphi(N)\to1\), and (4) gives \(D_0(N)<2\varphi(N)\) eventually.
Thus the exact logarithmic coefficient budget in degrees at most twice the orbit degree is \(\log q\), even at every prime of the fourth-logarithmic window, since these primes preserve the same cyclotomic orbit. [Powered controls](Yin_L_G0462_v227.md#controls)
Other odd conductors can require greater degree to attain the optimum.
For example \(N=1155=3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\) gives \(D_0(N)=1050>2\varphi(N)=960\), hence \(C_{960}(\Phi_{1155})>11\).
These exact costs are for cyclotomic controls, while (1) and (2) also apply to the irreducible positive-measure inputs.
