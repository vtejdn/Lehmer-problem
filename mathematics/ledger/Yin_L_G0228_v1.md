### 1. Original collision mass in an explicit typical lattice box

Let alpha be a nonzero algebraic integer with actual monic irreducible polynomial P of degree d.
Algebraic units, both torsion and nontorsion, are included.
Put m=log M(P)>=0.
Let tau:Q(alpha)->R^d be the real Minkowski embedding: each real embedding supplies one coordinate, and each nonreal conjugate pair supplies the real and imaginary parts of one representative.
Every coordinate i is assigned the modulus rho_i of that conjugate; a complex pair has two coordinates with the same modulus.
Write q for the number of coordinates with rho_i=1.

Define the real invertible matrix B by its columns tau(1),tau(alpha)...,tau(alpha^(d-1)), and set

    Delta=|det B|>0,
    a_i=(1/2) sum_(j=0)^(d-1) |B_(i,j)|.             (1)

Invertibility is the usual separability/Vandermonde fact, after the invertible conversion of conjugate complex coordinates to real and imaginary parts.
Thus Lambda=tau(Z[alpha])=B Z^d is a full lattice.
The half-open parallelepiped B[-1/2,1/2)^d has volume Delta and lies in the coordinate box with half-widths a_i.
Its lattice translates are disjoint up to measure-zero boundaries.

Let

    W_n=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) xi_j alpha^j,
    p_n=Pr{W_n=W_n'},                                  (2)

where all xi_j and their primed copies are independent fair bits.
No digit law is changed.
Center tau(W_n) at its actual mean mu_n; that mean need not be a lattice point.

For rho_i!=1, the corresponding centered coordinate is bounded deterministically by

    b_i(n)=rho_i^n/[2(rho_i-1)]    if rho_i>1,
    b_i(n)=1/[2(1-rho_i)]         if rho_i<1.            (3)

Indeed every centered digit has absolute value1/2, and each real or imaginary coordinate of tau(alpha^j) is bounded by rho_i^j.
Sum the geometric series.
For a neutral coordinate, independence and centering give variance at most n/4.
If q>=1, Chebyshev at radius sqrt(qn), followed by a union bound over the q coordinates, shows that all neutral coordinates simultaneously have magnitude at most sqrt(qn) with probability at least3/4.
If q=0 no random restriction is needed and the probability is one.
Correlations between different embedding coordinates are allowed throughout.

Let C_alpha be the explicit positive constant

    C_alpha=Delta^(-1)
      product_(rho_i>1) [1/(rho_i-1)+2a_i]
      product_(rho_i<1) [1/(1-rho_i)+2a_i]
      product_(rho_i=1) [2(sqrt(q)+a_i)].              (4)

The final product is empty when q=0.
For n>=1 the box just constructed contains at most

    C_alpha exp(nm) n^(q/2)                            (5)

lattice points.
To check this with an arbitrary center mu_n, translate the fundamental parallelepiped by every lattice point in the box.
Their disjoint volumes add, and their union lies in the box enlarged by a_i in coordinate i.
For expanding coordinates,

    2[b_i(n)+a_i]
       <=[1/(rho_i-1)+2a_i] rho_i^n;

for contracting coordinates the corresponding factor is exactly bounded by the factor in(4).
The neutral factor is at most2(sqrt(q)+a_i)sqrt(n).
The product of expanding moduli over the real coordinates is M(P)=exp(m), counting both dimensions of each complex block.
Division by Delta proves(5).
No projected coordinate is treated as an integer, and the actual order Z[alpha] is not replaced by a larger ring without its covolume.

Cauchy--Schwarz on the at-least3/4 probability mass in that finite set now proves, for every n>=1,

    p_n >= c_alpha exp(-nm) n^(-q/2),
    c_alpha=min{1,9/(16 C_alpha)}>0.                  (6)

The cap by one is harmless and convenient for n=0 comparisons.
This is a bound on the FULL original collision mass.
It improves the n^(-q) full-support packing baseline by using actual probabilities.
It is not an upper small-ball bound, a central limit theorem or a Gaussian statement after conditioning on expansion.
It requires no L2 density assumption and remains valid for arbitrary degree, with the explicitly input-dependent constant(4).

### 2. Full return series and the critical moment obstruction

Now restrict alpha to an algebraic unit so that the actual-core and canonical-law interfaces of L201 apply.
Let

    h=h_2=lim_n -log(p_n)/n,
    R=exp(h), R_M=exp(m), delta=m-h>=0,
    U(z)=sum_(n>=0) p_n z^n,
    F(z)=sum_(n>=1) f_n z^n.                           (7)

Here f_n is the original probability of FIRST return at time n, with every actual first-return word and its weight retained.
The identity U=1/(1-F) holds for0<=z<R, with nonnegative coefficients and monotone endpoint limits.
Bound(6) gives h<=m directly.
It also gives

    U(R) >= 1+c_alpha S_(q/2)(delta),
    S_a(delta)=sum_(n>=1) exp(-delta*n)/n^a,             (8)

where infinity is allowed.
When delta=0 and q<=2 the series on the right diverges.
Hence

    h=m and q<=2  imply  F(R)=1.                       (9)

This is a genuine recurrence statement for the complete critically weighted original return law.
It does not assert finite critical mean.
In particular, if a quartic Salem input has h=log beta, its critical return law is recurrent.
Neither equality of those rates nor its critical mean is asserted for that input here.

There is a further moment restriction for q>2.
Put

    k=ceil(q/2-1)>=1.

If h=m, (6) implies

    sum_(n>=1) n^k p_n R^n=infinity.                   (10)

Indeed k-q/2>=-1.
If also F(R)<1, then necessarily

    sum_(n>=1) n^k f_n R^n=infinity.                   (11)

Here is a complete coefficient proof, avoiding differentiation at a singular boundary.
Put b_n=f_n R^n and s=sum b_n<1.
If its k-th moment B_k=sum n^k b_n were finite, then for every integer ell>=1,

    sum_n n^k (b convolution ... convolution b)_n
       <= ell^k s^(ell-1) B_k.

For ell summands this follows from (n_1+...+n_ell)^k<=ell^(k-1)sum_i n_i^k.
Summing over ell is finite because s<1; the ell=0 term is at n=0 and contributes zero.
But coefficientwise renewal expresses p_n R^n as this full sum of convolutions, contradicting(10).
This proves(11), retaining the actual first-return weights rather than substituting a generic renewal model.

For3<=q<=4, k=1.
Thus a critically transient law with h=m must have R F'(R)=infinity.
For nontorsion irreducible inputs, real unit-circle conjugates would be plus or minus one and would force a degree-one cyclotomic polynomial.
All remaining unit-circle conjugates occur in nonreal pairs, so q is even.
The relevant new cases are therefore q=2 and4; the proof also permits the real torsion cases.

If q=0 and h=m, (6) gives U(tR)>=c_alpha/(1-t) for0<t<1, including n=0.
Then F(R)=1 and

    [1-F(tR)]/(1-t)<=1/c_alpha.

Positive-coefficient endpoint limits give R F'(R)<=1/c_alpha.
This implication is formally valid, but Section5 explains why h=m itself is unavailable for a positive-measure hyperbolic algebraic unit.
It is not an existence claim.

### 3. Consequence for the actual canonical information family

The actual admissible family Q_z of L201, for0<z<R, has information rate

    I(z)=log z - F(z)log F(z)/(z F'(z)),

and its critical limit is

    I_crit=h - F_R log F_R/(R F'(R)), F_R=F(R),         (12)

with a finite numerator divided by infinity interpreted as zero.
Its full original path likelihood and stationary occupation conventions are unchanged.
In particular it approaches the actual variational infimum h whenever F_R=1 or F'(R)=infinity.

Equations(9)--(11) therefore imply

    h=m and q<=4  imply  lim_(z up to R) I(z)=h.        (13)

For q<=2 use recurrence.
For2<q<=4 either F_R=1 already, or(11) forces the infinite derivative.
The q=0 formal case was addressed above.
This is convergence of the actual canonical full tilts; it neither supplies a stationary law at z=R nor assumes attainment.
If F_R<1 the infimum remains unattained, and if F_R=1 it is attained exactly when the critical mean is finite, as in L201.

For a Salem input of degree d, q=d-2.
Thus(13) applies, under h=log beta, to degrees4 and6.
The degree-ten Lehmer input has q=8: the corresponding conclusion under equality and F_R<1 is divergence of the THIRD first-return moment, not necessarily the first.
No claim of optimal canonical tilts at that input follows from this argument.

Without assuming h=m, (8) still controls the transient critical defect.
If S_(q/2)(delta) is finite, renewal gives

    1-F_R <= 1/[1+c_alpha S_(q/2)(delta)].              (14)

If that S is infinite the left side is zero.
For the information defect, the critical mean mu_R=R F'(R)/F_R is at least1 whenever finite.
Equations(12),(14) consequently give

    0<=I_crit-h
      <=log[1+1/(c_alpha S_(q/2)(delta))]
      <=1/(c_alpha S_(q/2)(delta)),                    (15)

with the zero interpretation for infinite S.
If the derivative is infinite the left side is already zero.
The first-return coefficient f_1=1/2 guarantees F_R>0 throughout, so no logarithm of zero has been introduced.

For q=2 and delta>0, S_1(delta)=-log(1-exp(-delta)); for q=0, S_0(delta)=1/(exp(delta)-1).
These follow by summing the geometric series and integrating it on a compact subinterval of(0,1), then taking the monotone limit.
In particular a small rate deficit gives an explicit bound on the possible canonical defect, but its constant c_alpha remains input dependent.
No uniform near-one conclusion follows by suppressing the lattice shape, degree or conjugate gaps in(4).

### 4. Actual cyclotomic controls and the sharp polynomial scale

The exponent q/2 in(6) cannot be improved over all actual algebraic units.
Use the fixed exact L206 control alpha=-1 and its full-degree radicals: let alpha_d be a root of t^d+1, where d is a power of two.
This is the actual irreducible cyclotomic polynomial Phi_(2d), with all d real Minkowski dimensions neutral, m=h=0 and q=d.

Write n=d ell+s,0<=s<d.
The integral basis by residue classes modulo d and the symmetric difference digit law give exactly

    p_n(alpha_d)=b_(ell+1)^s b_ell^(d-s),
    b_j=binom(2j,j)/4^j, b_0=1.                       (16)

This is the original collision probability, not a normalized auxiliary graph.
L206 proves the elementary all-j bounds 1/(2sqrt(j))<=b_j<=1/sqrt(j+1) for j>=1.
For each fixed d, (16) therefore gives constants c_d,C_d>0 with

    c_d n^(-d/2)<=p_n(alpha_d)<=C_d n^(-d/2)
    for every n>=1.                                  (17)

The finitely many n<d may be absorbed into the positive constants; no degree-uniform assertion is made.

For d=1 and2, U(1)=infinity and the critical law is recurrent.
For d=4, U(1)<infinity but U'(1)=infinity; renewal gives F(1)<1 and F'(1)=infinity.
For d>=8, both U(1) and U'(1) are finite, hence F(1)<1 and F'(1)<infinity.
These reproduce the actual L206/L201 change in canonical behavior at neutral dimension four.
They calibrate both(9) and the first-moment case of(11), and show why(13) cannot be extended to arbitrarily many neutral directions solely from the displayed probability scale.
They are torsion controls, not a near-one nontorsion family and not a counterexample to Lehmer positivity.
