# Recovering the odd quartic trace degree by reduction at one prime

<a id="maximum"></a>
## The largest possible least odd factor degree

Use the primitive quartic sections of [C400](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v400.md#statement) with odd \(a\), where \(b\) is even or \(b=6a+c^2\) with \(c\) odd and coprime to \(a\).
Take \(r=b/a\) sufficiently large and remove the full cyclotomic part as there.
Write the resulting monic trace polynomial as \(H=H_0H_1\cdots H_t\) over \(\mathbb Q\), with distinct monic irreducible factors, and put \(n_i=\deg H_i\).
Here \(n_0\) is the unique odd degree, all degrees are at least nine, and the lift of \(H_0\) contains the unique negative exterior root \(-\sigma\).
Call a rational prime \(p\) good if \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}H\), and let \(\ell(p)\) be the least odd degree of an irreducible factor of \(H\bmod p\).
For every integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
              n_0=\max_{\substack{p\ {\rm good}\\p\equiv1\bmod m}}\ell(p).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(\sigma\) fails to be Salem exactly when there exists one good prime at which every odd-degree irreducible factor of \(H\bmod p\) has degree at least \(r\).
Such a prime may be required to be \(1\bmod m\) for any prescribed modulus.

Indeed, the monic polynomial \(H_0\bmod p\) still has odd degree, so it contributes at least one odd-degree factor and \(\ell(p)\le n_0\).
Let \(L\) be the splitting field of \(H\) and \(G={\rm Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) acting on its rational orbits.
Its commutator equals \(D=\prod_{i=0}^t A_{n_i}\) by the [independent alternating actions](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#independence).
Inside \(D\), choose an \(n_0\)-cycle on the odd orbit and two disjoint cycles of lengths \(2,n_i-2\) on each even orbit.
All these permutations are even and together have a single odd cycle of length \(n_0\).
With \(K_m=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\), the group \({\rm Gal}(L/(L\cap K_m))\) contains \(D\), since \(L\cap K_m\) is abelian over \(\mathbb Q\).
The chosen tuple therefore extends to an automorphism of \(LK_m\) fixing \(K_m\).
Chebotarev supplies infinitely many good primes in the required class with this cycle type, whose cycle lengths are precisely their modular factor degrees.
This proves (1), and C400 identifies \(\sigma\) as Salem exactly when \(n_0<r\). [Cyclotomic fibers and factor degrees](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#primes) · [Chebotarev counting theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001)

## The maximum in every reduced residue class

Let \(E_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\operatorname{disc}H_0})\), allowing \(E_0=\mathbb Q\).
For \(u\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^*\), let \(\tau_u\) act on \(\zeta_m\) by the \(u\)-th power.
Then
\[
 \max_{\substack{p\ {\rm good}\\p\equiv u\bmod m}}\ell(p)
 =\begin{cases}
 n_0-2,& E_0\subseteq K_m\ \text{and }\tau_u|_{E_0}\ne1,\\
 n_0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                       \tag{2}
\]
Restriction to \(L\) identifies the fiber above \(\tau_u\) with a coset of \(H_m={\rm Gal}(L/(L\cap K_m))\).
The sign of the action on orbit zero is the action on its Vandermonde square root, with fixed field \(E_0\) inside \(L\).
If \(E_0\subseteq K_m\), this sign is prescribed by \(\tau_u\).
If \(E_0\not\subseteq K_m\), the sign on \(H_m\) is surjective since its fixed field \(L\cap K_m\) does not contain \(E_0\).
Then exactly half of the fiber permits an even sign on orbit zero.

Within any one \(D\)-coset, the permutations on different orbits range independently over their full prescribed sign classes.
On an even orbit the two-even-cycle choice above realizes the even sign, and a single \(n_i\)-cycle realizes the odd sign, with no odd cycles in either case.
Whenever even sign on orbit zero is available, choose its \(n_0\)-cycle to attain \(n_0\).
When odd sign is forced, an \(n_0\)-cycle is impossible, so the least odd cycle has odd length at most \(n_0-2\) on that orbit.
The cycle type \((n_0-2,2)\) there has odd sign and attains this upper bound together with the chosen permutations on the even orbits.
These are conjugacy-invariant cycle conditions, giving (2) by Chebotarev in the same compositum.

In fact the single-prime Salem exclusion works in **every** reduced residue class for sufficiently large \(r\), uniformly in the choice of class and modulus.
The marked factor has an odd number \(s_0\) of exterior roots, and the [uniform degree comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#counts) gives \(n_0/r=(s_0/2)(1+o(1))\).
If \(s_0=1\), then \(n_0/r\to1/2\) and every good prime has \(\ell(p)<r\) for large \(r\).
If \(s_0\ge3\), then \(n_0/r\ge(3/2)(1-o(1))\), so even \(n_0-2\ge r\) eventually.
Thus for every input of sufficiently large slope and each reduced class \(u\bmod m\), \(\sigma\) fails to be Salem if and only if some good \(p\equiv u\bmod m\) has \(\ell(p)\ge r\).
The modulus and residue class can be chosen separately for each input.

## The exterior count modulo four from two residue classes

Put \(m=8|\operatorname{disc}H|\) and write \(M_u\) for the maximum in (2).
Then the number \(s_0\) of exterior roots in the marked factor satisfies
\[
                        s_0\equiv 1+M_1-M_{-1}\pmod4 .
\]
Indeed the discriminant product formula gives \(\operatorname{disc}H_0\mid\operatorname{disc}H\), and the conductor of a quadratic field with squarefree radicand \(d\) is its absolute fundamental discriminant, either \(|d|\) or \(4|d|\).
Thus \(E_0\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\), also when \(E_0=\mathbb Q\).
Complex conjugation on the marked trace block has exactly \((s_0-1)/2\) transpositions, since each nonreal conjugate pair of exterior roots gives one pair of nonreal traces and all circle traces are real.
Consequently \(M_1-M_{-1}\) is two if \((s_0-1)/2\) is odd, and zero otherwise, by (2).
The modulus and both prime statistics in this formula are specified directly by \(H\).

## Explicit lower densities for attaining the maximum

For an even integer \(d\ge 4\) put
\[
             A(d)=2^{-d}\binom d{d/2}\frac{d-2}{d-1},
             \qquad P=\prod_{i=1}^t A(n_i),
\]
with empty product one.
In a reduced class allowing even sign on orbit zero, let \(w\) be the proportion of the cyclotomic fiber with that sign.
It is \(1\) if the sign is forced even and \(1/2\) if both signs are allowed.
The relative natural density in the progression of primes attaining \(n_0\) is at least \(w(2/n_0)P\).
In a class forcing odd sign on that orbit, the relative density attaining \(n_0-2\) is at least \(P/(n_0-2)\).

To check these constants, the cycle enumeration formula gives the series for the proportions of permutations with only even cycles as \(\exp(\sum_{j\ge1}z^{2j}/(2j))=(1-z^2)^{-1/2}\).
Weighting each permutation by its sign changes the exponent to its negative, giving \((1-z^2)^{1/2}\), since each even cycle has sign \(-1\).
For even \(d>0\) the two coefficients of degree \(d\) are \(c_d=2^{-d}\binom d{d/2}\) and \(-c_d/(d-1)\).
The conditional all-even-cycle proportions within the even and odd sign classes of \(S_d\) are respectively their sum and their difference, both at least \(A(d)\).
An \(n_0\)-cycle has conditional proportion \(2/n_0\) in the even sign class, and the type \((n_0-2,2)\) has conditional proportion \(1/(n_0-2)\) in the odd class.
Independence inside a \(D\)-coset gives the product lower bounds, and averaging over the fiber gives the indicated relative densities.
These are limiting proportions for each fixed trace polynomial and modulus.
This density argument provides neither a practical search cutoff nor a bound for the least certificate prime uniform in the input and modulus.
The high-slope threshold is the one in the preceding degree and trace-group arguments.
The existence of a sequence of marked Salem factors tending to one remains open.
