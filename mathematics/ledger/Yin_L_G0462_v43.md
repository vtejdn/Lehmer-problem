# Reconstructing a positive-constant Pisot polynomial from its interlacing numerator

<a id="inverse"></a>
## An exact test with the numerator fixed

Fix an integer polynomial \(C\) of degree \(h\ge0\) with positive leading coefficient \(j\) and simple roots \(y_1<\cdots<y_h\) in \((-2,2)\).
Put \(p=C/j\).
The following construction gives all positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomials with this [trace numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace).
Choose a positive divisor \(b\) of \(C(2)\), set \(q=1+C(2)/b\), and take a monic integer \(B\) of degree \(h+1=m\) with \(B(2)=-b\).
Compute
\[
      w_i=-\frac{B(y_i)}{p'(y_i)},\qquad
      E=\sum_{i=1}^h\frac{w_i}{4-y_i^2},\qquad
      E_* = 1+\frac{j}{2(q^2-1)} .
\]
Require every \(w_i>0\).
If \(E<E_*\), take \(e=1\), and if \(E=E_*\), take \(e=0\).
When \(E>E_*\) this \(B\) supplies no input.
The polynomial for either successful choice is
\[
  f(z)=\frac{z^m(z+1)^e}{1-z^2}
               \left[(q-z)B(z+z^{-1})+C(z+z^{-1})\right].               \tag{1}
\]
Its degree is \(2m+e-1\), it has constant \(q\) and first branch index \(j\).
The construction applies to arbitrary integral \(C\) as above, including nonmonic factors and torsion factors in the same numerator.
The [earlier simplex](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) fixes a Salem output and varies its Pisot preimage.
Here the denominator \(B\) and therefore the Salem or quadratic output can vary.

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Recovering both endpoint signs and all roots

Since \(B,p\) are monic, \(F=B/p\) has the unique expansion
\[
   F(x)=x-a-\sum_i\frac{w_i}{x-y_i}.
\]
The divisor condition gives \(F(2)=-j/(q-1)\).
Taking the difference at the endpoints gives \(F(2)-F(-2)=4-4E\).
Thus the strict condition is \(F(-2)<-j/(q+1)\), and equality is \(F(-2)=-j/(q+1)\).
If every residue weight is positive, \(F\) is strictly increasing on its intervals of definition and crosses zero exactly once on each interval, including the two unbounded ones.
The endpoint signs consequently place the smallest root of \(B\) above \(-2\) and the largest \(T\) above 2, with all intermediate roots in \((-2,2)\).
For \(h=0\) there is a single root \(T>2\).
In particular \(C/B=j/F\) has positive residues at every pole, and its value at \(2\) is \(1-q\).

Write \(x=z+z^{-1}\) and \(D=z^m(z+1)^eB(x)\).
The numerator on the right of (1) belongs to \(\mathbb Z[z]\) and vanishes at \(z=1\) by the endpoint value.
It vanishes at \(z=-1\) either by the factor \(z+1\), or by \(C(-2)/B(-2)=-(q+1)\) in the equality case.
Division therefore gives an integer polynomial, monic in the displayed degree and with constant term \(q\).
The rational function \(Y=f/D=(q-z+C/B)/(1-z^2)\) obeys \(Y(1/z)=z-z^2 Y(z)\).
By reciprocity of \(D\) this gives \(zf+f^*=D\).
It also gives \(f(1)=D(1)/2<0\), because \(B(2)=-b\).

At a circle root \(\zeta\ne\pm1\) of \(D\), \(f\) is nonzero since \(C\) is nonzero at its trace.
At \(-1\) in the case \(e=1\), the residue of \(Y\) is \((q+1+C(-2)/B(-2))/2>0\).
This uses \(F(-2)<-j/(q+1)\).
For \(e=0\), \(f(-1)=-D(-1)/2\ne0\) by the reciprocal identity and the odd degree of \(f\).
Every unit root of \(f\) would also be a unit root of \(D\), so \(f\) has none.
All the neutral roots of \(D\) are simple, and the residue of \(Y\) at each paired neutral root is the positive residue of \(C/B\) at its trace divided by \((1-\zeta^2)(1-\zeta^{-2})>0\).
Thus every circle crossing of \(W=zf/f^*\) at \(-1\) is upward by \(\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}Y=-1/(\zeta W'(\zeta))\), including the single root at \(-1\) when present.
There are \(\deg f-1\) such crossings, since \(D\) has one reciprocal real pair off the circle.
If \(N\) is the disk zero count of \(f\), the winding number of \(W\) is also \(1+2N-\deg f\).
Consequently \(N=\deg f-1\).
The unique exterior root is real and greater than one by \(f(1)<0\).
Any further monic integer factor would have nonzero constant of modulus less than one, proving irreducibility.
The [integer series at infinity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) recovers \(C\) and the index \(j\).

Conversely, every positive-constant Pisot input gives the stated \(B,C\), \(b=-B(2)>0\) and positive \(w_i\) by the [positive expansions](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#strip).
If \(e=0\), the rational formula for \(Y\) has no pole at \(-1\), forcing equality at that endpoint.
If \(e=1\), the [simple upward crossing there](Yin_L_G0462_v34.md#crossings) forces positive residue and hence the strict endpoint inequality, since \(F(-2)<0\) by the ordering of the trace roots.
The formula then reconstructs the original \(f\).

<a id="volume"></a>
## The volume for each divisor

For \(h\ge1\) the polynomials \(B\) in the strict case correspond to the integral points of an open bounded simplex in \(h\) real coefficient coordinates.
Indeed all monic choices with \(B(2)=-b\) differ by \((x-2)H(x)\), where \(\deg H<h\).
Use the ordinary coefficients of \(H\) so that \(\mathbb Z^h\) has covolume one.
Writing \(\Delta=\prod_{i<l}(y_l-y_i)\), interpolation gives absolute Jacobian \(\prod_i(2-y_i)/\Delta\) for the map to the \(w_i\).
This follows from the Vandermonde evaluation determinant and \(\prod_i|p'(y_i)|=\Delta^2\).
In the \(w_i\) coordinates the simplex has volume \(E_*^h\prod_i(4-y_i^2)/h!\).
Thus its coefficient volume is exactly
\[
       \frac{E_*^h}{h!}\,\prod_i(2+y_i)\,\Delta
            \ \le\ \frac{(2E_*)^h(h+1)^{(h+1)/2}}{h!}.                  \tag{2}
\]
For the inequality, include the additional point \(-2\) in the Vandermonde determinant and use the monic basis \(1,z+z^{-1},\ldots,z^h+z^{-h}\) in the trace variable.
On \([-2,2]\) all the nonconstant columns have entries of absolute value at most two, so Hadamard's inequality gives (2).
The choices for the odd degree of \(f\) lie on the outer face of the same simplex with all \(w_i>0\).
Only finitely many integral \(B\) occur at each \(C,b\), and \(C(2)\) has finitely many divisors.
In particular, each fixed numerator admits only finitely many positive-constant Pisot inputs.
Along any near-integer sequence, \(E_*\to1\) by the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
The upper bound in (2) then has logarithm \(-\tfrac12 h\log h+O(h)\) as \(h\to\infty\).
This volume does not determine whether exceptional simplices contain lattice points.

<a id="return"></a>
## What a sequence of successful tests would give

For the Pisot roots reconstructed by (1), \(\theta-q\to0\) if and only if \(j/(q-1)=jb/C(2)\to0\).
This follows in both directions from \(j/(\theta-q)=q+\theta^{-1}-H'_q(0)\), \(|H'_q(0)|\le2\) and \(j\ge1\).
By the [positive-constant Salem equivalence](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#statement), a sequence of lattice points in these simplices with \(jb/C(2)\to0\) exists if and only if Salem numbers tend to one.
Even the strict case suffices for this equivalence because the prescribed-constant inverse there has \(e=1\).

For example take \(C=2x-1\), \(b=1\), \(q=4\), \(y_1=1/2\).
The integral choices are \(B=x^2+u x-5-2u\).
Here \(w_1=(19+6u)/4\), \(E=(19+6u)/15\), and \(E_*=16/15\).
Precisely \(u=-3,-2,-1\) pass, all in the strict case.
For \(u=-2\) formula (1) gives the quartic \(z^4-5z^3+2z^2-3z+4\), with nonmonic trace numerator and branch index two.
