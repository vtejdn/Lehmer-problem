# Primitive resultant growth in a window of orders

<a id="linear-window"></a>
## A linear window for polynomials with few terms

Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\), let \(\Phi_j\) denote the \(j\)-th cyclotomic polynomial, and put
\[
 X_j(P)=\varphi(j)^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|,
 \qquad C_j(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,x^j-1)|.
\]
Use \(X_j=-\infty\) at a common root.
Fix \(0<A<B\) and \(\epsilon>0\).
For real polynomials \(P\) of degree \(d\to\infty\) with \(s\ge2\) nonzero coefficients, suppose
\[
                       s\log(2s)=o(\log\log(3d)).
\]
Then \(|X_j(P)-m(P)|>\epsilon\) holds at most \(d/2+o(d)\) times among the integers \(Ad\le j\le Bd\).
All estimates are independent of coefficient height and support positions.
If \(B-A>1/2\), a positive proportion of these orders satisfy both \(|X_j(P)-m(P)|\le\epsilon\) and \(\varphi(j)\ge c_{A,B}d\).

In particular fix \(t\ge2\), and let integer \(P\) have at most \(t\) nonzero coefficients, no root of unity and \(m(P)>0\).
For a monomial \(P=ax^d\) one has \(X_j(P)=m(P)=\log|a|\) directly.
The [fixed-term Mahler gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives \(m(P)\ge\eta_t>0\).
Taking \(\epsilon=\eta_t/2\), a positive proportion of \(d\le j\le2d\) give
\[
              \log C_j(P)\ge\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|
                         \ge c\,d\,m(P)
\]
for all sufficiently large \(d\) depending on \(t\).
The first inequality uses the other cyclotomic components of the full resultant, each a nonzero integer.
This permits adaptive orders for the six-term case where the [exact-degree analysis](Yin_L_G0462_v47.md) leaves very small values possible.

<a id="regularized-orbits"></a>
## A regularized mean over primitive roots

Let \(H\) be the maximum absolute coefficient of \(P\), write \(u=|P|/H\) on the circle, and use normalized arclength \(\mu\).
With \(a=1/(2(s-1))\), the [sparse regularization estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#regularity) yields for \(0<h\le1\)
\[
 0\le\int\tfrac12\log(u^2+h^2)\,d\mu-m(P)+\log H\le24s^2h^a,
 \qquad \mu\{u<h\}\ll s^2h^a .
\]
The second bound follows because the regularization loss on \(\{u<h\}\) is at least \((\log2)/2\).
Put \(\rho=(\log(2d))^{-1/8}\), \(J=\lceil\sqrt{\log(2d)}\rceil\), \(R=s+1\), and \(V=1-(u^2+\rho^2)/R^2\).
The regularized function \(f=\tfrac12\log(u^2+\rho^2)\) is approximated uniformly by
\[
                 f_J=\log R-\frac12\sum_{v=1}^J V^v/v .
\]
Indeed \(0\le V\le1-\rho^2/R^2\) and the tail is bounded by \((R^2/\rho^2)\exp(-J\rho^2/R^2)=o(1)\).
The Fourier coefficient absolute sum of \(V\) is at most \(2\), since that of \(u^2\) is at most \(s^2\).
Consequently the nonconstant coefficients of \(f_J\) have absolute sum \(O(2^J)\) and frequencies \(|k|\le Jd\).

The average of the \(k\)-th Fourier character over the primitive \(j\)-th roots is \(c_j(k)/\varphi(j)\), where Möbius inversion gives \(c_j(k)=\sum_{\ell\mid(j,k)}\ell\mu_{\rm M}(j/\ell)\) with \(\mu_{\rm M}\) the Möbius function.
For \(k\ne0\), summing absolute values gives \(\sum_{Ad\le j\le Bd}|c_j(k)|\le Bd\,\tau(|k|)\).
Use \(\varphi(j)\ge\sqrt{j/2}\) and the elementary bound \(\tau(k)\ll k^{1/8}\).
The first inequality follows by multiplying the prime-power bounds \(\varphi(p^b)\ge p^{b/2}\), with just the factor \(2^1\) requiring the adjustment \(1/\sqrt2\).
The divisor bound follows by using \(b+1\le p^{b/8}\) for \(p\ge256\) and absorbing the finitely many smaller primes in a constant.
The mean absolute difference over the orders between the primitive-grid and circle means of \(f_J\) is thus \(O_{A,B}(2^J(Jd)^{1/8}d^{-1/2})=o(1)\).
The same holds for \(f\), and its circle mean differs from \(m(P)-\log H\) by \(o(1)\).

<a id="exceptional-orders"></a>
## How many orders can contain an exceptionally small value

The primitive roots of all the orders in the window are distinct and have normalized angular spacing at least \((Bd)^{-2}\).
For \(0<h<1\) the sublevel set \(\{u<h\}\) has at most \(d\) open arc components.
Indeed \(u^2-h^2\) is a nonconstant real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(d\) with at most \(2d\) zeros counted with multiplicity.
It takes positive values since \(\sup u\ge1\), and each component accounts for two boundary incidences, with an isolated separating zero of even multiplicity accounting for both incidences there.
Thus the total number of nodes in this sublevel satisfies
\[
                       N(h)\ll_B d^2s^2h^a+d .
\]
Set \(T=10s\log(2d)\).
The total arclength of \(\{u<e^{-T}\}\) is less than \((Bd)^{-2}\) for all sufficiently large \(d\).
Each of its at most \(d\) components therefore contains at most one node.
Real coefficients pair these nodes under conjugation and each pair has the same order, with two distinct nodes once \(Ad>2\).
Remove the at most \(d/2\) orders containing such nodes.

For the remaining orders \(u\ge e^{-T}\) at every node.
At \(u\ge\sqrt{\rho}\) the loss \(f-\log u\) is at most \(\rho/2\).
At all other nodes it is bounded by \((\log2)/2+\log^+(\rho/u)\).
Summing over the remaining nodes and integrating their sublevel counts bounds the total loss by
\[
 O_B\!\left(\rho d^2+N(\sqrt{\rho})+
               \int_{\log(1/\rho)}^T N(e^{-v})\,dv\right)
 \ll_B d^2(\rho+s^2\rho^{a/2}+s^3\rho^a)+d(T+1)=o(d^2).
\]
Here \(s\log(2s)=o(\log\log(3d))\) implies \(s^k\rho^{a/2}=o(1)\) for each fixed \(k\).
For fixed \(\kappa>0\), the number of orders with \(\varphi(j)<\kappa d\) is \(O_{A,B}(\kappa d)\).
Indeed \(\sum_{j\le Bd}j/\varphi(j)\ll_B d\) follows by expanding \(j/\varphi(j)=\sum_{h\mid j}\mu_{\rm M}^2(h)/\varphi(h)\) and using convergence of \(\prod_p(1+1/(p(p-1)))\).
On the remaining orders with \(\varphi(j)\ge\kappa d\), the total loss bound shows that a normalized loss exceeding any fixed positive threshold occurs only \(o(d)\) times.
Combining this with the regularized mean estimate, then taking the limsup and letting \(\kappa\downarrow0\), proves the exceptional-order bound.
Keeping \(\kappa\) small and fixed proves the positive-proportion assertion when \(B-A>1/2\).

<a id="cyclotomic-clearing"></a>
## Dense polynomials with a sparse cyclotomic multiple

Let integer \(P\) of degree \(d\) have no root of unity, and suppose \(F=DP\) has degree \(e\le Kd\), where \(D\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials and \(K\) is fixed.
Assume the number of terms of \(F\) satisfies the preceding growth condition in \(d\).
Then \(|X_j(P)-m(P)|>\epsilon\) occurs at most \(e/2+o(d)\) times in the same window.
Indeed the preceding proof for \(F\) uses at most \(e\) sublevel components, frequencies at most \(Je\) and at most \(e/2\) exceptional orders.
All its other bounds remain valid for \(d\le e\le Kd\).
Also \(m(F)=m(P)\).
It suffices to show that outside \(o(d)\) common orders \(X_j(D)\) tends to zero in density, meaning that each fixed positive threshold is exceeded \(o(d)\) times.
For fixed term bound and \(m(P)>0\), any window with \(B-A>K/2\) therefore gives \(\log C_j(P)\gg_{A,B,K} d\,m(P)\) for a positive proportion of its orders.

Write \(D=\prod_k\Phi_k^{b_k}\) and \(E=\sum_k b_k\varphi(k)=O(d)\), taking \(E\ge1\) since the constant case is immediate.
Then \(b_k>0\) implies \(k\le2E^2\), and at most \(O(\sqrt d)\) distinct such \(k\) lie in the window by \(\varphi(k)\ge\sqrt{k/2}\).
For \(k\ne j\), the absolute cyclotomic resultant is \(1\) except when the larger index is the smaller times a prime power \(p^v\), in which case it is \(p^{\varphi(\min(k,j))}\).
To see this, reduce modulo a prime \(p\), where primitive root orders lose exactly their \(p\)-part by the identity \(\Phi_{hp^w}(x)=\Phi_h(x^{p^w})/\Phi_h(x^{p^{w-1}})\) for \(p\nmid h\) and \(w\ge1\).
When the smaller index is \(hp^u\) and the larger is \(hp^{u+v}\), evaluate this identity at every primitive \(hp^u\)-th root by differentiating numerator and denominator at their simple zeros.
The derivative ratio contributes \(p\) times a unit-modulus power of the root times a ratio of \(\Phi_h'\) values, whose products cancel in absolute value since both power maps cover the primitive \(h\)-th roots equally often.

For \(j<k\) the summed logarithmic contribution over all \(j\) in the window and all \(k\) is at most
\[
       \sum_k b_k\varphi(k)\sum_{p\mid k}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
                    \le E\log(2E^2)=o(d^2),
\]
using \(\sum_{v=1}^{v_p(k)}\varphi(k/p^v)=\varphi(k)/(p-1)\).
For \(j>k\) and fixed \(k\le R_0\), the orders \(j=kp^v\) number \(o_{R_0}(d)\) and can all be discarded.
Here \(\psi(x)=\sum_{p^v\le x}\log p\ll x\) is the elementary Chebyshev estimate, which implies \(o(x)\) prime powers by splitting off \(p\le x^{1/4}\).
For the remaining \(k>R_0\), the total log contribution is at most \(\sum_{k>R_0}b_k\varphi(k)\psi(Bd/k)\ll_B dE/R_0\).
Discard the \(O_{A,B}(\kappa d)\) orders with \(\varphi(j)<\kappa d\) as before.
At a fixed threshold for \(X_j(D)\), bounding the exceptional proportion by the summed logarithms divided by a constant times \(\kappa d^2\), then letting \(R_0\to\infty\) and \(\kappa\downarrow0\), proves convergence in density.

## A prime window without any coefficient or support restriction

There are absolute positive constants \(C_0,C_1,c\) with the following property.
For every monic complex polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\ge1\) and every \(Y\ge C_0d\log(2d)\), at least \(cY/\log Y\) primes \(p\in[Y,2Y]\) satisfy
\[
       \log|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_p)|
                        \ge (p-1)m(P)-C_1 d\log\log(3Y).
\]
For a root \(\alpha\) outside the unit disk, reciprocity gives \(|\Phi_p(\alpha)|=|\alpha|^{p-1}|\Phi_p(1/\alpha)|\).
Thus after extracting \((p-1)m(P)\), all \(d\) normalized points \(z\) are in the closed unit disk.
Write \(L\) for the number of window primes, so \(L\asymp Y/\log Y\) by the dyadic Chebyshev bounds.
For completeness the elementary upper estimate \(\psi(x)\le2x\log2+O(\log^2(2x))\) follows by summing \(\psi(2n)-\psi(n)\le\log\binom{2n}{n}\le2n\log2\) dyadically with integer rounding.
For the lower bound, \(\log\binom{2n}{n}\ge2n\log2-O(\log(2n))\), primes between \(2n/3\) and \(n\) contribute nothing for large \(n\), and prime powers of base at most \(\sqrt{2n}\) contribute \(O(\sqrt n\log(2n))\).
The remaining contributions below \(n\) are at most \(\psi(2n/3)\), which gives \(\sum_{n<p\le2n}\log p\gg n\).

If \(|\Phi_p(z)|<e^{-t}\) for \(t\) exceeding an absolute constant \(t_0\), then \(|1-z^p|=|1-z|\,|\Phi_p(z)|\le2e^{-t}\).
From the modulus and argument of \(z^p\), the nearest \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\) satisfies \(|z-\zeta|\ll e^{-t}/p\) and the normalized angular distance has the same bound.
Indeed \(1-|z|^p\le2e^{-t}\) implies \(1-|z|\le-\log(1-2e^{-t})/p\), and \(|\arg(z^p)|\ll e^{-t}\) in the principal branch.
This \(\zeta\) is nontrivial since \(\zeta=1\) would give \(|\Phi_p(z)-p|\le |1-z|p(p-1)/2\ll p e^{-t}\), a contradiction for large fixed \(t_0\).

Distinct nontrivial roots of the window primes have normalized angular spacing at least \(1/(4Y^2)\).
For each nonzero \(z\), delete one prime furnishing a primitive node closest in angle to \(z\) among the entire pool, choosing any tie.
The point \(z=0\) incurs no loss.
Among the remaining primes for this point, the number with \(\log^-|\Phi_p(z)|>t\), where \(\log^-w=\max(0,-\log w)\), is \(O(Y e^{-t})\) for \(t\ge t_0\).
Indeed all such primes furnish distinct nodes in an arc of length \(O(e^{-t}/Y)\), and the arc contains a closest node of the deleted prime whenever the count is positive.
Spacing bounds the node count in the arc by one plus its length divided by the separation, with the extra node supplied by the deletion.
Delete the union of these primes for the \(d\) points, at cost at most \(d\) primes.
Integration of the sublevel counts gives, per point, summed loss over the surviving primes at most
\[
           t_0L+\int_{t_0}^{\infty}\min(L,CY e^{-t})\,dt
                        \ll L\bigl(1+\log(Y/L)\bigr)\ll L\log\log(3Y).
\]
The assumption on \(Y\), with \(C_0\) sufficiently large, ensures \(L\ge4d\).
Summing over all \(d\) points and discarding primes where the loss exceeds a sufficiently large multiple of \(d\log\log(3Y)\) leaves a positive proportion of window primes, as asserted.

## Consequence for unrestricted algebraic units

For a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) with minimal polynomial \(P\), the [Dobrowolski bound with its logarithmic numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md) gives \(m(P)\gg(\log\log(3d)/\log(2d))^3\), after adjusting the absolute constant in bounded degree.
Set \(Y=C d\log^3(2d)/(\log\log(3d))^2\) with \(C\) sufficiently large.
Then a positive proportion of the primes in this window satisfy
\[
                    \log C_p(P)\ge\tfrac12(p-1)m(P).
\]
Indeed \((Y-1)m(P)\ge 2C_1 d\log\log(3Y)\) and \(|P(1)|\) is a positive integer.
For \(m(P)\ge\eta>0\), the original prime-window estimate already gives the same conclusion for \(Y=C_\eta d\log(2d)\) and large \(d\).
For unrestricted degrees and supports these windows are superlinear, and the estimate permits \(m(P)\to0\).
