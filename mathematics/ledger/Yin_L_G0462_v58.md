# Exact torsion factors and the cost of an integer multiplier on the two-term Chebyshev family

<a id="factor"></a>
## The primitive frequencies

Let \(n>m\ge1\), \(g=\gcd(n,m)\), \(N=n/g\), \(M=m/g\), and let \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
Put \(X_g(x)=2T_g(x/2)\), characterized by \(X_g(z+z^{-1})=z^g+z^{-g}\), and use \(U_k(\cos t)=\sin((k+1)t)/\sin t\).
The integer polynomial
\[
                 C(x)=2U_{n-1}(x/2)+\varepsilon U_{m-1}(x/2)
\]
has the factorization
\[
 C(x)=U_{g-1}(x/2)\bigl(X_g(x)^2-3\bigr)^e A(x),\qquad
 A(x)=
 \frac{2U_{N-1}(X_g(x)/2)+\varepsilon U_{M-1}(X_g(x)/2)}
      {(X_g(x)^2-3)^e}.                                            \tag{1}
\]
Here \(A\) is primitive and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) with leading coefficient two.
The first two factors of \(C\) in (1) have exactly its torsion traces as their roots, all simple.
The exponent \(e\) is one precisely when \(\gcd(N,12)=1\), \(M\equiv3\) or \(9\bmod12\), and
\[
                 2\sin(N\pi/6)+\varepsilon\sin(M\pi/6)=0.
\]
Otherwise \(e=0\).
In particular the nontorsion factor for coprime frequencies stays irreducible under every substitution \(x\mapsto X_g(x)\), and
\[
                            A(2)=2N+\varepsilon M .                    \tag{2}
\]

To see this, the Chebyshev sine formula first removes the common factor \(U_{g-1}(x/2)\) and leaves the stated numerator of \(A\).
All \(n-1\) roots of \(C\) are distinct and in \((-2,2)\).
Indeed \(2\sin(nt)+\varepsilon\sin(mt)\) takes strictly alternating signs at the \(n\) successive angles \(t=(k+1/2)\pi/n\) in \((0,\pi)\), and the \(n-1\) intervening intervals already account for the degree of \(C\).
The endpoint values are nonzero, since \(2n>m\).

For a torsion point \(z=e^{it}\ne\pm1\) over a root of \(C\), the relation \(2(z^n-z^{-n})+\varepsilon(z^m-z^{-m})=0\) makes \((z^m-z^{-m})/2\) an algebraic integer.
All its conjugates have modulus at most one, so it is zero or a root of unity by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/MV226/Yin_U001.md#mv226-u001).
In the first case \(z^{2m}=z^{2n}=1\), exactly the points \(z^{2g}=1\) with the two endpoints removed, giving the factor \(U_{g-1}(x/2)\).
In the second case \(|z^m-z^{-m}|=2\), so \(z^m=\pm i\) and the original equation makes \(z^n\) a primitive twelfth root.
Writing \(w=z^g\), its order divides \(\gcd(4M,12N)=4\gcd(M,3)\), and the order of \(w^N\) is twelve.
Thus \(w\) has order twelve, yielding the two residue restrictions in the statement.
The test at \(w=e^{i\pi/6}\) suffices for every primitive twelfth root by conjugacy.
Its trace and those of its conjugates are \(\pm\sqrt3\), so the corresponding factor in \(x\) is \(X_g(x)^2-3\).
All its roots lie over these allowed points and are simple by the root count for \(C\).

The removed factors are monic integer polynomials by the Chebyshev recurrences.
The quotient \(A\) is integral by monic division and primitive because \(C\) has leading coefficient two and is nonzero modulo two.
Every monic irreducible factor with all roots in \((-2,2)\) has torsion traces as roots, by applying Kronecker's theorem to its monic reciprocal circle lift.
No such root remains in \(A\).
Two nonconstant primitive nonmonic factors would have product of leading coefficients of absolute value at least four.
This proves irreducibility, and \(X_g(2)=2\) gives (2), since \(2^2-3=1\).

## The obstruction from integer multiples

Let \(L\) be the infimum of \(\|G\|_{[-2,2]}\) over \(G\in A\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(G(2)\ne0\).
It is attained, with the following values.

| Sign | Frequencies and dilation | Minimum \(L\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(+\) | all | \(2N+M\) |
| \(-\) | \(N,M\) both odd | \(2N-M\) |
| \(-\) | \(N,M\) of opposite parity, \(g\) odd | \(2N+M\) |
| \(-\) | \(N,M\) of opposite parity, \(g\) even | \(\min(2N+M,\,2(2N-M))\) |

Indeed \(G(2)\) is a nonzero integer multiple of (2), so \(L\ge 2N+\varepsilon M\).
For the uniform upper bound \(2N+M\), take the numerator of \(A\) in (1), which is admissible, and use \(X_g([-2,2])\subseteq[-2,2]\) and \(|U_{k-1}(y/2)|\le k\) there.
This proves the first row.
For the negative sign and \(N,M\) of the same parity, the Laurent expansion \(U_{k-1}((w+w^{-1})/2)=w^{k-1}+w^{k-3}+\cdots+w^{1-k}\) places the smaller support inside the larger.
The coefficients of the difference weighted by \(2,-1\) are nonnegative, giving norm exactly \(2N-M\) for the same multiplier.

Suppose \(N,M\) have opposite parity and the sign is negative.
Then \(e=0\), since the optional factor requires \(N,M\) odd.
At every \(y\in[-2,2]\) with \(X_g(y)=-2\), the endpoint signs of the two Chebyshev terms give \(|A(y)|=2N+M\).
To improve strictly on this bound, \(G=AR\) must therefore have \(|R(y)|<1\) at the whole fiber.
These \(y\) are torsion traces, so \(R(y)\) is an algebraic integer and all its conjugates also have modulus less than one.
Thus \(R\) vanishes on the fiber.
For odd \(g\) this includes \(-2\), forcing \(4\mid R(2)\).
Since \(4(2N-M)>2N+M\), no improvement is possible in the third row.
For even \(g\), \(X_g+2=X_{g/2}^2\) and \(X_{g/2}\) is monic with distinct real roots, so \(X_{g/2}\mid R\) and \(2\mid R(2)\).
Any strict improvement costs at least \(2(2N-M)\).
Conversely taking \(R=X_{g/2}\) in this case gives
\[
    G=2U_{2N-1}(X_{g/2}/2)-U_{2M-1}(X_{g/2}/2)
\]
by the double-angle identity for \(U\).
The doubled frequencies have the same parity, so the Laurent support calculation gives norm \(2(2N-M)\).
This proves the last row.

In every case \(N<L<3N\), so divergence of the normalized multiplier cost \(L^2/2\) is equivalent to \(N\to\infty\).
Once \(N\to\infty\), seeking another bounded integer multiple with nonzero endpoint cannot exclude the family by this norm test.

For a successful [Pisot inverse construction](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) on \(C\), the [general multiplier obstruction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) forces \(L^2/2\to\infty\) whenever \(\theta-q\to0\).
Taking \(h=n-1,\ d=n-m\) shows that any such sequence satisfies
\[
                    \frac{h+1}{\gcd(h+1,d)}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
Equivalently, the denominator of \(d/(h+1)\) in lowest terms must tend to infinity.
For bounded denominators in this sense, \(q\) is bounded and the excess is bounded below by a positive constant, by the [fixed-branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
This excludes exact rational ratios of bounded denominator even when \(h/d\) stays bounded and the [long progression criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#progression) need not apply.
Ratios approaching a fixed rational through fractions of growing denominator still satisfy the necessary multiplier condition.
