# The location and shape of the first coefficient departure on the leading-two path

<a id="window"></a>
## The first departure precedes every fixed fraction of the degree

Use \(C_h=2U_h-U_{h-1}\) in the half-trace variable and one of the [three surviving profiles](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#exclusions) on a successful subsequence with \(q\to\infty\).
Let \(B^\sharp=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})\), \(B_\infty=V/U_0=(2-z)/K\), and let \(n\) be the first coefficient at which \(B^\sharp\) differs from this fixed integral series.
Then \(n\to\infty\) and \(n/h\to0\).

The [first departure argument](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#path) already gives \(n\to\infty\) and \(n\le h+1\).
Suppose instead that \(n\ge\epsilon h\) along a further subsequence with \(\epsilon>0\).
Write \(P=U_0 B^\sharp\), \(L=\deg P=2h+2+\deg U_0\).
The signed reciprocal \(P\) agrees with the fixed polynomial \(V\) through index \(n-1\) and with its signed reversal at the opposite end.
Consequently its nonzero coefficients outside \([n,L-n]\) occupy boundedly many positions near the two ends.

On the interval \(\deg U_0+2\le k\le2h\), the coefficients \(p_k=[z^k]P\) are Fourier coefficients of a complex measure of bounded variation independent of \(h,q\).
Indeed \(B^\sharp=(1-z/2)J_q+O(z^{2h+1})\) and every required coefficient of \(J_q\) at a strictly positive index \(l\) is \(2\int v^l\,d\omega_q(v)\).
Multiplying \(2\omega_q\) by \((1-v^{-1}/2)U_0(v^{-1})\) gives the required measure on the entire interval.
The [signed measure formula](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) bounds its total variation, even as the degree grows.

Take \(b=\lfloor n/3\rfloor\).
Use a frequency cutoff equal to one on \([n,L-n]\), given on integer indices by convolution of \(1_{[n-b,L-n+b]}\) with the uniform probability on \([-b,b]\).
Its support lies in \([n-2b,L-n+2b]\), inside the Fourier interval above for all large \(h\).
It meets no end coefficients of \(P\) outside the central band, since the distance to either end tends to infinity.
The trigonometric polynomial with the cutoff as coefficients is a product of two interval sums divided by \(2b+1\).
Its circle \(L^1\) norm is at most \(\sqrt{(L-2n+2b+1)/(2b+1)}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz and orthogonality, bounded in terms of \(\epsilon\).
Convolution with the bounded measure (reflected to match the Fourier convention) gives the central part of \(P\) with bounded circle \(L^1\) norm.
This part has integer coefficients because the transition ranges contain only zeros of \(P\).

Integer trigonometric polynomials with bounded circle \(L^1\) norm have boundedly many terms.
For a proof using the [finite Fourier algebra theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001), embed the coefficient sequence in groups of arbitrarily large prime order without overlaps.
Its Fourier algebra norm is the grid average of the absolute value of the polynomial and tends to its circle integral.
The theorem writes the integer sequence on a sufficiently fine prime grid as a bounded signed sum of coset indicators.
These cosets are singletons or the full group.
Choose the prime larger than twice the degree span plus the coset bound.
There is then a zero of the sequence outside the exceptional singletons, so the full-group terms cancel and the support size is bounded by the coset bound.
Hence the entire \(P\) has boundedly many terms.
Its positive Mahler measure tends to one, contradicting the [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).

## The first coefficient discrepancies for each of the three profiles

For the three possible \(K\), the integral polynomial \(H\) of [first coefficient discrepancies](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#departure) must have one of the following forms.

| \(K\) | \(H\), up to an overall sign |
| --- | --- |
| \(2-z\) | \(1,\ 1-z,\ 1+z^2\) |
| \((2-z)/(1-z)\) | \((1-z)^2\) |
| \((2-z)(1+z)\) | \(1\) |

Here a row describes the limits at every fixed offset from \(n\); it does not prescribe all higher coefficients for any single \(h\).
Write \(x=z+z^{-1}\) on the circle.
In the first row the [density bound](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#departure) reads
\[
       |H(z)|^2\le (4-x)^2/(5-2x)\le4,
\]
with strict inequality in the interior of \([-2,2]\).
Its mean is \(7/2\), by dividing the fraction into \(11/4-x/2+9/[4(5-2x)]\) and using the circle mean \(1/3\) of \(1/|2-z|^2\).
Thus \(H\) has at most three terms with coefficients of modulus one.
If a trinomial's exponents are not in arithmetic progression, their three pairwise differences have different positive absolute values.
Hence \(\int|H|^4=9+6=15>4\int|H|^2\).
For exponents in arithmetic progression, alignment gives modulus three when the endpoint coefficients agree, and a point with the middle phase \(i\) gives modulus \(\sqrt5\) when they differ.
Thus no trinomial works.
Normalize \(H(0)=1\).
For a binomial \(1\pm z^l\), every point of modulus-two alignment must have \(z=\pm1\).
This leaves \(1+z,1-z,1+z^2\), and \(1+z\) fails for \(1<x<2\) since the cleared difference is \(3(x-1)(x-2)\).
The respective differences for \(1-z,1+z^2\) are \((3-x)(x+2)\) and \(2(x-2)^2(x+2)\), both nonnegative.

In the second row (1) of L462v49 becomes \(|H|\le3|1-z|^2/|2-z|\).
It forces a double zero at one, so \(H=(1-z)^2P_0\) with \(P_0\) integral and \(|P_0|\le3/|2-z|\).
The squared upper bound has mean three, and equality of means would force \((2-z)P_0\) to have constant circle modulus three.
A polynomial of constant nonzero circle modulus is a monomial, which cannot vanish at 2.
Thus \(P_0\) has at most two unit terms.
For a binomial \(P_0=1\pm z^l\) up to sign, all alignment points would require \(x\ge11/8\).
Their mean trace is zero for \(l\ge2\), ruling these out.
For \(l=1\) the minus sign fails at \(-1\), and the plus sign fails at \(x=0\), where \((5-2x)(2+x)=10>9\).
This leaves \(P_0=\pm1\).

In the last row the bound is \(|H|\le (3-x)/\sqrt{5-2x}\le5/3\).
The squared fraction decreases on \([-2,2]\).
Hence \(H\) has at most two unit terms by its squared mean, and no binomial can have supremum below two.
This leaves \(H=\pm1\).
