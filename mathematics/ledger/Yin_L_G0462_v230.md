# Five exact irreducible rows of the unit-coefficient family and a smooth-degree construction

For the unit-coefficient sections of seed degrees three, five and seven with first exponent one, the noncyclotomic part is irreducible whenever it is nonempty, at every positive second exponent.
The same conclusion holds for the two primitive quartic rows with first exponent one or two.
The [complete list](#rows) gives all empty cores and their cyclotomic factors.
A separate [construction with changed coefficients](#smooth) gives irreducibility with both primitive exponents growing.

<a id="poles"></a>
## The exterior pole count of every unit-coefficient seed

Put \(g_s(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\) and \(F_{a,b}=g_s(X^a)+X^bB_s(X^a)\).
The number \(t_s\) of exterior zeros of \(B_s\), counting multiplicity, is exactly
\[
                t_s=s-1-2\left\lfloor(2s-1)/6\right\rfloor .
\]
To count the interior zeros of \(g_s\), move the positive real parameter \(h\) from zero to one in \(T^s-h(T+1)\).
For small \(h\) all \(s\) roots are inside.
A crossing at \(z=e^{i\theta}\), \(-\pi<\theta<\pi\), requires
\[
 \theta=4\pi m/(2s-1),\qquad h=(2\cos(\theta/2))^{-1}.
\]
The point \(-1\) is never a root.
Crossings with \(h\le1\) occur exactly for \(|m|<(2s-1)/6\), since \(2s-1\) is odd.
All are simple outward crossings, because \(d\log z/d\log h=(s-z/(z+1))^{-1}\) has positive real part there by \(\Re(z/(z+1))=1/2\).
Reversal gives the formula.

At every slope there are at most \(at_s\) exterior roots of \(F_{a,b}\), counted with multiplicity.
Indeed \(X^b B_s(X^a)+v g_s(X^a)\) for \(0<v<1\) has the same interior root count as \(X^b B_s(X^a)\) by Rouché, using equal moduli of the blocks on the circle and the absence of circle zeros.
The degree stays \(sa+b\), so it has precisely \(at_s\) exterior roots.
Let \(v\) tend to one in disjoint disks around the exterior roots of the limit.
Every nontorsion irreducible factor of the limit receives an exterior root by the monic unit endpoints and Kronecker's theorem.

## Five primitive rows at every positive exponent

On the following rows the cyclotomic-free part is either one or a single simple rational irreducible polynomial for **every** primitive \(b\ge1\).
The table lists every \(b\) for which it is one, together with the cyclotomic conductors of the monic associate in parentheses.

For odd \(s=3,5,7\), the preceding pole formula gives \(t_s=2\).
The [complete real-root count](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#real) gives at most one real exterior root, always simple.
When it occurs, a further nonreal pair would exceed the bound of two.
Otherwise every exterior root belongs to a nonreal conjugate pair, which must stay together in a rational factor.
For \(s=4,a=1\) the bound is one, and for \(s=4,a=2\) the bound is two with no real exterior root by the same classification.
This proves the single-factor and simplicity conclusions whenever the core is nonempty.
These five rows exhaust the cases in which the full large-slope exterior set is one orbit under complex conjugation, by the formula for \(t_s\), the real-root classification, and the [large-slope pole allocation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation).

For \(s=4\) the [quartic phase count](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#phase) gives \(a\) exterior roots at \(b\ge6a\).
For odd \(s=3,5,7\) and \(a=1\) there is an exterior root for all \(b\ge60\).
For a rational disk certificate choose \(z=(-877+745i)/1000,(-1078+497i)/1000,(-1117+363i)/1000\) for the three \(s\), respectively.
Direct substitution gives \(1.15<|z|<1.19\), \(|B_s(z)|<0.003\) and \(|B'_s(z)|>2.6\) in each case.
On \(|w-z|\le0.01\) one has \(|B_s''(w)|<202\) by bounding the two monomials at radius \(1.25\).
On the boundary the linear Taylor term therefore dominates the constant and the remainder, and \(|B_s(w)|>0.026-0.003-0.0101>0.01\).
The disk contains one zero of \(B_s\), \(|w|>1.14\), and \(|g_s(w)|<1.25^7+1.25+1<8\).
Since \(1.14^{60}>800\), Rouché gives an exterior zero of \(F_{1,b}\) there for all the stated \(b\).

For completeness the finite verification uses only integer power sums.
Multiplying the cyclotomic factors in the table gives \(-F_{a,b}\) in every listed entry.
For the other entries write \(d=sa+b\), \(-F_{a,b}=\sum_{i=0}^d A_i X^{d-i}\), \(S_0=d\), and use Newton's recursion
\[
 S_k=-\sum_{i=1}^{\min(k-1,d)} A_iS_{k-i}
       -\begin{cases}kA_k&k\le d,\\0&k>d.\end{cases}
\]
The following short loop gives the complete integer certificate for the remaining range.
A power sum of absolute value greater than \(d\) requires an exterior root.

```python
from math import gcd

rows = [(3, 1, 60), (4, 1, 6), (4, 2, 12), (5, 1, 60), (7, 1, 60)]
for s, a, cutoff in rows:
    exceptional = []
    witnesses = []
    for b in range(1, cutoff):
        if gcd(a, b) > 1:
            continue
        d = s*a + b
        A = [0] * (d + 1)
        for exponent, sign in [(0, 1), (a, 1), (d-a, 1), (d, 1),
                               (s*a, -1), (b, -1)]:
            A[d-exponent] += sign
        S = [d]
        for k in range(1, 28):
            value = -sum(A[i]*S[k-i] for i in range(1, min(k-1, d)+1))
            S.append(value - (k*A[k] if k <= d else 0))
            if abs(S[k]) > d:
                witnesses.append(k)
                break
        else:
            exceptional.append(b)
    print(s, a, exceptional, max(witnesses))
```

The exceptional lists are exactly the third-column exponents in the displayed order, and the respective largest first witnesses are \(26,4,16,22,27\).
For all sufficiently large \(b\) in these rows, the [power-substitution theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#statement) also makes the noncyclotomic core of every common dilation a single irreducible factor.
Rows with several exterior conjugation orbits can still split under the pole count alone.

## Independent prime powers in the degree of a rational factor

Fix \(s\ge3,\ 1\le t<s\) and an odd positive squarefree integer \(c\) divisible by a prime \(p_0\equiv3,5\bmod8\).
Take the reciprocal seed
\[
 B(T)=T^s-c(T^t+1),\qquad A(T)=T^sB(1/T)=1-c(T^{s-t}+T^s).
\]
For any odd positive \(a\) and positive \(b\) with \(N=sa+b=2^j n\), \(n\) odd and \(j\ge J=\max\{2+v_2(t),v_2(2s-t)\}\), put
\[
 F(X)=A(X^a)+X^bB(X^a),\qquad D=2^j\prod_{p\mid c}p^{v_p(N)}.
\]
Every rational irreducible factor of \(F\) has degree divisible by \(D/2^j\), and every reciprocal factor has degree divisible by \(D\).
There is at least one reciprocal irreducible factor.
In particular, **the whole polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) whenever every odd prime divisor of \(N\) divides \(c\)**.
These conclusions hold at every positive slope and allow a common divisor of \(a,b\).

For \(p\mid c\), put \(e=p^{v_p(N)}\) and \(M=N/e\).
Modulo \(p\), \(F=(X^M+1)^e\), and \(X^M+1\) is separable.
We show that a root of \(X^M+1\) has its entire multiplicity \(e\) in one rational irreducible factor.
Work in the ring of integers of a finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) whose residue field contains all roots of \(X^M+1\).
Each residue root lifts by Hensel's lemma to \(\xi\) with \(\xi^M=-1\), so \(\xi^N=-1\).
The order of the residue root has two-part exactly \(2^{j+1}\).
Writing \(u=\xi^a\), direct substitution gives
\[
 F(\xi)=-c\big(u^{s-t}+u^s-u^{-s}-u^{-(s-t)}\big)
       =-c u^{-s}(1+u^t)(u^{2s-t}-1).
\]
Since \(a\) is odd, the residue of \(u\) still has two-part \(2^{j+1}\) in its order.
The residue of \(u^t\) has two-part at least eight in its order, and that of \(u^{2s-t}\) has two-part at least two, so both displayed binomial factors are units.
Thus \(F(\xi)\) has normalized \(p\)-adic valuation one.
In a monic integral factorization, each occurrence of a factor reducing to zero at the chosen residue root contributes at least one to that valuation, including repeated occurrences of the same factor.
Only one can contribute, proving the full multiplicity assertion.
Consequently \(e\) divides the degree of every rational factor, and multiplying over \(p\mid c\) proves the odd divisibility.

For a reciprocal factor, its residue roots at \(p_0\), counted with multiplicity, are stable under Frobenius and inversion.
Both actions preserve the multiplicities.
Raising the roots to the odd exponent \(M/2^j\), now with \(p=p_0\), maps each orbit equivariantly into the primitive \(2^{j+1}\)-st roots.
The two actions generate a transitive group on this latter set.
Indeed, choose the sign with \(w=\pm p_0\equiv5\bmod8\).
Then \(v_2(w-1)=2\), and squaring repeatedly gives \(v_2(w^{2^h}-1)=h+2\) since each new factor \(w^{2^h}+1\) has valuation one.
Thus \(w\) generates the \(2^{j-1}\) units congruent to one modulo four modulo \(2^{j+1}\), and adjoining \(-1\) gives all the units.
The image of each orbit is therefore the entire set of \(2^j\) primitive roots, with equal fiber sizes by transitivity.
Hence \(2^j\) divides the degree of every reciprocal factor.

On the upper unit semicircle the function \(e^{-iN\theta/2}F(e^{i\theta})\) is real by reciprocity.
It takes the value \(2-4c<0\) at zero.
At \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/2^{v_2(t)+1})\) one has \(\zeta^{at}=-1\) and \(\zeta^{N/2}=1\) since \(a\) is odd and \(j\ge v_2(t)+2\).
Each pair of terms with coefficient \(-c\) cancels, so the normalized function is \(2\) there.
An intermediate zero gives a circle root, whose rational irreducible factor is reciprocal because complex conjugation takes the root to its inverse.
This proves the asserted existence, and when \(D=N\) its degree exhausts the parent.
The polynomial also has a real root greater than one since \(F(1)<0\) and \(F\) is monic.
Thus the irreducible polynomials in this family are nontorsion.

For any prescribed finite set of odd primes choose \(c\) as the product of its members together with \(3\), using each prime once.
One fixed seed now gives irreducibility for all degrees \(2^j\prod_{p\mid c}p^{k_p}\), with independent \(j\ge J\) and \(k_p\ge0\).
Every positive odd \(a<N/s\) coprime to \(N\) gives a primitive direction \(b=N-sa\).
For example, along any such degrees tending to infinity one can choose \(a\equiv1\bmod 2c\) within \(2c\) of \(\sqrt N\), giving \(a\sim\sqrt N\) and \(b/a\sim\sqrt N\).
Taking \(t=s-1\) gives the exact six exponent positions of the [unit-coefficient sections](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#statement) before collisions.
Taking \(t=1\) gives \(J=2\) at every seed degree.
The denominator has simple roots since it is Eisenstein at \(p_0\).
The two blocks are coprime and have no circle roots.
Indeed a common root \(z\) would make both \(z,z^{-1}\) algebraic integers and would give \(z^t+z^{-t}=c^{-2}-2\), a nonintegral rational number.
A circle root would be common by conjugation and reciprocity.
Here the odd prime divisibility comes from the chosen coefficient \(c\), so it gives no such input at \(c=1\).
On the smooth degrees with \(b/a\to\infty\) this construction has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log c-o(1)\).
Indeed, for each exterior zero \(\tau\) of \(B\), each of its \(a\) lifts \(\xi^a=\tau\) gives a distinct root \(\xi\exp(u/a)\) with \(|u|=O_{s,c}(|\tau|^{-b/a})\).
After substituting this expression in \(X^{-b}F\), simplicity makes \(B(\tau e^u)\) dominate the other term on the circle \(|u|=K|\tau|^{-b/a}\) for sufficiently large fixed \(K\), exactly as in the [pole-lift estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#poles).
Summing the positive logarithms over these roots gives \(m(B)-o(1)\), and \(m(B)\ge\log|B(0)|=\log c\).
