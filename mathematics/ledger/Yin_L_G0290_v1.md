### 2. Exact truncated-section reformulation

Let a=log2 and let z be an attained endpoint.
Put s=X^T Q^+ z, so Xs=z and s is orthogonal to ker X.
Each original digit word has probability 2^(-b) exp[-a||xi||^2].
Therefore

    mu(z)=2^(-b) exp[-a||s||^2]
          sum_{xi in Z^b intersect [-1,1]^b, Xxi=z}
              exp[-a||xi-s||^2].

Here ||s||^2=z^TQ^+z, including rank-deficient X.
Orthogonal Pythagoras proves this identity directly.
Thus (C1) is EXACTLY the assertion that this translated, truncated kernel sum is at most

    sum_{xi in ker X intersect Z^b intersect [-1,1]^b} exp[-a||xi||^2].

Replacing either finite section by a full lattice theta series changes the problem.
In particular full-theta translation dominance alone does not compare the two removed tails.
This reformulation is an equality, not an accepted Gaussian domination theorem.

A restricted class follows immediately without that missing assertion: if X is injective on the entire ternary cube, each attained z has a unique word xi and the zero fiber contains only0.
Consequently mu(z)/mu(0)=2^(-||xi||^2)<=2^(-||s||^2), since s is its orthogonal projection.
This proves (C1) for that class only.

### 3. A complete sharp theorem for one integer relation

**Theorem.**
(C1) holds for every integer column matrix X whose real kernel has dimension at most one.
More generally it holds when ker X has a basis of integer vectors with pairwise disjoint supports.
No bound on the column size, ambient output dimension or primitive relation coefficients is assumed.

We first prove the one-relation assertion.
The zero-kernel case is injective and was proved above.
Otherwise choose a primitive integer generator k of ker X intersect Z^b.
Changing signs and permuting coordinates preserves the original law and the Euclidean metric, so its nonzero entries may be taken positive.
Coordinates outside its support give fixed independent digit factors in each fiber, and add exactly their squared norm to z^TQ^+z; they can be removed.
If max k_j>=3, two distinct ternary words cannot differ by a nonzero integer multiple of k, since their coordinate differences are bounded by2.
This is again the injective case.
It remains to treat primitive k with entries in{1,2}.

Let r be the number of entries2 and s the number of entries1.
In the case r>=1, primitivity implies s>=1.
The zero fiber is only0.
A nonsingleton fiber has exactly two words, since the coordinates corresponding to2 force their values to be respectively1 and-1.
At the s other coordinates the first word has h ones and s-h zeros; the second has h zeros and s-h negative ones.
Put D=4r+s and x=h-s/2.
The two-word mass relative to the zero atom and the minimal squared preimage norm are exactly

    mu(z)/mu(0)=2^(-r-h)+2^(-r-s+h)
               =2^(1-r-s/2) cosh(a x),
    E=z^TQ^+z=r+h-(2r+h)^2/D=s/4-x^2/D.

A singleton fiber has relative mass2^(-||xi||^2)<=2^(-E), so only these double fibers matter.
The desired logarithmic difference is

    f(x)=a(r+s/4-1+x^2/D)-log cosh(a x),
                  |x|<=s/2.

We verify f(x)>=0 on the WHOLE real interval, which includes all required h.
The function is even,

    f'(x)=a[2x/D-tanh(a x)],
    f''(x)=2a/D-a^2 sech^2(a x).

For x>=0, f'' is increasing and f'(0)=0.
Moreover

    tanh(a s/2)=(2^s-1)/(2^s+1)>=s/(s+4)>=s/D,

because 4(2^s-1)>=2s for integer s>=1.
Hence f'(s/2)<=0.
An increasing f'' makes f' first decrease and then increase (allowing either empty phase); with both endpoint derivatives nonpositive, f'<=0 throughout.
Thus f is minimized at s/2.
At that endpoint,

    f(s/2)=4a r^2/(4r+s)-log(1+2^(-s)).

The first term increases with r>=1, so it is at least4a/(s+4).
For integer s>=1, (s+4)2^(-s)<=5/2<4a; the first inequality follows by the strictly decreasing consecutive ratio(s+5)/(2(s+4)), and the second follows from log2>=2/3 by midpoint convexity of1/t on[1,2].
Since log(1+v)<=v, this proves f(s/2)>0 and the whole double-fiber case.

Now suppose r=0, so k=(1,...,1) on s coordinates.
For s=1 the projected output on this support is identically0, and the assertion is immediate.
For s>=2 the zero fiber consists of0,k,-k and has normalized mass1+2^(1-s), after dividing by2^(-b).
Every other nonsingleton fiber has exactly two words with h entries1 and s-h entries0 in the first word, where1<=h<=s-1.
A three-word fiber would be exactly the zero fiber.
With x=h-s/2, its relative mass is

    2^(1-s/2) cosh(a x)/(1+2^(1-s)),

and E=s/4-x^2/s.
Singleton fibers again satisfy(C1), since the central normalized mass is at least1.
The logarithmic difference is now

    g(x)=a(s/4-1+x^2/s)+log(1+2^(1-s))-log cosh(a x),
                  |x|<=s/2-1.

Its derivatives have the same form as f with D=s.
At the right endpoint,

    tanh[a(s/2-1)]=(2^(s-2)-1)/(2^(s-2)+1)>=1-2/s,

equivalent to2^(s-2)+1>=s, which holds for all integer s>=2 by induction.
The same increasing-second-derivative argument shows g is minimized at the endpoint (the interval is a singleton for s=2).
There the required inequality becomes

    (1+2^(2-s))/(1+2^(1-s)) <= 2^(1/s).

For s=2 it is (4/3)^2<=2; for s=3 it is (6/5)^3<=2.
For s>=4,

    log[(1+2^(2-s))/(1+2^(1-s))]
       =log[1+2^(1-s)/(1+2^(1-s))]
       <=2^(1-s)<=a/s,

since s2^(1-s)<=1/2<log2 (and this sequence decreases for s>=4).
This proves every remaining case and the one-relation theorem.

The argument keeps every fiber mass, including its central multiplicity.
It does not assume scalar log concavity, replace the integer lattice by a real integral, or use an unproved conditional Gaussian moment bound.
No numerical experiment is needed for this exact proof.

### 4. Disjoint relation blocks and actual first-relation windows

Suppose ker X has integer generators k_1,...,k_l with mutually disjoint supports I_1,...,I_l.
Their real span is the whole kernel, by hypothesis.
For an attained z, fix any word xi in its fiber.
Every other word is obtained by independently changing the coordinates on each I_j by a real multiple of k_j subject to integrality and the ternary cube.
The integer generator on each support may be made primitive, so these changes are precisely integer multiples.
Coordinates outside the supports are fixed.
Thus every fiber is the Cartesian product of these one-relation block fibers and singleton free-coordinate fibers.
The zero fiber factors in the same way.
The orthogonal projection to(ker X)^perp is the direct sum of the corresponding block projections and those free coordinates, since the supports are disjoint.
Therefore its squared norm E is the sum of the block squared norms and free-coordinate squares.
Multiplying the one-relation bounds proves(C1) for the full matrix.
This also proves the asserted tensorization without making an independence claim about the original output coordinates.

Let alpha be an actual nonzero algebraic integer of degree d, with monic minimal polynomial P.
In the integer power basis, the b-column map(1,alpha,...,alpha^(b-1)) has trivial kernel for b<=d and a one-dimensional real/integer relation kernel for b=d+1, generated by the primitive coefficient vector of P.
Multiplication by alpha^i, for ANY finite prefix i>=0, is invertible over the real algebra generated by alpha, so it changes neither the digit fibers nor the projection metric E.
Likewise changing to the full real Minkowski coordinates is an injective real linear change on the column span and does not change E: both expressions are the squared norm of the unique minimal real preimage of the same endpoint.
Consequently the sharp original-law profile is UNCONDITIONAL for all these actual first-relation blocks, with any prefix, irrespective of neutral conjugates or the sizes of P's coefficients.

A larger prescribed family follows.
If the actual minimal polynomial has the form P(t)=R(t^q), with degree d=q e, then for b<=d+q the polynomial relations among the first b powers are spanned by t^jP(t), 0<=j<b-d.
These have disjoint residue-class supports modulo q; when b<=d there are no such relations.
Polynomial division by monic P proves that there are no additional relations of degree<b.
Hence(C1) holds for EVERY b<=d+q and every prefix on this actual family.
Irreducibility of the given P is part of it being the minimal polynomial; no claim that arbitrary R(t^q) is irreducible is made.

Now apply precisely L286's conditional completion theorem only where its newly proved hypothesis holds.
