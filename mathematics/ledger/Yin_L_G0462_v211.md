# A signed pole resultant joins modular factors of the quartic sections

<a id="sign"></a>
## The sign forced at the two real poles

Let \(b>18\), \(3\nmid b\), \(D(X)=X^{12}+X^9-1\), \(A(X)=1+X^3-X^{12}\), and \(H_b=A+X^bD\).
The [quartic root counts](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#quartic) give a single negative exterior root \(-\sigma\) and one nonreal exterior pair.
The two real zeros of \(D\) are \(\xi=-\beta_4^{1/3}<-1\) and \(0<\eta<1\), by the [quartic pole calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#quartic) and the unique positive root of \(t^4+t^3-1\).
The [real root comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#real) gives \(\sigma>|\xi|\) for even \(b\) and \(\sigma<|\xi|\) for odd \(b\).

Every monic integral divisor \(P\) of \(H_b\) satisfies \(|\operatorname{Res}(D,P)|=1\).
Indeed at \(D(X)=0\) we have \(A(X)=X^3(1+X^6)\), and the product of all twelve zeros of \(D\) is \(-1\).
Putting \(t=X^3\), the product of \(1+t^2\) over the four zeros of \(t^4+t^3-1\) is one, since this quartic takes the values \(-i,i\) at \(i,-i\).
Each \(t\) has three lifts, so \(\operatorname{Res}(D,H_b)=-1\).

If \(P\) is the monic reciprocal factor isolating \(-\sigma\) in a split, then
\[
                         \operatorname{Res}(D,P)=(-1)^{b+1}.
\]
Indeed \(P(\eta)>0\), and \(P(\xi)\) is negative precisely when \(-\sigma<\xi<-\sigma^{-1}\), which occurs precisely for even \(b\).
All its other roots are nonreal, and all nonreal zeros of \(D\) enter the resultant in conjugate pairs of positive product.

For odd \(b\) a stronger conclusion holds for every monic reciprocal integral divisor of \(H_b/(X+1)\): its pole resultant equals \(+1\).
The [circle count](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#phase) makes the root \(-1\) of \(H_b\) simple, and \(H_b(1)=2\).
The only remaining real roots are the negative inverse pair, both strictly to the right of \(\xi\).
A monic reciprocal divisor has even degree here, and is positive both at \(\xi\) and at \(\eta\).

<a id="trace"></a>
## A resultant in the trace variable

For \(Y=X+X^{-1}\), write \(C_j(Y)=X^j+X^{-j}\), with \(C_0=2,\ C_1=Y,\ C_{j+1}=YC_j-C_{j-1}\).
For any monic trace polynomial \(f\) of degree \(k\), put \(P_f(X)=X^k f(X+X^{-1})\).
Then
\[
 W=C_{12}+C_9-C_3-3,\qquad
 \operatorname{Res}(D,P_f)=(-1)^k\operatorname{Res}(W,f).
\]
Indeed \(W\) is the monic product of \(Y-(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) over the zeros \(\alpha\) of \(D\), as follows by multiplying \(D(X)D(X^{-1})=-W(Y)\) and using \(\prod\alpha=-1\).
The same formula holds upon reduction modulo a prime.
Thus at an odd prime, modular trace factors can be grouped by their degrees and their signed pole resultants, with multiplicity and multiplication of residues inside each group.
For odd \(b\), a rational trace factor can lift only a group of product \(+1\).

## The degree-780 core at \(b=769\)

This direction lies beyond the [480 primitive directions through 720](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0175/readme.md#census).
The exact factorization is \(H_{769}=(X+1)R\), where \(R\) is an irreducible reciprocal polynomial of degree 780.
To see this, form the monic trace \(T\) of \(R=H_{769}/(X+1)\), so \(R=X^{390}T(X+X^{-1})\).
In \(\mathbb F_3[Y]\), \(T=fq\) with \(f=Y^6+Y^2+Y+1\) and \(q\) of degree 384, both irreducible.
The signed pole resultant of each is \(2\) modulo three.
Although the modular degrees permit proper rational trace factors of degrees 6 and 384, each candidate violates the positive sign from [the real poles](#sign).
Thus \(T\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The [circle count](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#phase) gives \(769+6\) simple circle roots of \(H_{769}\), so \(T\) has a root \(y\in(-2,2)\).
In the real field \(\mathbb Q(y)\) the discriminant \(y^2-4\) is negative and cannot be a square, proving that a root of \(X^2-yX+1\) has rational degree \(2\cdot390\).
Thus \(R\) is irreducible and contains all three exterior roots.

For reproducible arithmetic, use the polynomial coefficients \(R_j=[X^j]R\) and compute \(T=R_{390}+\sum_{j=1}^{390} R_{390+j}C_j\) modulo three.
Division by the displayed \(f\) gives zero remainder.
For each row the last column is \(\deg\gcd(h,Y^{3^j}-Y)\), in the listed order:

| \(h\) | \(j\) | Degrees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(f\) | \(2,3,6\) | \(0,0,6\) |
| \(q=T/f\) | \(128,192,384\) | \(0,0,384\) |

These entries certify irreducibility because \(2,3\) are the distinct prime divisors of each of 6 and 384, and \(Y^{3^j}-Y\) contains precisely the irreducibles of degrees dividing \(j\), once each.
Each row can be reproduced by starting at \(u=Y\), replacing \(u\) by \(u^3\bmod h\) at every step, and taking the monic gcd of \(h,u-Y\) at the stated indices.
Modulo three one has \(W=Y^{12}+Y^9+2Y^6+2Y^3+2\), with remainder \(2Y^5+Y^2+1\) modulo \(f\), and the Euclidean resultant gives \(\operatorname{Res}(W,f)=2\).
Both modular degrees are even and their product of pole resultants is \(+1\) by the real sign for \(R\), so the second residue is also \(2\).

## A forced companion in an infinite progression

For every odd primitive \(b>18\), \(f\) occurs in the trace of \(H_b/(X+1)\) modulo three exactly when \(b\equiv39\bmod365\).
In that progression the rational trace factor containing \(f\) must contain another modular constituent of pole resultant \(2\).
Indeed take a root \(y\) of \(f\) and either root \(z\) of \(X^2-yX+1\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_3\).
One has \(C_{365}=2\), \(C_{73}=Y^5+Y^4+Y^3+Y\) and \(C_5=Y^5+Y^3+2Y\) modulo \(f\), by the recurrence above.
Since \(z^j+z^{-j}=2\) exactly when \(z^j=1\), its order is \(365=5\cdot73\).
We know \(H_{769}(z)=0\), while \(D(z)\ne0\) by the unit resultant.
Consequently \(H_b(z)=0\) exactly when \(b\equiv769\equiv39\bmod365\), and removing \(X+1\) does not remove \(z\).
The reduction of \(H_b\) is squarefree since its derivative modulo three is \(bX^{b-1}D\), coprime to \(H_b\).
Thus the negative residue of \(f\) requires a distinct companion in the positive product for its rational trace factor.
The number and degrees of the other constituents can vary with \(b\), leaving splitting open in the rest of this progression.
