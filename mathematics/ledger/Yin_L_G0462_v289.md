# Periodic exponent sets for Lehmer’s degree-ten unit

<a id="density"></a>
## A period of nine and the limit of real domination

Let \(r>1\) be the real root of the [Lehmer polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v287.md#separation) \(H=X^{10}+X^9-X^7-X^6-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1\).
The union of the two classes \(0,4\pmod 9\) is admissible, meaning that all finite binary subset sums of its powers of \(r\) are distinct.
It improves the one-in-five spacing to
\[
              2/9\le D_1(H)\le \log_2 r.
\]
Here the upper bound is the [general density estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement).
The lower bound is the largest density obtainable by the sufficient condition that every retained power of \(r\) exceeds the sum of all preceding retained powers.
It is also the largest density of an admissible periodic set of any period at most 21, including sets whose powers fail that sufficient condition.

For the domination bound start with exponent zero and greedily take the earliest exponent whose power exceeds the sum so far.
For every other increasing list satisfying the same condition its first exponent is at least zero, and induction shows that each greedy exponent is at most the corresponding exponent in that list.
Indeed the new exponent of the other list lies beyond the preceding greedy exponent and its power exceeds even the preceding greedy sum.
Put \(x\) equal to the sum of all preceding powers divided by the current power.
The least positive gap \(g\) satisfies \(r^g>1+x\), after which the new ratio is \((1+x)/r^g\).
The first gap is one and the resulting ratio \(1/r\) belongs to \(I=[0.84,0.89]\).
On \(I\) the greedy gap is four and the resulting ratio lies in \(J=[0.96,0.99]\).
On \(J\) the gap is five and the resulting ratio lies in \(I\).
These rational inequalities follow from \(1.1762<r<1.1763\), checked below.
The greedy sequence therefore starts \(0,1,5,10,14,19,\ldots\) and has density \(2/9\).
Dropping zero and translating the remaining exponents down by one leaves the two claimed residue classes, and preserves domination.
For any finite signed relation supported there its largest term strictly dominates the sum of the smaller terms, proving admissibility.

## Every shorter-period improvement admits a relation

If an admissible periodic set of period \(p\) contains \(h\) residue classes, its natural density is \(h/p\le D_1(H)\le\log_2 r\).
For \(p\le21\), the inequalities \(2^{\lfloor 2p/9\rfloor+1}<(1.1763)^p\) leave only \(p=13\), with at most three selected residues.
There are fourteen orbits of triples modulo 13 under translation and reflection.
A relation on one representative gives a relation on every translate by multiplying by a monomial and on every reflection by taking the reciprocal and shifting, since \(H\) is reciprocal with nonzero constant term.
Every such triple supports a nonzero ternary multiple of \(H\) using at most seven periods.
The following code gives one multiple per orbit, listing its blocks from highest to lowest and the coefficients inside each block in increasing order of the three residues.
A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin found the relations on November 20, 2026 by pruning signed block prefixes at the real root, then checked the displayed words by integer polynomial division.
The exact verification uses standard Python.

```python
from fractions import Fraction as F
from itertools import combinations

H = (1, 1, 0, -1, -1, -1, -1, -1, 0, 1, 1)
lo, hi = F(11762, 10000), F(11763, 10000)
def evaluate(z):
    return sum(c*z**i for i, c in enumerate(H))
assert evaluate(lo) < 0 < evaluate(hi)
I, J = (F(84, 100), F(89, 100)), (F(96, 100), F(99, 100))
assert I[0] < 1/hi <= 1/lo < I[1]
for (a,b), g, (c,d) in [(I, 4, J), (J, 5, I)]:
    assert hi**(g-1) < 1+a and 1+b < lo**g
    assert c < (1+a)/hi**g <= (1+b)/lo**g < d
assert [p for p in range(1,22) if 2**(2*p//9+1) < hi**p] == [13]
assert hi**13 < 2**4

def representative(S):
    return min(tuple(sorted((s*x+b) % 13 for x in S))
               for s in (-1,1) for b in range(13))

words = {(0,1,a): '-+0 0-0 --0 -00 +-0' for a in range(2,8)}
words.update({
    (0,2,4): '-+0 0-- 0+0 --0 0+-',
    (0,2,5): '-+0 -0- ++- +++ 0-+',
    (0,2,6): '-+0 +-- -00 -00 +00 0+0 +--',
    (0,2,7): '-+0 00- 000 00- 00- -++',
    (0,3,6): '--+ +-0 +0+ 0-+ +--',
    (0,3,7): '--+ +-- +0- 0-0 +--',
    (0,3,8): '-+0 0-- -+0 ++0 +-0 -00 +--',
    (0,4,8): '--+ 0-- -0- --0 +--',
})
assert set(words) == {representative(S) for S in combinations(range(13),3)}
for S, blocks in words.items():
    digits = blocks.split()
    polynomial = [0] * (13*len(digits))
    for i, block in enumerate(reversed(digits)):
        for j, symbol in zip(S,block,strict=True):
            polynomial[13*i+j] = {'+':1, '0':0, '-':-1}[symbol]
    assert any(polynomial) and len(digits) <= 7
    while len(polynomial) >= len(H):
        leading = polynomial.pop()
        offset = len(polynomial) - len(H) + 1
        for j, c in enumerate(H[:-1]):
            polynomial[offset+j] -= leading*c
    assert not any(polynomial)
```
