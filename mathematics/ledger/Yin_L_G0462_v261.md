# Disk zeros, poles and coefficient size forced by repeated signed contact

<a id="ray"></a>
## Exact inertia on a common ray at every exponent

Fix an integer \(p\ge2\), a sign \(\epsilon\), and a nonzero integral polynomial \(A\) of formal width \(b\), meaning \(\deg A\le b\).
Set \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\) and \(G(X)=A(X^p)+\epsilon X A_b^*(X^p)\), as in the [single-polynomial criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v416.md#statement).
Suppose \(G\) has contact of order at least \(m_i\ge1\) at distinct points \(x_i\) with \(0<|x_i|<1\), with \(A(x_i^p)\ne0\) at every point.
Write \(M=\sum_i m_i\).
If all \(x_i\) lie on a common ray from zero, the reduced rational function
\[
                           R(Y)=-\frac{A(Y)}{\epsilon A_b^*(Y)}
\]
has at least \(\lceil M/2\rceil\) zeros and at least \(\lfloor M/2\rfloor\) poles in the open unit disk, counted with multiplicity.
In particular \(b\ge M\), and for a single point this holds for every argument and every multiplicity.

At \(z_i=x_i^p\) the denominator is nonzero by the graph equation and the numerator assumption.
The germ of \(R(u^p)-u\) vanishes to order \(m_i\) at \(x_i\).
Since \(p x_i^{p-1}\ne0\), \(R\) therefore agrees through order \(m_i-1\) with the local inverse of the power map at \(z_i\).
Writing \(x_i=e^{i\phi}r_i\), rotate the target map to \(e^{-i\phi}R(e^{ip\phi}Y)\).
At its positive nodes \(r_i^p\) the inverse germs are all \(Y^\alpha\), with \(\alpha=1/p\) and the branch positive on the positive real interval.
Thus the Hermitian matrix of the kernel \((1-R(z)\overline{R(w)})/(1-z\bar w)\) on the rotated value and derivative conditions is the confluent matrix of
\[
                 K(z,w)=\frac{1-z^\alpha w^\alpha}{1-zw},
                 \qquad 0<z,w<1.
\]
Here and below each derivative of order \(k\) can be divided by \(k!\), which preserves inertia.

For \(0<\alpha<1\) let \(c_\alpha=(\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}/(1+t)\,dt)^{-1}>0\).
Scaling this integral gives \(s^\alpha=c_\alpha\int_0^\infty s\,t^{\alpha-1}/(t+s)\,dt\) for \(s>0\).
Its divided difference at \(z,1/w\) gives
\[
          K(z,w)=c_\alpha w^\alpha
                     \int_0^\infty\frac{t^\alpha\,dt}{(t+z)(1+tw)}.   \tag{1}
\]
For increasing distinct \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\) and \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), expand the determinant of (1), permute the integration variables and integrate on \(0<t_1<\cdots<t_n\).
This gives the integral of
\(\det(1/(z_i+t_j))\det(1/(1+t_iw_j))\prod_j t_j^\alpha\), multiplied by \(c_\alpha^n\prod_j w_j^\alpha\).
The finite expansions and changes of integration order are justified by the integrability of the products in (1).
With \(\Delta(v)=\prod_{i<j}(v_j-v_i)\), the ordinary Cauchy formulas give
\[
 \frac{\det(K(z_i,w_j))}{\Delta(z)\Delta(w)}
  =(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}c_\alpha^n\prod_j w_j^\alpha
    \int_{t_1<\cdots<t_n}
    \frac{\Delta(t)^2\prod_j t_j^\alpha\,dt_j}
    {\prod_{i,j}(z_i+t_j)(1+t_iw_j)},\qquad t_j>0.                   \tag{2}
\]
In particular its absolute value is strictly positive.

Let the nodes coalesce in prescribed groups on either side.
On compact subintervals of \((0,1)\) the absolute integrand is bounded by a constant times \(\prod_j t_j^\alpha/(1+t_j)^2\), using \(\Delta(t)^2\le\prod_j(1+t_j)^{2(n-1)}\).
The limiting integral is still strictly positive.
Successive divided differences in each group turn the corresponding rows or columns into normalized derivatives in the limit.
Only positive differences between different groups remain from the two Vandermonde products.
Thus the sign of every such confluent determinant remains \((-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\).
Order the node groups in increasing order, listing jets in each group in increasing derivative order.
The same argument applies to every leading principal submatrix as its last group may be truncated.
Successive determinant ratios therefore have alternating signs starting with positive.
Symmetric elimination gives inertia exactly \((\lceil M/2\rceil,\lfloor M/2\rfloor)\).

## Passing from the kernel to the reduced rational function

The original numerator and denominator have equal moduli on the unit circle.
Their multiplicities at a point of that circle also agree, since the reciprocal sends the point to its complex conjugate and the coefficients are real.
All circle zeros cancel on reduction.
Write the reduced map as \(R=\gamma B_1/B_2\), where \(|\gamma|=1\) and \(B_1,B_2\) are the finite Blaschke products of its zeros and poles inside the disk.
Indeed, after these factors are removed the remaining function and its reciprocal are holomorphic in the closed disk with modulus one on the boundary, so the maximum principle makes it constant.
For one Blaschke factor \(b_a(z)=(z-a)/(1-\bar a z)\) its disk kernel is the positive rank-one product
\[
          \frac{1-b_a(z)\overline{b_a(w)}}{1-z\bar w}
             =\frac{1-|a|^2}{(1-\bar a z)(1-a\bar w)}.
\]
The identity \(K_{BC}=K_B+B(z)\overline{B(w)}K_C\) shows that any such product has positive semidefinite kernel matrices of rank at most its number of factors, also on jets by differentiating the finite sums of rank-one products.
Multiplying \(K_R\) by \(B_2(z)\overline{B_2(w)}\) gives \(K_{B_1}-K_{B_2}\).
This changes the jet matrices by an invertible triangular congruence at nodes where \(R\) has no pole.
Its positive and negative indices are thus bounded by the respective numbers of interior zeros and poles.
They together do not exceed \(b\), since the degree of the reduced quotient of the two Blaschke products is the sum of their factor counts.
There is no cancellation across the numerator and denominator in that representation because their interior divisor sets are disjoint and outside divisors are reciprocals of the reflected interior ones, including zero and infinity.

## Arbitrary arguments at exponent two and a cubic counterexample

When \(p=2\), the conclusion for a common ray holds for arbitrary distinct contact points in the punctured disk.
In the original \(u,v\) variables the jet matrix of the pulled-back kernel is that of
\[
                  \frac{1-u\bar v}{1-u^2\bar v^2}
                         =\frac1{1+u\bar v}.
\]
The change of coordinates \(u\mapsto u^2\) has invertible derivative at each point, and actual nonvanishing graph data force distinct powered nodes since \(R(x_i^p)=x_i\).
For distinct points the Cauchy determinant is
\((-1)^{n(n-1)/2}|\Delta(u)|^2/\prod_{i,j}(1+u_i\bar u_j)\).
The denominator is strictly positive, its off-diagonal factors occurring in conjugate pairs.
Dividing by the within-group Vandermonde factors and their conjugates gives the same sign for each confluent principal determinant.
This again proves the exact inertia on all the jets.

For \(p=3\), even three real conjugates away from a common ray can give inertia \((1,2)\) on their values.
Take the roots \(v_i\) of \(T^3-3T+1\), three distinct nonzero real numbers with sum zero.
The cubic is irreducible by the rational root test.
Put \(x_i=r v_i\) for positive rational \(r\) sufficiently small.
The kernel at these values has coefficients \(+1,-1,+1,-1,\ldots\) on the successive powers \(0,1,3,4,6,7,\ldots\) of \(x_i x_j\), by expansion of \((1-t)/(1-t^3)\).
Expanding its principal determinants by Cauchy–Binet is legitimate by absolute convergence inside the disk.
The first possible third-order term uses exponents \(0,1,3\).
Its generalized Vandermonde vanishes since \(v_i^3=3v_i-1\).
The next term, with \(0,1,4\), has positive coefficient and nonzero determinant since \(v_i^4=3v_i^2-v_i\).
The order-two leading term uses \(0,1\) and is negative.
Thus the three successive pivots have signs \(+,-,-\) for all sufficiently small \(r\).
These value data can occur for integral signed graphs.
At the six distinct nodes \(z_i=x_i^3,z_i^{-1}\) prescribe \(A(z_i^{-1})=1\) and \(A(z_i)=-\epsilon x_i z_i^5\).
The unique interpolant of degree at most five is rational by Galois symmetry, and clearing denominators realizes the data with nonzero numerator at each node.
The example concerns the value submatrix and supplies no coefficient cutoff.

## A coefficient bound uniform in the row width

If a fixed \(x\) with \(0<|x|=r<1\) is a zero of \(G\) of order at least \(m\ge2\), then
\[
       \log\|A\|_1
           \ \ge\ \lfloor m/2\rfloor p\log(1/r)-O_{m,r}(\log(2p)).    \tag{3}
\]
This bound allows arbitrary widths, and even allows numerator cancellation at the root.
First remove the largest common monomial \(Y^j\) of \(A,A_b^*\), replacing \(A\) by \(A/Y^j\) and \(b\) by \(b-2j\).
Both original signed rows are the new rows times \(Y^j\).
The one-norm and multiplicity at \(x\) are unchanged, and at least one of the two constant coefficients is now a nonzero integer.

List the allowed graph exponents in increasing order as \(\nu_{2k}=kp,\ \nu_{2k+1}=kp+1\).
The graph is nonzero since these two exponent classes are disjoint.
A polynomial supported on at most \(m\) distinct exponents cannot have a nonzero root with multiplicity \(m\), by the Vandermonde system from the first \(m\) Euler derivatives.
Thus \(\nu_m\) exists in the allowed list of the normalized graph.
Choose \(j=0\) or \(1\) for which the coefficient \(g_{\nu_j}\) is nonzero.
Apply \(\prod_{0\le i<m,\,i\ne j}(X\,d/dX-\nu_i)\) and evaluate at \(x\), obtaining zero.
Among exponents below \(\nu_m\) only the chosen term survives, and its multiplier has absolute value at least one.
At \(n=\nu_m+l\), \(l\ge0\), the absolute multiplier is at most \(n^{m-1}\le(1+\nu_m)^{m-1}(1+l)^{m-1}\).
Since \(\sup_{l\ge0}(1+l)^{m-1}r^l<\infty\) and \(\|G\|_1=2\|A\|_1\), summing the tail gives
\[
    r^{\nu_j}\le |g_{\nu_j}|r^{\nu_j}
       \le C_{m,r}\|A\|_1(1+\nu_m)^{m-1}r^{\nu_m}.
\]
Now \(\nu_m-\nu_j\ge\lfloor m/2\rfloor p-1\) and \(\nu_m\le mp+1\), which proves (3).
For example, when \(r^{-\lfloor m/2\rfloor}>2\), the height bound excludes every graph with this fixed contact from the cutoff \(2^p\) at all sufficiently large exponents.
The inertia condition gives a width restriction even for coefficients of unlimited size.
In either argument the estimates for a fixed disk point leave open passing sequences whose algebraic roots vary and approach the unit circle.
