### 1. A signature constraint independent of support and edge sizes

The classical external input used here is van der Blij's characteristic-vector congruence for an integral unimodular symmetric bilinear form: if w is characteristic, then (w,w) is congruent modulo8 to its real signature.
In particular every even unimodular form has signature divisible by8, by taking w=0.
The exact source is the original1959 paper, printed page292, equation(7); no definiteness hypothesis is imposed in that equation.
Its statement and applicability were checked directly in the three-page published scan.
The input is accepted as an established theorem; the original oscillatory-integral argument is not claimed to have received a complete analytic proof audit.
[vdB59-U001](../audits/literature/vdB59/Yin_U001.md#vdb59-u001) records that distinction.
The following discriminant-two deduction is written out locally, without using a lattice-classification theorem or the more general Milgram formula.

**Lemma.** If L is a nondegenerate even integral lattice with absolute determinant2, then its signature sigma satisfies sigma=1 or -1 modulo8.
In particular its rank is odd.

**Proof.** Here L is free abelian with the stated nondegenerate symmetric integral pairing, even means that every vector has even integral norm, and L* consists of rational vectors pairing integrally with L.
Its dual quotient L*/L has order2, by the determinant-index formula.
The induced bilinear pairing on this quotient is nondegenerate: a dual vector pairing integrally with every dual vector belongs to (L*)*=L.
For the nonzero class represented by g, it follows that (g,g)=1/2 modulo Z.
Consequently its norm modulo2Z is either1/2 or -1/2.
This norm modulo2Z is well defined, since replacing g by g+x with x in L changes it by2(g,x)+(x,x), an even integer.

Choose epsilon in{1,-1} so that (g,g)+epsilon/2 is an even integer.
Let e be orthogonal to L with (e,e)=2epsilon.
In the rational orthogonal sum enlarge L+Ze by the element g+e/2.
This produces an index-two lattice M: twice the new element lies in L+Ze, whereas its class is nonzero there.
Its pairings with the old lattice are integral, and the new element has even norm by the choice of epsilon.
Thus M is even integral, with determinant

    det M = det L * (2epsilon)/2^2 = epsilon det L/2 = 1 or -1.

It is nondegenerate and unimodular.
The accepted congruence gives sigma(M)=0 modulo8.
Passing to a finite-index overlattice does not change the real form, so sigma(M)=sigma(L)+epsilon.
Therefore sigma(L)=-epsilon modulo8, as claimed.
Signature and rank have the same parity, proving the last assertion.
This proof includes either determinant sign and arbitrary indefiniteness.

Now let V be any integral upper-unitriangular matrix of size7 satisfying

    det(tV+V^T)=(t+1)(t^6-t^5-t^3-t+1).                     (1)

There is no restriction on its support, upper-entry sizes, signs or vertex order.
The symmetric integral matrix C=V+V^T has every diagonal entry2 and

    det C = (1+1)P(1)=-2,    tr C=14.                       (2)

It is an even lattice form.
Its number k of negative eigenvalues is odd, because its determinant is negative.
For k=1,3,5,7 the possible signatures7-2k are5,1,-3,-7.
The lemma permits only1 or -7 in this list.
The latter would make C negative definite, contradicting (e_i,e_i)=2.
Thus every such realization necessarily has

    inertia(C)=(4 positive,3 negative).                     (3)

This is a necessary condition for all integral presentations of the exact degree-seven polynomial.
It is not restricted to the capped search or to a graph class, and it does not prove existence of a presentation with this inertia.

### 2. Every rank-seven realization has energy at least13

Write the negative eigenvalues of C as -a_1,-a_2,-a_3 and the positive ones as b_1,...,b_4, all a_i,b_j positive.
Put A=a_1+a_2+a_3.
By(2), the positive eigenvalues have sum14+A, and the product of all seven absolute values is2.
Arithmetic-geometric mean gives

    2 <= (A/3)^3 ((14+A)/4)^4.                              (4)

The right side is strictly increasing for A>0.
At A=2/3 it equals

    (2/9)^3 (11/3)^4 =117128/59049 <2.

Therefore A>2/3.
Cauchy-Schwarz on the two groups of eigenvalues yields

    tr(C^2) >= A^2/3+(14+A)^2/4
             > (2/3)^2/3+(44/3)^2/4 =1456/27.               (5)

Define the integral squared-entry energy E(V)=sum_(i<j)V_ij^2.
The diagonal of C consists of seven2s and its two strict triangles are the entries of V and their transposes, so tr(C^2)=28+2E(V).
Consequently

    E(V)>350/27,    hence E(V)>=13.                          (6)

The proof is independent of every coefficient of(1) except its degree and value at1.
More generally it proves the same inertia and energy bound for every rank-seven integral upper-unitriangular V with det(V+V^T)=-2.
Thus this is a reusable presentation obstruction for an entire endpoint class, not a finite exclusion table for P.

In particular no presentation with at most12 nonzero unit entries can realize(1).
The newly tested connected bicyclic support has eight unit entries and energy8, so it is excluded completely by(6), independently of the cap.
Any connected unit-entry rank-seven realization would require at least13 edges, hence cycle rank at least13-7+1=7.
Weighted presentations are constrained by the same energy but need not have that many support edges.
Neither condition is sufficient, and a higher-energy rank-seven presentation remains unresolved.
Energy is a coefficient cost, not a Mahler-measure lower bound for the absolute companion.

For other input sizes the mechanism retains an explicit form.
If an n by n upper-unitriangular integral V has |det(V+V^T)|=2, then n is odd and the signature n-2k is congruent to1 or -1 modulo8.
Whenever 0<k<n, put A equal to the sum of the absolute negative eigenvalues.
The same two elementary inequalities give

    2 <= (A/k)^k ((2n+A)/(n-k))^(n-k),
    E(V) >= [A^2/k+(2n+A)^2/(n-k)-4n]/2.                    (7)

These are necessary conditions after selecting the allowed inertia and determinant sign.
They do not identify the actual inertia in every rank, and no assertion is made that their coefficient cost controls absolute-cover measure or resolves unrestricted near-one coverage.
