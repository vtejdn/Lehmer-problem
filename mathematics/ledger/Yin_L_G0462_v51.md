# Sparse perturbations of the Chebyshev numerator cannot start at a positive fraction of its degree

<a id="stability"></a>
## A uniform gap even with varying middle coefficients

Let \(C\) be the [trace numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) of a positive-constant Pisot input on branch \(j=2\), of degree \(h\), and put
\[
       \Pi(z)=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1}),\qquad
       B^\sharp(z)=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1}) .
\]
For every fixed \(s\) and \(\epsilon>0\), the positive integer constant \(q\) is bounded on the following class:
\(\Pi\) has at most \(s\) nonzero coefficients and agrees with either \(2-z\) or \(2+z\) through index \(\lfloor\epsilon h\rfloor\).
In particular there is a uniform positive excess \(\theta-q\) on this class by the [derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
The positions and coefficients of the remaining terms can vary with \(h\).
This extends the [full exclusion for the unperturbed minus sign](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v8.md#statement).

Suppose instead that \(q\to\infty\) along such a sequence.
The degrees \(h\to\infty\) since \(q-1\mid C(2)\) and \(C(2)\le 2\cdot4^h\) by the location of its roots.
The measure of \(\Pi\) is two, since its endpoint coefficient has modulus two and all its zeros lie on the unit circle.
The [sparse coefficient bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) therefore bounds the coefficient heights solely in terms of \(s\), and hence bounds \(\|\Pi\|_\infty\) on the unit circle.
Choose a subsequence with fixed sign and replace the argument of \(\Pi,B^\sharp,K_q,J_q\) simultaneously by \(-z\) for the plus sign.
In both cases \(K_qB^\sharp=\Pi\), \(J_q=2/K_q\), and \(\Pi\) agrees with \(2-z\) through \(a=\lfloor\epsilon h\rfloor\).
Rotation preserves the signed measure's total variation, the integer Taylor coefficients, and the measure and reciprocal symmetry of \(B^\sharp\).
The only negative mass of the [signed measure for \(J_q\)](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) tends to zero.
The polynomial \(B^\sharp\) has Mahler measure strictly greater than one tending to one, since the exterior root of the [delay-one associate](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) tends to one.

<a id="limit"></a>
## The same fixed limits despite the changing middle terms

Pass to a locally convergent subsequence of \(K_q\) in the disk using the [fixed-branch comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit).
Its limit \(K\) has positive real part, constant two, integer coefficients, a nonzero continuous part in its Herglotz measure, and an eventually periodic coefficient sequence by the [strict moment theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#radial-limits) and [integer decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic).
These conclusions persist under \(z\mapsto-z\).
The first coefficient of \(K_q\) is odd by the coefficient of \(z\) in \(K_qB^\sharp=\Pi\).
The analytic rational function \(B_\infty=(2-z)/K\) has integer Taylor coefficients since the identity holds coefficientwise in the limit.
These are precisely the inputs of the [cyclotomic numerator reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#cyclotomic) and its [finite phase calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#phase).
In particular \(K\) belongs to the same nine-profile table and \(B_\infty=V/U_0\) with \(U_0,V\) fixed signed cyclotomic products normalized at zero.
No coefficient in the middle of \(\Pi\) enters this conclusion.

<a id="window"></a>
## A cutoff for the difference of the reciprocal series

Write \(J=2/K=B_\infty/(1-z/2)\).
The first discrepancy \(n\) of \(B^\sharp\) from \(B_\infty\) tends to infinity, with \(n=\infty\) allowed provisionally for equality.
We claim \(n/h\to0\).
Otherwise, after extraction, both \(B^\sharp\) and \(\Pi\) agree with their limits through \(A=\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) for some \(0<\eta<\min(\epsilon,1)\).
It follows from \(J_q\Pi=2B^\sharp\) that \(\Delta_q=J_q-J\) vanishes to order at least \(A+1\).
All its strictly positive Taylor coefficients are moments of a signed measure of bounded variation, uniformly in \(q\), by the signed measure formula for \(J_q\) and the positive Herglotz representation of its limit \(J\).
The constant difference is zero.

Choose \(L=2h+4+\deg U_0+\deg V\), which exceeds the degrees of \((2-z)U_0B^\sharp\) and \(V\Pi\).
There is an analytic polynomial \(T_q\) of bounded circle \(L^1\) norm agreeing with \(\Delta_q\) through degree \(3L\).
Indeed put \(b=\lfloor A/3\rfloor\) and take a cutoff on the integer indices equal to the convolution of the interval indicator \(1_{[A-b,3L+b]}\) with the uniform probability on \([-b,b]\).
Its support lies in the positive indices and its values are one on \([A,3L]\).
The corresponding trigonometric polynomial has \(L^1\) norm at most \(\sqrt{(3L-A+2b+1)/(2b+1)}=O_\eta(1)\) by Cauchy–Schwarz on the product of the two interval sums.
Multiplying the Fourier coefficients of the bounded measure by this cutoff gives \(T_q\), since indices below \(A\) at which the cutoff varies have zero coefficient.

The polynomial
\[
     S_q=(2-z)U_0 B^\sharp-V\Pi
              =\frac{(2-z)U_0}{2}\,\Pi\,\Delta_q
\]
has bounded \(L^1\) norm as well.
Replacing \(\Delta_q\) on the right by \(T_q\) gives an analytic polynomial of bounded \(L^1\) norm agreeing with \(S_q\) through degree \(3L\), since the other factors have bounded circle suprema.
To remove its possible higher frequencies, convolve with the trigonometric cutoff whose coefficients are \(1_{[-L,2L]}\) convolved with the uniform probability on \([-L,L]\).
This is one on \([0,L]\), zero above \(3L\), and its circle \(L^1\) norm is at most \(\sqrt{(3L+1)/(2L+1)}\).
The product before this last cutoff has zero coefficients on \((L,3L]\) by agreement with \(S_q\).
Thus the cutoff returns \(S_q\) exactly.
Since \(|2-z|\ge1\) on the circle and \(V\Pi\) is bounded there, we get \(\|U_0B^\sharp\|_1=O(1)\).
The [prime-grid Fourier algebra argument](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) then bounds the number of terms of this integral polynomial.
Its Mahler measure equals that of \(B^\sharp\), contradicting the fixed-support gap and proving the claim.

<a id="return"></a>
## Transfer of all harmonic restrictions

Now \(n\) is finite, \(n\to\infty\) and \(n/h\to0\), so \(kn+l\) lies strictly between zero and \(a\) eventually for every fixed \(k\ge1,\ l\in\mathbb Z\).
On that full window \(B^\sharp=(1-z/2)J_q\) as a coefficient identity.
The [first departure and density comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#departure) requires only this identity near \(n+l\), agreement of the earlier coefficients, integrality, the limit \(J\), and the positive lift after removal of a negative atom tending to zero in total mass.
It therefore gives the same six exclusions, including after the reflection, which moves the removed atom from 1 to \(-1\).
The proof of the [harmonic comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v52.md#harmonics) uses the same coefficient identity near each \(kn+l\) and extends the fiber moment constraints to every \(k\).
The [two phase contradictions](Yin_L_G0462_v52.md#slopes) and the [fourth-root specialization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v8.md#specialization), including its preceding binomial exclusion, then remove the other three profiles.
This contradiction proves the uniform assertion.
