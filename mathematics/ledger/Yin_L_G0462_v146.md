# Testing prime residue coverage after multiplication by cyclotomic factors

2026-11-18T05:16.

<a id="coverage"></a>
## Keeping the measure of the original irreducible polynomial

Consider a monic irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\).
Since \(P(0)=\pm1\), the gcd of differences between its occupied exponents equals the gcd of its positive occupied exponents.
If this gcd is \(q>1\), write \(P(x)=P_0(x^q)\).
The polynomial \(P_0\) is irreducible because any factorization would remain a factorization after substitution, and \(m(P_0)=m(P)\) by power substitution.
Its support differences have gcd one.
Hence we may work with \(P\) having this property.

Let \(C\) be any product of cyclotomic polynomials, including the empty product, and decompose \(CP\) modulo an odd prime \(p\) as
\[
 C(x)P(x)=\sum_{r\in R}x^r U_r(x^p),\qquad 0\le r<p .
\]
The row containing the leading coefficient of \(CP\) is monic, so the rows have joint content one and admit a monic gcd \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
The constant term of \(A\) is nonzero because \(U_0(0)=C(0)P(0)\ne0\).
If \(A\) had an irreducible noncyclotomic factor \(h\), every irreducible factor of \(h(x^p)\) would be noncyclotomic.
Indeed, a root of unity of \(h(x^p)\) would give a root of unity of \(h\) on raising it to the \(p\)-th power.
But \(h(x^p)\) divides \(CP\), whose only noncyclotomic irreducible factor is \(P\), with multiplicity one.
Consequently \(h(x^p)=P(x)\) up to sign, contrary to the gcd-one support differences.
Thus \(A\) is itself a product of cyclotomics and \(m(A)=0\).

The integral quotient \(\widetilde P=CP/A(x^p)\) has unit row gcd, the same occupied residues \(R\), and \(m(\widetilde P)=m(P)>0\).
A single occupied residue would leave a monomial after this division and is therefore impossible.
For any nonzero \(H\in\mathbb C[X]\), taking \(AH\) as common multiplier of the rows \(U_r/A\) gives
\[
 T(\widetilde P,p)\le\sum_{r\in R}\#(H U_r).
\]
The [prime-modulus term-count bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#statement) therefore applies directly to the term counts exhibited by the padded rows, subject to the bounds on \(k=|R|\), \(p\) and \(m(P)\) in its statement.
The multiplier \(C(x)\) changes the original input and has zero logarithmic measure by construction.
An unrestricted multiplier of the original polynomial could increase its measure beyond the theorem's range.
Common multiplication of the rows is already included in the comparison defining \(T\).

## A dense Salem family with a six-term cyclotomic multiple

Take \(f=x^3-x-1\) and the [Salem polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#plateau)
\[
 P_J(x)=\frac{x^J f(x)+f^*(x)}{x+1},
 \qquad f^*(x)=1-x^2-x^3 .
\]
For every sufficiently large multiple \(J\) of \(12600\), this is irreducible, is a unit, and has nonzero coefficients at all indices \(3,\ldots,J-1\).
For primes \(p\ge5\) tending to infinity choose, for example, \(J=12600p\).
The original support occupies all \(p\) residue classes and its differences have gcd one.
With \(C=x+1\) and \(b=J/p\), the only occupied residues and their rows are
\[
 U_0=1-X^b,\qquad U_1=-X^b,\qquad U_2=-1,\qquad U_3=X^b-1 .
\]
Indeed these rows recover the six distinct monomials \(x^{J+3}-x^{J+1}-x^J+1-x^2-x^3\).
The constant row \(-1\) makes the gcd a unit.
Taking \(H=1\) gives total row count six for every prime in this sequence.
With four classes the term-count bound would require at least \(b_4\sqrt{p}/\log p\) terms if the positive measures approached zero.
The six terms exclude that possibility, in agreement with the family calculation \(M(P_J)\to\theta\), where \(\theta>1\) solves \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).

For a putative shrinking sequence of arbitrary irreducible units, the unresolved coverage question is whether, after the power reduction, some subsequence admits cyclotomic multiples, growing primes and a fixed \(k\ge2\) with exactly \(k\) occupied residues and common row multiples of total count \(o(p^{1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor}/\log p)\).
Such coverage would contradict the term-count bound on that subsequence.
The dense Salem example verifies the representation and the measure return on one family with a positive limiting logarithmic measure.
It supplies no coverage statement for an arbitrary shrinking sequence.
