# The cost of simultaneous double factors at large prime exponents

<a id="rate"></a>
## The least coefficient cost for a fixed list

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\) be fixed, distinct, monic reciprocal minimal polynomials of nontorsion units, with positive logarithmic Mahler measures \(h_i=m(P_i)\).
Choose one root from each inverse pair in their combined root set.
Assume that for every two chosen roots \(x,y\), they are either multiplicatively independent, or have a relation \(x^u y^v=\eta\) with nonzero coprime integers \(u,v\) and a root of unity \(\eta\ne1\).
Changing one representative to its inverse preserves this hypothesis.
Fix integer multiplicities \(t_i\ge1\) and put \(W=\sum_i t_i h_i\).
At an odd prime \(p\), prescribe either sign in the graph
\[
 G=A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^p),\qquad A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y).
\]
Among integral rows of width \(b=b(p)\) for which \(\prod_iP_i^{t_i}\mid G\) and \(A(\alpha_i^p)\ne0\) at roots of every \(P_i\), let \(H_p\) be the least one-norm.
If \(b(p)\to\infty\) and \(b(p)=o(p)\), the minimum exists for every sufficiently large prime and
\[
                              \frac{\log H_p}{p}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac W2 .              \tag{1}
\]
For every \(\gamma>0\) there is also a single fixed width \(w\) giving rows with \(\log\|A\|_1/p<W/2+\gamma\) at every sufficiently large prime.
The width may depend on \(\gamma\) and the list.
In particular double factors cost \(h_1+\cdots+h_k\) in (1).

<a id="contraction"></a>
## Simultaneous contraction at primitive prime-order roots

Write \(F=\prod_iP_i^{t_i}\), and let \(\zeta\) be a primitive \(p\)-th root.
Let \(B_p(F)\) generate \((F(\zeta))\mathbb Z[\zeta]\cap\mathbb Z\) positively and \(N_p(F)=|\operatorname{Res}(F,\Phi_p)|\).
For large primes \(p\) one has \(p\nmid N_p(F)\), since \(F(1)\) is a fixed nonzero integer.
At every other prime \(\ell\), the [contraction formula](Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#contraction) gives \(v_\ell(B_p(F))=\max_{1\le j<p}v_\ell(F(\zeta^j))\), taking any extension of the normalized valuation.
Also \(B_p(F)\le N_p(F)^{1/2}\), since reciprocity pairs equal valuations at \(j\) and \(p-j\).
The [fixed-input norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#local) gives \(\log N_p(F)/p\to W\) by multiplication.
We obtain the full upper rate for the contraction,
\[
                           \log B_p(F)/p\ \longrightarrow W/2 .                    \tag{2}
\]

To prove it, work in a fixed Galois splitting field \(L\) for all \(P_i\).
For a finite prime \(\mathfrak l\) of \(L\), let \(w_x=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}\Phi_p(x)\) at each selected inverse-pair representative, with weight \(t_x=t_i\) for a representative from \(P_i\).
The sum of \(t_xw_x\), summed over \(\mathfrak l\) with weight \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\), equals \(\tfrac12\log N_p(F)\), since \(\Phi_p(x^{-1})\) differs from \(\Phi_p(x)\) by a unit.
The positive weights occur away from \(p\).
In a local splitting field for \(\Phi_p\) the primitive nodes have pairwise unit differences there.
If \(w_x>0\) exactly one node satisfies \(v_\ell(x-\zeta^j)=w_x/e_{\mathfrak l}\), for \(e_{\mathfrak l}=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}\ell\).
At that node \(F(\zeta^j)\) contains this difference to power \(t_x\), so \(e_{\mathfrak l}v_\ell(B_p(F))\ge\max_x t_x w_x\).
Summing this last inequality gives \(\log B_p(F)\) on its left, by the local degree formula.

The difference of the sum of nonnegative weights and their maximum is at most the sum of their pairwise minima.
For each independent pair \(x,y\), apply [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) to the independent ordinary units \(x^p,y^p\) in \(L\).
The pairs are distinct as \(p\) varies and their heights are \(O_{F}(p)\).
Since \(\Phi_p(x)\mid x^p-1\) in the integer ring of \(L\), the sum of \(\min(w_x,w_y)\) with the indicated logarithmic weights is \(o_F(p)\), as in the [fixed-input overlap calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#overlap).
For a dependent pair, the ideal generated by \(x^p-1,y^p-1\) contains \(\eta^p-1\), since the units \(x^p\) and \(y^p\) are both congruent to one there.
For all sufficiently large primes the latter is nonzero and ranges over a finite set.
This bounds that pair's sum by \(O_F(1)\).
Multiplying by the largest fixed \(t_i\) bounds the weighted minima as well.
There are finitely many pairs, which proves (2), including for each fixed subproduct.

For any nonzero two-class integral graph \(H=U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) divisible by \(F\), put \(b_0=\max(\deg U,\deg V)\) and \(H_*=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), allowing a zero row.
Remove the largest common power \((Y-1)^j\) from both rows, leaving rows \(U_1,V_1\), at least one nonzero at one.
The resulting graph is still divisible by \(F\), since \(X^p-1\) has only torsion roots.
At a maximizing pair of nodes \(\zeta^a,\zeta^{-a}\) the two evaluations give linear equations modulo \(\ell^{v_\ell(B_p(F))}\) for \(U_1(1),V_1(1)\).
Their determinant is a unit at \(\ell\ne p\).
Thus \(B_p(F)\) divides both integers for large \(p\).
Each has absolute value at most \(\binom{b_0}{j}H_*\), by evaluating the order-\(j\) Hasse derivative of the original row at one.
This also allows a one-class graph.
In particular \(\log H_*/p\ge W/2-o(1)\) for any such rows of widths \(o(p)\).

## Retaining every original contact on a short row

Use the [simultaneous integral jet count](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) with the fixed list, its specified \(t_i\) and two arbitrary rows of width \(b_0\).
Its proof counts the value jet alone when \(t_i=1\).
With \(l=2(b_0+1)\), \(J=\sum_i t_i\deg P_i\), \(L=p(b_0+1)\), and \(J'=\sum_i\deg(P_i)t_i(t_i-1)/2\), it supplies a nonzero graph \(G_0=U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) divisible by \(F\) and
\[
       \log\|G_0\|_1\le \frac{L W+J\log(6l)+J'\log(1+L)}{l-J}+\log(2l)
       \qquad (l>J).
\]
For \(b_0\to\infty,\ b_0=o(p)\) its right side divided by \(p\) tends to \(W/2\).
With \(b_0\) sufficiently large and fixed it gives an arbitrarily small fixed excess over \(W/2\) as \(p\to\infty\).

For large primes the \(p\)-th powers of the distinct original roots remain distinct, since any collision requires their quotient to have order \(p\).
Let \(Q_i\) be the powered minimum at \(P_i\), so \(m(Q_i)=ph_i\).
If both rows contain \(Q_i\), divide them both by \(Q_i\).
The resulting graph contains \(F/P_i\), since \(Q_i(X^p)\) is separable and the powered root sets are disjoint.
The remaining multiplicities have weighted sum \(W-h_i\), so the preceding lower bound gives log one-norm at least \(p(W-h_i)/2-o(p)\) for the larger quotient row.
This holds with the trivial lower bound zero for an empty remaining list.
Its logarithmic Mahler measure is at least that log one-norm minus \(b_0\log2\), by the elementary symmetric coefficient bound.
Restoring \(Q_i\) adds \(ph_i\) to its measure, so \(\log\|G_0\|_1\ge p(W+h_i)/2-o(p)\).
This contradicts the upper bound, choosing the fixed-width excess less than \(\min_i h_i/2\) in that case.
Thus neither row vanishes at any powered node, since vanishing of one forces vanishing of the other by the graph equation.

Apply the [two signed projections and one integer slope](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention) to these rows.
They retain all the reciprocal factors to their original orders at either prescribed sign and avoid zero numerator value simultaneously at all \(k\) powered nodes.
The final width is \(b_0+1\) and the one-norm grows by at most \(4(1+k)\).
Together with the endpoint lower bound, taking \(b_0=b(p)-1\) proves (1) and the fixed-width assertion.

## Any two distinct small measures admit suitable signs

For any two unequal positive irreducible measures \(h_1,h_2\) with \(h_1+h_2<(\log2)/8\), choose their reciprocal unit [all-power representatives](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm).
Distinct conjugates of each representative have nontorsion quotient.
Within one orbit any multiplicative dependence between conjugates forces their quotient or product to be torsion by equality of their absolute heights.
Thus choosing one representative per inverse pair gives independent pairs within each orbit.

If the two orbits have a dependent pair \(x,y\), invert \(y\) when needed and choose coprime positive integers \(u,v\) with \(y^v/x^u=\eta\) torsion.
The ratio \(u/v\) is determined by the positive absolute heights of the two orbits.
For each \(x\) there is at most one positively oriented \(y\) with this property, since two such \(y\)'s have torsion quotient.
Galois transitivity on the first orbit supplies the match for each \(x\) and shows that all its phases \(\eta\) are conjugate.
A negatively oriented match is the inverse of a positively oriented one.
If one phase equals one, all do.
In that case negate the whole first orbit when \(u\) is odd, or the second when \(v\) is odd, to make all phases minus one.
Torsion multiplication by minus one preserves all degrees, measures, reciprocity and independence relations modulo torsion.
When all phases already differ from one, use the original orbits.
This furnishes the hypothesis of (1) for two factors in all cases.

Putting \(B=(\log2)/4\), any two different positive irreducible values of sum below \(B/2\) therefore occur on a single passing row of the [fixed-double-factor test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) at every sufficiently large prime, with one fixed width and either prescribed sign.
For any \(b(p)\to\infty\) with \(\log b(p)=o(p)\), pad that fixed width with the [reciprocal monomial and linear multiplier](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) at cost at most a factor two.
Thus an infinite prime subsequence with fewer than two values on these widths forces every such pair to have sum at least \(B/2\).
Since one positive irreducible value \(h\) has the distinct [degree-preserving multiple](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#multiples) \(2h\), this exclusion would imply the unrestricted bound \(\lambda\ge B/6=(\log2)/24\), by factorization for inputs of small positive measure.
Moreover if \(\lambda<B/4\) under this exclusion, it must be attained and isolated, since at most one numerical value can lie strictly below \(B/4\), and every other positive irreducible value is at least \(B/2-\lambda\).
The implication still requires an exclusion on infinitely many primes.
The [original growing-order test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) has its separate conditional gap \((\log2)/12\).

For \(r\ge1\), a single all-power reciprocal unit \(\delta\) of measure \(h\) also gives the list of minima of \(-\delta^{2^j}\), \(0\le j<r\), of distinct measures \(2^j h\).
Here each cross-orbit dependent pair, after orientation, comes from the same conjugate of \(\delta\), by the within-orbit independence just proved.
The ratio between the higher-power root and the corresponding even power of the lower-power root is minus one.
Thus this list satisfies (1).
In particular \(2(2^r-1)h<B\) suffices to realize \(r\) different values in the fixed-double-factor test at every large prime, with the same fixed-width and padding conventions.
The estimates use a fixed input field before the prime tends to infinity.
