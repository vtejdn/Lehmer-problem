# Two exact comparison series on linear windows at delay two

<a id="window"></a>
## All coefficients up to any fixed multiple of the first discrepancy

Let \(f\) run through monic Pisot minimal polynomials whose delay-two Mahler measures are strictly greater than one and tend to one.
Use the [signed first discrepancy](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v29.md#statement) \(n\to\infty\), put \(p=n+1\), and extract a subsequence with discrepancy sign \(a\in\{-1,1\}\) fixed.
Write \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2F)\) for the integer comparison germ, with \(F=f(0)f/f^*\), and \(\mu\) for the [positive measure](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#positive) of one input.
Extract further so that the laws of \(y=v^p\) under \(\mu\) converge.
By the [power-marginal classification](Yin_L_G0462_v123.md#laws) the limit is arclength weighted by \(1+a\operatorname{Re}y\), or half arclength plus half the point mass at \(a\).
For each fixed \(C>0\) and all sufficiently large inputs, \(K^0\) agrees through degree \(\lfloor Cn\rfloor\) with the respective germ
\[
                     1+a z^p,\qquad \frac1{1-a z^p}.
\]
In particular \(\deg f/n\to\infty\) along every positive-excess sequence tending to one.

Write \(h_0=2\) and \(h_j=[z^{|j|}]K^0\) for \(j\ne0\).
By the [moment approximation](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#window), \(h_j-2\int v^j\,d\mu\to0\) uniformly on each window \(|j|\le Cn\).
The \(h_j\) are integers, they vanish for \(0<|j|<p\), and [their absolute values are at most one](Yin_L_G0462_v123.md#moving) on the nonzero indices in each such window for large inputs.
At multiples \(lp\) with fixed \(l\ge1\), the limiting marginal moments already give the asserted coefficients by integrality.
To deal with other indices, take any sequence of positive integers \(j=O(n)\) with \(p\nmid j\) and pass to a joint limit \(\Omega\) of \((y,X)=(v^p,v^j)\).
For every fixed integer \(k\), the mixed moment \(2\int X y^k\,d\Omega\) is an integer by the same approximation at \(j+kp\).

<a id="density"></a>
## A marginal density with a double zero

In the first case the complex measure on the \(y\)-circle obtained by weighting \(\Omega\) by \(2X\) has density \(Q(y)\) against normalized arclength, with
\[
               |Q(y)|\le W(y)=2+a(y+y^{-1}) .
\]
Its Fourier coefficients are integers and tend to zero in both directions by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.
Thus \(Q\) is an integer Laurent polynomial and its bound holds everywhere.
At \(y=-a\), \(W\) has a double zero, so \(Q\) does too unless it vanishes identically.
Since \(W=a y^{-1}(y+a)^2\), monic division after multiplying by a monomial gives \(Q/W\in\mathbb Z[y,y^{-1}]\).
Its circle modulus is at most one, so Parseval makes it zero or a signed monomial \(b y^l\).
In the latter case the conditional expectation of \(X\) given \(y\) equals \(b y^l\) almost everywhere, since \(W\) is the doubled marginal density.
Its modulus one forces \(X=b y^l\) almost surely.
The moment at the nonzero index \(2(j-lp)=O(n)\) would therefore converge to two after doubling, contrary to the absolute bound on \(h\).
Hence \(Q=0\) and \(h_j\to0\).

<a id="recurrence"></a>
## Excluding simultaneous concentration on two root sets

For positive integers \(q=O(n)\) and signs \(a,b\), the integrals
\[
             \int |(1-a v^p)(1-b v^q)|^2\,d\mu(v)
\]
cannot tend to zero along any subsequence.
Indeed \(D(z)=(1-a z^p)(1-b z^q)\) has constant one, leading coefficient \(ab\), circle roots, degree \(p+q=O(n)\), and coefficient absolute sum at most four.
Since its coefficients are real, \(|D(v)|=|D(\bar v)|\) on the circle.
The moment approximation at scale \(n\) therefore gives exactly the hypotheses of the [cyclotomic recurrence exclusion](Yin_L_G0462_v127.md#annihilator), whose [inverse comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v127.md#inverse) uses the pole of the positive-excess input.

<a id="atom"></a>
## A marginal with half its mass at a point

In the second case let \(\Omega_A\) and \(\Omega_B\) be the restrictions to \(y=a\) and its complement, each multiplied by two.
These are probabilities, and the \(y\)-marginal of \(\Omega_B\) is normalized arclength.
For any integer \(k\) put
\[
 A=\int X\,d\Omega_A,\qquad B_k=\int Xy^k\,d\Omega_B .
\]
Then \(a^k A+B_k\) is an integer at every \(k\), and \(B_k\to0\) as \(k\to+\infty\) by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma applied to the Haar marginal weighted by \(\mathbb E_{\Omega_B}[X\mid y]\).
The even subsequence shows \(A\in\mathbb Z\), so each \(B_k\) is an integer of absolute value at most one.
If \(B_k=b=\pm1\) then \(Xy^k=b\) almost surely for \(\Omega_B\).
On \(\Omega_A\) we already have \(y=a\).
Consequently \((1-ay)(1-bXy^k)\) vanishes on \(\Omega\), implying the excluded concentration with \(q=|j+kp|\).
Here \(q\ne0\) because \(p\nmid j\), and replacing a circle power by its inverse preserves the absolute value of that signed binomial.
Hence \(B_k=0\) for all \(k\).

Choose an integer \(l\) such that \(0<|j-lp|<p\), passing to a subsequence with \(l\) fixed since \(j=O(n)\).
The mixed moment at \(j-lp\) vanishes by the initial gap, so \(a^{-l}A+B_{-l}=0\).
It follows that \(A=0\), and hence \(h_j\to0\) also in this case.
Any failure of the asserted uniform agreement on a fixed linear window would supply a sequence of disagreeing indices.
At multiples of \(p\) their multipliers are bounded, and the other indices have just been excluded by integrality in each marginal case.
This proves the linear-window assertion, including for indices moving with the input.
It also determines every character moment of any joint limit of finitely many powers \(v^{j_t}\) with \(j_t=O(n)\), by applying the same window to each fixed integer linear combination of their exponents.

<a id="degree"></a>
## The seed degree grows faster than the first discrepancy

Write \(d_f=\deg f\).
The numerator and denominator of \(K^0\) have degrees at most \(d_f+2\) after clearing \(f^*\), and the denominator has nonzero constant.
Either comparison model has numerator and denominator degrees at most \(p\) and is analytic at zero.
If \(d_f/n\) were bounded on a subsequence, extract one of the two limiting marginals there and choose a fixed \(C\) such that \(\lfloor Cn\rfloor\ge d_f+2+p\).
The agreement already proved would make the crossed numerator of the difference vanish to order greater than its degree, so \(K^0\) would equal the corresponding model.
Both models are analytic in the disk while \(K^0\) has its interior pole.
This contradiction gives \(d_f/n\to\infty\), and leaves open whether either limiting marginal occurs at positive excess tending to zero.
