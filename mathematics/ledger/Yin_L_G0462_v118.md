# The side of the Pisot pole and a smaller exterior root at the first delay-two departure

<a id="side"></a>
## The distance of the Pisot seed from two

Use the [first-departure notation](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#departure) for a sequence of actual delay-two inputs with multiplicative Mahler measure \(1<\alpha\to1\), limiting associated quotient \(1/(1-2z)\), first discrepancy \(\delta\) at index \(n\to\infty\), and return function \(V\).
Thus \(f\) is the Pisot unit minimal polynomial of \(\theta\), \(s=f(0)\), \(A=sf\), \(Q=f^*\), and the [necessary sign](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#input) gives \(Q+z^2A\) with sole interior root \(\tau=1/\alpha\).
On any subsequence with \(V\to R/2\) as in the [Schur limit](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#schur), the [extreme-completion exclusion](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#extreme) gives \(|R(0)|=1\) and \(\delta=-R(0)\) eventually.
Then
\[
            2^n(\theta-2)\longrightarrow-\frac{R(1/2)}2.
\]
In particular \(\operatorname{sgn}(\theta-2)=\delta\) eventually along the original sequence.
The possible limits of \(2^n(\theta-2)\) are \(\delta/2\) for the constant profile and \((\delta-\eta 2^{-k})/2\) for \(R=-\delta+\eta z^k\).
Their absolute values lie in \([1/4,3/4]\), so \(n=\log_2(1/|\theta-2|)+O(1)\).
These consequences of the fixed first-departure result were obtained by agent-S for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) on 2026-11-17.

At the seed pole \(r=1/\theta\), one has \(Q(r)=0\) and \(A(r)\ne0\).
Substitution in the defining ratio
\[
 V(z)=\frac{(1-2z)A-Q}{z^n((z-2)Q-zA)}
\]
therefore gives \(2r-1=r^{n+1}V(r)\) and \(\theta-2=-r^nV(r)\).
Since \(r\to1/2\), compact convergence yields \(V(r)\to R(1/2)/2\) and \(|2r-1|=O((3/5)^n)\) eventually.
It follows that \((2r)^n\to1\).
For a binomial profile the term \(\eta2^{-k}\) is smaller in magnitude than the constant term, proving the sign assertion.
The original-sequence conclusions follow by extracting a convergent return profile from every subsequence.

## A smaller root of a polynomial of leading coefficient two

Write \(U=z^{-n}((1-2z)A-Q)\) as in the [reciprocal identity](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#parity).
For all sufficiently late inputs its sole exterior root \(\beta\) is real and satisfies
\[
            1<\frac{\alpha^{n+2}+1}{\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha}
                  \ \le\ \beta\ <\ \alpha .
\]
The primitive irreducible factor of \(\beta\), taken with positive leading coefficient, has leading coefficient two, constant of absolute value one and all its other roots strictly inside the unit disk.
The remaining factors of \(U\) are cyclotomic up to sign.
In particular \(M(U)=2\beta>2\) tends to two.

Indeed \(D=(z-2)Q-zA=sU^*\) has at most one disk zero counted with multiplicity by the circle argument, and \(V\) must have a disk pole when \(M(Q+z^2A)>1\).
Thus \(D\) has precisely one simple disk zero \(b\), necessarily real.
For \(-1<x<0\), the [finite Blaschke product](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#input) with pole removed gives \(|F(x)|\le1/|b_r(x)|<1/|x|\), where \(F=A/Q\) and \(b_r(z)=(r-z)/(1-rz)\).
The polynomial \(Q\) has no negative disk zero, and \(D(x)/Q(x)=x-2-xF(x)<0\) there.
At \(x=0\), \(D(0)=-2\).
For large inputs \(r<\tau<1\); \(Q(\tau)<0\) because \(Q(0)=1\) and its sole disk zero \(r\) is simple.
Since \(F(\tau)=-\tau^{-2}\), one has \(D(\tau)=Q(\tau)(1-\tau)^2/\tau<0\).
A single simple zero between \(0\) and \(\tau\) would reverse the sign.
Hence \(\tau<b<1\), giving the unique exterior root \(\beta=1/b\) of \(U\).

The rational function \(b_b(z)V(z)\) is Schur by cancellation of the disk pole and the unit boundary modulus.
The exact identity \(sU^*+z^{n+1}U=-2(Q+z^2A)\) gives \(V(\tau)=-\tau^{-n-1}\).
It follows that
\[
 \frac{b-\tau}{1-b\tau}\le \tau^{n+1},\qquad
 \frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha\beta-1}\le\alpha^{-n-1}.
\]
Rearranging gives the displayed lower bound, whose numerator exceeds its denominator by \((\alpha-1)(\alpha^{n+1}-1)>0\).

The polynomial \(U\) has leading coefficient \(-2s\) and unit constant \(\delta\).
Let \(P\) be the primitive irreducible polynomial of \(\beta\) with positive leading coefficient.
It is nonreciprocal since a reciprocal factor containing \(\beta\) would put \(b\) in both \(U\) and \(U^*\), contrary to their [disk coprimality](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#parity).
If \(P\) were monic, its measure would be \(\beta\to1\) along any such subsequence, contradicting the [nonreciprocal measure bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001).
Thus \(P\) eventually has leading coefficient two.
Any unit-circle root of \(P\) would be common to \(P\) and its reversal by real conjugation, contradicting nonreciprocity and irreducibility.
All its other roots are consequently strictly inside.
The remaining factor of \(U\) is monic up to sign, with unit constant and all roots in the closed unit disk, so Kronecker's theorem gives the asserted cyclotomic factorization.
In particular the minimum number of terms among all cyclotomic multiples of \(P\) tends to infinity by the [fixed-support endpoint gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#statement), since each such multiple has leading coefficient of absolute value two and measure \(2\beta\).

When \(\delta=-1\), there is also a positive zero \(y\in(0,\tau)\) of \(V\), since \(V(0)=1/2>0>V(\tau)\) and \(b>\tau\).
Every such choice has \(y\to1\), since all the surviving compact limits \(R/2\) are zero-free in the open disk.
This zero of \(U\) belongs to \(P\), so it is a positive conjugate of \(\beta\).
Removing this zero from the Schur function \(b_bV\) sharpens the bound to
\[
             \frac{b-\tau}{1-b\tau}
                      \ \le\ \tau^{n+1}\frac{\tau-y}{1-y\tau}.
\]
These tests give no exclusion of either discrepancy sign or of the constant or binomial second profiles.
