## A short control on what prefix supply alone measures

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be any monic reciprocal polynomial with constant one, and let \(A(z)\in1+z\mathbb Z[[z]]\) be any target series.
For each finite \(L\), there is a monic reciprocal cyclotomic product \(C_L\) of even degree and constant one such that
\[
                         F C_L=A+O(z^{L+1}).
\]
Indeed, suppose the coefficients below index \(j\) have been corrected and let the required change at index \(j\) be the integer \(d\).
Multiply by \((1+z^j+z^{2j})^d\) when \(d>0\), or by \((1-z^j+z^{2j})^{-d}\) when \(d<0\).
Both factors have only roots of unity and change the coefficient at \(j\) by exactly \(d\), leaving earlier coefficients unchanged.
Induction through \(L\) proves the assertion and preserves \(M(F C_L)=M(F)\).
In particular, it applies to each integral profile series in C881 v3, even when \(M(F)>1\) is fixed.
This supplies no strict chamber: for fixed positive \(m(F)\), the C881 upper bound prevents these padded outputs from satisfying all its hypotheses at arbitrarily large \(L\).
This is only a control on the evaluation criterion, not a new supply strategy or a criticism of S4's explicit chamber requirement.

<a id="negative-index-bound"></a>
## An automatic bound for all expanding eigenvalues

Let \(H\) be a nondegenerate real symmetric form of signature \((a,q)\), with \(a\ge q\), and suppose \(A^{\mathsf T}HA=H\).
Let \(V_+\) be the real invariant generalized eigenspace belonging to all complex eigenvalues of modulus greater than one.
Its real dimension is their total algebraic multiplicity, including both members of each nonreal conjugate pair.
For \(v,w\in V_+\), \(A^{-k}v,A^{-k}w\to0\), including the polynomial factors from Jordan blocks.
Therefore
\[
 H(v,w)=H(A^{-k}v,A^{-k}w)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(V_+\) is totally isotropic and has dimension at most \(q\).
For an integral isometry \(A\in\mathrm{GL}_{a+q}(\mathbb Z)\), its complete monic integer characteristic polynomial \(P_A\) consequently satisfies
\[
 N_+(A)\le q,\qquad
 m(P_A)=\sum_{|\lambda|>1}\log|\lambda|
                 \le q\log\rho(A).                       \tag{1}
\]
Every eigenvalue and multiplicity is included.
The isometry identity makes \(A^{-1}\) similar to \(A^{\mathsf T}\), so the multiset is reciprocal and \(\rho(A)\ge1\).
Hence \(m(P_A)>0\) is equivalent to \(\rho(A)>1\).

The [rotating symplectic law](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v5.md#rotating-symplectic-law) has no corresponding small bound on its expanding count.
Its exact cyclic-shift control has \(g\) expanding roots while the house decreases as a \(g\)-th root.
Such dilution is incompatible with fixed \(q\) in (1).
This is the new information supplied by the quadratic form, not an assertion that the house of the proposed orthogonal matrices is small.
Fixed \(q\) and \(1<\rho(A_j)\to1\), or varying \(q_j\) and \(0<q_j\log\rho(A_j)\to0\), would suffice for the original Task.

<a id="integer-generators"></a>
## Explicit integral generators and their lattice conditions

Use the lattice \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^g\oplus U^q\), where the first summand has its standard positive dot product and each hyperbolic plane has basis \(e_i,f_i\) with
\[
 (e_i,e_i)=(f_i,f_i)=0,\qquad(e_i,f_i)=1.
\]
Different summands are orthogonal, so the signature is \((g+q,q)\).
**Definition (Eichler transvection in the chosen lattice).**
For a nonzero integral isotropic vector \(e\) and an integral \(v\perp e\) of even norm, define
\[
 T_{e,v}(x)=x-(v,x)e+(e,x)v-\tfrac12(v,v)(e,x)e.          \tag{2}
\]
All coefficients are integral under these exact conditions.
For a direct check, put \(s_x=(e,x)\), \(t_x=(v,x)\), and \(a=(v,v)/2\), and similarly define \(s_y,t_y\).
The difference of the transformed and original pairings is
\[
 s_xt_y+s_yt_x-(t_x+as_x)s_y-(t_y+as_y)s_x+2as_xs_y=0.
\]
The same formula gives inverse \(T_{e,-v}\).
Thus (2) is an integral lattice isometry, without a unimodularity or evenness assumption on the entire lattice.

The formula is the standard Eichler transvection, identified in [GHS13's arXiv v2 edition](../audits/literature/GHS13/readme.md#arxiv-v2), Section 8.3, equation (49), printed/PDF p. 54.
The title page, complete supplied contents, cusp notation in Section 8.2, formula (49) and adjacent inverse identity were inspected in that edition.
The bounded source check on 9 November 2026 stopped after identifying the formula and its context; it was not a survey of integral orthogonal groups or a priority investigation.
The source is attribution for the self-contained pairing calculation above.
Its orthogonal modular-group assertions for signature \((2,n)\) are not transferred to the different signature or the possibly odd lattice used here, and no full-paper proof audit is claimed.

Let \(R_g\) cycle the positive coordinate vectors and fix every \(e_i,f_i\).
For even-norm vectors \(v_i,w_i\in\mathbb Z^g\),
\[
 A=R_g\prod_{i=1}^{q}T_{e_i,v_i}T_{f_i,w_i}              \tag{3}
\]
is an explicit integral isometry.
The output is the whole \(\det(zI-A)\); no low-measure factor is extracted from a larger output.
Any finite law on the listed integer vectors is an actual discrete law, but no strict small-shell atom is known from (3).

<a id="first-family"></a>
## A concrete first family with growing vector support

For a first symbolic test take \(q=1\), an integer \(r\ge1\), \(N=2^r\), and odd \(g>2N\).
Let \(v_r\in\mathbb Z^g\) have generating polynomial
\[
 V_r(z)=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-z^{2^j}),
 \qquad w_r=R_g^N v_r,
 \qquad A_{r,g}=R_gT_{e,v_r}T_{f,w_r}.                   \tag{4}
\]
The first \(N\) coefficients of \(v_r\) are all \(\pm1\), the remaining coordinates vanish, and \(\|v_r\|^2=\|w_r\|^2=N\) is even.
The two supports are disjoint, so \(v_r^{\mathsf T}w_r=0\).
The order of vanishing of \(V_r\) at one is \(r\).
This gives a concrete signed cancellation to test against the low-frequency rotation resolvent, rather than choosing unrelated random vectors.
It does not imply a small spectral radius; it might instead preserve neutrality or create a fixed positive spectral cost elsewhere.
One definite joint scaling to analyze is \(g=N^3+1\), with \(r\to\infty\), rather than first sending \(g\to\infty\) at fixed \(r\).
For \(q=1\), every nonneutral output has exactly one real expanding eigenvalue in absolute value and \(M(P_A)=\rho(A)\).

Here is an exact low-dimensional entrance for that symbolic test.
For general \(v,w\) in (4), put \(a=\|v\|^2/2\), \(b=\|w\|^2/2\), \(c=v^{\mathsf T}w\), and
\[
 K(z)=(zI-R_g)^{-1}R_g
    =\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{g}z^{g-j}R_g^j}{z^g-1}.
\]
At \(|z|>1\), eliminating the positive coordinates from \(A X=zX\) gives the exact two-by-two determinant
\[
 D_{v,w}(z)=\det
 \begin{pmatrix}
 z-1+c+v^{\mathsf T}K(z)w & z\bigl(a+v^{\mathsf T}K(z)v\bigr)\\
 b+w^{\mathsf T}K(z)w & z-1+z w^{\mathsf T}K(z)v
 \end{pmatrix}.                                         \tag{5}
\]
There is an exterior eigenvalue exactly when (5) vanishes there.
To verify the elimination, write \(X=x+\alpha e+\beta f\).
The eigen-equations are
\[
 x=\alpha K(z)w+z\beta K(z)v,
 \quad(z-1+c)\alpha+v^{\mathsf T}x+az\beta=0,
 \quad b\alpha+w^{\mathsf T}x+(z-1)\beta=0.
\]
If \(\alpha=\beta=0\), the positive component would have an exterior eigenvalue of \(R_g\), which is impossible.
Thus the determinant neither discards nor invents an exterior eigenvalue.
No global characteristic-polynomial normalization or cancellation at roots of unity is asserted solely from this exterior equation.

<a id="first-family-obstruction"></a>
## The first test rejects the displayed family

Evaluating (5) at two gives the following complete fixed-radius estimate.
For a real vector \(u\) and the cyclic orthogonal matrix \(R_g\),
\[
 \frac{\|u\|^2}{2}+u^{\mathsf T}K(2)u
 =\frac12u^{\mathsf T}(2I+R_g)(2I-R_g)^{-1}u
 \ge\frac{\|u\|^2}{6}.                               \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, the real part of the Cayley quotient on an eigenvalue \(e^{i\theta}\) is
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\frac{2+e^{i\theta}}{2-e^{i\theta}}
       =\frac{3}{5-4\cos\theta}\ge\frac13,
\]
which proves (6) by the orthogonal spectral decomposition.
Both factors \(a+v^{\mathsf T}K(2)v\) and \(b+w^{\mathsf T}K(2)w\) in (5) are therefore at least \(N/6\).

The cross terms have a different size.
Put \(B=v^{\mathsf T}K(2)w\) and \(C=w^{\mathsf T}K(2)v\), with \(v=v_r\), \(w=w_r\).
For \(1\le j\le g\), as \(R_g\) moves coordinates forward, \(w^{\mathsf T}R_g^jv\) vanishes outside \(1\le j\le2N-1\); inside that interval its absolute value is at most the overlap count \(\min(j,2N-j)\le j\).
The reverse pairing \(v^{\mathsf T}R_g^jw\) vanishes outside \(g-2N+1\le j\le g-1\), and at \(j=g-2N+k\) its absolute value is at most \(k\).
These assertions include the cyclic wrap, since \(g>2N\).
Using the finite resolvent sum gives
\[
 |C|\le\frac{\sum_{j\ge1}j2^{-j}}{1-2^{-g}}
       =\frac{2}{1-2^{-g}},\qquad
 |B|\le\frac{2^{1-(g-2N)}}{1-2^{-g}}.                  \tag{7}
\]
For \(g=N^3+1\) and \(N\ge2\), one has \(g\ge9\) and \(g-2N\ge5\), so the convenient bounds \(|B|<1\), \(|C|<3\) hold.
Since \(c=0\), (5)--(7) imply
\[
 D_{v,w}(2)
 =(1+B)(1+2C)
       -2\bigl(a+v^{\mathsf T}K(2)v\bigr)
          \bigl(b+w^{\mathsf T}K(2)w\bigr)
 <14-\frac{N^2}{18}<0\qquad(N\ge16).                  \tag{8}
\]
The determinant is continuous for real \(z>1\), and for each fixed pair \((r,g)\) its asymptotic expansion is \(D_{v,w}(z)=z^2+O(z)\) as \(z\to+\infty\).
It therefore has a real zero greater than two.
The exterior equivalence proved above and the \(q=1\) count then give
\[
                   M(P_{A_{r,N^3+1}})=\rho(A_{r,N^3+1})>2
                         \qquad(r\ge4).              \tag{9}
\]
Thus the precise growing-support family fails without a conductor scan or an asymptotic numerical inference.
The signs and the growing order of vanishing of \(V_r\) at one cannot offset the two positive diagonal terms at two.
The estimate uses only the displayed support geometry, coefficient magnitudes, and norms; it is not peculiar to the chosen signs.

## Coverage checks and outcomes that change allocation

The accepted [McM02 canonical Coxeter gap](../audits/literature/McM02/readme.md#canonical-coxeter-gap-input) applies to every word in the specified canonical geometric representation, not automatically to every integral lattice isometry.
The present matrices have not been shown outside that coverage.
In particular, for \((v,v)=2\), the reflection convention \(s_u(x)=x-(x,u)u\) gives \(T_{e,v}=s_{v-e}s_v\), so disguising a Coxeter word as transvections would supply no escape.
Any successor candidate requires that representation-applicability check; absence of a recognized Coxeter description is not evidence that the gap fails.
The standard Salem interpretation at \(q=1\) is also a restriction of the original problem, not a proof that every possible counterexample has this root type.

Fixed vector support must be tested against [DS17's accepted fixed-monomial gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) and the actual cyclotomic-completion reasoning in L464 v19.
The two displayed transvections do not by themselves prove bounded characteristic support: their vector entries and cyclic autocorrelations carry the growing complexity.
Conversely the growing support of \(V_r\) does not prove that the complete characteristic polynomial lacks a bounded-support cyclotomic multiple.
That actual completion, with the whole measure unchanged, must be checked before interpreting a degree limit as new coverage.

The first discriminating question was whether the explicit family (4) could avoid a fixed exterior radius in the joint scaling \(g=2^{3r}+1\).
The exact answer (9) rejects it, regardless of any further Coxeter or bounded-support classification.
No extension of that family is recommended on the strength of its signed cancellation at one.
For a genuinely different proposed family, a later researcher would first need an exact spectrum or resolvent calculation that addresses the obstruction at a fixed exterior point, together with the actual Coxeter and fixed-support applicability checks.
Passing those exclusions would only justify a further estimate; it would not imply the needed radius limit.

The first still-unproved bridge for the general orthogonal idea is an explicit integral family with fixed negative index, unbounded degree, nonneutrality, and an actual radius upper bound tending to one.
If the negative index is allowed to grow, using (1) to close the Task would require proving the sufficient target \(q\log\rho\to0\), which is not supplied here.
A local expansion at \(z=1\) without excluding larger exterior roots, or a small house inferred from isolated numerical points, does not close either bridge.
For \(q=1\), proposing such a family is still a Salem-type arithmetic supply problem; the form solves the expanding-root count, not the difficult small-radius existence problem.

No developed replacement for the current integer supply emerged from this bounded exploration.
The exact-prefix theorem should remain available to evaluate a genuinely new strict-chamber construction, but the inspected evidence does not justify another parameter enlargement or an unstructured integer-prefix campaign.
Likewise, the orthogonal construction should not receive a new campaign solely from the automatic root-count bound.
Its retained contribution is the explicit complete-measure reduction, actual integral generators, and the short failure of a concrete growing-support proposal.
