# Prime densities for the partial rational-trace test

<a id="density"></a>
## Fixed trace labels in a reduced congruence class

Let \(\gamma\) be a nontorsion reciprocal unit with distinct conjugates having nontorsion quotient, as for a reciprocal [torsion-class product](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels).
Fix \(M>0\), assume \(m(\gamma)\le M\), and let \(d=\deg\gamma\) be sufficiently large in terms of \(M\).
The \(n=d/2\) conjugates of \(t=\gamma+\gamma^{-1}\) are the inverse-pair labels.
Their splitting field \(L\) has permutation group \(D=(A_b)^u\le G\le S_b\wr S_u\), where \(n=bu\), \(b\ge9\), and \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\), by the [independent alternating actions](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).
Write \(X_p=n\rho_p\) for the number of these traces lying in \(\mathbb Q_p\).
Except at primes ramified in \(L\), this is the number of fixed letters of a Frobenius element, since it generates the decomposition group.

For every reduced class \(a\bmod m_0\) and \(1\le k\le n\), the relative natural density of primes in that class with \(X_p\ge k\) exists and is at most
\[
       \min\!\left\{1,\;2^{\min(u,\lfloor k/(b-1)\rfloor)}
                                \frac{u^k}{k!}\right\}.                  \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, in a uniform coset of \(D\) let \(s\) be the number of stationary blocks.
Only their letters can be fixed.
The induced permutations on these blocks are independent and uniform of specified parity.
Within one stationary block, the probability of fixing \(j\) prescribed letters is exactly \(1/(b)_j\) for \(0\le j\le b-2\), where \((b)_j=b!/(b-j)!\).
The remaining letters admit equally many completions of either parity.
For \(j=b-1,b\) the probability is at most \(2/(b)_j\).
Summing over \(k\)-subsets gives
\[
 \Pr(X\ge k)\le{\mathbb E}\binom Xk
 \le 2^{\min(s,\lfloor k/(b-1)\rfloor)}
       \sum_{\substack{j_1+\cdots+j_s=k\\j_i\ge0}}\prod_i\frac1{j_i!}
 =2^{\min(s,\lfloor k/(b-1)\rfloor)}\frac{s^k}{k!}.
\]
Here \(\binom b j/(b)_j=1/j!\) for \(j\le b\), and at most \(\lfloor k/(b-1)\rfloor\) blocks require the extra factor two.
For \(k\le b-2\) the binomial moment \(\mathbb E\binom Xk\) equals \(s^k/k!\).

Put \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{m_0})\).
In \({\rm Gal}(LE/\mathbb Q)\), the fiber over the residue automorphism of \(E\) restricts bijectively to a coset of \({\rm Gal}(L/(L\cap E))\) in \(G\).
The latter subgroup contains \(D\), since \(L\cap E\) is abelian and \(D\) is perfect.
The whole fiber and its fixed-count event are conjugacy invariant.
Applying [Chebotarev to this finite compositum](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) gives the conditional density as the proportion in this fiber, a union of \(D\)-cosets, proving (1).

<a id="threshold"></a>
## The exponential rate at the positivity threshold

Fix \(C>0\).
For \(q=p^j\le d^C\), a strictly positive right-hand side of the [partial trace estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v396.md#primes) requires \(\rho_p\log q>\log4\), hence
\[
       X_p\ge k_C=\left\lfloor\frac{d\log2}{C\log d}\right\rfloor+1 .
\]
More generally if \(k=(\lambda+o(1))d/\log d\) with fixed \(\lambda>0\), let \(\delta_a(k)\) denote the relative density in (1).
Uniformly in the reduced progression,
\[
      \delta_a(k)\le \exp\bigl(-(\lambda+o(1))d\bigr).
                                                                           \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(\log(k!)\ge k\log k-k\), \(\log u=O_M(\log\log(3d))\), and \(u=o(d)\).
The rate is attained for the event \(X_p\ge k\) in every identity progression \(p\equiv1\bmod m_0\), including the unrestricted primes.
Its fiber contains \(D\) with relative weight at least \([G:D]^{-1}\ge(2^u u!)^{-1}=\exp(-o_M(d))\).
Within \(D\), prescribe \(k\) fixed letters with at most \(b-2\) per block, possible for large \(d\) since \(k=o(n)\) and \(b\to\infty\).
The probability of this one event is at least \(b^{-k}\ge d^{-k}\), giving the matching lower logarithmic rate.
In particular \(\log\delta_1(k_C)/d\to-\log2/C\).
Cosets acting without a stationary block contribute zero to the density in other fibers.

## An intrinsic threshold for a fixed positive Mahler bound

Fix \(\eta>0\) and let \(X_p=k>0\).
The lower bound for \(m(\gamma)\) supplied by C396 at \(q=p^j\) is \((k\log q-d\log2)/q\).
Over all real \(q>1\) its maximum is attained at \(q=\exp(1+d\log2/k)\) and equals
\[
                   \frac{k}{e}\exp(-d\log2/k).
\]
Therefore obtaining a bound at least \(\eta\) at any prime power requires
\[
              k\log(k/(e\eta))\ge d\log2 .                               \tag{3}
\]
The first positive integer satisfying (3) is \((\log2+o_\eta(1))d/\log d\), by substitution on either side of that scale and monotonicity for \(k\ge e\eta\).
Thus (2) with \(\lambda=\log2\) bounds the density of the necessary fixed-count event for **every** such gain.
Also \(k\le d/2\) forces \(2\eta q/d\le\log(q/4)\), so \(q\le(1+o_\eta(1))d\log d/(2\eta)\).
Indeed at \(q=(1+\varepsilon)d\log d/(2\eta)\) the inequality fails for large \(d\), and \(\log(q/4)/q\) decreases for all larger \(q\).

Every density above takes the prime-counting limit for the individual splitting field, modulus and integer threshold first.
The exponential rates describe these limiting proportions as the compressed degree grows.
The actual prime powers able to supply the desired bound lie in a finite range for each input, and their occurrence there remains unresolved.
