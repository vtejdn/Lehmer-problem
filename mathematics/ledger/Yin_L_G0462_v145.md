# Repeating the ratio construction on triples and the obstruction to dividing a degree record by three

<a id="reverse"></a>
## The reverse direction from two exterior roots

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal unit polynomial of degree \(2m>6\) with two nonreal exterior roots \(\lambda,\overline\lambda\) of modulus \(R\).
Suppose the [signed exterior graph](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#statement) has balanced components of size three.
For each triple orient its representatives \(x,y,z\) so that \(xyz\) is torsion.
By the rank-one relation lattice of that triple this orientation is unique up to simultaneously taking reciprocals.
Form the product polynomial on all six ordered ratios of distinct representatives in each triple.
The polynomial is monic integral and reciprocal because a change of orientation inverts every ratio, while Galois automorphisms permute the triples and their permissible orientations.

The ordered ratios are all distinct.
Indeed two representing exponent vectors have coordinate sum zero on every triple in the chosen orientations.
Their difference cannot be a nonzero multiple of the equal-coordinate generator on any block.
The Galois action is transitive on unordered pairs in all triples: each such pair corresponds to the omitted vertex of its triple and the original vertex action is transitive.
Consequently the orbit of any ordered ratio contains at least one orientation for each unordered pair.
There is a neutral triple since \(2m>6\), so this orbit contains a circle root.
That root is nontorsion, since an ordered ratio does not represent a block relation.
Its complex conjugate is its inverse, and the rational minimal polynomial therefore contains inverses of all its roots.
It contains both orientations of all the pairs, proving irreducibility and preservation of degree.

In the exterior triple choose \(x=\lambda,\ y=1/\overline\lambda\) by the signed relation, and let \(z\) be its neutral third representative.
The exterior ordered ratios are \(x/y=R^2,\ x/z,\ z/y\).
The last two are conjugate and distinct by the distinctness of all ordered ratios.
Other triples contribute only circle roots.
Thus the ratio polynomial has one real exterior root of modulus \(R^2\) and a nonreal pair of modulus \(R\), and its Mahler measure equals \(M(P)^2\).
The [construction in the opposite direction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v57.md#ratios-of-roots) has exactly the cost \(M(P)^{3/2}\) when applied to a three-exterior polynomial with this logarithmic ratio.

<a id="twice"></a>
## The second transformation and a cube test

In either direction the new triple over \(x,y,z\) admits the oriented representatives \(x/y,y/z,z/x\), with product one.
Its relation lattice on each triple is generated by this product, since any relation in these ratios pulls back to a vector of coordinate sum zero in the original triple.
It also has no relations between triples except sums of these generators, by the original direct sum decomposition.
Thus the same ratio construction can be applied again, using exactly these triples.
Writing \(\omega=xyz\), its six values on this block are
\[
           (x^3/\omega)^{\pm1},\qquad
           (y^3/\omega)^{\pm1},\qquad
           (z^3/\omega)^{\pm1}.
\]
This follows by dividing one of the three cyclic ratios by another.
For the original mixed three-exterior case, C345v57 proves that the products \(\omega\) are signs.
For a general balanced two-exterior input the torsion products can depend on their block.
The second transformation has measure \(M(P)^3\).

There is a direct arithmetic restriction if the initial \(P\) realizes the least positive Mahler measure among monic integer polynomials of degrees at most \(2m\), with positivity here meaning \(M>1\).
Let \(Q\) be the first irreducible ratio polynomial of the same degree, in either of the two constructions above.
For every root \(\alpha\) of \(Q\), the polynomial \(X^3-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
If it were reducible it would have a root \(\gamma\) in that field, and \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) since \(\gamma^3=\alpha\).
The element \(\gamma\) would be an algebraic integer unit of the same degree and \(M(\gamma)^3=M(Q)\), by taking all embeddings once.
The two possible values of \(M(Q)\), namely \(M(P)^2\) and \(M(P)^{3/2}\), would both give \(1<M(\gamma)<M(P)\).
This contradicts minimality and proves the irreducibility assertion.
It follows by the degree tower that \(Q(X^3)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
