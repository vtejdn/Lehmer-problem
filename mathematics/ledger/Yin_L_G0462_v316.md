# Degree and coefficient height in permuted representations of a common sum

<a id="bounds"></a>
## Disjoint groups of powers in an exponent window

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), with an exterior conjugate of modulus \(e^H>1\).
Choose \(n\ge2\) distinct integer shifts with minimum zero and maximum \(J\), and put \(S=\sum_j\alpha^{j}\), where the sum runs over the chosen shifts.
Suppose \(S\ne0\) and \(n\) groups of distinct exponents, contained respectively in their exact spans \([l_i,u_i]\), each represent the same value \(c/S\) by at most \(p\) terms with nonzero rational integer coefficients of absolute value at most \(e^Y\), where \(0\ne c\in\mathbb Z\).
Order the groups so that \(l_{i+1}>u_i+J\), as needed to keep the groups strictly ordered for every assignment of the shifts in the [permutation construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v597.md#clusters).
An arbitrary shift set can be normalized to minimum zero by adding its former minimum to every group exponent and dividing every shift power by the corresponding common power of \(\alpha\).
The normalized groups then represent the new \(c/S\) and every permuted full array is unchanged.
Write \(X=(Y+\log(np))/H\).
If every group starts at a nonnegative exponent, then
\[
                 \left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor(d+1)\le X.
\]
When the groups may start on either side of the output at zero, one still has
\[
                 \left\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\right\rfloor(d+1)\le 2X.
\]
These inequalities hold for prescribed two- or three-term group representations as well as for groups made from rational affine planes.

At the reciprocal conjugate of modulus \(e^{-H}\) the shift sum has modulus at most \(n\), so every group value there has modulus at least \(1/n\).
Its own termwise estimate is at most \(p e^{Y-H l_i}\), giving \(l_i\le X\).
At the exterior conjugate the shift sum has modulus at most \(n e^{HJ}\), while the group value has modulus at most \(p e^{Y+Hu_i}\).
Thus \(u_i\ge -J-X\).
The difference of any two group representations is a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial vanishing at \(\alpha\), since the exponent supports are disjoint.
Clearing the lowest power preserves its span and yields an ordinary multiple of the degree-\(d\) minimal polynomial.
In particular the interval covering a pair of consecutive groups has at least \(d+1\) integer positions.
For nonnegative group exponents the first \(n-1\) groups lie in \([0,l_n-1]\), and disjoint consecutive pairs prove the first bound.
For signed group exponents, the \(n-2\) interior groups lie between \(u_1+J+1\) and \(l_n-1\), a window with at most \(2X\) positions since \(u_1\ge-J-X\) and \(l_n\le X\).
Pairing consecutive interior groups proves the second inequality, also when fewer than two interior groups are present.

## Consequences at the height and weight of the terminal witnesses

In the [actual witness ranges](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement), rational integer coefficient heights obey \(Y=O(\log(2d))\) and the total weight is \(O(d\log(2d))\).
Taking \(p\) to be the actual largest group size gives \(\log(np)=O(\log(2d))\) throughout this range.
Consequently on every sequence with \(dH/\log(2d)\to\infty\), at most two groups can be permuted when the normalized exponents are nonnegative, and at most three when they have arbitrary signs, for all sufficiently large degrees.
For a Salem number one can take \(H=m=\log M(\alpha)\).
The [accepted Dobrowolski estimate](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) implies \(dm/\log(2d)\to\infty\) at unbounded degree, so these bounds apply to every such Salem family, including a hypothetical one of shrinking measure.
More generally, if \(k\) embeddings expand, the largest \(H\) satisfies \(H\ge m/k\), and \(k=o(dm/\log(2d))\) suffices.

Independently of coefficient height, a construction using \(p=d+2\) terms in every group and total weight \(r=pn+1=O(d\log(2d))\) has \(n=O(\log(2d))\).
Thus its \(n!\) enumerated assignments contribute only \(\log(n!)/r\le \log n/(d+2)=o(1)\) at large degree.
The two obstructions apply to this class of ordered permutation constructions.
Interleaved groups, different assignments producing other relations, or constructions in which different groups do not represent the same field element require separate analysis.
