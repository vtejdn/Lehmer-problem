# The least perturbation degree for each fixed exceptional parent

Fix \(s\ge2\) in the [exceptional cyclotomic family](Yin_L_G0464_v20.md#real-degree).
Among real polynomial perturbations that give the strict terminal cut for arbitrarily large conductors \(p\), the least possible degree is
\[
 \begin{cases}
 3,&s=3,\\
 s+1,&s=2\text{ or }s\ge4 .
 \end{cases}
 \tag{1}
\]
The same minimum holds when the strict endpoint is required and the coefficients must be rational.
On 2026-11-16 the construction contributor for Yin's [unrestricted Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained (1) by completing the interval between the [multiplicity lower bound](Yin_L_G0464_v20.md#fixed-parent) and the degree \(2s\) construction.
The construction allows varying denominators, which are relevant to the [separate integral cutoff](Yin_L_G0457_v7.md#effective-cutoff).
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v47.md#fixed-degree-review) checks the full fixed-parent classification, including the strict signs on every grid node of the constructed sequences.

## Setting and lower bounds

Write \(p=2n+1>4s+3\), put \(r=p-2s\), \(m=(r-1)/2\), and set \(\theta_j=2\pi j/p\), \(b_j=2\cos\theta_j\) for \(1\le j\le n\).
The parent is \(F_s=(1+z^2)^2(1+z^2+\cdots+z^{2s-2})\), its trace is \(t^2E(t)\), and \(E(2\cos\theta)=\sin(s\theta)/\sin\theta\).
For \(Q(t)=t^2E(t)B_m(t)+H(t)\) the [phase and endpoint identities](Yin_L_G0464_v20.md#real-degree) give the residue signs and endpoint test as
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}\bigl(W(b_j)+(-1)^{j+1}H(b_j)\bigr),\qquad
 W(t)=\sqrt{2+t}\,t^2E(t)^2,\qquad
 -4s+(-1)^nH(-2)<0.
 \tag{2}
\]
At the last node \(W(b_n)\sim4s^2\pi/p\) as \(p\to\infty\).
The multiplicity bound requires \(\deg H\ge s+1\) for even \(s\) and \(\deg H\ge s\) for odd \(s\) along any unbounded sequence with a fixed degree bound.
These necessities use the signs alone.

For odd \(s\ge5\), suppose a sequence of \(H\) of degree at most \(s\) has the required signs for \(p\to\infty\).
Take a convergent subsequence of the polynomials normalized by their maximum absolute coefficient.
By the [vanishing argument](Yin_L_G0464_v20.md#fixed-parent), the limit has a zero at each of the \(s\) distinct zeros of \(J(t)=tE(t)\).
Thus it is a nonzero multiple of \(J\), for which \(|J(-2)|=2s\) and \(|J'(0)|=1\).
It follows that \(|H'(0)|/|H(b_n)|\to 1/(2s)\), and the terminal inequality \(|H(b_n)|>W(b_n)\) gives
\[
 \liminf p|H'(0)|\ \ge\ 2s\pi .
 \tag{3}
\]

Consider two adjacent initial nodes \(j,j+1\) near \(p/4\), and put \(y=j-p/4\).
All nodes at bounded index distance from \(p/4\) are initial for large \(p\), since the terminal block has length at most \(s+1\).
If the fractional part of \(p/4\) is \(1/4\), use one of the two left offsets \(-5/4,-1/4\).
If the fractional part is \(3/4\), use one of \(-3/4,1/4\).
Each choice of two offsets allows either parity of \(j\) and gives \(y^2+(y+1)^2\le13/8\).
The derivative of \(H\) has constant nonzero sign between these nodes for large \(p\), since its normalized derivative converges uniformly there to a nonzero multiple of \(J'(0)\).
When the derivative is positive choose \(j\) even, so \(H(b_j)<W(b_j)\) and \(H(b_{j+1})>-W(b_{j+1})\).
When the derivative is negative choose \(j\) odd, which gives the opposite two bounds.
In either case
\[
 |H(b_j)-H(b_{j+1})|<W(b_j)+W(b_{j+1}).
\]
Uniformly over the indicated offsets, \(b_j=-4\pi y/p+O(p^{-3})\), \(b_j-b_{j+1}\sim4\pi/p\), and \(W(b_j)=16\sqrt2\pi^2 y^2/p^2+o(p^{-2})\) since \(|E(0)|=1\).
The mean value theorem evaluates the difference quotient at a point tending to zero, where the derivative divided by \(H'(0)\) tends to one by normalized convergence.
Consequently \(\limsup p|H'(0)|\le 13\sqrt2\pi/2<10\pi\), contrary to (3).
This proves the lower bound \(s+1\) for every odd \(s\ge5\).

## Degree \(s+1\) from small shifts of the sine zeros

For fixed \(s\), take \(p\equiv1\pmod{2s}\) tending to infinity and write \(\alpha_k=k\pi/s\) for \(1\le k<s\).
At \(\alpha_k\) put \(\eta_k=k/(2s)\), the fractional part of \(p\alpha_k/(2\pi)\), and \(\lambda_k=-\eta_k+2\eta_k^2\).
For every \(y\in\mathbb Z-\eta_k\),
\[
                 |y-\lambda_k|\le2y^2 .
 \tag{4}
\]
At \(y=-\eta_k\) this is equality, and at \(y=1-\eta_k\) it follows from \(1-2\eta_k^2\le2(1-\eta_k)^2\).
All remaining \(y\) have \(|y|\ge1\), and \(|\lambda_k|\le1/8\).

Let \(G_p\) be the monic polynomial with the \(s-1\) roots \(2\cos(\alpha_k+2\pi\lambda_k/p)\).
It converges coefficientwise to \(E\).
Choose a fixed real \(K\) with
\[
  s\pi<K<\min\{3s\pi,\ s\pi/\sin(\alpha_{s-1}/2)\}.
\]
Take
\[
 H(t)=(-1)^{n+s-1}\frac Kp\,t^2G_p(t).
 \tag{5}
\]
Every grid trace \(b_j\) is nonzero because \(p\) is odd, and \(E(b_j)\ne0\) since \(p\) is coprime to \(2s\).
At nodes where \(G_p(b_j)\ne0\) put
\[
 V_p(j)=\frac{p\sqrt{2+b_j}\,E(b_j)^2}{|G_p(b_j)|}.
\]
Then \(V_p(j)>K\) implies positive residue independent of parity, and a node where \(G_p(b_j)=0\) is already positive.
We show that every preceding node \(j<n\) is positive for all sufficiently large \(p\) by considering any sequence of such nodes and passing to a subsequence on which \(\theta_j\) converges.

If its limit avoids \(\pi\) and all \(\alpha_k\), \(G_p(b_j)/E(b_j)\to1\) and \(V_p(j)\to\infty\), including when the limiting angle is zero.
If \(\theta_j\to\alpha_k\), write \(y=j-p\alpha_k/(2\pi)\in\mathbb Z-\eta_k\).
For \(|y|\to\infty\), the displacement \(2\pi\lambda_k/p\) is negligible compared to \(|\theta_j-\alpha_k|\).
The product formulas for \(E,G_p\) then give \(G_p(b_j)/E(b_j)\to1\), while \(p\sqrt{2+b_j}|E(b_j)|\sim 2\pi s |y|/\sin(\alpha_k/2)\to\infty\).
If \(y\) remains bounded, pass to a constant value.
When \(y=\lambda_k\) the grid trace is a root of \(G_p\).
Otherwise, the simple root at \(\alpha_k\) and the nonzero derivative of \(2\cos\theta\) there give \(G_p(b_j)/E(b_j)\to(y-\lambda_k)/y\).
Thus
\[
   V_p(j)\longrightarrow
   \frac{2\pi s\,y^2}{\sin(\alpha_k/2)|y-\lambda_k|}
   \ \ge\ \frac{s\pi}{\sin(\alpha_k/2)}>K
\]
by (4).

When \(\theta_j\to\pi\), \(E(-2)\ne0\) gives \(G_p(b_j)/E(b_j)\to1\).
Here \(\sqrt{2+b_j}|E(b_j)|=|\sin(s\theta_j)|/\sin(\theta_j/2)\sim s(\pi-\theta_j)\).
Since \(p(\pi-\theta_j)=(2(n-j)+1)\pi\), the lower limit of \(V_p(j)\) is at least \(3s\pi>K\) for \(j<n\).
These subsequences exhaust the possible limiting angles and grid offsets, proving the assertion at every preceding node.
For \(j=n\) the same calculation gives \(V_p(n)\to s\pi<K\).
Also \(G_p(b_n)\) has sign \((-1)^{s-1}\) for all sufficiently large \(p\), so (5) makes \((-1)^{n+1}H(b_n)\) negative with magnitude exceeding \(W(b_n)\).
The terminal block consists precisely of this node.
Equation (2) holds at the endpoint since \(H(-2)=O_s(1/p)\).
For each such \(p\) these finitely many strict inequalities persist on an open neighborhood of the coefficients of (5), which contains a rational polynomial of the same degree.

## The cubic at \(s=3\)

Here \(J(t)=t(t^2-1)\) and \(W(t)=\sqrt{2+t}\,J(t)^2\).
Take \(p\equiv13\pmod{24}\) tending to infinity and \(H(t)=-19J(t)/p\).
All grid values \(J(b_j)\) are nonzero because \(p\) is odd and coprime to three.
The perturbation term in (2) has sign \((-1)^j\operatorname{sgn}J(b_j)\).
At any initial node where that sign is negative it suffices to prove \(p\sqrt{2+b_j}|J(b_j)|>19\).

The three interior zeros have angles \(\pi/3,\pi/2,2\pi/3\) and respective trace values \(x=1,0,-1\).
At each, the two immediately straddling grid nodes have positive perturbation term.
Indeed the indices on the left are \(\lfloor p/6\rfloor,\lfloor p/4\rfloor,\lfloor p/3\rfloor\), of parities even, odd, even, respectively.
The derivative signs \(J'(1),J'(0),J'(-1)\) are positive, negative, positive, respectively, and trace decreases with angle.
Consequently any node with negative perturbation term near these zeros has angular index distance \(|y|=|j-p\alpha/(2\pi)|\) at least \(7/6,5/4,4/3\), respectively.
These bounds follow from the fractional parts \(1/6,1/4,1/3\) after removing the two straddling nodes.
For bounded \(y\) at a zero, pass to a constant value and use
\[
 p\sqrt{2+b_j}|J(b_j)|
 \longrightarrow 4\pi\sqrt{2+x}\,|J'(x)|\sin(\alpha)\,|y|.
\]
The three lower bounds on the limits are \(14\pi,\ 5\sqrt2\pi,\ 16\sqrt3\pi/3\), all strictly greater than \(19\).
The expression tends to infinity when \(\theta_j\) approaches one of these angles with \(|y|\to\infty\), or when its angular limit avoids the three zeros and \(\pi\).
Near \(\pi\) it is asymptotic to \(6(2(n-j)+1)\pi\), since \(J(-2)=-6\).
For \(j<n\) its lower limit there is at least \(18\pi\), whereas at \(j=n\) it tends to \(6\pi<19\).
Also \(n\) is even and \(J(b_n)<0\) for large \(p\), giving negative perturbation term at the last node.
The strict cut therefore holds after \(n-1\), and (2) gives the strict endpoint value \(-12+114/p\).
This supplies a rational cubic for \(s=3\).
For \(s=2\) the [explicit cubic for every \(p\equiv1\pmod8\), \(p\ge25\)](Yin_L_G0464_v20.md#sharp-cubic) also attains (1).
