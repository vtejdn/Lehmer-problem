# Reciprocal and integral coefficient limits are excluded for trace lifts of bounded reduced degree

<a id="limits"></a>
## The roots of every limiting lower polynomial

Fix \(K\ge1\) and consider actual positive-constant Pisot trace lifts of [bounded reduced lower degree](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v22.md#bounded-offsets).
Thus the normalized lift on branch \(j\) has the form
\[
       \Pi(z)/j=S_i(z^g)-z^N S_i(z^{-g}),\qquad
       N=2h+2,\qquad S_i(0)=1,\quad \deg S_i\le K ,
\]
where \(g\) is the gcd of all positive lower offsets and \(h\) is the trace degree.
For empty lower support set \(S_i=1,\ g=1\).
Along any sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\), every coefficient limit \(S\) of these lower polynomials, padded by zeros through \(K\), has at least one root on the unit circle and at least one root strictly outside it, with all roots of modulus at least one.
In particular \(S\) has degree at least two and \(0<|\operatorname{lc}(S)|<1\).
No such limit has integer coefficients or is reciprocal up to sign, with arbitrary variation of \(j\) and either degree parity.

The bounded-reduced-degree theorem gives bounded coefficient vectors, \(h\to\infty,\ g=o(h)\), and a nonconstant limit \(S\) with no open-disk root and with at least one circle root.
An integral \(S\) with these root restrictions would have leading coefficient of modulus one and all roots on the circle by their product and \(S(0)=1\).
An all-circle real polynomial with constant one satisfies \(w^nS(1/w)=\sigma S(w)\) for \(n=\deg S\) and \(\sigma=\operatorname{lc}(S)=\pm1\), since conjugation pairs reciprocal roots with multiplicities.
Conversely that reciprocal identity pairs each exterior root with an interior root, so under the known root restrictions it forces all roots onto the circle.
We exclude the reciprocal identity directly for real limiting coefficients.

<a id="fold"></a>
## Folding the denominator at the limiting degree

Suppose \(S_i\to S\) and \(w^nS(1/w)=\sigma S(w)\), with \(n\ge1\).
Put \(k=ng,\ p=N-k,\ A(z)=S(z^g)=\sum_{r=0}^k a_r z^r\), so \(p>k\) eventually and \(a_{k-r}=\sigma a_r\).
Write \(\|\cdot\|_{\rm coef}\) for the sum of absolute coefficients.
The exact form of the lift gives
\[
          \left\|\Pi/j-(1-\sigma z^p)A\right\|_{\rm coef}
                    \le2\|S_i-S\|_{\rm coef}=o(1).
\]
This estimate includes nonzero coefficients of \(S_i\) above the limiting degree \(n\).
Let \(B=\sum_{l=0}^N b_lz^l\) be the monic reciprocal integer denominator with constant one, and use its [actual signed inverse representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) \(B=(\Pi/j)J\).
The symmetric real signed measure \(\omega\) has mass one, negative mass tending to zero and support in the [zeros of \(\Pi\)](Yin_L_G0462_v64.md#dichotomy).
Write \(j_l=[z^l]J\) for \(l\ge0\) and \(j_l=0\) for \(l<0\).
Then \(j_0=1\), all \(j_l\) are uniformly bounded, and \(j_l+j_{-l}=2\int v^l\,d\omega(v)\) for every \(l\in\mathbb Z\).
Also \(M(B)>1\) tends to one, where \(M\) denotes multiplicative Mahler measure.

Let \(I=\sum_{0\le l<p}I_l z^l\) be the integer remainder of \(B\) modulo \(z^p-\sigma\).
Since \(N=p+k<2p\) and \(b_{p+l}=b_{k-l}\) for \(0\le l\le k\),
\[
            I_l=b_l+\sigma b_{k-l}\quad(0\le l\le k),\qquad
            I_l=b_l\quad(k<l<p).
\]
Throughout \(0\le l<p\) the coefficient norm estimate gives \(b_l=[z^l]AJ+o(1)\) uniformly by convolution with the bounded \(j_l\).
For \(0\le l\le k\), replacing \(r\) by \(k-r\) in the second summand of the folded expression gives
\[
            I_l=\sum_{r=0}^k a_r(j_{l-r}+j_{r-l})+o(1)
                =2\int v^l A(v^{-1})\,d\omega(v)+o(1).
\]
For \(k<l<p\) the same formula holds because all \(j_{r-l}=0\).
The comparison thus extends through and above \(h\), with the correct doubled constant when \(l=r\), including \(l=0,k\).

## Projection and integer rounding on a cyclic group

For every \(v\) in the support of \(\omega\),
\[
                    |A(v^{-1})|\,|1-\sigma v^p|=o(1)
\]
uniformly, since \(A\) is real, \(\Pi(v)=0\) and the coefficient norm bounds the circle supremum.
Project the complex measure \(2 A(v^{-1})\,d\omega(v)\) to its nearest roots \(\zeta^p=\sigma\) in angle, sharing a tied weight equally.
The resulting measure \(\nu\) has bounded variation and conjugate-symmetric weights.
If \(v=\zeta e^{i\phi}\) with \(|p\phi|\le\pi\), then \(p|\phi|\le(\pi/2)|1-\sigma v^p|\).
For \(0\le l<p\) it follows that \(|A(v^{-1})|\,|v^l-\zeta^l|=o(1)\) uniformly, including at tied nodes.
The projected real Fourier sequence \(f(l)=\int\zeta^l\,d\nu\) consequently satisfies \(\sup_{0\le l<p}|f(l)-I_l|=o(1)\).
On \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\), \(f(l+p)=\sigma f(l)\), and its Fourier algebra norm is bounded by the variation of \(\nu\).
Extend the integer function by \(I_{l+p}=\sigma I_l\) to obtain the same supremum approximation on the full cyclic group.

The [rounding lemma](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#rounding) gives a uniformly bounded Fourier algebra norm for this integer function, so the [finite-group coset theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#finite-abelian-fourier-algebra-norm) expresses it as a sum of a bounded number of signed coset indicators.
On \(0\le l<p\) each contributing coset is one finite progression with step \(t\mid2p\).
Multiplication of its generating polynomial by \(1-z^t\) leaves only its two ends, including for a singleton.
The product \(Q\) of these binomials, with one factor per contributing coset and with the empty product allowed, has bounded term count and \(M(Q)=1\).
The polynomial \(QI\) also has bounded term count, since each signed progression has had its own denominator cleared and all the other binomials number only \(O(1)\).

## Truncating the lower part and restoring the actual denominator

Put \(P_k=\sum_{l=0}^k b_lz^l\).
There is a multiplier \(U=1\) or \(U=1-\epsilon z^u\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\), such that \(UP_k\) has bounded term count after extraction.
For bounded \(g\) this follows from \(k\le Kg\).
For \(g\to\infty\), below \(g\) all positive Taylor coefficients of \(J\) equal the integer coefficients of \(B\).
The [local integer-prefix models](Yin_L_G0462_v68.md#low) apply at this scale because the actual signed measures have the single vanishing negative mass and \(2h\) distinct charged interior lifts, with \(g\le h\).
On \(0\le l\le\kappa g\) for some fixed \(0<\kappa<1/4\), these models are \(1\), \(1+\epsilon z^u\), or \((1-\epsilon z^u)^{-1}\).
The [all-interval estimate at the initial scale](Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial) uses only these matched models, integer coefficients of \(B\), and bounded support and absolute coefficient sum of \(\Pi/j\), which hold here with arbitrary coefficient denominators.
It therefore gives boundedly many terms of \(UB\) on each interval of length \(g\), taking \(U=1\) for the polynomial models or for \(u>\kappa g/4\), and \(U=1-\epsilon z^u\) in the remaining reciprocal case.
Indeed away from transitions the Gram matrix of characters weighted by \(U\) on short blocks is bounded by the matched model.
For the reciprocal model with large \(u\) the shorter blocks see only the zeroth moment, and with small \(u\) the binomial kills the atomic part.
On each fixed finite test the negative-part corrections tend to zero, so Bessel's inequality bounds the number of nonzero integral coefficients there.
Inside the transition intervals the ordinary characters have bounded Gram on pieces of diameter less than \(u\), after splitting at all active exponents and their \(u\)-translates.

These estimates cover \(UP_k\) through \(k\), where it agrees with \(UB\).
If \(U\ne1\), its remaining coefficients at \(k<l\le k+u\) equal \(-\epsilon b_{l-u}\).
On \(k-u<r\le k\) the unweighted coefficients \(b_r\) have bounded support as well.
Split at the at most \(2K+2\) numerator exponents, treating endpoints individually.
On each remaining piece the \(b_r\) are signed integrals of ordinary characters against twice the fixed partial sum \(\sum_{e<r}[z^e](\Pi/j)v^{-e}\), uniformly bounded on the circle.
All nonzero Gram differences have modulus less than \(u\) and their moments are zero in the matching reciprocal model, so the same finite-test Bessel bound applies.
This proves the truncation assertion at its upper edge.

Exact polynomial division and reciprocity give
\[
                   B=I+(z^p-\sigma)\,z^kP_k(1/z).
\]
For \(U^{\rm rev}(z)=z^{\deg U}U(1/z)\), the product \(U^{\rm rev}z^kP_k(1/z)\) is \(z^{k+\deg U}(UP_k)(1/z)\) and has bounded term count.
Multiplying the division identity by \(Q U^{\rm rev}\) therefore gives a nonzero integer polynomial of bounded term count, since \(QI\), \(Q\) and \(U^{\rm rev}\) have bounded term counts.
All multiplier factors are cyclotomic up to signs, so its measure is \(M(B)>1\) tending to one, contrary to the [fixed-support gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001).
This excludes every reciprocal limit.
Consequently a strictly exterior root occurs as well as a circle root.
The product of all root moduli is then greater than one, giving the stated strict upper bound on \(|\operatorname{lc}(S)|\) since \(S(0)=1\).

## Uniform separation in the fixed coefficient space

In the padded real coefficient space with constant one, let \(\mathcal R_K\) be the set of polynomials reciprocal up to sign in their own degree, including the constant one, and let \(\mathcal I_K\) be the integer coefficient vectors.
There are \(\varepsilon_K,c_K>0\) such that every input of reduced lower degree at most \(K\) with coefficient distance less than \(\varepsilon_K\) from \(\mathcal R_K\cup\mathcal I_K\) satisfies \(\theta-q\ge c_K\).
Here distance is taken in the maximum coefficient norm.
For each of the finitely many degrees and signs the reversal identities and vanishing of higher coefficients define a closed affine subset, so their union \(\mathcal R_K\) is closed.
The integer lattice is closed as well.
If the two uniform constants did not exist, a vanishing-excess sequence could approach this union arbitrarily closely.
The bounded normalized heights give a convergent coefficient subsequence whose limit would belong to the union, contrary to the exclusion just proved.
