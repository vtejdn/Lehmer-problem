# Six-factor rational normalization and two failed transfers

<a id="scope"></a>
## Question and inspection scope

On 2026-11-10, the main agent and an ordinary proof collaborator tested whether the [five-factor normalization](../claims/Yin_C_G0885_v6.md#argument) transfers directly to six factors.
The question is conditional on a supplied bounded middle pair; its algebra does not assume an unreviewed pair-selection theorem.
The complete calculations below show which arithmetic survives and give exact counterexamples to two proposed transfers: finite order of the new positive factor and neutrality from one effective zero.
The complete normalization and both controls have been [independently checked](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v14.md#six-factor-normalization-controls-review), with their exact conditional scope and failed-transfer limits retained.
No program, numerical root calculation or sampling experiment was used.

Use the positive integral lattice \(H\), standard hyperbolic plane, permitted even-norm parameters and fixed-cusp generators of C885 v6.
Let
\[
A=R E_vF_wE_uF_tE_sF_z,
\]
where \(R\) is an integral positive isometry.
All measures concern the complete original characteristic polynomial.

<a id="six-factor-normalization"></a>
## Exact normalization and an integral first parameter

Put
\[
a=u^2/2,\quad b=w^2/2,\quad c=(u,w),\quad
\kappa=1-c+ab>0,
\]
\[
h=u-aw,\quad j=(1-c)w+bu,\quad
r=h/\kappa,\quad q=(w-bu)/\kappa.
\]
The exact [middle-pair decomposition](../claims/Yin_C_G0885_v6.md#middle-pair-decomposition) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=R E_{v+r}D_{1/\kappa}QF_{t+q}E_sF_z\\
 &=R_0E_pD_{1/\kappa}F_TE_sF_z\\
 &=R_0D_{1/\kappa}E_PF_TE_sF_z,                         \tag{1}
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
R_0=RQ,\quad p=Q^{-1}(v+r),\quad T=t+q,\quad
P=\kappa p=Q^{-1}(\kappa v+h).
\]
All equalities are full matrix identities on the original real space.

Taking the \(H\)-adjoint of the formula for \(Q\) gives
\[
Q^{-1}x=x+\frac{w(h,x)-u(j,x)}{\kappa}.
\]
Since \(h^2=2a\kappa\) and \((j,h)=c\kappa\), one also has
\[
Q^{-1}h=(1-c)u+aw.
\]
Consequently
\[
P=\kappa v+w(h,v)-u(j,v)+(1-c)u+aw.                    \tag{2}
\]
This is an actual integral even-norm vector.
Every displayed coefficient is integral, and an integer combination of even-norm vectors has even norm because every cross pairing enters with a factor two.
Likewise,
\[
\kappa T=\kappa t+w-bu                                \tag{3}
\]
is integral of even norm.

Thus nonzero \(P,s,z\) have norm at least \(\sqrt2\), while nonzero \(T\) has norm at least \(\sqrt2/\kappa\).
In the middle form of (1), nonzero \(p=P/\kappa\) has the latter lower bound as well.
The entries of \(Q,Q^{-1},R_0,R_0^{-1}\), in the original integral basis, have denominators dividing \(\kappa\).
For \(R_0^{-1}\), use \(Q^{-1}R^{-1}\) and the actual integral inverse of \(R\).
No bound on the numerators or finite-order conclusion follows merely from this denominator statement.

If the supplied pair satisfies \(ab\le16\), then \(1\le\kappa\le25\).
This is the old Cauchy--Schwarz defect bound, without a new pair-selection premise.

<a id="dilation-and-cyclic-costs"></a>
## Opposite scaling and changed cyclic conjugacy

Pushing the dilation completely right instead gives
\[
A=R_0E_pF_{\kappa T}E_{s/\kappa}F_{\kappa z}D_{1/\kappa}.
\]
Both e- and f-parameter changes are necessary.

Put \(K=R_0D_{1/\kappa}\).
Exact covariance gives
\[
E_xK=K E_{\kappa R_0^{-1}x},\qquad
F_xK=K F_{\kappa^{-1}R_0^{-1}x}.                        \tag{4}
\]
For example, conjugation by the final f-transvection yields
\[
F_zAF_{-z}
=K F_{\kappa^{-1}R_0^{-1}z}E_PF_TE_s.
\]
Exchanging the cusps then reverses the dilation.
These remain exact complete-polynomial-preserving conjugacies, but the parameter changes are no longer just the norm-preserving rotations from the original integral positive-factor setting.

<a id="retained-real-expansion"></a>
## The boundary estimate survives with its multiplier

Use the middle expression in (1), and assume \(p,T,s,z\) are nonzero.
Put
\[
\ell_E=\min(\|p\|,\|s\|),\qquad
\ell_F=\min(\|T\|,\|z\|),\qquad \sigma=\ell_E\ell_F.
\]
Balancing by \(D_c\), where \(c=\sqrt{\ell_F/\ell_E}\), makes all four parameter lengths at least \(L=\sqrt{\sigma}\).
It commutes with the dilation and \(R_0\).

The four boundary maps of the balanced word are
\[
p'+\kappa^{-1}I(x),\quad -T'+I(x),\quad
s'+I(x),\quad -z'+I(x).
\]
Their full-vector multipliers are respectively \(\kappa\|x\|^2/2\) and \(\|x\|^2/2\) for each of the other three factors.
For \(\sigma>8\), choose \(\rho=(L+\sqrt{L^2-8})/2>\sqrt2\).
Since \(\kappa\ge1\), all four maps preserve \(\{\|x\|\ge\rho\}\).
The exact inversion-distance identity bounds the full Lipschitz product by \(\kappa^{-1}(2/\rho^2)^4<1\).
Its fixed point therefore gives an actual eigenvalue
\[
\Lambda\ge\kappa(\rho^2/2)^4
=\kappa\gamma(\sigma)^4.
\]
The proof retains every multiplier and the complete polynomial, just as in the [weighted v6 argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0885_v6.md#weighted-boundary-expansion).

In particular \(M(P_A)\le2\) implies \(\sigma<10\).
The nonzero floors above then bound one e-parameter and one f-parameter by \(5\sqrt2\,\kappa\).
This does not bound the other two effective parameters or dispose of the rational positive factor.

<a id="nonneutral-effective-zero"></a>
## An effective zero with the exact Lehmer core

In (1), \(P=0\) leaves
\[
A=R_0D_{1/\kappa}F_TE_sF_z.
\]
The two trailing transvections prevent the triangular characteristic-polynomial argument used for the four-factor case.
The following complete integer control has all six original parameters nonzero and the known positive Lehmer measure.

Take \(H=I_9\), let \(C b_i=b_{i+1}\) with indices modulo nine, and put \(\alpha=b_0+b_1\).
Choose
\[
u=b_3+b_4,\qquad w=b_4+b_5.
\]
Then \(a=b=c=1\), \(\kappa=1\), \(q=w-u=b_5-b_3\), and \(r=-q\).
The formula for \(Q\) has integral entries because its denominator is one, and the retained decomposition proves it is orthogonal.
Thus \(Q\) and \(Q^{-1}\) are integral positive isometries.

Set
\[
R=CQ^{-1},\qquad v=q,\qquad s=z=\alpha,
\qquad t=\alpha-C^{-1}\alpha-q
=b_1-b_8-b_5+b_3.
\]
The parameters \(v,w,u,s,z\) have squared norm two, and \(t\) has squared norm four.
They are all nonzero.
Here \(P=0\), \(R_0=C\), and \(T=\alpha-C^{-1}\alpha\), so
\[
A=C F_{\alpha-C^{-1}\alpha}E_\alpha F_\alpha.
\]
Conjugating the whole matrix by \(F_\alpha\) gives
\[
F_\alpha A F_{-\alpha}
=C F_{C^{-1}\alpha+\alpha-C^{-1}\alpha}E_\alpha
=C F_\alpha E_\alpha.
\]
Exchanging the two cusps produces the retained [L462 v14 Lehmer control](Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#lehmer-control), namely \(C E_\alpha F_\alpha\).
Consequently
\[
P_A(z)=(z-1)L(z),
\]
where
\[
L(z)=z^{10}+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1
\]
is that known Lehmer polynomial.
This disproves the six-factor implication from one effective zero to neutrality.
It is a known-value conjugacy control, not a new small measure or an obstruction to every longer-word normalization.

<a id="infinite-order-positive-factor"></a>
## A bounded integral pair with an infinite-order positive factor

Take
\[
H=I_2,\qquad R=I,\qquad u=(2,0),\qquad w=(0,2).
\]
Then \(a=b=2\), \(c=0\), \(ab=4\), \(\kappa=5\), and direct substitution gives
\[
Q=\frac15
\begin{pmatrix}-3&4\\-4&-3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(B_0=5Q\).
Direct integer multiplication gives
\[
B_0^2=
\begin{pmatrix}-7&-24\\24&-7\end{pmatrix}
\equiv4B_0\pmod5.
\]
Thus
\[
B_0^n\equiv4^{n-1}B_0\not\equiv0\pmod5
\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Its upper-left entry is also nonzero modulo five.
The least matrix denominator of \(Q^n\) is therefore exactly \(5^n\).
In particular \(Q\) has infinite order.

This bounded pair occurs in an actual six-factor integral word by setting \(v=t=s=z=(2,0)\).
The normalized parameters in (1) are
\[
P=(-4,12),\qquad T=(6/5,2/5),\qquad s=z=(2,0).
\]
No small-measure assertion is made for this word.

For \(x=(2,0)\), the rational correlation \((x,Q^nx)\) has denominator exactly \(5^n\) for every \(n\ge1\), by the upper-left entry calculation.
It is therefore neither integer-valued nor periodic.
Moreover
\[
\chi_Q(z)=z^2+\tfrac65z+1
\]
has all roots on the unit circle, while its integer denominator clearing
\(5z^2+6z+5\) has full Mahler measure five.
Thus the old neutral integer clearing cannot be applied to \(\chi_Q\) itself.

Both controls lie in integral orthogonal-basis lattices, already within the separate [canonical-cover exclusion](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v3.md#statement).
They test proof interfaces and are not proposed as counterexample search families.

<a id="remaining-original-arithmetic"></a>
## The remaining arithmetic question

Real orthogonality remains sufficient for the exterior eigenvalue estimates.
It is insufficient for applying the old finite cyclic correlation theorem to the new factor \(R_0\).
One effective zero must be treated on the actual remaining word instead of being declared neutral.

A continuation may try to recover a bounded original integral presentation.
Another possibility is to retain the original finite-order integral \(R\) and track every rational orthogonal correction as a bounded finite-rank factor, using the monic integrality of the full characteristic polynomial.
That possibility requires a complete new determinant, denominator and normalization argument.
The examples here do not refute it.

These exact controls expose the missing steps without establishing a general six-factor gap, a new finite target value or a sequence of positive measures tending to one.
The [unrestricted G0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) remains unresolved.
