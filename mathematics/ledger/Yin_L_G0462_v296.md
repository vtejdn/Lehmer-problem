# Exact runs at both ends of a relation on a Salem power progression

<a id="runs"></a>
## Two disjoint runs on a fixed progression below the threshold

Let \(r\) be a Salem number, write \(m=\log r\), and suppose \(m\) tends to zero along a sequence.
Take \(q=\lfloor(\log 2)/m\rfloor-1\), \(R=r^q\), and \(\delta=2-R\).
Then \(2(1-e^{-m})\le\delta<2(1-e^{-2m})\), so \(\delta\asymp m\).
The unit \(R\) has the same degree \(d\) as \(r\) because every embedding fixing \(R\) must send \(r\) to an exterior conjugate of modulus \(r\), of which there is only one.
Thus \(R\) is Salem and \(M(R)=R<2\).
The [density upper bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement) yields a nonzero ternary relation on its full progression of powers.

In every such relation, remove the common monomial and write \(F(X)=\sum_{i=0}^n c_i X^i\), with \(c_0,c_n\in\{-1,1\}\).
Set \(K=\lceil\log_R((R-1)/\delta)\rceil-1\).
Both intervals \([0,K]\) and \([n-K,n]\) are fully occupied, and each coefficient after the first when read inwards is opposite to its own endpoint.
For sufficiently small \(m\) the intervals are disjoint, hence every relation has weight at least \(2K+2=2\log_2(1/m)+O(1)\).
These are exact runs at step \(q\) for a prescribed progression, complementing the [variable-gap count](Yin_L_G0462_v292.md#critical).

Indeed, extend the coefficients by zero outside \([0,n]\).
Evaluation at \(R\) gives the nonnegative deficit identity
\[
 \sum_{i\ge1}(1+c_{n-i}/c_n)R^{-i}=\frac{\delta}{R-1}.
\]
Every positive digit costs at least \(R^{-i}\), which exceeds the full budget for \(i\le K\).
Reversal gives the same identity at the other end since \(R^{-1}\) is a conjugate.
Moreover the first positive deficit index \(h\) at either end satisfies
\[
 \log_R((R-1)/\delta)\le h\le\log_R(2R/\delta),
\]
by bounding the entire remaining tail by \(2R^{1-h}/(R-1)\).
For disjointness, the nonzero integral norm of \(1-r\) gives \(1\le (r-1)(1-r^{-1})2^{d-2}\), so \(d\ge 2\log_2(1/m)+2+o(1)\).
On the other hand \(K<\log_R((R-1)/\delta)\le\log_2(1/m)-1+o(1)\), using \(\delta\ge2m(1+O(m))\) and \(R=2-O(m)\).
As \(n\ge d\), the runs are disjoint.

## A Salem example with overlapping runs when the root condition is dropped

The [Salem construction](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#smy15-u002) at the integer Pisot number 2 exhibits the role of the small-measure root \(r\).
For \(n\ge4\), let \(F_n(X)=X^n+1-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X^j\).
It has a root \(R_n\in(1,2)\) because \(F_n(1)<0<F_n(2)\).
The polynomial \((X-1)F_n=X^n(X-2)+2X-1\) has exactly \(n-1\) distinct zeros on the unit circle.
Indeed the circle equation is \(z^n=(2z-1)/(2-z)\), where the right side is the degree-one Blaschke factor with zero \(1/2\).
Its argument derivative with respect to the argument of \(z\) is \(3/|2-z|^2\le3\), so the argument difference is strictly increasing with total variation \(2\pi(n-1)\).
The remaining two roots are \(R_n\) and its reciprocal, by reciprocity.
The integer minimal polynomial of \(R_n\) has constant term of modulus one and hence contains its contracting conjugate, since all other available roots have modulus one.
It cannot be quadratic because \(2<R_n+R_n^{-1}<5/2\), so \(R_n\) is Salem.
For each fixed \(1<x<2\), \(x^n(x-2)+2x-1<0\) eventually, whence \(R_n\to2\).
The identity \((2-R_n)R_n^n=2R_n-1\) now gives \(K=n+O(1)\) for this base.
Thus even the Salem conjugate condition at the powered base allows the two forced runs to overlap almost completely.
The small-measure root condition supplies the separate degree bound above, while the endpoint identities alone leave the middle of a long relation unrestricted.
