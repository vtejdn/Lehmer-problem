# Exact minima at the first possible row width and the two quartic seeds

<a id="short"></a>
## At width five only two quartic seeds need to be tested

Write \(M_{p,b}\) for the [finite coefficient minimum](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) with specified odd prime \(p\) and row degree cap \(b\).
Then \(M_{p,b}=2^p\) for every \(b\le3\).
For \(b=4,5\) the only seeds that could supply a qualifying coefficient sum at most \(2^p\), up to \(X\mapsto-X\), are
\[
  P_A=X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1,\qquad P_B=X^4-2X^3+X^2-2X+1.
\]
In fact \(M_{p,b}=2^p\) for all \(b\le5\) and all odd primes \(3\le p\le1009\), by the [exact two-seed computation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/readme.md#census).
For \(b=4\) a formula below gives the minimum for any odd prime from at most four rational nullspace calculations.

Let \(P\) denote a qualifying nontorsion reciprocal unit minimum of degree \(e\), and put \(z=\alpha^p\) for a root \(\alpha\).
By the [reflected elimination](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower), its powered minimum \(Q\) has degree \(e\) and \(Q^2\mid Y^b(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)\), a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(2b\).
Thus \(e\le b\) and the multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(P)\) is at most \(2\).
Its degree is even by inverse pairing.
In degree two a nontorsion reciprocal unit would have real trace of absolute value at least three, forcing \(M(P)\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2>2\).

For \(e=4\) write \(P=X^2T(X+X^{-1})\), where \(T\) is a monic irreducible quadratic over the integers.
If the two trace roots are nonreal, the two exterior roots of \(P\) have equal modulus \(\rho\le\sqrt2\).
The absolute imaginary part of a trace root is at most \(\rho-\rho^{-1}\le1/\sqrt2\), whereas a negative integral quadratic discriminant is at most \(-3\), requiring imaginary part at least \(\sqrt3/2\).
Hence both trace roots are real.
Any trace outside \([-2,2]\) has absolute value in \((2,5/2]\).
Two exterior traces of the same sign would differ by at most \(1/2\), contradicting their nonzero integral squared difference.
Two of opposite sign would have sum zero by integrality, hence would be \(\pm\sqrt c\) with an integer \(c\ge5\).
The corresponding two exterior roots would again have a common modulus \(\rho\le\sqrt2\), giving \(c=(\rho+\rho^{-1})^2\le9/2\).
Kronecker's theorem excludes both traces in \([-2,2]\), and irreducibility excludes either endpoint.
There is therefore exactly one exterior trace \(t\), which can be taken positive by changing \(X\) to \(-X\).
The other trace belongs to \((-2,2)\), so \(T=S^2-jS+c\) with \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\).
Since \(2<t\le5/2\), the integer \(c=t(j-t)\) lies in \([5j/2-25/4,2j-4)\).
The only choices are \((j,c)=(1,-3),(2,-1)\), giving the two displayed seeds.
Both have one exterior trace between \(2\) and \(5/2\) and one trace strictly inside the circle-trace interval.
Their quadratic trace polynomials are irreducible, and the negative value of \(t^2-4\) at the inner real embedding rules out its being a square in the trace field.
Both quartics are consequently irreducible.
Every positive power preserves their degree, since the fiber above the unique powered exterior root has size one and all fibers on conjugates have the same size.
At odd \(p\), the involution on row pairs \((U(Y),V(Y))\mapsto(U(-Y),-V(-Y))\) accounts for both seed signs with the same coefficient sums.

## A closed formula for the least cost at the width of a specified seed

Let \(P\) have even degree \(e\), take any degree-preserving integer power \(p\ge2\), and set \(b=e\).
For a polynomial of degree at most \(e\) write \(U^*(Y)=Y^eU(1/Y)\), retaining the same formal degree for every reversal.
For each sign compute the rational space of \(A\) of degree at most \(e\) satisfying
\[
        P(x)^2\mid A(x^p)\ \pm\ x A^*(x^p).
\]
This is a kernel over \(\mathbb Q\) obtained by taking the polynomial remainder modulo \(P^2\).
If either kernel has dimension different from one, no pair qualifies at width \(e\).
Otherwise let \(A,C\) be primitive integral generators at the plus and minus signs, and let \(A_i,C_i\) be their coefficients in degree \(i\).
If \(A_0C_e+A_eC_0=0\), there is again no pair.
In every other case the smallest qualifying cost for this seed, before the cutoff, is
\[
 \boxed{\quad\frac{\kappa}{2}\max\{\|A+C\|_1,\|A-C\|_1\},\qquad
 \kappa=
 \begin{cases}
  1&A\equiv C\pmod 2,\\
  2&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}\quad}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Generator signs do not affect (1).
Taking the least of the actual costs (1) for the two quartics and the fallback \(2^p\) gives \(M_{p,4}\) exactly for every odd prime.

To prove the formula, first drop the gcd and unequal-modulus conditions and let \(\mathcal K\) be the rational vector space of pairs whose graph is divisible by \(P^2\).
Reversing the graph about \(pe+1\) shows that \((U,V)\mapsto(V^*,U^*)\) is an involution on \(\mathcal K\).
For any two pairs in it, elimination of the term multiplied by \(x\) yields
\[
                Q^2\mid U_1V_2-U_2V_1 .
\]
Indeed \(P(x)^2\) divides the determinant evaluated at \(x^p\), the powered conjugates are distinct, and the power map is unramified at nonzero roots.
Every determinant has degree at most \(2e\), so is a rational scalar multiple of \(Q^2\).
If one row pair has unequal moduli, its determinant with its image under the involution is nonzero.
Cramer's rule expresses every member of \(\mathcal K\) as a rational **constant** combination of these two pairs, since all three determinants are scalar multiples of \(Q^2\).
The involution interchanges the two independent pairs, so it has one rational line of each sign.
Conversely, when the kernels have dimension one, the determinant of \((A,A^*)\) and \((C,-C^*)\) is \(-(AC^*+CA^*)\).
Its constant coefficient decides whether it vanishes, since \(Q(0)\) is a unit.
A mixed pair has a nonzero reflected determinant precisely when both of its components on these lines are nonzero and this constant coefficient is nonzero.

Every such mixed pair is automatically coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\).
Indeed, if \(Q\) divides both entries then they must be constant multiples of \(Q\), so their graph can be divisible by \(P^2\) only if the remaining linear factor vanishes at \(\alpha\).
This would force that linear polynomial to be zero.
Any other common irreducible divisor can be cancelled while retaining both copies of \(P\), because its substitution does not vanish at \(\alpha\).
The resulting pair would have lower width and still have unequal moduli, contradicting the degree bound \(e\le b\) from reflection alone.

For integral rows \(U+V^*=kA,\ U-V^*=lC\) with integers \(k,l\) by primitivity, and the necessary and sufficient parity condition is \(kA\equiv lC\bmod2\).
An admissible pair requires \(kl\ne0\).
Its cost is \(\frac12\max(\|kA+lC\|_1,\|kA-lC\|_1)\), an even convex function in either variable separately.
It is therefore nondecreasing in each absolute value with the other fixed.
The two integers must have the same parity because neither generator is zero modulo two.
Odd choices are possible exactly when \(A\equiv C\bmod2\).
The least permitted absolute values are thus attained simultaneously at \(k=l=\kappa\), giving (1).

## Uncapped positive and negative controls

At \(p=3\), width four on \(P_A\) gives the primitive coefficient vectors
\[
    A=(-1,1,-2,-2,2),\qquad C=(1,-3,6,-6,2)
\]
in ascending order.
Their constant determinant is zero by \((-1)2+2(1)=0\), so this seed has no qualifying pair of width four at any coefficient height for this prime.
For \(P_B\), the generators are \((1,-20,34,4,1)\) and \((41,-148,386,-316,89)\).
They agree modulo two, their constant test gives \(130\), and (1) gives the true minimum \(516\).

At width five and \(p=3\), \(P_A\) has least uncapped cost \(45\).
One attaining pair has ascending coefficient vectors \((0,2,-13,17,-10,3)\) and \((-1,8,-17,10,-1,-1)\).
Its graph is exactly
\[
     P_A(x)^2(-x^8+x^7+x^6+2x^5-3x^3-2x^2-x).
\]
The pair is coprime, its constant autocorrelation difference is nonzero, and the [integer lattice census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/readme.md#controls) verifies minimality and gives both seeds' uncapped minima at \(p=3,5,7\).
These witnesses exceed the cutoff in the finite minimum.
The exclusion through \(1009\) concerns a fixed bounded width, while the limit in C408 assumes that the allowed width tends to infinity.
