# Coprime rows with short reduced traces at the angular cost of the powered input

<a id="rows"></a>
## Both individual remainders and cofactors on the same rows

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal nontorsion unit polynomial of degree \(d=2m\ge4\), with a root \(\alpha\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(h=m(P)\).
For a degree-preserving prime \(p\ge5\), put \(\beta=\alpha^p,\ Q=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\beta),\ q=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), and \(L=ph+\log(2dp)\).
With normalized circle measure \(\mu\), set
\[
             J(Q)=\int_{\mathbb T}\big(\log|Q(X)|-ph\big)_+\,d\mu(X).
\]
For either \(\sigma=\pm1\) there are two primitive integral diagonals \(F_i=A_i(x^p)+\sigma x A_i^*(x^p)\), with \(A_i(0)\ne0,\ \deg A_i=n=4d,\ A_i^*(X)=X^nA_i(1/X)\), and \(\gcd(A_i,A_i^*)=1\).
Both are divisible by \(P^2\), have nonzero values \(A_i(\beta)\), and the two value and Euler derivative vectors at \(\beta\) have nonzero determinant.
Write \(R_i\) for the noncyclotomic part of \(F_i/P^2\) with multiplicities.
In the [odd trace chart](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd), each row has \(u_i\ne0,\ v_i/u_i\notin\mathbb Q\) and nonzero derivative remainder \(D_i\).
With \(N_+\) the norm from \(\mathbb Q(q)\), let \(S_{u_i}\) denote the [primitive squarefree part](Yin_L_G0462_v215.md#obstruction) of the reciprocal lift of its reduced first trace.
The simultaneous bounds are
\[
       m(S_{u_i}),\ \frac{\log|N_+D_i|}{m}\ll L,\qquad
       m(R_i)\ll L+J(Q).                                                \tag{1}
\]
All implied constants are absolute.
In particular, on a sequence with \(d\to\infty,\ h\to0\) in the [fourth-logarithmic window](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement), \(J(Q)=o(p)\) suffices for all three quantities in (1) to be \(o(p)\) on both rows.
Conversely, a fixed positive lower bound on \(m(R)/p\) for all such short-trace rows along that sequence would require \(J(Q)\gg p\) on the same powered inputs.

## Keeping the original jets while removing every common root

Take \(l=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) and the two short original rows \(A\) **before** fixed-factor division in the [space of L206](Yin_L_G0462_v206.md#space), with the [simultaneous nonvanishing choices](Yin_L_G0462_v206.md#remainders).
Their coefficients and those of their reduced \(U,V\) in the basis \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{m-1}\) have logarithmic size \(O(L)\).
Here \(C_j(X+X^{-1})=X^j+X^{-j}\).
Put \(A^\#=X^nA(1/X)\), including when an endpoint vanishes.
The difference \(E=A-A^\#\) is nonzero at \(\beta\), since
\[
  \beta^{-n/2}E(\beta)=-\sigma(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})U(q)\ne0
\]
by the first jet and its inverse conjugate.
The reciprocal polynomial \(Q\) has degree \(d\) and constant term one.
Consider the row
\[
 B=A+Q^2\{a(1+X^{2d})+bX^d\},\qquad a,b\in\{0,\ldots,n+2\}.
\]
The added polynomial is invariant under reversal about \(n\), so \(B-B^\#=E\).
At every nonzero root \(z\) of \(E\), one has \(Q(z)\ne0\) by irreducibility and the displayed evaluation.
The coefficient of \(b\) in \(B(z)\) is then nonzero.
The constant and degree-\(n\) coefficients of \(B\) are affine linear functions of \(a\) of slope one.
The product of these two endpoint forms and one form \(B(z)\) for each distinct nonzero root of \(E\) is a nonzero polynomial of total degree at most \(n+2\).
The grid contains a point where this product is nonzero, by the univariate root bound applied twice.
This choice gives both endpoints and coprimality.
The added multiple of \(Q^2\) preserves the value and first derivative at \(\beta\), so it also preserves both centered jets and divisibility of the diagonal by \(P^2\).
Dividing by the positive scalar content gives a primitive row and diagonal, as their two residue classes are disjoint.
Its two reduced traces and its remainder are divided by the same content, by the integral odd chart and uniqueness of the reduced representatives.
This preserves every stated nonvanishing and the two-row determinant up to nonzero scalar.

The nonzero remainder has the asserted norm bound by the [reduced-coefficient estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v199.md#norm).
For \(r=\deg U\), the lift \(X^rU(X+X^{-1})\) has coefficient sum in absolute value at most \(2m\) times the largest absolute Chebyshev coefficient.
The primitive squarefree part divides this nonzero integer polynomial, whose measure is bounded by the logarithm of that sum.
It is unchanged by scalar content removal.
On the unit circle the original diagonal has modulus at most \(\exp(O(L))\).
The added diagonal is \((1+\sigma x)Q(x^p)^2\{a(1+x^{2dp})+b x^{dp}\}\), of modulus at most \(6(n+2)|Q(x^p)|^2\).
The logarithm of the modulus of their sum is therefore at most \(O(L)+2(\log|Q(x^p)|-ph)_+\).
Integration, power invariance of \(\mu\) and removal of scalar content prove the cofactor bound in (1).

<a id="energy"></a>
## The angular fluctuation and the input discriminant

For a monic squarefree integer polynomial \(H\) of degree \(d\) and roots \(\gamma_i\), write \(z_i=\gamma_i\) if \(|\gamma_i|\le1\) and \(z_i=1/\overline{\gamma_i}\) otherwise, and put \(s_k=\sum_i z_i^k\).
Define \(V(H)=\int_{\mathbb T}(\log|H|-m(H))^2\,d\mu\).
The boundary logarithmic Fourier series and Parseval give
\[
 V(H)=\tfrac12\sum_{k\ge1}|s_k|^2/k^2 .
\]
Indeed each centered root contribution is \(\log|1-z_i e^{-it}|\).
The power series first applies to \(\rho z_i\) with \(\rho<1\), then converges in \(L^2\) as \(\rho\uparrow1\) by square summability of \(1/k\), and pointwise away from the finitely many circle roots.
In particular \(J(Q)\le\sqrt{V(Q)}/2\), since the centered function has integral zero.

The [regularized discriminant energy](../claims/Yin_C_G0390_v1.md#actual-powered-salem-discriminant-energy) used for Salem circle roots extends to all these reflected roots.
For \(0<\rho<1\), summing the absolutely convergent logarithmic series gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_\rho(H)&=\sum_{k\ge1}\rho^{2k}|s_k|^2/k
            =-\sum_{i,j}\log|1-\rho^2z_i\overline{z_j}|\\
 &\le d\log\frac1{1-\rho^2}-d(d-1)\log\rho
                  +2(d-1)m(H)-\log|\operatorname{disc}H| .             \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For the off-diagonal bound, \(|1-\rho^2w|\ge\rho|1-w|\) for \(|w|\le1\), by squaring.
Also \(|1-z_i\overline{z_j}|\ge |\gamma_i-\gamma_j|/(\max(1,|\gamma_i|)\max(1,|\gamma_j|))\).
For roots on opposite sides of the circle there is equality in the latter comparison.
For two interior or two exterior roots it follows from \(|1-z_i\overline{z_j}|\ge|z_i-z_j|\).
Summing over ordered pairs gives precisely the displayed discriminant and measure terms.
The diagonal uses \(1-\rho^2|z_i|^2\ge1-\rho^2\).

With \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\), the first \(d\) terms of \(2V(H)\) are at most \(e^2 E_\rho(H)\), and the remaining terms are at most \(d^2\sum_{k>d}k^{-2}\le d\).
For \(H=Q\), (2) thus gives \(V(Q)\ll d\log(2d)+dph\).
Consequently (1) gives simultaneous \(o(p)\) bounds for every degree-preserving prime on a shrinking sequence whenever \(d\log(2d)=o(p^2)\) and \(dh=o(p)\).
Keeping the actual discriminant in (2) also gives an individual sufficient test when its right side plus \(d\) is \(o(p^2)\).

## Averaging over primes of exponentiation

Let \(5\le Y\le d\) and let \(\Pi\) be any nonempty set of degree-preserving primes in \((Y,2Y]\).
For \(p\in\Pi\) write \(Q_p=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\alpha^p)\).
There is a uniform bound using the original input,
\[
 \frac1{|\Pi|}\sum_{p\in\Pi}\frac{V(Q_p)}{p^2}
       \ll \frac{d(\log(2d)+h)}{Y|\Pi|}+\frac1Y .                     \tag{3}
\]
Indeed reflection into the disk commutes with powering and all \(d\) conjugates are retained.
With \(s_k\) now formed from \(P\), the Fourier formula gives exactly
\[
 2\sum_{p\in\Pi}V(Q_p)/p^2
       =\sum_{n\ge1}\omega_\Pi(n)|s_n|^2/n^2,\qquad
 \omega_\Pi(n)=\#\{p\in\Pi:p\mid n\}.
\]
The distinct integers \(n/p\) for these primes are at most \(n/Y\), so \(\omega_\Pi(n)\le n/Y\).
For \(n\le D=d^2\) the sum is therefore at most \(e^2 E_{\exp(-1/D)}(P)/Y\), which is \(O(d(\log(2d)+h)/Y)\) by (2) and the nonzero integral discriminant.
For \(n>D\) use \(|s_n|\le d\) and sum over \(n=pk\).
Since \(D/p\ge1\), the tail is at most \(O(\sum_{p\in\Pi}d^2/(pD))=O(|\Pi|/Y)\).
This proves (3).

For example fix \(\eta>0\) and take \(Y=\sqrt d\,(\log(2d))^{1+\eta}\).
The [dyadic prime and degree argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#primes) gives \(\gg Y/\log Y\) primes in the interval and at most \(O(\log d/\log Y)\) failing degree preservation.
Along any sequence with \(d\to\infty,\ h\to0\), the right side of (3) for all good primes is \(o(1)\).
Outside a proportion \(o(1)\) of the primes in the interval one has \(J(Q_p)/p=o(1)\), for example by discarding those with \(V(Q_p)/p^2\) above the square root of the bound in (3).
On every remaining prime (1) gives both primitive coprime rows with small normalized cofactor, squarefree trace measure and individual remainder norm.
Their span is \(4pd+1\).
This averaged range is larger than the fourth-logarithmic range, where (1) supplies an input-specific angular condition and (3) alone leaves its required scale open.
