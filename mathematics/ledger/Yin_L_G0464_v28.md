# Rational linear cut factors cannot match the fourth zero-profile coefficient

<a id="rational-factor"></a>
## A polynomial factor on the full grid

Take the [fixed zero profile and its integral coordinates](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-coordinates) on the full odd grid \(p=2n+1\ge5\), \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\) for \(1\le j\le n\).
Write \(E_{-1}=0,\ E_0=1,\ E_{i+1}=tE_i-E_{i-1}\), \(B_i=E_i+E_{i-1}\), and \(h=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_i E_i\in\mathbb Z[t]\) for the canonical representative of degree below \(n\).
The residues are \(\rho_j=(4-b_j^2)h(b_j)/p\).
Require a strict cut with \(\rho_j>0\) through some \(1\le c<n\) and \(\rho_j<0\) thereafter.
Agreement of the corresponding lift with \(P_{*,0}=(1+z^2)^2/((1-z)(1-z^2))\) through index four prescribes \(a_0=2,a_1=0,a_2=4\) when \(n\ge3\).
Equivalently, its first three residue moments are
\[
                   \sum_j\rho_j=2,\qquad \sum_j b_j\rho_j=0,
                         \qquad \sum_j b_j^2\rho_j=6.             \tag{1}
\]

There is no such input at any odd conductor for which the polynomial identity
\[
       h(t)=(At+\beta)v(t),\qquad A\ge1,\quad
       \beta\in\mathbb Z,\quad\gcd(A,\beta)=1,\quad v\in\mathbb Z[t]
       \tag{2}
\]
holds and \(v(b_j)>0\) at every node.
There is no bound on coefficient heights and no endpoint hypothesis.
The [earlier rational linear factor times an integer square](../claims/Yin_C_G0880_v4.md#statement) is included when its square is nonzero on the full grid.
Here positivity is needed only at the grid nodes, and a different cofactor may be chosen at each conductor.
The separate conclusion for multiplication modulo \(B_n\) appears [below](#quotient-order).
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v65.md#rational-factor-review) includes the complete finite comparison and the distinction between the polynomial and quotient-order cases.
The [unrestricted-height quotient-order bound](Yin_L_G0464_v29.md#modular-cut) subsequently gave an explicit conductor ceiling for that second case.

The strict cut and the positive cofactor put \(\alpha=-\beta/A\) in the open gap \((b_{c+1},b_c)\).
For any separator \(u\) in that gap the terms \((2-b_j)(b_j-u)\rho_j\) are positive.
By (1) their sum is \(-6-4u\).
Thus \(3A/2<\beta<2A\).
Define positive numbers
\[
 F_j=(2-b_j)(4-b_j^2)(Ab_j+\beta)^2v(b_j).
\]
The same three moments give their exact sum
\[
                          \sum_j F_j=p(4\beta-6A).             \tag{3}
\]
In particular \((4\beta-6A)/A^2\le(2A-4)/A^2\le1/4\), since \(\beta\le2A-1\) and \((A-4)^2\ge0\).

<a id="deleted-product"></a>
## The product after omitting the nearest pole

The [covering box from the first two moments](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0155/versions/v2/readme.md#covering-box) gives \(|a_i|\le p^2(i+1)/4\), using the strict signs alone.
Put \(d=\deg h\), \(h^*(z)=z^dh(z+z^{-1})\), and \(v^*(z)=z^{d-1}v(z+z^{-1})\).
The coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm of \(h^*\) is at most \(\sum_{i=0}^d(i+1)|a_i|\le p^5\), by the \(i+1\) Laurent monomials in \(E_i(z+z^{-1})\).
Write \(w+w^{-1}=\alpha\) with \(|w|=1\), so \(h^*=A(z-w)(z-\bar w)v^*\).
For a polynomial of degree at most \(2d\), division by a unit-modulus linear factor bounds each quotient coefficient by the dividend's coefficient norm, by synthetic division from the leading end.
Two such divisions thus give \(\|v^*\|_1\le(2d)^2p^5/A\le p^7\).
The absolute leading coefficient of \(h\) is \(|a_d|\ge A\), which also gives \(A\le\lceil p^2n/4\rceil\).

Let \(e^{2\pi i k/p}\) be a nearest full-circle grid point to \(w\).
Remove the folded node of \(k\) if \(k\not\equiv0\bmod p\), leaving a set \(S\) of \(N\ge m=n-1=(p-3)/2\) half-grid nodes.
If \(\delta\) is the angular difference from \(w\) to that nearest point, then \(|\delta|\le\pi/p\) and
\[
 \prod_{\ell\ne k}|w-e^{2\pi i\ell/p}|
 =\left|\frac{\sin(p\delta/2)}{\sin(\delta/2)}\right|\ge\frac{2p}{\pi}.
\]
Here the limiting quotient at zero is \(p\), and the inequality uses the lower chord bound for the numerator.
Removing the indices \(0,-k\) as well, when present, costs at most two factors of magnitude at most two.
Folding the remaining conjugate pairs and using
\(|w-\zeta^j|\,|w-\zeta^{-j}|=|b_j-\alpha|\), where \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}\), gives \(\prod_{j\in S}|b_j-\alpha|\ge p/(2\pi)>p/8\).

The complete products \(\prod_j(2-b_j)\) and \(\prod_j(4-b_j^2)\) both equal \(p\) by [the endpoint values of \(B_n\)](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product).
The omitted node costs at most 16 in the product of these two weights.
Also \(\prod_j v(b_j)=\operatorname{Res}(B_n,v)\) is a positive integer and every \(v(b_j)\le p^7\).
It follows that
\[
 \prod_{j\in S} F_j\ge \frac{A^{2N}}{1024p^3},\qquad
 \frac{4\beta-6A}{A^2}\ge
 \frac{N}{p}(1024p^3)^{-1/N}
 \ge \frac{m}{p}(1024p^3)^{-1/m}.                              \tag{4}
\]
The middle inequality uses (3), positivity and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on \(S\).

For \(p\ge65\), \(1024p^3<(15/8)^m\).
At \(p=65\) this is the integer inequality \(1024\cdot65^3\cdot8^{31}<15^{31}\), and the left side \(1024p^3\) increases by a factor at most \((67/65)^3<15/8\) at each increment by two.
The last expression in (4) therefore exceeds \((31/65)(8/15)=248/975>1/4\), contradicting (3).
This proves the large-conductor exclusion.

<a id="finite-test"></a>
## The remaining conductors

For \(37\le p\le63\), \(1024p^3<3^m\) follows in the same way from \(1024\cdot37^3<3^{17}\) and \((39/37)^3<3\).
Equation (4) then gives \((4\beta-6A)/A^2\ge(17/37)/3>1/8\), whence \(A<16\) because \(4\beta-6A<2A\).
For smaller conductors use the leading-coefficient bound above.
At any conductor (4) further requires \((mA)^m\le(2p)^m1024p^3\).

Set \(R_i=A^i B_i(-\beta/A)\).
The recurrence gives \(R_0=1,\ R_1=A-\beta,\ R_{i+1}=-\beta R_i-A^2R_{i-1}\).
Using every node in the product of the \(F_j\), including the positive integral cofactor norm, gives the necessary inequality
\[
             [p(4\beta-6A)]^n\ge n^n p^2 R_n^2 .              \tag{5}
\]
The following exact integer calculation tests (5) on the entire remaining range.

~~~python
from math import gcd

tested = 0
survivors = []
for p in range(7, 64, 2):
    n = (p - 1) // 2
    m = n - 1
    cap = (p*p*n + 3) // 4 if p < 37 else 15
    for A in range(1, cap + 1):
        if (m*A)**m > (2*p)**m * 1024*p**3:
            continue
        for beta in range(3*A//2 + 1, 2*A):
            if gcd(A, beta) != 1:
                continue
            tested += 1
            previous, current = 1, A - beta
            for i in range(1, n):
                previous, current = current, -beta*current - A*A*previous
            if (p*(4*beta-6*A))**n >= n**n * p*p * current**2:
                survivors.append((p, A, beta))
assert tested == 7864 and survivors == [(7, 5, 9)]
~~~

At \(p=7\) the three prescribed coefficients exhaust the chart and force \(h=2+4E_2=4t^2-2\), which has no rational root.
The smaller allowed conductor \(p=5\) has degree-six reciprocal lifts, whose fourth and second coefficients must coincide, whereas the fixed profile gives eight and four respectively.
This disposes of every conductor in (2).

<a id="quotient-order"></a>
## Multiplication in the full quotient order

Suppose instead that \(h=(At+\beta)v\bmod B_n\), where \(v\in\mathbb Z[t]\) is positive on every node, with the same primitive \(A,\beta\), strict cut and fourth-index prefix.
Then (3) and (5) still hold since they use only grid values.
There is no sequence of such inputs at odd composite conductors tending to infinity, even with varying \(A,\beta\).
At prime conductors tending to infinity the same conclusion holds whenever
\[
         \log(A+1)=o\!\left((p/(\log p)^2)^{1/4}\right).       \tag{6}
\]
In particular it covers every fixed rational cut and every polynomial bound on \(A\), with no bound on the degree or height of \(v\).
For unbounded prime conductors outside (6), the polynomial-divisor conclusion above does not settle the modular problem.

Indeed (5) gives the exact lower bound
\[
 \frac{4\beta-6A}{A^2}\ge
          \frac np \left(p^2|B_n(\alpha)|^2\right)^{1/n}.      \tag{7}
\]
For \(\alpha=-\beta/A\in(-2,-3/2)\), the algebraic number \(w\) determined above is nontorsion, because a rational torsion trace would be an integer.
Its degree is two and its absolute logarithmic height is \(\tfrac12\log A\), from the primitive polynomial \(Az^2+\beta z+A\) and its two unit-circle roots.
Let \(k=\max(1,\lceil\log A\rceil)\) and choose any \(y\) with \(y^k=w\).
Its degree is between 2 and \(2k\), and \(h_{\mathrm{Weil}}(y)=h_{\mathrm{Weil}}(w)/k\le1\) by the power identity for heights.
The [bounded-height logarithmic-form estimate](../audits/literature/Dim16/Yin_U001.md#dim16-reported-bound), applied to \(y\) with exponent \(kp\), therefore gives
\[
      -\log|1-w^p|\le C k^4\log(2k)\log(kp)
\]
with an absolute constant.
Since \(|B_n(\alpha)|=|1-w^p|/|1-w|\), \(|1-w|\le2\), and \(|1-w|\ge1\) here, condition (6) makes \(n^{-1}\log|B_n(\alpha)|\to0\).
Equation (7) has lower limit at least \(1/2\), contradicting the upper bound \(1/4\).

For composite \(p\), take a grid point nearest to \(\alpha\) in the trace coordinate and choose an entire layer of exact root-of-unity order \(d\mid p,\ d>1\), that omits this point.
Such a layer exists since \(p\) has at least two divisors greater than one.
At each node in this layer \(|b_j-\alpha|\ge16/p^2\), by the [minimum full-grid spacing](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0155/versions/v2/readme.md#covering-box) and the triangle inequality with the nearest point.
The product of the positive \(v(b_j)\) over the layer is a positive integer by real cyclotomic conjugacy.
The canonical bound \(|h(b_j)|\le p^5\) and the identity at these nodes thus imply \(16A/p^2\le p^5\) on taking the geometric mean.
In particular \(A\le p^7\) and (6) applies.

## Orders of the factors that carry the signs

There is also an elementary restriction when only the grid signs are given by a product or quotient of real cyclotomic trace polynomials of orders \(q\ge3\), times a constant sign and a factor positive at every node.
Require each displayed numerator and denominator after cancellation to be nonzero on the grid.
Cancel the even exponents for this sign test.
Some order must remain, since otherwise the sign is constant.
If \(Q\) is the largest remaining order, the strict cut and the fourth-index prefix imply \(p\le\max(24,Q^2)\).

In normalized angles \(\theta/(2\pi)\in(0,1/2)\), the first sign change of the prescribed rational function is at \(1/Q\).
Indeed its real trace factor contains the primitive root with exponent one, and its remaining exponent is odd.
Distinct primitive angles of orders at most \(Q\) differ by at least \(1/Q^2\).
If \(p>\max(24,Q^2)\), the two grid nodes immediately flanking \(1/Q\) are therefore on opposite sides of exactly one sign change.
A grid node at \(1/Q\) would already violate the nonvanishing assumption.
Both flanking nodes have normalized angle at most \(1/3+1/p<3/8\), hence trace greater than \(-\sqrt2>-3/2\).
But (1) makes every separator smaller than \(-3/2\), so both nodes must be positive.
This proves the stated restriction and applies also when the positive multiplier and the magnitudes of the exponents vary unrestrictedly.
