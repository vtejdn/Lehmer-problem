## 3. A classical finite norm-two determinant lemma, with all needed steps

We recall the finite argument at the precise scope used below.

**Lemma.** Such a lattice is an orthogonal sum of indecomposable root lattices of ranks r_i, each with discriminant at most r_i+1.
More precisely the connected possibilities have discriminants r+1 for a path,4 for a three-arm graph of lengths(2,2,e), and3,2,1 for lengths(2,3,3),(2,3,4),(2,3,5), where each arm length includes its common center.

*Proof of the basis step.* Choose a real linear functional nonzero on every root, and call its positive roots positive.
A positive root is simple if it is not a sum of two positive roots.
Every positive root is an integral nonnegative sum of simple roots: repeatedly decompose, and note that each summand has smaller positive functional value in a FINITE root set.
All of R is therefore integrally spanned by the simple set B, and the assumed original generators show L=Z B.

Distinct simple roots have inner product0 or-1.
Integrality and Cauchy--Schwarz leave only0,+/-1 for distinct nonopposite norm2 vectors.
If their product were+1, their difference would have norm2 and one sign of that difference would be positive.
One of the original two roots would then decompose into two positive roots, a contradiction.
The simple roots are linearly independent: a nontrivial real relation can be separated into positive combinations of two disjoint subsets, say x=y.
Their cross inner product is nonpositive, whereas x=y nonzero would have positive norm.
Neither combination can be zero because the chosen functional is positive on its terms.
Thus B is an integral basis, with Gram matrix2I minus the adjacency matrix of a finite simple graph.

*Proof of the graph/determinant step.* Decompose this graph into connected components.
Each component is a tree: a cycle's root sum would have norm at most0, including any extra chords.
A vertex with four neighbors is impossible because twice its root plus the four neighboring roots has norm0 in the induced star.
