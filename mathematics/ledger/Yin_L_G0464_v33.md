# A stationary algebraic-unit law satisfying the marginal and ordered residue tests

<a id="stationary-law"></a>
## A common model for two necessary conditions

Let \(t=2\cos\theta\) with \(\theta\) uniform on \([0,2\pi]\).
Given \(t\), let \(\eta_t\) assign equal mass to the two roots of \(u+u^{-1}=4+t\), counting multiplicity.
Write \(\eta=\mathbb E_t\eta_t\).
It is nonatomic, has support \([3-2\sqrt2,3+2\sqrt2]\), has mean two by the sum of the roots, and has \(\int\log u\,d\eta=0\) by their product.

There is a stationary process with marginal \(\eta\) that satisfies every [independent arithmetic marginal test](../claims/Yin_C_G0893_v1.md#statement) and every [ordered dyadic inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#statement).
To define it, with probability \(1/4\) use one draw from \(\eta\) at all times.
With probability \(3/4\), sample \(t\) once and at integer time \(j\) draw independently from \(\eta_{(-1)^j t}\), conditional on \(t\).
The latter component is stationary by the symmetry of \(t\).
Write \((u,v)\) for successive draws and use \(T_m(z+z^{-1})=z^m+z^{-m}\).
The ordered tests are
\[
 \mathbb E\log|T_m(v-2)-T_{2m}(u-2)|\ \ge\ \log(2m),
                              \qquad m=1,2,4,\ldots .                   \tag{1}
\]
Its successive values satisfy the algebraic relation \((u-v)[uv(u+v-8)+u+v]=0\), and the diagonal has mass at least \(1/4\).
This positive mass violates the [ordered difference test below](#difference) for actual shrinking odd-grid sequences.
The independent reviewer verified the [stationary law and dyadic certificate](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v75.md#stationary-law-review) and the [ordered difference test](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v75.md#ordered-difference-review).

<a id="marginal"></a>
## Independent integer tests and the logarithmic potential

For \(0\ne F\in\mathbb Z[u_1,\ldots,u_h]\), take the product of its values over the two roots in each of the \(h\) independent fibers.
Separate symmetry and the monic quadratic equations make this product a polynomial in \(t_1,\ldots,t_h\) with integer coefficients.
It is nonzero because a choice of roots on each open half of the support gives a product of intervals on which the polynomial \(F\) cannot vanish identically.
Substitute \(t_i=z_i+z_i^{-1}\), preserving nonvanishing and Laurent integrality.
The Haar integral of the logarithm of the absolute value is nonnegative for every nonzero integral Laurent polynomial.
Indeed multiply by a monomial to obtain an ordinary polynomial and apply the one-variable circle mean formula in the last variable, bounding below by the logarithm of the absolute leading coefficient.
Induction ends with a nonzero integer, and bounded positive parts on the torus justify integration of the logarithms, including the null exceptional fibers of an identically zero specialization.
Dividing by \(2^h\) proves \(\int\log|F|\,d\eta^{\otimes h}\ge0\).

Let \(V(r)=\int\log|u-r|\,d\eta(u)\).
The same circle formula applied to \((u-r)(u^{-1}-r)=r^2-(4+t)r+1\) gives, for \(r>0\),
\[
       V(r)=\tfrac12\log r+
           \tfrac12\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\max\left\{1,
                      \frac{|r+r^{-1}-4|}{2}\right\}\right).
\]
In particular \(V(0)=0\) by the product in each fiber and \(V(r)=\tfrac12\log r\) on the support of \(\eta\).

<a id="diagonal"></a>
## A lower bound on the diagonal component

Put \(I_m=\int\log|T_m(u-2)-T_{2m}(u-2)|\,d\eta(u)\).
At \(m=1\), the roots of the difference are \(1,4\), so \(I_1=V(1)+V(4)=\log2\).
For even \(m\), the difference equals \(-(T_m(u-2)-2)(T_m(u-2)+1)\), its roots lie in \([0,4]\), and its leading coefficient has absolute value one.
Its root at zero is simple, and the product of all nonzero roots counted with multiplicity is \(3m^2\), the absolute derivative there.
This derivative follows from \(T'_j(-2)=(-1)^{j-1}j^2\), obtained by the cosine formula.
Consequently \(I_m\ge\log(\sqrt3\,m)\).

For \(m\ge64\) take
\[
                  r_j=2-2\cos(2\pi j/(3m)),\qquad 1\le j\le7 .
\]
Each is a root, since at that point \(T_m(u-2)=2\cos(2\pi j/3)\) for even \(m\).
The roots with \(j=3,6\) occur twice, since the cosine attains its maximum at those interior points.
They satisfy \(r_j<(j/30)^2(64/m)^2<1/16\) by \(2-2\cos s\le s^2\).
For \(0<r\le1/16\), the additional term in \(V(r)-\tfrac12\log r\) is at least \(\tfrac12\log(3/(8r))\), since \((r+r^{-1}-4)/2\ge3/(8r)\) and \(\operatorname{arcosh} y\ge\log y\) for \(y\ge1\).
Using the list \(J=(1,2,3,4,5,6,7,3,6)\), this gives
\[
 I_m\ge\log(\sqrt3\,m)
       +\tfrac12\sum_{j\in J}\log\left(\frac{675}{2j^2}
                                         \left(\frac{m}{64}\right)^2\right)
       >4\log(2m).
\]
At \(m=64\) the last comparison squares to the integer inequality
\(3\cdot64^2\cdot675^9>2^9(7!\cdot18)^2\,128^8\).
The lower expression grows by \(10\log(m/64)\) from its value at 64 compared to \(4\log(m/64)\) on the right.

## The reflected pair of fibers

Let \(J_m\) be the mean logarithm in (1) on the component with conditional laws \(\eta_t,\eta_{-t}\) at consecutive times.
Multiply the four differences over the choices of the two roots in each fiber to obtain \(R_m(t)\in\mathbb Z[t]\).
This polynomial is nonzero at \(t=2\).
There \(v=1\), so \(T_m(v-2)=-1\) at dyadic \(m\), and \(u=3\pm2\sqrt2\).
The conjugate values of \(T_{2m}(u-2)\) cannot equal \(-1\), since the larger conjugate exceeds two.
Consequently the lift \(P_m(z)=z^{\deg R_m}R_m(z+z^{-1})\) is a nonzero integer polynomial and \(4J_m=\log M(P_m)\ge0\) by the circle mean formula.
This and the diagonal bound prove (1) for all \(m\ge64\).

Here is an integer certificate for the six smaller orders.
For a fiber with the two transformed values \(y_1,y_2\), write \(S=y_1+y_2,\ H=y_1y_2\).
Applying \(T_2(y)=y^2-2\) to the two values gives
\[
             \Phi(S,H)=(S^2-2H-4,\ H^2-2S^2+4H+4).
\]
Start at \(m=1\) with \((A,B)=(-t,2t-3)\) for \(v-2\) and \((C,D)=\Phi(t,-2t-3)\) for \(T_2(u-2)\).
Apply \(\Phi\) to both pairs when doubling \(m\).
The product of the four differences is
\[
                    R_m=(D-B)^2+A(A-C)(D-B)+B(A-C)^2,
\]
by taking the resultant of the two monic quadratics.
For a polynomial \(p(z)=e(z^2)+z o(z^2)\), use \(G(p)(z)=e(z)^2-z o(z)^2\).
This squares the roots up to the irrelevant sign of the leading coefficient, so \(M(G(p))=M(p)^2\).
For every coefficient the elementary symmetric bound gives \(|[z^i]p|\le\binom{d}{i}M(p)\) at \(d=\deg p\).
With \(H_m=5(2m)^4/4\), the following rows give the integer comparisons \(|[z^i]G^s(P_m)|>\binom di H_m^{2^s}\).

| \(m\) | \(\deg R_m\) | \(d\) | \(s\) | \(i\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 5 | 10 | 4 | 1 |
| 2 | 9 | 18 | 7 | 3 |
| 4 | 19 | 38 | 4 | 4 |
| 8 | 39 | 78 | 2 | 6 |
| 16 | 79 | 158 | 1 | 29 |
| 32 | 159 | 318 | 1 | 7 |

The comparisons use only coefficient arithmetic.
With \(r=\deg R_m\), each monomial \(c t^j\) contributes \(c\binom j\ell\) to coefficient \(r+j-2\ell\) of \(P_m\) for \(0\le\ell\le j\).
Separating the even and odd coefficients and squaring the resulting coefficient lists gives each indicated iterate \(G^s\).
It follows that \(J_m>\log(2m)+\tfrac14\log(5/4)\) in the six rows.
Since \(3\log(5/4)>4\log(2/\sqrt3)\), the mean \(\tfrac34J_m+\tfrac14 I_m\) exceeds \(\log(2m)\) also for the five even orders in the table.
The comparison at \(m=1\) uses \(I_1=\log2\).

## The ordered residue difference on every odd grid

In an actual [strict integral chamber](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#statement), every limiting pair of a normalized residue and its doubled conjugate also satisfies
\[
                           \mathbb E\log|v-u|\ge0 .
\]
For all five profiles use \(U_j=Z_j/s_j\) and \(j'\) representing \(\pm2j\bmod p\) as in the [ordered orbit proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#orbit-test).
On each retained full orbit there is a negative node \(j>n-\kappa\), with \(p=2n+1\) and \(\kappa=o(p)\).
Its doubled representative is \(j'=p-2j\in[1,2\kappa-1]\), eventually in the positive initial block.
Thus \(Z_{j'}s_j-Z_j s_{j'}\) is nonzero there and at every conjugate on that orbit.
Its product over the retained orbits is a nonzero rational integer, since the numerators are algebraic integers and conjugation commutes with doubling.
The mean log of the positive denominator \(s_{j'}s_j\) tends to zero by the [ordered-pair denominator estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#limit), which also gives uniform integrability of \(\log^+|U_{j'}-U_j|\).
Truncate the difference logarithm below, pass to the pair law and remove the truncation as in that proof.
This establishes the inequality for both prime and composite odd conductors.
In the constructed law \(\log|v-u|=-\infty\) on a set of probability at least \(1/4\), while its positive part is bounded by compact support.
