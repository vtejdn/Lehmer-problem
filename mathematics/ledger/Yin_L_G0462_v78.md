# A product of prime ideals at their first return recovers the measure up to absolute factors

<a id="product"></a>
## Counting each prime ideal only at its own residue order

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), let \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and suppose its monic minimal polynomial \(P\) has \(0<m=m(P)\le1\).
For a nonzero prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) of \(\mathcal O_K\) over a rational prime \(\ell\), denote by \(o_{\mathfrak p}\) the multiplicative order of \(\alpha\bmod\mathfrak p\).
Form the integral ideal
\[
 \mathfrak I(\alpha)=
 \prod_{\substack{\mathfrak p\mid\ell,\ \ell>d^{1/5}\\
                         d\le o_{\mathfrak p}\le2d}}
       \mathfrak p^{\,v_{\mathfrak p}(\alpha^{o_{\mathfrak p}}-1)} ,
\]
where the prime-ideal valuation has value one on a local uniformizer.
The product ranges over the rational primes and their prime ideals satisfying the displayed conditions.
Each prime ideal contributes at one order only, and the product is finite because only prime divisors of the nonzero ideals \((\alpha^j-1)\) for \(d\le j\le2d\) can occur.
Uniformly as \(d\to\infty\),
\[
               c d^2m\ \le\ \log N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak I(\alpha)
                     \ \le\ \bigg(\sum_{j=d}^{2d}\varphi(j)+o(d^2)\bigg)m ,
\]
with an absolute \(c>0\).
Consequently a uniform positive Lehmer gap for nontorsion units is equivalent to \(\log N\mathfrak I(\alpha)\ge c' d^2\) for every sufficiently large \(d\) in this measure range, for some absolute \(c'>0\).
This aggregate criterion uses no selected witness order and keeps the actual initial valuation at each prime ideal.

<a id="valuation"></a>
## The exact norm identity

For \(\ell\nmid j\), reduction of \(x^j-1\) is separable in characteristic \(\ell\).
Thus \(\mathfrak p\mid\Phi_j(\alpha)\) holds at \(\mathfrak p\mid\ell\) exactly when \(o_{\mathfrak p}=j\).
At such a prime every factor \(\Phi_b(\alpha)\) with \(b\mid j,\ b<j\) is a unit, and hence \(v_{\mathfrak p}(\Phi_j(\alpha))=v_{\mathfrak p}(\alpha^j-1)\).
Conversely \(o_{\mathfrak p}\) always divides \(N\mathfrak p-1\), so \(\ell\nmid o_{\mathfrak p}\).
Using \(D_j=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\Phi_j(\alpha)|\) and taking the norm of each prime ideal to its full valuation now gives
\[
                        \log N\mathfrak I(\alpha)=\sum_{j=d}^{2d}T_j(P)
\]
for the [exact-order statistic](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#statement) with cutoff \(d^{1/5}\).
Its positive-proportion lower bound proves the lower estimate.
The identity counts distinct prime ideals above the same rational prime with their own residue degrees and orders.

<a id="upper"></a>
## The summed upper bound also controls rare large returns

Use the [Abel input estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#energy) at \(Y=d\), with \(L=\log(2d)\), \(t=1-(2d)^{-6}\).
The input vector has squared norm \(O(dL^7m^2)\).
The [Gram bound for the primitive root tests](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#tests) is \(O(dL)\).
Therefore the sum over all \(j\in[d,2d]\) of squared smoothed errors in \(\log D_j-\varphi(j)m\) is \(O(d^2L^8m^2)\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz their sum in absolute value is \(O(d^{3/2}L^4m)=o(d^2m)\).
The [one-sided removal of smoothing](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#actual) adds at most \(O(d(1-t)\sum_j\varphi(j))=o(d^2m)\) uniformly, using the [degree-dependent Mahler estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument).
Since \(T_j\le\log D_j\), the claimed upper estimate follows.
In particular \(\log N\mathfrak I\ll d^2 m\), and the proposed absolute quadratic lower bound forces an absolute positive lower bound on \(m\) for all sufficiently large degrees.
In the finitely many smaller degrees the coefficient bound \(|[x^k]P|\le\binom{d}{k}e^m\) leaves only finitely many polynomials in the stated range, each of positive measure.
The units outside the range have logarithmic measure greater than one.
The reverse implication is the uniform lower estimate above.
