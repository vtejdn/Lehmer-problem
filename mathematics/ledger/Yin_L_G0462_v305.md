# When a split of a Salem power relation can leave its exponent progression

<a id="period"></a>
## The permitted residues of an intermediate exponent

Let \(x>1\) be a Salem number, \(q\ge1\), \(y=x^q\), and \(A=\mathbb Z[y]\).
For a [minimal support](Yin_L_G0462_v295.md#splitting) \(S=qE\) with least exponent zero, minimality at \(x\) is exactly minimality of \(E\) at \(y\).
If \(S\) splits at an exponent \(h\) in its span, then \(x^h\in A\), since the relation on either child expresses \(\pm x^h\) as a signed partial sum on \(S\).
As \(y\) is a unit of \(A\), the group \(\{k\in\mathbb Z:x^k\in A^\times\}\) is \(e\mathbb Z\) for a positive divisor \(e\mid q\).
For each \(h\ge0\), membership \(x^h\in A\) already puts \(x^h\) in \(A^\times\), by the integral monic unit polynomial of \(x^h\).
Thus every possible \(h\) is divisible by \(e\).
If \(e=q\), then \(S\) splits at \(x\) if and only if \(E\) splits at \(y\), since both child supports can be divided by \(q\) or lifted by \(q\) and minimality is preserved in either direction.
A translated support \(a+qE\) has the corresponding constraint \(h\equiv a\pmod e\).

A modular test gives a lower bound for \(e\) without enumerating relations or testing every intermediate exponent.
If two roots \(u,v\) of the minimum of \(x\) in a common finite field satisfy \(u^q=v^q\), their evaluations agree on \(A\), and therefore \((u/v)^h=1\) whenever \(x^h\in A\).
In particular \(\operatorname{ord}(u/v)\mid e\).
For the [Lehmer root](Yin_L_G0462_v289.md#density) and \(q=4\), take the two roots \(u,-u\) of \(X^2+1\) over \(\mathbb F_9\).
They are both roots of the Lehmer polynomial modulo 3, as is seen by reducing the polynomial modulo \(X^2+1\).
Every intermediate exponent of a support in \(4\mathbb Z\) is consequently even, although membership in \(4\mathbb Z\) does not follow from this test.
Here \(x^4<2<x^5\) by the cited rational bounds on \(x\).
At \(q=2\) the same two roots give \(e=2\) exactly.
The minimum of \(x^2\) is \(G(T)=T^{10}-T^9-T^7+T^6-T^5+T^4-T^3-T+1\), as follows by multiplying the Lehmer polynomials at \(X\) and \(-X\) to obtain \(G(X^2)\) and using [power-degree preservation](Yin_L_G0462_v296.md#runs).
Every nonzero relation in \([0,10]\) at \(x^2\) is a multiple of \(G\), so its support \(E=\{0,1,3,4,5,6,7,9,10\}\) is minimal and cannot split within that interval.
Therefore \(2E\) is nonsplitting at the original Lehmer root, despite the empty positions inside its span on both the original and the halved exponent scales.

## A family with a unique shortest relation and no interior split

Let \(y=r_n\) be the [Salem root approaching two](Yin_L_G0462_v296.md#barrier) of \(F_n(X)=X^n+1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X^i\), for \(n\ge4\).
Up to overall sign, \(F_n\) is the only nonzero ternary relation supported on \([0,n]\), even when it has cyclotomic factors.
Indeed a relation of degree \(d\le n\) with leading coefficient one and remaining coefficients \(c_i\in\{-1,0,1\}\) requires a finite expansion
\[
 \sum_{i<d}(1+c_i)y^i=B_d,\qquad B_d=\frac{(2-y)y^d-1}{y-1}.
\]
Since \((2-y)y^n=2y-1\), one has \(B_{n-2}<0\) by \(2y-1<y^2\), \(B_{n-1}=1/y\), and \(B_n=2\).
The first value and monotonicity exclude all degrees at most \(n-2\), and the second lies strictly between zero and the smallest possible positive digit sum.
Since \((2-\sqrt2)(\sqrt2)^4>2\sqrt2-1\), the identity for \((X-1)F_n(X)\) gives \(F_n(\sqrt2)<0\), hence \(y>\sqrt2\) by uniqueness of its exterior root.
At degree \(n\) every digit of exponent at least two must therefore vanish, and irrationality forces the only representation \(2=2+0y\).
This proves uniqueness and shows that the full support \([0,n]\) is minimal.
It is nonsplitting because every integer in its span is already occupied and the intermediate exponent of a split must be absent from the original support.
Thus proximity of the powered base to two and both endpoint constraints allow nonsplitting minimal supports of arbitrarily large weight.
This family supplies one shape per weight at the input \(y=r_n\) itself and leaves open the existence of Salem roots \(x\to1\) with \(x^q=r_n\).
For an actual root \(x\) and an actual power \(y=x^q\), any extension of the conclusion to \(q[0,n]\) must also account for the intervening exponents allowed by the first section.
