# Positive Pisot excess near nonintegral real lower profiles with circle roots

<a id="profile"></a>
## A varying prefix and a bounded number of terms in the lift

Fix a real polynomial \(S(w)\) with \(S(0)=1\), no zero in the open unit disk and at least one nonintegral coefficient.
Fix positive integers \(K,N\) with \(N\ge\deg S\), and \(0<\eta\le1\).
There are \(\varepsilon,c>0\) with the following property for the [positive-constant Pisot inputs](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) of either parity and every branch \(j\).
Suppose the normalized trace lift \(F=\Pi/j\) has at most \(K\) terms, and through \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) its coefficients agree with \(S'(z^g)\) for some \(1\le g\le h\).
Here \(S'(0)=1\), its real coefficient vector padded through degree \(N\) is within \(\varepsilon\) of that of \(S\) in maximum norm, and \(\deg S'\le N\).
Then the excess at the Pisot root satisfies \(\theta-q\ge c\).

Assume otherwise and take \(S_i\to S,\ \theta_i-q_i\to0\).
Every \(F_i\) has constant one and all roots on the circle, so \(M(F_i)=1\).
The [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives uniformly bounded coefficients and circle norms.
The [first fractional index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) is \(o(h)\); it is at least \(g\) eventually since the given prefix starts with constant one up to degree \(g-1\).
Thus \(g=o(h)\).
Write \(B=B^\sharp=F_iJ_i\) for the actual monic reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2h+2\), with \(B(0)=1,\ 1<M(B)\to1\).
The coefficients of \(J_i\) at positive indices are the doubled moments of symmetric signed measures \(\omega_i\) of mass one with negative mass tending to zero, as in the [inverse representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality).
All coefficients of \(B\) are bounded integers by convolution.

## The interval of zero coefficients

Pass to a subsequence with fixed weak limit \(\mu\) of the laws of \(w=\bar v^g\) under \(\omega_i\).
Every such limit is a probability.
On testing \(B\) at \(rg\) for fixed integral \(r\), the limiting analytic product \(SJ_0\) has integer coefficients, where the positive coefficients of \(J_0\) are the doubled moments of \(\mu\) and its constant is one.
In particular \(\mu\) is not Haar, since \(S\) has a fractional coefficient.
At every intermediate scale \(g/t,t/h\to0\) take a joint weak limit \(\Omega\) of \((w,u)=(\bar v^g,v^t)\).
For every positive \(k\), the base projection \(\nu_k\) of \(2S(w)u^k\,d\Omega\) has integer Fourier coefficients by testing \(B\) at \(kt+rg\) for fixed integral \(r\).
Replacing \(S_i\) by \(S\) in those coefficients costs \(o(1)\) because only \(N+1\) bounded moments occur.
The [finite-valued joint decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#slopes) applies without a lower bound for \(|S|\): the zero layer of \(2|S|^2\Omega\) comes from \(S^*(SJ_0)+S(SJ_0)^*\), and its other layers from \(S^*\nu_k\) and their conjugates.
These transforms take finitely many values since the integer coefficients of \(SJ_0\) and all \(\nu_k\) have one uniform bound.
The components with full base projection are the full torus and the graphs \(u^b=\xi w^a\), with \(\xi\) torsion.
After division by the weight away from \(S=0\), each group of graphs of equal slope projects to a nonnegative density \(f\) with \(2Sf\) integer Laurent, by separation on large common multiples of \(b\,\operatorname{ord}(\xi)\).
Mass above the finite zero set of \(S\) contributes zero to each \(\nu_k\).

The [nonnegative density argument](Yin_L_G0462_v104.md) forces every such \(f\) to vanish for this nonintegral \(S\).
For the remaining full-torus term division by a base weight preserves finite harmonic support in \(u\).
If the last nonzero positive row with a full-dimensional density has index \(k\), its density \(H_k\) is an integer Laurent polynomial and \(|H_k|\le |S|f_0\), where \(f_0\) is the absolutely continuous density of \(\mu\).
Indeed the conditional distribution of \(u^k\) has no moments beyond its first, which has modulus at most \(1/2\), as in the [last harmonic argument](Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#rows).
Jensen gives \(M(f_0)<1\), since equality would exhaust \(\mu\) with Haar probability, while \(M(S)=1\) by its constant and root condition.
Thus \(M(H_k)<1\), impossible.
All \(\nu_k\) are supported on the finite set of torsion atoms of \(2|S|^2\mu\), independent of \(t\) and of the further joint limit: away from \(S=0\) the rows are absolutely continuous with respect to that measure and have no full-dimensional part.
This weighted marginal has a trigonometric polynomial density and at most finitely many torsion atoms by its finite-valued transform.

Take \(T\ge1\) divisible by the orders of those atoms, taking \(T=1\) for an empty list, and set \(U=1-z^{Tg}\).
Testing \(UB\) at each intermediate \(t\) now gives a limit of zero since \(1-w^T\) vanishes on the support of \(\nu_1\).
The tested coefficients are integers.
If no fixed \(L\) gave exact zero on the entire interval \(Lg\le t\le h/L\) eventually, diagonal extraction would give a violating intermediate sequence.
Choose such an \(L\) with \(1/L<\eta\).

## A product with coefficients in a finite real set

The [local estimate at the initial scale](Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial) gives an integer \(P=1\) or \(1-\epsilon z^d\), \(\epsilon=\pm1,\ 1\le d=O(g)\), with
\[
                 \sup_a\sum_{a\le n<a+g}|[z^n]PB|=O(1).
\]
Its proof requires only integral moments below the scale, available below \(g\), the actual inverse measures and boundedly many bounded coefficients of the full lift.
For bounded \(g\) take \(P=1\).
Put \(W=PU,\ C=WB,\ V=W S(z^g)\), using the fixed polynomial \(S\) in \(V\).
These multipliers have bounded absolute coefficient sums and degree \(O(g)\).
The interval of zeros for \(UB\) and the initial estimate for \(PB\) give \(\sum_{0\le n\le h/L}|C_n|=O(1)\): only \(O(g)\) initial indices can contribute, and \(C=U(PB)\) there.
Throughout the common prefix \(C=W S_i(z^g)J_i\), so every coefficient differs from that of \(VJ_i\) by at most \(O(\|S_i-S\|_{\rm coef})=o(1)\), uniformly even at moving indices.
Here \(\|\cdot\|_{\rm coef}\) is the sum of absolute coefficients.

Let \(V^*(z)=V(z^{-1})\).
For \(|r|\le h/(4L)\), the [two-sided coefficient identity](Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#gram) gives
\[
        2\int v^r |V(v^{-1})|^2\,d\omega_i
                 =[z^r](V^*C+VC^*)+o(1)
\]
with uniform error, by testing at indices at most \(|r|+\deg V\) and using \(J_i+J_i^*=2\widehat\omega_i\) with its correct zero coefficient.
The displayed polynomial expression has bounded sum of absolute coefficients on this interval by the initial bound for \(C\).
For any fixed \(k\) distinct indices in a block of that diameter, the Gram matrix of \(v^nV(v^{-1})\) in \(2\omega_i^+\) therefore has operator norm at most \(C_0+k\,o(1)\) with \(C_0\) independent of \(k\).
The passage to the positive part costs \(O(k\|\omega_i^-\|)\).
Only a fixed \(k\) is tested before passing to the limit.

Every coefficient of \(VB\) belongs to a fixed finite real set since \(B\) has bounded integer coefficients and \(P,U\) are signed binomials or one and \(S\) is fixed.
In particular nonzero values have modulus at least a fixed \(\delta>0\).
Outside the union of the \(K\) transition intervals \([e,e+\deg V]\) at support exponents \(e\) of \(F_i\), split into blocks of diameter \(h/(4L)\) on which the partial multiplier \(T_i(v)=\sum_{e<n}[z^e]F_i\,v^{-e}\) is fixed.
There \([z^n]VB=2\int v^n V(v^{-1})T_i(v)\,d\omega_i\), and \(\|T_i\|_\infty\) is uniformly bounded.
The positive-part integrals differ by \(o(1)\).
If \(k\) tested coefficients were nonzero, the sum of their squared moduli for the positive-part integrals would be at least \(k(\delta-o(1))^2\), while Bessel bounds it by \((C_0+k\,o(1))\,2\int|T_i|^2\,d\omega_i^+\).
Choosing \(k\) sufficiently large and fixed bounds the number of such coefficients per block.
On each transition interval \(VB=(US(z^g))(PB)\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum by the initial estimate on its boundedly many translated intervals of length \(O(g)\).
There are only boundedly many exceptional intervals and complementary blocks on the full support of degree \(O(h)\), hence \(VB\) has bounded support cardinality.

Finally \(V(0)B(0)=1\) and \(M(VB)=M(B)>1\) tends to one since \(M(P)=M(U)=M(S(z^g))=1\).
Extract one fixed ordered tuple of the nonzero real coefficients of \(VB\).
The [torus translation proof at a fixed tuple](Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations), applied at its constant endpoint by reversal, excludes these measures tending to one strictly from above.
This proves the stated neighborhood gap.
