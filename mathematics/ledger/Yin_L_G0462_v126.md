# Selecting the positive constant of a Pisot inverse by a trace power

<a id="seed"></a>
## An integer point in the inverse simplex

Let \(P=z^m Q(z+z^{-1})\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(1<\sigma\le2\), with \(d=2m\), \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), and \(H=Q/(x-T)\).
The roots \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) of \(H\) are distinct and lie in \((-2,2)\).
Given any real \(L\ge32m\), let \(k\) be the least positive integer with \(X=\sigma^k\ge L\), so \(L\le X<2L\).
Set \(D_0=2,\ D_1=x,\ D_{j+1}=xD_j-D_{j-1}\), so \(D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\).
Use the integer remainder \(p\) of \((x+2)(D_k(x)-2)Q'(x)\) on division by the monic \(Q\).
If \(g(x)=(x+2)(D_k(x)-2)\), then
\[
 \frac{p(x)}{Q(x)}
   =\frac{g(T)}{x-T}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{g(x_j)}{x-x_j}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The simple residues agree at every root and both sides tend to zero at infinity.
Consequently \(q=1+[x^{m-1}]p=1+g(T)-\sum_j w_j\), where \(w_j=-g(x_j)>0\).
In fact \(x_j=\zeta_j+\zeta_j^{-1}\) for a nontorsion circle conjugate, so \(0<2-D_k(x_j)\le4\) and \(w_j<16\).
At \(T\) we have \(g(T)=(T+2)(X+X^{-1}-2)\).
Since \(4<T+2\le9/2\), these estimates give
\[
    q>4X-16m+9>3L,\qquad q<1+(9/2)X<10L.                          \tag{2}
\]

Define the monic integer polynomial of degree \(d\)
\[
               f=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}p(z+z^{-1}).
\]
The added term is antisymmetric in the formal degree \(2m-1\), so \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\), and its constant gives \(f(0)=q\).
In the [integral inverse simplex](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) the parameter is \(-p\), so the required positive residues are
\[
           b_j=\frac{p(x_j)}{H'(x_j)}=(T-x_j)w_j>0 .
\]
The endpoint inequality is also strict, since
\[
       \sum_j\frac{b_j}{2+x_j}
            =\sum_j(T-x_j)(2-D_k(x_j))<18m<q+T/2 .
\]
Thus \(f\) itself is the minimal polynomial of a Pisot root \(\theta>q\).
This criterion proves the strict location of all the interior roots and irreducibility for the integer polynomial used here.

<a id="cost"></a>
## A bound uniform in the Salem polynomial

Put \(e=T-2\) and \(x_\theta=\theta+\theta^{-1}\).
The [exact residue formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) gives
\[
    \theta-q-e=\frac{e}{\theta-1}
                   +\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{(T-x_j)w_j}{x_\theta-x_j}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Each term on the right is positive.
We have \(T-x_j<9/2,\ e\le1/2,\ x_\theta-x_j>q-2\), and hence
\[
                 0<\theta-q-e<\frac{72m}{q-2}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For any \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), \(L=32m/\varepsilon\) gives \(q<320m/\varepsilon\) and \(\theta-q<e+\varepsilon\).
In particular \(q<160d\) suffices for an exact positive-constant inverse of any Salem number up to two, with the precision of (4).
These are bounds for a selected integer \(q\).
The [fixed-output theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#statement) instead constructs a seed at every sufficiently large prescribed \(q\) with constants depending on \(P\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Selection by the degree and the desired relative accuracy

For \(d=2m\), (2) with \(L=16d\psi(d)\) places \(q\) between \(48d\psi(d)\) and \(160d\psi(d)\) for any function \(\psi(d)\ge1\).
Along any sequence of Salem numbers tending to one the degrees tend to infinity, since bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound every integer coefficient.
If \(\psi(d)\to\infty\), (4) then gives \(\theta-q\to0\).
Conversely the [positive-constant Pisot criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#statement) turns any such near-integer sequence into Salem numbers tending to one.

For relative accuracy \(1/d\) take \(L=16d^2/e\), corresponding to \(\varepsilon=e/d\).
The [explicit lower bound of Voutier](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) gives \(\log\sigma=d h(\sigma)>\frac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\), since the Salem number is an algebraic integer with just one conjugate outside the unit circle.
As \(e=2\cosh(\log\sigma)-2\ge(\log\sigma)^2\), the selected \(q\) satisfies
\[
        q<2560 d^2\left(\frac{\log d}{\log\log d}\right)^6,\qquad
        0<\frac{\theta-q}{e}-1<\frac1d .
\]
The earlier [bound at every prescribed constant](Yin_L_G0462_v33.md#degree-uniform) has the same relative accuracy and the threshold \((2d)^4(32\sqrt d)^d\).
