# Parallel sides in a four-term Laurent polynomial of Mahler measure two

<a id="trapezoids"></a>
## The side lengths and all coefficient choices

Let \(A\) be a rank-two integral Laurent polynomial supported at the four vertices of a convex quadrilateral with a pair of parallel sides.
Translate the vertex of coefficient \(2\) to the origin.
Orient the parallel sides from that vertex toward the other endpoint of its side, and choose the starting point on the opposite side in the same orientation.
In torus characters the polynomial has the form
\[
          A=2+a z^m+u(b+c z^n),\qquad m,n>0,\qquad abc\ne0 .
\]
Here \(z\) is the character given by a primitive lattice vector in the common direction and \(u\) corresponds to the chosen starting point on the other side.
The two characters are independent, so their joint Haar distribution is Haar probability on the full two-torus even if their vectors generate a subgroup of finite index.
Put \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(a)\) and \(t=\operatorname{sgn}(bc)\).
The multiplicative Mahler measure is \(M(A)=2\) precisely in the following cases, where the order of the magnitudes of \(b,c\) is unrestricted.

| Ratio \(n/m\) | \(\lvert a\rvert\) | Unordered pair of magnitudes of \(b,c\) | \(t\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(1\) | \(1\) | \(1,1\) or \(1,2\) | \(s\) |
| \(1\) | \(2\) | \(1,1\) or \(2,2\) | \(s\) |
| \(2\) | \(1\) or \(2\) | \(1,1\) | \(-1\) |

Thus the edge through the specified vertex is always the shorter edge when the lengths differ.
In the group generated by the support, \(x=z^m,\ y=u\) are then a basis of characters, since \(m\mid n\).
The table classifies the shapes up to a lattice basis change in that group.
In a larger ambient lattice arbitrary finite-index embeddings preserve their measures.

<a id="jensen"></a>
## Jensen's equality on the opposite edges

Jensen in \(u\) gives \(\log M(A)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|2+a z^m|,|b+c z^n|)\,dz\) with normalized Haar measure.
Each edge separately has logarithmic measure at most this integral, giving \(|a|,|b|,|c|\le2\) when \(M(A)=2\).
With \(|a|\le2\), equality is equivalent to
\[
                    |b+c z^n|\le |2+a z^m|\qquad (|z|=1),
\]
since strict excess on any arc would increase the integral.

If \(|a|=2\), every root of \(z^m=-s\) must annul \(b+c z^n\).
Their quotients range over all \(m\)-th roots of unity and each quotient has \(n\)-th power one, so \(m\mid n\), and \(|b|=|c|=B\).
Write \(k=n/m\) and \(w=-s z^m\).
At \(w=1\) the vanishing requires \(t=-(-s)^k\).
The inequality is now \(B|1-w^k|\le2|1-w|\), equivalent to \(Bk\le2\) by the geometric sum and its value at one.
This gives exactly the listed possibilities with \(|a|=2\).

If \(|a|=1\), integration of the squared inequality gives \(b^2+c^2\le5\).
If \(b^2+c^2=5\), equality holds identically after squaring.
The nonconstant Fourier coefficients on the two sides then force \(n=m\) and \(bc=2a\), which suffice.
In the remaining case both magnitudes are one.
At every \(z^m=-s\) the inequality gives \(t\Re(z^n)\le-1/2\).
If \(m\nmid n\), the average of \(z^n\) on this coset of \(m\)-th roots is zero, a contradiction.
Writing \(k=n/m,\ w=-s z^m\) again, evaluation at \(w=1\) gives \(t=-(-s)^k\).
The required nonnegative difference of squared moduli at \(w=e^{i\phi}\) is
\[
                   3-4\cos\phi+2\cos(k\phi).
\]
For \(k\ge3\), setting \(\phi=\pi/k\) gives \(1-4\cos(\pi/k)<0\).
For \(k=1\) the difference is \(3-2\cos\phi\), and for \(k=2\) it is \((2\cos\phi-1)^2\).
This proves necessity and sufficiency in the remaining rows.

<a id="actual"></a>
## The two unequal-side shapes for an actual eight-term lift

On any positive-constant branch-two sequence of eight-term Pisot trace lifts with \(\theta-q\to0\), the [limiting lower polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight) has rank two and measure two after extraction.
Suppose its four support points form a quadrilateral with parallel sides.
Write \(d\) for the actual exponent of \(x=z^m\) and \(e\) for that of \(y=u\), now using \(Z\) for the one-variable specialization parameter.
Both exponents are positive since those endpoints are actual lower terms.
For equal sides the offsets are \(d,e,d+e\), excluded by the [sum-of-offsets theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v96.md#reciprocal).
For unequal sides the offsets are \(d,e,e+2d\), with either order of \(d,e\).
The two possibilities for the specialized lower polynomial are
\[
\begin{split}
  &2+s Z^d+\epsilon Z^e(1-Z^{2d}),\\
  &(1+s Z^d)\bigl(2+\epsilon Z^e(1-s Z^d)\bigr),\qquad
       s,\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{split}
\]
The second polynomial has coefficient \(2s\) at \(d\).
The first is irreducible in the rank-two Laurent ring, since \(2+s x\) and \(1-x^2\) are coprime and the polynomial is primitive of degree one in \(y\).
The argument below excludes the second for actual inputs.

## Removing the integral factor on the Taylor prefix

For the second shape there are absolute constants \(c>0,Q<\infty\) such that all actual branch-two inputs of either degree parity have \(\theta-q\ge c,\ q\le Q\).
Suppose otherwise that the excess tends to zero.
Put \(p=e,\ v=e+d,\ G=1+s Z^d\) and \(S_i=1+(\epsilon/2)Z^p-(s\epsilon/2)Z^v\).
Here \(p<v=o(h)\), and \(v/\gcd(p,v)\to\infty\) by the [eight-term reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight), because \(v\ge(e+2d)/2\).
The normalized numerator \(\Pi/2\) agrees with \(GS_i\) through \(h\) and has uniformly bounded circle supremum.
Let \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\) be the actual integer denominator with its [signed inverse measure](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#reduction).
The formal series \(X_i=B^\sharp/G\) is integral since \(G(0)=1\), and agrees with \(S_iJ_i\) through \(h\).

The [base and mixed-row tests](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#integer) at \(a p+b v\) and \(k t+a p+b v\), for fixed lattice indices and \(v\ll t\ll h,\ k>0\), read integer coefficients of \(X_i\) wherever the test index is positive.
They use the same signed measure and the same two signed halves as the triangle \(2+\epsilon x-s\epsilon y\).
The [slope exclusion](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) and [torsion annihilation](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#singular) thus give a product \(U_i\) of nonzero cyclotomic binomials of total degree \(O(v)\) with a fixed number of factors on the extracted subsequence.
With \(W_i=1-Z^{2(v-p)}\), the integral coefficients of \(U_iW_iX_i\) vanish throughout \(Lv\le l\le h/L\) eventually for some fixed \(L\).

For each fixed \(C\), the [local estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#support) give bounded absolute coefficient sums of \(W_iX_i\) through \(Cv\) and \(J_i\) through \(2(v-p)\).
For bounded \(v/p\), the first-coefficient tests at \(p+l u\) for \(u=o(p)\) use only the integral prefix \(S_iJ_i\), and the integral moments below \(p\) still belong to the actual \(J_i\).
The [character Gram bounds](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) apply to \(X_i\) on \([0,Cv]\), since every coefficient there uses only the three Taylor offsets \(0,p,v\), with the same bounded partial multipliers.
When \(v-p=o(p)\), the initial \(J_i\) sum through \(2(v-p)\) uses the same zero block or the models with \(v-p=u,2u\).
For \(v/p\to\infty\), the [two possible first marginals](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#hierarchy) follow by testing \(X_i\) at \(v+l p\), all within the available prefix.
They have no atoms, giving an intermediate zero window for \(X_i\) between the two offsets and a bounded absolute sum of \(J_i=X_i/(1+\epsilon Z^p/2)\) through \(v/L_1\) for some fixed \(L_1\).
The resulting Gram bound on blocks of this diameter and Bessel's inequality with the three active offsets give the local estimate through \(Cv\) in this case as well.
In both regimes the shifts used here are \(O(v)\) and eventually stay within the Taylor prefix whenever nonnegative.

Truncate \(U_iW_iX_i\) through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\), obtaining \(Q_i\) of degree \(O(v)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
Then \(Q_i/S_i\) agrees with \(U_iW_iJ_i\) through the cutoff.
The [two-sided moment comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#return) supplies bounded-variation measures for the positive coefficients of each series.
Its shift corrections use only the initial sums just proved and those of \(W_iJ_i=(W_iX_i)/S_i\) through fixed multiples of \(v\).
The latter are bounded because the geometric expansion of \(1/S_i\) over \(1+\epsilon Z^p/2\) has absolute coefficient sum at most \(2(1+\lfloor C\rfloor)\) through \(Cv\).
The global polynomial identity is
\[
 S_i U_iW_i B^\sharp-Q_i\Pi/2
            = S_i(\Pi/2)(U_iW_iJ_i-Q_i/S_i).
\]
The left polynomial has degree \(O(h)\), so the [two interval cutoffs](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#cutoff) bound \(\|S_i U_iW_i B^\sharp\|_1\).
The circle inequality \(|W_i|^2\le32|S_i|\) from the same comparison gives a bounded integral norm for \(U_iW_i^3 B^\sharp\).
The [integer support bound](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) and the [fixed-support Mahler gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) contradict \(M(U_iW_i^3 B^\sharp)=M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one.
The [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) converts the positive excess bound into the stated uniform bound for \(q\).
