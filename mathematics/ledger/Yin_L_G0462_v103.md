# Four sublinear lower exponents are necessary for bounded-support lifts on branch two

<a id="four-offsets"></a>
## The fourth lower exponent

Consider positive-constant Pisot inputs on branch \(j=2\), with exterior root \(\theta\), constant \(q\), trace degree \(h\), and trace lift \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
On every sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\) and bounded term count of \(\Pi\), eventually there are at least four nonconstant terms in the lower half \(1\le l\le h\).
Their fourth exponent \(d_4\) satisfies \(d_4=o(h)\).
Equivalently, for every fixed term bound \(K\) and \(\eta>0\), inputs with at most three such terms through \(\min(h,\lfloor\eta h\rfloor)\) and at most \(K\) terms overall have a uniform positive lower bound on \(\theta-q\) and a uniform upper bound on \(q\).
Both degree parities are included.

Suppose there were a contrary sequence.
By the [three-offset theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#three-offsets), its first three positive lower exponents \(d_1<d_2<d_3\) are \(o(h)\).
After extraction there is a fixed \(0<\gamma<1\) such that no additional lower term occurs through \(\lfloor\gamma h\rfloor\).
This includes a sequence with only three lower terms.
Write \(N=2h+2\).
The [trace root locations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) give \(M(\Pi)=2\), where \(M\) is multiplicative Mahler measure.
The [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) permits extraction of the fixed number and coefficients of all terms and bounds \(\|\Pi\|_\infty\) on the circle.
At least one of the three early coefficients is odd, since the [first nondivisible index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) is \(o(h)\).

<a id="face"></a>
## The lattice of the initial four terms

Apply the [stabilized-lattice construction](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#face) to all lower exponents and \(N\).
In its free quotient \(\Gamma\) the fixed full Laurent polynomial \(F\) has \(M(F)=2\) by Lawton's theorem.
The limits of the exponents divided by \(N\) define a linear functional zero on the first three vectors \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) and strictly positive on every later lower exponent and all reflected exponents.
Rational approximation within the subspace annihilating the three integral vectors gives an integral functional with the same zero and strict inequalities after clearing denominators.
Its zero face in \(F\) is \(A=2+\sum_{j=1}^3 a_j X^{v_j}\).
The three vectors are nonzero and distinct, since their evaluations are positive and distinct on every sufficiently late input.
Translation along the functional followed by Jensen gives \(M(A)\le M(F)\).
Conversely any sufficiently late evaluation is positive on all \(v_j\), exposing the vertex of coefficient two and giving \(M(A)\ge2\).
Thus \(M(A)=2\) on the torus of the subgroup generated by the three vectors, since restriction of characters from \(\Gamma\) onto this subgroup preserves Haar probability.

Rank three is impossible by the [common-phase argument](Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#common-phase), taking weight one on the three independent vectors.
In rank one the \(v_j\) are distinct positive multiples of one generator after orientation.
The fixed polynomial \(A(w)\) then has constant two and no root in the open disk by Jensen's formula.
Its specialization \(A(z^g)/2\) agrees with \(\Pi/2\) through \(\lfloor\gamma h\rfloor\), has a nonintegral coefficient and satisfies \(1\le g\le h\).
The [fixed rational prefix theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v21.md#statement) excludes this case.

In rank two the three vectors cannot be affinely collinear, since their affine line would miss the origin and the same common-phase argument would give \(M(A)>2\).
If two vectors were collinear with zero, their positive evaluations would orient them as two positive multiples of one character and the remaining character would be independent.
Writing \(A=T(x)+c y\), Jensen would give \(M(T)=2\) and \(\min_{\mathbb T}|T|\ge |c|\ge1\), impossible for a trinomial \(T\) by the [extremal minimum classification](Yin_L_G0462_v101.md#minimum).
The four points are therefore in general affine position.
If they form no quadrilateral with parallel sides, the [extreme-ray slice calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v23.md#slices) uses only this geometry, the positive evaluation and \(M(A)=2\).
It leaves \(2+x+y+x^2y^2\), ruled out by the [diagonal polynomial](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v23.md#diagonal).
It remains to exclude the parallel-sided shapes from their fractional prefix.

<a id="prefix"></a>
## Integer series for the parallel-sided faces

Use the [complete parallel-side table](Yin_L_G0462_v100.md#trapezoids) with the edge through zero oriented toward its other endpoint.
Its two independent characters evaluate to positive exponents \(d,e\), which are distinct and satisfy \(b_0/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\) for \(b_0=\max(d,e)\).
Indeed a bounded subsequence of this reduced ratio would give a fixed relation after extraction, contrary to independence in the stabilized lattice.
In the equal-side case the [signed factor classification](Yin_L_G0462_v94.md#shapes) applies directly to \(A\).
The all-even polynomial is excluded by the odd coefficient.
The product of an integral binomial and \(2\pm z^d\) or \(2\pm z^e\) is excluded by the fixed rational prefix theorem with the integral binomial as formal factor.

For the other possibilities write the normalized prefix as \(G_i S_i\), set \(A_i=2S_i\), and use the following four polynomials in their unsigned base characters \(x,y\).
In the first three rows \(x=z^d,\ y=z^e,\ G_i=1\).
The last row is the [unequal-side product](Yin_L_G0462_v100.md#integral-factor), with \(G_i=1+s z^d,\ x=z^e,\ y=z^{e+d}\).
The letters \(s,t\) stand for arbitrary signs.

| Shape | Effective polynomial \(A\) giving \(A_i\) on specialization |
| --- | --- |
| Equal sides, strictly exterior | \(2+s x+t y+stxy\) |
| Equal sides, reciprocal | \(2+s x+t y+2stxy\) |
| Unequal sides, irreducible | \(2+s x+t y(1-x^2)\) |
| Unequal sides, after the integral factor | \(2+t x-st y\) |

Each effective base still has rank two.
Each displayed \(A\) has \(M(A)=2\), in the last row by Jensen in \(y\) since \(|2+t x|\ge1\) on the circle.
The one-variable \(A_i\) also has \(M(A_i)=2\) at every sufficiently late specialization.
Indeed the [torus translation identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#endpoint) averages the log measures of the one-variable phase translates to \(\log2\).
They all have constant two and distinct exponents with fixed nonzero leading magnitude, so equality with their common lower bound holds at every phase by continuity.

Write \(p<b\) for the two specialized character exponents in increasing order.
Both carry signed halves in \(S_i\), and any third term is at index at least \(p+b\) and at most \(3b\).
In every row \(b=o(h)\), and \(b/\gcd(p,b)\to\infty\), including \(\{p,b\}=\{e,e+d\}\).
The actual [signed inverse representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) provides \(J_i(0)=1\) and \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\), where \(B^\sharp\) is integral with constant one and \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one.
The positive coefficients of \(J_i\) are the doubled moments of a symmetric real signed measure \(\omega_i\) of mass one with negative mass tending to zero.
The formal series \(X_i=B^\sharp/G_i\) is integral and agrees with \(S_iJ_i\) through \(\lfloor\gamma h\rfloor\).

<a id="filters"></a>
## Mixed rows and estimates before the later terms

On the two-character base, the [ordered lattice tests](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#integer) take coefficients of \(X_i\) at positive base frequencies and at \(kT+mp+nb\) for \(k>0\) and \(b\ll T\ll h\).
Fixed \(k,m,n\) only require the stated prefix.
After extraction the base marginal is fixed, the signed errors tend to zero, and all mixed rows weighted by \(A\) have integer Fourier coefficients.
The base cannot be Haar because of the signed halves.
The row and zero-layer identities give integer Fourier coefficients for the positive joint measure weighted by \(|A|^2\).
For the triangle, the [nonreciprocal slope and last-harmonic arguments](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) leave each positive row supported on finitely many proper torsion translates determined by the marginal.
For the strictly exterior square the [two-circle argument](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#two-circles) gives this conclusion using coprimality with the reversal.
For the irreducible trapezoid the [corresponding row argument](Yin_L_G0462_v99.md#rows) handles its two torus zeros, which contribute zero to \(A\)-weighted rows.
For the reciprocal square the [zero-graph proof](Yin_L_G0462_v96.md#rows) works for every pair of signs since the graph \(y=-(2+s x)/(t(1+2s x))\) is simple in the transverse coordinate.
An integral Laurent graph-slope density numerator \(H\) must vanish on the graph by integrability of \(H/A\), so \(A\mid H\) by primitive degree-one division.
The remaining positivity, last-harmonic and singular-support arguments use no relation between \(s\) and \(t\).
These calculations involve the two-circle polynomial and limiting moments alone.

The [annihilating characters](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#singular) thus give a product \(U_i\) of a fixed number of nonzero cyclotomic binomials of degree \(O(b)\).
Choose \(V_i=1-z^a\), with \(a=2(b-p)\) when \(b/p\) stays bounded and \(a=6p\) when \(p/b\to0\), after extraction.
The shifts in \(U_iV_i\) are fixed base-lattice combinations once their signs and the order of the two exponents are fixed.
For every \(b\ll T\ll h\), the coefficient at \(T\) of \(U_iV_iX_i\) tends to zero on the singular-support test and eventually vanishes by integrality.
Diagonal extraction gives a fixed \(L\), which may be increased to satisfy \(1/L<\gamma\), with vanishing throughout \(Lb\le T\le h/L\).

For each fixed \(C\), the absolute coefficient sum of \(V_iX_i\) through \(Cb\) and that of \(J_i\) through \(a\) are bounded.
For bounded \(b/p\), the [first-index tests](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#mixed) at \(p+lu\), \(u=o(p)\) and \(l\) fixed, see the first two signed halves when active and never reach a third offset.
The [comparable-offset proof](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#comparable) uses the bounded partial multipliers of \(S_i\) in the character Gram estimates, splitting at all active offsets and their translates.
It applies to the integral coefficients of \(X_i\) and \(V_iX_i\) on \([0,Cb]\).
If \((b-p)/p\) stays positive away from zero it bounds \(X_i\) there without the filter, giving the \(J_i\) bound through \(a\) by local division.
When \(b-p=o(p)\), the integral prefix below \(p\) has the same short zero interval or the same small-index models with \(b-p=u,2u\), giving the bound directly.
For \(p/b\to0\), below \(b\) the only nonconstant term of \(S_i\) is the first signed half.
The [sixth-root filter proof](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#separated) gives the short sum through \(6p\) and kills the intermediate coefficients of \(V_iX_i\).
Dividing there by the first binomial gives the bounded signed Gram norm for the \(V_i\)-weighted characters on blocks of diameter proportional to \(b\).
Splitting at the finitely many effective offsets and their translates and using the unweighted short-block Gram bound on the transition pieces gives the required sum through \(Cb\).
For bounded \(p\) these pieces contain boundedly many indices.
Every initial coefficient and every positive test index used in these estimates is eventually below \(\gamma h\).

For local division in all four rows, keep \(P_i=1\pm z^p/2\), whose inverse has absolute coefficient sum at most two.
Since \(S_i-P_i\) starts at \(b\) and has bounded absolute coefficient sum, the geometric expansion of \(1/S_i\) over \(P_i\) through \(Cb\) needs only powers \(0,\ldots,\lfloor C\rfloor\).
This gives a bounded absolute coefficient sum there for \(1/S_i\), and hence for \(V_iJ_i=(V_iX_i)/S_i\) through that range.

## Retaining the face polynomial in the denominator comparison

Put \(m_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega_i\) for all integers \(l\), so \(m_{-l}=m_l=[z^l]J_i\) for \(l>0\) and \(m_0=2\).
The sequence \(m_l-m_{l-a}\) is represented by a measure of bounded variation.
At nonnegative indices it differs from \(V_iJ_i\) by corrections of total size \(O(1+\sum_{l=1}^a |[z^l]J_i|)=O(1)\).
Correct them by monomial densities, leaving the negative coefficient at \(-l\), \(l>0\), equal to \(-[z^{l+a}]V_iJ_i\).
The preceding local bound shows that shifting this representation by any \(O(b)\) nonnegative index costs bounded total corrections on the positive indices.
The coefficients of \(S_iU_i\) have bounded absolute sum and its degree is \(O(b)\), so the positive Taylor coefficients of \(S_iU_iV_iJ_i\) have a representing measure of uniformly bounded variation.

Truncate \(U_iV_iX_i\) through \(H=\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q_i\).
Its degree is \(O(b)\) by the vanishing window and its absolute coefficient sum is bounded by the initial \(V_iX_i\) estimates and the finitely many shifts of \(U_i\).
It agrees with \(S_iU_iV_iJ_i\) through \(H\).
For the actual denominator the polynomial identity is
\[
      A_i U_iV_i B^\sharp-Q_i\Pi
          =\Pi\bigl(S_i U_iV_i J_i-Q_i\bigr).
\]
The left polynomial has degree \(O(h)\) and the parenthesized difference on the right has bounded positive-index representing variation and zero coefficients through \(H\).
The [two interval cutoffs](Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) and the bounded circle supremum of \(\Pi\) give bounded circle \(L^1\) norm for the left polynomial.
Adding \(Q_i\Pi\) leaves a bounded \(L^1\) norm and hence [bounded integer support](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) for \(A_iU_iV_iB^\sharp\).
Its constant coefficient has modulus two, since all added binomials have constant one.
Its Mahler measure is \(2M(B^\sharp)>2\) and tends to two, contradicting the [sparse endpoint gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#statement).
This return uses prefix agreement even for the reciprocal square with either sign pairing.
It excludes the contrary sequence and proves \(d_4=o(h)\).
The [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives the corresponding uniform bound on \(q\).
