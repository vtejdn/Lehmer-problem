# Which joint tests actually restrict dense integral trace polynomials?

2026-11-25.

<a id="results"></a>
## The global unit relation is visible at one finite place

The [preceding sparse estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#statement) controls the distance to the next nonzero coefficient from a dyadic evaluation of the prefix already seen.
A coefficient at every position satisfies those separate upper bounds.
For the dense case begin instead with the exterior root \(T\), the fractional linear coordinate \(Y=((q+1)T-q)/(q+1-qT)\), and the exact pole \(q^{2D}F((q+1)/q)=(2q+1)^2\).
Among irreducible rational reciprocal orbits of the prescribed root signature at this pole, integrality is equivalent to multiplicative dependence of \(T,Y\). [Finite-place height formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement)
At a true solution both coordinates are powers of the same unit, and the same exponents must work at every conjugate.
This condition applies to a polynomial with completely dense coefficients.

For fixed height and degree, the [one-place reconstruction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#statement) uses \(O(\log\log(9d))\) low bits of **each** coefficient to leave at most one true minimum of reciprocal degree \(d\).
The common real exponent ratio then determines the exterior root by strict monotonicity.
Across different unknown heights and degrees at most \(X\), a common monic factor at precision \(2^b\), \(b=O(\log(2X))\), has degree at most four for distinct solutions.
The only possible reduced roots of this factor have order one or three. [Separation across solutions](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v182.md#statement)
A comparison of two solutions can prove uniqueness without excluding a single isolated solution.
For an individual solution the corresponding local logarithmic derivative has a numerator of degree at most two.
It forces at most three conjugates per closed dyadic disk of radius \(1/8\), and at most eight roots over \(\mathbb Q_2\). [Individual disk bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v183.md#statement)
Local bounds for polynomials with few terms also occur in [Avendaño and Krick](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2011.01.006); the disk calculation here uses the fractional linear relation of the designated trace lift.

## Testing the distinction on actual models

For every sufficiently large \(q\equiv4\bmod8\) and each fixed odd denominator prime, there are rational irreducible conjugate sets in arbitrarily large admissible degrees with the exact pole, long zero prefix and every powered capacity test, whose entire coefficient class **modulo eight** is disallowed for genuine solutions. [Congruence control](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#tests)
The two quotient rings at two require even values for both coprime exponent parameters.
A separate family meeting the same archimedean tests has arbitrarily many rational dyadic roots in a single disk, violating the three-root bound. [Root-cluster control](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v183.md#control)
When the denominator at the omitted prime is sufficiently large for the degree and height, every nontrivial monomial in the two coordinates still has a bad finite place, and taking radicals or twisting by roots of unity cannot make it a unit. [Denominator cancellation](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#remaining)

Two different global coefficient tests complement the local ones.
For the real trace polynomial \(H\) of degree \(D\), the number of its negative coefficients is \(D/(2q+1)+O(\sqrt{D\log D})\); at sufficiently large realized degrees this count recovers \(q\).
The integral carries of its successive prefixes are governed by tails of a single sum of independent Bernoulli variables. [Rounding and sign profile](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v178.md#statement)
This counts signs in \(H\), whereas the support restriction in the reciprocal \(F\) persists under arbitrary cyclotomic completion.
Dense coefficient words made of a bounded number of bounded-order rational recurrence pieces are also effectively finite on this branch. [Cyclotomic completion](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v180.md#statement) · [Recurrence windows](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v176.md#statement)

## Enumeration and the first unresolved existence question

The two power exponents have effective bounds polynomial in a numerical trace-degree ceiling \(X\).
Rational factorization of their sections gives a [deterministic polynomial-bit-time census in \(X\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#census) over all possible heights, coefficients and grids.
For the [executed census through trace degree 40](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/dense_trace_census.md#census) the calculation instead used the existing integral Newton sum and discriminant budget algorithm.
It excluded all heights in that range, including candidates of reciprocal degree up to 80.

The remaining case is a single dense, locally admissible integral polynomial in unbounded degree.
The separation bounds restrict how its dyadic conjugates can cluster, whilst the real root conditions require a Salem orbit with one exterior conjugate near \((q+1)/q\).
No contradiction follows yet between these two requirements.
A concrete next question is whether the exact pole and the real distribution force an inadmissible dyadic disk, or equivalently for this proposed argument, whether an appropriate disk occupancy lower bound can be proved for every surviving candidate.
An affirmative result for all sufficiently large heights would close this particular sufficient counterexample construction.
The [unrestricted Lehmer question](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) also includes inputs outside the trace-pole branch.
