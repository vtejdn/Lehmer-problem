# Bounding the first power in the two-exterior construction

<a id="exponent"></a>
## A deterministic exponent for dependent or independent neutral phases

Let \(P\) be as in the [two-exterior power theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#statement), outside the equal-modulus matching case, with \(q=m-2\ge2\) neutral inverse pairs.
Put \(M=M(P)>1\), and let \(d\) be the degree of its splitting field.
The existence of a power placing one neutral trace in each of the \(q\) equal angular bins can be made effective in terms of \(m,d,M\), including the components with dependent neutral phases.

In the dependent case let \(b\ge3\) be the common component size, \(t\) the number of complete neutral blocks and \(p=q-t\).
Let \(L\) be the least common multiple of the orders of the torsion products in the [neutral relation lattice](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#relations).
Then \(L\le2d^2\).
In fact the roots of unity in the splitting field form a finite group, since a primitive root of order \(j\) in this field has \(\varphi(j)\le d\) and \(\varphi(j)\ge\sqrt{j/2}\) by the [prime-power bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#count).
A finite group of complex roots of unity is cyclic, so there is a primitive root whose order is the exponent of the whole group.
In the independent case take \(b=1,t=0,L=1,p=q\).

In either case define
\[
 s=\frac{1}{32bq},\qquad
 \delta=1-\cos(2\pi s),\qquad
 J=\left\lceil\frac{\pi\sqrt p}{\sqrt\delta}\right\rceil+1 .
\]
There is a successful exponent at most
\[
             L\left(1+\left\lceil
                    8pJ^p(1+M^{LJ})^{d-1}/\delta
                      \right\rceil\right).
\]
The estimate concerns the complete powered target of degree \(2m\), with its two exterior inverse pairs unused in this interlacing.
It allows the first successful exponent to grow with the degree.

## Centering the finite test on the relation torus

Take the configuration inside the prescribed bins from the [signed-block construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#bins).
Its distance from every boundary in the folded angle is at least one quarter of a bin width.
Choose \(p\) free neutral coordinates by omitting one variable per equation.
The omitted variable is the signed product of the other \(b-1\) variables in that block on the multiples of \(L\).
In normalized circle coordinates a bin has length \(1/(2q)\).
Moving each free coordinate by less than \(s\) changes every omitted coordinate by circular distance at most \((b-1)s<1/(8q)\).
Thus every free configuration in the chosen box succeeds.
For independent neutral phases take the bin midpoints and use the same margin.

Apply the [positive trigonometric test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v56.md#test) to the free coordinates, with dimension \(p\), radius \(s\), frequency parameter \(J\) and the centers just chosen.
Its integral is greater than \(\delta/2\), since \(p(1-\cos(\pi/(J+1)))<\delta/2\).
The sum of absolute Fourier coefficients is at most \(2pJ^p\), and all the coordinate frequencies have modulus at most \(J\).

Write \(u_j\) for the free neutral representatives.
For every nonzero tested frequency \(\boldsymbol l\), the unit \(\gamma=\prod_{j=1}^p u_j^{L l_j}\) differs from one.
Indeed no nonzero vector supported on the free labels belongs to the neutral relation lattice, since its distinct generators have disjoint supports and each has a nonzero omitted coordinate.
Under any Galois automorphism the image of this product uses at most one representative of each inverse pair, so \(|g\gamma|\le M^{LJ}\).
The [integer norm and finite geometric sum](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v56.md#norm) now give a positive mean of the test on the first \(1+\lceil8pJ^p(1+M^{LJ})^{d-1}/\delta\rceil\) multiples of \(L\).
The derived exponent bound follows.

For a sequence of this type with \(M\to1\), a successful \(n\) satisfies \(n\log M\ge\delta_2>0\) by the [bounded-deficiency gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#statement) applied to the powered polynomial of measure \(M^n\).
The upper estimate above gives a computable search interval and the fixed positive lower bound on the powered measure leaves the original measure sensitive to the size of \(n\).
