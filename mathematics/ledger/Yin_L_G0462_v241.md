# Recovering the possible seeds of an integer row from its trace polynomials

<a id="candidates"></a>
## At most three seeds for a fixed first polynomial

In the [two-row integer test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#statement) let \(e=2m\ge4,\ B=4e\), and take its prescribed prime \(p=p_e\).
Write \(k^\iota(Y)=(\iota k)(Y)=k(1/Y)\), \(S=Y+Y^{-1}\), \(D=Y-Y^{-1}\), and \(\mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\).
For a first polynomial \(a\) of degree \(B\) with both endpoint coefficients one, put \(f=Y^{-2e}a\).
Define \(A_a,B_a\in\mathbb Z[S]\) by
\[
          f+\iota f=A_a(S),\qquad f-\iota f=D B_a(S).
\]
Indeed \(Y^j+Y^{-j}=C_j(S)\) and \(Y^j-Y^{-j}=D W_j(S)\) for \(j\ge1\), where \(C_0=2,\ C_1=S,\ W_0=0,\ W_1=1\) and both sequences satisfy \(H_{j+1}=SH_j-H_{j-1}\).
Here \(\deg W_j=j-1\).
The two extreme terms of \(f\) cancel in \(f-\iota f\), so \(\deg B_a\le2e-2\) whenever \(B_a\ne0\).

For a seed root \(\alpha\) write \(z=\alpha^p,\ t=z+z^{-1},\ \Delta=t^2-4\), and let \(T\) be the monic minimum of \(t\).
Powering preserves degree at this prime, by the [distinct powered conjugates](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers).
Inversion acts nontrivially on \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and its fixed field is \(F=\mathbb Q(t)=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
Thus \(\deg T=m\), and \(d=z-z^{-1}\) satisfies \(d^2=\Delta\) and is outside \(F\).
The identity
\[
  2\mathcal E f=D A_a'(S)+S B_a(S)+(S^2-4)B_a'(S)
\]
follows from \(\mathcal E S=D\) and \(\mathcal E D=S\).
Consequently \(f(z),(\mathcal E f)(z)\in F\) exactly when \(B_a(t)=A_a'(t)=0\).
In that case their values in the trace field are
\[
                     u=A_a(t)/2,\qquad \ell=\Delta B_a'(t)/2.          \tag{1}
\]
The required \(u\ne0\) and \(\ell/u\notin\mathbb Q\) imply \(\ell\ne0\).
They exclude \(B_a=0\) and require \(T\) to occur with multiplicity one in \(B_a\).
Thus the candidates are among the distinct monic integral irreducible factors of degree \(m\) of \(\gcd(B_a,A_a')\) that occur simply in \(B_a\).
There are at most \(\lfloor(2e-2)/m\rfloor=3\).
This count uses the irrationality requirement of the individual C406 row.

Each such \(T\) can underlie at most one actual seed polynomial.
It determines the powered polynomial \(Q(Y)=Y^mT(Y+Y^{-1})\), and for a valid seed this is the degree-\(e\) minimum of \(z\).
In \(\mathbb Q(z)\) there is at most one \(p\)-th root of that specified \(z\), since the ratio of two different ones would be a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity of degree \(p-1>e\).
Conjugating \(z\) conjugates the recovered root, so its rational minimal polynomial is unique.
In particular the bound of three applies with \(a\) fixed even when its partner \(b\) varies.

## Exact tests in the trace field of degree \(m\)

Start with lists satisfying the ordinary integer conditions of C406, including both endpoints of \(a\), \(0\le\deg b<B\), and \(\gcd(a,Y^{B-1}b(1/Y))=1\).
For each candidate \(T\) above work in the field \(F=\mathbb Q[S]/(T)\), with \(t\) the class of \(S\).
Require \(\Delta=t^2-4\) to be nonsquare in \(F\), which can be tested by factoring \(X^2-\Delta\) over \(F\).
This gives \([\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]=2m\) for a root \(z\) of \(X^2-tX+1\), because \(t=z+z^{-1}\).
Such a \(z\) and its inverse are algebraic integers since \(T\) is monic integral.
Test nontorsion by excluding \(C_j(t)=2\) for \(1\le j\le2e^2\).
Indeed \(C_j(t)=2\) exactly when \(z^j=1\), and a root of unity of degree \(e\) has order at most \(2e^2\) by the [elementary totient bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#count).
Use (1) to require \(u\ne0\) and \(\ell/u\notin\mathbb Q\).

For \(g=Y^{-2e}b\) compute \(A_b=g+\iota g\) and \(B_b=(g-\iota g)/D\) as polynomials in \(S\).
In \(F\) set \(\tau=-A_b(t)/u,\ c=B_b(t)/u\).
The remaining individual conditions are precisely
\[
   \tau^2-\Delta c^2=4,\qquad C_p(\tau)=t,\qquad cW_p(\tau)=1,          \tag{2}
\]
\[
   tB_b(t)+\Delta B_b'(t)=(\ell+u/p)\tau,\qquad
       A_b'(t)=-(\ell+u/p)c .                                           \tag{3}
\]
For a pair of rows take a common candidate \(T\), apply the individual tests to both and require \(u_1\ell_2-u_2\ell_1\ne0\) in \(F\).
All the field computations in these tests use coefficients in degree \(m\).
The argument below uses the quadratic extension to verify the formulas.

Formally take \(d^2=\Delta\) and \(z=(t+d)/2\), so \(z^{-1}=(t-d)/2\).
The second value condition \(g(z)=-u\alpha^{-1}\) and its inverse conjugate force
\[
                  \alpha=(\tau+dc)/2,\qquad \alpha^{-1}=(\tau-dc)/2 .
\]
The first equation of (2) makes the two expressions inverses.
The sum and difference of their \(p\)-th powers are \(C_p(\tau)\) and \(dcW_p(\tau)\), so the last two equations of (2) are equivalent to \(\alpha^p=z\) under that norm condition.
In particular \(\alpha\) is a nontorsion unit, since its power and the inverse power are algebraic integers and \(z\) is nontorsion.
Also \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(z)\) and \(\mathbb Q(\tau)=F\), the latter since \(t=C_p(\tau)\).
The seed can be returned explicitly as \(X^m T_0(X+X^{-1})\), where \(T_0\) is the rational minimal polynomial of \(\tau\) in \(F\).
Switching the sign of \(d\) exchanges inverse roots of this same polynomial.
For a common candidate, uniqueness of the \(p\)-th root ensures that the two rows recover the same \(\alpha\) with this choice of \(d\).

Expanding \(2(\mathcal E g)(z)=dA_b'(t)+tB_b(t)+\Delta B_b'(t)\) in the basis \(1,d\) gives (3) exactly when \((\mathcal E g)(z)=(\ell+u/p)\alpha^{-1}\).
These are both value and derivative conditions of the [prescribed-offset equations](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#jets) at the positive sign and \(v=1\).
They imply the remaining square divisibility in C406 directly.
The centered seed diagonal is \(x^{-2ep}G(x)=f(x^p)+x^{-1}g(x^{-p})\).
At \(\alpha\) its value is zero, and its Euler derivative is \(p\ell+u-p(\ell+u/p)=0\), by applying inverse conjugation to the two data of \(g\).
Separability now gives \(P_0^2\mid G\), as in the [single-row extension](Yin_L_G0462_v239.md#extension).
This proves necessity and sufficiency without factoring either large diagonal or its derivative.
It gives the same finite coefficient-box test as [C406](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#integer).

## Bounds for all seeds of one row and uniqueness of a short second polynomial

Use logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(\cdot)\) in this section.
For fixed \(a\), let \(\mathcal S\) be the distinct seed polynomials for which some partner \(b\) makes an individually admissible row, and write \(h_P=m(P)\).
Their distinct powered polynomials \(Q_P\) all divide the nonzero integral polynomial \(a(Y)-Y^Ba(1/Y)=Y^{2e}D B_a(S)\) if \(\mathcal S\) is nonempty.
Indeed each powered root annihilates that difference by (1), and the uniqueness above prevents two seeds from giving one \(Q_P\).
Since \(m(Q_P)=p h_P\), monic division and the coefficient Euclidean norm give
\[
        p\sum_{P\in\mathcal S}h_P
               \le \log(2\sqrt{B-1}\,\|a\|_\infty).                    \tag{4}
\]
Here both extreme coefficients of the difference vanish and all its remaining \(B-1\) coefficients have absolute value at most \(2\|a\|_\infty\).
For any nonzero integral polynomial \(J\), Jensen's formula gives \(m(J)\ge0\), and concavity of the logarithm applied to \(|J|^2\) together with Parseval gives \(m(J)\le\log\|J\|_2\).
If the set is empty, (4) holds since the endpoints of \(a\) are one.

For one fixed pair \(a,b\) of maximum coefficient magnitude \(H\), let \(\mathcal S_b\) be the subset it realizes.
The [reflected elimination](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity) shows that \(\prod_{P\in\mathcal S_b}Q_P^2\) divides \(Y^B(aa^\iota-bb^\iota)\).
The latter polynomial has constant and leading coefficient one.
On the circle its modulus is at most \(|a|^2+|b|^2\).
Logarithmic integration and Parseval yield
\[
           p\sum_{P\in\mathcal S_b}h_P
               \le \tfrac12\log(\|a\|_2^2+\|b\|_2^2)
               \le\log(\sqrt{2B+1}\,H).                               \tag{5}
\]

For two distinct partners \(b_1,b_2\) of the same \(a\), each of coefficient magnitude at most \(H\), take \(\mathcal S_{12}=\mathcal S_{b_1}\cap\mathcal S_{b_2}\).
At a powered root of each common seed the two centered second polynomials have the same value and Euler derivative, since both equal the data prescribed by \(a,\alpha\).
Thus \(Q_P^2\mid b_1-b_2\) by the [kernel of the two evaluations](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#jets).
Monic division and the same Euclidean norm inequality give
\[
       2p\sum_{P\in\mathcal S_{12}}h_P
                \le m(b_1-b_2)\le\log(2\sqrt B\,H).                   \tag{6}
\]
Moreover \(b_1-b_2\) has degree \(<4e\), whereas two distinct powered squares have combined degree \(4e\), so two different partners can share at most one seed.
For a specified seed \(P\) and fixed \(a\) there is therefore at most one qualifying partner in a box with \(H<\exp(2ph_P)/(2\sqrt B)\).
In particular uniqueness holds in the range \(H<\exp(2ph_P)/(2(B+1))\).
These are upper bounds on possible seeds and partners and do not assert that an arbitrary row has one.
