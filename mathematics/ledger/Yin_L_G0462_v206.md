# Two low-measure reciprocal witnesses with a small nonzero derivative determinant

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous bounds on the final coprime rows

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal nontorsion unit polynomial of degree \(d=2m\ge4\), \(\alpha\) a root, \(h=m(P)=\log M(P)\), and \(p\ge5\) a degree-preserving prime for \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(N=N_{K/\mathbb Q}\), \(q=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(K^+=\mathbb Q(q)\), and \(E=X\,d/dX\).
For each \(1\le l<m\) set \(L_l=(d/l)(ph+\log(2dp))\).
There are two primitive reciprocal or antireciprocal two-class diagonals of the same span, each of the form
\[
  F_i(x)=A_i(x^p)+\epsilon x^S A_i^*(x^p),\qquad
  A_i^*(X)=X^a A_i(1/X),\qquad i=1,2 .
\]
Here \(a=\deg A_i\), \(A_i(0)\ne0\), \(S>0\) is congruent to \(1\) or \(-1\bmod p\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\), \(\gcd(A_i,A_i^*)=1\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(P^2\mid F_i\).
The common span is at most \(4pd+1\).
Let \(R_i\) be the product of the primitive noncyclotomic factors of \(F_i/P^2\) with multiplicities.
The two final rows have nonzero evaluations at \(\beta\) and a nonzero determinant
\[
       W=A_1(\beta)(EA_2)(\beta)-A_2(\beta)(EA_1)(\beta).
\]
They satisfy, with absolute constants,
\[
       m(R_i)\ll L_l,\qquad
       \frac{\log|NA_i(\beta)|}{d}\ll L_l,\qquad
       \frac{\log|NW|}{d}\ll L_l .                                      \tag{1}
\]
Each final row can simultaneously have nonzero [centered derivative remainder](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd) and nonproportional reduced trace data in the sense specified [below](#remainders), for whichever parity occurs.

Taking \(l=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) gives \(L_l\ll ph+\log(2dp)\).
In the [fourth-logarithmic prime window](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement), \(m(R_i)/p\), \(\log|NA_i(\beta)|/(pd)\) and \(\log|NW|/(pd)\) therefore all tend to zero on any supplied sequence with \(h\to0\) and \(d\to\infty\).
The two coprime row ratios are distinct by \(W\ne0\) and their common endpoint normalization, so the [two-graph lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#determinant) still applies to the sum of their measures.

<a id="space"></a>
## A large space of rows with simultaneously short trace data

Put \(n=4d,\ k=pn/2,\ J=2k+1\), and initially fix either sign \(\sigma=\pm1\).
Use \(n+1\) coefficients for \(A(X)\) of degree at most \(n\), and \(m\) coefficients each for \(U,V\) in the integral basis \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{m-1}\), with \(C_j(x+x^{-1})=x^j+x^{-j}\) for \(j\ge1\).
Consider the two algebraic integers
\[
  \alpha^{-k}A(\beta)-(1-\sigma\alpha)U(q),\qquad
  \alpha^{-k}\{2p(EA)(\beta)-J A(\beta)\}-(1+\sigma\alpha)V(q).
\]
When both vanish they give precisely the odd centered value and derivative conditions, hence \(P^2\) divides the formal signed diagonal \(A(x^p)+\sigma x^J A(x^{-p})\).
Indeed the first equality cancels the two values at \(\alpha\), and the second cancels the centered Euler derivatives after inverting \(\alpha\).

For coefficients in \(\{0,\ldots,T\}\), at a conjugate \(z\) of \(\alpha\) put \(s_z=|\log|z||\).
Each displayed integer has absolute value at most \(5(n+1)(J+1)T e^{ks_z}\) there, since \(|pj-k|\le k\) for the powered monomials, and the trace terms have Laurent exponents of magnitude at most \(m\).
Split each real embedding coordinate and both real coordinates of one embedding per complex pair into intervals of length at most \(1/3\).
Two algebraic integers in the same coordinate box coincide by the integral norm of their difference.
Since \(\sum_z s_z=2h\), the number of distinct pairs is bounded by
\[
 (T+1)^{2d} e^B,\qquad B=2d\log(100(n+1)(J+1))+4kh.
\]
Take \(T=\lceil e^{B/l}\rceil\).
One fiber in the coefficient cube contains more than \((T+1)^{n+1-d-l}\) points.
Differences from one of these points have coordinate magnitudes at most \(T\).
On any \(r\)-dimensional rational subspace some \(r\) of the coordinates give an injective projection, so a translate meets this cube in at most \((T+1)^r\) points.
Thus the rational span \(\mathcal V\) of all integral solutions of the two homogeneous equations with coefficient magnitudes at most \(T\) has dimension \(r>n+1-d-l\).
Projection to the row \(A\) is injective since \(1\pm\sigma\alpha\ne0\) and the reduced trace representatives are unique.
For a basis choose actual short solutions.
The map to \((U(q),V(q))\in(K^+)^2\) has kernel contained in the multiples of \(Q^2\), where \(Q\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
Such multiples have dimension \(n+1-2d\) at most.
The image therefore has dimension \(>d-l>m\).

<a id="division"></a>
## Removing fixed factors and obtaining coprime rows

Homogenize all rows in \(\mathcal V\) to binary forms of degree \(n\), with reversal interchanging the two variables \(X,Y\).
Take the primitive integral gcd \(G(X,Y)\) of all basis rows and their reversals together, choosing either sign.
Write \(g=\deg G\), \(G(Y,X)=\delta G(X,Y)\) with \(\delta=\pm1\), and divide every row by \(G\).
Gauss's lemma gives integral quotient forms for the integral basis.
One has \(G(\beta,1)\ne0\), since otherwise all first trace values would vanish and the trace image could have dimension at most \(m\).

In fact \(g<l\).
First the dimension of multiples of \(G\) gives \(n-g+1\ge r\), hence \(n-g\ge2d-1\).
The evaluation and first derivative of a polynomial of that available degree range freely over \(K^2\) by rational Hermite interpolation modulo \(Q^2\).
Multiplication by \(G(X,1)\) acts invertibly on these two values at \(\beta\).
Requiring the original two centered equations with arbitrary rational trace data imposes exactly \(d\) linear conditions on \(K^2\), since each of the two allowed trace components has dimension \(m\).
Consequently \(r\le n-g+1-d\), proving the bound.

After division the two rows of each diagonal are the quotient form and its signed reversal with sign \(\sigma\delta\).
They have no common projective base point shared by the whole space of pairs, by the definition of \(G\).
A generic rational combination gives a coprime pair.
To see this, let \(B_j\) be the quotient basis and \(y_j\) indeterminates.
Over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}[X,Y]\), the generic form \(\sum_j y_jB_j\) is its fixed factor \(\gcd_j B_j\) times a primitive linear form in the parameters, which is irreducible by Gauss's lemma.
The reversed family has the corresponding factorization.
A shared fixed factor is excluded by construction.
If the two generic forms had a common factor of positive binary degree over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(\mathbf y)\), clearing denominators shows that the two primitive linear factors would have to be associated.
This would make all ratios \(B_j(X,1)/B_j(1,X)\) identical as rational functions.
A space with one such ratio has dimension at most \(\lceil(n-g+1)/2\rceil\).
Indeed evaluations at that many generic nonzero points determine the evaluations at their reciprocals, giving more than \(n-g\) distinct evaluation points.
This contradicts \(r>n+1-d-l\) for \(n=4d\) and \(l<m\).
The homogeneous resultant in the parameters is thus a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(2n\).

For every generic quotient row let \(t_0,t_1\) be its first and last occupied exponents after dehomogenization.
The two fixed nonzero endpoint linear forms give the same \(t_0,t_1\) generically.
Remove the resulting monomial and use the orientation with positive shift, exchanging the rows if necessary as in the [reciprocal normalization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row).
The signed shift before orienting is \(1+p(n-g-t_0-t_1)\), so it is nonzero and the two final tuples use the same normalization.
Their span has the parity of \(p(n-g)+1\), hence is odd exactly when \(g\) is even.

## Nonproportional traces and the determinant under division

In the final odd normalization put \(u,v\) equal to the two [odd trace data](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd).
In the even normalization put \(J'=pa+S=2j\), \(B_0=\alpha^{-j}A(\beta)\), \(B_1=\alpha^{-j}(2p(EA)(\beta)-J'A(\beta))\), and \(\Delta=\alpha-\alpha^{-1}\).
For \(\epsilon=-1\) use \(u=B_0,\ v=B_1/(2\Delta)\).
For \(\epsilon=1\) use \(u=2B_0/\Delta,\ v=(B_1-qu)/(q^2-4)\).
The [even trace congruences](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#even) make these integral traces, with remainder \(v-U_0'(q)\) for the reduced representative \(U_0\) of \(u\).
For the positive even sign this is the remainder after dividing the expression in L189 by \(q^2-4\).
In the odd case the remainder is \(v+u-2(2\epsilon-q)U_0'(q)\).
Each choice is a rational linear coordinate system for the two permissible trace components.
Dividing by \(G\), removing a fixed monomial, and exchanging the rows preserve the rational dimension of the image in value and first derivative, by invertibility at \(\beta\) and the inverse involution.
The final trace image still has dimension \(>m\).

In either chart the zero remainder is a graph over \(u\), of dimension \(m\).
The locus of pairs rationally proportional in the chosen trace basis also cannot contain this image.
Indeed a linear space of proportional pairs with two independent first components must have the same proportionality scalar on both and their sum, and then throughout the space by linearity.
If the first components span a single line then all second components lie on that line as well, by adding a pair with nonzero first component.
If all first components vanish the dimension is at most \(m\) again.
Thus we may require for both rows \(u\ne0,\ v/u\notin\mathbb Q\) and nonzero remainder.
The same argument permits these conditions simultaneously on the original odd rows before division.
Also some pair of original trace vectors has \(U_1(q)V_2(q)-U_2(q)V_1(q)\ne0\), because a single line over \(K^+\) has rational dimension \(m\).

To make all choices with small integral coefficients, use the nonzero resultant and endpoint forms on each row, one nonzero linear component of each required remainder, one nonidentically zero quadratic coordinate minor for each nonproportionality condition, and one nonzero rational component of the two-row determinant.
Their product in the two parameter tuples has degree at most \(4n+24\).
It is nonzero somewhere on the integer grid from zero through that degree in every coordinate, by the univariate root bound successively in each variable.
The resulting original rows and both their trace representatives have coefficient magnitudes at most \(r(4n+24)T\).
The original diagonal has at most \(2(n+1)\) coefficient positions, so its logarithmic measure is at most \(\log(2(n+1)r(4n+24)T)\ll L_l\).
Division by \(G(x^p,1)\) keeps \(P^2\) and can only decrease the measure, as does division by positive scalar content.
This proves the cofactor estimate on both primitive coprime diagonals.

Write \(\widetilde A_i,\widetilde W\) for the rows and determinant before division.
Their original trace equations give exactly
\[
  2p\beta^{-n}\widetilde W
       =(1-\alpha^2)(U_1(q)V_2(q)-U_2(q)V_1(q)).
\]
Writing \(H_*=r(4n+24)T\), each \(U_i,V_i\) is bounded at the trace of a conjugate \(z\) by \(2mH_*e^{(m-1)s_z}\).
Using \(\sum_z s_z=2h\), \(|N(1\pm\alpha)|\le2^d e^h\) and the unit \(\beta\) in this identity and the first trace equation gives \(\log|N\widetilde W|,\log|N\widetilde A_i(\beta)|\ll dL_l\).
Dividing both rows by \(G(X,1)\) divides their determinant by \(G(\beta,1)^2\), since the derivative terms of the common factor cancel.
Its nonzero integral norm is at least one.
A common monomial change contributes a unit to the determinant, while reversing both rows applies the automorphism \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\) and multiplies by a signed unit, by the chain rule.
Individual scalar contents divide its norm further, and the same norm comparisons hold for the first evaluations.
This proves all of (1) on the final rows.

The [small nonzero individual remainders](Yin_L_G0462_v199.md#obstruction) in L199 were obtained with unbounded possible cofactor cost.
Before division the reduced-coefficient bound gives \(\log|N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}D_i|\ll m L_l\) for each nonzero odd remainder \(D_i\), simultaneously with the measure bound \(O(L_l)\) for its unstripped diagonal.
After division the two-row determinant retains its small norm on the final coprime rows.
If \(G=1\) the final individual odd remainders also retain their small norm by the [reduced-coefficient estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v199.md#norm).
Indeed monomial normalization preserves the odd centered components and exchanging rows changes both by the same sign, by the value and centered derivative cancellation.
For general \(G\), division can change both the parity and the reduced representatives, leaving the final individual remainder norms uncontrolled by (1).
The choice \(l=\lceil d\sqrt{h+\log(2dp)/p}\rceil\) is eventually allowed along any shrinking sequence in the fourth-logarithmic window and gives \(g=o(d)\) while all three normalized costs in (1) still tend to zero.
