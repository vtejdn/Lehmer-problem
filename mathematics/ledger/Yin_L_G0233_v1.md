Let P be monic irreducible, reciprocal and nontorsion in Z[t], with degree d and P(0)=1.
Thus P*(t)=t^d P(1/t)=P(t).
Write O=Z[alpha], m=log M(P)>0 and G=P mod2.
The precise class studied is

\[
 F\in(P,2)\subset\mathbb Z[t],\qquad F\ne0,\qquad
 S_2(F)=\left(\sum_j c_j^2\right)^{1/2}<2.           \tag{1}
\]

Membership means F=P U+2V with U,V integral, equivalently G divides F mod2.
At every original root, in a dyadic splitting field normalized by v(2)=1, it supplies v(F(alpha_i))>=1 whenever the evaluation is nonzero.
Therefore the specified all-root floor is A=d log2, and its strict L229 coefficient-energy surplus is exactly the inequality S_2<2.
This is a class defined by an actual-order congruence, not by the weaker condition v_2 Res(P,F)>=d.
Even separate inequalities v(F(alpha_i))>=1 are not asserted to imply (1) for a nonmaximal order.
Other primes or greater actual depths can pay a larger coefficient budget and are outside this particular strict-floor-one classification.

Integer coefficients and S_2^2<4 force every nonzero coefficient to be plus or minus one and their number to be at most three.
A single monomial cannot belong to (P,2), because t is a unit modulo G.
Remove an initial monomial and an overall sign, so the remaining cases are

\[
 1\mathbin{\pm}t^a\quad(a>0),\qquad
 1+\epsilon t^a+\delta t^b\quad(0<a<b,\ \epsilon,\delta=\pm1).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

The removed monomial changes neither coefficient norms nor the absolute resultant, since alpha is a unit; it only decreases degree.
All original monomial factors and their original degree are still charged when applying a bound to the original F.
Every polynomial in(2) is primitive.
The effective support rho=S_1^2/S_2^2 is respectively two or three.

Every such evaluation is nonzero on the stated reciprocal nontorsion domain.
This is immediate for binomials.
For completeness, normalize a trinomial instead as f=t^b+eta t^a+xi, eta,xi=plus or minus one.
If P divided f, reciprocity would also make it divide f*, and

\[
 f-\xi f^*=\eta(t^a-\xi t^{b-a}).                  \tag{3}
\]

At alpha, if 2a!=b this forces a nonzero power of alpha to be plus or minus one.
If 2a=b, it first forces xi=1; then alpha^a solves y^2+eta y+1=0 and is a root of unity.
Both contradict nontorsion.
Thus (1)--(2) give actual nonzero integer resultants without a presumed generic coprimality.
This proves applicability, not near-one coverage.

### 2. All exponents and all reduction multiplicities

Factor the full reduction as G=product_phi phi^(e_phi) over F_2.
Each factor has nonzero constant term and the order o_phi of any of its roots is odd.
Put

\[
 E=\max_\varphi e_\varphi,\quad
 L=\operatorname{lcm}_\varphi o_\varphi,\quad
 k=\lceil\log_2 E\rceil,\quad h=2^k L.              \tag{4}
\]

Then h is exactly the order of tau=t mod G in the unit group of F_2[t]/(G).
Indeed for n=2^v n_odd, t^n-1=(t^(n_odd)-1)^(2^v), and the first factor has simple roots.
Hence G divides t^n-1 if and only if every o_phi divides n_odd and every e_phi<=2^v.
This proves(4) with all nilpotent multiplicities included, without a good-prime or maximal-order hypothesis.
Consequently a binomial in(2) is in (P,2) exactly when h divides a; its minimum normalized degree is h.

Suppose now G divides 1+t^a+t^b,0<a<b.
Reciprocity of G implies divisibility of the reversed polynomial 1+t^(b-a)+t^b.
Subtraction and the fact that tau is a unit give

\[
 \tau^b=\tau^{2a},\qquad
 1+\tau^a+\tau^{2a}=0,\qquad
 \tau^{3a}=1.                                         \tag{5}
\]

Moreover tau^a cannot be one, because 1+1+1=1 in characteristic two.
Thus its order in the cyclic subgroup generated by tau is exactly three.
In particular 3 divides h, and the exponent pair modulo h must be either

\[
 (a,b)\equiv(h/3,2h/3)\quad\hbox{or}\quad
 (a,b)\equiv(2h/3,h/3)\pmod h.                     \tag{6}
\]

Having order three in a ring with zero divisors does not by itself imply 1+tau^a+tau^(2a)=0.
The extra exact criterion is

\[
 \nu_3(o_\varphi)=\nu_3(L)>0
                    \quad\hbox{for every factor }\varphi.    \tag{7}
\]

Necessity follows from(5): after reduction at each factor, tau^a must have order three, since the roots of y^2+y+1 in characteristic two have exactly that order.
Because (6) makes a congruent to h/3 or2h/3, any o_phi with smaller 3-adic valuation would instead divide a and give residue one.
Thus all valuations are the common positive maximum.

Conversely assume(7).
Set a_0=h/3=2^k(L/3).
For each residue root its a_0-th power has order three, so it is a root of 1+t^(a_0)+t^(2a_0).
The polynomial 1+t^(L/3)+t^(2L/3) has simple nonzero roots: L/3 is odd and its formal derivative is t^(L/3-1).
Taking its 2^k-th power gives 1+t^(a_0)+t^(2a_0), with multiplicity exactly2^k at those roots.
This covers each e_phi and proves divisibility by the complete G.
Reducing exponents modulo h now proves that every pair in(6) works.
The four choices of the two integer signs have the same binary reduction, so the criterion includes all of them.

We have therefore classified all normalized trinomials in (1): they exist if and only if(7) holds, and then exactly the exponent pairs(6) occur.
The least possible degree is 2h/3, attained by every signed version of 1+t^(h/3)+t^(2h/3).
If(7) fails, no such trinomial exists at any degree, not merely in a chosen search window.
After allowing binomials too, the least degree in the whole class(1) is

\[
 T_{\min}=\begin{cases}
 2h/3,&\text{if (7) holds},\\
 h,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{8}
\]

This accounts for possible repeated reductions and for every coefficient sign.
A shifted representative can only increase the degree in(8).

### 3. The exact common-time boundary of this nonmultiple class

\[
 C_h=|\operatorname{Res}(P,t^h-1)|\ge2^d,
 \qquad h\le3T/2.                                     \tag{9}
\]

The first statement follows by monic division of t^h-1 by P: binary divisibility makes the integral remainder even, so each original root supplies at least one dyadic valuation.
