# Two small resultants at the degree of a Salem polynomial

<a id="degree-returns"></a>
## Exact full-turn and half-turn products

Let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(f=z^3-z-1\), let \(g=z^3f(1/z)=1-z^2-z^3\), and let \(L\) be the least common multiple of the orders \(m\) with \(\varphi(m)\le12\).
This is finite since \(\varphi(m)=\prod_{\ell^a\parallel m}\ell^{a-1}(\ell-1)\) bounds every prime power in such an \(m\).
For all sufficiently large multiples \(N\) of \(L\), set
\[
 P_N(z)=\frac{z^Nf(z)-g(z)}{z-1},\qquad d=N+2 .
\]
Then \(P_N\) is irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(d\), has coefficients of height one, and has a unique exterior root, a Salem number in \((1,\theta)\) tending to \(\theta\).
All positive powers of a root retain degree \(d\).
Nevertheless
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Res}(P_N,z^d-1)\right|=N-7,\qquad
 \left|\operatorname{Res}(P_N,z^d+1)\right|=9 .
\]
In particular every monic integer divisor of \(z^{2d}-1\), including one chosen separately for each \(P_N\), has absolute resultant at most \(9(N-7)\).
The [earlier family with a degree-sized half-turn certificate](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v3.md#adaptive-return) approaches the same plastic number from below.
The main difference here is the position of the delay relative to the degree after the torsion factors have been removed.
The strategy-discovery contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained this comparison on 2026-11-16.

<a id="irreducible"></a>
## The torsion factors and the Salem root

Write \(D_N=z^N f-g\) and \(G=g/f\).
The numerator is antireciprocal of odd degree \(N+3\).
The coefficients of the quotient \(P_N\) at indices \(0,1,2\) are \(1,1,0\), at indices \(3,\ldots,N-1\) they are \(-1\), and the last three coefficients are \(0,1,1\), by multiplication by \(z-1\).

At any root of unity \(\zeta\) satisfying \(D_N(\zeta)=0\), the denominator \(f(\zeta)\) is nonzero, since the other two roots of \(f\) have modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}\) by its negative discriminant and constant coefficient.
If its order \(m\) is odd, twice an odd number, or a multiple of four, a conjugate of \(\zeta\) is respectively \(\zeta^2,-\zeta^2,-\zeta\).
Indeed the corresponding exponents \(2,m/2+2,m/2+1\) are coprime to \(m\) in their respective cases.
Writing the conjugate as \(\xi\zeta^h\), both equations \(D_N=0\) imply \(G(\xi\zeta^h)=G(\zeta)^h\) since \(N\) is even.
At zero \(G(z)=-1+z+O(z^2)\).
For \(h=2\) the two series have different constants, and for \((\xi,h)=(-1,1)\) their linear coefficients differ.
Clearing denominators gives a nonzero polynomial over the rationals of degree at most \(6h\le12\).
It follows that \(m\mid L\), so \(\zeta^N=1\) and
\[
 0=f(\zeta)-g(\zeta)=(\zeta-1)(2\zeta^2+3\zeta+2).
\]
The quadratic would give the nonintegral rational trace \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=-3/2\).
Thus the only torsion root is \(1\), and \(D_N'(1)=7-N\ne0\).

As in the [Salem root count](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v3.md#salem), on \(|z|=1\) one has \(|g|=|f|>0\).
For \(0<t<1\), Rouché's theorem compares \(z^Nf-tg\) with \(z^Nf\), giving at most one root outside the disk also at \(t=1\) by continuity.
For each fixed \(s\in(1,\theta)\), one has \(D_N(s)<0<D_N(\theta)\) for large \(N\).
Hence the unique exterior root tends to \(\theta\) from below.
Reciprocity gives its inverse inside the disk.
Every other irreducible factor would be a monic integer unit polynomial with all roots on the circle, hence cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem, whereas the quotient has no torsion root.
Indeed the factor containing the exterior root contains the unique interior root by the modulus of its constant coefficient.
If two conjugates have the same positive power, a Galois automorphism sending the first to the exterior root sends the second to a root of that same maximal modulus.
Uniqueness implies equality, giving the asserted degree preservation.

<a id="two-norms"></a>
## Evaluation at the degree

The identities
\[
 f-z^2g=(z-1)\Phi_3(z)^2,\qquad
 f+z^2g=-(z+1)\Phi_{12}(z)
\]
follow by expansion, where \(\Phi_j\) is the \(j\)-th cyclotomic polynomial.
At \(z^d=1,\ z\ne1\), the first identity gives \(P_N=z^{-2}\Phi_3(z)^2\).
The product of \(|\Phi_3|\) over all \(d\)-th roots is \(3\), by taking the product of \(|1-\omega^d|\) over the two primitive cube roots and using \(d\equiv2\bmod3\).
Removing \(z=1\) divides this product by \(\Phi_3(1)=3\), leaving one.
The remaining factor \(|P_N(1)|=N-7\) proves the first formula.
At \(z^d=-1\), the second identity gives \(P_N=z^{-2}(z+1)\Phi_{12}(z)/(z-1)\).
The products of \(|z+1|\) and \(|z-1|\) over these nodes are both two since \(d\) is even.
For each primitive twelfth root \(\omega\), \(\omega^d\) is a primitive sixth root, since \(12\mid N\).
Thus \(|1+\omega^d|=\sqrt3\), giving \(9\) from the four roots of \(\Phi_{12}\).
The divisor bound follows by multiplicativity since the absolute resultants of the divisor and its monic integer complementary factor are positive integers.

<a id="shifted-return"></a>
## Recovery three indices earlier

There is an infinite subsequence of these same polynomials with \(n=d-3=N-1\) for which
\[
 \log\left|\operatorname{Res}(P_N,z^n+1)\right|
       = n\log\lambda+O(1),\qquad \lambda>2 .
\]
At \(z^n=-1\) one has \(D_N=-zf-g=-W\), where \(W=z^4-z^3-2z^2-z+1\).
The substitution \(t=z+z^{-1}\) gives \(W/z^2=t^2-t-4\).
Its positive trace \((1+\sqrt{17})/2>5/2\) gives real reciprocal roots \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) with \(\lambda>2\), and its other trace in \((-2,2)\) gives \(\gamma,\bar\gamma\) on the circle with \(\gamma\ne-1\).
The product over the nodes of \(|z-1|\) is two, so the absolute resultant equals \(\tfrac12\prod_{W(r)=0}|1+r^n|\).
Choose unbounded multiples \(N\) of \(L\) with \(\gamma^N\to1\).
Such multiples exist by taking differences of widely separated indices of a convergent subsequence of \((\gamma^{Lj})_{j\ge1}\).
Then \(\gamma^n\to\gamma^{-1}\ne-1\), which proves the logarithmic estimate.
Since \(|\operatorname{Res}(P_N,z^n+1)|\le2^d M(P_N)^n\), this return gives the positive lower bound \(\log(\lambda/2)+o(1)\) for \(\log M(P_N)\) along that subsequence.
The two degree-index failures therefore leave open the effect of choosing an index in a degree-sized interval.

## Both degree-index products below the certificate cost with small residue degrees excluded

A different delay permits a growing small-prime sieve in the same comparison.
Fix \(T\ge3\) and \(K=6T^2+12\), and let \(D\) be an arbitrarily large common multiple of \(5,L\), and of \(p^3,p^j-1\) for all primes \(p\le T\) and \(1\le j\le K\).
Put \(H=z^2f-g=z^5-1\) and
\[
 S_D=\frac{z^{D+2}f-g}{H}
     =1+(z^5-z^3-z^2)\sum_{j=0}^{D/5-1}z^{5j}.
\]
This is monic and reciprocal of degree \(D\) and height one.
The torsion-degree argument [above](#irreducible) applies verbatim with the even delay \(D+2\).
Every torsion root has order dividing \(L\), so at that root \(z^{D+2}f-g=H\) and the root belongs to \(z^5=1\).
At each such root \(\omega\), the displayed quotient takes value \(1+(D/5)(1-\omega^3-\omega^2)\), nonzero for all sufficiently large \(D\) since there are just five values of the parenthetical factor.
The same root count and irreducibility argument gives Salem measures tending to \(\theta\) from below.
In characteristic \(p\le T\), every nonzero element of degree at most \(K\) has order dividing \(D\), and \(z^D-1\) vanishes there to order at least \(p^3>5\).
Consequently \(S_D=1+z^2f(z^D-1)/H\) equals one at each such element and at zero.
Every factor of \(\bar S_D\) therefore has degree greater than \(K\).

On \(z^D=1\), only the five nodes in \(H=0\) can give values different from one.
Their displayed linear expressions bound \(\left|\operatorname{Res}(S_D,z^D-1)\right|\) by \(O(D^5)\).
At \(z^D=-1\) the numerator is \(-z^2f-g=-(z+1)(z^4-z^3-z^2-z+1)\).
The quartic trace is \(t^2-t-3\), whose positive root lies strictly between \(2\) and \(5/2\) and whose other root lies in \((-2,2)\).
Its only exterior root \(\lambda_*\) thus satisfies \(1<\lambda_*<2\).
Interchanging the order of the monic resultant products gives
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Res}(S_D,z^D+1)\right|
   =2^{-5}\prod_{(r+1)(r^4-r^3-r^2-r+1)=0}|1+r^D|
   =O(\lambda_*^D),
\]
since every root of \(H\) has \(D\)-th power one.
Taking \(T\to\infty\) and successively large \(D\) gives arbitrarily large excluded residue degrees and prime ranges while both degree-index products stay below \(2^D\).
This sieve concerns the degrees of reduction factors.
The exact mixed valuations in [C162v3](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v3.md#statement) are a separate condition.
