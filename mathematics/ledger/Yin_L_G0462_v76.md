# A uniform Pisot excess for the quadratic prefix \(2\pm2w+w^2\)

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## The two offsets \(d,2d\) with middle coefficient of modulus two

For positive-constant Pisot inputs on branch \(j=2\), \(q\ge2\), there is an absolute positive lower bound for \(\theta-q\) when the trace numerator is
\[
 C(x)=2U_h(x/2)+2s U_{h-d}(x/2)+U_{h-2d}(x/2),
 \qquad s\in\{-1,1\},\qquad 1\le 2d\le h,\quad d\in\mathbb Z .
\]
Equivalently \(q\) is absolutely bounded here by the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
Together with the [unit middle coefficient](Yin_L_G0462_v74.md#quadratic), this covers both middle coefficients of absolute value one or two with last coefficient \(+1\) at this ratio on branch two.
The two ingredients suggested by the responsible agent during development were reduction modulo two at torsion pairs and testing the first half-integral coefficient at \(2d\).

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Four possible cyclotomic factors before rotation

Write \(A=2+2w+w^2,\ A^*=1+2w+2w^2\).
Both roots of \(A\) have modulus \(\sqrt2\).
If \(w\) and \(\lambda=A(w)/A^*(w)\) are roots of unity, then \(A(w)=\lambda A^*(w)\) gives \(\lambda\equiv w^2\bmod 2\) in the ring of integers of their joint cyclotomic field.
All conjugates of the algebraic integer \((\lambda-w^2)/2\) have modulus at most one.
If it is nonzero, the absolute norm is at least one, so equality holds at every conjugate and in particular \(\lambda=-w^2\).
The zero case gives \(\lambda=w^2\).
The two identities
\[
 A-w^2A^*=2(1+w)(1-w^3),\qquad
 A+w^2A^*=2(1+w+w^2+w^3+w^4)
\]
show that the orders are precisely \(1,2,3,5\).
For middle sign \(s=-1\) apply the result at \(-w\).

## Nine projected Herglotz transforms

Suppose \(J\) is the Herglotz transform of a symmetric circle probability, with constant one and \(B=(A/2)J\in\mathbb Z[[w]]\).
Then \(K=2/J=A/B\) has positive real part and integer Taylor coefficients.
By the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) applied to the doubled representing measure of \(K\), it has eventually periodic coefficients and is rational.
The rational series \(B\) has bounded integer coefficients from the moments of \(J\).
Its forward tail recurrence therefore has finitely many states and its coefficients are eventually periodic.
As in the [quadratic profile argument](Yin_L_G0462_v74.md#profiles), write \(B=V/U\) with coprime squarefree signed cyclotomic products \(U,V\) having constant one.
Indeed a period \(T\) gives \(U\mid1-w^T\), and the integral numerator \(V\) has no open-disk zeros since \(A\) has none and \(\Re J>0\).
Its reciprocal is monic and integral with all roots in the closed disk, so Kronecker's theorem gives its cyclotomic factorization.
At any circle zero \(\xi\) of \(V\), the lower bound \(\Re J(r\xi)\ge(1-r)/(1+r)\) and \(A(\xi)U(\xi)\ne0\) force multiplicity one.

Set \(n=\deg V-\deg U,\ \epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\).
The regular doubled density of \(K=AU/V\) on the circle is
\[
 \rho=K(w)+K(1/w)=(U/V)P,\qquad P=A+\epsilon w^{n-2}A^* .
\]
The finite Herglotz poles contribute opposite principal parts to the two summands, so \(\rho\) is a nonnegative Laurent polynomial on the circle.
It is nonzero, since at any zero of \(A\) the first summand vanishes and the reciprocal point is in the open disk where \(K\ne0\).
Every circle zero of \(\rho\) has even multiplicity.
Therefore the odd-multiplicity circle zeros of \(P\) are exactly the zeros of \(UV\).
At these roots of unity \(A/A^*=-\epsilon w^{n-2}\) is torsion, so only the preceding four factors are possible.

In the table the labels \(1,2,3,5\) stand for \(1-w,\ 1+w,\ 1+w+w^2,\ 1+w+w^2+w^3+w^4\).
A dash denotes product one, and \(x=w+w^{-1}\).
The last column gives the measure of \(K\) at each individual root of the indicated factors of \(V\), in factor order.

| \(-2\) | \(1,2\) | — | \((4-x^2)(x+1)^2\) | — |
| \(-1\) | \(1\) | — | \((2-x)(x^2+3x+3)\) | — |

The four factor degrees sum to eight, so run \(n=-8,\ldots,8\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
For each factor compute the parity of its multiplicity in \(w^{\max(0,2-n)}P\) by successive polynomial division.
Distribute each odd factor once between \(U,V\), keeping the prescribed degree difference and product of leading coefficients.
The surviving pairs are precisely the table and \(n=0,\ U=(2),\ V=(1)\), whose density is \(-(x+2)^2\).
For a check on the parity calculation, the cleared brackets at the surviving \((n,\epsilon)\), up to overall sign, are as follows.
Writing \(F_m\) for the corresponding signed factor, they are \(F_1F_2F_3^2\) at \((-2,-)\), \(F_1(w^4+3w^3+5w^2+3w+1)\) at \((-1,-)\), \(w^4+2w^3+4w^2+2w+1\) at \((0,+)\), \(F_1F_2^3\) at \((0,-)\), \(F_2(w^2+3w+1)\) at \((1,+)\), \(F_1F_3\) at \((1,-)\), \(F_1F_2\) at \((2,-)\), and \(2F_5,\ 2F_1F_2F_3\) at \((4,+),(4,-)\).
Substitution gives the densities displayed above and the masses \(-A(\xi)U(\xi)/(2\xi V'(\xi))\).
All retained densities are nonnegative on \([-2,2]\).
Subtract the indicated positive atom kernels from each retained \(K\) and harmonically extend the remaining regular density to see \(\Re K>0\) in the disk.
Thus \(J=2/K\) realizes each row with \(B=V/U\).
The atoms of \(J\) can occur only at the roots of \(U\), all in \(\{1,-1\}\), also after rotation \(w\mapsto-w\).

## Extinction of the continuous rows

Put \(\sigma=2\Re J\) off its atoms and \(D=|A|\sigma/2\).
Suppose that \(c_k(w)\), \(k\ge1\), are the moments of a common conditional circle probability at almost every regular \(w\) where \(\sigma>0\), and \(H_k=(A/2)\sigma c_k\) are integer Laurent polynomials.
Then all \(H_k\) vanish.
Indeed \(|H_k|\le D\), and \(\sigma=2\rho/|K|^2,\ M(K)=M(A)=2\) give \(M(D)=M(\rho)/2\), where \(M\) denotes the exponential of the circle mean logarithm.
Except at \(K=A,\ A(1-w)\), the table gives \(M(D)\le(3+\sqrt5)/4<\sqrt2\).
For this bound \(x+1,\ 2\pm x\) have measure one and \(M(x+3)=(3+\sqrt5)/2\) from the roots of \(w^2+3w+1\).
The [separated-block inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes) gives finitely many nonzero dominated shapes up to monomial shifts.

For \(K=A\), \(D=2\Re A/|A|\le2\), and \(\Im A(e^{it})=2\sin t(1+\cos t)\) is nonzero except at \(w=\pm1\).
Thus \(M(D)<2\) and \(\int D^2<4\) against Haar probability.
Parseval leaves at most three unit coefficients.
The [trinomial fourth-moment and alignment argument](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#extinction) excludes three, and a binomial of exponent difference \(l\) has \(l\) alignments requiring \(w=\pm1\).
For \(K=A(1-w)\), set \(Y=|A|^2=2x^2+6x+5\).
On the circle \(D=(x^2+3x+3)/|A|=(Y+1)/(2\sqrt Y)\), so \(4D^2=Y+2+1/Y\).
The mean of \(Y\) is nine by Parseval.
The first four Taylor coefficients of \(1/A\) are \(1/2,-1/2,1/4,0\), and each block of four is \(-1/4\) times the preceding block since \(A(w)(2-2w+w^2)=4+w^4\).
The mean of \(1/Y\) is consequently \((9/16)/(1-1/16)=3/5\).
This gives \(\int D^2=29/10<3\) and \(M(D)\le\sqrt{29/10}<2\).
Parseval leaves at most two unit coefficients.
Here \(D=1\) at \(w=-1\), so all sufficiently fine binomial alignment grids meet an open arc where \(D<2\).
This bounds the exponent difference and finishes the finiteness proof in every case.

For completeness, the [prime specialization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) applies to these finite shapes with the present quadratic.
Each nonzero shape \(T\) has \(M(T)<2\), so the irreducible \(A\) of measure two does not divide it.
The rational function \(T(w)A(1/w)/(T(1/w)A(w))\) has a pole at \(-1+i\), since \(A\) has no reciprocal root.
Its squaring identity fails by iteration of this exterior pole.
At sufficiently large prime-order nodes the squaring automorphism therefore excludes a prime-order value of this rational phase, simultaneously for the finite list.
Choose the prime also away from the zeros and poles of the shapes and \(\sigma\).
The moment functions \(c_k=2H_k/(A\sigma)\) give probability moments at every such node by continuity of their finite positivity tests.
Their nonzero values are nonreal, since the quotient by complex conjugation is the rational phase times a prime-order shift.
At any chosen node the two-sided moment sequence takes finitely many values because shifts have prime order.
Each level indicator is a polynomial in that sequence by interpolation, so it is an integer Fourier–Stieltjes transform, hence periodic outside a finite set.
On positive multiples of a common period the eventual moment equals the mass on the corresponding group of roots of unity by Cesàro averaging, forcing it to be zero.
The averages on the other residue classes then also vanish, so all late moments vanish.
Our choice of node implies the late \(H_k\) vanish as polynomials.
A largest nonzero harmonic would satisfy \(|c_k|\le1/2\), since the conditional probability pushed forward under the \(k\)-th power has no moments above its first.
This would give \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(D)/2<1\).
Rotation \(w\mapsto-w\) gives the result for the negative middle sign.

## The first half-integral coefficient and the short intervals

Suppose to the contrary that \(\theta-q\to0\).
Take constant sign \(s\) by extraction, set \(S(w)=A(sw)/2\) and let \(N=2h+2\).
The normalized lift is \(\Pi/2=S(z^d)-z^N S(z^{-d})\).
Its circle norm is bounded and its first half-integral coefficient is at \(2d\), so the [divisibility obstruction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) gives \(2d=o(h)\).
Write \(B^\sharp=\sum b_lz^l=(\Pi/2)J_i\), where \(J_i=\sum j_lz^l=2/K_q\) has [symmetric signed measure](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) \(\omega_i\) of mass one and negative mass \(\gamma_i=1/[2(q-1)]\to0\) at \(1\).
Below \(2d\) the normalized numerator is integral with constant one, so each \(j_l\) there is integral.
For each fixed \(L\), \(B^\sharp\) has boundedly many nonzero coefficients on \([0,Ld]\).
For bounded \(d\) this follows directly from the interval length.

To see this when \(d\to\infty\), take any positive indices \(u=o(d)\) and extract a weak limit probability \(\Omega\) of \((v^u,v^{2d})=(a,b)\) under \(\omega_i\).
Put \(T(r,k)=2\int a^rb^k\,d\Omega\).
For \(r>0\), \(T(r,0)\) is the limit of integers \(j_{ru}\).
For \(r<0\), \(j_{2d+ru}\) is likewise integral, hence \(T(r,1)\in\mathbb Z\).
At \(2d\) the term \(s j_d\) is integral and \(j_0=1\), so \(T(0,1)+1/2\in\mathbb Z\).
For \(r>0\), use the coefficient at \(2d+ru\) and remove \(s j_{d+ru}\), integral since \(d+ru<2d\) eventually.
This gives \(T(r,1)+T(r,0)/2\in\mathbb Z\).
These are exactly the [one-seam moment conditions](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#seam) with positive half-coefficient, so the [Haar marginal conclusion](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) applies.
Thus \(j_u=b_u\) eventually vanishes for every such sequence.
By diagonal extraction the positive moments through \(\lfloor c d\rfloor\) vanish eventually for some fixed \(0<c<1\).

Away from \(0,d,2d\), each \(b_l\) on \([0,Ld]\) equals \(\int v^l F(v)\,d\omega_i\), where the three successive multipliers are \(2,\ 2+2s v^{-d},\ 2+2s v^{-d}+v^{-2d}\).
On blocks of diameter at most \(cd\), the signed Gram matrix of the characters is the identity.
For 26 indices the positive-part correction has norm at most \(26\gamma_i\).
Since \(\|F\|_\infty\le5\), testing 26 nonzero integer coefficients would give \(26(1-5\gamma_i)^2\le(1+26\gamma_i)25(1+\gamma_i)\) by the [Gram bound](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#sparse), impossible eventually.
Splitting at the three exceptional indices and taking boundedly many blocks proves the support assertion.

## Period \(2d\) on the intermediate window

For any sequence \(d/t\to0,\ t/h\to0\), take a joint weak limit of \((v^d,v^t)\) under \(\omega_i\).
The first marginal has Herglotz series \(J\) with \(SJ\) integral by testing the actual coefficients of \(B^\sharp\) at fixed multiples of \(d\).
It therefore has one of the nine profiles, with the indicated rotation.
For every fixed \(k\ge1,\ l\in\mathbb Z\), all three indices \(kt+(l-j)d\), \(j=0,1,2\), are positive and inside the prefix ending before \(N-2d\).
The [integer Fourier row argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows), weighting the doubled joint measure by its second coordinate to the \(k\)-th power and multiplying the reflected projection by \(S\), gives Fourier coefficients \(\lim b_{kt+ld}\).
Its continuous densities are integer Laurent polynomials \(H_k=S\sigma c_k\) with the common conditional probability moments used above.
These rows vanish, and the remaining measure is supported on \(\{1,-1\}\).
Comparing \(l=0,2\) at \(k=1\) proves \(b_{t+2d}=b_t\) eventually by integrality and subsequence compactness.
Diagonal extraction gives the equality throughout \(Ld\le t\le h/L\) eventually for one fixed \(L\ge4\).

Take \(W=1-z^{2d}\) and truncate \(WB^\sharp\) at \(H=\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q\).
Its degree is at most \((L+2)d\) by the period, and the short-interval result bounds its term count.
Its coefficient heights are bounded by the moment representation.
The comparison \(J_*=Q/(W S(z^d))\) agrees with \(J_i\) through \(H\), and its strictly positive coefficients have a Fourier measure of bounded variation uniformly on the sequence.
Indeed the Taylor coefficients \(a_m\) of \(1/S\) satisfy \(\sum_m(1+m)|a_m|<\infty\) by analyticity up to every radius less than \(\sqrt2\).
For each monomial \(z^e\) of \(Q\), the shift \(e+md\) of the geometric series \(1/W\) requires one progression of step \(2d\) and \(O(1+(e+md)/(2d))\) singleton corrections on the positive indices, as in the [progression comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#comparison).
Summation against \(|a_m|\) gives the asserted uniform bound.
In the identity
\[
 S(z^d)W B^\sharp-Q\Pi/2=S(z^d)W(\Pi/2)(J_i-J_*),
\]
the left degree is \(O(h)\), and the right series difference vanishes through \(H\).
The polynomial factors on the right have bounded circle norms, so the [bounded interval cutoffs](Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) control the \(L^1\) norm of the left side.
Now \(Q\Pi/2\) is uniformly bounded and \(\min_{|w|=1}|S(w)|>0\), giving \(\|WB^\sharp\|_1=O(1)\).
The [integer support estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) bounds its term count, whereas \(M(WB^\sharp)=M(B^\sharp)>1\) tends to one by the [delay-one return](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This contradicts the fixed-support Mahler gap there and proves the uniform excess.
