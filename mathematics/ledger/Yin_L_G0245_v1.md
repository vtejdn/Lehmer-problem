### 2. The complete weighted coefficient bound and its rank floor

For any nonzero complex coefficient vector c and F(t)=sum_(j=0)^(N-1)c_j t^j whose values at the original roots are nonzero, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives

    (1/d)sum_i log|F(alpha_i)|
      <=(1/2)log[(1/d)sum_i w_i |F(alpha_i)|^2]
                                    -(1/2)log g(w)
      <=log||c||_2+(1/2)log Q_N(w).                  (2)

The first inequality is applied to the d positive numbers w_i|F(alpha_i)|^2.
Its omitted-looking weight correction is exactly -(1/(2d))sum_i log w_i and cannot be discarded.
For integral F with nonzero resultant, the left side is d^(-1)log|Res(P,F)| because P is monic.
Any content of F scales both its actual resultant and its coefficient norm; it supplies no free gain in(2).
Taking the infimum over weights is valid since the left side is fixed.

The matrix A_N(w) is positive semidefinite of rank exactly d, since the first d columns of E_N form an invertible Vandermonde matrix.
Write

    s_i(N)=sum_(j=0)^(N-1) r_i^(2j)>0.

Its trace and rank imply

    lambda_max(A_N(w)) >= (1/d^2)sum_i w_i s_i(N)
                      >= (1/d) g(w) (product_i s_i(N))^(1/d).

The second inequality is AM--GM over the d roots.
Consequently

    Q_N(P)>=(1/d)(product_i s_i(N))^(1/d)>=N/d.       (3)

For each i the N summands have geometric mean r_i^(N-1), so s_i(N)>=N r_i^(N-1).
Multiplying over all original roots uses exactly product_i r_i=1.
If N>=2 and P is nontorsion, some r_i differs from one, and that individual AM--GM is strict.
Hence the stronger root-dependent lower bound in(3) is strictly larger than N/d.
This is a strict bound for the infimum, not merely strictness for each particular weight.

For completeness, no advantage relevant to real integer auxiliaries is hidden by complex coefficient notation.
Conjugate roots may be given equal weights without worsening the bound: average the whole weight vector with its permutation under complex conjugation.
The new matrix is (A_N(w)+overline(A_N(w)))/2.
Its largest eigenvalue does not increase, while concavity of log makes g(w) not decrease.
The resulting matrix is real symmetric.
Its largest Rayleigh quotient is attained by a real coefficient vector and its rank is still d.
For arbitrary unsymmetrized weights the real quadratic form already equals that of Re A_N(w), so this same symmetrization preserves its numerator and can only improve its geometric-mean denominator.
Thus the same infimum is available using conjugate-symmetric weights and real quadratic forms; passing to real integral auxiliaries does not remove this full-quadratic rank floor.

In the L237 window N=2d, every such all-coefficient weighted quadratic estimate pays at least(1/2)log2, strictly more at each fixed nontorsion input.
It cannot yield an o(1) error as m tends to zero in a hypothetical sequence.
L237's guaranteed dyadic margin is log(2/sqrt3)<(1/2)log2; its rational-projection guaranteed margin is exactly(1/2)log2.
Neither guarantee alone exceeds this complete cost.
This does not say that a particular auxiliary has no stronger actual margin, that every bound on that particular vector must pay the operator norm, or that a weighted nonquadratic/signed argument is impossible.
It excludes precisely the proposed vanishing-error upgrade through a positive root weighting followed by an upper quadratic bound valid for all coefficients in the full window.

For any N the invariant here is Q_N(P), not Delta G_N.
Their different definitions and inequality directions are retained.
A test acting only on the actual constructed vector, a nonpositive signed identity, or a genuine change of window is outside this obstruction and would need its own complete norm and degree charge.

### 3. Changing the window does not separate the two trace guarantees

The retained L237 rational-projection calculation extends by direct substitution to any N>d.
Its actual remainder matrix V_N has row rank d, so its residual projection R_N has trace d.
Some coordinate j satisfies

    0<sigma_j^2=||R_N e_j||_2^2=(R_N)_(j,j)<=d/N<1. (4)

Strict positivity follows because V_N e_j is the nonzero power of a unit.
The matrix R_N is rational.
Clear a positive integer denominator q of its column, then divide the resulting coefficient polynomial by its positive content c.
Exactly as in L237, c divides q in the actual order: the value after division is(q/c)alpha^j, and multiplying by the integral unit alpha^(-j) gives q/c in O intersect Q=Z.
Set Q=q/c.
The primitive integral auxiliary has

    F(alpha_i)=Q alpha_i^j for every i,
    |Res(P,F)|=Q^d,
    S_2(F)=Q sigma_j,
    eta(F)=log Q-log S_2(F)
                   =-log sigma_j>=(1/2)log(N/d).      (5)

Here Q>=2, since a nonzero integral polynomial has S_2>=1 while sigma_j<1.
It is nonconstant: a constant value proportional to alpha^j with j>0 would make a positive power of a unit rational and hence +/-1, contrary to nontorsion; j=0 with a constant polynomial would instead have sigma_j=1.
Its full degree is at most N-1, with no claim that N is linear in d.
Every root and every prime dividing Q belongs to the one actual norm.
This is the same retained projection mechanism at a variable window, not a newly claimed auxiliary-existence theorem.

Compare the lower guarantee in(5) with the unavoidable upper-estimate cost in(3): both start at exactly(1/2)log(N/d).
Thus increasing or decreasing the window, while using only the trace-average projection guarantee and a positive root-weighted quadratic bound on the entire coefficient space, never produces a strict surplus between these guarantees.
At every fixed nontorsion input the cost floor is actually larger.
This conclusion is about those guarantees; the true eta(F) may exceed its trace lower bound, and information about the actual selected column could change the comparison.
Dyadic rounding adds its separately charged coefficient error and cannot turn the trace guarantee into an extra free margin.

The dimension/window floor is sharp if torsion is allowed.
Take P=t^d+1 with d a power of two (including d=1), and N=q d with integer q>=1.
These are actual irreducible cyclotomic inputs; the elementary shifted Eisenstein proof is already retained in L206.
Their roots form one rotated d-point grid.
With uniform weights, the columns of E_N split into d orthogonal residue classes modulo d.
Each residue class consists of q copies of one column up to signs, so A_N has exactly d nonzero eigenvalues, all equal to q.
Thus Q_N(P)=q=N/d by(3).
This sharpness control is explicitly torsion and does not assert a nontorsion near-one sequence.

### 4. Exact weight reduction on genuine radical inputs

Let p be a fixed monic irreducible nontorsion unit of degree d_0 with roots alpha_i and total logarithmic measure m_0.
Let P(t)=p(t^ell) be actually irreducible, and take N=ell N_0 with N_0>=d_0.
All ell roots in every original fiber are included; write them beta_(i,b)=gamma_i zeta^b, where gamma_i^ell=alpha_i and zeta is primitive of order ell.

A cyclic permutation b->b+1 of the weights changes A_N by conjugation with the diagonal unitary having entries1,zeta,...,zeta^(N-1), up to the immaterial inverse choice.
Its largest eigenvalue is unchanged.
Average the weights over all ell such rotations.
Convexity of the largest eigenvalue makes the new numerator no larger, while concavity of log makes their geometric mean no smaller.
Therefore the infimum defining Q_N(P) is unchanged if weights are restricted to one value w_i on each complete fiber.
This is an optimization over the full actual root set, not a selection of one root per fiber.

Order coefficient positions by j=a+ell k,0<=a<ell and0<=k<N_0.
Summing zeta^(b(a'-a)) over all b gives zero unless a=a'.
Consequently the fiber-averaged matrix is exactly the orthogonal block sum

    A_N(P;w) is permutation-conjugate to
       direct_sum_(a=0)^(ell-1)
          A_(N_0)(p; (w_i r_i^(2a/ell))_i),
    g_P(w)=g_p(w).                                    (6)

No fiber multiplicity is left outside the factor1/(d_0 ell); it cancels precisely in this displayed block formula.
Since product_i r_i=1, every block's new weight vector has geometric mean g_p(w).
Hence

    Q_(N_0)(p)<=Q_(ell N_0)(P)
                         <=exp(m_0) Q_(N_0)(p).       (7)

For the upper bound take any positive base vector u and put w_i=u_i r_i^(-(ell-1)/ell).
The weights in block a are u_i r_i^((2a-ell+1)/ell).
Because |log r_i|<=m_0 and the last exponent lies in[-1,1], each block is at most exp(m_0)A_(N_0)(p;u) as a positive semidefinite matrix, and g_p(w)=g_p(u).
Taking the infimum proves the bound without assuming existence of a base minimizer.
This is a full-weight result; arbitrary nonsymmetric weights were already included by the averaging step.

Both bounds keep the fixed positive total measure m_0.
They show that the optimized cost remains bounded independently of growing radical degree, but never improves below its base optimum.
They do not supply or refute a near-one uniform bound.
The block statement concerns a new quadratic root-weight optimization; L241's coefficient-projection blocks and L4's determinant blocks are related retained controls but not substitutes for the weighted calculation(6).

### 5. Complete optimization on one actual irreducible family

Reuse the actual integral family retained in [C214](../claims/Yin_C_G0214_v1.md#argument) and [L206's reciprocal control](Yin_L_G0206_v1.md#nontorsion-radical-attainment-controls):

    p(t)=t^2-4t+1, r=2+sqrt3, m_0=log r,
    P_ell(t)=t^(2ell)-4t^ell+1, ell=2^s, s>=0,
    d=2ell, N=ell N_0, N_0>=2.                      (8)

The irreducibility is not inferred from the analytic blocks.
The retained proof translates by one: modulo2, P_ell(t+1) is t^(2ell), and its constant coefficient is-2, so Eisenstein at two applies.
These are genuine irreducible reciprocal nontorsion units with total logarithmic measure exactly m_0 and no unit-circle roots.
The family and its fixed-measure boundary are old; the optimized root-weight value below is the changed calculation.

Let

    S=sinh(N_0 m_0)/sinh(m_0),
    tau=m_0(ell-1)/ell.

Then the complete optimization has the closed answer

    Q_(ell N_0)(P_ell)
       =[S cosh(tau)+sqrt(S^2 sinh(tau)^2+N_0^2)]/2.  (9)

To prove it, Section4 reduces every weight choice to two fiber weights without increasing cost.
Scale them so their product is one, writing w_+=u,w_-=u^(-1).
The two base evaluation rows are(1,r,...,r^(N_0-1)) and(1,r^(-1)...,r^(-(N_0-1))).
Their inner product is N_0, and their squared norms are respectively r^(N_0-1)S and r^(-(N_0-1))S.
The nonzero eigenvalues of block a are the eigenvalues of its two-by-two row Gram matrix.
Its larger eigenvalue is

    Lambda(x_a)=[S cosh(x_a)+sqrt(S^2 sinh(x_a)^2+N_0^2)]/2,
    x_a=log u+(N_0-1)m_0+2a m_0/ell.                 (10)

This follows directly from the trace and determinant of one half of the matrix with diagonal entries S exp(x_a),S exp(-x_a) and off-diagonal entries N_0.
Lambda is even and strictly increasing with |x_a|.
Minimizing max_a |x_a| therefore centers the two extreme values a=0,ell-1 around zero and gives their common magnitude tau.
The minimizing u is r^(-N_0+1/ell).
This proves both the lower bound and attained upper bound in(9), including ell=1 where tau=0.
On each actual lifted root the optimal weight can equivalently be written |beta|^(-(N-1)); this identity is proved only for this control family, not asserted for every reciprocal input.

In the universal-auxiliary window N=2d, N_0=4 and S=56: cosh m_0=2, sinh m_0=sqrt3, and sinh(4m_0)/sinh m_0=4 cosh m_0 cosh(2m_0)=56.
Hence

    Q_4(p)=30,
    30<=Q_(4ell)(P_ell)<56+2sqrt589,
    Q_(4ell)(P_ell)->56+2sqrt589 as ell->infinity.     (11)

The strict upper inequality holds for finite ell because tau<m_0 and Lambda is strictly increasing.
The limiting square root is exact: [56*2+sqrt(56^2*3+16)]/2=56+2sqrt589.
No root approximation, scan, claimed small measure or new numerical certificate is involved.
The normalized complete weighted quadratic expense is one half the logarithm of these values.
Thus arbitrary root weights, including weights differing across every original conjugate, cannot lower it below(1/2)log30 on these actual inputs, despite their unbounded degree and houses tending to one.
The total measure remains the fixed log(2+sqrt3), so this is a genuine optimization control, not a near-one counterexample.
