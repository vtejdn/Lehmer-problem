# From two prescribed residue moments to lower bounds for the entire lift

For Yin's [G0001 investigation](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract), the main agent and a delegated research agent derived the following bounds for the [logarithmic ternary cuts](Yin_L_G0464_v8.md#small-norm-terminal-block).
Use the full odd trace grid \(p=2n+1\), the prescribed negative residues at its \(k\) smallest poles, positive residues elsewhere, \(C=Dh\bmod B\), and the monic lift \(P=z^{n+1}Q(z+z^{-1})\).
Write \(M=M(P)\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure of the complete lift.
First take the profile \(x=0\), shift \(a=0\), and impose just its first two integers \(K=\sum r_j=2,\ L=\sum b_jr_j=0\), together with \(P(-1)>0\).
For \(p=243,\ k=44,\ \Delta=2^{53}\), the [endpoint and norm sieve](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#layer-valuations-and-orbits) leaves only this profile and two possible positive-unit orbits.
On the orbit of the original representative one has \(M>83/50\), and on the other one has \(M>199/100\).
Both conclusions cover every integral input in the indicated class, whenever the two moments and the endpoint hold.
For the \(x=0\) profile at \(p=729\) and \(2187\) the respective bounds \(M>7/5\) and \(M>7/6\) hold for every nonzero resultant.
The final test treats \(x=-1\) and gives \(M>6/5\) for all its strict double-moment hits at 729.
At 2187 the same profile has \(M>26/25\), with \(M>6/5\) when its third moment is at least 8.

<a id="third-moment"></a>
## The integer after the required pair

Put \(N=\sum_j b_j^2r_j\), an integer by the expansion of \(C/B\) at infinity.
For any separator \(y\) in the prescribed gap, let
\[
 W=\sum_j (2-b_j)(b_j-y)r_j=(2+y)L-2yK-N.
\]
Every summand is positive.
The [layer norm notation](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#endpoint-moment-test) gives
\[
 pW\ \ge\ \sum_\ell d_\ell (9A_\ell|F_\ell(y)|)^{1/d_\ell},
 \qquad
 pW\ \ge\ n(p^2\Delta|B(y)|)^{1/n}.
\]
Indeed \(\nu_j=(4-b_j^2)/p\), and on each ternary layer the product of \(2-b_j\) is \(3\) while the product of \(2+b_j\) is \(1\).
The whole-grid inequality uses \(\prod(2-b_j)=p,\ \prod(2+b_j)=1\) and holds also at general odd conductors.
At the angular midpoint \(c=-2\cos(2\pi k/p)\) one has \(|B(c)|\ge1\) by the [odd-grid product](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product).
In particular \(W>1/2\), since \((p/(p-1))^n<\sqrt{\mathrm e}<2\).
If \(x=0\) and the next moment also matches its [arcsine profile](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#prefix-cut), then \(N=\int t^4\,d\alpha=6\) and \(W=-4c-6\).
This requires \(c<-13/8\).
Consequently \(k/p\ge1/10\) precludes that three-moment match at \(x=0\), since then \(c\ge-2\cos(\pi/5)=-(1+\sqrt5)/2>-13/8\).
All logarithmic ternary cuts \(3^m,\ 3\le m\le9\) have \(10\lfloor p/\log p\rfloor\ge p\).
The criterion eventually ceases to apply as \(k/p\to0\).

At \(p=243\) take \(y=-33/40\), so \(W=33/10-N\).
The componentwise strict lower bounds \((157,297,139,107,102)/100\) for \((9|F_\ell(y)|)^{1/d_\ell}\) are verified by [rational powers](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#finite-exclusions).
For layer valuations \((2,2,2,1,0)\) and \((8,0,2,1,0)\), respectively, the two weighted sums give \(W>23236/24300\) and \(W>60127/24300\).
Integrality therefore gives \(N\le2\) and \(N\le0\), respectively, without imposing an endpoint.
The [quadratic-separator certificate](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0151/readme.md#quadratic-separator) independently excludes \(N=6\) using a single full resultant.

<a id="pairing"></a>
## Accounting for every exterior root

Write \(b_*\) for the smallest positive-residue pole, which need not itself be positive.
With poles in increasing order, choose one trace root in the interval from \(-2\) to the first pole and one in every same-sign gap, using the [strict endpoint matching](Yin_L_G0464_v1.md#positive-resolvent).
Pair each chosen root with the right pole of its interval.
These disjoint intervals account for \(n-1\) root-pole pairs, leaving the pole \(b_*\) and two trace roots \(v_1,v_2\), counted with multiplicity.
For any real polynomial \(f\), let \(\mathcal D(f)=\sum_{Q(q)=0} f(q)-\sum_{B(b)=0} f(b)\), summing with multiplicity.
The matched differences are bounded above by the negative variation \(V_-(f)=\int_{-2}^2\max(0,-f'(t))\,dt\).
Thus
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^2\operatorname{Re}f(v_i)\ \ge\ \mathcal D(f)+f(b_*)-V_-(f).
\]
For the two remaining reciprocal pairs choose roots \(\lambda_i\) of moduli \(\rho_i\ge1\) with \(v_i=\lambda_i+\lambda_i^{-1}\).
All matched roots lift to the unit circle, so \(M=\rho_1\rho_2\), also when the two remaining traces are real or repeated.
For \(T_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\), the upper bound on \(\sum_i\operatorname{Re}T_j(v_i)\) is \(M^j+M^{-j}+2\).
Indeed \(\cosh u+\cosh v\le1+\cosh(u+v)\) for \(u,v\ge0\).

Expansion of
\[
 \log\frac{Q(t)}{t B(t)}
   =\frac{2}{t^2}+\frac{N-2}{t^4}+O(t^{-5})
\]
gives \(\mathcal D(t)=\mathcal D(t^3)=0,\ \mathcal D(t^2)=-4,\ \mathcal D(t^4)=8-4N\).
Since \(\mathcal D(1)=1\), this gives \(\mathcal D(T_4)=26-4N\).
The negative variation of \(T_4(t)=t^4-4t^2+2\) on \([-2,2]\) equals \(8\).
It follows that
\[
                       M^4+M^{-4}\ \ge\ 16-4N+T_4(b_*).
\]
At \(p=243\), the [integer separator check](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0151/shell.py) gives \(b_*>-41/50\), and \(b_*<0\) since its decreasing-grid index is \(77>p/4\).
On \((-\sqrt2,0)\) the function \(T_4\) increases, with \(T_4(-41/50)=-1484239/6250000\).
Substituting \(N\le2\) gives a right side strictly greater than \((83/50)^4+(50/83)^4\).
Substituting \(N\le0\) gives a right side strictly greater than \((199/100)^4+(100/199)^4\).
Monotonicity on \(M\ge1\) proves both claimed bounds.

<a id="cubic"></a>
## Two larger conductors without a norm assumption

The cubic \(f(t)=t^3-2t^2\) is nonpositive on \([-2,2]\), has negative variation \(32/27\), and satisfies \(\mathcal D(f)=8\).
The pairing bound therefore gives \(\sum_i\operatorname{Re} f(v_i)\ge184/27+f(b_*)\).
Put \(w=M+M^{-1}-2\) and use \(f=T_3-2T_2+3T_1-4\).
If \(\lambda_i=\rho_i\exp(\mathrm i\theta_i)\), the difference between \(\operatorname{Re}T_j(v_i)\) and \(T_j(2\cos\theta_i)\) is bounded in absolute value by \(\rho_i^j+\rho_i^{-j}-2\).
The sum of these bounds for the two indices is at most \(M^j+M^{-j}-2\) by the same hyperbolic inequality.
Since \(f(2\cos\theta_i)\le0\), it follows that
\[
                      20w+8w^2+w^3\ \ge\ 184/27+f(b_*).
\]
At the logarithmic cuts for \(729\) and \(2187\), the [exact rational separators](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#finite-exclusions) give \(b_*>-7/6\) and \(b_*>-37/27\), respectively, with \(b_*<0\) by the listed pole indices.
The cubic increases for negative arguments.
Putting \(M=7/5\) and \(M=7/6\) on the left gives values strictly below \(184/27+f(-7/6)\) and \(184/27+f(-37/27)\), respectively.
This excludes all smaller whole measures for the stated \(x=0\) profiles.

<a id="negative-one"></a>
## The remaining profile at 729

For \(x=-1\) take \(a=-2,\ K=3,\ L=4\), again with strict endpoint.
In [the endpoint notation](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#endpoint-moment-test), \(U=\sum r_j/(2+b_j)\ge1\).
The positive sum
\[
 E=\sum (b_j-y)r_j\frac{(2-b_j)^2}{2+b_j}
   =N-yL-6(L-yK)+16\{K-(2+y)U\}
   \le N-8-2y
\]
uses \((2-b)^2/(2+b)=b-6+16/(2+b)\).
The whole-grid product gives \(pE\ge n(p^3\Delta |B(y)|)^{1/n}\).
At the [rational separators](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#finite-exclusions) \(p|B(y)|>2\), hence \(E>1/2\).
At \(p=729,\ y=-7/6\) this forces \(N\ge7\).
At \(p=2187,\ y=-37/27\) it gives \(N\ge6\).
The upper inequality \(W>1/2\) from the first section also gives \(N\le10\) at 2187.

For these moments the expansion of \(\log(Q/(tB))\) gives \(\mathcal D(t)=-2,\ \mathcal D(t^2)=-2,\ \mathcal D(t^3)=-2,\ \mathcal D(t^4)=18-4N\).
Take \(g_u(t)=-(t+u)^2(t-2)^2\), which is nonpositive on the matched interval.
For \(u=1/2\) or \(1\) it decreases only from \(-u\) to \((2-u)/2\) there, with negative variation \((2+u)^4/16\).
In both applications \(y<b_*<-1\) by the [pole indices](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#finite-exclusions), since the index of \(b_*\) exceeds \(p/3\).
Thus \(g_u(b_*)>g_u(y)\).
Write \(E_j=M^j+M^{-j}-2\), so the comparison with the projections \(2\cos\theta_i\) above bounds the total exterior increase for \(T_j\) by \(E_j\).

At 729 take \(u=1/2\).
One has \(\mathcal D(g_{1/2})=19/2+4(N-7)\), and the nonconstant Chebyshev coefficients in decreasing order are \((-1,3,-17/4,6)\).
Consequently
\[
 E_4+3E_3+(17/4)E_2+6E_1
    >19/2-625/256+g_{1/2}(-7/6)=53951/20736 .
\]
At \(M=6/5\) the left side is \(455089/202500\), strictly smaller.
This proves \(M>6/5\) at 729 for the entire \(x=-1\) profile.
Together with the \(x=0\) bound and the [three other exclusions](Yin_L_G0464_v11.md#finite-exclusions), it rules out the strict shell \(1<M\le6/5\) there under the first two moments, at every nonzero resultant.

At 2187 take \(u=1\) and suppose \(N\ge8\), which includes the [matching next moment](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#prefix-cut) for \(x=-1\).
Here \(\mathcal D(g_1)=4N-24\ge8\), and the nonconstant coefficients are \((-1,2,-1,2)\).
The pairing gives
\[
 E_4+2E_3+E_2+2E_1
   >47/16+g_1(-37/27)=11728127/8503056 .
\]
The left side at \(M=6/5\) is \(1110001/810000\), again strictly smaller.
At 2187 Cauchy-Schwarz eliminates \(N=6\) using only the two original moments and the endpoint.
Write \(\mu_j=(b_j-y)r_j>0\).
Since \(2+b_j\) varies on its support,
\[
 (4-3y)^2=\left(\sum\mu_j\right)^2
 <\left(\sum\mu_j(2+b_j)\right)\left(\sum\frac{\mu_j}{2+b_j}\right)
 \le(N+8-10y)(1-y).
\]
The last upper bound uses \(U\ge1\) and \(\sum\mu_j/(2+b_j)=3-(2+y)U\).
For \(N=6\) the right side minus the left side is \(y^2-2<0\) at \(y=-37/27\).
Thus \(N\ge7\).

If \(N=7\), take
\[
 f(t)=-t^4+\frac52t^3+\frac78t^2-\frac{147}{32}t.
\]
Its derivative has roots \(-3/4,7/8,7/4\), its maximum on \([-2,2]\) is \(657/256\), and its negative variation is \(21197/4096\), by evaluating at these roots and the two endpoints.
The trace difference is \(199/16\), and \(f(b_*)>f(-37/27)\) by increase to the left of \(-3/4\).
Subtracting twice its maximum from the pairing bound and comparing to the unit-circle projections yields
\[
 E_4+(5/2)E_3+(25/8)E_2+(93/32)E_1
   >199/16+f(-37/27)-21197/4096-2(657/256)
    =234637667/2176782336.
\]
The coefficients of the \(E_j\) are the absolute values of the nonconstant Chebyshev coefficients of \(f\).
At \(M=26/25\) the left side is \(59280474179/714025000000\), strictly smaller.
This gives \(M>26/25\) also in the last integral case at 2187.
The interval arguments use complete lifts at the stated conductors and give no uniform gap along growing conductors.
