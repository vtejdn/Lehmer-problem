<a id="single-factor-failure"></a>
## A simple profile root need not make the whole output neutral

The [double-factor argument](Yin_L_G0462_v11.md#double-factor-obstruction) excludes a retained factor \((t-x)^2\) in the strict odd-grid chamber.
An attempted strengthening to a single factor \((t-x)\) fails.
The root \(x\) can be the mandatory real root chosen in one of the same-sign pole gaps.
Removing that copy leaves a residual quadratic which can still have a nonreal conjugate pair.
The positive signs of that residual quadratic at the poles therefore do not force it to contain \(x\).
The following exact original-input example realizes this possibility.

<a id="simple-root-atom"></a>
## An actual integer atom with a simple profile root

Use the [full odd-grid conventions](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#original-domain), and set
\[
 p=9,\qquad n=4,\qquad x=0,\qquad \kappa=1,
 \qquad h=2E_0+2E_3=2+2t^3-4t.
 \tag{1}
\]
The coordinates are \((a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)=(2,0,0,2)\).
The grid is \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/9)\), with
\[
                    b_1>1>b_2>0>b_3=-1>b_4>-2.
\]
Its polynomial factors as
\[
 B_4=t^4+t^3-3t^2-2t+1=(t+1)(t^3-3t+1).
\]
For \(j=1,2,4\), the cubic identity gives \(h(b_j)=2b_j\), while \(h(-1)=4\).
Thus all four original residues have the strict signs \(+,+,+,-\).
This checks the entire single-cut domain at these parameters without rounding a real proposal or assuming a square class.

The exact remainder identity from C881v3 gives
\[
 C=2B_3+2B_0=2t^3+2t^2-4t,
 \qquad Q=tB_4+C=t^5+t^4-t^3-3t=t\,g(t),
\]
where
\[
                         g(t)=t^4+t^3-t^2-3.
 \tag{2}
\]
Since \(B_4(-2)=1\) and \(Q(-2)=-2\), the strict endpoint is satisfied and \(P(-1)=2\).
Also \(Q(0)=0\), but \(Q'(0)=-3\), so the profile root is simple.
The coordinates \(a_0=2,a_1=0\) give agreement through coefficient index 3 with
\[
                         P_{*,0}(z)=\frac{(1+z^2)^2}{(1-z)^2(1+z)}.
\]
Agreement through index 4 fails, since it would require \(a_2=4\), whereas (1) has \(a_2=0\).
This is a length-3 example, not evidence for a long exact prefix.

The entire reciprocal lift is
\[
\begin{split}
 P(z)={}&z^{10}+z^9+4z^8+4z^7+4z^6+6z^5\\
       &+4z^4+4z^3+4z^2+z+1.
\end{split}
 \tag{3}
\]
Every factor and its original multiplicity remain in (3).

<a id="nonreal-pair"></a>
## Exact positivity of the complete measure

The derivative of the quartic in (2) is \(g'(t)=t(4t^2+3t-2)\).
Its three critical points are zero and \(t_{\pm}=(-3\pm\sqrt{41})/8\).
At zero its value is \(-3\).
At either other critical point, reduction by \(4t^2+3t-2=0\) gives
\[
                    g(t_{\pm})=(41t_{\pm}-214)/64<0,
\]
because both critical points are less than one.
The quartic tends to positive infinity at both ends and all three critical values are strictly negative.
It therefore has exactly two simple real roots and a nonreal conjugate pair.

For the three cubic poles, \(g(b_j)=2(b_j+2)(b_j-1)\).
Hence \(g(-2)=1>0>g(b_4)\), while \(g(b_2)<0<g(b_1)\).
The two real quartic roots lie respectively in \((-2,b_4)\) and \((b_2,b_1)\).
The remaining mandatory root of \(Q\) is exactly zero, lying in \((b_3,b_2)\).
After these three distinct real roots are removed, the residual quadratic is the nonreal pair just proved.
This accounts for all five trace roots, with no multiplicity discarded.

A nonreal trace root \(v\) cannot have a unit-circle lift, since \(|z|=1\) implies \(z+z^{-1}\in\mathbb R\).
The roots of \(z^2-vz+1\) have product one, so one has modulus strictly greater than one.
Therefore the complete integer polynomial (3) satisfies \(M(P)>1\).
No decimal Mahler estimate is needed for this positivity conclusion.

An explicit finite discrete law fixes \(a_0=2,a_1=0\) and samples \((a_2,a_3)\) uniformly from \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^2\).
All 25 labels give actual integer polynomials through the original map.
The label \((0,2)\), of mass \(1/25\), has the strict signs, endpoint and positive complete measure proved above.
This supplies an atom at one fixed length and degree, not a positive probability in an arbitrarily prescribed small shell.

<a id="central-deformation"></a>
## The example is a central deformation of a neutral block

In [v11's neutral family](Yin_L_G0462_v11.md#neutral-integer-supply), take \(r=4,s=5,x=0\).
Direct cancellation gives
\[
 P^{(0)}=(1+z^2)^3(1+z+z^2+z^3+z^4),
 \qquad Q^{(0)}=t^3(t^2+t-1).
\]
The example is therefore the exact central change
\[
                        Q=Q^{(0)}-3t,
 \qquad P=P^{(0)}-3z^4-3z^6.
 \tag{4}
\]
It lowers the profile-root multiplicity from three to one, preserves the length-3 prefix, and supplies the nonreal pair.
Thus requiring \(Q(x)\ne0\) as a universal positivity filter would discard legitimate original inputs.

A fixed perturbing polynomial is not yet an all-conductor mechanism.
The neutral pole formula and the derivative of \(B_n\) give
\[
 |Q^{(0)}(b_j)|\le
 \frac{4(b_j-x)^2}{(2-b_j)\sqrt{2+b_j}}.
 \tag{5}
\]
For \(x\in\{-1,0,1\}\), this is \(O((b_j-x)^2)\) in a fixed neighborhood of \(x\), uniformly in the neutral block lengths.
If a fixed nonzero \(R\in\mathbb Z[t]\) has order zero or one at \(x\), then \(Q^{(0)}+R\) has the same sign at two consecutive grid poles immediately to the right of \(x\) for all sufficiently large conductors.
Indeed, \(R\) has a fixed leading local sign and dominates the quadratic bound (5) on that side.
If the cut separator lies below both poles, their positive residues require opposite signs of \(Q\), because the derivatives of the monic grid polynomial alternate in sign.
Thus this fixed perturbation eventually leaves that chamber.
If \(R\) instead has order at least two at \(x\), both summands retain \((t-x)^2\), and the existing double-factor obstruction applies to every admissible output.
This comparison concerns fixed \(R\), an interior profile point and the stated cut position; it does not exclude growing perturbation degree or height.

<a id="first-test"></a>
## A concrete next question, left unperformed

The surviving question is whether central perturbations of growing degree can preserve a long prefix while using the simple-root freedom in (4).
One symbolic first test fixes \(x=0\), chooses even \(r\) and odd \(s\) with \(r+s=2n+1\), and sets
\[
 Q=Q^{(0)}_{r,s,0}-3tB_{n-L-1},
 \qquad 3\le L<\min(r,s),\qquad L\le n-1.
 \tag{6}
\]
The perturbation has degree \(n-L\), so its reciprocal lift starts at coefficient index \(L+1\) and exactly preserves the profile prefix through \(L\).
At \(n=4,L=3,r=4,s=5\), it is precisely (4).
The original residue-coordinate perturbation is \(-3tE_L\) modulo \(B_n\), since \(DE_L\equiv B_{n-L-1}\pmod{B_n}\).
When also \(r=2m\le n-1\), the unperturbed residue representative is \(t^2E_{m-1}^2\), and the exact pole values become
\[
                    h(b_j)=b_j^2E_{m-1}(b_j)^2-3b_jE_L(b_j).
 \tag{7}
\]
This last convenient restriction on \(r\) does not hold at conductor 9; that example uses the separate direct calculation above.

The first discriminating task would decide symbolically whether (6), or a justified modification of its perturbation, admits unbounded \(L\) with one strict terminal negative block, the strict endpoint and a nonreal residual pair.
It should first settle the pole-sign pattern in (7), before a new coefficient search or Mahler computation.
A positive all-length answer would combine with the [whole-measure prefix bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#statement) to yield the original small-shell conclusion.
The present example gives no reason to assume that answer: oscillatory zeros can create extra sign transitions, and the endpoint may fail independently.
An exclusion would concern this concrete integer deformation and leave the full affine integer count unresolved.
No growing-length test was performed in this bounded exploration.

The actual return is a correction to the proposed single-factor rigidity and a finite integer construction identifying one surviving freedom.
No mechanism crossing the all-length integer-count gap has been established in the inspected scope.
