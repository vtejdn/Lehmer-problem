# The three lower exponents of an escaping eight-term lift cannot lie on an affine line in the limiting lattice

<a id="eight"></a>
## A restriction on the two gaps between lower exponents

Consider actual positive-constant Pisot trace lifts on branch \(j=2\), with exactly eight terms and lower exponents \(0<d_1<d_2<d_3\le h\).
Along every sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\),
\[
      \frac{d_3-d_1}{\gcd(d_2-d_1,d_3-d_1)}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
In particular, for each fixed bound on this ratio there is a positive lower bound for \(\theta-q\) and a finite upper bound for \(q\), both uniform over the three exponents and their coefficients.
In the [rank-two limiting lattice](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight), the primitive generator \(\rho\) of the persistent relations between the three lower exponents must satisfy \(\rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_3\ne0\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## A sharp norm bound for integer Laurent polynomials

Let \(H=\sum_{s=1}^r a_s X^{v_s}\) have distinct exponents in a finite-rank free abelian group and nonzero integer coefficients, with \(r\ge3\).
Then \(\|H\|_\infty\ge\sqrt5\) on the dual torus.
To prove this, specialize along an integer linear functional taking distinct values on the finitely many \(v_s\), available by avoiding the finitely many hyperplanes defined by their differences.
The resulting circle lies in the torus, so it suffices to treat one variable.
Use normalized Haar measure throughout.
Parseval gives the bound if \(\sum a_s^2\ge5\), leaving exactly three or four coefficients, all signs.

For three terms, translate the exponents to \(0,u,v\) with \(0<u<v\).
If \(v\ne2u\), the three positive differences \(u,v,v-u\) are distinct.
Thus \(|H|^2\) has constant coefficient \(3\) and six further coefficients of modulus one, giving
\[
             15=\int |H|^4\le \|H\|_\infty^2\int |H|^2
                =3\|H\|_\infty^2 .
\]
If \(v=2u\), put \(w=z^u\).
When the two end signs agree, choose \(w=1\) or \(-1\) to align all three terms.
When the end signs differ, the modulus at \(w=i\) is \(\sqrt5\).
The polynomial \(1+w-w^2\) attains the bound, since its squared modulus is \(1+4\sin^2 t\) at \(w=e^{it}\).

For four signs let \(c_k=[z^k]|H|^2\) for \(k>0\), interpreting \(|H|^2\) on the circle as a Laurent polynomial.
The largest difference occurs once and its coefficient is odd.
The sum of the \(c_k\) is even, being the sum of six signed products, one from each unordered pair.
At least two \(c_k\) are therefore odd.
It follows that \(\int |H|^4=16+2\sum_{k>0}c_k^2\ge20\), while \(\int|H|^2=4\).
This proves the same bound for four terms.

<a id="common-phase"></a>
## Exponents lying in an affine hyperplane away from the origin

Suppose an integer linear functional \(\ell\) takes the same nonzero value \(b\) on every \(v_s\).
This holds whenever their rational affine span omits the origin, by solving the equations \(\ell(v_s)=1\) over the rationals and clearing denominators.
For \(A=2+H\), write \(m(A)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure on its torus.
At each \(u\) on the unit circle translation by the character \(v\mapsto u^{\ell(v)}\) preserves Haar measure and rotates \(H\) by \(u^b\).
Averaging over \(u\) and applying Jensen's formula gives
\[
                   m(A)=\int\log\max(2,|H(X)|)\,dX>\log2 .
\]
The inequality is strict because \(|H|>2\) on a nonempty open set by the norm bound.

<a id="relation"></a>
## A bounded relation with a nonzero coefficient sum or a degree term

For every fixed \(r\ge3\) there is a finite \(B_r\) such that every actual branch-two trace lift with \(r\) positive lower exponents \(d_s\) and degree \(N=2h+2\) has an integer relation
\[
          \sum_{s=1}^r k_s d_s+k_0N=0,\qquad
          \max_{0\le s\le r}|k_s|\le B_r,\qquad
          (k_0,\ \sum_{s=1}^r k_s)\ne(0,0).
\]
This strengthens the [bounded-relation sieve](Yin_L_G0462_v92.md#relation) at \(j=2\).
If the assertion failed, take inputs on which no such relation has size at most \(i\), as \(i\to\infty\).
The [trace root locations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) give \(m(\Pi_i)=\log2\), and the [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) lets us fix the lower coefficient tuple \((a_1,\ldots,a_r)\) by extraction.
Extract again so that every fixed integer relation on \((d_1,\ldots,d_r,N)\) either holds eventually or fails eventually.
The group \(R\) of eventual relations is saturated and each member has \(k_0=0\) and \(\sum k_s=0\).
Write \(R_0\) for its lower-coordinate subgroup and \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z^r/R_0\), so the full quotient is \(\Gamma\oplus\mathbb Z\).
The images \(v_s\) of the coordinate vectors are distinct because the \(d_s\) are distinct on each input.
The coordinate-sum functional descends to \(\Gamma\) and takes value one on every \(v_s\).

In a basis of this free quotient, the shortest nonzero relation of the specialization vectors tends to infinity.
Indeed a finite basis of \(R\) vanishes on every sufficiently late input, and any bounded sequence of nonzero relations in the quotient would yield a fixed one on a subsequence, contrary to the extraction.
For \(A=2+\sum a_sX^{v_s}\), the polynomial \(A(X)-tA(X^{-1})\) specializes to the actual lifts.
[Lawton's specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) therefore gives \(m(A(X)-tA(X^{-1}))=\log2\).
The two coefficients in \(t\) have equal modulus on the torus, so Jensen in \(t\) gives \(m(A)=\log2\), contradicting the strict bound above.

Whenever \(d_r=o(N)\), the degree coefficient in every relation of this bounded size must eventually be zero.
Thus a bounded lower relation of nonzero coefficient sum is necessary in that regime.
For eight terms the [three-offset theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#three-offsets) gives \(d_3=o(N)\) on escape, and the persistent lower relation group after extraction has rank one.
Its primitive generator must have nonzero sum since it divides a bounded relation of nonzero sum fixed by extraction.
The three images \(v_s\) lie on an affine line precisely when they admit a nontrivial relation with coefficient sum zero, so this excludes all collinear patterns.
Finally, if the displayed ratio of exponent gaps stayed bounded on a subsequence, write \(d_2-d_1=mg,\ d_3-d_1=ng\) with \(g\) their gcd and fix \(0<m<n\) by extraction.
Then \((m-n,n,-m)\) is a persistent nonzero relation with sum zero, a contradiction.
The uniform positive excess for each bounded ratio follows by contradiction, and the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives the corresponding bound on \(q\).

<a id="trapezoid"></a>
## A remaining pattern with offsets \(d,e,e+2d\)

The polynomial \(A(x,y)=2+y+x(1-y^2)\) meets the necessary equality \(m(A)=\log2\).
Indeed on \(|y|=1\),
\[
           |2+y|^2-|1-y^2|^2=(1+2\Re y)^2\ge0 .
\]
Jensen first in \(x\) gives \(m(A)=m(2+y)=\log2\).
The torus zeros have \(y\) a primitive cube root and \(x=-1\), since at either such \(y\) one has \(1-y^2=2+y\ne0\).
Throughout the torus, \(|1-y^3|^2=|1-y|^2(1+2\Re y)^2\le20|A(x,y)|\) by the displayed identity, \(|2+y|+|1-y^2|\le5\) and the reverse triangle inequality.
Specializing \(y=z^d,\ x=z^e\) for \(0<d<e\) gives the lower exponents \(d,e,e+2d\).
The last exceeds the sum of the first two and the three corresponding exponent vectors in the two-variable lattice are noncollinear.
Realization of this rank-two pattern by actual Pisot trace lifts on an escaping sequence remains open here.
