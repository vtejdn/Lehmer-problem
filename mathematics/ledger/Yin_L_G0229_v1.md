### 2. Actual angular moments at all frequencies up to T

Retain the explicit L209 quantities

\[
 c=\log2/4,\quad h_0=c e^{-20},\quad
 b(u)=\max\{u,\sqrt{u/c}\log\sqrt{c/u}\}\quad(0<u\le h_0).
\]

They are increasing on this interval.
For 0<h<=h_0 define

\[
 \varepsilon(h)=\min\{1,15\sqrt{b(h)}+4h\}.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

Then uniformly for every integer1<=n<=T,

\[
 \left|\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d z_i^n\right|
                         \le\varepsilon(h).            \tag{4}
\]

Here is the actual-degree argument and compact test.
Put beta=alpha^n, N=[Q(beta):Q], u=h_abs(beta)=nm/d.
Each distinct conjugate beta_j occurs exactly d/N times among the alpha_i^n.
Thus the angular average in(4) equals the average of beta_j/|beta_j| over the actual N conjugates.
The retained house-to-height step gives N>=sqrt(c/u); at u<=h_0 it exceeds55 and

\[
 \max\{\log N/N,u\}\le b(u)\le b(h).                \tag{5}
\]

No degree preservation or common splitting-field degree replaces N.

For a real angle theta define q(z)=z/max(1,|z|) and let chi(z) equal1 for |z|<=1, equal2-|z| for1<=|z|<=2, and vanish outside the radius-two disk.
The map q has modulus at most1 and global Lipschitz constant at most2: the difference of its numerators contributes at most |z-w|, and that of the denominator contributes at most another |z-w|.
The cutoff has Lipschitz constant1.
Hence

\[
 \varphi_\theta(z)=\chi(z)\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}q(z))
\]

is real, globally3-Lipschitz and supported in |z|<=2.
On the unit circle it is cos(arg z-theta), whose normalized circle average is zero.
Pri11 therefore bounds its average over the actual beta conjugates in absolute value by15sqrt(b(u)).

For every nonzero z, with r=|z|, its difference from cos(arg z-theta) is at most2|log r|.
For r<=1 use1-r<=-log r.
For1<=r<=2 use r-1<=2log r, and for r>=2 use1<=2log r.
As beta is a unit, the average of |log|beta_j|| is2u.
Thus the angular cosine average has absolute value at most15sqrt(b(u))+4u.
This estimate is uniform in theta; choose theta to be the argument of the complex angular average to bound its modulus.
The trivial modulus bound1 and monotonicity give(3)--(4).
In particular

\[
 \varepsilon(h)=O\bigl(h^{1/4}(1+\log(1/h))^{1/2}\bigr)
                         \longrightarrow0.             \tag{6}
\]

The same bound holds for negative n by conjugation.
This is a uniform finite-frequency statement with n up to T and h=Tm/d, not an interchange of an infinite Fourier series and an input limit.

### 3. The complete joint coefficient-energy cost

For every auxiliary in Section1 with0<h<=h_0,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,F)|}{d}
 \le\log S_2+h+
 \frac12\log\bigl[1+(\rho-1)\varepsilon(h)+4\rho h\bigr].
 \quad}                                                   \tag{7}
\]

The coefficient heights may vary arbitrarily; their scale is retained exactly in S_2, while rho is scale invariant.
In particular rho may be replaced by k for a weaker bound.

First, the joint angular second moment has diagonal terms S_2^2.
All off-diagonal frequencies a_j-a_l are nonzero and have absolute value at most T.
Equation(4) gives

\[
 \frac1d\sum_i|F(z_i)|^2
 \le S_2^2+\varepsilon(h)(S_1^2-S_2^2).              \tag{8}
\]

No independence of the phases or of their different powers is asserted.
This is the complete Gram expansion over the original conjugates, with every off-diagonal term bounded by an actual powered-field estimate.

It remains to justify using the actual F(alpha_i), including all expanding and contracting roots.
Put

\[
 B_i=F(\alpha_i)/\max(1,r_i)^T.
\]

For r_i<=1 the coefficient of c_j z_i^(a_j) in B_i is r_i^(a_j); for r_i>=1 it is r_i^(a_j-T).
Both lie in[0,1], and their difference from1 is at most min{1,T|log r_i|}.
Therefore

\[
 |B_i-F(z_i)|\le S_1\min\{1,T|\log r_i|\},\qquad
 |B_i|,|F(z_i)|\le S_1.
\]

Taking squared-modulus differences and averaging with(1) yields

\[
 \frac1d\sum_i|B_i|^2
 \le\frac1d\sum_i|F(z_i)|^2+4S_1^2Tm/d
 \le S_2^2[1+(\rho-1)\varepsilon(h)+4\rho h].        \tag{9}
\]

The full radial expense is still exactly present:

\[
 \frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,F)|}{d}
       =h+\frac1d\sum_i\log|B_i|.
\]

All B_i are nonzero by hypothesis.
The concavity of log gives the upper bound (1/2)log(d^(-1)sum_i|B_i|^2) on the latter average.
Combining with(9) proves(7).
No leading coefficient, primitive content, root multiplicity, complementary factor or radial correction has been deleted.

For reference let

\[
 E_\rho(h)=h+\tfrac12\log[1+(\rho-1)\varepsilon(h)+4\rho h].
                                                               \tag{10}
\]

This is increasing in h and in rho>=1 on the stated interval.
If h->0 and rho epsilon(h)->0, then E_rho(h)->0; the same condition controls rho h because epsilon(h)>=h on this small interval.
Thus(7) allows growing effective support, not only a fixed number of terms.
For example, for T<=C d, any fixed0<delta<1/4 and rho<=m^(-1/4+delta) make the error tend to zero as m->0.
This example keeps C fixed and uses(6); it is not a claim that any desired auxiliary exists in that range.

### 4. Congruence-only capture and the exact net-income gate

The estimate applies directly to an auxiliary defined by integral congruences, without requiring any literal multiple of a cyclotomic polynomial.
For each rational prime p in a finite set, use a p-adic splitting field merely to label the same d original roots, with v_p(p)=1.
All F(alpha_i) are integral there.
Suppose an actual certificate provides nonnegative real lower bounds s_(p,i) on their valuations, possibly zero on unselected roots.
Since the resultant is a nonzero rational integer,

\[
 A:=\sum_p(\log p)\sum_{i=1}^d s_{p,i}
 \le\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,F)|,
 \qquad
 \frac A d-\log S_2\le E_\rho(Tm/d).                 \tag{11}
\]

Indeed v_p(Res(P,F)) is exactly the sum of the valuations over the d original roots.
Nonnegative valuations at every unselected root and prime justify discarding only those terms from this lower bound.
Several primes can be added here because they concern the same nonzero integer Res(P,F).
No independence of primes, common cyclic exponent, unramifiedness or power-basis index hypothesis is used.

Here is one precise way to obtain those bounds.
If H_p is an integral polynomial and s_p is a positive integer, an actual congruence

\[
 F-H_p=P U_p+p^{s_p}V_p,\qquad U_p,V_p\in\mathbb Z[t],
                                                               \tag{12}
\]

implies at each original root

\[
 v_p(F(\alpha_i))
 \ge\min\{v_p(H_p(\alpha_i)),s_p\}.                 \tag{13}
\]

This follows from the ultrametric inequality and integrality of V_p(alpha_i).
If H_p(alpha_i)=0, use v_p(0)=+infinity in the minimum; F(alpha_i) must still be nonzero separately.
Thus L221's actual H, whose selected-root valuations are at least two, supplies the same selected floor under a congruence (12) with s_p>=2.
A modulus with smaller s_p retains only that smaller floor.
Exceptional depth above the modulus is not automatically preserved.
Coefficientwise congruence is the special case U_p=0; working in the actual quotient Z[t]/(P) is sufficient.
No H_p|F assertion is involved.

For example, fix C>0, K>=1 and eta>0.
Suppose a class of actual inputs admits nonvanishing integral auxiliaries satisfying T<=Cd, rho<=K and a certificate in (11) with

\[
                 A/d-\log S_2\ge\eta.                \tag{14}
\]

Choose h_* in (0,h_0] so small that E_K(h_*)<eta.
If an input in that class had m<=h_*/C, then (11) and monotonicity would contradict (14).
Hence every such input has m>h_*/C.
This is a fully charged sufficient gap criterion for the covered class, not a claim that it covers all near-one inputs.
Neither integrality, residue-order coverage nor the existence of H_p proves the required coefficient-energy surplus.
With varying rho and h->0, rho epsilon(h)->0, (11) instead gives the necessary condition A/d-log S_2<=o(1).

As a transparent numerical threshold, suppose F has k nonzero coefficients, all plus or minus one, T<=Cd, and v_2(F(alpha_i))>=2 for all d original roots.
Then S_2^2=k, rho=k and A/d>=log4.
For any fixed k<=15, the margin log4-(1/2)log k is positive, so (14) excludes an arbitrarily small total m in such a covered class.
At k=16 this argument has zero margin and yields no positive gap by itself.
This threshold concerns the new actual-resultant bound; it neither constructs these auxiliaries nor imports the L225 literal-multiple hypothesis.
More generally a selected mass fraction Gamma at prime p and depth s has margin s Gamma log p-(1/2)log k.
A positive lower bound on that margin is sufficient under the degree and bounded-rho hypotheses of (14), or rules out a proposed near-one sequence when the stated growing-rho error tends to zero.

Scalar normalization is exact and cannot improve the net income for free.
If F=c F_0 with positive integer coefficient content c, then

\[
 \operatorname{Res}(P,F)=c^d\operatorname{Res}(P,F_0),
 \quad S_2(F)=cS_2(F_0),\quad\rho(F)=\rho(F_0).
                                                               \tag{15}
\]

Thus the exact quantity d^(-1)log|Res(P,F)|-log S_2(F) is invariant under removing content.
In a lower-bound certificate, the valuations of F_0 are those of F minus v_p(c); a safe retained root floor is max{0,s_(p,i)-v_p(c)}.
Keeping the old floor after division would be invalid.
Similarly every nonconstant factor of F remains charged in (7); no bound for a quotient is obtained by silently keeping the full resultant income.
These are the explicit normalization boundaries reused from L193/L194, now written for (11).

### 5. Exact nonmultiple controls and a universal no-surplus certificate

The first control is the already retained irreducible reciprocal family in [L193](Yin_L_G0193_v1.md#exact-resultant-content-collapse).
For each odd prime q>=5 put

\[
 P_q(t)=t^{2q}-3t^q+1,\quad d=2q,\quad
 \beta=(3+\sqrt5)/2,\quad m(P_q)=\log\beta.           \tag{16}
\]

L193 proves irreducibility at this exact domain, with all q-th roots of beta and beta^(-1) as the d original conjugates.
Its full total logarithmic measure is fixed positive.
The new evaluations below reuse this family, not a newly claimed construction or a near-one family.
Write x=t^q and set

\[
 F_q=1+x-x^2,\qquad Q_q=x^2-7x+1,\qquad H_q=x^4+x^2+1.
                                                               \tag{17}
\]

Here H_q is exactly L221's auxiliary for all the original root mass at p=2.
To see this without a residue-degree shortcut, the reduction of P_q is Phi_3(t^q), with derivative t^(q-1), so it is squarefree over F_2.
Since q is an odd prime other than three, the only occurring root orders are 3 and 3q, and Phi_3(t^q)=Phi_3(t)Phi_(3q)(t).
Each E_o is one and each L221 lift exponent is r_o=1.
Therefore its full product is Phi_3(t^2)Phi_(3q)(t^2)=Phi_3(t^(2q))=H_q.
Its degree is 4q=2d.
Both F_q and Q_q have actual degree d, are primitive, and hence are not polynomial multiples of this H_q.

At a root of P_q, its value x satisfies x^2-3x+1=0.
Exact integral identities give

\[
 \begin{split}
 F_q&=-P_q+2(1-x),\\
 Q_q&=P_q-4x,\\
 H_q&=P_q(x^2+3x+1)+8x^2.
 \end{split}                                               \tag{18}
\]

All x are p-adic units.
At p=2 their residues have order three, so 1-x is also a unit.
Consequently every original root has valuation respectively 1, 2 and 3 for F_q, Q_q and H_q.
In particular F_q-H_q belongs to (P_q,2), and Q_q-H_q belongs to (P_q,4); the latter need not preserve H_q's depth three.
This is an actual truncation example for (13), with no ramification assumption.

The products of all the x values and of all the (1-x) values are respectively one and (-1)^q=-1.
More explicitly x=beta occurs q times and x=beta^(-1) occurs q times, and (1-beta)(1-beta^(-1))=-1.
Hence the signed full resultants are

\[
 \operatorname{Res}(P_q,F_q)=-2^d,\qquad
 \operatorname{Res}(P_q,Q_q)=4^d,\qquad
 \operatorname{Res}(P_q,H_q)=8^d.                         \tag{19}
\]

Every original multiplicity is included.
These identities prove nonvanishing and also show that the specified dyadic depths exhaust the full absolute norm income; no other prime contribution is hidden.

The coefficient costs and exact circle maxima are

| Complete auxiliary | Actual degree | S_2^2 | rho | max on the unit circle | log absolute resultant divided by d |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| F_q=1+x-x^2 | d | 3 | 3 | sqrt5 | log2 |
| Q_q=x^2-7x+1 | d | 51 | 81/51 | 9 | log4 |

For F_q, direct multiplication gives |1+z-z^2|^2=3-2cos(2theta) for z=exp(i theta), whose maximum is five.

\]

Thus a primitive three-term nonmultiple can meet the arithmetic and coefficient-energy side of (14), while the simpler maximum-circle expense has no positive margin.
The latter is the ordinary pointwise bound |F(alpha_i)|<=max_|z|=1|F(z)| max(1,|alpha_i|)^T, obtained by the maximum modulus principle inside the disk and by reversal outside it.

Equation(20) distinguishes the new expense from merely renaming that bound.
The present inputs themselves have h=m(P_q)=log beta for F_q and Q_q, and h=2log beta for H_q, far outside the small-h hypothesis in (7).
Therefore (7) is not being applied to them, and (20) is not a contradiction or a quantitative near-one example.

For comparison, there is a simple actual full-coverage construction with no coefficient-energy surplus, for every input P in Section1.
Let p_0=P(0) be plus or minus one, let p be any rational prime and let s>=1 be an integer.
The monic primitive polynomial

\[
 F=P+p_0p^s
 \quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad
 F(\alpha_i)=p_0p^s,\quad
 |\operatorname{Res}(P,F)|=p^{sd},\quad
 S_2(F)\ge1+p^s.                                       \tag{21}
\]

Its constant coefficient has absolute value 1+p^s, and its leading coefficient one proves primitivity.
Its actual degree is d, and it belongs to (P,p^s).
Thus it supplies all-root depth s at one prime for every actual input, but its exact net margin satisfies

\[
 d^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,F)|-\log S_2(F)
 \le \log(p^s)-\log(1+p^s)<0.                          \tag{22}
\]

This does not presume the existence of a near-one sequence.
It shows on every actual input why full root coverage by a congruence auxiliary alone is insufficient for (14), even after requiring a primitive degree-linear representative.
This representative is already primitive; passing to a proper factor would be a different auxiliary whose nonzero income and coefficient cost must be computed anew.
