# Endpoint evaluations and the two coefficient congruences one degree above a reciprocal seed

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Projection onto the two endpoint values

Fix a monic reciprocal irreducible unit minimum \(P\) of degree \(e=2s\), with nontorsion inverse-pair representatives multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
Let \(h=m(P)>0\), let \(F=F(P)\) be the [minimum-width rate](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#rate), and put \(w=e+1\).
All limits below run through positive integers \(p\to\infty\) for this fixed seed.
Write \(B_+,B_-\) for the primitive integral first coordinates of the two width-\(e\) signed rows and \(R_\varepsilon=(B_\varepsilon,\varepsilon (B_\varepsilon)^*_e)\), where \(A^*_j(Y)=Y^j A(1/Y)\).
At width \(w\), write \(\Lambda_\varepsilon\) for the saturated lattice of integral first coordinates \(A\) satisfying \(P^2\mid A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A_w^*(X^p)\).
For all sufficiently large \(p\), endpoint evaluation \(\pi A=(A(1),A(-1))\) is injective on each real signed plane and
\[
 \log[\mathbb Z^2:\pi\Lambda_\varepsilon]=p(F+h)+o(p),\qquad
 \|A\|_2\le \exp(o(p))\,\|\pi A\|_2.                                  \tag{1}
\]
The error in the second bound is uniform in the vectors of that plane.
Also, for each nonzero fixed rational \(\xi\),
\[
                  \log|B_\varepsilon(\xi)|=pF+o(p).                    \tag{2}
\]

To prove (2), consider the raw cofactor tuple in the [minimum-width support calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support).
When the absolute weights are positive and pairwise distinct, any maximizing assignment of the \(2s\) columns omits precisely the center column \(s\).
Omitting another column loses a positive distance paired against a positive weight.
Thus the coefficient of the leading monomial in the evaluation of the cofactor polynomial at \(\xi\) is the leading coefficient of the center cofactor times \(\xi^s\), up to the cofactor sign.
It is nonzero.
Continuity of finite support functions gives the same maximum weight for this evaluation and the whole tuple at every real weight vector, including ties.
At each embedding, the [nondegenerate recurrence estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives the logarithm of the evaluation modulus minus the logarithm of the maximum coordinate modulus as \(o(p)\).
Specialized raw cofactors and the primitive rational generator are proportional, so the common normalization cancels in this difference.
The generator has log maximum \(pF+o(p)\), proving (2).

At width \(2s+1\) the same strict rearrangement now omits exactly the two columns \(s,s+1\).
The exterior square of \(\pi\) takes the signed complementary maximal minors to the fixed linear combination with coefficients given by the two-column minors of the evaluation matrix.
On the central columns the coefficient is \((-1)^{s+1}-(-1)^s\ne0\).
The [outer-column determinant](Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#support) has a nonzero leading monomial there, and all other omissions have strictly smaller weight.
The same continuity and recurrence argument shows that this single determinant has log modulus within \(o(p)\) of the log maximum of the Plücker coordinates, both before and after scaling to the primitive exterior product of a lattice basis.
The [signed volume formula](Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#volume) gives log covolume \(p(F+h)+o(p)\), and in this fixed dimension the exterior norm and its maximum coordinate differ by bounded factors.
The determinant is a nonzero integer in primitive normalization and its absolute value equals the index in (1).
The product of the singular values of \(\pi\) restricted to the plane is this index divided by the original covolume.
Its logarithm is \(o(p)\), while the larger singular value is bounded above by the fixed ambient operator norm of \(\pi\).
This gives (1).

## The saturation indices share at most a factor of four

Set \(J=1+Y,\ K=Y-1,\ A_\varepsilon=J B_\varepsilon,\ D_\varepsilon=K B_{-\varepsilon}\).
These are primitive and form a rational basis of the signed plane by the [next-width construction](Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#next).
Let \(I_\varepsilon=[\Lambda_\varepsilon:\mathbb Z A_\varepsilon+\mathbb Z D_\varepsilon]\), equivalently the positive gcd of the two-by-two coefficient minors of these two basis vectors by integer Smith normal form.
The two endpoint images of the generators lie on the axes, at \(2B_\varepsilon(1)\) and \(-2B_{-\varepsilon}(-1)\).
Consequently (1) and (2) give
\[
                        \log I_\varepsilon=p(F-h)+o(p).                \tag{3}
\]
Let \(Q\) be the monic reciprocal minimum of a \(p\)-th power of a root of \(P\), of degree \(e\) by independence.
The [double-root elimination](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) and the nonzero width-\(e\) determinant give a nonzero integer \(k_p\) such that
\[
     B_+(B_-)^*_e+(B_+)^*_e B_-=k_p Q^2.
\]
Here integrality follows from monicity and equality of the degree bound \(2e\) with the degree of \(Q^2\).
At \(Y=1\) the left side is \(2B_+(1)B_-(1)\).
Factoring \(Q(1)\) into \(1-x^p\) over all conjugates \(x\) gives \(\log|Q(1)|=ph+o(p)\), by the two-term recurrence estimate at each unit-circle conjugate and direct comparison at the others.
Thus \(\log|k_p|=2p(F-h)+o(p)\).
The divisibilities are
\[
                  I_\varepsilon\mid k_p,\qquad
                  \gcd(I_+,I_-)\mid4,\qquad
                  I_+ I_-\mid4k_p.                                    \tag{4}
\]

For each sign, all coefficient minors vanish modulo \(I_\varepsilon\).
Primitivity of \(D_\varepsilon\) gives an integral linear functional taking it to one.
Contracting the vanished exterior product with this functional gives
\(A_\varepsilon\equiv u_\varepsilon D_\varepsilon\bmod I_\varepsilon\), with \(u_\varepsilon\) a unit modulo this index by primitivity of \(A_\varepsilon\).
This includes prime powers and composite moduli, with a vacuous condition at modulus one.
Reversing this congruence at width \(w\) changes the sign of \(K\).
Multiplying the original congruence by \((B_{-\varepsilon})^*_e\), the reversed one by \(B_{-\varepsilon}\), and adding gives \(J k_p Q^2\equiv0\bmod I_\varepsilon\).
The leading coefficient is \(k_p\), proving the first assertion.
Modulo \(m=\gcd(I_+,I_-)\), multiply the two original congruences to obtain
\((J^2-u_+u_-K^2)B_+ B_-\equiv0\bmod m\).
The product \(B_+ B_-\) is primitive, so it can be cancelled over \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\).
Indeed, Gauss's lemma makes the content of its product with any integer polynomial equal to the content of that polynomial.
The constant and linear coefficients now give \(u_+u_-\equiv1\) and \(2\equiv-2\bmod m\), respectively.
This proves (4), including the possible powers of two.

These identities determine the exponential size of the ordinary leading-coefficient index studied for [quartics at width five](Yin_L_G0462_v248.md#index).
For \(j=e,w\), let \(G_j\) be the entire lattice of integral row pairs \((U,V)\) of width \(j\) with \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\).
Let \(f_j=[G_j:G_j^++G_j^-]\) for the two formal reversal signs and let \(d_j\) be the index in \(\mathbb Z^2\) of the image of \(G_j\) on the coefficient pair at \(Y^j\).
The sign sublattices have direct sum, with quotient killed by two since each doubled row splits integrally under the involution.
Thus \(1\le f_j\le2^{\operatorname{rank}G_j}\).
At width \(e\) the leading vectors of \(R_+,R_-\) have determinant of absolute value \(|k_p|\), so the leading map has trivial kernel and \(d_e=|k_p|/f_e\).
At width \(w\) the kernel of the leading map is \(G_e\), while its image on \(G_e+YG_e\) equals the width-\(e\) leading image.
Hence \([G_w:G_e+YG_e]=d_e/d_w\).
Let \(T\) be generated by \(J R_+,K R_+,J R_-,K R_-\).
Then \([G_w:T]=f_w I_+I_-\).
The two summands \(G_e,YG_e\) are independent over \(\mathbb Q\), since \(R_+,R_-\) have nonzero polynomial determinant and \(1,Y\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
For each of the two inherited signs the coefficient change from \(1,Y\) to \(J,K\) has determinant two, giving \([G_e+YG_e:T]=4 f_e^2\).
Combining the three indices proves
\[
                   d_w=\frac{4 f_e |k_p|}{f_w I_+I_-},\qquad
                   \log d_w=o(p).                                      \tag{5}
\]

## Two rational approximation problems determine the next-width cost

Write \(c_\varepsilon\equiv-u_\varepsilon\bmod I_\varepsilon\), with either integral lift.
Every element of the saturated plane lattice, and only such an element, has a unique expression
\[
        A=\frac{a A_\varepsilon+bD_\varepsilon}{I_\varepsilon},
        \qquad a,b\in\mathbb Z,\quad b\equiv c_\varepsilon a\pmod{I_\varepsilon}.
\]
The index annihilates the quotient by the span of the two generators, and the preceding congruence and primitivity of \(D_\varepsilon\) give the necessary and sufficient integrality condition.
Its endpoint coordinates are \(2B_\varepsilon(1)a/I_\varepsilon\) and \(-2B_{-\varepsilon}(-1)b/I_\varepsilon\).
The two absolute scaling factors have logarithms \(ph+o(p)\) by (2) and (3).
For an integer unit residue \(c\bmod I\) put
\[
       \mu(I,c)=\min_{\substack{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\\
                                    b\equiv ca\bmod I}}\max(|a|,|b|).
\]
It follows from (1) and the fixed upper operator norm of \(\pi\) that the least nonzero size in each signed lattice has logarithm \(ph+\log\mu(I_\varepsilon,c_\varepsilon)+o(p)\).
For the genuine uncapped minimum \(H_{p,w}(P)\) of nonzero coprime rows with unequal moduli and the prescribed double factor, one obtains
\[
           \log H_{p,e+1}(P)=ph+
               \max_{\varepsilon=\pm1}\log\mu(I_\varepsilon,c_\varepsilon)+o(p).
                                                                         \tag{6}
\]
Indeed every genuine pair of height \(H\) provides nonzero vectors \(U+V_w^*,U-V_w^*\) of one-norm at most \(2H\).
Conversely for \(s\ge2\) the [two-sign construction](Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#next) mixes a shortest vector from each plane.
Each avoids the powered factor and both inherited directions for every sufficiently large \(p\).
Dividing the resulting pair by its polynomial gcd multiplies its one-norm bound by at most a fixed degree-dependent factor.
Equivalence of the coefficient norms at fixed width therefore gives the reverse comparison up to a bounded factor.
For \(s=1\) the same construction uses vectors at the second minimum, and both successive minima in each plane have the common rate \(3h/2\).
Thus passing to the second minimum there changes the logarithm by \(o(p)\), which suffices for (6).

For the [octic Salem seed](Yin_L_G0462_v252.md#specialization) \(X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1\), put \(h=\log\rho\), where \(\rho>1\) is its exterior root.
Here \(F=8h\), and both moduli have log size \(7ph+o(p)\).
At the cutoff \(2^p\) the exponent for both congruence minima is
\[
                       \theta=\frac{\log2-h}{7h},\qquad
                       \frac14<\theta<\frac4{15}.
\]
For the displayed bounds, the seed has opposite signs at \(32/25\) and \(1281/1000\), which bracket its unique exterior root.
The integer comparisons \((1281/1000)^{11}<16\) and \((32/25)^{43}>2^{15}\) give the two strict inequalities.
Equation (6) gives the necessary conditions \(\mu(I_\varepsilon,c_\varepsilon)\le I_\varepsilon^{\theta+o(1)}\) for both signs along any sequence meeting the cutoff.
In the other direction every nonzero signed row at sufficiently large \(p\) avoids \(Q\) by the two-node argument at width \(e+1\).
Its signed reversal therefore has a nonzero powered value, and the [single-row norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#statement) with \(e=8,\ w=9,\ D(P)=14h\) gives the lower rate \(21h/8\) for that sign.
Subtracting the endpoint scale \(ph\) shows that each \(\mu(I_\varepsilon,c_\varepsilon)\) is at least \(I_\varepsilon^{13/56-o(1)}\).
Conversely a fixed positive margin below \(\theta\) for both signs guarantees passage eventually, and a fixed positive margin above it for either sign guarantees failure.
The error term leaves equality at the exponent undecided.

In continued-fraction terms, choose \(0<c<I\) in a unit residue class for \(I>1\).
If \(1\le T<I\), let \(q_k\) be the last convergent denominator of \(c/I\) at most \(T\), with the next denominator \(q_{k+1}\).
Every \(1\le a\le T\) has \(\min_{n\in\mathbb Z}|ac-In|\ge|q_kc-Ip_k|\), where \(p_k/q_k\) is the corresponding convergent.
Indeed consecutive numerator-denominator pairs form an integral basis with errors of opposite signs or with final error zero.
Writing \((n,a)\) in that basis, a nonzero coefficient of the later pair forces an opposite, nonzero coefficient of the earlier pair since \(0<a<q_{k+1}\).
The earlier error therefore cannot be cancelled.
The continued-fraction recurrence gives
\(I/(q_{k+1}+q_k)\le |q_kc-Ip_k|\le I/q_{k+1}\) by writing the tail after the \(k\)-th convergent between the next partial quotient and that quotient plus one.
Consequently \(\mu(I,c)\le T\) implies \(q_{k+1}\ge I/T-T\), and \(q_{k+1}\ge I/T\) suffices.
In particular an octic passing sequence requires a jump from a denominator \(\le I_\varepsilon^{\theta+o(1)}\) to one \(\ge I_\varepsilon^{1-\theta-o(1)}\) for each sign.

At \(p=17\), take \(B_+,B_-\) in the orientations returned by the width-eight \(\texttt{row_system}\) of the [integer kernel program](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/minimum.py).
Computing the coefficient minors and solving \(A_\varepsilon+c_\varepsilon D_\varepsilon\equiv0\bmod I_\varepsilon\) gives the following integers.

| Sign | \(I_\varepsilon\) | \(c_\varepsilon\) | Absolute endpoint scales | \(\mu\) | Minimizing \((a,b)\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| \(+\) | 23,458 | 10,229 | \(768,2304\) | 133 | \((133,-107)\) |
| \(-\) | 1,978 | 475 | \(256,768\) | 25 | \((25,7)\) |

For the plus sign the last convergent denominator below 133 is 39, whose least residue in absolute value is 145.
For the minus sign the last denominator below 25 is four, with least residue 78.
These certify minimality in the two congruences.
The moduli have gcd two, and the exact genuine row cost in the original coefficient norm is [91,698](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/versions/v2/readme.md#census).
