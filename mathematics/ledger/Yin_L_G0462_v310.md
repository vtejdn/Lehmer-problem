# Congruences for rational outputs of primitive dilations

<a id="test"></a>
## A test using the common integer output

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), and put \(d=\deg P\).
Fix a nonconstant Laurent polynomial \(F(X)=\sum_i a_iX^{w_i}\) with integer coefficients, combining equal exponents.
These hypotheses include the rational coefficients in the [terminal height window](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement) at \(B=1\).
For every positive integer \(q\) giving rational output \(c_q=F(\alpha^q)\), integrality of all powers of the unit gives \(c_q\in\mathbb Z\).
For each \(n\ge1\), put \(k_n=\operatorname{content}(P\bmod\Phi_n)\), using the positive gcd of the coefficients of the ordinary remainder by the monic cyclotomic polynomial.
This gcd is positive since \(P\) is irreducible and noncyclotomic.
Only \(\varphi(n)\le d\) can give \(k_n>1\), since otherwise the remainder is monic.
For \(t\mid n\), reduce \(F(X^t)\) modulo \(\Phi_n\) to its representative \(V_{n,t}\) of degree less than \(\varphi(n)\), first reducing signed exponents modulo \(n\).
Then every rational output satisfies
\[
 V_{n,\gcd(n,q)}(X)\equiv c_q\pmod {k_n}
\]
coefficient by coefficient.
In particular all nonconstant coefficients must vanish modulo \(k_n\).
The constant coefficients for different \(n\) must agree modulo the gcd of the two moduli.
Conversely, for a prescribed list of gcds with finitely many orders, these two tests are sufficient for an integer to satisfy the indicated system of congruences, by the Chinese remainder theorem.
This converse concerns the congruences alone.

Indeed, clearing negative powers in \(F(X^q)-c_q\) gives a multiple of \(P\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) by monic division.
At a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta\), we have \(P(\zeta)\in k_n\mathbb Z[\zeta]\) by definition.
Since \(\zeta\) is a unit, \(F(\zeta^q)\equiv c_q\pmod {k_n\mathbb Z[\zeta]}\).
For \(t=\gcd(q,n)\), choose a unit \(b\) modulo \(n\) congruent to \(q/t\) modulo \(n/t\).
Such a lift exists by imposing the residue 1 at each prime dividing \(n\) but not \(n/t\), and using the Chinese remainder theorem.
The automorphism \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^b\) takes \(\zeta^t\) to \(\zeta^q\) and preserves both the integer output and the ideal.
The integral power basis of \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]\) now gives the coefficient test, including when primes dividing \(k_n\) divide \(n\).

<a id="prime"></a>
## Forced divisors and the local pair estimate

For a prime \(\ell\), write \(P_j\) for the sum of the coefficients of \(P\) at exponents congruent to \(j\bmod\ell\), and \(A_j\) for the corresponding sum for \(F\).
Then \(k_\ell=\gcd_{0\le j<\ell-1}(P_j-P_{\ell-1})\), since \(\Phi_\ell=1+\cdots+X^{\ell-1}\).
If \(A_1,\ldots,A_{\ell-1}\) are not all congruent modulo \(k_\ell\), every rational-output dilation is a multiple of \(\ell\).
Otherwise a dilation prime to \(\ell\) gives \(c_q\equiv A_0-A_{\ell-1}\pmod {k_\ell}\).
A dilation divisible by \(\ell\) always gives \(c_q\equiv F(1)\pmod{k_\ell}\).
These formulas allow prime powers in the modulus even for repeated roots in the reduction of \(P\).

Let \(D\) be the product of distinct primes forced by this test for the fixed list.
For hypotheses admitting a local independent pair with period \(J\) as in the [local pair proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v586.md), only \(J/\gcd(D,J)\) residue classes modulo \(J\) can occur.
The bound of \(4s\) per class for a list of length \(s\) thus gives at most \(4sJ/\gcd(D,J)\) positive dilations.
Other orders can be tested directly on a class when their order divides \(J\).
For example, ranks 21 and 49 in the [72-input table](Yin_L_G0462_v307.md#benchmark) both have local period 5 at 3.
At both ranks \(k_5=3\), as follows by collecting the coefficients in [the source rows](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0173/least_exponents.py) into the five residue classes and applying the displayed gcd.
Thus any eligible list whose four nonzero residue sums are not all equal modulo 3 admits at most \(4s\) positive dilations on either input.
Across those 72 rows, testing all primes \(\ell\le d+1\) gives \(k_\ell>1\) for 26 rows at 2, 18 at 3 and six at 5, with no larger primes contributing.
The other four rows at 5 have ranks 11, 27, 36 and 58, all with \(k_5=2\).

## Fixing the output from its height

Put \(K(q)=\operatorname{lcm}_{n\mid q} k_n\), including \(k_1=|P(1)|\).
At every such order the representative is \(F(1)\), so \(K(q)\mid c_q-F(1)\).
If \(|c_q|\le H\) and \(K(q)>H+|F(1)|\), this forces \(c_q=F(1)\).
At a strict degree record put \(m=\log M(\alpha)\).
For any set \(Q\) of distinct positive dilations meeting this inequality and the height bound,
\[
 m\sum_{q\in Q}q\le\log\bigl(\textstyle\sum_i|a_i|+|F(1)|\bigr).
\]
Indeed, all \(\alpha^q\) are zeros of the same nonzero Laurent polynomial \(F(X)-F(1)\).
The [power-field identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm) gives \(\log M(\alpha^q)=qm\), so their minimal polynomials are distinct by their different measures.
Their product divides the polynomial obtained by clearing negative powers, and its Mahler measure is bounded by the coefficient sum in the display using the unit-circle integral.
In the rational terminal window one can take \(H=e^Y\) and \(\sum_i|a_i|\le s e^Y\).
The test gives a weighted count when the cyclotomic moduli are large enough.
Whether these moduli grow along a sequence of strict records remains open here.
