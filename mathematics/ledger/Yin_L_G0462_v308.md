# Inversion and the radial spread of conjugates in terminal dilation counts

<a id="inversion"></a>
## Rational outputs away from reflection symmetry

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit, so its conjugates are closed under inversion.
Put \(H=\max_\beta\log|\beta|>0\), where \(\beta\) ranges over the conjugates, and let \(F(X)=\sum_{t\ne0}a_tX^t\) have finite support and rational coefficients.
Write \(d_t=a_t-a_{-t}\) for \(t>0\), taking missing coefficients to be zero, and let \(p\) be the number of nonzero \(d_t\).
If \(p>0\), the number of positive integers \(q\) with \(F(\alpha^q)\in\mathbb Q\) is less than
\[
             p\left\lceil\frac{\log(64(p+1)^2)}{H}\right\rceil .
\]
Indeed a conjugate \(\beta\) attaining \(H\) and its inverse give the same rational output, whence \(\sum_{t>0}d_t\beta^{qt}=\sum_{t>0}d_t\beta^{-qt}\).
The right side is bounded in modulus by \(\sum|d_t|\), so the [separated evaluation estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v585.md#matrix) applies with the surviving positive exponents.
For a terminal list of weight at least four all exponents are distinct, including the distinguished zero, by the [two-term split test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v585.md#rays).
In particular an even terminal weight always has \(p>0\), since reflection pairs all nonzero positions when \(p=0\).

<a id="spread"></a>
## Symmetric coefficients and two different conjugate radii

Put \(h_0=\min_\gamma |\log|\gamma||\) over conjugates and \(\delta=H-h_0\).
Suppose \(F(X)=\sum_{i=1}^p b_i(X^{u_i}+X^{-u_i})\), with distinct positive integer \(u_i\) in increasing order and nonzero rational \(b_i\).
If \(\delta>0\), the symmetric list has fewer than \(pL\) rational-output dilations, where \(L=\lceil\log(64(p+1)^2)/\delta\rceil\).
Take \(\beta\) as above and \(\gamma\) attaining \(h_0\).
For any \(q\) giving a rational output the vector \((b_i)\) annihilates the row with entries
\[
 A_{qi}=\beta^{qu_i}+\beta^{-qu_i}-\gamma^{qu_i}-\gamma^{-qu_i}.
\]
For \(q\ge L\), each entry is \(\beta^{qu_i}(1+e_{qi})\) with \(|e_{qi}|\le 3e^{-\delta q u_i}\le 3z\), where \(z=e^{-\delta L}\le 1/(64(p+1)^2)\).
For \(p\) rows separated by at least \(L\), let \(D\) be the modulus of the ordered diagonal product of the unperturbed matrix \((\beta^{qu_i})\).
The inversion count in the [determinant proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v585.md#matrix) bounds its off-diagonal permutation products in total by \(D((1-z)^{-p}-1)\), since \(H\ge\delta\).
In \(A\) those terms have total modulus at most \(D(1+3z)^p((1-z)^{-p}-1)\), while the ordered diagonal product has modulus at least \(D(1-3z)^p\).
As \(pz\le 1/64\), the latter is at least \(61D/64\), and the former is at most \(De^{3/64}/63\) by Bernoulli's inequality.
The selected rows would thus form an invertible matrix, inconsistent with their common nonzero kernel vector.
Greedy covering above \(L-1\) proves the estimate, including the single-column case.

<a id="patterns"></a>
## What remains after division by the dilations

For a shrinking-measure sequence of reciprocal units put \(m=\log M(\alpha)\), and take a weight \(r\to\infty\) and fixed rational outgoing coefficient tuple from the [terminal count with scalar degree one](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement).
As in its proof the minimum power-field degree tends to infinity, so this choice applies eventually.
At least half the \((c_0/m)^{r-2}\) guaranteed realizations are asymmetric about the distinguished term or at least half are symmetric there.
In the asymmetric case division by \((r-1)\lceil\log(64r^2)/H\rceil\) gives a lower bound for the number of primitive marked lists of that type from the corresponding number of realizations.
When \(\delta>0\), the same conclusion for *all* lists holds with \(H\) replaced by \(\delta\), by the two estimates above.
In particular the primitive lower bound \(\exp((1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m))\) follows whenever \(\delta>0\) and \(\log^+(1/\delta)=o((r-2)\log(1/m))\), where \(\log^+ x=\max(0,\log x)\).
For the asymmetric subfamily containing at least half the realizations the corresponding condition uses \(H\).
Forgetting the mark costs at most \(r\) in both conclusions.
On a shrinking-measure sequence with bounded expanding and contracting counts, units without neutral conjugates already have bounded degree and hence can occur only finitely often.
Thus the radial spread criterion has additional scope when these counts grow.

A finite support with at least two points has at most one reflection center, its extreme-point midpoint.
Moving the distinguished position away from that center after translation therefore breaks symmetry of the support, but now places the old distinguished coefficient \(-F(\alpha^q)\) among the outgoing coefficients.
It may vary with \(q\), preventing direct use of a fixed-coefficient estimate after this change.
For example a positive root \(\alpha>1\) of \(X^2-3X+1\) has \(\alpha^q+\alpha^{-q}\in\mathbb Z\) for all \(q\ge1\) by the integral trace recurrence.
Appending the negative trace gives a nondegenerate three-term relation with no eligible two-term split at weight three, on the single symmetric primitive marked list \((-1,1)\).
This rules out an unconditional finite dilation bound for every symmetric terminal list and every reciprocal unit.
For reciprocal units realizing strict minima through their degree, the [two-circle descent](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v587.md#degree) gives positive spread in degree greater than four.
Its size relative to the growing weight remains open.
