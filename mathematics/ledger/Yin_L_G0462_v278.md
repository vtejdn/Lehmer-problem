# The degree cost of splitting a signed graph across a gap

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## Width-one signed rows attain the degree dependence

Let \(P\) be the minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(d=\deg P\), and \(m=\log M(P)\).
If \(P\) divides an integral Laurent polynomial \(G\), the [classical cut inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) bounds \(gm\) by \(d\log\|G\|_1\) whenever a gap of length \(g\) separates two blocks with nonzero values at a root of \(P\).
Here \(g\) is the difference of the closest exponents on the two sides.
The factor \(d\) is necessary even for passing signed rows of width one, with genuine contact at a Salem unit all of whose positive powers have degree \(d\).
In fact the supremum of \(gm/(d\log\|G\|_1)\) over these rows is one for each graph sign.

Fix an integer \(a\ge3\), an odd integer \(n\ge3\) and \(\eta\in\{-1,1\}\).
At formal width one the row \(A(Y)=\eta-aY\) and its reversal \(A^*(Y)=\eta Y-a\) give
\[
 G(X)=A(X^n)+\eta X A^*(X^n)=X^n(X-a)+\eta(1-aX).
\]
The two blocks have gap \(n-1\) and no common nonzero root.
The [Salem–Boyd construction](../audits/literature/Hir06/Yin_U001.md#hir06-u001) supplies the exterior-root count, and the [special case \(a=3,\eta=1,n=5^r\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0136_v1.md#salem-involution-actual-family) gives the norm argument for excluding cyclotomic factors.
On the unit circle one has \(|X-a|=|1-aX|\), so for \(0\le t<1\) Rouché's theorem gives exactly one exterior zero, counted with multiplicity, of \(X^n(X-a)+t\eta(1-aX)\).
By continuity of roots \(G\) has at most one exterior zero.
For fixed \(a\), on any sufficiently small circle about \(a\) lying outside the unit disk, Rouché's theorem gives one zero of \(G\) inside that circle for every sufficiently large odd \(n\).
Thus the exterior zero \(\beta=\beta_{n,a,\eta}\) is simple, real and positive, and tends to \(a\).
The identity \(X^{n+1}G(1/X)=\eta G(X)\) shows that \(\beta^{-1}\) is the only interior root.

If a root of unity \(\zeta\) were a root of \(G\), the equation \(a(\zeta^n+\eta\zeta)=\zeta^{n+1}+\eta\) would bound the modulus of every conjugate of the algebraic integer \(\zeta^n+\eta\zeta\) by \(2/a<1\).
Its integer norm forces \(\zeta^n=-\eta\zeta\), and substitution gives \(\zeta^2=1\).
For odd \(n\) the positive sign therefore gives no root of unity.
For the negative sign both \(1\) and \(-1\) are simple roots, since the derivatives there are \(n+1-a(n-1)\) and \(-(n+1)-a(n-1)\).
Every monic irreducible factor of \(G\) has unit constant coefficient.
Consequently the factor \(P\) containing \(\beta\) also contains \(\beta^{-1}\), and Kronecker's theorem makes every other factor cyclotomic.
It follows that \(P=G\) for the positive sign and \(P=G/(X^2-1)\) for the negative sign at all sufficiently large odd \(n\).
In particular \(d=n+1\) or \(d=n-1\), respectively, and \(m=\log\beta\).
The unit \(\beta\) has no distinct conjugate of equal modulus, so \(\mathbb Q(\beta^k)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) for each positive integer \(k\).
Indeed a second embedding over \(\mathbb Q(\beta^k)\) would send \(\beta\) to another exterior root.

The contact is genuine at every conjugate since \(A(\beta^n)\ne0\).
The row norm is \(a+1\), so \(\big((2n+1)(a+1)^2\big)^4<2^n\) at every sufficiently large \(n\) for each fixed \(a\).
The graph norm is \(2(a+1)\), whence
\[
 \lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\ {\rm odd}}}
 \frac{(n-1)\log M(P)}{d\log\|G\|_1}
 =\frac{\log a}{\log(2(a+1))}.
\]
Letting \(a\) tend to infinity proves the asserted supremum.
Already at any one \(a\) these rows refute an estimate \(gm\le o(d)\log\|G\|_1\) uniform in the seed.
The measures in each sequence tend to \(\log a>0\).

<a id="multiplicity"></a>
## Splitting with prescribed multiplicity and arbitrary integer shifts

Let \(G=L+H\) be a sum of nonzero integral Laurent polynomials with separated supports, and put \(v=\min\operatorname{supp}H\) and \(g=v-\max\operatorname{supp}L>0\).
Write \(w_L,w_H\) for the differences of the extreme exponents in the respective blocks.
Assume \(P^t\mid G\) for \(t\ge1\), where divisibility is in \(\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\).
If the two blocks are not both divisible by \(P^t\), their orders at \(P\) coincide at some \(0\le j<t\), since unequal orders give their minimum as the order of the sum.
In particular \(jd\le\min(w_L,w_H)\).
For \(B=L,H\), put \(s_B=\min\operatorname{supp} B\) and
\[
 C_j(B)=\sum_{k\in\operatorname{supp}B}
                 |[X^k]B|\binom{k-s_B}{j}.
\]
Then for this \(j\) one has
\[
                 gm\ \le\ d\log\max\{C_j(L),C_j(H)\}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus a strict reverse inequality for each \(j<t\) with \(jd\le\min(w_L,w_H)\) forces \(P^t\) to divide both blocks.
A zero weighted norm makes the corresponding order \(j\) impossible.
These tests only use positions relative to the beginning of each block, so they also allow negative exponents and arbitrary monomial shifts of the graph.

For a unit root \(x\) of \(P\), consider the Taylor expansions at \(T=0\) of \(B(x(1+T))(1+T)^{-s_B}\).
The coefficient of \(T^j\) is the value at \(x\) of \(B_j=\sum_k [X^k]B\binom{k-s_B}{j}X^k\).
Both blocks vanish to exact order \(j\), so the multipliers \((1+T)^{-s_B}\), whose constant terms are one, preserve each block's first nonzero Taylor coefficient.
Because \(P^t\mid G\), these coefficients satisfy \(L_j(x)=-H_j(x)\ne0\) at all conjugates.
For \(|x|>1\) bound the common absolute value by \(C_j(L)|x|^{v-g}\), and for \(|x|<1\) bound it by \(C_j(H)|x|^v\).
On the unit circle either weighted norm bounds the common value.
The common value is a nonzero algebraic integer even for negative exponents because \(x\) is a unit.
Multiplying over all conjugates and using \(\prod_{|x|>1}|x|=M(P)\) and \(\prod_{|x|<1}|x|=M(P)^{-1}\) proves (1).
More precisely, if \(d_+,d_-,d_0\) conjugates lie outside, inside and on the circle, respectively, the upper bound for \(M(P)^g\) is
\[
           C_j(L)^{d_+}C_j(H)^{d_-}\min\{C_j(L),C_j(H)\}^{d_0}.
\]
Each \(C_j(B)\) is bounded by \(\binom{w_B}{j}\|B\|_1\).
This extends the value estimate in [the project account of Lenstra's gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) to multiplicities with a cost depending on the separate block widths.

<a id="degree"></a>
## A degree-independent cut test for several repeated factors

Suppose \(P_i^{t_i}\mid G\) for distinct minima of nontorsion units, where every \(t_i\ge2\).
Write \(d_i=\deg P_i\), \(m_i=\log M(P_i)\), and \(j_i=\min(t_i,\operatorname{ord}_{P_i}H)\).
Then
\[
             \sum_i(t_i+j_i-1)d_i\ \le\ w_L+w_H.                       \tag{2}
\]
In particular if a single prescribed power \(P^t\), \(t\ge2\), fails to split completely at the cut, its common block order \(j<t\) satisfies
\[
          gm\ \le\ \frac{w_L+w_H}{t+j-1}\log\max\{C_j(L),C_j(H)\}.
\]
For several factors the corresponding necessary condition is
\[
  g\sum_{i:\,j_i<t_i}\frac{(t_i+j_i-1)m_i}
                     {\log\max\{C_{j_i}(L),C_{j_i}(H)\}}
                  \ \le\ w_L+w_H.
\]
The logarithms in this sum are strictly positive by (1).
For example, if both widths are at most \(w\ge1\), both norms are at most \(K\), and \(P^2\) fails to split, then either \(gm\le2w\log K\) at \(j=0\) or \(gm\le w\log(wK)\) at \(j=1\).
These bounds apply uniformly as the degrees and exponents vary.

To prove (2), write \(u=\min\operatorname{supp}L\), \(A_0=X^{-u}L\), \(B_0=X^{-v}H\), \(N=v-u>0\) and \(F=A_0+X^N B_0\).
The derivative eliminates the distant block in
\[
 D=(NB_0+XB_0')F-X B_0 F'
   =N A_0B_0+X(A_0B_0'-A_0'B_0).
\]
This polynomial has degree at most \(w_L+w_H\) and nonzero constant term \(NA_0(0)B_0(0)\).
In its first expression \(F\) vanishes to order at least \(t_i\) and \(B_0\) to order at least \(j_i\) at \(P_i\).
Differentiation lowers the order by at most one, so \(P_i^{t_i+j_i-1}\mid D\) for every \(i\).
Multiplying the coprime powers proves (2), and (1) at those factors that fail to split gives the claimed estimates.
The derivative elimination is related to the [resultant argument for separated polynomial blocks](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v162.md#cofactors).

For each fixed \(t\), the ordinary polynomial \(G^t\) from the first section has \(P\) to exact order \(t\).
When \(n>t\), its lowest block is \(\eta^t(1-aX)^t\), and its next exponent is \(n\), with nonzero coefficient \(-ta\eta^{t-1}\).
The gap is \(n-t\), the low block is coprime to \(P\), and the total norm is at most \((2(a+1))^t\).
This gives the same linear degree obstruction for exact multiplicity \(t\) in arbitrary integral polynomials at fixed \(t,a\).
For the original signed rows the exhibited contact has order one.
Whether small-measure seeds admit the simultaneous exclusion of the [two prescribed double contacts](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests) remains open.
