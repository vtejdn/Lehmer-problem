# The first denominator coefficient for a moving binomial lower part

<a id="offset"></a>
## Possible offsets of the first nonzero coefficient

Consider Salem numbers \(\sigma_i\to1\) with monic trace minimal polynomials \(S_i\) of degree \(m_i=k_i+1\) and reciprocal lifts \(P_i=z^{m_i}S_i(z+z^{-1})\).
Suppose a strict interlacer has reciprocal lift
\[
 C_i^\sharp=2+z^{h_i}+z^{2k_i-h_i}+2z^{2k_i},
 \qquad h_i\to\infty,\quad h_i=o(k_i).
\]
The width assumption is consistent with the [frequency concentration for full interlacers](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v42.md#statement).
Assume additionally that \(P_i\to1\) coefficientwise.
The [argument for the adjacent Chebyshev traces](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#profiles) establishes such a prefix for its fixed lower part \(2+z\).
How to obtain it for the moving lower parts here remains open.

Let \(p_j=[z^j]P_i\), suppressing \(i\), and let \(n\) be the first positive index with \(p_n\ne0\).
Then \(n\to\infty\) and \(n\le m\) by reciprocity and the exterior root.
Along all sufficiently late indices with \(n\ge h\), the offset \(n-h\) belongs to \(\{0,2,4\}\) and \(p_n=1\).
Along any infinite subsequence with \(n<h\) one has \(h-n\to\infty\) and \(|p_n|=1\) eventually.
On that subsequence, for each fixed \(J\ge2\), the block beginning at \(n\) through degree \(n+J\) eventually agrees with the corresponding coefficients of \(p_nz^n(1-z^2)\).
These are necessary conditions on the indicated coefficientwise branch.
The argument gives neither such a sequence nor a uniform gap on the entire moving family.

<a id="law"></a>
## A positive measure for the moving moments

Use the [positive interlacing weights and formal moment expansion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) with the present lift.
The weights \(c_x=C_i(x)/S_i'(x)\) sum to two, and \(t_i=c_{\sigma_i+\sigma_i^{-1}}\) is the exterior trace weight.
Let \(\lambda_i\) place half the corresponding weight at each of the two circle lifts of every interior trace.
With \(g_j=[z^j]G_i\) the generating function and the positive moments are
\[
 G_i=(1-z^2)C_i^\sharp/P_i,\qquad
 g_j=2\int z^j\,d\lambda_i+t_i(\sigma_i^j+\sigma_i^{-j})\quad (j>0).
\]
On fixed initial degrees \(G_i\) agrees eventually with \(2(1-z^2)\).
Consequently \(\lambda_i+t_i\delta_1\) tends weakly to \(w\,dm_{\mathbb T}\), where \(m_{\mathbb T}\) denotes normalized arclength and \(w(z)=|1-z^2|^2\).
Indeed the measures have mass two and all their positive and negative Fourier coefficients have these limits.
Positivity and the absence of an atom at one in \(w\,dm_{\mathbb T}\) imply \(t_i\to0\) and hence \(\lambda_i\to w\,dm_{\mathbb T}\).

For the integer indices \(b=n\), or \(b=h\) when \(n-h\to+\infty\), take a joint weak limit \(\Omega\) of the pushforwards of \(\lambda_i\) by \(z\mapsto(z,z^b)\), writing \(u\) for the second coordinate.
The complex measure obtained by integrating \(u\) in the second coordinate has density \(f\) with respect to \(m_{\mathbb T}\) satisfying \(|f|\le w\) almost everywhere, by the first marginal.
At \(b-1<n\) the coefficient \(g_{b-1}\) is bounded by one for large \(i\), since it is then zero or a coefficient from \((1-z^2)z^h\).
The upper band starts at \(2k-h\), whose distance above \(n\) tends to infinity.
The moment formula and \(\lambda_i(\mathbb T)\le2\) give \(t_i\sigma_i^b\le5\sigma_i\).
We may extract a limit \(t_i\sigma_i^b\to s\) and, for \(b=n\), a fixed nonzero \(p=p_n\), since \(g_n\) is bounded by the moment formula and differs from the bounded numerator coefficient by \(-2p_n\).

Write \(\widehat f(e)=\int z^{-e}f(z)\,dm_{\mathbb T}(z)\).
For any fixed integer \(e\), the moment identity gives
\[
             \widehat f(e)=\tfrac12\lim_i g_{b-e}-s/2.
\]
Here \(t_i\sigma_i^{e-b}\to0\), and the stated limits of the coefficients exist after taking the joint weak limit.
In each case below \(g_{b-e}=0\) eventually for every sufficiently large fixed positive \(e\).
Since \(f\) is integrable, approximation by trigonometric polynomials gives \(\widehat f(e)\to0\) as \(e\to+\infty\).
It follows that \(s=0\), including when the index \(b\) itself varies faster than the fixed-scale moments control.

<a id="upper-offset"></a>
## Testing the numerator before or at the first denominator change

If \(n-h\to+\infty\), take \(b=h\).
Then all fixed offsets \(h-e\) are strictly below \(n\) eventually, and their coefficients are \(1\) at \(e=0\), \(-1\) at \(e=-2\) and zero otherwise.
Fourier uniqueness gives \(f=(1-z^{-2})/2\), violating \(|f|\le|1-z^2|^2\) on the positive-length arcs where \(0<|1-z^2|<1/2\).
Thus the positive offsets \(n-h\) are bounded.

On a fixed offset \(d=n-h\ge0\), choose \(b=n\).
For all \(e\ge0\) the exact numerator coefficients up to \(n\) and the single denominator change give
\[
       \widehat f(e)=\tfrac12{\bf1}_{e=d}
                    -\tfrac12{\bf1}_{e=d-2}-p{\bf1}_{e=0}.
\]
In particular \(z^{-d}f(z)\), as a function of \(v=z^{-1}\), has only nonnegative Fourier powers.
Its Poisson integral defines a bounded analytic function \(J(v)\) with boundary modulus at most \(|1-v^2|^2\).
The function \(H(v)=J(v)/(1-v^2)^2\) is bounded by one in the disk.
Indeed \(J(v)/(1-\rho v^2)^2\) has boundary modulus at most \(4/(1+\rho)^2\) for \(0<\rho<1\), by maximizing \(|(1-v^2)/(1-\rho v^2)|^2\) on the circle.
Take the same bound in the disk and then let \(\rho\uparrow1\).
The Taylor coefficients of \(H\) through degree \(d\) are those of
\[
                       \frac{1}{2(1-v^2)}-p v^d .
\]
For \(d=0\) the constant bound gives \(p=1\).
For positive \(d\) the constant coefficient is \(1/2\), and Parseval gives \(|[v^d]H|\le\sqrt{3}/2\).
This excludes odd \(d\), where that coefficient is \(-p\), and forces \(p=1\) at even \(d\), where it is \(1/2-p\).
For \(d\ge5\) the coefficients in degrees \(0,2,4\) all equal \(1/2\).
Hence the squared circle norm of \(H(v)(1+v^2+v^4)\) is at least \(1/4+1+9/4=7/2\) from these three coefficients of the product.
The bound \(|H|\le1\) gives an upper bound of three, a contradiction.
Only the three stated offsets remain.

<a id="first-block"></a>
## Equality in the moment pairing when the denominator changes first

On indices with \(n<h\) use \(b=n\).
Now \(\widehat f(e)=0\) for every positive \(e\) and \(\widehat f(0)=-p\).
For \(Q_N(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-j/N)z^{2j}\) we obtain
\[
          \int u\,\overline{Q_N(z)}^2\,d\Omega=-p.
\]
In \(L^2(w\,dm_{\mathbb T})\) these polynomials converge to \(Q=(1-z^2)^{-1}\), since \((1-z^2)Q_N=1-N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N z^{2j}\).
In particular \(\|Q_N\|^2=1+1/N\) and \(\|Q\|^2=1\).
Taking the limit and applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|p|=1\) by integrality.
Equality forces \(u\overline Q=-pQ\) almost everywhere for \(\Omega\), so \(u=pz^{-2}\) because \(Q/\overline Q=-z^{-2}\) away from \(z=\pm1\).

For each fixed \(j\ge0\), the resulting \(g_{n+j}\) tend to \(2p\int z^{j-2}w(z)\,dm_{\mathbb T}(z)\), using \(t_i\sigma_i^n\to0\) from the joint limit.
The integers at these lags therefore stabilize to the coefficients of \(-2p(1-z^2)^2\), in order beginning at lag \(n\).
Pass to a subsequence on which \(L=h-n\) is a fixed positive integer or tends to infinity.
In fixed degrees through any \(J\), formal division at the first change gives the coefficient block of \(G_i\) beginning at \(n\) as
\[
      (1-z^2)\left(z^L-2\sum_{j=0}^J p_{n+j}z^j\right)\pmod {z^{J+1}} .
\]
The square of the nonconstant part of \(P_i\) starts at \(2n\), and the product of that part with the numerator term at \(h\) starts at \(n+h\), both beyond \(n+J\) eventually.
The reciprocal upper numerator band also lies beyond this range.
Comparison with the stabilized block and division by \(1-z^2\) require
\[
      \sum_{j=0}^J p_{n+j}z^j
            \equiv p(1-z^2)+\tfrac12z^L\pmod {z^{J+1}} .
\]
A fixed positive \(L\) contradicts integrality as soon as \(J\ge L\), including \(L=2\).
Thus \(h-n\to\infty\) on the negative-offset branch and the fixed blocks have the asserted coefficients.
Every subsequence on either branch admits the extractions used above, so the eventual restrictions apply without choosing a weak limit in their statement.
