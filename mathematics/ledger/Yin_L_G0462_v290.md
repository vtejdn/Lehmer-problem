# Many distinct minimal supports are necessary at a long universal threshold

<a id="shapes"></a>
## Counting supports up to translation

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(P\) be its monic minimum, and let \(1\le t<N\).
Suppose that this unit witnesses the [universal ternary threshold](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v572.md#statement) at length \(N\) and cardinality \(t\) with multiplicity one.
Call a finite exponent set minimal if it supports a nonzero ternary polynomial divisible by \(P\) and none of its proper subsets does.
Every relation on a minimal set uses all of its exponents.
Each translation class has a representative with least exponent zero.
Let \(A_j\) be the number of classes of minimal sets of size \(j\) and span less than \(N\), where the span is the largest exponent minus the smallest.
If \(N\ge2t\), some \(3\le j\le t+1\) satisfies
\[
                      A_j\ \ge\ \left(\frac{N}{4t}\right)^{j-1}.
\]
This weight must tend to infinity along any sequence of witnesses with \(N/t\to\infty\).

To see this, put \(d=t/N\) and choose positions independently in the interval with probability \(p\in[0,1]\).
For each selected minimal support of size at most \(t+1\), delete one of its positions, taking the union of the deletions.
The residual set has at most \(t\) elements, since any \(t+1\) of its elements would contain a minimal support left untouched.
A normalized support \(E\) has exactly \(N-\max E\) translates in the interval and a specified translate is selected with probability \(p^{|E|}\).
One obtains
\[
 p-d\ \le\ \sum_{3\le |E|\le t+1}\left(1-\frac{\max E}{N}\right)p^{|E|}
       \ \le\ \sum_{j=3}^{t+1} A_j p^j.
\]
There are no one-term or two-term relations because \(\alpha\) is nonzero and nontorsion.
At \(p=2d\), division by \(p\) gives a lower bound of \(1/2\) for the last sum with exponent \(j-1\).
If every \(A_j\) were smaller than \((4d)^{1-j}\), this sum would be less than \(\sum_{j\ge3}2^{1-j}=1/2\).
This proves the assertion and applies even though every possible choice of signs on one support counts only once.

For each fixed \(j\), the [normalized power-relation bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0276_v1.md#statement) from [Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt, Theorem 1.1](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess02-u001) gives
\[
           A_j\le 2^{j-1}\exp\!\big(j[6(j-1)]^{3(j-1)}\big).
\]
Indeed after translation and fixing the sign at zero, the remaining ordered \(j-1\) powers form a tuple in a group of rank \(j-1\), and minimality excludes a vanishing subsum.
For each sign pattern the tuple determines the exponents by nontorsion.
This bound is independent of the unit and interval, and hence a bounded set of weights cannot realize the displayed lower bound along a diverging sequence.
Writing \(L=\log\log(N/t)\), logarithms of the two bounds even give \(j\ge(1/3-o(1))L/\log L\) as \(N/t\to\infty\), since they imply \(L\le 3(j-1)\log(6(j-1))+O(1)\).
In particular a sufficient route to a uniform Lehmer gap is a bound \(A_j\le K^{j-1}\) with absolute \(K\) for all weights, intervals and nontorsion units, since this gives \(N/t\le\max(2,4K)\) for every witness.
The remaining covering question asks how these shapes meet each individual large exponent set.

<a id="dilations"></a>
## Removing common dilations when a real conjugate exists

Assume in addition that \(\alpha\) has a real conjugate \(r\).
For each minimal support normalized at zero, divide the exponents by their positive greatest common divisor and call the result its primitive shape.
A fixed primitive shape of weight \(j\) occurs with at most \((j-1)2^{j-1}\) distinct dilation factors at this input.
Indeed \(|r|\ne1\), and if the dilation is \(q\) then \(|r|^q\) is a positive zero of a polynomial on the primitive shape with all coefficients of absolute value one.
The possible signs of a negative real conjugate are absorbed into these coefficients.
There are at most \(2^{j-1}\) sign patterns after normalizing the constant, and any real polynomial with \(j\) nonzero monomials has at most \(j-1\) distinct positive zeros.
For the latter assertion divide by the least monomial, differentiate and apply Rolle's theorem inductively, starting with one nonzero monomial.
Distinct dilation factors give distinct positive numbers \(|r|^q\).
Thus at the weight given above there are at least \((N/(8t))^{j-1}/(j-1)\) distinct primitive shapes, including for every Salem input.
This excludes explaining the required proliferation on real-conjugate witnesses by translations and dilations of a bounded collection of primitive patterns.
