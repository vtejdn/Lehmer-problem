# A semicircle distribution of trace residues for the controlled delay-one inverse

<a id="residues"></a>
## Exact residues and branch index

Let \(1<\sigma\le2\) be a Salem number of degree \(d=2m\) with minimal polynomial \(P=z^m S(x)\), \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
Write \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), let \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) be the circle traces and put \(x_m=T\).
Use the [controlled Pisot inverse](Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#seed) with \(D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\),
\[
     g(x)=(x+2)(D_k(x)-2),\qquad p(x)\equiv g(x)S'(x)\pmod {S(x)},\qquad\deg p<m .
\]
The monic seed \(f=P-z^{m-1}(z-1)p(x)\) has \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\) and constant \(q=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^m g(x_\ell)\).
Here \(k\) is chosen with \(\sigma^k\) sufficiently large compared with \(d\) as in that inverse.
Its [interlacing rational trace](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) and [branch index](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) are
\[
   \mathcal R(x)=1-q+(x-2)\frac{p(x)}{S(x)}
        =\sum_{\ell=1}^m\frac{w_\ell}{x-x_\ell},
   \qquad
   j=\sum_{\ell=1}^m w_\ell,\qquad
   w_\ell=(x_\ell^2-4)(D_k(x_\ell)-2)>0 .
\]
Indeed \((1-z^2)f/(zf+f^*)-(q-z)=1-q+(x-2)p/S\), and the simple-pole residues of \(p/S\) are \(g(x_\ell)\).
The coefficient of \(1/x\) in \(\mathcal R\) gives \(j\).
No circle conjugate is a root of unity, so each interior weight is strictly positive.

<a id="limit"></a>
## A prescribed branch-to-degree ratio for every hypothetical Salem sequence

Fix any real \(\lambda\ge0\).
For every sequence of Salem numbers \(\sigma\to1\) one can choose actual seeds above, of the same degrees \(d\), with root \(\theta\), such that
\[
 \frac qd\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \theta-q\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \frac{q(T-2)}d\longrightarrow\lambda,\qquad
 \frac jd\longrightarrow 2+\lambda .
\]
Moreover the finite positive measures \(d^{-1}\sum_\ell w_\ell\delta_{x_\ell}\) converge weakly to \(2\mu_{\rm sc}+\lambda\delta_2\), where
\[
             d\mu_{\rm sc}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2\pi}\,dx\qquad (-2<x<2).
\]
For \(\lambda=0\) the normalized residue probabilities \(j^{-1}\sum w_\ell\delta_{x_\ell}\) thus have the semicircle limit, with vanishing exterior mass.

First \(d\to\infty\) by finiteness at bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure.
For use in choosing \(k\), write \(u=T-2\).
Since \(S(2)\) is a nonzero integer, \(\log(1/u)\le\sum_{\ell<m}\log(2-x_\ell)\).
The conjugates on the circle equidistribute for \(\sigma\to1\), by [Bilu's theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) in dimension one.
Indeed the height is \(\log(\sigma)/d\to0\), and these positive numbers are nontorsion.
The circle traces then have the arcsine limit \(\rho(dx)=dx/(\pi\sqrt{4-x^2})\).
Truncating the logarithm from below gives
\(\limsup d^{-1}\sum_{\ell<m}\log(2-x_\ell)\le\tfrac12\int\log(2-x)\,d\rho=0\).
The last integral is the circle mean of \(\log|1-e^{it}|^2\).
Thus \(\log(1/u)=o(d)\).

For \(\lambda>0\) choose \(k\) so that \(\sigma^k\sim\lambda d/(4u)\).
For \(\lambda=0\) take \(\sigma^k\sim d L\), where \(L=\min(\log(d+2),u^{-1/2})\).
In each case the least power above the target suffices since \(\sigma\to1\).
Then \(k\to\infty,\ \sigma^k/d\to\infty,\ k\log\sigma=o(d)\).
The trace construction gives \(q=(T+2)(\sigma^k+\sigma^{-k}-2)+O(d)\), since \(|g(x_\ell)|\le16\) at each circle trace.
It follows that \(q\sim4\sigma^k\) and \(qu/d\to\lambda\).
The [uniform excess bound](Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#cost) for the constructed seeds gives \(\theta-q=u+O(d/q)\to0\).

For the joint pair \((\sigma,\sigma^k)\) the sum of its coordinate heights is \((1+k)\log(\sigma)/d\to0\).
For each fixed nonzero integer pair \((r,s)\), the equation \(\sigma^{r+sk}=1\) fails eventually because \(k\to\infty\).
This verifies strictness in the fixed two-dimensional torus even though each individual pair has a multiplicative relation.
Hence Bilu's theorem gives independent Haar phases in the limit for the two coordinates of the Galois orbit.
The orbit has \(d\) points since the first coordinate is \(\sigma\).
Using bounded continuous functions of the phases, discard the two real images of mass \(2/d\).
On the remaining images the two traces are \(x_\ell\) and \(D_k(x_\ell)\), counted twice.
For any bounded continuous test \(\varphi\), this gives
\[
 \frac1d\sum_{\ell<m} w_\ell\varphi(x_\ell)
      \longrightarrow
      \frac12\iint(4-x^2)(2-y)\varphi(x)\,d\rho(x)d\rho(y)
      =2\int\varphi\,d\mu_{\rm sc}.
\]
At the exterior trace the weight is \(u g(T)\); its ratio to \(d\) tends to \(\lambda\) since \(g(T)=q+O(d)\), \(u\to0\).
Taking \(\varphi=1\) also gives the asserted branch ratio.

Conversely, any such sequence of positive-constant Pisot seeds with \(\theta-q\to0\) supplies Salem numbers tending to one by the [positive-constant equivalence](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#statement).
Thus for testing the existence of a Salem sequence one can impose, in particular, the single ratio \(j/d\to2\) and the semicircle residue law simultaneously.
This conclusion concerns the Salem part of the unrestricted Lehmer problem.
