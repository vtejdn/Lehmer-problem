# The second harmonic excludes two limiting Chebyshev profiles

<a id="exclusions"></a>
## The two profiles with an endpoint factor

Take the [inverse construction](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) with \(C_h(x)=2U_h(x/2)-U_{h-1}(x/2)\).
Of the [three possible limits](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#exclusions) of the normalized Pisot series \(K_q\) for \(q\to\infty\), neither \((2-z)/(1-z)\) nor \((2-z)(1+z)\) can occur.
The argument compares the first and second Fourier coefficients of a positive measure on the circle conditional on its first coordinate.

<a id="harmonics"></a>
## Integer coefficient windows at each positive multiple of the first discrepancy

Keep the [first discrepancy index](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) \(n\), so \(n\to\infty\) and \(n/h\to0\).
Put \(B^\sharp=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})\), \(B_\infty=(2-z)/K\), \(J_q=2/K_q\) and \(J=2/K=B_\infty/(1-z/2)\).
We have \(B^\sharp=(1-z/2)J_q+O(z^{2h+1})\), and both \(B^\sharp\) and \(B_\infty\) have integer Taylor coefficients.
For each fixed \(k\ge1\) and \(l\in\mathbb Z\), the index \(kn+l\) lies in this Taylor range for all sufficiently large inputs.

Use the positive two-coordinate limiting measure \(\Omega\) of the [first discrepancy argument](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#departure), with coordinates \((v,u)=(v,v^n)\) before passage to the limit.
Its first marginal \(\tau\) has continuous density \(\sigma(v)=2\Re J(v)\) relative to normalized arc length and possibly a finite atomic part.
Pass to the subsequence there with \(v^n\) constant at each torsion atom \(v\) of \(\tau\), writing \(\xi_v\) for its value.
That argument proves that the atom at \(v\) of \(\int u\,d\Omega(v,u)\) equals \(\xi_v\tau(\{v\})\).
Its modulus equals the whole fiber mass, so \(u=\xi_v\) almost surely over that atom, by integrating \(|u-\xi_v|^2\).
Hence the atom of \(\int u^k\,d\Omega(v,u)\) equals \(\xi_v^k\tau(\{v\})\) for every \(k\).

After reflecting \(v\) in the circle as in the first discrepancy argument, disintegrate the continuous part of \(\Omega\) over the marginal \(\sigma(v)\,dm(v)\), where \(m\) is normalized arc length.
Write \(\lambda_v\) for its conditional probability measure on the \(u\) circle, \(c_k(v)=\int u^k\,d\lambda_v\) and \(F_k=\sigma c_k\).
With the Fourier convention \(F_k(v)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb Z}\widehat F_k(l)v^l\), one has
\[
           \widehat F_k(l)=\lim_q [z^{kn+l}](J_q-J).
\]
Indeed the negative mass removed from the signed measure of \(J_q\) tends to zero even at the moving indices.
The continuous part of \(\tau\) has coefficients tending to zero there, and the atomic contributions just computed equal those from \(J\).
The resulting identity is the weak convergence test with character \(v^l u^k\) before reflection.
This proves that every Fourier coefficient of \(H_k(v)=(1-v/2)F_k(v)\) is the limit of the corresponding integer coefficient difference in \(B^\sharp-B_\infty\).
The continuous rational density \(\sigma\) is bounded in all three profiles once its separate atoms are removed.
Since \(|c_k|\le1\), Parseval gives square summability of the integer coefficients of \(H_k\), so \(H_k\) is a Laurent polynomial.
It satisfies \(|H_k(v)|\le |1-v/2|\sigma(v)\) away from the finite exceptional points and hence everywhere by continuity.
The first discrepancy gives \(H_1\ne0\).

When \(H_k\ne0\), shifting its smallest exponent to zero preserves this bound.
The [polynomial classification](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#shapes) therefore applies up to an arbitrary integral monomial factor to every \(H_k\), including \(k=2\).
The continuous representatives of the resulting \(c_k=H_k/[(1-v/2)\sigma]\) satisfy
\[
                  |c_2-c_1^2|\le1-|c_1|^2 .                         \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(\int(u-c_1)^2\,d\lambda_v=c_2-c_1^2\), and \(\int|u-c_1|^2\,d\lambda_v=1-|c_1|^2\).
The inequality holds almost everywhere and extends wherever these expressions are continuous.

<a id="slopes"></a>
## The order of contact at the endpoint

For \(K=(2-z)/(1-z)\), the density is \(\sigma=3|1-v|^2/(2|1-v/2|^2)\).
Every nonzero \(H_k\) has form \(\pm v^{d_k}(1-v)^2\), so every nonzero \(c_k\) is
\[
                  c_k(v)=\pm v^{d_k}\,(1-2v)/3 .
\]
In particular \(|c_1(-1)|=1\).
Inequality (1) forces \(c_2(-1)=c_1(-1)^2\), so \(c_2\) is nonzero.
For \(v=-e^{it}\), the right side of (1) vanishes to order two at \(t=0\).
Consequently the angular phase derivatives of \(c_2\) and \(c_1^2\) agree there.
The phase derivative of \((1+2e^{it})/3\) at zero is \(2/3\), which would give \(d_2+2/3=2d_1+4/3\), contradicting integrality.

For \(K=(2-z)(1+z)\), away from the separate atom of \(J\) at \(-1\), the continuous density is \(\sigma=(3-x)/(2|1-v/2|^2)\), \(x=v+v^{-1}\).
Every nonzero \(H_k=\pm v^{d_k}\) gives
\[
                 c_k(v)=\pm v^{d_k}\frac{2-v^{-1}}{3-x}.
\]
Here \(|c_1(1)|=1\), so \(c_2\) is nonzero and agrees with \(c_1^2\) at one.
For \(v=e^{it}\) the exact variance is
\[
               1-|c_1|^2=\frac{(2-x)^2}{(3-x)^2}=O(t^4).
\]
Thus (1) forces the phases of \(c_2\) and \(c_1^2\), normalized to agree at zero, to agree through degree three in \(t\).
The real denominator \(3-x\) is positive, and Taylor expansion gives \(\arg(2-e^{-it})=t-t^3+O(t^5)\).
A monomial changes only the linear term, so the cubic terms would be \(-1\) and \(-2\), respectively.
This contradiction excludes the second profile.
