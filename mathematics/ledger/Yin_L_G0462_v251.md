# The coefficient cost of one repeated factor at several nearby powers

<a id="common"></a>
## A fixed number of exponents with ratios tending to one

Fix a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(e\) with monic minimum \(P\), conjugates \(x_i\), log moduli \(\ell_i\), log measure \(h\), and \(D=\sum_{i<j}|\ell_i-\ell_j|\).
Let \(q_1,\ldots,q_r\) be distinct positive integers, with \(r\) fixed, \(p=q_1\to\infty\) and \(\Delta=\max_l|q_l-p|=o(p)\).
Suppose the same integer pair \(U,V\) satisfies \(P^2\mid U(X^{q_l})+XV(X^{q_l})\) for every \(l\), with \(V(x_1^{q_l})\ne0\).
Write \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) and let \(b\ge2r\) bound both row degrees.
Then
\[
    \log H\ \ge\ \frac{p}{e}\{2rD-(b-2r)h\}-o_{P,r}(p).
\]
Here the error along each such sequence is uniform over the rows and \(b\).
At reciprocal Salem seeds its rate per sum of exponents, for fixed \(b\), is at least \((4e-2-b/r)h/e\).
Thus the relevant width for this bound is the width per exponent.

<a id="finite"></a>
## Finite-place contributions from each exponent

In the fixed splitting field \(L\) put \(z=x_i^p\).
The graph recovers \(x_j\) from \(x_j^{q_l}\) at each exponent, so the powered values at that exponent are all distinct.
Values at different exponents also differ, since the positive absolute logarithmic height of \(x_j^q\) is \(qh/e\).
For \(j\ne i\) and \(q=q_l\), put \(R_{jq}=\nu(z-x_j^q)\) at a finite place with integral valuation \(\nu\).
The [local inverse argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#contact) applies with the jet at \(x_j^q\) and the unchanged value \(U(z)=-x_iV(z)\).
It gives \(\nu V(z)\ge2R_{jq}-2(\nu q+\nu(x_i-x_j))\).
More generally multiplicity \(m\) gives \(mR_{jq}-m\{\nu(q^{m-1}(m-1)!)+\nu(x_i-x_j)\}\).
The total normalized cost of these fixed-many error terms is \(O_{P,m,r}(\log(2p))\).

Two conjugates are in the same class here when their ratio is a root of unity.
If \(x_j\) shares the class of \(x_i\), the normalized sum of \(R_{jq}\) is \(O_P(1+\Delta)\): their ratio after powering is a root of unity times \(x_j^{p-q}\), and its difference from one is a nonzero algebraic integer of logarithmic height \(O_P(1+\Delta)\).
Among the remaining terms the overlap of two valuations is bounded by the valuation of the difference of the two comparison nodes.
When those nodes have bases in the same class, the same argument bounds its normalized sum by \(O_P(1+\Delta)\), including two exponents at the very same base.
For three distinct classes, the vectors \(v_j=(\log|\sigma x_j|)_\sigma\) for embeddings of \(L\) form three distinct points on a sphere, as in the [one-exponent argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#overlap).
The vectors \(qv_j/p-v_i,\ q'v_k/p-v_i\) tend to linearly independent limits.
Hence the unit ratios \(x_j^q/z,x_k^{q'}/z\) are eventually multiplicatively independent, and their heights are of order \(p\).
The [gcd bound for independent unit differences](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) makes the normalized overlap \(o(p)\).
Taking the maximum over all contact inequalities at each place therefore permits adding all \(R_{jq}\) at total loss \(o(p)\).

For each fixed \(q\) among these exponents, the sum over \(j\ne i\) and finite places, with weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\), is \(pD/e+O_P(\Delta+\log(2p))\).
Indeed the product formula transfers each integral difference to the archimedean embeddings.
For unequal log moduli its logarithm is \(p\max(\ell_i,\ell_j)+O_P(1+\Delta)\).
For equal nonzero log moduli and distinct exponents the ratio of the larger modulus to the smaller one is at least \(e^{|\ell_i|}\), giving the same estimate.
For equal exponents use the [powered difference estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#overlap).
For two circle conjugates at distinct exponents the difference is nonzero, and the [logarithmic form bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) applied to \(p\log x_i-q\log x_j-2k\log(-1)\) contributes \(O_P(\log(2p))\) with the nearest argument and \(|k|=O_P(p)\).
Averaging over all embeddings sends \(i\) uniformly to each index and sums over all other partners, giving \(2\sum_{i<j}\max(\ell_i,\ell_j)/e=D/e\).
Consequently at multiplicity \(m\) the normalized log norm of \(V(z)\) is at least \(mrpD/e-o(p)\).

<a id="derivative"></a>
## Saving one layer for each common exponent at both ends

Fix an exterior \(x=x_i\) and let \(f(T)=T^b(U(1/T)+xV(1/T))\).
Its one-norm is at most \((1+|x|)H\), and it vanishes at all \(t_l=x^{-q_l}\).
On the unit circle every factor \(T-t_l\) has modulus at least \(1/2\) for large \(p\).
By the maximum principle, \(f(T)/\prod_l(T-t_l)\) has modulus \(O_{P,r}(H)\) throughout the closed disk.
At \(t_1\) it follows that
\[
 |t_1 f'(t_1)|\ll_{P,r} H\,|x|^{-rp+O_r(\Delta)}.
\]
Indeed \(|t_1-t_l|\le2|x|^{-p+\Delta}\).
The double-root equation gives \(xV(x^p)=-p x^p(U'+xV')(x^p)\).
At the zero of \(f\) this bounds \(|V(x^p)|\) by \(O_{P,r}(pH)|x|^{(b-r)p+O_r(\Delta)}\).
For an interior \(x\) apply the same argument directly to \(U(Y)+xV(Y)\) with the disk zeros \(x^{q_l}\), obtaining \(|V(x^p)|\ll_{P,r}pH|x|^{rp-O_r(\Delta)}\).
The circle bound is \(H\).
Multiplying over the embeddings gives normalized log norm at most \(\log H+(b-2r)ph/e+O_{P,r}(\Delta+\log(2p))\), proving the assertion.

<a id="seven"></a>
## Exclusion with row degree seven per exponent

For fixed \(r\), in the [common-prime coefficient test](Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) impose that the same reciprocal irreducible seed qualifies at all \(r\) nearby odd primes.
At every sufficiently late tuple no such genuine pair of row degree at most \(7r\) has \(H\le2^{\sum_l q_l}\).
Indeed the [distinct tangencies](Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) imply \(re\le b\) and \(h\sum_l q_l\le\log H\).
Hence \(e\le7\) and \(M(P)\le2\).
Pad to \(b=7r\) and apply the bound above to the finite [quartic and sextic classification](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v412.md#seeds).
After division by \(rp\), the strict lower rates are the one-exponent bounds at width seven, whereas \(\sum_l q_l/(rp)\to1\).
The conclusion includes equality at the stated cutoff.
With different seeds at different exponents, the common zeros of \(U+xV\) used in the disk argument are no longer available in general.
The [zero-gap criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap) ultimately needs unbounded width per prime.
