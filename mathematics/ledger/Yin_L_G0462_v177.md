# Integral power coordinates and independent alternating actions across cubic factors

<a id="orders"></a>
## Powering preserves the full integral order

Put \(g(T)=T^3-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\), \(f=-g/B\), \(A(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) for coprime positive \(a,b\), and \(D(T)=T^bB(T)^a-(-g(T))^a\).
The [bijection of complex roots](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#projection) comes from an isomorphism of the full integral polynomial algebras
\[
              \mathbb Z[T]/(D)\simeq\mathbb Z[X]/(A),\qquad T\longmapsto X^a.
\]
Both distinguished coordinates are units since the defining polynomials have unit leading and constant coefficients.
In the left algebra \(B(T)\) is a unit because modulo \(B(T)\) the identity \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\) makes \(g\) a unit, whereas the equation \(D=0\) gives \(g^a=0\) there.
Consequently \(y=f(T)\) belongs to that algebra and \(y^a=T^b\) makes \(y\) a unit.
For integers \(u,v\) with \(au+bv=1\), the element \(X_0=T^u y^v\) satisfies \(X_0^a=T\) and \(X_0^b=y\).
It annihilates \(A\), and substitution in either direction gives the identity.
This argument includes nonreduced fibers and generalizes the [single-factor order calculation for \(b=1\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#seed).

In particular \(\mathbb Z[\alpha^j]=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) for every root \(\alpha\) of \(A\) and every positive divisor \(j\) of \(a\).
For distinct roots \(\alpha,\beta\), the inverse integral polynomial \(X=W(T)\) shows \((\alpha-\beta)/(\alpha^a-\beta^a)\) is integral.
Its reciprocal is integral by the difference-of-powers identity, hence is a unit.
Each \(\alpha-\zeta\beta\) is therefore an algebraic unit for every nonidentity \(\zeta^a=1\), by factoring the difference of powers.
Taking \(\beta=\alpha^{-1}\) for any nontorsion \(\alpha\) gives \((\alpha^{2a}-1)/(\alpha^2-1)\) a unit.
Thus each nontorsion irreducible factor \(P\) satisfies \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|=1\) for \(j\mid 2a,\ j>2\).

<a id="independence"></a>
## Multiplicative independence across rational factors

Let \(N=\max(a,b)\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\), two nontorsion roots belonging to distinct monic rational irreducible factors are multiplicatively independent.
In fact the following proof excludes every pair of nontorsion roots \(\alpha,\beta\) other than equal or inverse roots.
Write \(F(U,V)=g(U)+VB(U)\), \(L=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), and take \(N\) large enough for the [torsion-quotient criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement).
The graph is absolutely irreducible, since \(g,B\) are coprime, and its support differences generate \(\mathbb Z^2\).
Consequently distinct nontorsion roots have nontorsion quotient, so \(L(\alpha^j)=L(\alpha)\) and \(L(\beta^j)=L(\beta)\) for every \(j\ge1\).
Both heights are \(O(1/N)\) by the [primitive-direction estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement).

Suppose a multiplicative dependence holds.
Replacing \(\beta\) by its inverse if needed preserves the root set by reciprocity of \(A\).
There are then coprime positive integers \(s,t\) with \(\alpha^t/\beta^s\) torsion.
Choose \(i,j\) with \(is+jt=1\) and set \(\gamma=\alpha^i\beta^j\).
There are roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\) with \(\alpha=\xi\gamma^s\) and \(\beta=\eta\gamma^t\), by taking classes modulo roots of unity.
The equality of the powered fields gives \(L(\alpha)=L(\beta)\), so \(\gamma\) lies in that field of degree at most \(3a+b\le4N\) over \(L\).
The [relative abelian height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) gives \(h(\gamma)\gg1/(N(\log(2N))^4)\).
Since \(s h(\gamma)=h(\alpha)\) and \(t h(\gamma)=h(\beta)\), both \(s,t\) are \(O((\log(2N))^4)\).
The same height bound at \(\alpha\) gives \([L(\alpha):L]\gg N/(\log(2N))^4\).

For any integer \(q\ge1\) and nonzero constants \(c,e\), the curve \(F(cU^q,eV^q)=0\) is absolutely irreducible over \(\mathbb C\).
Indeed \(g(cU^q)\) has a simple nonzero zero disjoint from the zeros of \(B(cU^q)\).
At that zero the polynomial in \(V\) is Eisenstein, and its coprime end coefficients make it primitive over \(\mathbb C[U]\).
If \(s\ne t\), the curves with \((q,c,e)=(s,\xi^a,\xi^b)\) and \((t,\eta^a,\eta^b)\) have distinct \(V\)-degrees.
They are consequently coprime, with total degrees \(4s,4t\).
Their intersection contains \((\gamma^a,\gamma^b)\), a point generating \(L(\gamma)=L(\alpha)\) over \(L\), since \((a,b)=1\) and \(\alpha=\xi\gamma^s\).
All conjugate points over \(L\) remain in the intersection, so Bézout gives \([L(\alpha):L]\le16st\ll(\log(2N))^8\), contradicting the lower bound.
The alternative \(s=t\) gives \(s=t=1\), and the absence of distinct torsion twists yields \(\alpha=\beta\) after the possible inversion.

The [two-coordinate height product bound](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) now implies that for every pair of nontorsion roots with \(\beta\notin\{\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\}\),
\[
       [\mathbb Q(\alpha,\beta):\mathbb Q]\gg N^2/(\log(2N))^{212}.
\]
Here \(2\kappa(2)=212\), the joint degree is at most \((4N)^2\), and \(h(\alpha)h(\beta)\ll N^{-2}\).

## Independent actions on all trace polynomials

For \(N\) sufficiently large, let \(P_i\) be the distinct nontorsion rational factors of \(A\), and put \(n_i=\deg(P_i)/2\).
The [finite-inertia argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#pairs) gives \(A_{n_i}\) or \(S_{n_i}\) on the inverse pairs of each factor, and \(\min_i n_i\to\infty\) over nonempty collections.
For the compositum \(T\) of the rational splitting fields of all the corresponding trace polynomials,
\[
           {\rm Gal}(T\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}/\mathbb Q^{\rm ab})
                    =\prod_i A_{n_i}
\]
in those natural permutation actions.
Indeed \(G={\rm Gal}(T/\mathbb Q)\subseteq\prod_i S_{n_i}\), and the commutator \(G'\) projects onto each \(A_{n_i}\).
By the [subdirect product argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v268.md#normal), failure of equality with the full product would identify two isomorphic simple factors diagonally.
They have the same \(n\) by their orders.
Every automorphism of \(A_n\) is induced by relabeling for \(n\ge9\). [Natural alternating actions](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition)
After such a relabeling the two-coordinate projection of \(G'\) is the ordinary diagonal.
Since \(G\) normalizes \(G'\), its two-coordinate projection lies in the normalizer of that diagonal in \(S_n^2\), which is the diagonal \(S_n\) because the centralizer of \(A_n\) in \(S_n\) is trivial.
Corresponding trace roots thus have the same stabilizer in \(G\) and generate the same number field of degree \(n\).
Choosing a root of each original factor above these traces gives \([\mathbb Q(\alpha,\beta):\mathbb Q]\le4n=O(N)\), contrary to the joint-degree lower bound.
Hence \(G'=\prod_i A_{n_i}\), and passing to the maximal abelian base gives the displayed group.

In particular any proposed small part \(k_P=o(a)\) in the [escaping partition](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#small-parts) must have a full alternating trace action independent of those of the other rational factors.
These restrictions apply whether or not the proposed small parts exist.
