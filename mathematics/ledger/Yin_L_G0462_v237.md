# Cubic divisibility at the endpoints of the compressed two-jet rows

<a id="cubic"></a>
## A value constraint from the derivative of the second row

Use the [integer test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#statement) with its offset one, or the same seed equations at any odd prime \(p\) preserving the degree of the nontorsion reciprocal unit \(\alpha\).
Write \(P_0=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\alpha)\), \(e=\deg P_0=2m\), \(h=m(P_0)\), \(q=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(z=\alpha^p\), \(s=z+z^{-1}\) and \(T=\min_{\mathbb Q}(s)\).
The orders \(S=\mathbb Z[s]\subset R=\mathbb Z[q]\) have fraction field \(K^+=\mathbb Q(q)\).
Set \(D=s^2-4\), \(\delta=z-z^{-1}\), and write \(W=W_p(q)\), \(W'=W_p'(q)\), where \(W_j(q)=(\alpha^j-\alpha^{-j})/(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\) is the integral recurrence polynomial from [the value projection](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#value).
Then \(D=(q^2-4)W^2\).
With \(\mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\), the compressed equations on \(f=Y^{-2e}a,\ g=Y^{-2e}b\) are
\[
 (f(z),(\mathcal E f)(z))=(u,l),\qquad
 (g(z),(\mathcal E g)(z))=\alpha^{-1}(-u,l+u/p),\qquad u,l\in K^+.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The other signed convention follows by negating \(g\).

The [powered trace identity](Yin_L_G0462_v233.md#different) gives \(2l=DT'(s)c\) with \(c\in S\).
Expand \(g(Y)=A(t)+YB(t)\), \(t=Y+Y^{-1}\), with \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[t]\), using \(Y^{-1}=t-Y,\ Y^2=tY-1\).
Since \(\alpha^{-1}=(W_{p+1}(q)-z)/W\), equality of the \(z\)-coordinates of the values gives \(B(s)=u/W\).
Apply the sum of the identity and inverse involutions to \((\mathcal E g)(z)\).
The term \(\delta A'(s)\) cancels, the term \(zB(s)\) contributes \(sB(s)\), and \(z\delta B'(s)\) contributes \(DB'(s)\).
The prescribed derivative thus gives
\[
     DB'(s)+s u/W=q(l+u/p).
\]
Differentiating the Laurent identity defining \(W_p\) gives \((q^2-4)W'=ps-qW\).
Combining these equalities yields the integral identity and its consequence
\[
  2 W'u=pW^3\{qT'(s)c-2B'(s)\},\qquad
                   u\in W^3R[1/(2p)].                                \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \((q^2-4)sW'\equiv4p\bmod W\), by \(s^2\equiv4\bmod W\).
Thus \(W'\) is invertible modulo \(W^3\) after inverting \(2p\).
In particular a nonzero determinant \(\Delta=u_1l_2-u_2l_1\) of two columns belongs to both fractional lattices
\[
           2\Delta\in WD T'(s)\,S,\qquad
             \Delta\in W^3D T'(s)\,R[1/(2p)].                         \tag{3}
\]
The first inclusion uses \(u_i/W\in S,\ 2l_i/(DT'(s))\in S\), and the second uses (2).

## The exact determinant ideal away from the powered trace discriminant

Let \(\lambda\) be a rational prime not dividing \(2p\operatorname{disc}T\), and put \(S_\lambda=S\otimes\mathbb Z_\lambda\).
For the integral solution lattice \(\Lambda\) of columns \((u,l)\) in (1), put \(\Lambda_\lambda=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Z_\lambda\).
Then
\[
         \Lambda_\lambda=W^3 S_\lambda\times D S_\lambda,\qquad
         \big(\det(v_1,v_2):v_i\in\Lambda_\lambda\big)
                              =W^3D S_\lambda.                          \tag{4}
\]
Here \(S_\lambda=R\otimes\mathbb Z_\lambda\), since the squared index of the two trace orders divides \(\operatorname{disc}T\).
The two inclusions into the product in (4) follow from (2) and \(2l=DT'(s)c\).

For the reverse inclusion, any prescribed value and ordinary derivative in \(S_\lambda^2\) can be attained at \(s\) by a polynomial in \(\mathbb Z_\lambda[t]\).
First lift the value, then add \(T\) times a polynomial to adjust the derivative by an arbitrary multiple of the unit \(T'(s)\).
Use the local Laurent representation \(H_0(t)+(Y-Y^{-1})H_1(t)\), permissible since \(2\) is a unit.
Its value and Euler derivative at \(z\) are
\[
 H_0(s)+\delta H_1(s),\qquad
 \delta H_0'(s)+sH_1(s)+D H_1'(s).
\]
For \(f\) choose \(H_0(s)=u,H_1(s)=H_0'(s)=0,H_1'(s)=l/D\).
For \(g\) put \(v=l+u/p\) and use \(\alpha^{-1}=q/2-\delta/(2W)\).
The required two values and their two ordinary derivatives are
\[
   -qu/2,\quad u/(2W),\quad -v/(2W),\quad
                         (qv-su/W)/(2D),
\]
in the order \(H_0(s),H_1(s),H_0'(s),H_1'(s)\).
For \(u\in W^3 S_\lambda,\ l\in D S_\lambda\), all four belong to \(S_\lambda\), since \(D=(q^2-4)W^2\) and \(qu/p-su/W=-(q^2-4)W'u/(pW)\).
This constructs both local jets.
The [integral support reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#jets) works over \(\mathbb Z_\lambda\) as well.
With these bounded representatives the defining equations are a finite system of rational linear equations in integral coefficients.
Clearing denominators and using flatness of \(\mathbb Z_\lambda\) identifies the local columns with \(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Z_\lambda\).
The determinant assertion now follows from the two coordinate vectors of the product lattice.

In trace degree \(m\ge2\), imposing the test's nonzero values, irrational slopes, independent columns, primitive endpoints and seed coprimality leaves this local determinant ideal unchanged if coefficient heights are unrestricted.
Indeed \(\Lambda\) spans \((K^+)^2\) over \(\mathbb Q\), because the [jet image test](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#jets) can be met for any rational pair after clearing denominators.
Choose integral columns approximating the two local coordinate vectors closely enough that their determinant generates the ideal in (4) in every component of the product of discrete valuation rings \(S_\lambda\).
One may vary each column in a full-rank congruence sublattice while preserving this property.
These arithmetic cosets are Zariski dense in rational coordinates, so one can exclude zero values and proportionality of the two rational coordinate lists of \(u_i,l_i\) for each individual column.
The [unit endpoint completion](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#endpoints) on the seed side retains each such jet and avoids the zeros of the reversed second row.
It requires only \(u_i\ne0\) for this seed gcd and no degree enlargement.
Indeed the seed graph divided by \(x^{2ep}\) is \(f(x^p)+x^{-1}g(x^{-p})\), and (1) and its inverse conjugate give value and Euler derivative zero at \(\alpha\).
Thus these normalized seed rows also retain \(P_0^2\).
Formula (4) establishes exactness at the specified primes independently of an archimedean coefficient budget.

## An integer cube dividing both evaluations at each sign

For \(\epsilon=\pm1\) and a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\), let \(B_\epsilon\) generate the contraction \((P_0(\epsilon\zeta))\mathbb Z[\zeta]\cap\mathbb Z\), with positive sign.
Let \(b_\epsilon\) be the largest divisor of \(B_\epsilon\) coprime to \(2p\operatorname{disc}P_0\).
Every integral row (1) satisfies
\[
         b_\epsilon^3\mid a(\epsilon),\qquad b_\epsilon^3\mid b(\epsilon).
                                                                         \tag{5}
\]
This finite test at \(p=p_e\) does not require an asymptotic assumption on the input, or good reduction for the powered trace polynomial at the auxiliary primes.

For \(\lambda\mid b_\epsilon\), the [contraction exponent](Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#contraction) gives \(k=v_\lambda(b_\epsilon)=\max_{1\le j<p} v_\lambda(P_0(\epsilon\zeta^j))\), using an extension with \(v_\lambda(\lambda)=1\).
At a maximizing node exactly one root of \(P_0\) reduces to the same residue, since \(\lambda\nmid\operatorname{disc}P_0\).
Work at the embedding which sends \(\alpha\) to this root.
The product over the other roots is a unit there, so \(v_\lambda(\alpha-\epsilon\zeta^j)=k\).
The coprimality of \(p\) to \(\lambda\) gives \(v_\lambda(z-\epsilon)=k\) by the binomial expansion at the unit \(\epsilon\zeta^j\).
The node has residue of order \(p\) or \(2p\), so \(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}\) is a unit.
Also \(v_\lambda(z-z^{-1})=k\) since \(\lambda\ne2\).
It follows that \(v_\lambda(W)=k\), \(v_\lambda(D)=2k\), and (2) gives \(v_\lambda(u)\ge3k,\ v_\lambda(l)\ge2k\).

For either Laurent polynomial \(L=f,g\), its values at \(z,z^{-1}\) thus have valuation at least \(3k\), and its ordinary derivatives there have valuation at least \(2k\), by (1), inversion and the unit denominators.
Multiplying \(L\) by \(Y^{2e}\) gives the same bounds for the resulting polynomial \(M=a,b\), by the product rule.
Expand \(M(Y)=\sum_j c_j(Y-z)^j\) with integral local coefficients.
We already have \(v_\lambda(c_0)\ge3k\) and \(v_\lambda(c_1)\ge2k\).
The evaluation at \(z^{-1}\), at distance of valuation \(k\), therefore gives \(v_\lambda(c_2)\ge k\), since all terms except possibly the quadratic have valuation at least \(3k\).
Evaluation at \(\epsilon\), also at distance of valuation \(k\), gives \(v_\lambda(M(\epsilon))\ge3k\).
This proves (5).

If \(\deg a\le4e,\ \deg b<4e\) and \(\gcd(a,Y^{4e-1}b(1/Y))=1\), the two integer evaluations at a fixed sign cannot both vanish.
Consequently the largest coefficient magnitude \(H_*\) of this individual row satisfies
\[
      (4e+1)H_*\ \ge\ \max(b_+^3,b_-^3).                              \tag{6}
\]
For fixed \(P_0\) and all sufficiently large primes \(p\), \(b_\epsilon=B_\epsilon\).
The prime \(p\) eventually avoids the fixed integer \(P_0(\epsilon)\ne0\), so it does not divide the cyclotomic norm.
For each of the finitely many other excluded primes \(\lambda\mid2\operatorname{disc}P_0\), the roots of \(P_0\bmod\lambda\) form a fixed finite set of elements of fixed multiplicative orders.
For \(p\ne\lambda\), a primitive \(p\)-th root retains its order on reduction.
Thus \(\epsilon\zeta^j\) cannot meet those roots at any sufficiently large prime \(p\).
The [fixed-seed trace contraction estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#fixed-input) gives \(\log B_\epsilon/(p-1)\to h/2\) at both signs.
It follows for every such compressed row that
\[
            \liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log H_*}{ph}\ge\frac32.         \tag{7}
\]
For the short [offset-one lifted witnesses](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#short) on representatives chosen at those primes, the [seed coefficient and cofactor comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v234.md#height) transfers (7) to the actual lifted noncyclotomic cofactor divided by \(ph\).
This gives a restriction on the individual compressed jets, already before the rank-two test.
The fixed-seed onset in (7) supplies no uniform contraction estimate for the changing degree-only primes and seeds of C406, and (4) leaves open any additional archimedean cost of rank two in that test.

## A nontrivial exact local factor at the prescribed prime of degree four

Take \(P_0=x^4-x^3-3x^2-x+1\), whose trace polynomial is \(q^2-q-5\).
Its roots are \((1\pm\sqrt{21})/2\), one in \((-2,2)\) and one in \((2,3)\).
The first real embedding shows that \(q^2-4\) is not a square in the quadratic trace field, so \(P_0\) is irreducible.
Its positive logarithmic measure is less than \(\log((3+\sqrt5)/2)<1\).
The test chooses \(p=4099\), the first prime above \(4096\).
At the auxiliary prime \(\lambda=6353449\), the two trace roots modulo \(\lambda\) are \(356245,5997205\), and their powered traces modulo \(\lambda\) are \(2,4977833\), respectively.
They are distinct, so \(\lambda\nmid\operatorname{disc}T\).
At the first trace root the lift modulo \(\lambda^2\) is \(a_0=15304423385058\) and \(W_p(a_0)\equiv479276\lambda\bmod\lambda^2\).
These integers can be checked by trial division of \(4099,\lambda\) through their square roots, by \(4097=17\cdot241\), by substituting the trace roots and the lift in \(q^2-q-5\), and by iterating \(v_{j+1}=qv_j-v_{j-1}\) with initial pairs \((2,q)\) for powered traces and \((0,1)\) for \(W_j\) in the respective residue rings.
In this component \(v_\lambda(W)=1,\ v_\lambda(D)=2\), so every independent pair pays at least five powers of \(\lambda\) in its determinant, and (4) attains exactly five locally.
At the same component the original trace root is different from \(\pm2\) and the powered trace is \(2\), so the reduction of \(\alpha\) has order \(p\).
The original polynomial has simple reduction, as its two distinct trace roots both avoid \(\pm2\).
The proof of (5) therefore gives the explicit divisibility \(\lambda^3\mid a(1),b(1)\) on every individual admissible row.
