# Only conjugates exactly on the circle require exceptional primitive orders

<a id="circle"></a>
## Primitive and full resultants in linear windows

Let \(P\) be an integer polynomial of degree \(d\), with no root of unity and with \(m=m(P)>0\) its logarithmic Mahler measure.
Write \(s\) for its number of **distinct** roots exactly on the unit circle and put \(D_j=|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|,\ C_j=|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^j-1)|\).
Fix \(A>0\), let \(Y\to\infty\) and suppose \(d\le AY\).
Uniformly over these inputs, outside at most \(s/2+o_A(Y)\) integer orders in \([Y,2Y]\),
\[
                     \log D_j=(1+o_A(1))\,\varphi(j)m.
\]
If \(s=o(Y)\), then on a set of relative density one in the same windows both \(\log D_j/(\varphi(j)m)\) and \(\log C_j/(jm)\) tend to one.
In particular this applies at \(Y=d\) to all nonreciprocal irreducible units of increasing degree, to reciprocal units having \(o(d)\) circle conjugates and to all polynomials having no circle roots.
Factors, multiplicities and nonunit leading coefficients are allowed in the general assertion.
The [previous linear-window estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#linear) charged every distinct nonreal pair, even if both roots lay strictly inside the circle.
For a Salem polynomial with \(d-2\) circle conjugates the new exceptional bound is still \((d-2)/2+o_A(Y)\).

## A discriminant bound on the radial distance deficit

Set \(t=1-(2Y)^{-6}\), \(L=\log(2Y)\), and for a root \(\alpha\) put \(A_\alpha=\max(1,|\alpha|)\), \(w_\alpha=\alpha/A_\alpha^2\) and \(r_\alpha=|w_\alpha|\).
For \(0\le r<1\) write
\[
                  H_t(r)=\log\frac{1-tr^2}{1-r^2}\ \ge0 .
\]
We first claim the following estimate summed with root multiplicity:
\[
                 \sum_{|\alpha|\ne1} H_t(r_\alpha)
                       \ll_A d\,(m+L).                                  \tag{1}
\]
It suffices to treat a primitive irreducible factor \(F\ne z\) of degree \(D\).
Complete its nonzero root set under the involution \(\alpha\mapsto1/\bar\alpha\) by forming the primitive squarefree product \(T\) of the distinct factors among \(F\) and its reciprocal \(F^*\), up to sign.
The roots of \(F\) are closed under conjugation, so this product has the required roots.
Its degree \(n\le2D\) and its measure \(m(T)\le2m(F)\), by invariance of Mahler measure under reversal.

For distinct roots \(\alpha,\beta\) of \(T\) put \(\delta_{\alpha\beta}=|\alpha-\beta|/(A_\alpha A_\beta)\).
The [reflected distance inequality](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#energy) gives
\[
       \log\frac{|1-tw_\alpha\bar w_\beta|}{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}
                       \ge \tfrac12\log t .
\]
At the ordered pair \((\alpha,1/\bar\alpha)\) for \(|\alpha|\ne1\), the two roots are distinct, the two reflected points coincide and \(\delta_{\alpha,1/\bar\alpha}=1-r_\alpha^2\).
Thus the displayed logarithm there equals \(H_t(r_\alpha)\).
These are distinct ordered pairs as \(\alpha\) varies.

The product defining the discriminant gives
\[
 -\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}\log\delta_{\alpha\beta}
     =2(n-1)m(T)-\log|\operatorname{disc}T|\le 2(n-1)m(T).
\]
The full smoothed energy \(-\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\log|1-tw_\alpha\bar w_\beta|\) is nonnegative by its squared Fourier norm.
Consequently the off-diagonal sum of the smoothed logarithms is at most \(-\sum_\alpha\log(1-tr_\alpha^2)\le n\log(1/(1-t))\).
Adding these two bounds and discarding the other off-diagonal pairs at cost at most \(-\tfrac12n^2\log t\) proves
\[
        \sum_{\alpha\text{ root of }T,\ |\alpha|\ne1}H_t(r_\alpha)
          \le 2(n-1)m(T)+n\log(1/(1-t))-\tfrac12n^2\log t.
\]
The summands are nonnegative, so this bounds the corresponding sum on \(F\).
Summing with the multiplicity of each \(F\) gives (1), since \(m(F)\le m\), the degrees counted with multiplicity sum to at most \(d\), \(d\le AY\), and a zero root has \(H_t(0)=0\).

If \(F\) is nonreciprocal and irreducible, a direct argument improves its bound to \(2D m(F)-\tfrac12D^2\log t\).
Indeed \(F\) and \(F^*\) are coprime, so \(F\) has no circle root.
For each ordered pair of roots of \(F\), the quantity \(|1-\alpha\bar\beta|/(A_\alpha A_\beta)\) equals \(|1-w_\alpha\bar w_\beta|\) when both are on the same side of the circle and equals \(|w_\alpha-w_\beta|\) otherwise.
In the latter case it is at most \(|1-w_\alpha\bar w_\beta|\).
Taking the product over all ordered pairs and using \(|\operatorname{Res}(F,F^*)|\ge1\) therefore gives
\[
       -\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\log |1-w_\alpha\bar w_\beta|
                    \le 2D m(F)-\log|\operatorname{Res}(F,F^*)|.
\]
The factors of the leading coefficient in the resultant cancel exactly against those in \(\exp(2D m(F))\).
Subtract the nonnegative full smoothed energy, then discard the off-diagonal differences, each bounded below by \(\tfrac12\log t\).
The remaining diagonal differences are \(H_t(r_\alpha)\), giving the sharper bound.

## Removing smoothing at all off-circle roots simultaneously

For each distinct circle root delete the order of a closest dyadic primitive node in angle, choosing conjugate nodes for conjugate roots.
There are no real circle roots, so this deletes at most \(s/2\) orders.
For every nonzero reflected point, including those off the circle, all nodes except one chosen closest have distance \(\gg Y^{-2}\) by [primitive-node separation](Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#composite).
There are \(O(Y^2)\) nodes altogether, and at each of these nonclosest nodes the positive part of
\[
                    \log\frac{|1-tw_\alpha\bar\zeta|}{|1-w_\alpha\bar\zeta|}
\]
is \(O((1-t)Y^2)\) by the triangle inequality.
The aggregate contribution of the nonclosest nodes is \(O(dY^{-2})\).

At a closest node for an off-circle root, the triangle inequality bounds this positive part by \(\log(1+(1-t)r/(1-r))\), where \(r=r_\alpha\).
For \(r<1/2\) this is \(O(1-t)\).
For \(r\ge1/2\) it is at most \(3H_t(r)\) since \((1+r)/r\le3\) and \(1+3x\le(1+x)^3\) for \(x\ge0\).
Thus the sum of positive losses over **all** remaining primitive nodes and roots, counting multiplicities, is \(O_A(d(m+L))\) by (1).

The [smoothed primitive and full estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#tests) remain valid outside \(o_A(Y)\) orders when \(d\le AY\).
Indeed the input energy estimate uses \(D\le AY\) only in \(D^2(1-t)=O_A(1)\) and in the [degree-dependent lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument) \(m(F)\gg_A L^{-3}\) for positive-measure factors.
The two test Gram matrices depend only on the dyadic window, and their exceptional-count bound \(O_A(dY^{-5/8}L^{10})\) is still \(o_A(Y)\).
The actual upper estimates follow as before from \(|1-w\bar\zeta|\le t^{-1/2}|1-tw\bar\zeta|\).

Outside \(O(Y/L)\) orders, \(\varphi(j)\ge Y/L\) by the bounded mean of \(j/\varphi(j)\).
The number of undeleted orders at which the total positive loss exceeds \(Y^{-1/8}(Y/L)m\) is
\[
             O_A\!\left(\frac{d(m+L)L}{Y^{7/8}m}\right)=o_A(Y),
\]
using \(m\gg_A L^{-3}\), including when a nontrivial content accounts for all the measure.
This proves the two-sided primitive estimate on the claimed set.

## Divisor transfer for the full return

If \(s=o(Y)\), the primitive estimate has density one also in each of the boundedly many dyadic windows covering \([eY,2Y]\) for fixed \(0<e<1/2\).
Their lower ends can be taken at least \(eY/2\), so the admissible degree constant is \(2A/e\), independent of the input.
Each exceptional divisor in that interval affects at most \(2/e\) indices of \([Y,2Y]\).
The [small-divisor totient estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#actual) bounds the total weight of divisors below \(eY\) by \(\sqrt e\,Y\) outside \(O(\sqrt e\,Y)\) indices.
All \(\log D_b\ge0\), and summing over the remaining large divisors in \(C_j=\prod_{b\mid j}D_b\) gives \(\log C_j/(jm)\ge1-\sqrt e-o_{A,e}(1)\) outside \(O(\sqrt e\,Y)+o_{A,e}(Y)\) indices.
Taking \(e\downarrow0\) and using the density-one full upper estimate proves convergence in density.
