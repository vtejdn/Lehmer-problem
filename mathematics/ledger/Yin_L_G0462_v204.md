# Salem outputs in a fixed trace field and the cost of an integral trace

<a id="fields"></a>
## A square-class test and the degree of an independent output

Let \(\sigma,\tau>1\) be Salem numbers, \(T_x=x+x^{-1}\), \(d=\deg\sigma\), \(K=\mathbb Q(T_\sigma)\), and suppose \(T_\tau\in K\).
Set \(h=[K:\mathbb Q(T_\tau)]\), \(a=\log\sigma\), and \(b=\log\tau\).
Thus \([K:\mathbb Q]=d/2\) and \(\deg\tau=d/h\).
The following alternatives hold at every degree.

* If \((T_\tau^2-4)/(T_\sigma^2-4)\) is a square in \(K\), then \(h=1\) and \(\sigma=\eta^u,\ \tau=\eta^v\) for positive integers \(u,v\), where \(\eta\) is the smallest Salem number in \(\mathbb Q(\sigma)\).
* Otherwise \(\sigma,\tau\) are multiplicatively independent, \([\mathbb Q(\sigma,\tau):\mathbb Q]=2d\), and for an absolute \(c>0\)
\[
              hab\ge\frac{c d}{(\log(6d))^{212}},\qquad
              \deg\tau\le c^{-1}ab(\log(6d))^{212}.                     \tag{1}
\]

Indeed \(K\) is totally real and neither Salem number lies in a totally real field, since each has nonreal conjugates.
The quadratic extensions \(K(\sigma)\) and \(K(\tau)\) are obtained by adjoining the square roots of \(T_\sigma^2-4\) and \(T_\tau^2-4\).
They coincide exactly under the square-class condition.
In that case \(\tau\in\mathbb Q(\sigma)\), so the [fixed-field Salem generator theorem](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U001.md#smy15-u001) gives the stated positive powers and the equality \(\mathbb Q(\tau)=\mathbb Q(\sigma)\).
In particular \(h=1\).
Conversely, a multiplicative relation between the two positive numbers greater than one gives \(\sigma^i=\tau^j\) for some \(i,j>0\).
Positive powers preserve each Salem field by the same theorem, proving equality of the fields and hence of the quadratic extensions.
When \(h>1\), the square-class obstruction is also visible at the \(h-1\) other real embeddings of \(K\) extending the distinguished embedding of \(\mathbb Q(T_\tau)\).
At each of these \(T_\tau\) stays above two and \(T_\sigma\) maps into \((-2,2)\), so the ratio is negative.

For distinct quadratic extensions their compositum over \(K\) has degree four, and it is \(\mathbb Q(\sigma,\tau)\).
The absolute logarithmic heights are \(a/d\) and \(hb/d\).
[Amoroso and David, Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001), at rank two with \(2\kappa(2)=212\), bounds their product below by \(c_2/(2d(\log(6d))^{212})\).
This gives (1) with \(c=c_2/2\).
The [rational substitution bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v77.md#independent) treats an output itself contained in the input field.
Here the output trace is in \(K\) while the independent Salem root generates a quadratic extension even over \(\mathbb Q(\sigma)\).

Thus for fixed \(B>0\), \(a,b\le B\) and bounded \(h\), all sufficiently large degree inputs give commensurable outputs.
The same conclusion holds along any sequence with \(h=o(d/(\log(6d))^{212})\) and \(a,b\le B\).
In particular, if \(K\) has no intermediate fields strictly between \(\mathbb Q\) and \(K\), then \(T_\tau\), being irrational, must generate \(K\).
For \(d\) sufficiently large in terms of \(B\), **every** such bounded output is a power of \(\eta\), with no restriction on the degree or coefficients of the rational expression for its trace.
This applies, for example, when \(d/2\) is prime.
If \(\sigma\) is the smallest Salem generator, all these outputs are ordinary positive powers of \(\sigma\).

## A degree bound for a trace function distinct from a Chebyshev polynomial

Write \(F/G\in\mathbb Q(X)\) in lowest terms, of degree \(k=\max(\deg F,\deg G)\ge1\), and assume \(G(T_\sigma)\ne0\) and \(F(T_\sigma)/G(T_\sigma)=T_\tau\).
In both alternatives \(h\le k\), since \(F(X)-T_\tau G(X)\) is a nonzero polynomial over \(\mathbb Q(T_\tau)\) vanishing at \(T_\sigma\).
Thus (1) already gives \(k\gg_B d/(\log(6d))^{212}\) in the independent case when \(a,b\le B\).

In the dependent case let \(t=T_\eta\) and \(C_j(x+x^{-1})=x^j+x^{-j}\), with \(C_0=2\).
Evaluating at \(t\) gives
\[
                    F(C_u(t))-C_v(t)G(C_u(t))=0 .
\]
If \(F(C_u(X))-C_v(X)G(C_u(X))\) is identically zero, coprimality is preserved by composition with \(C_u\), by composing a Bézout identity.
Hence \(G(C_u)\), and so \(G\), is constant.
Comparison of degrees gives \(v=ku\), and \(C_v=C_k\circ C_u\) forces \(F/G=C_k\).
Otherwise \(t\) has degree \(d/2\) and the polynomial has degree at most \(ku+v\), or at most \(\max(ku,v)\) when \(G\) is constant.
Consequently
\[
 \frac d2\log\eta\le k a+b,\qquad
 \frac d2\log\eta\le \max(ka,b)\quad\text{for polynomials}.             \tag{2}
\]
By [Voutier's bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height),
\(\log\eta>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\).
For \(a,b\le B\) and \(F/G\ne C_k\), (2) gives \(k\gg_B d(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) in the dependent case for large \(d\).
If \(u=1\), one has \(v=b/\log\eta\ll_B(\log d/\log\log d)^3\), so a distinct rational function representing this same power requires \(k\ge d/2-v\), or \(k\ge d/2\) for a polynomial at sufficiently large \(d\).
In particular, throughout the bounded input and output range, \(k=o(d/(\log(6d))^{212})\) forces the rational function itself eventually to equal \(C_k\).
The [angular mesh bound](Yin_L_G0462_v203.md#powers) for integral polynomials also applies when \(b\) is unbounded.

## Exact admissible powers at the field and at the trace order

Call a Salem output admissible if it admits the [fixed-input half-mass construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v392.md#statement), and let \(e(x)\) denote the least admissible exponent for a Salem number \(x\).
For \(\sigma=\eta^u\), all commensurable Salem outputs are \(\eta^v,\ v\ge1\).
Each trace \(C_v(t)\) belongs to \(K=\mathbb Q(T_\sigma)=\mathbb Q(t)\) and hence is a rational-coefficient polynomial in \(T_\sigma\).
The least admissible commensurable output at this field level is therefore \(\eta^{e(\eta)}\).

Integral-coefficient polynomials in \(T_\sigma\) give exactly the order \(B_u=\mathbb Z[C_u(t)]\subset A=\mathbb Z[t]\).
Both orders have rank \(d/2\) by preservation of the trace field under powering.
Let \(J=[A:B_u]\), so \(JA\subset B_u\).
The least admissible commensurable output represented by an integral polynomial in \(T_\sigma\) is \(\eta^{v_u}\), where
\[
 v_u=\min\left\{1\le v\le u\,e(\sigma):
      v\text{ satisfies the admissibility criterion for }\eta,\
      C_v(t)\bmod JA\in B_u/JA\right\}.                                 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the congruence is equivalent to membership in \(B_u\), and \(v=u\,e(\sigma)\) qualifies because \(C_{u e(\sigma)}(t)=C_{e(\sigma)}(T_\sigma)\).
In particular \(e(\eta)\le v_u\le u\,e(\sigma)\).
To compute \(J\) and \(B_u\), reduce the power basis in \(C_u(t)\) in the power basis in \(t\) modulo its monic minimal polynomial.
The absolute determinant is \(J\) and the columns give the membership lattice.
For \(J>1\), the recurrence \((r,s)\mapsto(s,ts-r)\) is invertible on \((A/JA)^2\), so the membership test is periodic starting at \((2,t)\).
For \(J=1\) every residue passes.
If only \(\sigma\) is supplied, the possible primitive exponents \(u\) are bounded by \((\log\sigma)(\log(3d))^3/2\) by Voutier's Corollary 2.
Among positive integers \(j\) up to this bound one can take the largest for which the positive real \(j\)-th root of \(\sigma\) is Salem of degree \(d\), testing the factor containing it in \(P_\sigma(X^j)\).
Such a root generates \(\mathbb Q(\sigma)\) and the fixed-field theorem identifies the largest \(j\) with \(u\).

For \(a\le B\), sufficiently large \(d\) and a trace field with no proper intermediate field, the least outputs just computed give the exact minima in the window \(0<\log\tau\le B\) whenever they lie therein.
If the respective minimum exceeds the cutoff then the corresponding window is empty.
In particular on a supplied sequence \(\sigma_j\to1\) with these trace fields, rational trace functions can produce admissible \(\tau_j\to1\) exactly when \(e(\eta_j)\log\eta_j\to0\).
For integral polynomials the criterion is \(v_{u_j}\log\eta_j\to0\).
Indeed the degrees must grow by finiteness of monic Salem polynomials of bounded degree and bounded measure, so (1) rules out the independent case eventually for any bounded outputs.
For primitive generators \(u_j=1\), both criteria recover the [ordinary power cost](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v392.md#changing).

## A quartic example attaining the upper limit in the order test

Let \(t=(1+\sqrt{13})/2\), and let \(\eta>1\) solve \(x+x^{-1}=t\).
The other trace lies in \((-2,2)\), so \(t^2-4\) is negative at the other embedding and the lift is a quartic Salem number with polynomial \(x^4-x^3-x^2-x+1\).
It is primitive in its field.
Indeed \(t<5/2\) gives \(\eta<2\), whereas any quartic Salem number \(\zeta\) with conjugate trace \(t'\in(-2,2)\) satisfies
\[
       1\le |(2-T_\zeta)(2-t')|<4(T_\zeta-2).
\]
Hence \(T_\zeta>9/4\), excluding \(\zeta\le\sqrt2\) since \(\sqrt2+1/\sqrt2=3/\sqrt2<9/4\).
A proper Salem root of \(\eta\) in its field would have degree four and lie below \(\sqrt2\).

The reduction of the quartic is \(\Phi_5\bmod2\), whose four roots all have order five.
Thus the admissible powers of \(\eta\) are exactly the multiples of five.
Take \(\sigma=\eta^4\).
Since \(t^2=t+3\), \(C_4(t)=3t+2\) and \(B_4=\mathbb Z+3\mathbb Zt\).
Modulo three, \(A/3A=\mathbb F_3\times\mathbb F_3\) by evaluation at \(t=0,1\), and the order consists of the preimage of the diagonal.
The recurrence for \(C_v\) at zero cycles through \(2,0,1,0\) modulo three beginning at \(v=0\), while at one its values alternate \(2,1\).
They agree exactly when \(4\mid v\).
Thus the least admissible commensurable trace-field output is \(\eta^5\), while the least one represented by an integral polynomial in \(T_\sigma\) is \(\eta^{20}\).
Here \(e(\sigma)=5\), so equality holds on the right of (3).
