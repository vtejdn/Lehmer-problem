# Higher multiplicities at good primes of a powered order

<a id="bound"></a>
## A bound independent of the two row degrees

Let \(P\) be the degree-\(d\) monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), let \(p\) be a prime preserving this degree on powering, and put \(I_p=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\alpha^p]]\).
Suppose \(P^e\) divides \(U(x^p)+x^k V(x^p)\) in \(\mathbb Q[x^{\pm1}]\), where \(e\ge2\), \(p\nmid k\), and \(U,V\) are nonzero integral polynomials with nonzero constant terms, coprime over \(\mathbb Q\).
These are the rows after stripping individual monomials from a two-class witness.
Write \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\), and fix a prime \(\ell\nmid p\operatorname{disc}P\) with \(v(\ell)=1\).
For \(A\ge1\) define
\[
 C_A=\max\{3,\ 2+A\log_\ell(A)/e,\ (e-1)/A\}.
\]
Then
\[
              v_\ell(H)\ \ge\
        \frac{2(e-1)^2}{C_A(C_A+1)}\,v_\ell(I_p).                       \tag{1}
\]
In particular one may use \(C=2+\sqrt{\max(1,\log_\ell e)}\) in place of \(C_A\).
The loss compared with a coefficient of order \(e^2\) is at most logarithmic in \(e\), independently of the input degree, the powering prime and the depths of the collisions.
For \(\ell\ge e\) the coefficient in (1) is at least \((e-1)^2/6\).
For \(e=2\) the separate [double-root argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#statement) gives the larger coefficient \(2/9\).

<a id="fractional"></a>
## The denominator of a fractional depth

The conjugates \(\alpha_i\) of \(\alpha\) lie in a finite unramified splitting extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) and have distinct residues.
The marks \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) are distinct, and within each positive-depth disk the ratios \(U(\beta_i)/V(\beta_i)=-\alpha_i^k\) have different residues.
Use the [two generic pencils and their paths](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#paths), removing any common \(\ell\)-content first.
At center \(b=\beta_i\) let \(g_b(t)\) be the minimum of the Taylor coefficient valuations of both rows with \(jt\) added at index \(j\).
At rational depth \(t>0\), write \(A_0,B_0\) for the reductions after substituting \(X=b+\tau Y\), \(v(\tau)=t\), and dividing the two rows by a common scalar of valuation \(g_b(t)\).
Let \(n=\max(\deg A_0,\deg B_0)\), with \(\deg 0=-\infty\).
One generic pencil \(G=U+cV\) then has \(n\) roots in the disk, counted with multiplicities, and its root count along a path equals \(g_b'\) between the finitely many breaks.
The second pencil uses a different generic residue so the local intersection length is preserved.

At a nonconstant disk let \(u=\deg(A_0/B_0)\) after cancelling common factors.
If \(t=a/h\) in lowest terms, all nonzero coefficients of \(A_0,B_0\) have indices in a single congruence class modulo \(h\).
Indeed all Taylor coefficients before scaling have integral valuations, and the indices surviving in either row attain the same \(g_b(t)\).
Consequently the ratio equals \(\psi(Y^h)\) for a nonconstant rational function \(\psi\).
Substituting \(Y^h\) in a coprime numerator and denominator preserves coprimality and multiplies the degree of their quotient by \(h\).
Thus \(h\mid u\), including when \(\ell\mid h\).

Put \(j=\lceil t\rceil-1,\ \delta=t-j\) and \(a_B=g_b(t)-g_b(j)\) for this nonconstant disk \(B\).
Let \(R_B\) count the roots of \(G\) in \(E_B^+=\{v(X-b)>j\}\), and let \(m_B\ge1\) count the marks in \(B\).
Integration of the nonincreasing root count gives
\[
                    n\le a_B/\delta\le R_B .
\]
The [normalized double-row jets](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#jets) give \((Y-\bar y_i)^e\mid A_0+\bar\alpha_i^k B_0\) for each marked node with scaled coordinate \(y_i\).
Nonconstancy makes \(A_0+\bar\alpha_i^kB_0\) a nonzero polynomial, so \(n\ge e\).

First normalize the polynomial rows at depth \(j\), then scale to \(t\).
In the resulting integral polynomials \(a_0,b_0\) every coefficient at index \(i\) has valuation at least \(i\delta-a_B\).
The critical polynomial \(K=pX(a_0'b_0-a_0b_0')-\tau k a_0b_0\), with derivatives in \(Y\), therefore has coefficient valuation at least \((i+1)\delta-2a_B\) at index \(i\), as \(t\ge\delta\).
The [mixed-characteristic derivative estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v372.md#derivative) gives at least \((e-1)m_B\) zeros in the disk and minimum coefficient valuation at most the logarithm to base \(\ell\) of the inseparable degree, hence at most \(\log_\ell u\).
Twisting the final normalized ratio by the inverse \(k\)-th power of the unit \(p\)-th root branch gives the same critical polynomial and generic Taylor coefficient regardless of the intermediate scale.
After dividing \(K\) by its content, its reduced degree counts all roots in the disk and is at most \((2a_B+\log_\ell u)/\delta-1\).
Thus
\[
                  (e-1)m_B+1\le 2a_B/\delta+(\log_\ell u)/\delta .        \tag{2}
\]
For integer \(t\) this is at most \(3a_B\), since \(\delta=1\) and \(\log_\ell u\le n\le a_B\).
For fractional \(t\), we have \(1/\delta\le h\le u\).
If \(u\le A\), (2) is therefore at most \(2R_B+A\log_\ell A\le (2+A\log_\ell A/e)R_B\), since \(R_B\ge n\ge e\).

<a id="cover"></a>
## Covering the marks on a constant disk

Take a disk \(D\) of positive integer depth with \(m\ge2\) marks and constant reduced ratio.
Among the nonconstant proper subdisks centered at marks select the maximal disks \(B\) that have either integer depth or fractional depth with \(u\le A\).
There are finitely many candidates because nonconstancy requires a tie of two distinct minimizing Taylor indices on one of the finitely many marked rays.
The selected disks are disjoint.
Each \(E_B^+\) lies in \(D\) and contains exactly the same marks as \(B\), since a valuation between marked centers greater than \(j\) is an integer at least \(j+1\ge t\).
The \(E_B^+\) are therefore pairwise disjoint, since nested open disks would put the smaller disk's marked center in both selected disks.
The previous estimates give
\[
                        (e-1)\sum_B m_B\le C_A\sum_B R_B .                \tag{3}
\]

Let \(r\) be the number of remaining marks.
If \(r\ge2\), take their finite connecting tree with ends truncated close enough to retain each residue label.
Every pair of distinct ends has a disk of nonconstant reduction on its path by the [change-of-residue argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#paths).
Each such disk \(T\) on this tree has fractional depth and reduced quotient degree \(u_T>A\), since otherwise a maximal selected disk would contain a remaining mark.
Branching of marked paths occurs only at integral depths.
Deleting all the nonconstant points therefore cuts the tree into at most one more component than the number of deleted points, with at most one marked end in each component.
Thus \(r-1\) is at most the number of these points.

At \(T\) choose a remaining mark as center, so all marks inside \(T\) reduce to \(Y=0\) in its scaled coordinate.
For its reduced quotient \(\phi_T\), choose \(-\bar c\) away from \(\phi_T(0),\phi_T(\infty)\) and the images of common-factor zeros.
The fiber over \(-\bar c\) has exactly \(u_T\) points counted with multiplicities in finite nonzero directions, since the map has degree \(u_T\), including its inseparable degree.
The normalized pencil factors show that each such directional multiplicity is the number of actual roots of \(G\) in that open direction.
All choices for the finitely many disks and trees can be met by a single generic residue in a finite residue extension, and likewise for the second pencil.
Directions assigned at distinct \(T\) are disjoint because they leave the connecting tree and contain no marked center.
An assigned direction cannot contain \(E_B^+\), which has a mark.
If the direction were contained in \(E_B^+\), the marked center of \(T\) would also lie there, since its distance in the valuation scale from that direction is fractional while the boundary depth of \(E_B^+\) is integral.
These are the [disjoint directions of the double-root argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#constant), now counted with their full fiber multiplicities.
It follows that \(\sum_T u_T+\sum_B R_B\le n_D\), the root count of \(G\) in \(D\).
For \(r\le1\) take the sum over \(T\) to be empty.
Since \((e-1)(r-1)\le C_A\sum_Tu_T\), adding (3) yields
\[
                             (e-1)(m-1)\le C_A n_D .                    \tag{4}
\]

<a id="sum"></a>
## Paying for the entire index

For every positive integer-depth \(D\) with \(m\ge2\), let \(a_D\) be the increment of \(g_b\) on the incoming unit interval.
It satisfies \(a_D\ge n_D\ge e\), since two different residue labels in the scaled jet identities would force both reductions to vanish identically if \(n_D<e\).
For nonconstant \(D\), (2) gives \(3a_D\ge(e-1)m+1\), and for constant \(D\) use (4).
Hence
\[
                \frac{a_D}{e-1}\ \ge\ \max\{1,(m-1)/C_A\}
                         \ \ge\ \sqrt{\frac{m(m-1)}{C_A(C_A+1)}} .
\]
The last inequality follows by splitting at \(m=C_A+1\).
The [local intersection sum over the incoming intervals](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#sum) pays at least \(\sum_D a_D^2\) by integration of the squared generic root counts and Cauchy’s inequality on each interval.
The same disks give \(v_\ell(I_p)=\sum_D\binom{m_D}{2}\) because all original residues are distinct and all marked separations have integral valuations.
Restoring common scalar content can only increase \(v_\ell(H)\), so (1) follows.
Finally set \(L=\max(1,\log_\ell e)\) and \(A=e/\sqrt L\ge1\).
Then \(A\log_\ell A/e\le\sqrt L\) and \((e-1)/A\le\sqrt L\), proving the asserted choice of \(C\).
The unresolved higher-multiplicity question is whether a coefficient bounded below by an absolute constant times \((e-1)^2\) holds even for fixed small \(\ell\) as \(e\) grows.
