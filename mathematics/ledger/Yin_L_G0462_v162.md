# Computing the powered-order exponent by one polynomial inverse

<a id="conductor"></a>
## The conductor at every multiplicity

Use the [power orders](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#layers) \(A_e=\mathbb Z[x]/(P^e)\) and \(B_e=\mathbb Z[X]/(Q^e)\subset A_e\), with \(X=x^p\), \(P\) the minimal polynomial of an algebraic unit \(\alpha\), and \(Q\) that of \(\beta=\alpha^p\) of the same degree \(d\).
Let \(L(x)=Q(x^p)/P(x)\).
In their common rational algebra write \(M_e=p x^{p-1}/L(x)^e\), which exists since \(L(\alpha)\ne0\).
Then the conductor and the integer exponent \(s_e=\exp(A_e/B_e)\) satisfy
\[
       \{t:tA_e\subseteq B_e\}=M_e^{-1} A_e,\qquad
       s_e=\min\{a\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:aM_e\in A_e\}.                      \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(s_e\) is the least common denominator of the coefficients of \(M_e\) reduced in \(\mathbb Q[x]\) modulo \(P^e\).
In particular, the exponent \(s=s_1\) screening the [constant-row test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#statement) is exactly the denominator of \(P'(\alpha)/Q'(\beta)\) in the power basis of \(\alpha\).
This gives an input-only computation by polynomial division and inversion over \(\mathbb Q\), with no factorization of the integer order index.

For a monic polynomial \(H\) of degree \(n\), let \(\lambda_H\) take the coefficient at degree \(n-1\) of the remainder modulo \(H\).
The lattice \(\mathbb Z[z]/H\) is self-dual for the pairing \((a,b)\mapsto\lambda_H(ab)\).
Indeed its matrix in the power basis has zeros for exponent sums below \(n-1\) and ones on that antidiagonal, hence determinant \(\pm1\).
Over \(\mathbb C\), \(\lambda_H(f)\) is the sum of the residues of \(f(z)\,dz/H(z)\) at the distinct roots of \(H\), allowing multiple poles.
This follows by polynomial division and comparison of the coefficient at \(z^{-1}\) in the partial fractions at infinity.
In this formula for \(H=Q^e\), change coordinates at each \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) by \(X=x^p\) using the local branch at \(\alpha_i\).
The derivative \(p\alpha_i^{p-1}\ne0\), so residues are unchanged by this local change of coordinates, and all powered conjugates occur once.
It follows that \(\lambda_{Q^e}(f)=\lambda_{P^e}(M_e f(x^p))\), since the two local differentials agree there and the polynomial representative modulo \(P^e\) leaves every residue unchanged.
By self-duality in \(B_e\), \(tA_e\subset B_e\) is equivalent to integrality of \(\lambda_{Q^e}(t A_e B_e)\), identifying the rational algebras.
As \(1\in B_e\subset A_e\), this is precisely integrality of \(\lambda_{P^e}(M_e t A_e)\), hence \(M_e t\in A_e\) by its self-duality.
This proves (1).

<a id="weights"></a>
## A lower bound from collisions at individual conjugates

Put \(I=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\beta]]\), and for each prime \(\ell\) use the extension of \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\) in a splitting field.
Formula (1) at \(e=1\) gives
\[
 v_\ell(s)\ \ge\ \max_i\sum_{j\ne i}
 \big(v_\ell(\beta_i-\beta_j)-v_\ell(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)\big)
 \ \ge\ \frac{2}{d}v_\ell(I).                                           \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(sP'(\alpha_i)/Q'(\beta_i)\) is integral at each conjugate, and averaging the sums gives twice the Vandermonde valuation divided by \(d\).
Consequently \(I^{2/d}\le s\le I\), with the upper bound following from the order of the quotient group.
At the Salem inputs with \(6p\mid d\), the [exact exponent](Yin_L_G0462_v161.md#group) is \(3^{p-1}\), whereas \(I^{2/d}=3^{(1-2/d)(p-1)}\).
For general inputs an absolute lower bound for \(\log s/p\) must still be converted into sufficiently large valuations for every allowed row witness.

## The exact denominator at the powering prime of good reduction

For degree-preserving \(p\), one has \(p\nmid I\) exactly when \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(P)\).
Indeed the image of \(B_1\) in \(A_1/pA_1=\mathbb F_p[x]/\bar P\) is the image of Frobenius.
The index is prime to \(p\) exactly when the image is the whole quotient, by the power-basis determinant.
On a finite \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra Frobenius is bijective exactly when the algebra is reduced, which here means that \(\bar P\) is squarefree.

When \(p\nmid I\) and \(1\le r<p\), the exact power of \(p\) in the additive order of \(x^r\) modulo \(B_e\) is
\[
                   p^{\,k+v_p(k!)},\qquad k=e-1.                         \tag{3}
\]
It is also the \(p\)-part of the exponent of the whole quotient \(A_e/B_e\).
If \(I=1\), this is its entire exponent and the order of each indicated class, since the [index formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#layers) excludes all other primes.

Work over the integers localized at \(p\).
There is a polynomial \(G\) there such that \(G(\beta)=\alpha\), since the two localized orders agree.
Its image is a unit in the localized \(B_e\), because it is a unit modulo \(Q\) and the ideal generated by \(Q\) is nilpotent.
Write \(X-G(X)^p=Q(X)C(X)\) by monic division.
Differentiating and reducing at \(\beta\) modulo \(p\) gives \(Q'(\beta)C(\beta)=1\bmod p\).
In particular \(C\) is a unit modulo \((p,Q)\) and also modulo \((p,Q^e)\).
Set \(V=C/G^p\) in the localized \(B_e\), so \(X/G^p=1+QV\).
In the rational algebra \(x/G\) is congruent to one modulo the nilpotent ideal \((P)\).
It is the unique \(p\)-th root of \(1+QV\) with that property, by successive comparison of nilpotent layers in characteristic zero.
Therefore for every integer \(i\ge0\),
\[
              x^i=G^i\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{i/p}{j}Q^j V^j .
\]
The \(p\)-part of each coefficient denominator divides \(p^{j+v_p(j!)}\), giving the upper bound for all powers of \(x\).
When \(p\nmid i\), all numerator factors \(i-hp\) in this coefficient are units, so the valuations attain these bounds.
The exponents \(j+v_p(j!)\) increase strictly.
After multiplication by \(p^{k+v_p(k!)}\), only the \(j=k\) term survives modulo \(p\), and it is a unit times \(Q^k\) in \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(\bar Q^e)\).
That class is nonzero because \(\bar Q\) is monic of positive degree.
This proves (3), including the trivial denominator at \(e=1\).
