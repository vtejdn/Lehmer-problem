# Two partial trace grids force empty gaps or a bounded conductor ratio

<a id="two"></a>
## Two Chebyshev factors of positive relative degrees

Let \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\) run through monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomials with \(1<M(P)\to1\).
Put \(D_n(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(n\theta)\).
Suppose \(k/m\to A,\ l/m\to B\) with \(0<A,B\le1\), put \(g=\gcd(k,l)\), and take the distinct union of the roots of \(D_k,D_l\).
Let \(E\) count its open internal gaps containing no real root of \(S\).
If \(\max(k/g,l/g)\to\infty\), then
\[
                         \liminf E/m\ \ge\ AB.
\]
Consequently if both grids belong to the nodes of a strict interlacer, their reduced exponents \(k/g,l/g\) must stay bounded, regardless of the number and total degree of the other interlacer factors, provided both grid degrees are at least a fixed positive fraction of \(m\).
Indeed the limits \(A,B\) can be extracted on a contrary subsequence, and between any two distinct interlacer nodes there is a target root.

<a id="triangle"></a>
## Distinct nearby nodes and the available area for a target root

Use the phase coordinate \(x=\arg z/(2\pi)\) on \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\).
More generally let \(U,V\) be fixed nonempty finite sets of phases invariant under negation, of cardinalities \(u,v\).
Use the full circle grids \(kx\in U,\ lx\in V\), and take the distinct trace nodes \(2\cos(2\pi x)\) in \([-2,2]\).
Let \(\delta_U,\delta_V\) be the least consecutive circular spacings in the two fixed sets, taking one for a singleton.
For fixed positive finite \(A,B\), under the same divergence of reduced exponents one has
\[
       \liminf E/m\ \ge\ \frac{uv}{4}\,AB(2\eta-\eta^2)
       \quad\left(0<\eta<\min(2\delta_U/A,\,2\delta_V/B)\right).
\]
In particular two fixed profiles of displaced grids, including the nodes of \(C(D_k)\) and \(D_l\) for fixed \(C\) with simple interior trace roots, also miss a positive fraction when both degrees have positive relative limits.

Take \(r_0=\eta/(2m)\).
For fixed \(u_0\in U,v_0\in V\) the differences of all pairs of nodes \(kx_1=u_0,\ lx_2=v_0\) run through a translate of the equally spaced subgroup of order \(kl/g\), with each value counted \(g\) times.
There are \(2r_0kl+O(g)\) such pairs of distance less than \(r_0\), including at most \(g\) coincidences.
Here \(g=o(m)\) since the two degrees are comparable to \(m\) and their reduced exponents are unbounded along the sequence.
Every noncoincident pair counted bounds a distinct union gap, since its length is strictly smaller than the minimum spacing in either grid for large \(m\).
An endpoint belonging to both grids cannot create a second description of such a gap for the same reason.
Reflection pairs the full-circle gaps, except for the bounded number touching or straddling the real endpoints.
Thus there are \((uv\,AB\eta/2+o(1))m\) such short internal trace gaps.

For a point \(x\) of the circle inside the short arc joining \(x_1,x_2\), the signed displacements of \(kx,lx\) from \(u_0,v_0\) have lifts \(X,Y\) of opposite signs satisfying \(|X|/k+|Y|/l<r_0\).
Modulo one this puts \((kx,lx)\) in the images of two triangles of combined Euclidean area \(klr_0^2\), translated by \((u_0,v_0)\).
For a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), the heights of \(\alpha^k,\alpha^l\) tend to zero as \(h(\alpha)=\log M(P)/(2m)\).
The joint sequence is strict, since a fixed nonzero character \((a,b)\) would require \(ak+bl=0\) for nontorsion \(\alpha\), which fails eventually by divergence of the coprime reduced pair.
The [two-dimensional small-points theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) gives independent Haar phases for the pair, with the original conjugates counted with equal multiplicities over the joint orbit.
The limiting torus area of the union is at most \(uv\,AB\eta^2/4\), even if some projected triangles overlap.
For the upper bound on the empirical mass, enclose each moving triangle in an arbitrarily small fixed enlargement of its limiting closed triangle and then shrink the enlargement.
Each occupied short trace gap supplies two distinct conjugate circle lifts in the union, and distinct occupied gaps use different lifts.
Their number divided by \(m\) is therefore at most \(uv\,AB\eta^2/4+o(1)\).
Subtracting gives the bound.
For \(D_k,D_l\) take \(U=V=\{1/4,3/4\}\) and let \(\eta\uparrow1\).

The same count applies to primitive root-of-unity trace grids of orders with a fixed radical \(R\), by using the multiplier \(k=N/R\) and fixed set \(U=\{j/R:0\le j<R,\ \gcd(j,R)=1\}\) for order \(N\).
For orders \(N\) with \(\varphi(N)/N\to1\) and \(N=O(m)\), use the full grid \(Nx=0\) and discard the \(o(m)\) nonprimitive nodes.
Deleting one distinct node changes at most two former empty gaps, so the normalized lower bound persists for such deletions in either grid.

<a id="sharp"></a>
## A sharp irreducible target at measure one

Fix \(0<A,B<1\) with \(1,A,B\) rationally independent, and take \(m=2^n,\ k=\lfloor Am\rfloor,\ l=\lfloor Bm\rfloor\).
The target \(S=D_m\) has irreducible cyclotomic lift \(P=z^{2m}+1=\Phi_{4m}\), and \(E/m\to AB\).
Indeed in the variable \(\theta/\pi\in(0,1)\) its roots are \((j+1/2)/m\).
Each open cell between consecutive target nodes contains at most one node of each of the \(k,l\) grids.
Apart from end cells and coincidences it therefore contributes exactly one empty union gap when it contains nodes of both grids, and none otherwise.
Grid coincidences number \(O(\gcd(k,l)+\gcd(k,m)+\gcd(l,m))=o(m)\), because bounded reduced exponents on any subsequence for any of these pairs would contradict the chosen rational independence.
For the target cells starting at \((j+1/2)/m\), the phase pairs
\[
              \left(k(j+1/2)/m,\,l(j+1/2)/m\right)\pmod1
\]
are asymptotically Haar on the two-torus.
For each fixed nonzero character \((a,b)\) its complete \(m\)-term average vanishes eventually since \((ak+bl)/m\to aA+bB\notin\mathbb Z\).
Each cell contains a \(k\)-grid point when its first phase lies in the interval of length \(k/m\) preceding \(1/2\), and likewise for \(l\).
Thus the asymptotic fraction containing both is \(AB\).
This sharpness concerns the angle and gap count at measure one.

## Fixed reduced exponents and the least degree allowed by the spacings

Now let \(k=ag,\ l=bg\) for fixed coprime \(a,b\), with \(g\to\infty\) and \(m/g\to L\in(0,\infty)\).
For two prescribed folded profiles their distinct union reduces exactly to a single fixed profile in \(D_g\), since \(D_k=D_a(D_g)\) and \(D_l=D_b(D_g)\).
Take the distinct trace roots of that profile to be \(2\cos(\pi x_j)\), \(0<x_1<\cdots<x_s<1\), and set \(x_0=0,\ x_{s+1}=1\).
Let \(b_0=b_s=1/2,\ b_j=1\) for \(0<j<s\).
The [folded phase count](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v41.md#folding) gives \(b_jg+O(1)\) gaps of each type against at most \(m(x_{j+1}-x_j)+o(m)\) available circle traces in that bin.
Here \(h(\alpha^g)\to0\) since \(g=O(m)\), and nontorsion excludes target roots at the two folded extrema.
Consequently
\[
     \liminf E/m\ \ge\ \sum_{j=0}^s\bigl(b_j/L-(x_{j+1}-x_j)\bigr)_+ .
\]

For the two Chebyshev grids, \(o(m)\) empty gaps thus require \(L\ge2ab\) for opposite parity, or \(L\ge ab\) if both \(a,b\) are odd.
In fact the reciprocal of \(\max_j b_j/(x_{j+1}-x_j)\) is the least spacing on the reflected circle \(\theta/\pi\bmod2\), including the doubled endpoint distances.
Across the two grids the possible differences there are \(\big(b(2j+1)-a(2h+1)\big)/(2ab)\) modulo two.
The numerators run through all residues of the given parity modulo \(4ab\), by \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
The least nonzero cross spacing is therefore \(1/(2ab)\) for opposite parity and \(1/(ab)\) for two odd exponents.
Within-grid spacings \(1/a,1/b\) are no smaller.
For two odd exponents the common trace nodes are exactly the roots of \(D_g\), by the same congruences, so the product \(D_kD_l\) itself has repeated roots and cannot divide a strict interlacer.
The bound in that case is for the distinct union, which is the larger grid alone when one reduced exponent is one and the other is odd.
For opposite parity the grids are disjoint.

Both degree thresholds allow exact cyclotomic interlacing for the distinct union.
In opposite parity take \(m=2abg\) and target \(D_m\).
The two grids belong to the strict interlacer with roots \(2\cos(j\pi/m)\), \(1\le j<m\), namely \(U_{m-1}(x/2)\) for the second-kind Chebyshev polynomial \(U_{m-1}\).
For two odd exponents the union lies in \(D_{abg}\).
With \(n=abg\), the integral target \((x^2-4)U_{n-1}(x/2)\) has roots at all angles \(j\pi/n\), \(0\le j\le n\), and is strictly interlaced by \(D_n\).
Its degree satisfies \(m/g=(n+1)/g\to ab\).
These two examples have measure one, and do not assert irreducibility for all choices of the commensurate exponents.
