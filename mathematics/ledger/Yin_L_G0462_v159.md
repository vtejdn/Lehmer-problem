# The least Pisot number of prescribed positive norm and limits at fixed norm

<a id="minimum"></a>
## The sharp exclusion at a fixed integer

Let \(q\ge2\) be an integer, and let \(\beta_q\) denote the positive root of
\[
                   p_q(X)=X^3-(q-1)X^2-qX-q .
\]
Among **noninteger** Pisot numbers whose minimal polynomial \(f\) has \(|f(0)|=q\), the minimum is \(\beta_q\).
It is also the minimum among noninteger Pisot numbers of field norm \(+q\), since \(p_q\) has odd degree and constant \(-q\).
For the sign restriction \(f(0)=+q\) every such number is strictly larger than \(\beta_q\).
The identity
\[
                    \beta_q-q=\frac{q}{\beta_q(\beta_q+1)}>0          \tag{1}
\]
excludes convergence to the integer \(q\) from above, in any of these three classes.
The integer \(q\) itself has degree one and field norm \(q\).

Boyd gives the cubic immediately after (40) in his 1978 study of near-integer Pisot numbers, referring to the coefficient method of Dufresnoy and Pisot.
He proposes the quadratic value \((q+\sqrt{q^2+4})/2\) for a different quantity, the least accumulation point in his associated-function class.
The following finite calculation proves the cubic minimum and its equality case for actual nonlinear minimal polynomials. [Boyd's statement](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#least-fixed-norm) · [Dufresnoy–Pisot coefficient intervals](../audits/literature/DP55/Yin_U001.md#coefficient-intervals)

<a id="cubic"></a>
## Two Schur estimates at the pole

The polynomial \(p_q\) has one positive root by its coefficient signs and \(p_q(q)=-q<0\).
For \(x\ge0\), \(p_q(-x)=-x^3-[(q-1)x^2-qx+q]<0\), since the quadratic has negative discriminant for \(q\ge2\).
The two nonreal roots thus have modulus \(\sqrt{q/\beta_q}<1\).
The cubic is irreducible, since any proper monic factor omitting its sole exterior root would have a nonzero integer constant of modulus less than one.
The formula \(p_q(X)=(X-q)X(X+1)-q\) gives (1), in particular \(\beta_q-q<1/(q+1)\).

For an arbitrary candidate let \(\theta\) be its Pisot root, \(\delta=\theta-q>0\), \(t=1/\theta\), \(g=f^*\), and
\[
            F=\operatorname{sgn}(f(0))f/g=q+u_1 z+u_2z^2+\cdots .
\]
The \(u_i\) are integers since \(g(0)=1\).
The [first-coefficient estimate for both signs](Yin_L_G0462_v113.md#nonunit) gives
\[
             j=u_1-q^2+1\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
             \delta(q+t-2)\le j\le\delta(q+t+2).                     \tag{2}
\]
If \(\theta\le\beta_q\), the right-hand side is strictly less than \((q+q^{-1}+2)/(q+1)=1+1/q<2\).
Hence \(j=1\) and \(u_1=q^2\) throughout the part of the interval still to test.

Put \(S=z(qF-1)/(F-q)\).
To see it is Schur, write \(A=\operatorname{sgn}(f(0))f\), so the denominator after clearing \(g\) is \(A-qg\).
On the circle \(|A|=|g|>0\), and Rouché's theorem gives just one disk zero for \(A-qg\), at zero and simple.
The prefactor \(z\) cancels it, and \(|qA-g|=|A-qg|\) on the circle.
The maximum principle bounds \(|S|\) by one, and evaluating at the pole of \(F\) gives \(S(t)=qt\).
This reduction also appears in the [finite Schur interpolation argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#schur).

Let \(a=S(0)=1-q^{-2}\).
Then \(G=(S-a)/(z(1-aS))\) is Schur, by Schwarz's lemma.
Write \(u_2=q^3+v\).
Expanding at zero gives
\[
 b=G(0)=\frac{q^3-(q^2-1)v}{2q^2-1}.
\]
Since \(b\le1\) and \(v\) is integral, \(v\ge q-1\): the numerator at \(q-2\) is \(2q^2+q-2>2q^2-1\).
Thus \(b\le b_*=(q^2+q-1)/(2q^2-1)<1\).
At the real point \(t\), Schwarz–Pick gives
\[
 \frac{qt-a}{t(1-aqt)}=G(t)\le\frac{b+t}{1+bt}
                         \le\frac{b_*+t}{1+b_*t}.
\]
For a constant \(G=-1\) the same inequality holds directly.
All denominators are positive since \(q<\theta\).
Upon clearing them, the numerator of the left side minus the right side is \(a[qt^3+qt^2+(q-1)t-1]\).
Thus \(p_q(\theta)\ge0\), forcing \(\theta\ge\beta_q\).
The cubic constructed above attains the bound.
Equality specifies the algebraic number \(\beta_q\) itself, whose unique monic minimal polynomial is \(p_q\), so equality is impossible at positive polynomial constant.

<a id="square"></a>
## Consequences at the square constant

At \(q=d^2\), (1) gives a lower excess asymptotic to \(1/d^2\) for all nonlinear candidates, since \(q<\beta_q<q+1/(q+1)\).
Prescribing even degree \(d\) and \(f(0)=+q\) precludes the cubic attaining equality.
In this smaller class the [exclusion of the first coefficient branch](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) supplies an absolute \(\eta>0\) with \(\delta\ge\eta\) if \(j=1\).
On the same branch (2) gives \(\delta\le1/(q-2)\) for \(q>2\).
Consequently every positive-constant seed has \(j\ge2\) for all sufficiently large \(q\), and (2) then gives
\[
                   \theta-q>\frac{2q}{(q+1)^2}.
\]
For the even-degree minima \(\eta_d\) at \(q=d^2\), this proves \(\liminf_{d\to\infty,\,2\mid d}d^2\eta_d\ge2\).
This lower bound tends to zero before rescaling and leaves the [unscaled limiting Salem trace question](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v370.md#statement) open.

## The unit limit of a fixed Salem grid

Take an input allowed by the [explicit grid inverse](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v374.md#grid), with fixed \(q\), a fixed Salem minimal polynomial \(P_0\) of even degree \(m\) and fixed auxiliary Pisot polynomial \(f_0\) of degree \(m\) and constant \(q+1\).
Here \(zf_0+f_0^*=(z+1)P_0\).
The degree \(m+2k\) polynomials obtained there for \(k\ge1\) satisfy
\[
 f_k=(z^{2k}+1)f_0+(z^{2k-1}-1)P_0=z^{2k-1}A+B,\qquad
 A=zf_0+P_0,\quad B=f_0-P_0.                                        \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the trace interpolation uses \(D_k=V_{k-1}+V_{k-2}\); its sine formula, or the defining recurrence, gives \((z-1)z^{k-1}D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^{2k-1}-1\).
Write \(\theta_k\) for the Pisot root of \(f_k\).

The polynomial \(A\) is monic of degree \(m+1\), with \(A(0)=1\) and \(A(1)=2P_0(1)<0\).
It therefore has a real exterior root \(\lambda>1\).
On compact subsets of \(|z|>1\) the functions \(z^{1-2k}f_k\) converge uniformly to \(A\).
Rouché's theorem on small disjoint circles about its exterior roots shows that their total multiplicity is at most one, because each \(f_k\) has just one exterior root.
The root \(\lambda\) is simple and \(\theta_k\to\lambda\).
On \(|z|=1\), reciprocity of \(P_0\) and the identity for \(zf_0+f_0^*\) give
\[
                |A(z)|^2-|B(z)|^2
                =2\Re((z+1)f_0(z)\overline{P_0(z)})
                =|z+1|^2|P_0(z)|^2.                                 \tag{4}
\]
If \(A\) vanishes there, (4) forces \(B=0\), so either \(z=-1\) or \(f_0(z)=P_0(z)=0\).
The latter is impossible since \(f_0\) is Pisot, and \(f_k(-1)=-2A(-1)>0\) by the root signs for an even-degree Pisot minimal polynomial.
Thus every remaining root of \(A\) is strictly inside the circle.
The integer-factor constant-term argument makes \(A\) irreducible, so \(\lambda\) is a Pisot unit of degree \(m+1\).

If \(\sigma\) is the positive exterior root of \(P_0\), then (4) of the [grid calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v374.md#grid) gives \(\theta_k>q+\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2>\sigma\) with a fixed positive margin.
Hence \(\lambda>\sigma\), \(P_0(\lambda)>0\), and \(f_k(\lambda)=B(\lambda)=-(1+\lambda^{-1})P_0(\lambda)<0\).
Thus \(\theta_k>\lambda\) and \(A(\theta_k)>0\).
Equation (3) gives \(f_{k+1}(\theta_k)=(\theta_k^2-1)\theta_k^{2k-1}A(\theta_k)>0\), whence \(\lambda<\theta_{k+1}<\theta_k\).
The field norms of the approximants are \(q\), whereas the odd-degree limit has field norm \(-1\).

## An explicit decreasing sequence for every \(q\ge2\)

Take \(P_0=z^2-3z+1\) and \(f_0=z^2-(q+3)z+q+1\).
They satisfy the reciprocal identity used in (3), and give
\[
       A=z^3-(q+2)z^2+(q-2)z+1,\qquad B=q(1-z).
\]
At \(-1,0,1,q\) the signs of \(A\) are respectively negative, positive, negative and negative, with \(A(q)=-q^2-2q+1\).
Thus \(A\) has one root in \((-1,0)\), one in \((0,1)\) and one \(\lambda>q\).
Its exterior root is again a cubic Pisot unit by the same integer-factor argument.
The quadratic \(P_0\) has no circle zero, so (4) gives \(|B|<|A|\) on the circle except at \(-1\).
For each \(k\ge1\), Rouché at \(z^{2k-1}A+sB\), \(0\le s<1\), gives exactly \(2k+1\) interior roots.
At \(s=1\) there is still no circle zero: the only possible location is \(-1\), where \(f_k(-1)=-2A(-1)=4q>0\).
Thus the monic polynomial \(f_k=z^{2k-1}A+B\) has constant \(q\), degree \(2k+2\), and a unique exterior root \(\theta_k>\lambda\) since \(f_k(\lambda)=q(1-\lambda)<0\).
Its interior roots force irreducibility, and the argument after (4) gives \(\theta_k\downarrow\lambda\).
This provides all even degrees from four onward at every fixed \(q\ge2\) and identifies their common limit for this sequence.
It does not determine the least possible accumulation point of the positive-constant class.
