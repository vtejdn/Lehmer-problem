### 1. Positive actual radius and the full measure equations

Let alpha be a nontorsion algebraic unit of actual degree d, with monic irreducible minimal polynomial P, m=log M(P)>0 and O=Z[alpha].
On X=Hom(O,R/Z), write Haar probability as H and use the actual power basis to identify X with R^d/Z^d.
Multiplication by alpha has an integral unimodular dual matrix M, acting by S.
As in L240,

    w(theta)=cos^2(pi theta(1)),
    p_n=Pr{sum_(j=0)^(n-1) xi_j alpha^j
                 =sum_(j=0)^(n-1) xi'_j alpha^j},
    h=lim_n -log(p_n)/n, R=exp h,
    U(t)=sum p_n t^n=1/(1-F(t)) for0<t<R.             (1)

The bits are independent and fair, and the difference digits have their ORIGINAL law1/2,1/4,1/4.

In this nontorsion domain R>1.
To keep the dependence explicit, choose an expanding conjugate lambda, |lambda|>1, and an integer k>=1 with |lambda|^k>2.
Such a conjugate exists because m>0.
Condition the binary endpoint on all bit positions except0,k,2k,...<n.
The remaining sums are distinct even if lambda is nonreal: for a nonzero difference, its largest power has modulus strictly greater than the sum of the moduli of all lower powers, since |lambda|^k>2.
Hence each conditional endpoint atom is at most2^(-ceil(n/k)), and the same holds after averaging over the conditioned bits.
Collision probability is at most the largest atom, so

    p_n<=2^(-ceil(n/k)),
    h>=log2/k>0,       1<R<=2.                       (2)

This is the sparse-position mechanism of L215 with its actual conjugate and spacing supplied, not a degree-uniform lower bound.
The spacing k may grow with the input.

Retain L240's forward/backward Abel measures a_t,b_t, with Fourier coefficients A_t(x),B_t(x) in[0,1] on ALL x in O.
The limiting a_R,b_R exist on the entire torus.
Set eta_t=1-F(t), including the critical value eta_R=1-F(R).
The measure equations hold for every0<t<=R:

    a_t=eta_t H+t w (S^(-1)_*a_t),
    b_t=eta_t H+t S_*(w b_t).                         (3)

At R this follows by weak passage in L240, whether eta_R is zero or positive.
All terms are nonnegative measures.
The measures are even and their Fourier coefficients are real and nonnegative, properties inherited from the actual autocorrelations.
No stationary invariance of a_t or b_t under S is asserted.

### 2. Quantitative absence of atoms, including at criticality

Use the coordinate sup metric on the torus and let B(x,r) be its closed cube of radius r around x.
Put

    L=max{2, ||M||_infinity, ||M^(-1)||_infinity}.     (4)

Here each matrix norm is the maximum absolute row sum in the actual power basis.
Fix any t0 in(1,R), then choose0<delta<1/4 so that

    kappa=cos^2(pi delta), q=t0 kappa>1,
    gamma=log q/log L>0.                             (5)

These are explicit fixed-input parameters.
For every t in[t0,R] and either mu=a_t or mu=b_t,

    mu(B(0,r))<=q(r/delta)^gamma        (0<r<=delta),
    mu(B(x,r))<=2^d q(2r/delta)^gamma
                       (x in X,0<r<=delta/2).       (6)

First consider the centered cube.
On B(0,r), r<=delta, w>=kappa.
Since S B(0,r) contains B(0,r/L), the first equation in(3) gives

    a_t(B(0,r))>=t kappa a_t(S B(0,r))
                        >=q a_t(B(0,r/L)).          (7)

The set inclusion is checked by taking the representative y with sup norm at most r/L and applying M inverse; its image has sup norm at most r.
No global lift of every torus point is assumed.
For the second equation, evaluate on S B(0,r/L).
Haar nonnegativity and S B(0,r/L) contained in B(0,r) give

    b_t(B(0,r))>=b_t(S B(0,r/L))
                        >=q b_t(B(0,r/L)).          (8)

Iteration yields mu(B(0,delta L^(-n)))<=q^(-n).
Taking n=floor(log(delta/r)/log L) and using monotonicity proves the first line of(6), including the harmless leading q.

To move the center without an unjustified translation invariance, use the periodic tent T_r(u)=(1-|u|/r)_+ on the circle, with |u| the distance to its zero representative and0<r<1/2.
It equals the convolution of the indicators of[-r/2,r/2], divided by r.
Its Fourier coefficients are

    T_r_hat(j)=r [sin(pi j r)/(pi j r)]^2>=0,          (9)

with the continuous value r at j=0.
They are absolutely summable.
The d-fold product Phi_r(theta)=product_i T_r(theta_i) has nonnegative absolutely summable coefficients, is at most one, is supported in B(0,r), and is at least2^(-d) on B(0,r/2).
The Fourier coefficients of mu are nonnegative, so the absolutely convergent expansion gives

    integral Phi_r(theta-x) dmu(theta)
           <= integral Phi_r(theta) dmu(theta)
           <=mu(B(0,r)).                             (10)

Indeed every real cosine is at most one and every coefficient multiplying it is nonnegative.
Setting the tent radius to2r proves the second line of(6).
This explicit smoothing comparison, not stationarity of mu or a presumed radial density, justifies the all-center bound.

As r decreases to zero, (6) proves that a_t and b_t have NO atoms, uniformly for t in[t0,R].
In particular the critical measures at a recurrent nontorsion input are singular continuous: L240 proved pure singularity, and (6) now excludes every atom.
At a transient input they are L1 densities as before and are also atomless.
Convolution with a probability preserves the same all-center bound by averaging translated cubes, so c_t=a_t*b_t is atomless too.
An infinite-mean critical convolution may still have a singular, absolutely continuous or mixed continuous decomposition; boundedness remains exactly L240's separate finite-derivative condition.

The constants in(4)--(6) depend on the actual integer basis and on t0<R.
They may deteriorate as h tends to zero, the degree grows or the coefficients increase.
No uniform positive gamma is inferred for the unrestricted near-one class.

### 3. An actual Mahler-scale obstruction to subcritical Lp regularity

For1<t<R, let f_t and g_t be L240's genuine L1 densities.
For any finite p>=1,

    p log t>m  implies f_t,g_t are NOT in Lp(H).       (11)

Moreover f_t and g_t are essentially unbounded for every1<t<R.
This concerns the subcritical densities themselves, not an informal density assigned to the singular critical measures.

Here is a complete local volume argument with all expanding multiplicities.
Because the actual minimal polynomial is separable, the real matrix M is real-conjugate by an invertible Q to blocks consisting of real scalar eigenvalues and two-dimensional rotation-dilations for nonreal conjugate pairs.
Let ||y||_* be the maximum of absolute values on real blocks and Euclidean norms on complex blocks.
Choose K>=1 with ||Qy||_infinity<=K||y||_*; for example the sum of the Euclidean lengths of the corresponding row blocks, maximized over rows and enlarged to at least one, works.
Let v_* be the volume of the product of unit real intervals and unit disks.
Write rho_b for each block's eigenvalue modulus, with real dimension d_b=1 or2.
The complete expanding determinant is

    product_(rho_b>1) rho_b^(d_b)=exp m.             (12)

For0<epsilon<1/4 and integer N>=1, take the real block region

    E_N^+={Qy: ||y_b||<=epsilon K^(-1)
                              min(1,rho_b^(-N)) for all b}.

Each M^j E_N^+ lies in the coordinate cube B(0,epsilon) before projection to the torus for0<=j<=N.
This follows block by block: rotations preserve Euclidean norm, and rho_b^j min(1,rho_b^(-N))<=1.
E_N^+ itself lies in the injective coordinate neighborhood B(0,epsilon), so its Haar mass is exactly its ordinary real volume,

    H(E_N^+)=C epsilon^d exp(-Nm),
    C=|det Q| v_* K^(-d)>0.                          (13)

All neutral blocks contribute their fixed epsilon volume, and every complex expanding block is counted twice.
Thus no growing-degree constant is concealed in the exponent.
For M inverse the same construction gives E_N^- of the SAME volume: a unit has sum of positive log moduli equal to sum of negative log moduli in absolute value, so its inverse also has expanding determinant exp m.
The same Q,K may be used, with the block rotations inverted.

Put kappa_epsilon=cos^2(pi epsilon).
On E_N^+ all N factors of W_N are at least kappa_epsilon; on E_N^- the same holds for the N backward factors of V_N.
The nonnegative Abel sums therefore give

    f_t>=U(t)^(-1)(t kappa_epsilon)^N on E_N^+,
    g_t>=U(t)^(-1)(t kappa_epsilon)^N on E_N^-.

Consequently, for finite p>=1,

    integral f_t^p dH, integral g_t^p dH
       >=C epsilon^d U(t)^(-p)
                  exp[N(p log(t kappa_epsilon)-m)].  (14)

If p log t>m, choose epsilon so small that the exponent coefficient remains positive.
The right side then grows without bound as N tends to infinity, proving(11).
For essential unboundedness, choose epsilon with t kappa_epsilon>1; the displayed lower values tend to infinity on sets of positive Haar measure.
No point evaluation at theta=0 or interchange of divergent series is used to prove an essential-norm statement.

The criterion is one-sided.
It does not assert Lp membership when p log t<=m, and it does not classify the equality case.
Since h<=m and log t<h, it never contradicts the original L1 statement.
If F(R)<1, the same proof applies to the actual critical L1 densities with t=R and finite U(R); if F(R)=1 there are no such critical Haar densities and(14) is not used with U(R)=infinity.

For a fixed p>1, whenever p h>m, the interval

    exp(m/p)<t<R                                    (15)

is nonempty and both factors fail Lp throughout it.
In particular if h=m, every fixed p>1 fails sufficiently near R.
A proposed proof using uniform Lp control of the individual factors must account for this actual obstruction.
It cannot be supplied merely from their probability normalization or from L240's good convolution behavior.
Conversely h<m/p would not establish the desired Lp estimates.

### 5. A quantitative whole-order coefficient bound

The same small-cube estimate also constrains occupation coefficients without assuming that their full sum is finite.
For integer N>=1 with1/(4N)<=delta, set r=1/(4N).
Formula(9) gives, for every |j|<=N,

    T_r_hat(j)>=r(8/pi^2),

because |pi j r|<=pi/4 and sin u/u>=sin(pi/4)/(pi/4)=2sqrt2/pi on this interval.
One may verify this monotonicity by differentiating sin u/u; sin u-u cos u has derivative u sin u>=0.
Taking products and using all nonnegative Fourier coefficients in(10),

    r^d(8/pi^2)^d sum_(||x||_infinity<=N) mu_hat(x)
           <=integral Phi_r dmu
           <=q(r/delta)^gamma.

Therefore uniformly in t in[t0,R],

    sum_(||x||_infinity<=N) A_t(x),
    sum_(||x||_infinity<=N) B_t(x)
       <=q delta^(-gamma)(pi^2/8)^d
                                      (4N)^(d-gamma). (18)

These coefficient cubes are in the actual power basis of O, not in the torus.
Since0<=sigma_t(x)=A_t(x)B_t(x)<=min(A_t(x),B_t(x)), the SAME bound holds for their product and hence for the complete corrected occupation coefficients of L236.
At critical recurrence it applies directly to that unnormalized occupation measure, even if its total mass is infinite.

This is a sub-volume growth bound with exponent d-gamma and a fully input-dependent constant.
It does not make the infinite sum finite, prove a dimension-independent confinement scale, or force a gap.
In particular a zero density of occupation coefficients in large coordinate boxes is compatible with both finite and infinite critical mean.
The exact Fourier positivity is doing work here; the conclusion is not borrowed from a generic stationary probability or from the different normalized quotient chain.
