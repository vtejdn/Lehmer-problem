<a id="low-rank-original-input-scope"></a>
## Scope and fixed interfaces

The question is whether a nonunitary rational integer germ with low first-coefficient rank can retain only one copy of the original polynomial's pole cost while making its boundary singular-value cost comparable to its total Mahler measure.
[L133 v8](Yin_L_G0133_v8.md#matrix-original-input-scope) retains the earlier actual matrix constructions and exact denominator, positivity and basis restrictions.
[L133 v9](Yin_L_G0133_v9.md#trace-gram-cost-scope) retains the natural trace-Gram comparison and the arithmetic failure of its cheap real realization.
Versions v1--v9 remain fixed.
The present calculations concern new scalar transfer outputs and products of two shears, not another application of the full standard-companion or trace-Gram candidate.

The exact analytic price interface used below is the [C868 v5 statement](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v5.md#statement), with its [complete argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v5.md#argument) and [selected rational-price assessment](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v5.md#decision-whole-rational-price-review-2026-11-05).
That adoption supplies the exact infimum at its stated rational-matrix scope; the singular-value, pole and Jensen calculations specific to this exploration are proved directly here.
For a rational germ F(0)=I, that interface identifies
\[
 \tau_{\min}(F)=J(F)+N(F),\qquad
 J(F)=\int_{\mathbb T}\sum_i\log^+\sigma_i(F)\,d\mu,
 \quad N(F)=\sum_{|a|<1}\delta_a(F)\log\frac1{|a|}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The local delta is the full pole-cancellation colength, not the net determinant pole order.
The admissible Q,U are complex disk Schur functions with U=FQ, Q(0) invertible and total price -log|det Q(0)|.
No division by the matrix size or original polynomial degree occurs.
The [earlier matrix integer-germ bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v3.md#matrix-column-determinant-gap) supplies the original arithmetic motivation, not the sharper shear formulas below.
No rational-number or integer coefficients are inferred for an optimizing or approximating Schur multiplier.

Unless specified otherwise, P is monic irreducible reciprocal in Z[z], has degree d>=2 and P(0)=1.
Its total logarithmic measure is denoted m=m(P)>=0; the original G0001 comparison concerns m>0.
Irreducibility in characteristic zero makes its roots simple, and its unit constant coefficient implies
\[
 \sum_{|a|<1,\ P(a)=0}\log\frac1{|a|}=m(P).
 \tag{2}
\]
Circle roots have weight zero in (2), but can contribute real boundary singularities to J.
All circle integrals use normalized Haar measure.

<a id="rank-one-shear-exact-cost"></a>
## Rank-one normalization, exact singular values and actual cancellation

Let u,v in Z^r be nonzero, r>=2, with v^T u=0, and put
\[
 \kappa=\|u\|_2\|v\|_2\ge1,\qquad
 F=I_r+f(z)uv^T,
 \qquad f(z)=\frac{A(z)}{P(z)^s},\quad s\ge1,
 \tag{3}
\]
where A is a nonzero integer polynomial with A(0)=0.
Then F(0)=I, its Taylor coefficients are integral and det F=1.
Both vectors are real and orthogonal, so an orthogonal change of coordinates sends the nontrivial block to I_2+kappa f E_12; all other singular values are one.
This is a norm computation, not an arithmetic change of basis used to claim a new integral realization.

If such a fixed integral rank-one nilpotent direction has nonprimitive content, that content can be absorbed into f and primitive integral u,v chosen.
For primitive u and v the entries of uv^T have greatest common divisor one.
An integer linear combination of those entries is therefore one, so integrality of all Taylor coefficients of F-I forces integrality of the Taylor coefficients of f.
Multiplying by P^s then makes its polynomial numerator integral.
A fractional scalar hidden in a nonprimitive direction cannot avoid the required arithmetic normalization.

Write f=C/P^k after canceling all common factors, where k>=0, C is a nonzero integer polynomial and P does not divide C when k>0.
If s copies of P cancel, k=0 and f is polynomial.
The monicity of P makes these divisions integral.
For t=kappa|f|, the two nontrivial singular values are L(t) and L(t)^(-1), where
\[
 L(t)=\frac{\sqrt{4+t^2}+t}{2},\qquad
 \log L(t)=\operatorname{arsinh}(t/2).
 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed their product is one, while the squared singular values have sum 2+t^2; these two relations determine them.
Consequently
\[
 J(F)=\int_{\mathbb T}\operatorname{arsinh}\left(\frac{\kappa|C/P^k|}{2}\right)d\mu.
 \tag{5}
\]

At every root a of P, a genuine pole of f has order k.
Irreducibility and P not dividing C ensure that C(a) is nonzero at every such root.
The nontrivial two-by-two block has local exponents -k,+k, with the other r-2 exponents zero: its smallest entry order is -k and its determinant is one.
Thus delta_a(F)=k.
For k=0 no finite pole remains.
In either case (2) gives
\[
 N(F)=k m(P),\qquad
 \tau_{\min}(F)=k m(P)+J(F)
 \tag{6}
\]
at the exact-price interface (1).
The state dimension or a scalar denominator repeated on r columns would overcount this actual pole expense.

<a id="integer-numerator-boundary-floor"></a>
## Every nonzero integer numerator retains a boundary floor

Define
\[
 h(x)=\operatorname{arsinh}(e^x/2),\qquad
 h''(x)=\frac{4e^x}{(4+e^{2x})^{3/2}}>0.
 \tag{7}
\]
The boundary logarithm of a nonzero rational function is integrable, including its finitely many circle zeros and poles.
Its positive growth in (5) is likewise logarithmically integrable.
The scalar mean identity is integral log(kappa|f|)=log kappa+m(C)-k m(P).
Jensen's inequality for the strictly convex function h therefore gives
\[
 J(F)\ge
 \operatorname{arsinh}\left(\frac{\kappa e^{m(C)-k m(P)}}2\right)
 \ge\operatorname{arsinh}\left(\frac{\kappa e^{-k m(P)}}2\right).
 \tag{8}
\]
The second step uses m(C)>=0, which follows from the integer leading coefficient and the usual root formula for Mahler measure.
This bound applies to every nonzero integral numerator, not only a chosen monomial or a bounded-degree search.

For a genuinely simple original denominator k=1, (8) leaves a positive limit at least arsinh(1/2) as m(P) tends to zero.
Thus on a hypothetical sequence with m(P) tending to zero, this shear construction cannot have J(F)<=C_tr m(P) with one fixed C_tr.
No existence of that sequence is asserted.
Allowing a large denominator power does not make the total cost free: for x>=0,
\[
 \frac{d}{dx}\left[x+\operatorname{arsinh}(\kappa e^{-x}/2)\right]
 =1-\frac{\kappa e^{-x}}{\sqrt{4+\kappa^2e^{-2x}}}>0.
\]
Equations (6)--(8) imply the complete lower bound
\[
 \tau_{\min}(F)\ge\operatorname{arsinh}(\kappa/2)
                         \ge\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2).
 \tag{9}
\]
A fully canceled polynomial numerator also satisfies (9), now with k=0.
These are costs of the supplied shear; they do not supply an upper cost bound by the original measure.

<a id="circle-root-shear-log-cost"></a>
## A circle root becomes an integrable but paid logarithmic singularity

Suppose k>0 and beta=e^(i theta_0) is a root of P on T.
For the reduced numerator C, C(beta) is nonzero and P'(beta) is nonzero.
As theta tends to theta_0 along the circle,
\[
 \operatorname{arsinh}\left(\frac{\kappa|f(e^{i\theta})|}{2}\right)
 =k\log\frac1{|\theta-\theta_0|}
   +\log\frac{\kappa|C(\beta)|}{|P'(\beta)|^k}+o(1).
 \tag{10}
\]
This follows by first expanding P(e^(i theta))=i beta P'(beta)(theta-theta_0)+O((theta-theta_0)^2), then using arsinh(t/2)=log t+o(1) for t tending to infinity.
For an arc |theta-theta_0|<epsilon containing no other pole, its normalized contribution is
\[
 \frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}\left[
 k\log\frac1\varepsilon+k+
 \log\frac{\kappa|C(\beta)|}{|P'(\beta)|^k}\right]+o(\varepsilon).
 \tag{11}
\]
It is finite and positive for sufficiently small epsilon, but the permitted arc size and constant are input-dependent.
There is no free omission of this expense merely because the root contributes zero to (2).

The existing Salem quartic in [C206, Section 6](../claims/Yin_C_G0206_v1.md#argument) provides an actual example:
\[
 P_S=z^4-z^3-z^2-z+1
    =z^2\bigl((z+z^{-1})^2-(z+z^{-1})-3\bigr).
 \tag{12}
\]
That retained argument verifies irreducibility and the trace roots (1+sqrt13)/2>2 and (1-sqrt13)/2 in (-2,2), hence two circle roots and one exterior-interior reciprocal pair.
For f=z^2/P_S, the shear has N=m(P_S) and exact boundary cost
\[
 J=\int_{\mathbb T}
 \operatorname{arsinh}\frac1{2|(2\cos\theta)^2-2\cos\theta-3|}\,d\mu.
 \tag{13}
\]
The two genuine circle singularities obey (10) with k=1.
The [circle-unitary pole rigidity](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v3.md#original-circle-pole-rigidity) does not apply to this nonunitary shear, but the expense is present in its actual J.
No numerical integral or new classification of this quartic is being asserted.

<a id="companion-siso-shear-transfer"></a>
## A single companion transfer removes the repeated pole rank, not the boundary floor

Use the standard column companion C_P for P, with zero-based coordinate vectors e_0,...,e_(d-1).
Its subdiagonal entries are one and its last column is the negatives of P's coefficients below the leading term.
Reciprocity gives det(I-zC_P)=z^d P(1/z)=P(z).
The inverse cofactor in its last row and first column is z^(d-1): after deleting the first row and last column, the remaining upper-bidiagonal minor has diagonal -z, and its cofactor sign cancels (-1)^(d-1).
Therefore the exact scalar transfer is
\[
 f_P(z)=e_{d-1}^T(I-zC_P)^{-1}e_0
       =\frac{z^{d-1}}{P(z)}.
 \tag{14}
\]
As d>=2, f_P(0)=0 and its first nonzero coefficient is one at degree d-1.
The matrix I_2+f_P E_12 consequently has first positive coefficient rank one, all Taylor coefficients integral and
\[
 N=m(P),\qquad
 J=\int_{\mathbb T}\operatorname{arsinh}\frac1{2|P|}\,d\mu
 \ge\operatorname{arsinh}(e^{-m(P)}/2).
 \tag{15}
\]
No root cancels against z^(d-1), because P(0)=1.
This scalar output really avoids the degree-fold scalar-copy pole price.
It is not the full normalized-companion matrix rejected in the earlier comparison.
But the integer-numerator boundary floor survives exactly, so low output rank alone does not supply the original measure comparison.

<a id="log-derivative-degree-cost"></a>
## Logarithmic derivatives retain the full original degree

Another integral scalar output is
\[
 f_{\rm der}(z)=\frac{zP'(z)}{P(z)},\qquad
 F_{\rm der}=I_2+f_{\rm der}E_{12}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The numerator vanishes at zero and is coprime to P, so N(F_der)=m(P).
For the same companion, differentiating its determinant also identifies this as
\[
 f_{\rm der}=-z\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(C_P(I-zC_P)^{-1}\bigr).
\]
On any circle arc avoiding roots, reciprocity and real coefficients give
\[
 P(e^{i\theta})=e^{id\theta/2}R(\theta),\qquad R(\theta)\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}.
\]
Differentiating this equality gives i f_der=i d/2+R'/R, with R'/R real.
Hence the exact boundary identity and its cost consequence are
\[
 \operatorname{Re}f_{\rm der}=d/2,\qquad
 J(F_{\rm der})\ge\operatorname{arsinh}(d/4).
 \tag{17}
\]
Finitely many circle roots do not affect this almost-everywhere inequality.
Even a rank-one shear output has therefore retained an original-degree boundary expense.

For a fully explicit irreducible fixed-positive-measure control, let
\[
 h=5^\ell\ (\ell\ge0),\qquad P_h(z)=z^{2h}-3z^h+1,\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},\quad m_0=\log\lambda.
 \tag{18}
\]
Modulo five, P_h(z)=(z^h+1)^2=(z+1)^(2h), using the Frobenius identity for h=5^ell.
Thus P_h(z-1) is monic and all nonleading coefficients are divisible by five, while its constant coefficient is P_h(-1)=5, since h is odd.
Eisenstein's criterion at five proves that every P_h is irreducible over Q, including h=1.
Its h exterior roots have modulus lambda^(1/h), so m(P_h)=m_0 for every ell.
It has no circle roots.

The exact derivative numerator is
\[
 zP_h'=h z^h(2z^h-3),\qquad m(zP_h')=\log(3h).
 \tag{19}
\]
Indeed the binomial's h roots all have modulus (3/2)^(1/h), making its Mahler measure three before the scalar h is included.
The reduced denominator is still P_h, so the complete price satisfies
\[
 \tau_{\min}(F_{{\rm der},h})=m_0+J(F_{{\rm der},h}),\qquad
 J(F_{{\rm der},h})\ge
 \operatorname{arsinh}\frac{3h}{2\lambda}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{20}
\]
Equation (17) also gives the direct pointwise lower bound arsinh(h/2).
This excludes a uniform price C_tr m(P_h) for this logarithmic-derivative construction on an actual irreducible positive-measure family, without removable cyclotomic content.
It is not a near-one sequence.

<a id="two-shear-noncommuting-cost"></a>
## Two noncommuting shears: exact boundary mixing and the fixed-column floor

Let f and g be rational germs vanishing at zero, and set
\[
 M=(I_2+fE_{12})(I_2+gE_{21})
   =\begin{pmatrix}1+fg&f\\g&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Its determinant is one.
If its two singular values are L and L^(-1), L>=1, the trace of M*M gives the exact pointwise density
\[
 \sum_i\log^+\sigma_i(M)
 =\operatorname{arsinh}\left(
 \frac{\sqrt{|f|^2+|g|^2+|fg|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(fg)}}2\right).
 \tag{22}
\]
The radicand is nonnegative, as follows either from this trace or from the completion
\[
 |f|^2+|g|^2+|fg|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(fg)
 =\frac{|f|^4}{1+|f|^2}
 +(1+|f|^2)\left|g+\frac{\overline f}{1+|f|^2}\right|^2.
 \tag{23}
\]
The two factors need not commute, and the boundary price need not equal the sum of their individual prices.

Nevertheless the second column remains exactly (f,1)^T, so
\[
 \sum_i\log^+\sigma_i(M)=\log\|M\|_{\rm op}
       \ge\tfrac12\log(1+|f|^2).
\]
For f=A/P with nonzero integer A and A(0)=0, convexity of x to (1/2)log(1+e^(2x)) and scalar Jensen give
\[
 J(M)\ge\frac12\log\bigl(1+e^{2(m(A)-m(P))}\bigr)
       \ge\frac12\log(1+e^{-2m(P)}).
 \tag{24}
\]
Thus this two-factor order still has a fixed boundary floor near m(P)=0, even with arbitrary g.
The pointwise minimizing choice g=-conjugate(f)/(1+|f|^2) in (23) need not be a holomorphic or integral rational germ; it is not a free arithmetic construction.

If specifically f=A/P and g=B/P, with nonzero integer A,B vanishing at zero and neither divisible by irreducible P, then every original root is a simple pole of both scalar functions.
At such a root the (1,1) entry in (21) has genuine pole order two and det M=1, so the local exponents are -2,+2 and delta=2.
Consequently
\[
 N(M)=2m(P).
 \tag{25}
\]
If either scalar numerator cancels P, this argument must be changed: with just one remaining simple pole the offdiagonal entry gives delta=1, and with no scalar poles delta=0.
The order-two assertion is not imposed after a canceled denominator has been discarded.

<a id="boundary-improves-total-cost-worsens"></a>
## A concrete boundary improvement that costs more in total

For the irreducible family P_h in (18), now use the different primitive numerator
\[
 f_h=z^h/P_h,\qquad S_h=I_2+f_hE_{12},\qquad
 M_h=(I_2+f_hE_{12})(I_2-f_hE_{21}).
 \tag{26}
\]
On the circle, with x=z^h,
\[
 f_h=\frac1{x+x^{-1}-3}\in\mathbb R,
 \qquad |f_h|=\frac1{3-2\cos(h\theta)}\le1.
\]
Both are nonconstant integral germs normalized at zero, and the denominators are genuine.
Their first positive coefficients occur at h and are E_12 and E_12-E_21, of ranks one and two respectively, independently of the original degree 2h.
Write J_1=J(S_h) and J_2=J(M_h).
Equations (5) and (22) give
\[
 J_1=\int\operatorname{arsinh}(|f_h|/2)\,d\mu,
 \qquad
 J_2=\int\operatorname{arsinh}(|f_h|^2/2)\,d\mu<J_1.
 \tag{27}
\]
The strict inequality holds because 0<|f_h|<1 away from finitely many circle points.
Both integrals are independent of h by Haar invariance under z to z^h.
Moreover
\[
 J_1\le\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)=m_0/2,
 \quad N(S_h)=m_0,\quad N(M_h)=2m_0.
 \tag{28}
\]
Thus the single shear has the actual upper infimum bound tau_min(S_h)<=3m_0/2, while the two-factor mixture satisfies
\[
 \tau_{\min}(M_h)-\tau_{\min}(S_h)
   =m_0+J_2-J_1\ge m_0/2>0.
 \tag{29}
\]
The boundary improvement is real, but the full matrix pole cost more than offsets it.
Since J_1<m_0/2, the rational approximation assertion at interface (1) also supplies admissible complex rational pairs for S_h with price strictly below 3m_0/2.
The finite-rank positive example remains within the already separated fixed-measure quadratic-substitution class; it supplies no small-measure input.

<a id="low-rank-return-and-limits"></a>
## The remaining original-input boundary

One scalar companion transfer pays only the actual original pole measure, yet every integral numerator in a rank-one shear retains the Jensen boundary floor.
The logarithmic-derivative output additionally retains an explicit full-degree expense, with a genuine irreducible fixed-measure divergent control.
Two noncommuting factors can reduce boundary growth, but the displayed order keeps a fixed column floor and can increase the complete pole charge.
These results concern the exact constructions and hypotheses above.

A longer matrix product can alter the fixed column or fixed diagonal structure used in these bounds.
The [earlier circle-unitary models](Yin_L_G0133_v8.md#matrix-power-pullback-realization), with zero boundary singular-value cost on their stated no-circle-root input, already show why the shear floors cannot be promoted to every integral matrix germ.
Conversely, the [power-pullback pole obstruction](Yin_L_G0133_v8.md#power-pullback-circle-pole-rigidity) remains a restriction on circle-unitary constructions at inputs with a circle conjugate, not on every nonunitary matrix.
No argument here excludes all multi-factor or general matrix constructions.
The missing original-input supply and uniform upper bound for J+N remain explicit, and G0001 is not resolved by this comparison.
