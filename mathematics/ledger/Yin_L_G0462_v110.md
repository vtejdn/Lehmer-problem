# The first fractional trace coefficient approaches an integer on sparse sequences

<a id="first"></a>
## Coefficients on the scale of the first fractional index

Fix a bound \(K\) on the number of terms of an actual normalized trace lift \(F_i=\Pi_i/j_i\) on any positive-constant Pisot branch, in either parity.
On any sequence with \(\theta_i-q_i\to0\), let \(d_i\) be the index of the first nonintegral coefficient of \(F_i\).
This index exists and satisfies \(d_i=o(h_i)\) by the [first nondivisible index theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) and the sparse height bound.
For every fixed \(A\ge1\),
\[
                    \max_{0\le l\le A d_i}
                  \operatorname{dist}\big([z^l]F_i,\mathbb Z\big)\longrightarrow0 .
\]
In particular the coefficient at \(d_i\) itself has reduced denominator tending to infinity, while its distance to the integers tends to zero.
This locates a diverging denominator at the first fractional coefficient, refining the [existence of a sublinear index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#statement).
The conclusion allows the limiting nearest integer there to be zero.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## A nonnegative Fourier density on a torus

Let \(\Gamma\) be a free abelian group of finite rank with a translation-invariant total order.
Suppose \(S\in\mathbb R[\Gamma]\) has constant one and all its other exponents are positive, its constant is a vertex, and \(M(S)=1\).
Let \(\mu\) be a probability on its dual torus and let \(J\) have coefficients \(2\widehat\mu(v)\) at positive \(v\), one at zero and zero at negative \(v\).
The product \(SJ\) uses finite convolution.
If \(SJ\) has integer coefficients and \(S\) has a nonintegral coefficient, there is no nonzero nonnegative \(f\) with \(f\,dm\le\mu\) and \(H=2Sf\in\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\), where \(m\) is Haar probability.

Indeed \(L=H/S=2f\) is real and integrable.
It is a Laurent polynomial over \(\mathbb C\) by the [slice division argument](Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#division): choose an integral exposing direction separating the exponents of \(S\), so its leading coefficient is a scalar times a character of the transverse variables and its constant is one.
Every slice on the transverse torus is free of open-disk roots by equality in Jensen and continuity at fixed degree.
Almost everywhere a nonzero finite pole of \(H/S\) would be on the circle or would force its reciprocal conjugate pole in the open disk.
Integrability and the root condition exclude both possibilities.
After clearing monomials, polynomial division has zero remainder on almost every slice, so the Laurent polynomial remainder over \(\mathbb C\) vanishes identically.
If \(L\) is a positive constant, \(L=1\) or \(2\) by the constant of \(H\) and the mass bound.
The first gives \(H=S\) integral and the second exhausts \(\mu\) as Haar, giving \(SJ=S\) integral.

Otherwise choose a real linear functional \(\ell\) injective on \(\Gamma\).
Let \(a,b\) maximize it on the supports of \(S,L\) respectively, so \(\ell(b)>0\) by reality of \(L\).
Every vertex coefficient of \(S\) has modulus at most \(M(S)=1\) by vertex Jensen.
Hence \(|L_b|\ge1\) and \(L_b\) is real since \(S_aL_b\) is a nonzero integer.
Averaging over the annihilator of the subgroup \(\mathbb Z b\) leaves just \(L_0+L_b X^b+\overline{L_b}X^{-b}\), because no other multiple of \(b\) occurs.
Nonnegativity and the mass of \(f\) give \(2\ge L_0\ge2|L_b|\ge2\).
It follows that \(L_b=\epsilon=\pm1\) and \(f\,dm=\mu\).

The average vanishes on \(X^b=-\epsilon\); by continuity and nonnegativity, \(L\) vanishes on this entire set.
Write \(b=m_0 b_0\) with \(m_0\ge1\) and \(b_0\) primitive and use \(y=X^{b_0}\) as one coordinate.
At each of the \(m_0\) roots of \(y^{m_0}=-\epsilon\) the value and the angular derivative vanish identically in the remaining torus variables.
Fourier uniqueness gives divisibility by \((y^{m_0}+\epsilon)^2\) after clearing a monomial.
Thus \(2+\epsilon(X^b+X^{-b})\) divides \(L\) in the Laurent ring.
Width with respect to \(\ell\) is additive in a nonzero product; both the divisor and \(L\) have width \(2\ell(b)\).
The quotient has singleton support since \(\ell\) is injective.
The largest exponent and its coefficient give the quotient one.
Consequently the ordered germ \(J=1+\epsilon X^{b'}\), with \(b'\) the positive one of \(b,-b\) in the given order.
Integrality of \(SJ\) implies integrality of \(S\) by the coefficient recurrence on each coset of \(\mathbb Z b'\), starting below the finite support on that coset.
This contradiction proves the assertion in every rank.

## Frozen real faces and the first fractional scale

Suppose the coefficient conclusion fails.
Pass to a subsequence on which one of the boundedly many exponents \(e_i\le A d_i\) has a coefficient tending to a noninteger.
The normalized coefficients are bounded since \(M(F_i)=1\) and the term count is bounded.
Extract the full ordered limiting coefficient tuple and stabilize all relations on the exponents, including the eventual signs of fixed lattice elements.
The [logarithmic sublevel estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#moving) gives measure one for the frozen full lattice polynomial even if some coefficients tend to zero.
Partition the scales from \(d_i\) through \(h_i\) as in the [separated faces](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#faces), with successive representatives \(s,H\) satisfying \(s/H\to0\).
At each step the terms with exponents \(O(s)\) include the distinguished \(e_i\).
Freezing their coefficients gives a polynomial \(S\) on their stabilized lattice with nonintegral coefficient there, constant one at a vertex and all other exponents positive in the eventual order.
Zero limiting terms may be omitted.
The face exposure and Jensen argument applies to the real frozen numerator, giving \(M(S)=1\).
Write \(S_i\) for its evaluation at the original exponents, including any real signs.
Its term count and absolute coefficient sum are uniformly bounded, its constant is one, and \(M(S_i)\to1\) by Lawton.
Up to any sufficiently small fixed fraction of \(H\), \(F_i\) agrees with the actual early polynomial whose coefficient absolute sum distance from \(S_i\) tends to zero.

At any scale \(s/t,t/H\to0\) the ordered base tests and positive mixed row tests of the [intermediate-row argument](Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#rows) are consequently integral in the limit.
Each replacement error is \(o(1)\) because all Taylor coefficients of the actual inverse series are uniformly bounded.
The transform of \(2|S|^2\Omega\) takes finitely many values: on each positive layer it multiplies a uniformly bounded integer transform by \(S^*\), and on the zero layer it takes the coefficients of \(S^*(SJ)+S(SJ)^*\).
The [finite-valued decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#slopes) uses interpolation and the integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem in any finite rank.
Dividing away from \(S=0\) gives the separated slope densities \(f\,dm\le\mu\) with \(2Sf\) integral Laurent by the mixed row tests.
On the zero set all weighted rows vanish.
The preceding torus lemma excludes every such density, and the last positive full-dimensional harmonic is excluded as in L462v105 because \(M(S)=1\) and \(SJ\) integral forbids the Haar marginal.
The remaining projected rows are supported on fixed proper torsion translates from the weighted base marginal.
The [character multipliers](Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#multiplier) therefore give a product \(U_i\) of boundedly many cyclotomic binomials of degree \(O(s)\) such that \(U_i B_i^\sharp\) vanishes on \(Ls\le n\le H/L\) for a fixed \(L\), after extraction.

## Iterating the real coefficient bound

Initially the Taylor coefficients of the inverse series below \(d_i\) are integers by formal division, since the prefix of the actual \(F_i\) below \(d_i\) is integral with constant one.
The [initial all-interval estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial) gives \(P=1\) or a signed binomial of degree \(O(d_i)\) and bounded sums of absolute coefficients of \(PB\) on every interval of length \(d_i\), for the actual \(B=B_i^\sharp\).
Inductively \(P\) has degree \(O(s)\), constant one, bounded support and coefficients in a fixed finite real set, with this all-interval estimate at \(s\).
Put \(C=PU_i B,\ V=PU_i S_i\).
On a prefix through \(H/L\) the zero interval and the estimate at \(s\) give bounded coefficient absolute sum for \(C\).
Also \(C\) and \(VJ_i\) differ coefficientwise there by \(o(1)\) uniformly because \(\|PU_i\|_{\rm coef}=O(1)\) and the actual early numerator converges in coefficient absolute sum to \(S_i\).
Enlarging \(L\) ensures the tested prefix is below every omitted exponent.

As in the [approximate Gram estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v108.md#return), on any fixed \(k\) indices in an interval of diameter \(H/(4L)\) the positive Gram matrix for \(v^n V(v^{-1})\) has norm at most \(C_0+k\,o(1)\) with \(C_0\) independent of \(k\).
This follows from the two-sided moment identity using the bounded initial sum for \(C\), with the \(o(1)\) coefficient errors and the vanishing negative mass.
The coefficients of \(VB\) lie in a fixed finite real set, since \(B\) has bounded integer coefficients and \(P,U_i,S_i\) each have boundedly many terms from fixed finite sets.
Each nonzero coefficient has modulus at least some fixed \(\delta>0\).
Outside the boundedly many intervals \([e,e+\deg V]\) at the actual support exponents of \(F_i\), the coefficients are moments against the fixed partial sum on each piece, uniformly bounded since \(K\) is fixed.
A fixed sufficiently large \(k\) in the Bessel estimate bounds the number of nonzero coefficients per block of diameter \(H/(4L)\).
On the exceptional intervals of length \(O(s+1)\), \(VB=(U_iS_i)(PB)\) has bounded coefficient absolute sum by the previous estimate.
This gives the same all-location bound at scale \(H\) with \(V\) replacing \(P\), whose bounded real coefficients and support satisfy the induction hypotheses.

At the final scale \(h_i\) the entire product \(PB\) has bounded support and coefficients in one finite real set.
Its constant is one, every specialized face has \(M(S_i)\ge1\) by its constant and has \(M(S_i)\to1\), and the number of scales is fixed after extraction.
Thus \(M(PB)>1\) tends to one, contradicting the [fixed-tuple endpoint argument](Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations).
This proves the uniform convergence on each interval of length \(A d_i\).
A noninteger rational of reduced denominator at most \(D\) has distance at least \(1/D\) from the integers, so the first fractional denominator diverges as claimed.
