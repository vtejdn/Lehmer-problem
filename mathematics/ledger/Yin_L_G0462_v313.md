# Recovering the two largest terms of a reciprocal-unit relation

<a id="recovery"></a>
## An injective projection for fixed coefficients

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), put \(m=\log M(\alpha)\), and let \(\iota\) be the involution of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) with \(\iota(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}\).
Fix a complete tuple of nonzero rational integer coefficients in a zero relation, including its marked term.
Select two labeled terms with coefficients \(a,b\), prescribing which has the smaller exponent \(s<t\).
Let \(z\) denote the negative sum of all the other terms, so the question is whether \(z=a\alpha^s+b\alpha^t\).
Assume \(z\ne0\), as holds for a nondegenerate relation of weight at least three.
Then the other terms, with their actual exponents, determine at most one pair \(s,t\).
Indeed,
\[
        z\iota(z)=a^2+b^2+ab(\alpha^{t-s}+\alpha^{s-t}).
\]
A value \(v+v^{-1}\) determines the unordered pair \(\{v,v^{-1}\}\) as the roots of a single quadratic.
Since \(\alpha\) has infinite order, it determines at most one positive gap \(t-s\).
The equality \(\alpha^s=z/(a+b\alpha^{t-s})\) then determines \(s\) uniquely.
The denominator is nonzero in every realization.
This also gives injectivity on translation classes when the remaining terms are given up to a common translation, since translating them scales \(z\) by the corresponding power and leaves \(z\iota(z)\) unchanged.
A fixed order of all the labels makes the pair orientation known; allowing either orientation gives at most two completions.

In particular, for a relation with \(r\) distinct exponents in a window of span at most \(W\), deleting the two chosen terms gives at most \(\binom{W}{r-3}\) possible translation classes for each fixed order of all the labeled coefficients.
The remaining \(r-2\) positions can be normalized to start at zero and chosen in \([0,W]\).
For the [full-tuple witness](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#statement) the unmarked coefficients are already ordered; inserting the marked term into that order costs at most \(r\) possibilities.
This projection retains the actual gaps between the smaller terms and may lose injectivity if one also divides their exponents by their own gcd.

<a id="norm"></a>
## The congruence and the span of the smaller terms

Take the two largest coefficient magnitudes \(A=|a|,\ B=|b|\), put \(\Delta=|A-B|\), \(\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}(ab)\), and \(u=t-s>0\).
Write \(S\) for the sum of the other coefficient magnitudes and \(w\) for the span of their exponents.
The identity above becomes
\[
      z\iota(z)-\Delta^2
             =\epsilon AB\,\alpha^{-u}(\alpha^u+\epsilon)^2 .
\]
In particular the left side is nonzero and belongs to \(AB\,\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Both the divisibility and the required square times a power of \(\alpha\) concern data computable after the two exceptional terms have been deleted.
At any embedding with image \(\beta\) for \(\alpha\), translate the remaining exponents to the interval \([0,w]\) in the product \(z\iota(z)\).
Its modulus is then at most \(S^2\exp(w|\log|\beta||)\).
Taking the absolute norm and using \(\sum_\beta|\log|\beta||=2m\) gives
\[
     \log(AB)+\frac2d\log|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^u+\epsilon)|
          \le \log(S^2+\Delta^2)+\frac{2wm}{d}.
\]
The norm inside the logarithm is a nonzero integer.
With one unit-circle conjugate, the relation there also forces \(\Delta\le S\) by the triangle inequality.
Hence if \(AB>2S^2\) the remaining terms already span at least \((d/(2m))\log(AB/(2S^2))\), regardless of the positions of the exceptional terms themselves.
The [weighted witness bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#statement) allows polynomial dependence on the product of \(1+|b_i|\) over these remaining terms.
The trace test and divisibility give exact restrictions for that upper-bound problem.
Controlling the number of admissible configurations of the remaining terms in the full window is still open.
