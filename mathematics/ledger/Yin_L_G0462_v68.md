# Four-term trace numerators with middle coefficient close to the branch index

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform gap on both sides of ratio one

There exist absolute \(\eta,\delta>0\) with the following property.
Let a positive-constant Pisot input have branch index \(j\ge1\), constant \(q\ge2\), exterior root \(\theta\) and [trace numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(C\) of degree \(h\).
Suppose its lift has the four-term form
\[
     \Pi(z)=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})
       =j+a z^d-a z^{N-d}-j z^N,\qquad N=2h+2,\quad 1\le d\le h,\quad a\ne0 .
\]
If \(\big||a|/j-1\big|\le\eta\), then \(\theta-q\ge\delta\).
This covers both signs of \(a\), every \(d\) in the stated range, and unbounded \(j\).

Otherwise pass to a sequence on which \(\theta-q\to0\), \(r=|a|/j\to1\), and \(s=\operatorname{sgn}a\) is constant.
Set \(p=N-d>d\), and write \(B^\sharp=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N} b_\ell z^\ell=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})\) for the monic reciprocal lift of the trace denominator.
The [actual inverse measure and return](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) give
\[
             B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J,\qquad J=j/K_q,\qquad
             1<M(B^\sharp)\longrightarrow1 .
\]
The [signed measure](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) \(\omega\) of \(J\) has mass one, sole negative atom of mass \(-\alpha=-j/[4(q-1)]\) at one, and \(\alpha\to0\).
Its support is contained in the zeros of \(\Pi\), consisting of the two endpoints and the inverse pairs over the roots of \(C\).
In particular its positive part charges the \(2h\) distinct nonendpoint lifts.
Put \(m_\ell=2\int v^\ell\,d\omega(v)\) for all integer \(\ell\); then \(m_0=2,\ m_{-\ell}=m_\ell,\ J(0)=1,\ [z^\ell]J=m_\ell\) for \(\ell>0\).

<a id="fold"></a>
## The remainder modulo \(z^p-s\)

Take \(I(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1} I_\ell z^\ell\) to be the remainder of \(B^\sharp\) modulo \(z^p-s\).
Thus \(I_\ell=b_\ell+s b_{d-\ell}\) for \(0\le\ell\le d\) by reciprocity, and \(I_\ell=b_\ell\) for \(d<\ell<p\).
Uniformly in this full range,
\[
          I_\ell=m_\ell+s m_{\ell-d}+O(|r-1|).
\]
Indeed \(b_\ell=m_\ell\) for \(0<\ell<d\), \(b_d=m_d+sr\), and \(b_\ell=m_\ell+sr m_{\ell-d}\) for \(d<\ell<p\).
The comparison is exact for \(0<\ell<d\); its errors at \(0,d\) are \(r-1,s(r-1)\), and all moments are bounded by \(2(1+2\alpha)\).

At every support point \(v\) of \(\omega\), the equation \(\Pi(v)=0\) implies
\[
        |1+s v^d|\,|1-s v^p|
            =|(1+s v^d)(1-s v^p)-\Pi(v)/j|\le2|r-1|.
\]
Project the complex measure \(2(1+s v^{-d})\,d\omega(v)\) to the nodes \(\zeta^p=s\) by choosing the nearest node in angle, splitting equally between the two nearest in a tie.
The resulting measure \(\nu\) has total variation at most \(4(1+2\alpha)\) and satisfies conjugate symmetry of its weights.
For any chosen node, if \(v=\zeta e^{it}\) with \(|t|\le\pi/p\), then \(p|t|\le(\pi/2)|1-s v^p|\).
Thus for \(0\le\ell<p\), the replacement of \(v^\ell\) by \(\zeta^\ell\) costs \(O(|r-1|)\) after integration, since \(|1+s v^{-d}|=|1+s v^d|\).
The same inequality applies to each tied choice.

Define \(f(\ell)=\int\zeta^\ell\,d\nu(\zeta)\) on \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\).
It is real and its Fourier algebra norm is at most \(4(1+2\alpha)\), since its Fourier weights are precisely the masses on these \(2p\)-th roots.
Extend the integer coefficients by \(I_{\ell+p}=sI_\ell\) on the second half of the group.
Then \(f(\ell+p)=s f(\ell)\) exactly, so the first-half estimate gives
\[
                      \|f-I\|_\infty=O(|r-1|)
\]
on the entire group, with an absolute constant.
The [near-integer rounding lemma](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#rounding) bounds the Fourier algebra norm of this integer function uniformly, as \(r\to1\) and the norms of \(f\) are eventually at most five.
By [Sanders's finite-group theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#finite-abelian-fourier-algebra-norm) it is a sum of at most a fixed number \(L\) of signed coset indicators on \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\).
On \(0\le\ell<p\), each such coset gives an empty set or a finite arithmetic progression of step \(t\mid2p\).
Its polynomial of coefficients becomes a difference of two monomials on multiplication by \(1-z^t\).
Taking the product \(Q\) of one such binomial per nonempty coset yields a nonzero product of cyclotomic factors up to sign, with at most \(2^L\) terms and with \(QI\) having at most \(2L\,2^L\) terms.
Empty products are one.

## A cyclotomic multiple of the low-degree truncation

Set \(P=\sum_{\ell=0}^d b_\ell z^\ell\).
After extraction there is a polynomial \(U\), either \(1\) or \(1-\epsilon z^u\) with \(\epsilon=\pm1\), for which \(UP\) has boundedly many terms on the sequence.
If \(d\) stays bounded, take \(U=1\).
Suppose \(d\to\infty\).
Here \(m_\ell=b_\ell\in\mathbb Z\) for every \(1\le\ell<d\).
The [actual-input moving-index proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#atoms) then works at scale \(d\) to give a constant \(0<c<1/4\) along the sequence and a model
\[
       J_*=1,\qquad 1+\epsilon z^u,\qquad\text{or}\qquad
                       (1-\epsilon z^u)^{-1},\qquad 1\le u\le L_0=\lfloor cd\rfloor ,
\]
with \(J,J_*\) agreeing through \(L_0\), with the index \(u\) needed only in the last two models.
In detail, for any one or two indices \(o(d)\), all fixed combinations have absolute index \(o(d)\), inside the integral range.
In the limiting torus argument a nonzero polynomial annihilator, multiplied by a character difference to vanish at one, would specialize to a nonzero polynomial of degree \(o(d)\) with zero signed integer squared norm and hence zero positive squared norm.
This contradicts the \(2h\) distinct charged lifts since \(d\le h\).
The [mass-two classification and local sum and difference laws](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#model) therefore apply with \(d\) in the prefix argument.
Failure on every fixed fraction of \(d\) would provide violating indices \(o(d)\); hence some \(c\) works.

Write \(\nu_*=2\omega_*\) for the doubled probability measure of the matching model and \(\mu=2\omega^+=2\omega+2\alpha\delta_1\).
The following bound uses only subsets of fifty indices to control the error from that atom.
For \(W=1\) or \(W=1-\epsilon z^u\), suppose \(E\) is a set of indices such that
\[
    |i-i'|+\deg W\le L_0\quad(i,i'\in E),\qquad
                |W|^2\,d\nu_*\le4\,dm
\]
where \(m\) denotes normalized arc length.
For any fifty indices from \(E\), the Gram matrix of the functions \(v^i W(v^{-1})\) in \(L^2(\mu)\) has norm at most \(4+400\alpha\).
Indeed its signed part equals the model Gram by the matched moments, zero index included, and the density bound controls its norm by four through character orthogonality.
The added atom has matrix \(2\alpha|W(1)|^2{\bf1}{\bf1}^{\mathsf T}\) of norm at most \(400\alpha\).
If all fifty signed integrals against \(2\omega\) were nonzero integers, the corresponding integrals against \(\mu\) would have absolute value at least \(1-4\alpha\).
The Gram bound applied to the constant function would give
\[
             50(1-4\alpha)^2\le(4+400\alpha)(2+2\alpha),
\]
which fails for all sufficiently small \(\alpha\).
Thus at most forty-nine indices in such a block can have nonzero integer signed integrals, irrespective of its total length.

For the two polynomial models use \(W=1\): their doubled measures have densities \(2\) or \(2+2\epsilon\cos(u\theta)\), at most four.
Partition \([1,d-1]\) into \(O(1+d/L_0)\) blocks of diameter at most \(L_0\).
The tested integrals there are \(m_i=b_i\), so \(P\) has bounded support, counting its two endpoints separately.
If the geometric model has \(u>L_0/4\), its moments up to \(\lfloor L_0/4\rfloor\) instead agree with the constant model, giving the same conclusion on shorter blocks.

It remains to treat \(u\le L_0/4\) in the geometric model.
Put \(U=W=1-\epsilon z^u\).
Its doubled model measure is the sum of normalized arc length and the uniform probability on \(v^u=\epsilon\), as is seen from their Fourier coefficients.
Thus \(|W|^2d\nu_*=|W|^2dm\le4\,dm\).
For \(u+1\le i\le d-1\), the coefficient of \(UP\) is \(b_i-\epsilon b_{i-u}=m_i-\epsilon m_{i-u}\), the signed integral of the tested function.
Use \(O(1+d/L_0)\) intervals of diameter at most \(\lfloor L_0/2\rfloor\) to bound the number of nonzero coefficients here.
At the ends, the indices \(i=0,u,d,d+u\) cost at most four terms.
All other coefficients are either \(b_i\) with \(1\le i<u\) or \(-\epsilon b_{i-u}\) with \(d-u<i-u<d\).
Each of these two length-\((u-1)\) ranges has differences strictly smaller than \(u\), on which the moments agree with those of \(2\,dm\) because the geometric model has no nonzero moments of indices \(1,\dots,u-1\).
Use the same fifty-index estimate with \(W=1\) and the constant model on each range, including the empty ranges for \(u=1\).
This proves bounded support for \(UP\) in every case.

<a id="return"></a>
## Restoring the reciprocal tail

Polynomial division and reciprocity give
\[
              B^\sharp=I+(z^p-s)T,\qquad
              T(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^d b_{p+\ell}z^\ell=z^dP(1/z).
\]
With \(U^*(z)=z^{\deg U}U(1/z)\), the polynomial \(U^*T\) is the reversal of \(UP\) and has equally many terms.
Multiply the displayed identity by \(Q U^*\).
The first summand \(U^*(QI)\) has bounded support, as does \(Q(z^p-s)U^*T\) since \(Q\) and \(U^*T\) each have bounded support.
Hence \(QU^*B^\sharp\) is a nonzero integer polynomial with boundedly many terms.
Its measure equals \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) since the extra factors are cyclotomic up to signs, so convergence to one contradicts the [fixed-term Mahler gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This proves the claimed uniform positive excess, via the simultaneous negation of the two positive constants in the statement.
