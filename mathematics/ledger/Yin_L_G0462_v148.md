# Bounding cyclotomic padding for two residue classes independently of the offset

2026-11-18T06:17.

<a id="two-row"></a>
## Distinct torsion points and excess multiplicities

In the [two-row normal form](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v341.md#rows), write
\[
 J(x)=f(x^p)+\sigma x^S g(x^p),\qquad
 g(X)=X^n f(1/X),\quad n=\deg f>0,\quad f(0)=\pm1,\quad\gcd(f,g)=1 ,
\]
where \(f\in\mathbb Z[X]\), \(\sigma=\pm1\), \(p\ge2,\ S>0\) and \(\gcd(p,S)=1\).
Then the degree of the complete cyclotomic part of \(J\), with multiplicities, is at most \(24n\).
The estimate holds for every such reduced modulus, independent of \(S\).
The restriction to the displayed normal form matters at \(p=2\), where reciprocity alone need not exchange the two residue classes.
The excluded constant-seed case gives a unit times a cyclotomic binomial, with zero logarithmic measure and no nontorsion core.

The plane curve \(f(X)+\sigma Yg(X)=0\) is the graph of the rational function \(-\sigma f/g\) on the part of the torus where \(g\ne0\).
Its defining Laurent polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb C\) by coprimality and linearity in \(Y\).
Since both rows have endpoints \(0,n\), its Newton polygon is a rectangle of area \(n\).
Its positive area rules out a binomial component because the whole curve is irreducible.
The [Beukers–Smyth area bound](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) thus gives at most \(22n\) torsion points on the graph.
Each root of unity \(\xi\) of \(J\) maps to \((\xi^p,\xi^S)\) on the graph, and this map is injective by coprimality of \(p,S\).
This uses the area in the original graph coordinates, including for \(S\gg pn\).
The [odd-order fiber estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v30.md#odd-fibers) treats a restricted set of target orders, and the [specialization-width bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#torsion) uses the span of the diagonal.
Here the graph has area \(n\) for all target orders and offsets.

Put \(R(x)=x^S g(x^p)/f(x^p)\), and set
\[
 K(X)=S f(X)g(X)+pX\{f(X)g'(X)-f'(X)g(X)\}.
\]
Its degree is at most \(2n\) and \(K(0)=Sf(0)g(0)\ne0\).
At every root \(\xi\) of \(J\), the quantities \(\xi,f(\xi^p),g(\xi^p)\) are nonzero by the constant term and the gcd condition.
At that point \(R=-\sigma\), and the logarithmic derivative satisfies
\[
                   x R'(x)/R(x)=K(x^p)/(f(x^p)g(x^p)).
\]
A root of \(J\) of multiplicity \(m\) is a zero of order \(m\) of \(R+\sigma\), so the order of \(K\) at \(\xi^p\) is \(m-1\).
The map \(x\mapsto x^p\) is unramified there.
Moreover \(\xi^p\) determines \(\xi^S=-\sigma f(\xi^p)/g(\xi^p)\) and hence determines \(\xi\) among the roots.
Thus the excess multiplicity of all roots of \(J\) is at most \(\deg K\le2n\).
Adding this to the distinct torsion count proves the asserted \(24n\).

## Finite enumeration for a fixed noncyclotomic core

Suppose \(J\) has noncyclotomic part of degree \(D\).
Its degree is \(pn+S\), so
\[
                     (p-24)n+S\le D.                               \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for \(p>24\) we have \(p\le D+23\), \(1\le n\le\lfloor(D-1)/(p-24)\rfloor\) and \(1\le S\le D-(p-24)n\).
For a prescribed core, enumerate possible \(J\) up to sign by multiplying it by each product of cyclotomics of total degree at most \(24\lfloor(D-1)/(p-24)\rfloor\), and testing the two rows and their gcd.
This is finite since only orders with totient at most that degree are allowed and the multiplicities are bounded.
A degree bound on the core without a measure or coefficient bound does not specify a finite set of cores.
If its Mahler measure is also bounded, the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds give finiteness for the cores as well.
For \(p\le24\), (1) still holds but gives no uniform upper bound on \(n\).

For a prescribed irreducible unit core \(P\) below the plastic number with support differences of gcd one, a [cyclotomic multiple with two classes](Yin_L_G0462_v146.md#coverage) at an odd prime \(p\) reduces to such a \(J\).
Indeed the common row factor \(A(x^p)\) is cyclotomic, so removing it preserves the two classes, noncyclotomic core and complete Mahler measure.
The resulting coprime rows have the stated normal form because the measure is below the reciprocity threshold.
The [general finite test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v348.md#statement) already applies to two and three classes when \(p>48\).
The two-row graph additionally bounds the effective offset \(S\) itself.
The finite list concerns these reduced diagonals.
Adding unrestricted factors \(A(x^p)\) back can give arbitrarily many original multiples with the same two residue classes.

For these primitive irreducible cores the [Galois intersection estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v349.md#torsion) counts the cyclotomic roots of a carrying graph with multiplicity at a cost of ten times its Newton area.
Here the entire graph is irreducible, carries the core and has area \(n\).
Writing \(S=ap+r\), \(0<r<p\), simply shears the graph by replacing \(Y\) by \(X^aY\) and preserves its area.
The [resulting two-class estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v349.md#two) is
\[
 (p-10)n+S\le D,\qquad p\le D+9\ \ (p>10).                         \tag{2}
\]
For \(p>10\), one may use the finite range \(n\le\lfloor(D-1)/(p-10)\rfloor\), \(S\le D-(p-10)n\) and cofactor degree at most \(10n\).
The direct bound (1) applies to a normalized graph even when several noncyclotomic factors survive in its diagonal.

## A prime cutoff for the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial

Take the [irreducible Salem polynomial](../claims/Yin_C_G0226_v1.md#lehmer-joint-prime-control)
\[
 P(x)=x^{10}+x^9-x^7-x^6-x^5-x^4-x^3+x+1.
\]
Its support differences have gcd one and its unique exterior root \(\lambda>1\) satisfies \(117/100<\lambda<118/100\).
Indeed \(P(117/100)<0<P(118/100)\) by substitution, and there is only one root greater than one.
This interval also puts its measure below the plastic number.
No cyclotomic multiple of \(P\) can occupy exactly two classes at any prime \(p\ge17\).

To see this, (2) with \(D=10\) excludes \(p\ge23\) and leaves only \(p=17,19\) in the stated range.
In each case \(n=1\) and \(1\le S\le3\).
Up to sign \(f=1+AX\) with \(A\) a nonzero integer.
Since \(P\mid J\), evaluating the two rows at \(\lambda\) gives
\[
             |A+\sigma\lambda^S|
             =\frac{|\lambda^{2S}-1|}{|\lambda^p+\sigma\lambda^S|}.
\]
Now \((117/100)^{17}>14\) and \((118/100)^3<1.65\), so the right side is less than \((1.65^2-1)/(14-1.65)<0.14\).
For \(S=1,2,3\), respectively, the bounds \((117/100)^S<\lambda^S<(118/100)^S\) place \(\lambda^S\) in
\[
 (1.17,1.18),\quad(1.36,1.40),\quad(1.60,1.65).
\]
Every such interval has distance at least \(0.17\) from the integers, contradicting the displayed bound.
This excludes two classes for the specified core and prime range.
It leaves open which representations occur at smaller primes or for irreducible cores of growing degree near measure one.
