# A uniform phase estimate from the number of nonzero coefficients

<a id="sparse-phases"></a>
## All offsets with a sparse cyclotomic multiple

Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) have nonzero constant and degree \(r\), and put \(g=z^r f(1/z)\).
Suppose \(f,g\) are not proportional and \(M(f)\le\Theta\).
Assume there is an integer polynomial \(D\) of Mahler measure one, reciprocal up to sign and with nonzero constant, such that \(F=Df\) has at most \(s\) nonzero coefficients.
The polynomial \(D\), the exponents and the seed may all vary.
Then \(E=Dg\) is the reversal of \(F\) up to sign and obeys the same coefficient count.
Write \(a=\max(k,0),\ b=\max(-k,0)\) for an arbitrary integer \(k\).
For \(j\ge1,\ \sigma=\pm1\), put \(V_j=(z^a f)^j-(\sigma z^b g)^j\).
Uniformly in every such input,
\[
        \left|\,j^{-1}\log M(V_j)-\log M(f)\,\right|
                          \le C(s,\Theta)\,j^{-1/(4s-1)} .               \tag{1}
\]
There is no bound on either sign of the offset, the number of roots outside the circle, the seed degree or the cyclotomic degree.
The strategy contributor identified sparse cyclotomic clearing as a way to recover the limit at diverging negative offsets; the main researcher obtained (1) by regularization and integration over the phases.

<a id="regularity"></a>
## Uniform approximation of the phase integral

We use the [one-variable identity-map case of Proposition 4.10](../audits/literature/BGMP24/Yin_U001.md#bgmp24-u001).
For a polynomial \(P\) of coefficient maximum \(L>0\) with \(u\ge2\) nonzero terms, take the source parameter \(\alpha=1/2\).
With normalized circle measure and \(m=\log M\) this gives
\[
  0\le \int \tfrac12\log(|P|^2+\delta^2)-m(P)
              \le 24u^2(\delta/L)^{1/[2(u-1)]}.                         \tag{2}
\]
In (2) the integral applies to the regularized logarithm and \(m(P)\) is subtracted after integration.
The identity map has no nonzero lattice relation, so the source condition on the support diameter is automatic.
For \(u=1\) the left-hand difference equals \(\tfrac12\log(1+(\delta/L)^2)\).
Taking \(\delta=L\), the regularized mean is at least \(\log L\).
It follows that every coefficient of \(F,E\) has modulus at most \(H=e^{24s^2}\Theta\), since \(M(F)=M(E)=M(f)\).

For \(|w|=1\) let \(P_w=z^aF-w\sigma z^bE\) and put \(t=2s,\ L_0=1/(2H)\).
The two coefficient vectors being combined are nonproportional.
For \(k\ne0\) their valuations at zero differ, and for \(k=0\) the hypothesis on \(f,g\) applies.
Two coefficient positions therefore give a nonzero integral minor.
Subtracting \(w\) times the second vector from the first in this minor shows that \(1\le 2H\max|\operatorname{coeff}(P_w)|\), for every phase including possible cancellations.
Each \(P_w\) has at most \(t\) terms.
For \(0<\rho\le1\) fix \(\delta=L_0\rho\).
Equation (2) and the monomial case bound the difference between the regularized and original means uniformly by \(24t^2\rho^{1/[2(t-1)]}\).
The derivative of \(x\mapsto\tfrac12\log(x^2+\delta^2)\) on \(x\ge0\) is at most \(1/(2\delta)\), while \(\|P_w-P_v\|_\infty\le sH|w-v|\) on the circle.
Hence the regularized means at \(w,v\) differ by at most \(sH|w-v|/(2\delta)\).

Average over the \(j\)-th roots of unity in the phase variable, and compare to the circle average by assigning a centered arc to each root.
The distance from a point on its arc to the center is at most \(\pi/j\).
The error for the original means is at most
\[
       48t^2\rho^{1/[2(t-1)]}+\frac{\pi sH}{2j L_0\rho}.
\]
The discrete mean is \(m(V_j)/j\), by multiplication and \(m(D)=0\).
The continuous mean is \(m(f)\): at each unit-circle node Jensen's formula in \(w\) gives \(\log\max(|F|,|E|)=\log|F|\).
For interchanging integrals one can subtract the logarithm from its constant upper bound and apply Tonelli's theorem; the resulting integral is finite by the one-variable Mahler formula.
Putting \(\rho=j^{-2(t-1)/(2t-1)}\) proves (1).

## Actual cyclic norms for jointly growing sparse Pisot seeds

For the [moving Pisot seeds](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v3.md#statement), assume \(1<\theta_0\le\theta\le\Theta\), nonreciprocity, and the above \(s\)-term hypothesis.
Choose a single \(j=j(s,\theta_0,\Theta)\) large enough that the error in (1) is at most \(\tfrac12\log\theta_0\) and \(\tfrac j2\log\theta_0>\log2+1\).
Thus \(m(V_j)>\log2+1\) even when \(N-d\to-\infty\).
If \(r^3\log(2r)\log(2d)=o(d)\), the [uniform transfer](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v3.md#transfer) gives \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,z^{jd}-1)|\ge d(\log2+1/2)\) eventually, on the original Salem polynomial.
This conclusion also applies to the [multinacci example with constant limiting numerator](Yin_L_G0462_v29.md#multinacci) whenever its original seed and delay meet that growth condition.

<a id="fixed-eight"></a>
## A positive rate at the same eighth multiple for every sparsity bound

If a positive exponential rate suffices, the exponent can be fixed at \(8d\) independently of \(s,\theta_0,\Theta\).
Indeed \(D^8 V_8=(z^aF)^8-(z^bE)^8\) has at most \(2\binom{s+7}{8}\) terms by the multinomial expansion.
Its Mahler measure equals \(M(V_8)>1\) by the [eight-phase parity argument](Yin_L_G0462_v27.md#eight-phases).
The [Dobrowolski–Smyth fixed-support theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives \(m(V_8)\ge\eta_s>0\), with \(\eta_s\) depending only on \(s\).
Under the same moving-seed transfer hypotheses, \(\log C_{8d}/d\ge\eta_s/2\) eventually.
This rate on the actual integer resultant is enough for the [general-unit small-height criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0391_v1.md#bounded-power-degree-escape).
Unlike the larger multiplier selected in (1), the eighth multiple need not pay the elementary \(\log2\) cost by this proof.

## What the sparse estimate tests on a general reciprocal input

For the [half-degree encoding](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v1.md#universal-half-degree-delay-encoding), (1) still tests the phase polynomial of a sparse reciprocal input.
Indeed a fixed bound on the number of terms of \(P\) gives a fixed term bound on \(f\) by the displayed encoding there.
A fixed upper bound on \(M(P)\) bounds its largest coefficient by (2), hence also the coefficient sum and measure of its encoded seed.
The seed has integral coefficients, is monic and nonreciprocal after the coprime choice, so its nonreciprocal irreducible factor has measure at least the plastic number by the [nonreciprocal measure bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-reciprocal-number).
The estimate therefore gives uniform positive phase growth in this encoding.
More precisely for \(d\ge6\) the [odd-order argument](Yin_L_G0462_v30.md#arbitrary-input) makes the seventh phase polynomial noncyclotomic.
Its term count is bounded in terms of that of \(P\) alone, so the same fixed-support theorem already yields a positive auxiliary rate at \(j=7\), independent of coefficient height.
Here \(r=d/2+1\); recovering a positive rate for the original norm with that seed degree requires a different transfer estimate.
