# Cyclotomic divisors and individual factor heights of the reciprocal cubic seed

<a id="cyclotomic"></a>
## Exact cyclotomic part for every positive odd exponent

Let \(g(t)=t^3-t-1\), \(b(t)=g^*(t)=1-t^2-t^3\), \(f=-g/b\), and \(A_n(x)=g(x^n)+xb(x^n)\) for positive odd \(n\).
Write \(\Phi_j\) for the \(j\)-th cyclotomic polynomial.
The monic product of the cyclotomic factors of \(A_n\), counted with multiplicity, is
\[
 C_n=\begin{cases}x^4+1 & n\equiv1\pmod8,\\ 1 & n\not\equiv1\pmod8.\end{cases}
\]
In particular \(A_1=-\Phi_8\), and for every odd \(n\ge3\) the remaining monic polynomial \(-A_n/C_n\) has positive degree \(3n+1-4\mathbf1_{n\equiv1\bmod8}\).

The [cubic seed calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#seed) gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(g,b)|=1\) and shows that neither cubic has a root on the unit circle.
Suppose that \(\xi\) is a root of unity satisfying \(A_n(\xi)=0\).
Set \(M=\operatorname{ord}\xi\) and \(t=\xi^n\), so \(\xi=f(t)\).
Automorphisms of \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\) commute with the rational function \(f\) at these points.

If \(M\) is odd, the squaring automorphism gives \(f(t^2)=f(t)^2\).
Clearing denominators gives
\[
 -g(t^2)b(t)^2-g(t)^2 b(t^2)=-2tL(t),\qquad
 L(t)=t^{10}+t^9-t^7-t^6-t^5-t^4-t^3+t+1 .
\]
If \(k=\operatorname{ord}t\), then \(k\) is odd and \(\Phi_k\mid L\), so \(\varphi(k)\le10\).
The value \(L(1)=-1\) excludes \(k=1\) and all prime powers, since \(\Phi_{q^a}(1)=q\) for prime \(q\) and \(a\ge1\).
Of the remaining odd \(k\), only \(15\) has \(\varphi(k)\le10\).
Indeed two distinct odd prime divisors other than the pair \(3,5\) give a product of their prime-minus-one factors at least \(12\), and any additional prime factor or higher power multiplying \(15\) increases its totient past \(10\).
Division by \(\Phi_{15}\) leaves the nonzero remainder \(-(t+1)(2t^5+t^3+t^2-2t+1)\), of degree six.
This excludes odd \(M\).

If \(M=2u\) with \(u\) odd, the exponent \(2+u\) is coprime to \(M\), giving an automorphism \(\xi\mapsto-\xi^2\).
Since \(n\) is odd, it sends \(t\) to \(-t^2\), which gives \(f(-t^2)=-f(t)^2\).
The cleared numerator factors as
\[
 -g(-t^2)b(t)^2+g(t)^2b(-t^2)=2\Phi_{12}(t)\Phi_{30}(t).
\]
The order of \(t\) is twice an odd number, excluding order \(12\).
For order \(30\), the identity \(g(t)+t^9 b(t)=-(t^4+1)\Phi_{30}(t)\) gives \(\xi=t^9\), of order \(10\).
Then \(t=\xi^n\) cannot have order \(30\).

If \(4\mid M\), the exponent \(1+M/2\) is coprime to \(M\), giving \(\xi\mapsto-\xi\) and \(t\mapsto-t\).
Thus \(f(-t)=-f(t)\), and the cleared numerator satisfies
\[
 -g(-t)b(t)-g(t)b(-t)=-2(t-1)(t+1)(t^4+1).
\]
The order of \(t\) is divisible by \(4\), so \(t^4+1=0\).
The identity \(g(t)+t b(t)=-(t^4+1)\) gives \(\xi=t\), and \(\xi^n=\xi\) for primitive eighth roots holds exactly when \(n\equiv1\pmod8\).
Conversely, each such root then satisfies \(A_n(\xi)=0\).
At these roots the derivative equals
\[
 A_n'(\xi)=n A_1'(\xi)-(n-1)b(\xi)
          =(-3n-1)\xi^3+(n-1)\xi^2-(n-1).
\]
This nonzero polynomial of degree three cannot vanish at a primitive eighth root, which proves the asserted multiplicity.

<a id="factor-height"></a>
## Two-sided measure comparison for every nontorsion factor

Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) and let \(h\) denote the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
There are constants \(0<c\le C<\infty\), independent of \(n\) and of the factor, such that every monic irreducible nontorsion divisor \(P\) of \(-A_n\), of degree \(d\), satisfies
\[
                        c\,\frac dn\le m(P)\le C\,\frac dn .             \tag{1}
\]
For a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), put \(\beta=\alpha^n\).
Both numbers are algebraic integer units, \(f(\beta)=\alpha\) implies \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), and the integral Bézout relation for \(g,b\) makes \(b(\beta)\) a unit.
We have \(m(P)=d h(\alpha)=(d/n)h(\beta)\).
Thus it suffices to bound \(h(\beta)\) above and away from zero.

Since \(g,b\) have no common zero, for some \(0<c_0<1\) one has \(\max(|g(z)|,|b(z)|)\ge c_0\max(1,|z|)^3\) on \(\mathbb C\).
Indeed the ratio of the two sides without \(c_0\) is positive and continuous, and tends to \(1\) at infinity.
Over all \(d\) complex embeddings of the common field, the mean of \(\log|\sigma b(\beta)|\) is zero.
The equality \(\alpha=-g(\beta)/b(\beta)\) and the cubic estimate therefore give
\[
 h(\alpha)=\frac1d\sum_\sigma\log\max(|g(\sigma\beta)|,|b(\sigma\beta)|)
          \ge \log c_0+3h(\beta).
\]
Consequently \(h(\beta)\le-\log c_0/(3-1/n)\le-\tfrac12\log c_0\).

For the lower bound, suppose that a sequence of these nontorsion \(\beta\)'s had heights tending to zero.
Their absolute Galois orbit measures would tend weakly on \(\mathbb C^\times\) to Haar probability on the unit circle, since every proper algebraic subgroup of the one-dimensional torus consists of torsion points. [Bilu, Theorem 1.1 and weak convergence convention](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures)
Every conjugate of \(\alpha\) has modulus at most \(\sqrt6\).
Indeed \(M(A_n)\le\|A_n\|_2=\sqrt6\) by Jensen and Parseval for odd \(n\ge3\), since the six distinct coefficients there have modulus one.
For \(n=1\) all roots have modulus one.
Since the leading coefficient is a unit, \(M(A_n)\) bounds the maximum modulus of its roots whenever that modulus is at least one.
Thus all conjugates of all the \(\beta\)'s lie in the closed set \(B=\{z:|g(z)|\le\sqrt6\,|b(z)|\}\).
On \(B\) the modulus \(|b|\) is bounded below by a positive constant, since \(g,b\) have no common zero and \(|b(z)|\) tends to infinity with \(|z|\).
The same lower bound, after reduction if necessary, holds on the unit circle.
Choose \(K>0\) large enough that \(\log|b|\ge-K\) on \(B\) and \(|\log|b||\le K\) on the unit circle.
The bounded continuous function \(\min(K,\max(-K,\log|b(z)|))\), extended by \(-K\) at zeros, is no larger than \(\log|b|\) on \(B\).
Its orbit mean is therefore at most zero for every \(\beta\), because \(b(\beta)\) is a unit.
Its circular mean equals \(m(b)=\log\theta>0\), where \(\theta\) is the real root of \(g\), contradicting weak convergence.
This proves the existence of \(c>0\) in (1).
Equivalently the [fixed rational function height gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0852_v1.md#fixed-rational-function-height-gap) applies to the nonmonomial \(f\) and the nontorsion \(\beta\), since \(h(f(\beta))=h(\beta)/n\).

<a id="degree-and-repetitions"></a>
## Near-linear degree and eventual squarefreeness

Uniformly over all the nontorsion factors as odd \(n\) tends to infinity,
\[
          d\gg n\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^3,\qquad
          \#\{\text{nontorsion factors counted with multiplicity}\}
             \ll\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)^3 .                \tag{2}
\]
Indeed a nontorsion unit has degree \(d\ge2\).
Combining (1) with the inequality \(m(P)>2/(\log(3d))^3\) gives \(d\gg n/(\log(3n))^3\), since \(d\le3n+1\).
Consequently \(\log d\sim\log n\) and \(\log\log d\sim\log\log n\) uniformly, and the bound \(m(P)>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) gives the first assertion of (2).
Both measure inequalities are [Voutier's, applied in the factor's own degree](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height).
Summing degrees with multiplicity up to \(3n+1\) proves the second assertion.

If \(\alpha\) is a repeated root, writing \(A_n(x)=b(x^n)(x-f(x^n))\) in a neighborhood of \(\alpha\) gives \(n\beta f'(\beta)=f(\beta)\).
Thus \(\beta\) is a zero of \(n t(g'b-gb')-g b\), of degree at most six.
This polynomial has constant term \(1\), and so \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]=d\le6\).
The lower bound in (2) excludes every nontorsion repeated root for sufficiently large odd \(n\).
All torsion roots were shown above to be simple, so \(A_n\) is squarefree for all sufficiently large odd \(n\).

## Remaining degree proportion and square-root lifts

For a sequence of such factors, (1) shows that \(m(P)\to0\) exactly when \(d/n\to0\).
In particular the largest-degree nontorsion factor of each seed has a uniform positive measure lower bound exactly when its degree is bounded below by a fixed positive fraction of \(n\).
The exact total degree after cyclotomic removal and (2) still allow a number of factors growing with \(n\), each of degree \(o(n)\).
They leave that fixed-fraction question open.

For a [square-root lift](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#lift) \(\gamma^{2^k}=\alpha\) of any such root, let \(d'=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\) and \(P_\gamma\) be its monic minimal polynomial.
The power identity for heights gives exactly
\[
 m(P_\gamma)=\frac{d'}{2^k d}m(P),\qquad
 c\frac{d'}{2^k n}\le m(P_\gamma)\le C\frac{d'}{2^k n}.
\]
Each step has relative degree one or two, since the previous root is the square of the next.
Thus the multiplier \(d'/(2^k d)\) equals \(2^{-j}\), where \(j\) counts the steps of relative degree one.
A positive lower bound along a selected sequence of lifts is equivalent to both a uniform positive lower bound on \(d/n\) and a uniform bound on \(j\), since \(d/n\le3+1/n\).
For a fixed base the [unit-ray saturation bound](Yin_L_G0450_v1.md#unit-ray-saturation) already controls this loss by a base-dependent constant.
