# Attainable neutral residue distributions for Pisot polynomials at the square constant

<a id="calibration"></a>
## Which distributions can occur if Salem numbers tend to one?

Suppose \(\sigma_i\to1\) is a sequence of Salem numbers.
For each \(i\) one can choose an actual Pisot minimal polynomial of even degree \(d_i\), constant \(q_i=d_i^2\), and positive root \(\theta_i\), whose full return has precisely the prescribed Salem factor and whose excess \(\delta_i=\theta_i-q_i\) tends to zero.
The choices allow both families of neutral residues in the [integer branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches) to have the same weak limit \(F^2\,d\mu_{\rm sc}\) for any prescribed nonzero \(F\in\mathbb Z[x]\), where
\[
        d\rho(x)=\frac{dx}{\pi\sqrt{4-x^2}},\qquad
        d\mu_{\rm sc}(x)=\frac{4-x^2}{2}\,d\rho(x),\qquad -2<x<2 .
\]
Another choice gives the arcsine probability \(\rho\) as the limiting branch measure and \(\rho+\lambda\delta_2\) as the limiting inverse measure, for every prescribed finite \(\lambda\ge0\), where \(\delta_2\) denotes unit point mass at two.
These are conditional realizations for every proposed sequence of shrinking Salem inputs.
Conversely any sequence of actual square-constant returns with \(\theta_i-q_i\to0\) has exterior traces tending to two by the [positive excess formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return).
Their exterior factors are Salem or reciprocal quadratic, and the latter have positive trace excess at least one, so the Salem factors themselves tend to one. [Factorization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#infimum)
The choice \(F=1\) shows that the constant \(1/2\) in the [limiting weighted test](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#angular) would be sharp even with the square constant and actual integer branches.

<a id="grid"></a>
## An integer correction with prescribed weights on a cyclotomic grid

Fix the minimal polynomial \(P_0(z)=z^{m/2}Q_0(z+z^{-1})\) of a Salem number \(\sigma\) of degree \(m\ge4\).
Put \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}=2+u\) and \(H_0=Q_0/(x-T)\), whose \(h=m/2-1\) roots \(x_i\) lie in \((-2,2)\).
For \(k\ge1\) take \(B=C_k\), where \(C_k(2\cos t)=2\cos(kt)\), and write \(y_j=2\cos t_j\), \(t_j=(2j-1)\pi/(2k)\), \(1\le j\le k\).
The enlarged return \(P=P_0(z)(z^{2k}+1)\) has degree \(d=m+2k\) and trace polynomial \(Q=Q_0B\).
Its \(k\) new traces are simple and distinct from every old one since all old unit-circle roots are nontorsion and all new ones are roots of unity.
Neither grid endpoint is a root.

Let \(V_\ell(2\cos t)=\sin((\ell+1)t)/\sin t\) for \(\ell\ge0\), and \(V_{-1}=0\).
The polynomials \(B,V_\ell\) are monic integral for \(k\ge1,\ell\ge0\).
For \(D^0_k=V_{k-1}+V_{k-2}\) one has \(D^0_k(y_j)/B'(y_j)=(1+y_j/2)/k\). [Grid identities](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v374.md#grid)
Given a nonzero integral \(F\) of degree \(l\), let \(D\) be the remainder of \(D^0_kF^2\) modulo \(B\).
It has integral coefficients and degree less than \(k\).
Require \(2l+2<2k\) and \(F(y_j)\ne0\) for each \(j\).
There are infinitely many permitted indices even after restricting to multiples of any given integer.
Indeed \(z^lF(z+z^{-1})\) has finitely many torsion zeros, and taking \(k\) divisible by their orders excludes all of them from \(z^{2k}=-1\).
Then
\[
                  w_y=\frac{D(y)}{B'(y)}
                     =\frac{(1+y/2)F(y)^2}{k}>0 .
\]
Define two quantities independent of \(k\) by
\[
 a=\sum_y w_y=\int(1+x/2)F(x)^2\,d\rho(x),\qquad
 b=\sum_y(2-y)w_y=\int F(x)^2\,d\mu_{\rm sc}(x).                 \tag{1}
\]
Both quadratures are exact since the averages at \(t_j\) of \(\cos(nt_j)\) vanish for \(1\le n<2k\), by pairing for odd \(n\) and summing a geometric progression for even \(n\).
The partial fractions \(D/B=\sum_y w_y/(x-y)\) show that \(a=[x^{k-1}]D\) is a positive integer.
Writing \(F=\sum_{\ell=0}^l f_\ell V_\ell\) gives \(f_\ell\in\mathbb Z\), and sine orthogonality gives \(b=\sum_\ell f_\ell^2\).
Hence \(b\ge1\), with equality exactly when \(F=\pm V_{r-1}\) for some \(r\ge1\).
In fact, for every integral \(D\) of degree less than \(k\) with strictly positive \(w_y=D(y)/B'(y)\), the first two Laurent coefficients of \(D/B\) at infinity are integers, namely \(\sum w_y\) and \(\sum y w_y\).
Consequently \(\sum(2-y)w_y\) is a positive integer for any such split integral correction.
The assertion requires integrality of the grid correction itself, which need not follow from integrality of an arbitrary full inverse.

<a id="squares"></a>
## Arbitrarily small old residues at the required square constants

Put \(q=(m+2k)^2\) and \(q_0=q+a\).
For any \(\varepsilon>0\) there exist arbitrarily large \(k\) in every arithmetic progression on which the grid restrictions above hold eventually, together with \(R_0\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(h\) and leading coefficient \(1-q_0\), for which
\[
 b_i=-R_0(x_i)/H'_0(x_i)>0,\qquad
 0<s_0:=\sum_i b_i<\varepsilon,\qquad
             \sum_i\frac{b_i}{2+x_i}<\varepsilon.                 \tag{2}
\]
To verify this at squares, write \(H_0=x^h+\sum_{\ell<h}e_\ell x^\ell\).
The coefficients \(1,e_{h-1},\ldots,e_0\) are rationally independent, since they are monic polynomials in \(T\) of successive degrees \(0,\ldots,h\) and \([\mathbb Q(T):\mathbb Q]=h+1\). [Old coefficient lattice](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order)
The lower coefficient vector of \(L=R_0-(1-q_0)H_0\) varies through \((q+a-1)e+\mathbb Z^h\).
The map from these coefficients to \((-L(x_i)/H'_0(x_i))_i\) is an invertible real linear map by interpolation at distinct nodes.
Choose the open box \(0<b_i<\varepsilon\min(1,2+x_i)/h\).
Its inverse image projects onto a nonempty open subset of the coefficient torus.
Membership there gives an actual integral choice of \(R_0\) with (2).

The [quadratic equidistribution theorem on a progression](../audits/literature/Tao10/Yin_U001.md#tao10-u001) follows here by a single differencing step.
Writing \(k=k_0+Mv\), \(M\ge1\), gives each nonzero integer character \(n\in\mathbb Z^h\) a phase \(p(v)=(q+a-1)n\cdot e\) with irrational quadratic coefficient \(4M^2 n\cdot e\).
For a fixed nonzero integer lag \(t\), the mean of \(\exp(2\pi i[p(v+t)-p(v)])\) tends to zero, by the geometric sum with irrational linear coefficient.
For \(H\) fixed the mean of \(\exp(2\pi i p(v))\) differs by \(O(H/N)\) on the first \(N\) terms from the mean of its first \(H\) shifts averaged together.
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the squared modulus of the latter by the mean squared modulus of that average of shifts.
Expanding the square and using the lag limits bounds its limsup by \(1/H\).
Let \(H\to\infty\), then approximate continuous torus functions by trigonometric polynomials to obtain equidistribution.
A continuous nonnegative function supported in the indicated open set with positive integral gives infinitely many visits after any lower cutoff on \(k\).
The cutoff can depend on the entire Salem input, \(F\), the chosen progression and \(\varepsilon\).

<a id="inverse"></a>
## Full Pisot polynomials and their integer branches

With \(Q=(x-T)H\), \(H=H_0B\), set
\[
                   R=BR_0+Q_0D,\qquad
             f(z)=P(z)+(z-1)z^{d/2-1}R(z+z^{-1}).
\]
The coefficient of \(x^{h+k}\) in \(R\) equals \(1-q_0+a=1-q\).
At an old trace the factors \(B(x_i)\) in \(-R(x_i)/H'(x_i)\) cancel and leave \(b_i\) from (2).
At a new trace the inverse and branch residues are respectively
\[
                 b_y=(T-y)w_y,\qquad c_y=(2-y)w_y.                 \tag{3}
\]
At every trace \(x\) the branch residue satisfies \(c_x=(2-x)b_x/(T-x)\). [Branch residues](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches)
The new contribution to \(\sum b_x/(2+x)\) is exactly \(\frac12\int(T-x)F^2\,d\rho\) by (1)'s quadrature.
This is fixed before the choice of \(k\).
Enlarge the cutoff so that this integral divided by two, plus the bound \(\varepsilon\) for the old contribution, is less than \(q+T/2\).
The [strict inverse criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return) now gives an irreducible monic Pisot \(f\) of degree \(d\) with \(f(0)=q\), exterior root \(\theta>q\), and full return \((zf+f^*)/(z+1)=P\).
Thus both the Salem factor and every cyclotomic factor are specified.

Let \(\delta=\theta-q\), \(j=[z]\frac{f}{f^*}-q^2+1\in\mathbb Z\), and \(s=\sum_x b_x\).
The sums and the exact branch identity give
\[
       s=b+ua+s_0,\qquad
       j=b+(q+a-1)u+s_0,\qquad
       b<\sum_x c_x<b+\varepsilon .                              \tag{4}
\]
For \(0<\varepsilon<1\) this forces \(j=b+\lceil(q+a-1)u\rceil\), and the distance to the integer just above \((q+a-1)u\) is strictly between zero and \(\varepsilon\).
Here \(u\) is irrational since the Salem trace has degree at least two.
These congruence and residue assertions hold for arbitrarily large \(d=m+2k\) in each of the indicated progressions.
For bounded \(T\), as \(q\to\infty\), the same inverse formula and the [two-point bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#two-points) give uniformly
\[
      q(\delta-u)=\frac{qu}{\theta-1}
                +\sum_x\frac{q b_x}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x}
                =u+s+O_T\big((u+s)/q\big).                       \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(\theta=q+O_T(1)\) and \(|x|<2\) for every summand.

## Changes of Salem input and the limiting weighted constant

Now let \(u_i\to0\), and at each input choose \(\varepsilon_i\downarrow0\) and an admissible \(k_i\) using (2).
They can be chosen strictly increasing and with \(m_i/k_i\to0\) by imposing lower cutoffs, with no uniform bound on the first visit of the varying coefficient torus.
For a fixed \(F\), the old masses vanish by (2) and \(0<c_x<b_x\).
The grid branch measures converge to \(F^2\mu_{\rm sc}\) by midpoint sums against continuous tests, and the grid inverse measures differ from them by positive measures of mass \(u_i a\to0\).
Equations (4) and (5) give \(s\to b\), \(q(\delta-u)\to b\) and \(j-b-(q+a-1)u\to0\) through positive values.
In particular \(\delta\to0\).

For the mass-one case \(F=V_{r-1}\) with fixed \(r\ge1\), the limiting density relative to \(\rho\) at \(x=2\cos t\) is \(g=1-\cos(2rt)\).
It satisfies \(\int\log g\,d\rho=-\log2\) since \(g=2\sin^2(rt)\) and the circular mean of \(\log|1-e^{it}|\) is zero.
Consequently
\[
      \inf_{\substack{w\ \mathrm{continuous}\\w>0}}
        \frac{\int w g\,d\rho}{\exp(\int\log w\,d\rho)}=\frac12 .  \tag{6}
\]
The arithmetic-geometric mean gives the lower bound.
Testing with \(w=(g+\eta)^{-1}\) and letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves equality since the finitely many logarithmic singularities are integrable.
Thus if Salem numbers tend to one, then the constant \(1/2\) in the weighted restriction for all positive continuous tests is optimal for both kinds of actual residues, even when their total mass tends to one.
Whether total mass can tend to \(1/2\) is a separate question.
The integrality in (1) places the total branch mass of these split corrections at least at one.

## A prescribed atom at the exterior endpoint

Take \(F=V_{r-1}\) with \(r\) allowed to grow.
Choose \(k>r\) divisible by \(r\).
No grid value of \(F\) vanishes, since \(\sin(rt_j)=0\) would require \(2(k/r)\) to divide \(2j-1\), and the degree condition \(2r<2k\) holds.
The new weights satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
  c_{y_j}&=\frac{2\sin^2(rt_j)}k,\qquad
  w_{y_j}=\frac{\sin^2(rt_j)}{k(1-\cos t_j)}
     =\frac1{2k}\left|\sum_{\ell=0}^{2r-1}e^{i\ell t_j}\right|^2,\\
  b_{y_j}&=c_{y_j}+u w_{y_j},\qquad
   \sum_j c_{y_j}=1,\qquad \sum_j w_{y_j}=r .
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{7}
\]
The last sum follows by expanding the square and midpoint averaging its cosines of degrees at most \(2r-1\).
Moreover \(\int V_{r-1}^2\,d\rho=r\), since its square is even and \(a=\int(1+x/2)V_{r-1}^2\,d\rho=r\).
Hence the new charge at the stability endpoint is exactly \(\sum b_y/(2+y)=Tr/2\), and the old charge can be made smaller than \(\varepsilon\) by (2).

Given \(u_i\downarrow0\) and \(0\le\lambda<\infty\), select \(r_i\to\infty\) with \(u_i r_i\to\lambda\), for example \(r_i=\lceil u_i^{-1/2}\rceil\) at \(\lambda=0\) and \(r_i=\lceil\lambda/u_i\rceil\) otherwise.
Monotonicity of the input is unnecessary.
Choose \(k_i\) arbitrarily far out along multiples of \(r_i\) so that (2), the stability budget and \(k_i/r_i\to\infty\) all hold.
For fixed \(n\ge0\) the discrete integral of \(\cos(nt)\cos(2r_it)\) is eventually zero since \(n<2r_i\) and \(2r_i+n<2k_i\).
Therefore the grid branch measures in (7) converge to \(\rho\) by uniform approximation by polynomials in \(x=2\cos t\).
The measures assigning mass \(w_{y_j}/r_i\) to the grid nodes are probabilities, whose mass on \(t\ge\alpha>0\) is at most \(1/[r_i(1-\cos\alpha)]\).
They converge to unit point mass at \(x=2\).
Together with (2) this proves the two asserted limits \(\rho\) and \(\rho+\lambda\delta_2\).
Here \(j-1-(q+r_i-1)u_i\to0\) from above, while \(s=j-(q-1)u_i\to1+\lambda\) and \(q(\delta-u_i)\to1+\lambda\).
The inverse endpoint charge obeys \(u_i\sum_x b_x/(2+x)\to\lambda\).
These profiles concern actual square-constant inverses conditional on the supplied changing Salem inputs.
The construction gives no mechanism for producing those inputs or bounding their traces away from two.
