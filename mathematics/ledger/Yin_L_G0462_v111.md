# Sublinear prime orders recover the Mahler rate for every fixed Pisot seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## Recovering the limiting Mahler rate at one varying prime

Fix a nonempty finite set \(\mathcal F\) of monic minimal polynomials of Pisot numbers \(\theta_f>1\).
For each \(f\) assume it is not proportional to \(g(z)=z^r f(1/z)\), where \(r=\deg f\).
This includes nonunit Pisot numbers and degree-one seeds.
For \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\) and all sufficiently large positive integers \(N\), let \(P_{f,\sigma,N}\) be the minimal polynomial of the unique exterior root of \(H_{f,\sigma,N}(z)=z^N f(z)-\sigma g(z)\), and put \(d_{f,\sigma,N}=\deg P_{f,\sigma,N}\).
These are Salem minimal polynomials.

There is a single choice of odd prime \(p_N\) for each sufficiently large \(N\) with
\[
 p_N\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 p_N=o\!\left(\min_{f,\sigma}d_{f,\sigma,N}\right),\qquad
 \max_{f,\sigma}\left|
   \frac{\log G_{p_N}(P_{f,\sigma,N})}{p_N-1}-\frac{\log\theta_f}{2}
 \right|\longrightarrow0 .
\]
The same assertion holds for \(B_{p_N}(P_{f,\sigma,N})\) with the same primes.
Here \(G_p\) is the [largest common integer modulus](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) of the Salem trace polynomial and the minimal polynomial \(S_p\) of \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) for primitive \(p\)-th roots \(\zeta\), and \(B_p\) is their absolute resultant.
Consequently the maximum of \(|\log G_{p_N}/(p_N-1)-m(P_{f,\sigma,N})/2|\) over the stated finite list tends to zero, where \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.

On 2026-11-17 agent-S extended the [neighboring-prime calculation for two cubic seeds](Yin_L_G0462_v109.md#growing) for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
A [countable diagonal choice](#all-seeds) supplies one sequence of primes working for every fixed eligible seed, with seed-dependent onset.

<a id="cofactor"></a>
## The exterior minimal factor and the bounded torsion cofactor

Fix one \(f\), write \(\theta=\theta_f\), and suppress \(f,\sigma\) where the choice is fixed.
The [fixed-seed circle and torsion argument](Yin_L_G0462_v27.md#fixed-seed-return) applies because \(f\) is irreducible, \(f(0)\ne0\), and \(f,g\) have the same degree and are not proportional, hence are coprime.
On the unit circle \(|f|=|g|>0\).
For \(0<s<1\), Rouché's theorem gives the same number of disk zeros to \(z^N f-s\sigma g\) as to \(z^N f\), leaving exactly one exterior zero.
Since all these polynomials are monic of degree \(N+r\), letting \(s\uparrow1\) gives at most one strictly exterior zero of \(H_N\), counting multiplicity.
At two fixed positive real points greater than one bracketing the simple root \(\theta\), the signs of \(H_N\) eventually equal those of \(f\).
Thus \(H_N\) has one positive exterior root \(\alpha_N\) for large \(N\), and \(\alpha_N\to\theta\).

The identity \(z^{N+r}H_N(1/z)=-\sigma H_N(z)\) gives \(\alpha_N^{-1}\) as the sole interior root.
The constant of \(H_N\) is \(-\sigma\), so its monic irreducible factor \(P_N\) containing \(\alpha_N\) has constant of absolute value one.
The product of its roots in absolute value forces it to contain \(\alpha_N^{-1}\).
Consequently \(P_N\) is reciprocal, its remaining roots form nonreal conjugate pairs on the unit circle, and its even degree \(d_N\) gives a monic integral trace polynomial \(T_N\) by \(P_N(z)=z^{d_N/2}T_N(z+z^{-1})\).
The remaining monic factor \(C_N=H_N/P_N\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials by Kronecker's theorem.

Write \(V=g/f\), so \(V(\eta)=\sigma\eta^N\) at a torsion root \(\eta\) of \(H_N\).
According as its order \(m\) is odd, twice an odd integer, or divisible by four, the root \(\xi\eta^h\) is conjugate to \(\eta\), with \((\xi,h)=(1,2),(-1,2),(-1,1)\), respectively.
The corresponding exponent is \(2\), \(m/2+2\), or \(m/2+1\), coprime to \(m\) in its respective case.
Therefore \(\eta\) satisfies
\[
                  V(\xi z^h)=\xi^N\sigma^{h-1}V(z)^h .
\]
For \(h=2\) the right side has a pole at \(\theta\) and the left side is finite there since \(|\xi\theta^2|>\theta\).
For \((\xi,h)=(-1,1)\) the left side is again finite since \(f(-\theta)\ne0\).
Clearing denominators gives three nonzero polynomials of degrees at most \(4r,4r,2r\) for each fixed \(N,\sigma\).
They range over a finite set as \(N,\sigma\) vary, since the scalar multipliers are signs.
Thus all torsion orders are bounded in terms of \(f\), and at each \(N,\sigma\) there are at most \(10r\) distinct torsion roots.

At a repeated torsion root one also has \(N=\eta V'(\eta)/V(\eta)\), by differentiating at that root and using \(f(\eta)g(\eta)\ne0\).
Each root in the finite set therefore permits repetition at at most one delay.
Outside these finitely many delays every torsion root is simple, \(\deg C_N\le10r\), and
\[
                        N-9r\le d_N\le N+r .
\]
Eventually \(d_N\ge4\), so \(P_N\) is the minimal polynomial of a Salem number and \(m(P_N)=\log\alpha_N\).
In particular \(m(P_N)\to\log\theta\) for either sign.
For \(\sigma=1\) the factor \(z-1\) always occurs and is eventually simple, also directly from \(H_N'(1)=Nf(1)+f'(1)-g'(1)\) and \(f(1)\ne0\).
For \(\sigma=-1\) one has \(H_N(1)=2f(1)\ne0\).

## Exact transfer from one delay to another

Let \(N,a\) be beyond the preceding fixed-seed exceptions for the same \(f,\sigma\).
Let \(p\) be an odd prime dividing \(N-a\) and larger than every possible torsion order for this seed.
For each delay put \(C_N=(z-1)^e U_N\), where \(e=1\) if \(\sigma=1\) and \(e=0\) otherwise.
Every factor \(\Phi_m\) of \(U_N\) or \(U_a\) has \(1<m<p\) and \(\Phi_m(\zeta)\) is a unit of \(D=\mathbb Z[\zeta]\).
Indeed, in \(\Phi_m(z)=\prod_{j\mid m}(z^j-1)^{\mu(m/j)}\), every \(\zeta^j-1\) has absolute norm \(p\), and \(\sum_{j\mid m}\mu(m/j)=0\).
The value is an algebraic integer of absolute norm one, giving an inverse in \(D\) by its monic polynomial.

Since \(\zeta^N=\zeta^a\), the two values of \(H\) agree exactly.
Cancelling the same nonzero \((\zeta-1)^e\) in this equality gives \(P_N(\zeta)U_N(\zeta)=P_a(\zeta)U_a(\zeta)\).
Both Salem factors have nonzero values there.
Writing \(t=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), their trace ratio is consequently the unit
\[
             \frac{T_N(t)}{T_a(t)}
               =\zeta^{(d_a-d_N)/2}\frac{U_a(\zeta)}{U_N(\zeta)}
                  \ \in D^\times .
\]
The exponent is integral because the two Salem degrees are even, for any parities of the original delays.
The ratio is real, and every real element of \(D\) lies in \(A=\mathbb Z[t]\).
Indeed \(\zeta^2=t\zeta-1\) gives \(D=A+A\zeta\), and a real quantity of the form \(b+c\zeta\) with \(b,c\in A\) has \(c=0\).
Applying this to the ratio and its inverse shows that \(T_N(t)\) and \(T_a(t)\) generate the same ideal in \(A\).

Lifting through \(A=\mathbb Z[X]/(S_p)\) gives
\[
                        (T_N,S_p)=(T_a,S_p)\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[X].
\]
Evaluation at any integer \(b\) gives equality of the two gcds with \(S_p(b)\), including their full prime powers.
Taking maxima gives \(G_p(P_N)=G_p(P_a)\), while taking absolute norms of the associated trace values gives \(B_p(P_N)=B_p(P_a)\).
This transfers the actual primitive Salem factor even when the two cyclotomic cofactors or the parities of the delays differ.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Choosing the same prime for the whole finite list

Fix an integer \(k\ge1\).
For each residue \(0\le\rho<k\), choose \(a_\rho\equiv\rho\bmod k\) beyond all fixed-seed exceptions above and with \(a_\rho\ge9\max_{f\in\mathcal F}\deg f\).
Take every \(a_\rho\) large enough that \(|m(P_{f,\sigma,b})-\log\theta_f|<1/k\) for every \(f,\sigma\) and both \(b=a_\rho,a_\rho+k\), using the convergence of the Salem roots.
For sufficiently large \(N\equiv\rho\bmod k\), let \(u=(N-a_\rho)/k\) and take \(p(N,k)\) to be the greatest prime divisor of \(u(u-1)\).
For fixed \(k,\rho\), this prime tends to infinity with \(N\) in that residue class.
Otherwise on an infinite subsequence both positive integers \(u,u-1\) would belong to a fixed finitely generated multiplicative group of rationals.
The [finite-rank unit equation theorem](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound) applied to \(x-y=1\) in the square of that group gives only finitely many such pairs, since both single-term subsums are nonzero.
There are just \(k\) residue classes, so \(p(N,k)\to\infty\) for fixed \(k\) through all \(N\).
Also \(p(N,k)\le u=(N-a_\rho)/k\le d_{f,\sigma,N}/k\) for all sufficiently large \(N\) and every member of the list.
Eventually this prime exceeds two and all the fixed torsion orders.
It divides exactly one of \(u,u-1\), so it divides \(N-b\) for the corresponding \(b=a_\rho\) or \(a_\rho+k\).
The [ideal transfer](#ideals) therefore uses the same shorter delay \(b\) for every seed and sign.

For the finitely many Salem polynomials at the \(2k\) chosen delays, the [all-prime modulus theorem and trace norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#local) give
\[
 \frac{\log G_p(P_{f,\sigma,b})}{p-1}\longrightarrow
 \frac{m(P_{f,\sigma,b})}{2},\qquad
 \frac{\log B_p(P_{f,\sigma,b})}{p-1}\longrightarrow
 \frac{m(P_{f,\sigma,b})}{2}.
\]
Thus for all sufficiently large \(N\), transferring at \(p(N,k)\) gives both rates for the original delay within \(2/k\) of \((\log\theta_f)/2\) for all seeds and signs.

Choose strictly increasing thresholds \(N_k\) so that for all \(N\ge N_k\) the conclusions at stage \(k\) hold and \(p(N,k)\ge k\).
For \(N_k\le N<N_{k+1}\), set \(p_N=p(N,k)\).
Then \(p_N\ge k\), its ratio to every original degree is at most \(1/k\), and both rate errors are at most \(2/k\).
The [logarithmic window examples](Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#log-window) still preclude an arbitrary prescribed sublinear window, since their common moduli equal one at every odd prime up to a constant times the logarithm of the degree.

<a id="all-seeds"></a>
## One sequence for all fixed seeds

Enumerate the eligible integer polynomials as \(f_1,f_2,\ldots\).
At stage \(k\) apply the preceding construction to \(f_1,\ldots,f_k\) with both signs and rate errors at most \(2/k\), again choosing increasing thresholds.
This gives one sequence \(p_N\to\infty\) with \(p_N=o(N)\), since \(d_{f_1,1,N}=N+O_{f_1}(1)\) and its ratio bound holds at every stage.
For each fixed \(i\) the bounds apply for every \(k\ge i\), proving both limits for \(f_i\) with these same primes.
The onset for a prescribed accuracy can depend on \(i\).
An arbitrary sequence of varying seeds would still need control of its short-delay polynomials, its removable cyclotomic factors and the onset of its prime-order estimates.
