# Complete exterior obstruction for the first growing-cycle four-transvection commutator

## Question and current scope

This records the complete first growing-cycle commutator test selected by [S4](../strategies/Yin_S_G0004.md#cyclic-commutator-first-test) under the active [G0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
The main research agent developed and checked this argument on 2026-11-10.
The result is a complete exterior-root obstruction for this exact family: every \(n\ge1\) has \(M(P_A)>2\).
The [complete independent finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v8.md#growing-cycle-commutator-review) checks every determinant identity, both small parameters and the entire stated conclusion.
No numerical experiment, root search or sampling law was run for this candidate.

The [two-transvection gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0885_v2.md#decision-positive-lattice-two-transvection-gap) and [short-word reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#short-word-reduction) motivate changing the actual word.
The [all-word E8-block cover](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v1.md#decision-e8-block-canonical-cover) additionally requires growing block-permutation order for any strict positive-to-one sequence in this domain.
The present construction meets that necessary order condition, but the direct determinant argument below proves that its complete measure is always greater than two.

<a id="candidate"></a>
## Exact family

Let \(n\ge1\), \(N=2^n\) and \(g=N^3+1\).
In \(H=E_8^{\oplus g}\), choose the same norm-two root \(\theta_j\) in each block, indexed by \(j\in\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\).
Let \(R\) identify successive blocks by the fixed common basis, so \(R\theta_j=\theta_{j+1}\), and extend it to fix the standard hyperbolic basis \(e,f\), with \((e,f)=1\).
Write
\[
\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-z^{2^k})=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\epsilon_j z^j,
\qquad
v=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\epsilon_j\theta_j,\qquad w=R^Nv.
\]
Every \(\epsilon_j\) is one or minus one, by the unique binary expansion of \(j\).
Since \(g>2N\), the two supports are disjoint and
\[
(v,v)=(w,w)=2N,\qquad (v,w)=0.
\]
Use the integral transvection convention in [L462 v13](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#integer-generators) and put
\[
C=T_{e,v}T_{f,w}T_{e,-v}T_{f,-w},\qquad A=RC.
\]
Rightmost factors act first.
The specified word has block-permutation product \(R\), of order \(g\); the transvection factors carry no such permutation.
Thus C886 gives only its \(g\)-dependent gap for this presentation, not a uniform absolute obstruction.
It does not rule out a different presentation with a stronger bound.

<a id="active-polynomial"></a>
## Separate every neutral complementary factor

Let
\[
V=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{g-1}\mathbb Q\theta_j,\qquad
W=V\oplus\mathbb Q e\oplus\mathbb Q f.
\]
Both \(R\) and all four transvections preserve \(W\).
On its integral basis \((\theta_0,\ldots,\theta_{g-1},e,f)\), the pairing is \(2I_g\oplus U\) and the restrictions are integer matrices.
The rational orthogonal complement is the sum of the \(g\) seven-dimensional root complements in the positive E8 blocks.
Every transvection fixes this complement pointwise, and \(R\) cyclically permutes its identical seven-dimensional copies.
Consequently
\[
P_A(z)=(z^g-1)^7 P_{\mathrm{act}}(z),\qquad
P_{\mathrm{act}}(z)=\det(zI-A|_W)\in\mathbb Z[z].       \tag{1}
\]
No integral direct-sum decomposition of the E8 block into a root and its complement is required: the invariant rational decomposition proves the complete characteristic identity.
All removed factors are cyclotomic, with their full multiplicities.
The [negative-index-one argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#negative-index-bound) gives
\[
M(P_A)=M(P_{\mathrm{act}})=\rho(A).
\]
This equality supplies no estimate making that radius close to one.

<a id="four-dimensional-word"></a>
## The complete four-dimensional word

Inside \(W\), let \(S:\mathbb Q^4\to W\) have columns \(v,w,e,f\), and define the coordinate projection
\[
\Pi(x+\alpha e+\beta f)
=\bigl((v,x)/(2N),(w,x)/(2N),\alpha,\beta\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Then \(\Pi S=I_4\).
The four transvections fix the kernel of \(\Pi\) pointwise and preserve its complementary span, so their whole product is
\[
C|_W=I_W+S(C_N-I_4)\Pi.
\]
In the ordered basis \(v,w,e,f\), write
\[
E_\sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&\sigma\\
0&1&0&0\\
-2\sigma N&0&1&-N\\
0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F_\tau=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\
0&1&\tau&0\\
0&0&1&0\\
0&-2\tau N&-N&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \sigma,\tau\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
These follow directly from the transvection formula and the three displayed pairings of \(v,w\).
The squared-norm entries remain \(-N\) for either sign.
Multiplying in the specified order gives
\[
C_N=E_+F_+E_-F_-=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-2N^2&2N^3-2N&N-N^3&N^2\\
2N&1-2N^2&N^2&-N\\
2N^3&2N^2-2N^4&1-N^2+N^4&-N^3\\
-2N^2&2N^3&-N^3&1+N^2
\end{pmatrix}.                                          \tag{2}
\]
For example, successive application to coordinates \((u,t,\alpha,\beta)\) first gives
\[
F_-:\ (u,t-\alpha,\alpha,\beta+2Nt-N\alpha)
\]
and then
\[
E_-F_-:\ \bigl(u-\beta-2Nt+N\alpha,\ t-\alpha,\
2Nu-2N^2t+(1+N^2)\alpha-N\beta,\
\beta+2Nt-N\alpha\bigr).
\]
Applying \(F_+\) and \(E_+\) to this expression gives all four rows of (2), with no omitted action outside this span.

<a id="complete-determinant"></a>
## A four-by-four determinant for the entire polynomial

On the positive span \(V\), put
\[
K(z)=(zI-R|_V)^{-1}R|_V,\qquad
s(z)=\frac{(v,Kv)}{2N},\quad
x(z)=\frac{(v,Kw)}{2N},\quad
y(z)=\frac{(w,Kv)}{2N},\quad
k(z)=\frac1{z-1}.
\]
The pairing in these expressions is \(2I_g\).
Since \(w=R^Nv\) and \(K\) commutes with the orthogonal map \(R\), one has \((w,Kw)=(v,Kv)\).
For \(z^g\ne1\), define
\[
Q(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
s&x&0&0\\
y&s&0&0\\
0&0&k&0\\
0&0&0&k
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The block \(kI_2\) uses that \(R\) fixes both hyperbolic vectors.
More precisely,
\[
Q(z)=\Pi(zI-R|_W)^{-1}R|_W S.
\]
The determinant identity for \(I-XY\) and \(I-YX\), applied to
\[
zI-A|_W=(zI-R|_W)-R|_W S(C_N-I_4)\Pi,
\]
therefore gives
\[
P_{\mathrm{act}}(z)
=(z^g-1)(z-1)^2
\det\!\bigl(I_4-Q(z)(C_N-I_4)\bigr).                    \tag{3}
\]
Initially this is a rational identity away from the eigenvalues of \(R|_W\); multiplication by the displayed factors extends it to the complete polynomial identity.
Combining (1) and (3) retains every original root and multiplicity.

There is also an exact finite correlation expression for its inputs.
Let \(a=(\epsilon_0,\ldots,\epsilon_{N-1},0,\ldots,0)^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb Z^g\), let \(R_g\) be the cyclic permutation matrix in this coefficient basis, and put \(c_j=a^{\mathsf T}R_g^j a\), with indices modulo \(g\).
Since
\[
K(z)=\frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^g z^{g-\ell}R_g^\ell}{z^g-1},
\]
the three rational functions are
\[
s(z)=\frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^g z^{g-\ell}c_\ell}{N(z^g-1)},
\quad
x(z)=\frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^g z^{g-\ell}c_{\ell+N}}{N(z^g-1)},
\quad
y(z)=\frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^g z^{g-\ell}c_{\ell-N}}{N(z^g-1)}. \tag{4}
\]
The factor two in the lattice pairing cancels against the \(2N\) normalization.
The different signs of the two shifts follow from \(w=R^Nv\) and orthogonality of \(R\).

<a id="two-dimensional-exterior-test"></a>
## The determinant at two reduces to dimension two

The last two rows of \(C_N-I_4\) in (2) are minus \(N\) times its first and second rows, respectively.
Thus
\[
C_N-I_4=BD,\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\-N&0\\0&-N\end{pmatrix},
\quad
D=\begin{pmatrix}
-2N^2&2N^3-2N&N-N^3&N^2\\
2N&-2N^2&N^2&-N
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinant identity used in (3) reduces its remaining factor further:
\[
\det(I_4-QBD)=\det(I_2-DQB).
\]
At \(z=2\), write \(s=s(2)\), \(x=x(2)\), \(y=y(2)\) and \(h=1+2s\), so \(k(2)=1\).
Direct multiplication gives
\[
I_2-DQ(2)B=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-N^4+N^2h-2N(N^2-1)y&
N^3(2-h)+N(h-1)+2N^2x\\
N^3+N(1-h)+2N^2y&
1+N^2(h-2)-2Nx
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Expanding this complete two-dimensional determinant yields
\[
\begin{split}
F_N={}&1+N^2(h^2-1)-2N^4\\
     &-2N(2N^2-1)y-2N(N^2+1)x-4N^2xy.                 \tag{5}
\end{split}
\]
In particular the apparent sixth-degree terms cancel; the remaining negative quartic term must be compared with the actual correlation bounds.

The positive isometry \(R|_V\) has operator norm one for the given positive pairing.
Its convergent Neumann series gives
\[
K(2)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}2^{-\ell}R^\ell,\qquad \|K(2)\|\le1.
\]
The two vector norms are both \(\sqrt{2N}\), so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|s|\le1,\qquad |x|\le1,\qquad |y|\le1,\qquad |h|\le3.    \tag{6}
\]
All these quantities are real.
For \(N\ge8\), equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
F_N\le1-2N^4+6N^3+12N^2<0.                            \tag{7}
\]
Indeed \(N^2-6N-12\ge4\) for \(N\ge8\), so the right side is at most \(1-N^4\).
No detailed Thue--Morse correlation estimate is needed for this tail.

<a id="two-small-parameters"></a>
## The two remaining parameters

For \(n=1\), one has \(N=2\), \(g=9\), and the coefficient vector starts \((1,-1)\).
Its only nonzero cyclic correlations are \(c_0=2\) and \(c_1=c_8=-1\).
Formula (4) therefore gives exactly
\[
s=-\frac{128}{511},\qquad
x=-\frac1{511},\qquad
y=-\frac{32}{511},\qquad
h=\frac{255}{511}.
\]
In (5), the term \(N^2(h^2-1)\) is negative and the cross term \(-4N^2xy\) is negative.
The remaining two correlation terms sum to \(916/511<2\), so
\[
F_2<1-32+2=-29<0.                                     \tag{8}
\]

For \(n=2\), one has \(N=4\), \(g=65\), and the coefficient polynomial is
\[
a(t)=(1-t)(1-t^2)=1-t-t^2+t^3.
\]
The two shifted supports do not wrap around the cycle.
The finite sums in (4) give
\[
y=\frac{2^{-N}a(2)a(1/2)}{N(1-2^{-g})},\qquad
x=2^{2N-g}y.
\]
To see the first identity, put \(j=\ell-N\) in its numerator; the nonzero terms have \(-(N-1)\le j\le N-1\), and their sum is \(2^{g-N}\sum_j2^{-j}c_j=2^{g-N}a(2)a(1/2)\).
For \(x\), the nonzero terms are centered at \(\ell=g-N\), giving the stated positive ratio.
Here \(a(2)=3\), \(a(1/2)=3/8\), so \(x,y>0\).
The last three terms of (5) are therefore negative, and (6) gives
\[
F_4\le1+8\cdot16-2\cdot256=-383<0.                    \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) cover every \(N=2^n\) with \(n\ge1\).

<a id="complete-commutator-obstruction"></a>
## Complete exterior obstruction and research consequence

At \(z=2\), the prefactors in (1) and (3) are strictly positive.
The negative determinant proved above consequently gives \(P_A(2)<0\).
The complete polynomial is real and monic, so it is positive for all sufficiently large positive arguments.
The intermediate value theorem supplies an actual real root greater than two.
Therefore every member of this exact family satisfies
\[
M(P_A)>2\qquad(n\ge1).                                \tag{10}
\]
This conclusion uses the whole polynomial, including all multiplicities; it is not an estimate on one chosen factor substituted for the original measure.
The negative-index-one equality earlier remains consistent with it but is not needed to infer (10) from the exterior root.

The growing block-permutation order meets the necessary condition in C886 but does not lower the measure in this construction.
The formal commutator does cancel higher determinant terms, yet its remaining quartic term defeats the bounded correlations.
This closes both shrinking measures and the finite \(6/5\) target for this precise candidate, so no sampling campaign for it is warranted.
No general four-factor gap, exclusion of arbitrary parameter pairs, or theorem for all positive integral lattices follows.
The complete [G0001 objective](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) remains unresolved.
