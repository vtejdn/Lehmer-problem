# Two possible circle distributions at the first discrepancy of a delay-two input

<a id="marginal"></a>
## The power marginal has integral doubled moments

Consider monic Pisot seeds whose delay-two Mahler measures are strictly greater than one and tend to one.
Use the [signed first discrepancy](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v29.md#statement) at index \(n\to\infty\), extract a subsequence with its sign \(a=\delta\in\{-1,1\}\) fixed, and put \(p=n+1\).
Let \(\mu\) be the [positive measure](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#positive) for one input, and take any weak limit \(\nu\) of its pushforwards by \(v\mapsto y=v^p\).
Then \(\nu\) is either normalized arclength with density \(1+a\operatorname{Re}y\) or the half-sum of normalized arclength and the point mass at \(a\).

Indeed, define \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2F)\) for the signed seed quotient \(F=e f/f^*\), where \(e=f(0)=\pm1\).
Write \(h_0=2\) and \(h_j=[z^{|j|}]K^0\) for nonzero integers \(j\).
The [window estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#window) gives
\[
          \sup_{|j|\le Cn}\left|h_j-2\int v^j\,d\mu(v)\right|\longrightarrow0
\]
for every fixed \(C\), including zero since the masses tend to one.
The \(h_j\) are integers, with \(h_j=0\) for \(1\le j<p\) and \(h_p=a\) by the [centered identity](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#slice).
The probability \(\nu\) is invariant under conjugation and \(2\int y^l\,d\nu=\lim h_{lp}\) is an integer for each \(l\ge1\).
Its Herglotz transform has constant one and first coefficient \(a\).
The [periodic decomposition of integral doubled moments](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) expresses \(\nu\) as a finite measure supported on roots of unity plus a trigonometric-polynomial density.
In particular, a purely atomic \(\nu\) would be supported on \(y^m=1\) for a fixed positive integer \(m\).

<a id="period"></a>
## Exact periodicity forces an impossible disk-pole identity

We first exclude any sequence of integers \(2\le L=O(n)\) such that \(\int |1-v^L|^2\,d\mu(v)\to0\).
A purely atomic \(\nu\) would supply such a sequence with \(L=mp\), by weak convergence of the pushed-forward measures.
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds \(2|\int v^j(1-v^L)\,d\mu|\) by a quantity tending to zero uniformly in the integer \(j\).
Since \(L=O(n)\), the window estimate on a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(n\) gives \(h_{j+L}=h_j\) for every \(|j|\le Cn\) for all sufficiently large inputs, for each fixed \(C\).
The equality is exact by integrality.
In particular \(h_L=2\), and \(h_{L-j}=h_j\) for \(1\le j<L\) by reflection.
Define the rational function
\[
       S(z)=\frac{1+\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}h_j z^j+z^L}{1-z^L}.
\]
Its Taylor constant is one, its positive coefficients repeat with period \(L\), and the coefficient at each positive multiple of \(L\) is two.
Consequently \(K^0\) and \(S\) agree through degree \(4L+12\) for all sufficiently large inputs by the exact recurrence.
The symmetry of the numerator gives \(S(1/z)=-S(z)\).

Invert the comparison formula by putting
\[
        A=1-z-S,\qquad B=z^2S-z(1-z),\qquad F'=A/B .
\]
Since \(S(0)=1\) and \(S'(0)=h_1=0\), both \(A,B\) have leading Taylor term \(-z\), so \(F'\) is analytic at zero with value one.
Also \(B(z)=z^2A(1/z)\), giving \(F'(1/z)F'(z)=1\).
The coefficients are real, so the reduced \(F'\) has modulus one on the circle away from finitely many points.
It has no circle pole, since the modulus would then diverge along the circle near that pole.
Clearing \(1-z^L\) in \(A,B\) shows that \(F'\) has rational degree at most \(L+2\).
The inverse identity gives \(S=(1-z)(1+zF')/(1+z^2F')\), whence
\[
       K^0-S=\frac{z(1-z)^2(F-F')}{(1+z^2F)(1+z^2F')}.
\]
Thus \(F,F'\) agree through degree \(4L+11\).

Let \(B_0\) be a product of disk Blaschke factors, one for the [single disk pole of \(F\)](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#input) and one for each disk pole of \(F'\) with multiplicity.
It has degree at most \(L+3\).
Both \(B_0F\) and \(B_0F'\) are rational and analytic on the closed disk with circle modulus one, hence Schur.
The latter is a finite Blaschke product of degree at most \(2L+5\), by the rational degrees before cancellation.
Multiplication preserves the common Taylor jet, so [finite Blaschke uniqueness](Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#unique) implies \(B_0F=B_0F'\) and hence \(K^0=S\).
But the [interior root](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#input) \(\tau\in(0,1)\) of the positive-excess matched delay polynomial satisfies \(F(\tau)=-\tau^{-2}\).
It is a pole of \(K^0\), since the numerator there is \((1-\tau)(1-1/\tau)\ne0\), whereas \(S\) has no disk pole.
This rules out the proposed concentration and hence excludes a purely atomic \(\nu\).

<a id="moving"></a>
## Every positive coefficient on a linear window is zero or a unit

For every fixed \(C>0\), all sufficiently large inputs satisfy \(|h_j|\le1\) simultaneously for \(1\le j\le Cn\).
Indeed the window estimate and the masses tending to one already give \(|h_j|\le2\) by integrality.
If some \(j=O(n)\) had \(h_j=2\epsilon\) on a subsequence with fixed \(\epsilon=\pm1\), then \(\int |v^j-\epsilon|^2\,d\mu\to0\) by moment approximation.
The inequality \(|1-v^{2j}|\le2|v^j-\epsilon|\) would give the excluded concentration at \(L=2j\).
In particular, for any moving frequency \(1\le j=O(n)\), a subsequential law of \(v^j\) cannot be purely atomic.
Indeed it again has integral doubled moments, so by the periodic decomposition a purely atomic law would be supported on some fixed \(y^m=1\).
This would force \(h_{mj}\to2\) on a linear window.

<a id="laws"></a>
## The two transforms with a continuous part

The [integral Herglotz classification](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) for a probability with a nonzero continuous part gives the transforms \(1\), \(1+\epsilon w^d\), or \((1+\epsilon w^d)^{-1}\), with \(d\ge1\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
The first coefficient \(a\) therefore leaves exactly \(1+aw\) and \((1-aw)^{-1}\).
The first has density \(1+a\operatorname{Re}y\) against normalized arclength.
The second equals \(\tfrac12+\tfrac12(1+aw)/(1-aw)\), giving the stated half arclength and half point mass.
This constrains every power-marginal limit for a fixed sign and leaves open whether positive-excess inputs tending to one exist.
