# A cyclotomic recurrence cannot persist on a window of accurate integer moments

<a id="annihilator"></a>
## Concentration on a union of torsion conditions

Consider actual positive-excess delay-two unit Pisot inputs tending to measure one, with signed quotient \(F\), integer comparison \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2 F)\) and [positive measure](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#positive) \(\mu\).
Let \(h_0=2,\ h_j=[z^{|j|}]K^0\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\).
Assume there is a scale \(s\to\infty\) such that for every fixed \(C>0\),
\[
               \sup_{|j|\le Cs}|h_j-2\int v^j\,d\mu(v)|\longrightarrow0 .
\]
There cannot be real integer polynomials \(D=\sum_{m=0}^d d_m z^m\) along these inputs with constant one, degree \(1\le d=O(s)\), all roots on the unit circle, leading coefficient \(\pm1\), bounded coefficient absolute sum, and \(\int |D(\bar v)|^2\,d\mu\to0\).
In particular one cannot have concentration of this kind on a union of two binomial root sets with positive exponents of order \(O(s)\).

For every fixed \(C\), Cauchy–Schwarz and the window assumption give
\[
              \sum_{m=0}^d d_m h_{j-m}=0\qquad(|j|\le Cs)
\]
for all sufficiently large inputs, since these integers approach zero uniformly.
The masses of \(\mu\) approach one, and the enlarged interval needed for the approximation still has size \(O_C(s)\).
The recurrence, with unit constant and unit leading coefficient, extends the bilateral data uniquely from \(d\) consecutive indices to a full sequence \(b_j\).
For precision, take initial data \(b_0,\ldots,b_{d-1}=h_0,\ldots,h_{d-1}\).
Using the recurrence in both directions gives \(b_j=h_j\) on \(|j|\le 4d+12\) for large inputs, taking \(C\) larger than the constant in \(5d+12\le Cs\).
Since \(D^*=\pm D\), the reflected sequence \(b_{-j}\) satisfies the same recurrence and agrees with \(b_j\) on the initial block, so \(b_{-j}=b_j\) everywhere.

Set \(S(z)=1+\sum_{j>0} b_j z^j\).
The recurrence makes \(N=D S\) a polynomial of degree at most \(d\).
In particular \(S\) has no pole in the open disk.
The Laurent polynomial \(D(z)(S(z)+S(1/z))\) vanishes identically.
Indeed both summands after multiplication are Laurent polynomials, the second by reciprocity of \(D\), and the coefficient at \(z^j\) of their sum is precisely \(\sum d_m b_{j-m}\), using the Taylor expansion for \(S(z)\), its reflected expansion for \(S(1/z)\), and \(b_0=2\).
It follows that \(S(1/z)=-S(z)\).

## Returning to the actual positive-excess seed

The [inverse interpolation argument](Yin_L_G0462_v123.md#period) now applies with this \(D\) in place of \(1-z^L\).
In detail, \(S(0)=1,\ S'(0)=h_1=0\), and \(F'=(1-z-S)/(z^2S-z(1-z))\) has a removable singularity at zero with value one.
The denominator is \(z^2\) times the numerator evaluated at \(1/z\).
Thus the reduced real rational function \(F'\) has circle modulus one and no circle poles.
Clearing \(D\) gives rational degree at most \(d+2\).
Its comparison germ is \(S\), and the comparison difference is
\[
 K^0-S=\frac{z(1-z)^2(F-F')}{(1+z^2 F)(1+z^2F')}.
\]
Thus \(F,F'\) agree through degree \(4d+11\).
Multiply both by the disk Blaschke factors for all disk poles of \(F'\) and the single pole of \(F\), using multiplicities.
Both products are Schur, and the product for \(F'\) is a finite Blaschke product of degree at most \(2d+5\).
The [finite Schur uniqueness argument](Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#unique) identifies both products and hence gives \(K^0=S\).
However the [actual positive-excess input](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#input) has an interior root \(\tau\in(0,1)\) with \(F(\tau)=-\tau^{-2}\), a genuine pole of \(K^0\).
This contradicts the analyticity of \(S\) there.
