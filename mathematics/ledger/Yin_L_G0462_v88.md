# Common rational divisors of the two signed norms of a unit

<a id="resonance"></a>
## The exponents with equal signed norm magnitudes

Let \(Q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and write \(a_1,\ldots,a_D\) for its conjugates in a splitting field \(L\).
For \(N\ge1\) put
\[
    A_N=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^N+1)|,\qquad
    C_N=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^N-1)|.
\]
Both are positive integers.
Form the finite list of roots of unity \(\zeta=a_j/a_i^\sigma\) occurring for \(1\le i,j\le D\) and \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\).
Let \(E_Q\) consist of the exponents \(N\) for which some \(\zeta\) in this list has \(\zeta^N=-1\).
Each even order \(t\) in the list contributes the class \(N\equiv t/2\pmod t\), and odd orders contribute nothing.
With \(m(Q)=\log M(Q)>0\), one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
   N\in E_Q &: \quad A_N=C_N,\qquad
                 \log\gcd(A_N,C_N)=Nm(Q)+O_Q(\log(2N)),\\
   N\notin E_Q &: \quad \log\gcd(A_N,C_N)=o_Q(N)
                         \qquad(N\to\infty).
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
In particular, all sufficiently large exponents with \(A_N=C_N\) belong to \(E_Q\), since \(\log A_N=Nm(Q)+O_Q(\log(2N))\) by the [signed resultant estimate, (2)](Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution).

For \(N\in E_Q\) one has \(a_j^N=-a_i^{\sigma N}\).
Taking the Galois orbit with multiplicity shows that the two multisets \(\{a_r^N\}_{r=1}^D\) and \(\{-a_r^{\sigma N}\}_{r=1}^D\) coincide.
Indeed the automorphisms of \(L/\mathbb Q\) distribute \(a_i\) and \(a_j\) equally often over their common set of conjugates.
Taking products of the differences from one gives \(C_N=A_N\), using \(\big|\prod_r a_r\big|=1\) when \(\sigma=-1\).
The signed resultant estimate then gives the first line of (1).
For example, the two roots of \(Q=X^2-X-1\) satisfy \(a_2=-a_1^{-1}\).
Their absolute values show that the only torsion ratios in the list are \(1,-1\), so \(E_Q\) consists exactly of the odd exponents.

<a id="valuation"></a>
## Controlling common prime powers in rational norms

Use the integral valuation \(v_{\mathfrak P}\) at each finite prime of \(L\), and put \(s=[L:\mathbb Q]\).
For fixed \(N\), let \(x_i=a_i^N+1\) and \(y_j=a_j^N-1\).
For nonnegative real lists \((b_i),(c_j)\) one has
\(\min(\sum_i b_i,\sum_j c_j)\le\sum_{i,j}\min(b_i,c_j)\).
Indeed, if the first sum is smaller, then \(\sum_j\min(b_i,c_j)\ge\min(b_i,\sum_j c_j)=b_i\) for every \(i\), and the other ordering is symmetric.
Applying this at each prime to the valuations of \(x_i,y_j\) gives
\[
 \log\gcd(A_N,C_N)\le
  \sum_{i,j}\frac1s\sum_{\mathfrak P}
     \min\{v_{\mathfrak P}(x_i),v_{\mathfrak P}(y_j)\}\log N\mathfrak P .
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
On the left the sum before division by \(s\) is the log norm of the rational gcd's extended ideal, because \(A_N,C_N\) are, up to sign, the respective products of the two lists.

If \(a_i,a_j\) are multiplicatively dependent, choose a relation \(a_i^r a_j^t=1\) with \(r,t\) nonzero.
Their common absolute logarithmic height \(h(\alpha)=m(Q)/D>0\) gives \(|r|=|t|\) by taking heights on either side of \(a_i^r=a_j^{-t}\).
Thus \(a_j=\zeta a_i^\sigma\) for one of the listed roots of unity and one choice of sign.
For \(N\notin E_Q\), the ideal generated by \(x_i,y_j\) contains \(1+\zeta^N\).
In fact for \(\sigma=1\) one has \(\zeta^N x_i-y_j=1+\zeta^N\), and for \(\sigma=-1\) one has \(x_i+a_i^N y_j=1+\zeta^N\).
The finite set of possible nonzero \(1+\zeta^N\) therefore bounds this pair's contribution in (2) by \(O_Q(1)\), including the primes above two.

For an independent pair use \(u=-a_i^N,\ v=a_j^N\).
Squaring any putative multiplicative relation of \(u,v\) shows that these units are independent, and both heights are \(Nh(\alpha)\).
Take \(S\) to consist of the archimedean places of \(L\).
By [CZ05, arXiv v2, Proposition 2, (2.8), printed p. 7](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd), for each \(\epsilon>0\) the normalized log norm of the ideal \((u-1,v-1)\) is at most \(\epsilon Nh(\alpha)\) apart from finitely many independent \(S\)-unit pairs.
This is the summand for \(i,j\) in (2) since \(u-1=-x_i,\ v-1=y_j\).
The units \(a_j^N\) are distinct as \(N\) varies, so only finitely many exponents can give an exceptional pair.
Each independent pair of conjugates thus contributes \(o_Q(N)\).
Summing over the fixed \(D^2\) pairs proves the second line of (1).

<a id="dyadic-lcm"></a>
## Growth of the least common multiple of dyadic norms

Set \(N_k=2^{k-1}\) and let \(W_k\) be the odd part of \(A_{N_k}\) for \(k\ge1\).
If \(N_k\in E_Q\), the root of unity witnessing this has exact order \(2^k\) and belongs to \(\mathbb Q(a_i,a_j)\).
Its degree \(2^{k-1}\) is at most \([\mathbb Q(a_i,a_j):\mathbb Q]\le D^2\), so all \(N_k>D^2\) avoid \(E_Q\).
Since \(C_{N_k}=C_1\prod_{j<k}A_{N_j}\) by repeated difference of squares, (1) yields
\[
  \log\gcd\!\left(W_k,C_1\prod_{j<k} W_j\right)=o_Q(N_k).
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
The [local multiplicity calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#local) gives \(v_2(A_{N_k})=\operatorname{ord}_{X=1}(Q\bmod2)\) once \(N_k>D\).
The signed resultant estimate consequently gives \(\log W_k=N_k m(Q)+O_Q(k+1)\).
Putting \(H_0=1\) and \(H_k=\operatorname{lcm}(W_1,\ldots,W_k)\), we obtain
\[
    \log(H_k/H_{k-1})=N_km(Q)+o_Q(N_k),\qquad
    \log H_k=2^k m(Q)+o_Q(2^k).                                     \tag{4}
\]
The increment is the logarithm of \(W_k/\gcd(W_k,H_{k-1})\), whose denominator divides the gcd in (3).
Summing the errors in the increments gives \(o_Q(2^k)\) because \(\sum_{j\le k}2^{j-1}=2^k-1\) and the relative error is arbitrarily small beyond a fixed initial segment.
For a reciprocal base write \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), and for \(k\ge2\) let \(w_k\) be the odd part of \(\big|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{2^{k-2}}+\alpha^{-2^{k-2}})\big|\).
Factoring \(\alpha^{2^{k-1}}+1\) into \(\alpha^{2^{k-2}}\) times that trace element and taking the norm through \([K:F]=2\) gives \(W_k=w_k^2\), as in the [trace norm identity](Yin_L_G0462_v83.md#trace-norm).
Thus \(\log\operatorname{lcm}(w_2,\ldots,w_k)=2^{k-1}m(Q)+o_Q(2^k)\), since dropping \(W_1\) changes the logarithm in (4) by at most \(\log W_1\).
The quotient in (4) counts increases of rational prime valuations, which can include primes already dividing an earlier term.

<a id="prescribed"></a>
## Removing the common divisor at the prescribed order

For the [irreducible substitutions \(P(z)=Q(z^p)\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#statement), let \(d=Dp,\ 2^k=2^{\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}\), and set
\[
     B_k=\frac{W_k}{\gcd(W_k,C_{N_k})}.
\]
The power map with exponent \(p\) permutes each set of primitive dyadic roots of unity and fixes \(1\).
Hence all \(W_j\) and \(C_1\), as well as \(C_{N_k}\) by the same telescoping product, agree for \(P\) and \(Q\).
For each fixed \(Q\), (1) gives \(\log B_k=(1+o_Q(1))N_km(Q)\).

There is a nondecreasing integer-valued \(g(d)\to\infty\) for which this relative estimate holds uniformly on the prescribed class with \(D\le g(d)\).
For each \(r\ge1\) there are only finitely many bases \(0<m(Q)\le1,\ D\le r\), since the elementary symmetric formula bounds the absolute coefficient of \(X^{D-i}\) by \(e\binom Di\).
Choose strictly increasing integer thresholds \(d_r\) so that for all those bases and every \(d\ge d_r\) the relative error in the displayed fixed-base estimate is at most \(1/r\).
This is possible because \(N_k\ge d/2\).
For \(d\ge d_1\) take \(g(d)=\max\{r:d_r\le d\}\), extending it by zero below \(d_1\).

On this restricted class the positive Lehmer gap is equivalent to \(\log B_k\ge c d\) for some absolute \(c>0\) at all sufficiently large \(d\).
A positive gap gives the bound by uniformity and \(N_k\ge d/2\).
Conversely, fix any base with \(0<m(Q)\le1\) and take arbitrarily large admissible \(p\equiv1\bmod4\) by the [fixed-base lift argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#criterion).
The restriction \(D\le g(d)\) eventually holds, and \(N_k<d\) gives \(m(Q)\ge c\) on passing to the limit.
The units with \(m(Q)>1\) and the nonunit algebraic integers of positive measure already have absolute lower bounds.
The diagonal choice of \(g\) gives no explicit growth rate or positive absolute lower bound for \(\log B_k/d\).
