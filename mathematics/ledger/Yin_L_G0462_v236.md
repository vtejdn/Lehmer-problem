# The coefficient boxes already excluded and a degree-ten calculation

<a id="boxes"></a>
## An exclusion with a degree-dependent exponent

Let \(p_e\) be the least prime above \(16e^4\) in the [finite integer test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#integer), and let \(H_*\) be the largest absolute coefficient in just one admissible pair \(a,b\) of seed polynomials.
Put \(b_e=\max\{(\log\log e/\log e)^3/4,\ 2/(\log(3e))^3\}\).
The individual row bound and [Voutier's theorem and Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) give
\[
             \log H_*>p_e b_e-\log(4e+1).
\]
For \(k_e=\lceil 2\log(2)/b_e\rceil\), all sufficiently large even degrees therefore exclude every single admissible row with coefficients bounded by \(2^{\lfloor p_e/k_e\rfloor}\).
Indeed \(\log(4e+1)<p_e b_e/2\) eventually, while the logarithm of the box bound is at most \(p_e b_e/2\).
This gives \(k_e=O((\log e/\log\log e)^3)\).
The unrestricted criterion asks for one constant integer \(k\) and only needs to exclude two admissible rows whose value and derivative columns are independent.

## The prescribed prime and coefficient size at the Lehmer polynomial

At \(e=10\) the [degree-only choice](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#uniform) gives \(p=160001,\ n=3520021\), with \(n=22p-1\).
Trial division through the integer square root certifies that both displayed numbers are prime, and \(160001\) is the first integer above \(16\cdot10^4\).
For \(X=\exp(0.9p^{1/4})/30\) one has \(2188665<X<2210663\), using \(18<0.9p^{1/4}<18.01\).
For example, for \(x=18,18.01\) bound \(\exp(x)\) below by its Taylor sum through degree 99 and above by adding \(x^{100}/(100!(1-x/101))\).
The first candidates \(jp-1>X\), \(14\le j\le21\), have divisors \(3,2,5,2,7,2,3,2\), respectively, and \(22p-1<2X\).
The inequalities \(18<\log(30n)<19\) give \(\lceil(\log(30n))^4\rceil<p<2\lceil(\log(30n))^4\rceil\).

For the [degree-ten Lehmer polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v148.md#lehmer)
\[
         P_0=x^{10}+x^9-x^7-x^6-x^5-x^4-x^3+x+1,
\]
substitution gives \(P_0(117/100)=-14912584160608812851/10^{20}<0\).
It has a real root greater than \(117/100\), so the coefficient bound on each admissible row gives
\[
     H_*\ \ge\ \exp(p\,m(P_0))/41\ >\ (117/100)^{160001}/41\ >\ 10^{10900}.
\]
The last comparison is the integer inequality \(117^{160001}>41\cdot100^{160001}10^{10900}\).
This estimates the size of actual coefficients on the classical input without enumerating the box and supplies no lower bound for other seeds beyond their own measures.
