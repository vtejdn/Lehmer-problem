# Excluding small continuous densities on every fixed Pisot coefficient branch

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction on the continuous part of a possible limit

Fix a first coefficient \(j\ge1\) of the [normalized series](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) of a positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomial.
Suppose \(K_q\) tends coefficientwise to \(K\) along a sequence of integer constants \(q\to\infty\).
Write \(\mu\) for the Herglotz measure of \(K\), of mass \(j\).
The [classification of its Fourier coefficients](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) writes \(\nu=2\mu\) as a finite atomic measure supported on roots of unity plus a nonnegative integer Laurent polynomial \(\rho\) times normalized arc length.
The [strict moment bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#statement) ensures that \(\rho\) is nonzero.
Then at least one of the following holds:
\[
  I_-=\int_{\mathbb T}\frac{d\mu(v)}{2+v+\bar v}=\infty,
  \qquad M(\rho)\ge \frac87 .                                      \tag{1}
\]
Here \(M\) is multiplicative Mahler measure, unchanged on multiplying a Laurent polynomial by a monomial.
For these measures \(I_-\) is finite precisely when there is no atom at \(-1\) and \(\rho(-1)=0\), since a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial vanishing there vanishes at least quadratically.
More precisely every candidate violating (1) has a finite coefficient prefix that no actual \(K_q\) can match once \(q\) is sufficiently large.
Both thresholds can depend on the candidate.
The first-discrepancy method is motivated by [Boyd's narrow-branch analysis](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#narrow-branches); here the initial interval must be bounded for varying \(q\).

For example, for every fixed \(j\) this excludes the entirely continuous density
\[
             \rho(z)=|1+z+\cdots+z^{2j-1}|^2,\qquad |z|=1 .
\]
It has mean \(2j\), vanishes at \(-1\), and has measure one since the geometric polynomial is a product of cyclotomic polynomials.
At \(j=2\) the excluded series is \(2+3z+2z^2+z^3\).
The proof applies with arbitrary finite atomic mass subject to (1), independently of the leading coefficient of the trace numerator.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## All real Schur completions at a fixed prefix

Use \(Y=(q-z)/(1-z^2)+zK/(1-z^2)^2,\ F=Y/(1-zY)\) and \(S=z(qF-1)/(F-q)\) for any candidate \(K\) with \(K(0)=j\).
For an actual positive input \(S\) is a [real Schur function](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#schur), meaning analytic and bounded by one on the disk with real Taylor coefficients.
Always \(u_1=F'(0)=q^2-1+j\).
Write \(d=j/u_1\), so \(S(0)=1-d\).
For an arbitrary real Schur completion with this constant, \(H=(1-S)/d\) has positive real part and \(H(0)=1\).
Solving the three rational formulas at zero gives
\[
              K=\frac{d(q^2-1)H(1-z^2)}
                       {1-z^2-d(1+qz)H}.
\]
For each fixed \(R<1\), the Schwarz lemma on \((H-1)/(H+1)\) bounds \(|H|\) on \(|z|\le R\) in terms of \(R\).
Consequently \(K=jH+O_{j,R}(1/q)\) there for large \(q\), uniformly over all these Schur completions.
This comparison also applies to their real, possibly nonintegral, next coefficients.

Assume \(M(\rho)<8/7\).
There is a real polynomial \(p\), \(p(0)=1\), vanishing on the finite atoms, such that
\[
                            \int |p|^2\,d\nu<8/7 .                  \tag{2}
\]
Indeed factor \(\rho(e^{it})=|E(e^{it})|^2\) with \(E\) real, no zeros in the open disk, and \(E(0)>0\).
This follows by pairing roots of the reciprocal Laurent polynomial off the circle with their reciprocals, taking the exterior ones and half the even multiplicities on the circle, with conjugate roots paired to keep real coefficients.
Normalizing at a point of positive density gives the squared modulus identity, and Jensen's formula gives \(E(0)^2=M(\rho)\).
For \(b<1\) approaching one, \(p_b=E(0)/E(bz)\) has constant one and \(\int |p_b|^2\rho\,dm\to E(0)^2\), where \(m\) is normalized arc length.
For each boundary root the ratio \(|1-w|/|1-bw|\) is at most two on \(|w|=1\) for \(1/2<b<1\); all exterior-root ratios are uniformly bounded.
Thus dominated convergence applies.
For fixed \(b\), real Taylor polynomials of \(p_b\) approximate it uniformly on the circle.
Multiply one such polynomial by \(\prod_v(1-N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^N(\bar v z)^k)\) over all distinct atoms.
This real polynomial factor is one at zero, vanishes at every atom, is bounded uniformly in \(N\), and tends to one almost everywhere.
Choosing \(b\), the approximation order and \(N\) in turn proves (2).

Fix \(n>2\deg p+\deg_+\rho\), where \(\deg_+\) is the largest Laurent exponent.
For any positive measure \(\nu'\) of mass \(2j\) sharing the real symmetric moments of \(\nu\) through index \(n-1\), \(\int|p|^2\,d\nu'=\int|p|^2\,d\nu\).
The integral \(\int z^{-n}p(z)^2\,d\nu\) vanishes by the zeros at the atoms and the degree bound on the density.
Since \(p(0)=1\), replacing \(\nu\) by \(\nu'\) in this integral gives exactly the difference of the \(n\)-th moments.
Its absolute value is at most the left side of (2).

Therefore, for some \(r<8/7\) and this fixed \(n\), all sufficiently large \(q\) give an interval of radius at most \(r\) for the next \(K\) coefficient over real Schur completions of the candidate prefix through \(n-1\).
Otherwise choose a sequence of completions whose next coefficients differ from the candidate by more than some \(r\) strictly between (2) and \(8/7\).
The compact-disk bounds above give a subsequential limit \(jH_\infty\); twice its Herglotz measure provides a forbidden \(\nu'\).
The same reasoning works for any initially chosen larger fixed \(n\).

<a id="central"></a>
## One disk pole and two integral completions at every later index

Suppose \(I_-<\infty\).
The rational function \(K=U/V\) can be written with integral \(U,V\), \(V(0)=1\), and \(V\) a signed product of cyclotomic polynomials.
This follows from the eventually periodic integral coefficients.
For the central rational function \(F=A/B\) use the integral pair
\[
 A=(q-z)(1-z^2)V+zU,\qquad B=(1-qz)(1-z^2)V-z^2U .
\]
They satisfy \(B+zA=(1-z^2)^2V\), so common factors are cyclotomic up to sign.
In the open disk poles of \(F\) correspond to exterior solutions \(w=1/z\) of
\[
     w-q=\frac{zK(z)}{1-z^2}
        =\int\frac{d\mu(v)}{w+w^{-1}-(v+\bar v)} .
\]
Pairing conjugate directions gives the integral identity.
Nonreal solutions in the exterior are impossible by opposite imaginary signs.
On each exterior real ray the difference of the two sides is strictly increasing.
On the positive ray it is negative at \(q\) and tends to infinity.
On the negative ray its limit at \(-1\) is \(-1-q+I_-<0\) for large \(q\).
Hence \(F\) has just one disk pole, real and simple, since \(Y=1/z\ne0\) there.
On the circle away from finitely many exceptional points direct substitution gives
\[
            |B|^2-|A|^2=|1-z^2|^2|V|^2\rho .                       \tag{3}
\]
In fact \(1-2\Re(zY)=\Re K/(2\sin^2 t)\) at \(z=e^{it}\), and \(2\Re K=\rho\) away from the atoms.
The Laurent identity (3) then holds everywhere.
The reduced pair has no circle poles by boundedness along punctured arcs.
The reduced denominator has exactly one disk zero and dominates its numerator in modulus on the circle.

The outermost coefficient of the nonzero Laurent polynomial \(W=BB(1/z)-AA(1/z)\) has absolute value one.
Indeed by (3) it has the absolute value of the outermost coefficient of \(\rho\), a nonzero integer at most \(M(\rho)<2\).
The additional factors in (3) have outermost coefficients of modulus one.
Both \(L=\deg_+ W\) and the degrees of \(A,B\) are bounded in terms of \(K\), independently of \(q\).
For every \(m>\max(\deg A,\deg B)\) take
\[
         F_m^\pm=\frac{A\pm z^m B(1/z)}{B\pm z^m A(1/z)} .
\]
These integral series have constant \(q\).
Their difference from \(F\) is \(\pm z^m W/[B(B\pm z^m A(1/z))]\), so their first discrepancies are opposite units at index \(m-L\).
Both are rational of unit modulus on the circle after reduction.
Their denominators have at most one disk zero, counted with multiplicity.
Indeed factor out the common cyclotomic factor of \(A,B\), using its signed reciprocity also in the reversed term.
For the reduced pair, multiply the shifted term by a fixed \(0<u<1\) and apply Rouché on the circle to the dominating denominator; pass to \(u=1\) for the upper bound on interior zeros.
Because \(F_m^\pm(0)=q>1\), the reduced quotient must actually have a disk pole.
For either resulting quotient \(F_m^\pm=A_m/B_m\), its Schur transform \(z(qA_m-B_m)/(A_m-qB_m)\) is a real Schur function: in the reduced pair the denominator of the transform has just one interior zero by Rouché, namely the cancelled zero at the origin, and its boundary modulus is one.

## Uniqueness after the first integral discrepancy

On a common prefix the difference at coefficient \(k\ge1\) of \(K\) equals the difference at coefficient \(k+1\) of \(F\), by the formulas for \(Y,F\).
The difference at coefficient \(k\) of \(S\) is \(-(q^2-1)/u_1^2\) times that difference, since both denominators \(F-q\) start with \(u_1z\).
In particular the scale of the real coefficient intervals is independent of \(k\).
The reciprocal completions give an integral \(K\) series: \(F_m^\pm\) has integral coefficients, and the original polynomial formula in \(F_m^\pm/(1+zF_m^\pm)\) for \(K\) requires only an exact division by \(z\).
By taking \(m=k+1+L\), they give both unit discrepancies for every sufficiently large \(k\), with a threshold independent of \(q\).

For completeness, the radius \(R_k\) of the \(k\)-th coefficient interval for real Schur functions with earlier coefficients fixed is the product of the factors \(1-\gamma_i^2\) for \(0\le i<k\).
Here \(\gamma_i\in[-1,1]\) are the successive constant terms under the real Schur algorithm.
Indeed inversion at one step is \(H=(\gamma+zH_1)/(1+\gamma zH_1)\), multiplying the difference at the first free coefficient of \(H_1\) by \(1-\gamma^2\) and raising its index by one.
Every value \(\gamma_k\in[-1,1]\) is possible, so the interval is full, with \(\gamma_k\) its rescaled coordinate about its center.
The next radius is \(R_k(1-\gamma_k^2)\), including radius zero for an extremal choice.
The radii measured in \(K\) coefficients obey the same recursion by the constant change of scale just computed.

Choose the fixed \(n\) of the comparison large enough that the two reciprocal completions are available at every \(k\ge n\).
If an actual \(K_q\) agrees with the candidate through \(n-1\), the subsequent radii along common prefixes are at most \(r\) by monotonicity.
The series must eventually differ since the actual one has a [genuine interior pole](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) whereas the candidate has none.
At the first discrepancy, denote the current radius by \(R\).
Since the interval contains both unit deviations from the central coefficient, \(1\le R\le r\) and its midpoint differs from that coefficient by at most \(R-1\).
Integrality forces the chosen deviation to be \(1\) or \(-1\), at distance at least \(2-R\) from the midpoint.
The next radius is therefore at most
\[
              R\left(1-\frac{(2-R)^2}{R^2}\right)
                      =4-\frac4R<\tfrac12 .
\]
This bound persists thereafter.
An interval of length less than one contains at most one integer, so the actual series agrees forever with the integral reciprocal completion giving the same first deviation.
Inverting the rational normalization gives \(f/f^*=F_m^\pm\).

The polynomial \(z^m B(1/z)\pm A(z)\) is monic and has nonzero constant \(\pm q\).
It is divisible by the actual irreducible \(f\), since \(f,f^*\) are coprime.
By the denominator root count for \(F_m^\pm\), also valid before removing the common cyclotomic factor, the monic polynomial has at most one exterior root.
The actual \(f\) uses that root, so the remaining factor is cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
The displayed \(A,B\) give this multiple a number of terms bounded in terms of \(K\), for all \(q,m\).
The [positive-constant support bound](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) then bounds \(\theta-q\) away from zero, contrary to \(\theta-q=O_j(1/q)\) on the fixed branch.
This proves (1) and the asserted finite-prefix exclusion.
