# Estimating the cost of two residue classes for a varying input

2026-11-18T10:31.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## Sparse coefficient height and the integral relations of the original root

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit \(\alpha\), of degree \(d\).
Fix \(p\ge2\), two distinct residues, and a degree cap \(N\).
Let \(E\) consist of every integer from \(0\) to \(N\) in either residue class, and write \(s=|E|\).
For a multiple \(F\ne0\) of \(P\) using both classes, let \(R\) be the product of the primitive noncyclotomic factors of \(F/P\) other than \(x\), with multiplicities.
Put \(m=\log M\), and let \(C\) be the minimum of \(m(R)\), either over all such multiples or with the additional requirement of coprime rows in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\), where \(X=x^p\).
The minimum exists whenever the family is nonempty, by finiteness of primitive integral factors of bounded degree and measure.
If either class has no available exponent, both families are empty.

For each \(e\in E\), form the column of coefficients of \(x^e\bmod P\) in the basis \(1,x,\ldots,x^{d-1}\).
Let \(A\) be the matrix of these columns and \(\Lambda=\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\).
A vector \(z\in\Lambda\) gives an integral multiple \(F_z=\sum_{e\in E}z_ex^e\).
For the unrestricted minimum, call a vector admissible if each of its two coordinate blocks is nonzero.
For the coprime minimum use a different linear test.
Writing \(F_z=x^{r_1}U(x^p)+x^{r_2}V(x^p)\) with residues \(0\le r_i<p\), exclude the subspace \(U(\alpha^p)=V(\alpha^p)=0\).
Inside \(\Lambda\) it suffices to test one of these two evaluations, since \(\alpha\ne0\).
The test uses the same remainder columns applied to the corresponding single block.

For either admissibility condition let \(\lambda\) be the smallest sup norm of an admissible integer vector.
If no such vector exists, the corresponding family is empty.
For each nonempty family,
\[
     \log\lambda-(s-2)\log2\ \le\ m(P)+C\ \le\ \log\lambda+\log s.       \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(F\) may be made primitive by division by its integer content, so \(m(F)=m(P)+m(R)\).
The [Dobrowolski–Smyth sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives \(H(F)\le 2^{s-2}M(F)\) for a polynomial with between two and \(s\) terms, where \(H\) is maximum coefficient modulus.
Every primitive witness gives an admissible vector in its variant, proving the lower estimate.
In the other direction a vector realizing \(\lambda\) is primitive and \(M(F_z)\le\sum_e|z_e|\le s\lambda\).
In the coprime variant take the primitive integral gcd \(G\) of \(U,V\).
Its value at \(\alpha^p\) is nonzero, so division by \(G(x^p)\) keeps \(P\), keeps both rows and reduces or preserves the measure.
Gauss's lemma gives integral quotient rows and division only lowers their degrees.
Every intervening exponent in a row was allowed by the definition of \(E\), so the quotient respects the cap.
This also proves the two assertions about emptiness.

<a id="reduction"></a>
## A polynomial-time estimate from a reduced integer basis

Let \(b_1,\ldots,b_h\) be a \(3/4\)-LLL-reduced basis of the full integer kernel, where \(h=s-\operatorname{rank}A\).
For each variant keep the admissible basis vectors.
In the unrestricted variant also keep the sums \(b_i+b_j\) of vectors supported in opposite single blocks.
Let \(H_0\) be the smallest sup norm in the resulting list.
An empty list detects an empty family.
Otherwise
\[
         \lambda\ \le\ H_0\ \le\ 2^{(h+1)/2}\sqrt{s}\,\lambda.             \tag{2}
\]
In the coprime variant \(2^{(h-1)/2}\) suffices.
For a vector \(z\) realizing \(\lambda\), let \(i\) be its last nonzero basis coordinate.
Orthogonal projection perpendicular to \(b_1,\ldots,b_{i-1}\) gives \(\|z\|_2\ge\|b_i^*\|_2\), where the star denotes the Gram–Schmidt vector.
The LLL size and Lovász conditions give \(\|b_t^*\|^2\le2^{i-t}\|b_i^*\|^2\) for \(t\le i\).
For \(j\le i\), the bound \(|\mu_{jt}|\le1/2\) in the orthogonal expansion of \(b_j\) gives
\[
  \|b_j\|^2\le\left(2^{i-j}+\tfrac14\sum_{t<j}2^{i-t}\right)\|b_i^*\|^2
             \le 2^{i-1}\|b_i^*\|^2 .
\]
If every one of these \(i\) vectors is supported in just one block, the presence of \(z\) implies that both blocks occur, and two of the basis vectors sum to an admissible vector.
For the coprime variant at least one of the \(i\) vectors is outside the excluded linear subspace.
Since \(\|z\|_2\le\sqrt{s}\lambda\), (2) follows.
These arguments also cover rank one, while a zero kernel yields the empty list.

For completeness one can obtain the full kernel basis by a single reduction of the integer graph lattice
\[
                      \{(z,KAz):z\in\mathbb Z^s\}\subset\mathbb Z^{s+d}.
\]
If \(r=\operatorname{rank}A=s\) the kernel is zero; if \(r=0\) use the coordinate basis.
Otherwise let \(T=\max(1,\max_{ij}|A_{ij}|)\), \(J=(rT)^r\) and \(K=2^s(r+1)J+1\).
Choose \(r\) independent rows and \(r\) pivot columns with invertible intersection.
For each free column set its coefficient to the pivot determinant, all other free coefficients to zero, and solve for the pivot coefficients by Cramer's rule.
These \(h\) independent kernel vectors have norm at most \(\sqrt{r+1}J\) by Hadamard's inequality.
At least one of them has last coordinate index \(i\ge h\) in the reduced graph basis, because they are independent.
The preceding Gram–Schmidt estimate then bounds each of the first \(h\) graph basis lengths by \(2^{(s-1)/2}\sqrt{r+1}J<K\).
Every such vector has zero in its graph component \(KAz\).
They span the real kernel by dimension, and form a basis of the full integer kernel because they are an initial segment of a lattice basis.
Their first \(s\) coordinates inherit the same reduction conditions.

The classical reduction input is §1 of [Lenstra, Lenstra and Lovász](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454).
The [reduced-basis estimate and complexity bound](../audits/literature/LLL82/Yin_U001.md#reduction) use conditions (1.4)–(1.5), inequality (1.7), Proposition (1.26) and Remark (1.38) of that paper.
The graph basis has integral Gram matrix, and \(\log K\) is polynomial in the dimensions and bit length of \(A\).
The polynomial-time claim concerns the explicit integer matrix and basis reduction; the graph and candidates have polynomial bit size in that input.
The remainder columns themselves may have size growing with the degree cap as a number even when the exponents have short binary encodings.
For example with \(L=\max(2,1+\|P\|_1)\), multiplication by \(x\bmod P\) has operator \(1\)-norm at most \(L\), and the columns at \(e\le N\) have at most \(O(1+N\log L)\) bits per entry.
Successive applications of this map give a polynomial bound in the numerical cap, degree, and coefficient bit length.
No polynomial bound in \(\log p\) alone is used.

Consequently \(\log H_0\) estimates \(m(P)+C\) with lower error at most \((s-2)\log 2+\frac{h+1}{2}\log 2+\frac12\log s\) and upper error at most \(\log s\), in the sense of (1)–(2).
For the cap \(p(b+2)\) of the [fixed-input rate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement), \(s\le2(b+3)\).
Thus on any nonempty sequence with \(b=o(p)\) the difference \((m(P)+C-\log H_0)/p\) tends to zero uniformly in the original input and the two residues.
This reduces the moving-input lower-rate question to growth of an explicit lattice quantity, without proving that growth for arbitrary varying inputs.

## Direct lower estimates from the last pair of coefficients

Let \(H\) be the height of a primitive witness and \(g\) the smaller cyclic distance of its two residues.
Successive occupied exponents differ by at least \(g\).
For an exterior conjugate \(\gamma\) of \(\alpha\) with \(\rho=|\gamma|>1\), dominance of its largest exponent gives \(H\ge\rho^g/(s-1)\).
For moving Salem inputs, \(m(P)=\log\rho\) at the positive exterior root.
Hence when \(g/p\to1/2,\ s=o(p)\) and \(m(P)\) stays bounded, (1)'s sparse coefficient bound yields \(C/p\ge m(P)/2-o(1)\) with no fixed-input threshold.

For any offset, a monomial shift represents the two classes by exponents \(pi\) and \(pi+g\), allowing finitely many negative \(i\).
Let \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\) be the coefficients at the greatest occupied row index \(i\).
Dividing the equation \(F(\gamma)=0\) by \(\gamma^{pi+g}\) bounds the remaining at most \(s\) terms by
\[
                            |a\gamma^{-g}+b|\le sH\rho^{-p}.             \tag{3}
\]
The same integers \(a,b\) work for every exterior conjugate.
If \(\gamma\) is real and a reduced fraction \(u/q\), \(q\ge2\), satisfies
\[
       |\gamma^{-g}-u/q|\le q^{-2},\qquad sq^2\le\rho^p ,
\]
then \(H\ge q/2\).
Indeed if \(H<q/2\), the integer \(au+bq\) is nonzero, since its vanishing would force \(q\mid a\) and then \(a=b=0\).
The change from \(au/q+b\) to \(a\gamma^{-g}+b\) is less than \(1/(2q)\), contradicting (3).
A fraction can be supplied and verified directly, for example from a continued fraction.
For bounded-measure moving Salem inputs, a denominator with \(\log q\ge(p/2)\log\rho-o(p)\) satisfying this test proves \(C/p\ge m(P)/2-o(1)\) for the chosen offset.
The hypotheses can fail if useful approximation denominators jump over the required scale.

For two exterior conjugates put \(y_j=\gamma_j^{-g}\) and \(\rho_j=|\gamma_j|\).
Subtracting their copies of (3) gives
\[
 H\ge \frac{|y_1-y_2|}{s(\rho_1^{-p}+\rho_2^{-p})}.
\]
If \(a=0\), the same inequality follows from \(sH\rho_j^{-p}\ge|b|\ge1\) and \(|y_1-y_2|\le2\).
In particular a nonreal exterior pair gives \(H\ge\rho^p|\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{-g}|/s\).
When this is the only exterior pair, \(m(P)=2\log\rho\), so a subexponential loss in the imaginary part gives the same full lower rate for bounded-measure inputs with \(s=o(p)\).
These certificates apply also to reciprocal witnesses.

## A fixed subplastic measure with different feasibility for the two minima

Let \(P_0\) be any fixed nontorsion reciprocal unit minimal polynomial and let \(\eta\) be a root.
For [Lehmer's degree-ten example](../claims/Yin_C_G0376_v2.md#irreducible-lehmer-radical-control), irreducibility after substitution is already known for every prime \(p>40\).
For all sufficiently large primes \(p\), \(P=P_0(x^p)\) is irreducible and \(m(P)=m(P_0)\).
Indeed if \(X^p-\eta\) is reducible over \(K=\mathbb Q(\eta)\), a root \(\gamma\) has degree \(1\le e<p\) over \(K\).
For \(v=\operatorname{Norm}_{K(\gamma)/K}(\gamma)\) one has \(v^p=\eta^e\).
Bézout's identity for \(p,e\) gives \(\eta=u^p\) with \(u\in K\).
Such a \(u\) is a unit of degree \(\le d_0=\deg P_0\) and has \(m(\minpoly u)=m(P_0)/p\), since \(\mathbb Q(u)=K\).
There are only finitely many algebraic integers of bounded degree and bounded measure, by the coefficient bounds on their minimal polynomials.
A fixed nontorsion \(u\) contributes at most one such exponent, proving eventual irreducibility by the tower law.
The measure identity follows by taking all \(p\)-th roots of each root of \(P_0\).

For \(0<r<p\), \(F=P_0(x^p)(1+x^r)\) has only the two displayed classes and has \(R=1\).
It fits a cap \(p(b+2)\) for every sufficiently large \(b\).
Conversely a representation at these classes with coprime rows would give \(\gamma^r=-U(\eta)/V(\eta)\in K\).
Since \(\gamma^p=\eta\) and \(\gcd(r,p)=1\), this forces \(\gamma\in K\), contradicting \([K(\gamma):K]=p\).
The same holds after translating the residues or using another nonzero difference.
Thus the unrestricted cost can vanish for arbitrarily large support primes even at a fixed positive subplastic measure, while the coprime family is empty for these inputs.
Their support differences have gcd divisible by \(p\), leaving the corresponding question for primitive support open.
