# The least hereditary exponent and the information lost in a single-prime tower

<a id="least"></a>
## The least exponent and a formula from the terminal supports

Let \(P\) be the primitive integer minimal polynomial of a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number, with positive leading coefficient.
Write \(r(N)\) for the number of rational irreducible factors of \(P(X^N)\).
These factors are simple and are always normalized to be primitive integral with positive leading coefficient.
Call \(A\) an endpoint if each factor \(B\) of \(P(X^A)\) is hereditarily irreducible, meaning that \(B(X^n)\) is irreducible for every \(n\ge1\).
The [bounded-length theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v390.md#statement) supplies the endpoint \(A=2R_L\) with \(L\) equal to the coefficient length of \(P\).
Fix any one endpoint \(A\).
There is a unique positive integer \(a(P)\) such that the endpoints are exactly its multiples.
If \(s(B)\) is the gcd of the positive exponents in the support of \(B\), then
\[
              a(P)=\frac{A}{\gcd\bigl(A,\{s(B):B\mid P(X^A)
                                 \text{ irreducible}\}\bigr)}.          \tag{1}
\]

The count cannot decrease on passing from \(N\) to a multiple \(Nn\), since distinct factors still have disjoint root sets after substitution.
At \(AN\) it equals \(r(A)\) by heredity, so \(r(A)\) is the global maximum.
Every endpoint has this maximum by comparing a common multiple with \(A\).
Conversely \(r(N)=r(A)\) forces every factor at \(N\) to stay irreducible after every substitution, since one split would give a strictly larger count at a multiple.
The [coprime pullback formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v390.md#gcd) gives \(r(N)=r(\gcd(N,A))\).
Consequently the gcd of any two endpoints is an endpoint, and all multiples of an endpoint are endpoints.
The endpoints among the finitely many divisors of \(A\) have a gcd which is an endpoint and divides every endpoint by the same pullback formula.
This gives \(a(P)\).

For \(k\mid A\), the level \(A/k\) is an endpoint exactly when every terminal \(B\) is a polynomial in \(X^k\).
Indeed heredity at \(A/k\) gives \(B(X)=H(X^k)\) for the normalized factors \(H\) there.
Conversely if \(B(X)=H_B(X^k)\) for every \(B\), injectivity of substitution gives \(P(Y^{A/k})=\prod_B H_B(Y)\).
The \(H_B\) are distinct primitive polynomials with positive leading coefficient.
A proper factorization of \(H_B(Y^n)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) would remain proper after \(Y=X^k\), contradicting heredity of \(B\).
Thus \(A/k\) is an endpoint, and the largest admissible \(k\) is the gcd in (1).
For a primitive product of distinct irreducible seeds with positive multiplicities and nonzero nontorsion roots, the endpoints are the common endpoints of its seeds.
Their least element is the lcm of the individual least exponents, since factors originating at distinct seeds have disjoint roots, and substitution preserves their original multiplicities.

<a id="projection"></a>
## Recovering all divisor factorizations and the minimum individual measure

One rational factorization of \(P(X^A)\) suffices to recover the factors at every \(d\mid A\) without a further rational factorization.
Put \(k=A/d\), and for each terminal factor \(B\) form
\[
                T_{B,k}(Y)=\operatorname{Res}_X(B(X),Y-X^k).
\]
Divide \(T_{B,k}\) by its polynomial gcd with its derivative over \(\mathbb Q\), then choose the primitive integer representative with positive leading coefficient.
The resulting polynomials, after duplicates are removed, are precisely all the factors of \(P(Y^d)\).

Indeed write \(b>0\) for the leading coefficient of \(B\) and let \(\beta\) be a root.
All \(\beta^k\) from the roots of \(B\) form one conjugacy orbit.
Every member is reached exactly \(e=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q(\beta^k)]\) times, by extension of embeddings.
If \(H\) is the normalized minimal polynomial of \(\beta^k\) with leading coefficient \(h\), the root formula for the resultant gives
\[
                     T_{B,k}(Y)=b^k\bigl(H(Y)/h\bigr)^e.                \tag{2}
\]
This accounts for both the repetitions and the leading coefficients in the projection.
In fact \(T_{B,k}=H^e\) in the primitive normalization.
For every prime \(\ell\), the reduction of \(B\) is a nonzero polynomial and every root of \(X^k-Y\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell(Y)\) is transcendental over \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
Using the product formula in the roots of the monic \(X^k-Y\) shows that the reduction of the resultant is nonzero, even if the degree of \(B\) drops or \(X^k-Y\) has repeated roots there.
Thus \(T_{B,k}\) is primitive, and (2) with its positive leading coefficient gives \(b^k=h^e\).
Every root of \(P(Y^d)\) has a \(k\)-th root at level \(A\), so every old factor occurs.
Its multiplicity at level \(d\) is one, even when different terminal factors yield the same projection.
For an arbitrary \(N\), take \(d=\gcd(N,a(P))\) and substitute \(X^{N/d}\) in each distinct projected factor at \(d\).
The coprime pullback formula with endpoint \(a(P)\) gives the complete normalized factorization.

If \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure, the exact least measure of any radical of a fixed root of \(P\) is
\[
         \min_{N\ge1,\ \beta^N=\alpha}m(\beta)
              = \frac{\min_{B\mid P(X^A)}\deg B}{A\deg P}\,m(P),        \tag{3}
\]
where \(B\) ranges over the irreducible factors.
In fact the [height identity for an arbitrary radical](../claims/Yin_C_G0206_v1.md#statement) gives \(m(H)=\deg H\,m(P)/(d\deg P)\) for each factor \(H\) at \(d\), also for nonmonic seeds.
Every terminal factor has a root above the fixed \(\alpha\) by extension of embeddings.
The [minimum-at-an-endpoint argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v390.md#gcd) then gives (3).
The same divisor calculation lists the full set of individual measures at every order, with one entry for each distinct factor.

<a id="mixed"></a>
## Which splittings detect each prime exponent

For a fixed prime \(p\), the exponent \(v_p(a(P))\) is the largest \(j\ge1\) for which there exists \(u\ge1\), \(p\nmid u\), such that
\[
                         r(up^j)>r(up^{j-1}),                           \tag{4}
\]
with value zero for an empty set.
Beyond \(v_p(a(P))\) both arguments have the same gcd with the endpoint \(a(P)\), so their counts agree.
If \(p^t\Vert a(P)\) with \(t>0\), take \(u=a(P)/p^t\).
Then the upper count at \(j=t\) is maximal and the lower one is strictly smaller because \(a(P)/p\) is no endpoint.
Thus a mixed comparison always detects the exact exponent.

Pure \(p\)-power comparisons alone can miss it entirely.
For \(P=X+27\) they miss the prime two.
For any root \(\beta^{2^j}=-27\), taking the norm to \(\mathbb Q\) and its \(3\)-adic valuation gives \(2^j\mid 3[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\).
Thus every \(P(X^{2^j})\) is irreducible.
On the other hand
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P(X^3)&=(X+3)(X^2-3X+9),\\
 P(X^6)&=(X^2+3)(X^2+3X+3)(X^2-3X+3).
\end{aligned}
\]
The quadratic at level three has negative discriminant and all three factors at level six stay Eisenstein at three after every power substitution.
Thus six is an endpoint, while the counts at \(1,2,3\) are \(1,1,2\), respectively, below the terminal count three.
It follows that \(a(P)=6\).

<a id="arbitrary"></a>
## Arbitrarily many undetected levels at any prescribed prime

For every prime \(p\) and \(t\ge1\) there exists a monic irreducible integer polynomial \(P\) with a nonzero nontorsion root such that every \(P(X^{p^j})\) is irreducible and \(p^t\mid a(P)\).
Write \(h=p^t\).
Choose a prime \(q\mid 1+p^{h/p}+\cdots+p^{(p-1)h/p}\).
The sum is odd and congruent to one modulo \(p\), so \(q\nmid4p\).
The order of \(p\) modulo \(q\) divides \(h\), and cannot divide \(h/p\), since then the displayed sum would be congruent to \(p\) modulo \(q\).
Consequently this order is \(h\) and \(h\mid q-1\).

Take \(\theta=\zeta_{4h}\) primitive, \(K=\mathbb Q(\theta)\), and a primitive \(q\)-th root \(\xi\).
The compositum \(K(\xi)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{4hq})\) has degree \(q-1\) over \(K\) by the [cyclotomic degree formula](../audits/literature/Bel17/Yin_U002.md#bel17-u002).
Its Galois group over \(K\) acts on \(\xi\) by power maps, so it is the full cyclic group \(\mathbb F_q^\times\).
Let \(E/K\) be its cyclic subextension of degree \(h\), and \(\sigma\) a generator of \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\).
As a linear map on \(E\) over \(K\), \(\sigma\) has order exactly \(h\) and is diagonalizable because \(X^h-1\) splits with distinct roots in \(K\).
At least one eigenvalue has order exactly \(h\), since all smaller orders divide \(h/p\).
Choose a corresponding nonzero eigenvector \(\gamma\), multiplying by a positive integer to make it integral.
Then \(c=\gamma^h\in K\) is not a \(p\)-th power in \(K\).
Indeed if \(b^p=c\) in \(K\), then \(\gamma^{h/p}/b\in\mu_p\subset K\), whereas \(\gamma^{h/p}\) has nontrivial eigenvalue of order \(p\).

For an integer \(M\) put \(b=c(M+\theta)^h\) and \(\alpha=b^q\).
For every sufficiently large \(M\), \(\alpha\) is nontorsion and generates \(K\).
Its modulus grows without bound at a fixed complex embedding.
For any two distinct embeddings \(\rho,\tau\) of \(K\), the polynomials \(\rho(c)^q(Y+\rho(\theta))^{hq}\) and \(\tau(c)^q(Y+\tau(\theta))^{hq}\) have different roots, so their values differ for all but finitely many integers \(M\).
Choose such an \(M\) for all pairs.
The class of \(b\) in \(K^\times/K^{\times p}\) is nontrivial, and so is the class of \(\alpha\) because \(p\nmid q\).
At two, \(i\in K\) also makes \(-\alpha\) a nonsquare.
The [prime binomial and norm induction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#substitutions) now proves \(X^{p^j}-\alpha\) irreducible over \(K\) for every \(j\).
Since \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), the monic integer minimal polynomial \(P\) of the algebraic integer \(\alpha\) has \(r(p^j)=1\) for every \(j\).

The \(q\)-th root \(z=b\xi\) of \(\alpha\) generates \(K(\xi)\), since its \(q\)-th power generates \(K\) and \(\xi=z/b\).
In this same field an \(h\)-th root of \(z\) is the product of \(\gamma(M+\theta)\) and the \(h\)-th root of \(\xi\) in \(\mu_q\), which exists because \(\gcd(h,q)=1\).
All powers along the resulting chain of \(t\) successive \(p\)-th root extractions from \(z\) belong to \(K(\xi)\) and still generate it, since their suitable powers are \(z\).
Each extraction therefore exhibits a split at its next level, while distinct factors at the preceding level remain disjoint.
It follows that \(r(qp^j)>r(qp^{j-1})\) for every \(1\le j\le t\).
Equation (4) proves \(v_p(a(P))\ge t\).
The seeds in this construction vary with \(p,t\), consistently with the length-dependent bound on a hereditary exponent.
