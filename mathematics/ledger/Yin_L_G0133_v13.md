Let d>=1, epsilon in {+1,-1}, and
\[
 p(z)=3+\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_j(z^j+z^{-j}),\qquad
 p_j\in\mathbb Z,\quad p_d=\varepsilon,\quad p>0\text{ on }\mathbb T.
 \tag{1}
\]
Then P=epsilon z^d p is monic reciprocal of degree 2d, has integer coefficients and P(0)=1.
Irreducibility is imposed only when returning to the original input below.
Assume an actual identity
\[
 2p=\sum_{j=1}^{r}v_jv_j^\star,\qquad
 0\ne v_j\in\mathbb Z[z],\qquad \deg v_j\le d.
 \tag{2}
\]
The involution is v^star(z)=v(z^{-1}), so this identity is a sum of squared absolute values on the circle.
Zero rows have been deleted.
The number r counts actual nonzero square summands, not an unspecified Gram rank.
No certificate is inferred merely from (1).

Put
\[
 A=z^d+\varepsilon,\qquad S_k=z^k+z^{-k},\qquad
 B_{d,\varepsilon}=3+\varepsilon S_d.
 \tag{3}
\]
All measures below are total Mahler measures of the original P.

<a id="central-three-four-certificate-forms"></a>
## Four complete certificate forms

Permuting rows, changing a row's overall sign, and replacing an individual row v(z) by z^d v(z^{-1}) preserve its circle norm, integrality and degree-at-most-d support.
Only these explicitly specified operations are used.
In particular no general rational or integral change of row basis is treated as an isometry.

Every certificate (2), up to those operations, has one of the following forms.

| Nonzero certificate rows | Integer parameters | Resulting p |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, A, z^k, z^l | 0<=k,l<=d | B_(d,epsilon) |
| A+sigma z^h, A, z^l | d=2h, h>=1, 0<=l<=d, sigma=+/-1 | B_(d,epsilon)+sigma(1+epsilon)S_h/2 |
| A+sigma z^a, A+tau z^a | 1<=a<d, sigma,tau=+/-1 | B_(d,epsilon) if tau=-sigma; abs(A+sigma z^a)^2 if tau=sigma |
| 2+epsilon z^d, z^l | 0<=l<=d | B_(d,epsilon) |

Conversely every displayed row tuple gives exactly the stated integer symbol and has total coefficient energy six and highest lag coefficient 2epsilon.
The first, second and fourth forms are strictly positive.
In the third form, the equal-sign case belongs to the present strict-positive domain precisely when A+sigma z^a has no circle zero; the opposite-sign case is always strictly positive.
Thus the table, with this explicit positivity requirement, is necessary and sufficient for actual certificates under (1).
In particular r is one of 2, 3 or 4.

Equivalently, for a given strictly positive p satisfying (1), a certificate (2) exists if and only if p belongs to at least one of these three symbol families:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &B_{d,\varepsilon}=3+\varepsilon S_d,\\
 &|z^d+\varepsilon+\sigma z^a|^2,
       &&1\le a<d,\quad \sigma\in\{+1,-1\},\\
 &H_{h,\sigma}=3+S_{2h}+\sigma S_h,
       &&d=2h,\quad \varepsilon=+1,\quad \sigma\in\{+1,-1\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
The middle expression denotes the exact Laurent product, not an auxiliary numerical approximation.
The families can overlap; in particular |z^(2h)-1+sigma z^h|^2=B_(2h,-1).
They are not asserted to exhaust all positive symbols with central coefficient three.

<a id="central-three-endpoint-energy-exhaustion"></a>
## Endpoint energy leaves only two integral configurations

Write the coefficient vector of all rows as
\[
 v(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_kz^k,\qquad a_k\in\mathbb Z^r,
 \qquad x=a_0,\quad y=a_d.
\]
The constant and highest-lag coefficients of (2) give
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{d}\|a_k\|^2=6,\qquad x^Ty=2\varepsilon.
 \tag{5}
\]
For the row remainders u_j of v12, write u_0 for the vector of their constant coefficients; then t=y and u_0=x-epsilon y.
Together with the intermediate coefficients, its remainder identity gives
\[
 \sum_j\|\operatorname{coeff}(u_j)\|^2=2,\qquad
 \|t\|^2+\varepsilon t^Tu_0=2.
 \tag{6}
\]
The latter identity is exactly the highest-lag constraint in (5), not an additional existence assumption.
The endpoint proof below keeps every integer possibility while avoiding any enumeration over d or over supports.

For one row, a nonzero endpoint product x_j y_j of absolute value at least three requires endpoint coefficient energy at least eight.
This follows by considering integer endpoint magnitudes: if one is one and the other at least three, their square sum is at least ten; if both are at least two, it is at least eight.
Such a row is impossible under total energy six.
A product of absolute value two has endpoint magnitudes one and two and consumes energy five.
It leaves insufficient energy for any further nonzero endpoint product, so the product must be exactly 2epsilon.

If there is no such product, every nonzero endpoint product has absolute value one and consumes energy two.
There are at most three of them.
Their signed sum must equal 2epsilon, so there are exactly two, both equal epsilon; three signed units have odd sum.
All additional endpoint coefficients have zero product in their own rows.

Consequently there are precisely two cases.
In the first, one row has endpoint magnitudes one and two, and total energy one remains.
After a sign change and, if necessary, a degree-d reversal, its endpoint part is 2+epsilon z^d.
In the second, two rows have endpoint part A after sign changes, and total energy two remains.
In the second case any additional endpoint coefficient lies in a separate row, because both endpoints of each A row are already occupied.
The residual energy consists of one or two distinct coefficients of magnitude one.
A coefficient of magnitude two would already consume four units.

<a id="central-three-all-lag-parity-proof"></a>
## Every lower-lag parity condition is retained

Every coefficient of the Laurent polynomial 2p is even.
This simple condition finishes the endpoint classification.

First consider the endpoint row with magnitudes one and two.
Its remaining unit coefficient cannot occupy an intermediate position a in the same row.
If a differs from d-a, its two cross contributions occur at those distinct lags, with one odd and one even coefficient.
If a=d-a, their combined coefficient is still odd, since the endpoint coefficients have opposite parities.
There is no other residual contribution available to cancel this parity.
The remaining unit coefficient therefore forms a separate monomial row.
This is the fourth form in the table.

Now consider the two endpoint rows A, with two unit coefficients left.
If the units lie in the same new row, they cannot occupy both endpoints, because all nonzero endpoint products have already been exhausted.
Their cross term therefore produces one odd coefficient at a positive lag less than d.
If they lie at two distinct internal positions a,b in one A row, the positive lower-lag cross terms occur at
\[
 a,\quad d-a,\quad b,\quad d-b,\quad |a-b|.
 \tag{7}
\]
Each contribution has coefficient +1 or -1.
There are five contributions with multiplicity.
Even if some frequencies coincide, their total sum is odd, so the resulting coefficient at some positive lag remains odd.
The other A row contributes no lower-lag term.
Both possibilities contradict the even coefficients of 2p.
Hence the two residual units lie in different rows.

If neither unit lies in an A row, they are separate monomial rows, giving the first form.
Their exponents can be any integers from zero through d.

If exactly one unit lies in an A row, that row is A+sigma z^a with 0<a<d.
Its added Laurent cross term is
\[
 \sigma L_a,\qquad
 L_a=\varepsilon S_a+S_{d-a}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The other unit is in a separate monomial row and contributes no cross term.
If a differs from d-a, (8) has odd coefficients at the two distinct lags, which is impossible.
Thus d=2h and a=h.
Then L_h=(1+epsilon)S_h, giving exactly the second form in the table.
For epsilon=-1 this term vanishes; for epsilon=+1 its coefficient in p is sigma.

Finally suppose each A row receives one unit, initially at positions a,b strictly between zero and d.
After normalizing endpoint signs, the rows are A+sigma z^a and A+tau z^b.
Modulo two, a nonsymmetric position a contributes precisely the two distinct positions a,d-a; a midpoint contributes zero.
Thus every lower coefficient can be even only if
\[
 b=a\quad\hbox{or}\quad b=d-a.
 \tag{9}
\]
For example a single midpoint and a nonmidpoint cannot cancel, and two distinct midpoint positions do not exist.
When b=d-a, reverse the second row and multiply it by epsilon:
\[
 \varepsilon z^d
     \bigl(A(z^{-1})+\tau z^{-b}\bigr)
       =A+\varepsilon\tau z^a.
 \tag{10}
\]
This permitted operation reduces the pair to the equal-position third form.
Opposite unit signs then cancel the two cross terms, giving B_(d,epsilon); equal signs give the doubled norm of one trinomial.
These are exact Laurent identities, so every lag equation, not only its parity, is satisfied by each listed normal form.
This proves exhaustiveness and necessity of the table.

For sufficiency, use |A|^2=2+epsilon S_d and expand each displayed tuple.
In the three-row case,
\[
 \frac12\bigl(|A+\sigma z^h|^2+|A|^2+1\bigr)
   =B_{d,\varepsilon}
       +\frac{\sigma(1+\varepsilon)}2S_h.
 \tag{11}
\]
The other rows give the stated formulas directly.
The base symbol satisfies B>=1 on the circle.
For the nonbase three-row symbol, putting c=cos(h theta) gives
\[
 H_{h,\sigma}=4c^2+2\sigma c+1
             =4(c+\sigma/4)^2+3/4>0.
 \tag{12}
\]
The trinomial norm is strictly positive exactly under the circle-zero exclusion already stated.
No further lower-lag equations or support assumptions remain hidden.

<a id="central-three-two-row-and-minimum-count"></a>
## Two-row certificates and exact numbers of summands

A two-row certificate is therefore, up to the stated operations, either
\[
 (2+\varepsilon z^d,\ z^l)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 (A+\sigma z^a,\ A+\tau z^a),
 \tag{13}
\]
with the parameter ranges in the table.
It gives B or a single trinomial norm.
No one-row certificate exists under (1)--(2), even before imposing irreducibility, by the exhaustive endpoint argument.

The base B always has two- and four-row certificates, respectively the fourth and first forms.
It has a three-row certificate if and only if epsilon=-1 and d is even.
Indeed the second form is the only three-row possibility, and its added term vanishes precisely in that case.
For d=2h and epsilon=-1 the original polynomial is already reducible:
\[
 z^{4h}-3z^{2h}+1
   =(z^{2h}+z^h-1)(z^{2h}-z^h-1).
 \tag{14}
\]

The symbol H_(h,sigma) has minimum three rows and in fact every certificate in the degree window has three rows.
It cannot be a base symbol.
A nonbase trinomial norm has two distinct intermediate support positions with coefficients of magnitude one, or one midpoint coefficient of magnitude two; it cannot equal H, whose only intermediate coefficient has magnitude one at the midpoint.
The table therefore excludes its two- and four-row cases.
The explicit three-row certificate is
\[
 2H_{h,\sigma}
  =|1+\sigma z^h+z^{2h}|^2+|1+z^{2h}|^2+1.
 \tag{15}
\]
Likewise a trinomial-norm symbol outside B has only two-row certificates in this degree window.
These are classifications of actual degree-at-most-d rows.
No general support-compression theorem for wider Laurent certificates is inferred.
The separate [v11 endpoint argument](Yin_L_G0133_v11.md#quartic-three-squares-any-width) already gives the arbitrary-width minimum for the original quartic.

<a id="central-three-irreducible-original-return"></a>
## Irreducibility leaves two exact original-input families

Now impose irreducibility of the original P over Q.
If p=|f|^2 with f=z^d+epsilon+sigma z^a, define the degree-d polynomial reversal f^*(z)=z^d f(z^{-1}).
Then
\[
 P=\varepsilon z^dp=\varepsilon f f^*.
 \tag{16}
\]
Both integer factors have degree d>=1 and nonzero constant coefficients, so (16) contradicts irreducibility.
Thus the exact possible original polynomials are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(z)&=z^{2d}+3\varepsilon z^d+1,
       &&d\ge1,\quad\varepsilon=\pm1;\\
 P(z)&=z^{4h}+\sigma z^{3h}+3z^{2h}+\sigma z^h+1,
       &&h\ge1,\quad\sigma=\pm1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{17}
\]
Only the members that are actually irreducible belong to this original-input domain.
No classification of all substitution exponents preserving irreducibility is needed or asserted.
Conversely each irreducible member of (17) has the required strictly positive central-three symbol and an explicit degree-bounded certificate above.
In particular two-row certificates in the irreducible domain force the first family.
The second family has exactly three rows in every such certificate.

The total measures of these actual original polynomials have only two possible values:
\[
 \lambda=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
 \mu_4=\frac{1+\sqrt{21}+\sqrt{6+2\sqrt{21}}}{4}.
 \tag{18}
\]
For the first family, the base quadratic z^2+3epsilon z+1 has reciprocal root moduli lambda and lambda^(-1), so its measure is lambda.
For the second family, [C480, Section 6, equation (18)](../claims/Yin_C_G0480_v1.md#complete-sextic-leading-root-cost) already proves the exact measure mu_4 for (z^2-1)(z^4+z^3+3z^2+z+1).
The factor z^2-1 has measure one, so the same exact value belongs to P_4=z^4+z^3+3z^2+z+1.
The sign-minus quartic is P_4(-z), and changing the circle argument by pi preserves its measure.

For every nonzero polynomial R and positive integer h, the exact Haar substitution gives
\[
 m(R(z^h))
  =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|R(e^{ih\theta})|\,d\theta
  =m(R).
 \tag{19}
\]
This is an equality for the actual finite substitution, not a delay limit or a measure divided by h.
It proves that all degrees and substitutions in (17) retain the values in (18).

The already retained quartic calculation also gives
\[
 \mu_4+\mu_4^{-1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{21}}2.
\]
Since 4<sqrt(21)<5, this last value lies strictly between 5/2 and 3.
The function x+x^(-1) is strictly increasing for x>1, while 2+1/2=5/2 and lambda+lambda^(-1)=3.
Therefore
\[
 2<\mu_4<\lambda.
 \tag{20}
\]
Combining (17)--(20) yields the exact, attained lower bound on the supplied-certificate irreducible branch:
\[
 \boxed{M(P)\ge\mu_4>2.}
 \tag{21}
\]
The original P_4, with h=1 and sigma=+1, is irreducible by the [adopted C868 v6 quartic proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v6.md#doubled-quartic-minimum-rank) and has the certificate (15), so equality at mu_4 is achieved.
If the supplied certificate has two rows, its measure is exactly lambda.

<a id="central-three-certificate-coverage-boundary"></a>
## What this classifies and what remains unsupplied

The new information is the complete finite row classification of an actual central-three doubled integer certificate, together with its exact original irreducible return.
It is stronger coverage information than applying the general reflection gap to this branch.
For example any irreducible central-three input with M(P)<mu_4, if one exists, cannot have the stated degree-bounded doubled certificate.
No such new counterexample is supplied here.

Strict positivity and central coefficient three alone are not assumed to imply a certificate.
The equality and quotient conditions of v12 remain necessary conditions for all such inputs, whereas the four row forms classify only those satisfying (2).
The proof does not decide whether the quotient Q from v12 is generally cyclotomic, does not classify the entire central-three positive domain, and does not address growing central coefficients or unrestricted Laurent support.
It supplies neither a universal certificate mechanism nor a new all-input strategy for G0001.
Further numerical searches or optimization within these already fixed two measure values are unnecessary for this coverage diagnosis.
