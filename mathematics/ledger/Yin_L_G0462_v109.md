# Prime orders on both sides of a Salem periodic-point plateau

<a id="neighbors"></a>
## Prime divisors at offsets up to two

Let \(J\) be a sufficiently large multiple of \(12600\) and put \(d=J+2\).
Consider the two [Salem minimal polynomials](Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#plateau)
\[
 P_J=\frac{z^J f+f^*}{z+1},\qquad Q_J=\frac{z^Jg-g^*}{z-1},
 \qquad f=z^3-z-1,\qquad g=z^3-2z^2-z+1 ,
\]
where \(u^*=z^3u(1/z)\).
Both have [common modulus one](Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#periods) at every odd prime dividing \(J\).
On 2026-11-17 agent-S investigated which nearby exponents can give an exponentially large common modulus for Yin's [Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).

For each monic reciprocal polynomial \(H\) of even degree write \(H(z)=z^{\deg(H)/2}T_H(z+z^{-1})\) for its trace representation.
For an odd prime \(p\), write \(S_p\) for the monic minimal polynomial of \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) with \(\zeta\) a primitive \(p\)-th root.
Use the [trace resultant and largest common integer modulus](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement)
\[
 B_p(H)=|\operatorname{Res}(T_H,S_p)|,\qquad
 G_p(H)=\max_{a\in\mathbb Z}\gcd(T_H(a),S_p(a)).
\]
Put \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), and let \(L_p=\phi^p+(-\phi^{-1})^p\) be the Lucas number.
For \(c=3,4,5\), write \(H_c=z^2((z+z^{-1})^2-(z+z^{-1})-c)\).
The entries below give the pair \((B_p,G_p)\).
An entry \(H_c\) means precisely \((B_p(H_c),G_p(H_c))\).

| Order condition | \(P_J\) | \(Q_J\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(p\mid J-2\) | \(H_3\) | \(H_5\) |
| \(p\mid J-1\) | \(H_4\) | \((L_p,L_p)\) |
| \(p\mid J\) | \((1,1)\) | \((1,1)\) |
| \(p\mid J+1\) | \((1,1)\) | \((p,p)\) |
| \(p\mid J+2\) | \((1,1)\) | \((L_p,L_p)\) |

At \(p\mid J-1\) the integer \(3\) realizes \(G_p(Q_J)\).
At \(p\mid J+1\) the integer \(2\) realizes it.
The two Lucas entries give the exact rate
\[
 \frac{\log G_p(Q_J)}p=\log\phi+\frac{\log(1-\phi^{-2p})}p .
\]
In particular \(G_p(Q_J)\ge(1-\phi^{-6})\phi^p\) for either entry, independently of \(p/d\).

## Reduction in the real cyclotomic order

Set \(t=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), \(A=\mathbb Z[t]=\mathbb Z[X]/(S_p)\), \(k=(p+1)/2\) and \(u=\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k}\).
The element \(u=\zeta^{-k}(1+\zeta)\) is a unit of \(A\).
Indeed \(N_{\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q}(1+\zeta)=\Phi_p(-1)=1\), so its real associate \(u\) has norm of absolute value one in \(\mathbb Q(t)\) and an integral polynomial in \(u\) gives its inverse.
For \(v,w\in A\), write \(v\doteq w\) when they generate the same ideal.
If \(T_H(t)\doteq V(t)\) for \(V\in\mathbb Z[X]\), their ideals lift to \((T_H,S_p)=(V,S_p)\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
Evaluating these ideals at any integer \(a\) gives identical positive gcds, and taking absolute norms in \(A\) gives identical absolute resultants.

If \(p\mid J-1\), then \(d/2\equiv 1+k\bmod p\).
The identities
\[
 zf+f^*=z^2\big((z+z^{-1})^2-(z+z^{-1})-4\big),\qquad
 zg-g^*=(z-1)(z+1)(z^2-3z+1)
\]
give \(T_{P_J}(t)=u^{-1}(t^2-t-4)\) and \(T_{Q_J}(t)=u(t-3)\).
This gives the asserted reduction to \(H_4\) for \(P_J\).
For \(Q_J\), \(B_p=|S_p(3)|\) and the ideal equality at \(a=3\) gives \(G_p=|S_p(3)|\).
The identity \(S_p(z+z^{-1})=z^{-(p-1)/2}(z^p-1)/(z-1)\) at \(z=\phi^2\) gives \(S_p(3)=\phi^p-\phi^{-p}=L_p\), since \(\phi-\phi^{-1}=1\) and \(p\) is odd.

If \(p\mid J+1\), one has \(d/2\equiv k\bmod p\).
The identities \(f+zf^*=-(z^4+1)\) and \(g-zg^*=-(z-1)^3(z+1)\) give
\[
 T_{P_J}(t)=-\zeta^{-k-1}\frac{\zeta^4+1}{\zeta+1},
 \qquad T_{Q_J}(t)=-u(t-2).
\]
The first value is a unit because the numerator and denominator of the fraction each have norm one and the full expression belongs to \(A\).
For the second value \(S_p(2)=p\) follows by evaluating the geometric sum at \(z=1\).

If \(p\mid J+2\), one has \(d/2\equiv0\bmod p\).
The factorizations
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  f+z^2 f^*&=-(z+1)(z^4-z^2+1),\\
  g-z^2 g^*&=-(z-1)(z^4-3z^2+1)
 \end{aligned}
\]
give \(T_{P_J}(t)=-(t^2-3)\) and \(T_{Q_J}(t)=-(t^2-5)\).
Here \(p>3\) since \(3\mid J\).
The factor \(z^4-z^2+1=(z^6+1)/(z^2+1)\) has unit value at \(\zeta\) because both powers \(\zeta^6,\zeta^2\) have order \(p\).
For \(Q_J\), the automorphism \(\sigma_2\) of \(A\) induced by \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2\) gives \(t^2-5=\sigma_2(t-3)\).
It follows that \(B_p=L_p\) and \(A/(t^2-5)\) is isomorphic as a unital ring to \(A/(t-3)=\mathbb Z/(L_p)\).
Explicitly define \(D_n\) by \(D_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\), so \(D_n\in\mathbb Z[X]\) by the two-term recurrence.
The map \(A\to\mathbb Z/(L_p)\) that first applies \(\sigma_2^{-1}\) and then evaluates at \(3\) sends \(t\) to the residue of \(D_k(3)\), since \(2k\equiv1\bmod p\).
Its kernel contains \(T_{Q_J}(t)\), so this one integer realizes \(G_p=L_p\).

Finally, at \(p\mid J-2\) the normalization gives \(d/2\equiv2\bmod p\).
Direct division of \(z^2 f+f^*\) by \(z+1\) and \(z^2g-g^*\) by \(z-1\) gives \(z^4-z^3-z^2-z+1\) and \(z^4-z^3-3z^2-z+1\), respectively.
After division by \(z^2\) these are precisely the traces of \(H_3,H_5\).
For \(p=3\) under the stated \(J\) hypothesis only the row \(p\mid J\) applies.
If one formally takes an even \(J\equiv1\bmod3\), the reductions for the two Lucas entries of \(Q_J\) also agree, since \(S_3=X+1\) gives absolute norm \(4=L_3\) for both \(t-3\) and \(t^2-5\).

## Growing prime orders within the degree

For the logarithmic-window examples \(J=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)\), every prime divisor of \(J-1\) exceeds \(y\).
Consequently \(Q_J\) has an exponential Lucas modulus at each such prime, all at most \(d-3\), while every odd prime through \(y\) gives modulus one.

More generally, for every sequence of these \(Q_J\) with \(J\to\infty\), one can choose \(p\le d\) tending to infinity at which \(G_p(Q_J)=L_p\).
Take the largest prime divisor of \((J-1)(J+2)\).
If it stayed bounded on a subsequence, both \(J-1\) and \(J+2\) would belong to the same finitely generated multiplicative group of rational numbers.
The equation \((J+2)/3-(J-1)/3=1\) has only finitely many solutions there by the [finite-rank unit equation theorem](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound), after adjoining \(3\) to the group and taking its square for the two variables.
Both entries are positive and neither summand is zero, so its nondegeneracy hypothesis holds.
This proves that the chosen prime tends to infinity.

There are also growing prime orders \(p\le d-3\) that recover a positive exponential rate for both polynomials of each pair.
Choose the largest prime divisor of \((J-1)(J-2)\) and apply the same finiteness theorem to \((J-1)-(J-2)=1\).
For \(c=3,4,5\), the trace \(X^2-X-c\) is irreducible and has roots \(t_c=(1+\sqrt{1+4c})/2>2\) and \(1-t_c\in(-2,2)\).
The quadratic lift is irreducible over the trace field since \(t_c^2-4\) is negative at the second real embedding and cannot be a square.
Thus \(H_c\) is a fixed Salem minimal polynomial with exterior root \(\lambda_c=(t_c+\sqrt{t_c^2-4})/2\).
The [fixed-base all-prime theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) gives \(\log G_p(H_c)/p\to(\log\lambda_c)/2\) for each of the three fixed inputs.
The table then gives a simultaneous lower limit of at least \(\min(\log\phi,(\log\lambda_3)/2)>0\) for \(\min(\log G_p(P_J),\log G_p(Q_J))/p\).
These rates use the order \(p\) as their denominator and allow \(p/d\) to approach zero.
Their proof uses the displayed fixed cubic seeds to obtain low-degree trace polynomials at the chosen orders.
Producing an exponential modulus at growing prime orders for arbitrary reciprocal units remains open.
