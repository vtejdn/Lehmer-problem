# Exact denominator search for six-term Pisot trace lifts

<a id="exclusion"></a>
## The first six-term numerators with constant at least 77

On 2026-11-16 the construction contributor searched for a [positive-constant Pisot input](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) on branch two for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
Let \(m=h+1\), \(1\le d<e\le h\), \(a,b\in\{-1,1\}\), and put
\[
 A(z)=2+az^d+bz^e,\qquad \Pi(z)=A(z)-z^{2m}A(1/z).
\]
Then
\[
 C(x)=2U_h(x/2)+aU_{h-d}(x/2)+bU_{h-e}(x/2),\qquad
 \Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1}).
\]
The [exact parameter and coefficient enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0163/readme.md#six-term-search) excludes every \(q\ge77\) in this family through \(h=26\).
The necessary endpoint divisibility requires \(u\mid C(2)\), \(u>0\), \(q=1+C(2)/u\), with \(C(2)=2m+a(m-d)+b(m-e)\).
For \(h\le26\) the maximum \(4m-3\) is below \(152\), so only \(u=1\) can qualify.
There are no cases below \(h=19\).
The 737 qualifying parameter choices give 318,483 positive prefixes through the center, of which 10,456 satisfy both endpoint tests.
Every endpoint survivor fails a necessary moment test before index \(2h-1\).
This includes the first eleven mixed-sign choices, one at \(h=25\) and ten at \(h=26\).
The search uses root simplicity and interiority only as necessary conditions on a successful inverse input.

## Necessary positive moments and an exact terminal test

For a possible trace denominator \(B\), let \(B^\sharp=z^m B(z+z^{-1})=\sum_{k=0}^{2m} b_k z^k\), with \(b_0=1\) and \(b_k=b_{2m-k}\).
Put \(J_q=2B^\sharp/\Pi=\sum_{k\ge0}j_k z^k\), so \(j_0=1\).
Writing \(\pi_i=[z^i]\Pi\), expansion gives
\[
                     j_k=b_k-\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^k\pi_i j_{k-i}.
\]
The [signed measure of this series](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) has negative atom \(-1/[2(q-1)]\) at 1 and an atom of weight at least \(1/[2(q+1)]\) at \(-1\).
Adding \(c\delta_1-t\delta_{-1}\), \(c=1/[2(q-1)],\ t=1/[2(q+1)]\), produces a positive measure with mass \(s_0=1+c-t\) and moments \(s_k=j_k/2+c-(-1)^k t\) for \(k\ge1\).
Every successful inverse puts positive mass on all \(2h\) distinct circle nodes above the trace roots.
The Toeplitz matrices \((s_{|i-j|})_{0\le i,j\le k}\) must therefore be positive definite through \(k=2h-1\).

To enumerate the free integers \(b_k\), scale all moments by
\[
           S=2^{2h+1}\operatorname{lcm}(2(q-1),2(q+1)).
\]
The recurrence makes \(2^k j_k\) integral, hence \(Ss_k\) and \(Sj_k\) are integers at all tested indices.
For each prefix the [Toeplitz Schur complement](Yin_L_G0462_v53.md#recursion) gives the strict interval \(-D_{n+1}<X<D_{n+1}\) at \(k=n+1\), where \(D_i\) is the leading \(i\)-row determinant after scaling.
The coefficient of \(b_k\) in \(X\) is \(D_nS/2>0\).
Taking the complete integer interval gives a finite enumeration without a separate coefficient-height cutoff.
At the center both endpoints require
\[
 B(2)=b_m+2\sum_{i<m}b_i=-u,\qquad
 (-1)^h(q+1)B(-2)\le-\bigl(2m+a(-1)^d(m-d)+b(-1)^e(m-e)\bigr).
\]
Here \(B(-2)=b_m+2\sum_{i<m}(-1)^{m-i}b_i\), and the parentheses are \((-1)^h C(-2)>0\) by \(d,e>0\).
After the center there is just one candidate integer at each step by reciprocity.

A surviving branch can be checked directly over \(\mathbb Q\).
Take the signed remainder sequence starting with \(C\) and \((-B)\bmod C\).
Reject a nonconstant common divisor.
The sign variation at \(-2\) minus that at \(2\) equals the sum of the signs of \(-B(y)/C'(y)\) at simple interior roots when all poles are simple.
Indeed at a zero of an intermediate remainder its two neighbors have opposite signs, and at a simple zero of \(C\) the variation drops by exactly this sign.
Even in the presence of a multiple pole each distinct pole contributes at most one, as seen from the change of the signs of the first two sequence entries across it.
Thus a difference of \(h\) certifies that all \(h\) roots are simple and interior and every inverse residue \(-2B(y)/C'(y)\) is positive.
The endpoint inequality already distinguishes the strict and equality choices in the inverse formula.

## The degree-19 example and a positive control

For \((h,d,e,a,b)=(19,1,3,1,1)\), \(C(2)=76\) gives \(q=77,\ B(2)=-1\).
Of the 320 moment-positive prefixes through the center, just one passes both endpoint tests.
It gives \(B(x)=2T_{20}(x/2)-x-1\) with \(B(-2)=3\).
Its first reflected coefficient at index 21 fails the strict positive determinant test.
Independently the [exact rational interval check](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0163/readme.md#benchmark) finds a simple descending zero of \(C\) in \((11/10,23/20)\), with \(B<0\) throughout that interval.
The corresponding inverse residue is negative.
For \((19,1,2,1,1)\) the endpoint instead gives \(q=78\), and all nine endpoint survivors fail by index 27.

For a control that exercises the successful inverse, take \(h=2,\ d=1,\ e=2,\ a=b=1,\ q=10\).
The numerator is \(C=(2x-1)(x+1)\).
One returned denominator is \(B=x^3-x^2-3x+1\), with \(B(2)=-1,\ B(-2)=-5\).
At the roots \(-1,1/2\) the two inverse residues are \(4/3,5/12\), and \(2B(-2)/C(-2)=-2<-2/11\).
The strict inverse produces the Pisot minimal polynomial \(z^6-10z^5-2z^4-4z^3+3z^2+z+10\).
The threshold-\(77\) exclusion concerns the enumerated degrees only.
