## Original restricted scope and mathematical limits

An explicit irreducible reciprocal unit family has, at the fixed good prime 2, full original root mass in residue degrees of order d/log d, uniformly bounded order/degree ratio, and exactly controlled minimal valuation depths.
Nevertheless its maximum 2-primary cyclic-norm contribution over every fixed window n<=C d is o(d).
A full lower and upper Mahler comparison proves m(P) is of order d, so this is a general-input coordination obstruction outside the near-one domain; neither the other-prime contributions nor the full cyclic norm are bounded.
Genuine small-measure coordination and alternative arithmetic auxiliaries remain open.

## Fixed-prime high-degree orders need not coordinate at a linear exponent

Date: 2026-10-10.
The original starting question was arithmetic beyond low residue degrees.
The result below is an actual-input obstruction to one proposed bridge from efficient high residue orders to a common exponent.
It is not a near-one counterexample or a bound on the full cyclic norm.

<a id="fixed-prime-efficient-orders-obstruction"></a>
### 1. The changed test and complete statement

Write m(P)=log M(P), with natural logarithms and ordinary Mahler measure.
For a monic irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial P of actual degree d, put

\[
 C_n(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,t^n-1)|,
 \qquad A_2(n)=(\log2)v_2(C_n(P)).                 \tag{1}
\]

The [L213 full selected-depth theorem](Yin_L_G0213_v1.md#full-depth-fixed-scope) leaves high residue degrees outside its selected range.
A potentially useful bridge would say that, at a fixed good prime, full original root mass with residue order bounded by a fixed multiple of residue degree forces linear local income at some exponent n<=Cd.
This would coordinate the remaining root mass without paying its full common period.
That bridge is false on general reciprocal nontorsion units, even when all initial depths are exactly controlled.

More precisely, there are monic irreducible reciprocal nontorsion unit polynomials P_K, for every integer K>=2, of degrees d_K tending to infinity, with the following properties.

* The prime 2 is prime to the polynomial discriminant.
  Every irreducible reduction factor has a degree f and root order o satisfying o/f=15/4.
  All d_K original roots occur in these factors; there are no filler factors or uncounted multiplicities.
* All these residue degrees are comparable to d_K/K, and d_K is comparable to K 3^K.
  Thus their degrees grow on the scale d_K/log d_K.
* For every fixed real C>0,

\[
 \max_{1\le n\le Cd_K}\frac{A_2(n)}{d_K}
       =O_C\!\left(\frac{(\log K)^2}{K}\right)
       =O_C\!\left(\frac{(\log\log d_K)^2}{\log d_K}\right)
       \longrightarrow0.                              \tag{2}
\]

An empty range of integer n has maximum zero.
The proof gives an exact formula for every n, not just a first-depth upper bound.
On the other hand, for all sufficiently large K,

\[
 \frac{d_K}{48}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}4
       \le m(P_K)\le \frac{d_K}{2}\log2+\log19.          \tag{3}
\]

The lower bound is important to the interpretation: these controls have measure growing linearly with degree.
They do not test a hypothetical m(P)->0 sequence, and (2) does not bound contributions from primes other than 2.
In particular it does not assert that log C_n(P_K)=o(d_K).

### 2. High-degree factors of efficient binary order

For a,b>=1 set o=3^a5^b.
The elementary orders are

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{3^a}(2)=2\,3^{a-1},\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{5^b}(2)=4\,5^{b-1}.
\]

Indeed 2 has orders 2 and 4 modulo 3 and 5.
Starting from v_3(2^2-1)=1 and v_5(2^4-1)=1, the odd-prime binomial expansion gives respectively v_3(2^{2\cdot3^r}-1)=r+1 and v_5(2^{4\cdot5^r}-1)=r+1.
Multiplication of the exponent by an integer prime to the corresponding prime leaves that valuation unchanged, since the geometric-sum quotient reduces to a nonzero residue.
These exact valuations give the displayed minimal orders at every power.
Consequently

\[
 f:=\operatorname{ord}_o(2)=4\,3^{a-1}5^{b-1}
       =\frac4{15}o,
 \qquad \varphi(o)=2f.                                 \tag{4}
\]

Moreover -1 is not a power of 2 modulo o.
An exponent giving -1 modulo 3^a is congruent to 3^{a-1} modulo 2\,3^{a-1}, hence is odd.
An exponent giving -1 modulo 5^b is congruent to 2\,5^{b-1} modulo 4\,5^{b-1}, hence is even.
Here -1 is the unique order-two element modulo each odd prime power, which also follows by factoring x^2-1.
The two parity requirements cannot hold simultaneously.

Since o is odd, the reduction of Phi_o modulo 2 has distinct roots of exact order o.
Each Frobenius orbit has length f, so Phi_o modulo 2 has exactly two irreducible factors, both of degree f.
The preceding exclusion of -1 says that inversion exchanges, rather than preserves, those two factors.

There is a unique monic S_o(x) in Z[x] of degree f such that

\[
 \Phi_o(t)=t^f S_o(t+t^{-1}).                            \tag{5}
\]

For completeness, t^{-f}Phi_o(t) is a symmetric integral Laurent polynomial.
The symmetric terms t^j+t^{-j} are monic integral polynomials in x=t+t^{-1}, by the recurrence V_{j+1}=xV_j-V_{j-1}, V_0=2, V_1=x.
Their triangular leading terms give (5) without a division by two.

The reduction of S_o modulo 2 is irreducible of degree f.
To see this, let zeta be a primitive o-th root in the field with 2^f elements and set theta=zeta+zeta^{-1}.
If theta^{2^j}=theta, the quadratic equation with trace theta shows that zeta^{2^j} is zeta or zeta^{-1}.
The latter option is excluded by -1 not being a power of 2 modulo o.
The former holds exactly when f divides j.
Thus theta has degree f.
The trace polynomials for different o have disjoint reductions: equality of two traces makes the corresponding roots equal or inverse, and hence forces equality of their orders.

For K>=2 define

\[
 \begin{split}
 N&=5\,3^K,\\
 b_i&=1+\left\lceil (K-i)\frac{\log3}{\log5}\right\rceil,
       \qquad o_i=3^i5^{b_i},\quad 1\le i\le K,\\
 f_i&=\frac4{15}o_i,\qquad
 s=\sum_{i=1}^K f_i,\qquad d=2s.
 \end{split}                                           \tag{6}
\]

The ceiling gives

\[
 N\le o_i<5N,\qquad
 \frac8{15}NK\le d<\frac83NK.                           \tag{7}
\]

The o_i are distinct, because their 3-adic valuations are distinct, and b_i is nonincreasing.
Adjacent values of b_i can coincide; no strict divisibility-antichain assertion is used.
Put

\[
 T_0(x)=\prod_{i=1}^K S_{o_i}(x).
\]

Its degree is s, and its reduction modulo 2 is the product of K distinct irreducibles of respective degrees f_i.
The corresponding degree-d reciprocal polynomial is product_i Phi_{o_i}, whose reduction is squarefree and has exactly 2K factors.
Equations (6)--(7) also prove f_i comparable to d/K and d comparable to K3^K, with absolute comparison constants.

### 3. Prescribe both binary depths and retain an actual irreducible unit

We first choose H in Z[x], deg H<s, so that every monic trace polynomial satisfying

\[
 T\equiv T_0+2H\pmod4                                  \tag{8}
\]

has the desired local depths.
Work for a moment with a fixed i.
In the unramified degree-f_i extension of Q_2 choose the primitive o_i-th root zeta lifting a chosen primitive residue root, and write theta=zeta+zeta^{-1}.
Such an extension can be constructed from any monic lift of an irreducible polynomial of degree f_i over F_2; its finite free complete integer ring has residue field F_{2^{f_i}} and uniformizer 2, by the elementary argument in L64.
Simple-root Hensel lifting applied to t^{o_i}-1 supplies zeta.
Its order is exactly o_i by reduction.
Thus T_0(theta)=0, and T_0'(theta) is a unit because T_0 has squarefree reduction.

There is a unique root z of T near theta.
There is then a unique root alpha of t^2-zt+1 near zeta: its derivative reduces to zeta-zeta^{-1}, which is nonzero because the odd order o_i is greater than one.
These roots all belong to the same unramified extension.
The identities

\[
 T(\theta)-T(z)=(\theta-z)U,\qquad
 z-\theta=(\alpha-\zeta)\left(1-\frac1{\alpha\zeta}\right)
                                                               \tag{9}
\]

have unit factors U and 1-1/(alpha zeta), with U congruent to T_0'(theta) modulo 2.
Since T(theta) is congruent to 2H(theta) modulo 4, first-order expansion in this ring gives the exact residue relation

\[
 a_i:=\overline{\frac{\alpha^{o_i}-1}{2}}
   =-\overline{o_i}\,\overline{\zeta}^{-1}
      \frac{\overline H(\overline\theta)}
      {\overline{T_0'(\theta)}(1-\overline\zeta^{-2})}
       \quad\hbox{in }\mathbb F_{2^{f_i}}.              \tag{10}
\]

All denominators are nonzero.
In deriving (10), alpha-zeta is divisible by 2, so every term of quadratic or higher order in that difference is divisible by 4.
Formula (10) is therefore valid even before requiring either depth to equal one.

As the residue H(theta) varies, a_i is a nonzero scalar multiple of it.
Each f_i is at least 4, so the field has more than two elements.
We can choose H(theta) so that a_i is neither 0 nor 1.
The pairwise distinct irreducible factors S_{o_i} modulo 2 allow all K choices simultaneously, by polynomial Chinese remaindering in F_2[x], with deg H<s.
Choosing arbitrary integral representatives gives the required H.
For the Frobenius conjugates, a_i is replaced by a Frobenius conjugate and still avoids 0 and 1.
For the inverse root, both valuations below are unchanged.
Thus every root in the two degree-f_i factors satisfies

\[
 v_2(\alpha^{o_i}-1)=1,
 \qquad v_2(\alpha^{o_i}+1)=1.                          \tag{11}
\]

The second equality follows because (alpha^{o_i}+1)/2 differs from (alpha^{o_i}-1)/2 by 1.
This is why excluding only zero in (10) would not have been sufficient at p=2.

Now impose the independent congruence

\[
 T\equiv(x-2)^s+3\pmod9.                               \tag{12}
\]

Coefficientwise Chinese remaindering modulo 4 and 9 gives a monic integral T satisfying (8) and (12).
Choose each of its s lower coefficients to be the representative in [-18,17] of its residue modulo 36.
In particular every lower coefficient has absolute value at most 18.
Define

\[
 P_K(t)=t^sT(t+t^{-1}).                                \tag{13}
\]

This is a monic integral reciprocal polynomial of degree d=2s with constant term one.
Substitution in (12) gives

\[
 P_K(t+1)\equiv t^{2s}+3(t+1)^s\pmod9.                 \tag{14}
\]

Every nonleading coefficient in (14) is divisible by 3 and the constant coefficient is 3 modulo 9.
The polynomial is therefore Eisenstein at 3, so P_K is irreducible over Q.
Also T is irreducible: any factorization into monic integral polynomials would give a nontrivial factorization of (13) after multiplying each trace factor by the appropriate power of t.
In particular T has simple zeros in characteristic zero.

Modulo 2, (13) is exactly product_i Phi_{o_i}.
Thus 2 is prime to the polynomial discriminant; there are exactly two unramified degree-f_i local factors for each i.
The preceding local construction covers all their roots with their actual multiplicities.
No maximal-order or ramification correction is needed at this good prime.
The all-exponent calculation in the next section also proves directly that the local roots cannot be roots of unity; hence the irreducible polynomial P_K is nontorsion.
Neither that fact nor irreducibility is inferred from a decimal root computation.

### 4. Exact all-depth income and the common-exponent cost

Let alpha be any root in the group of order o_i.
If o_i does not divide n, its reduction shows v_2(alpha^n-1)=0.
If o_i divides n, put r=v_2(n).
The factorization

\[
 \alpha^{2o_i}-1=(\alpha^{o_i}-1)(\alpha^{o_i}+1)
\]

and (11) give depth exactly two at exponent 2o_i.
Whenever v_2(y-1)>=2, the two terms in y^2-1=2(y-1)+(y-1)^2 have distinct valuations, so v_2(y^2-1)=1+v_2(y-1).
Taking an odd power does not change any positive depth: its geometric-sum quotient reduces to 1.
Consequently for every n>=1,

\[
 v_2(\alpha^n-1)=
 \begin{cases}
  1+v_2(n),&o_i\mid n,\\
  0,&o_i\nmid n.
 \end{cases}                                          \tag{15}
\]

All these valuations are finite.
This proves the nontorsion assertion just used and ensures that C_n is nonzero for every n.
Summing over the 2f_i roots in each group gives the exact original-degree norm identity

\[
 v_2(C_n(P_K))=(1+v_2(n))
                    \sum_{i:o_i\mid n}2f_i.            \tag{16}
\]

Equivalently, each of the two degree-f_i unramified prime factors contributes f_i times the displayed depth.
Thus (16) counts all residue degrees and all original roots, and includes every exceptional first depth and every later slope.

Fix C>0 and an integer n<=Cd.
If there is no i with o_i dividing n, (16) is zero.
Otherwise let i_min and i_max be the smallest and largest selected indices, put t=i_max-i_min, and again write r=v_2(n).
Monotonicity of b_i gives the exact endpoint lcm

\[
 \operatorname{lcm}(o_{i_{\min}},o_{i_{\max}})
        =o_{i_{\min}}3^t\ge N3^t.
\]

There are at most t+1 selected indices, including any missing intermediate ones in this upper bound.
Because the lcm is odd, (7) implies

\[
 2^r3^t\le n/N<\frac83 CK.                             \tag{17}
\]

Each group has 2f_i<8N/3 original roots.
Combining (7), (16) and (17), and writing L_{C,K}=log^+((8/3)CK), yields

\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac{A_2(n)}d
 &\le\frac{5\log2}{K}(r+1)(t+1)\\
 &\le\frac{5\log2}{K}
     \left(1+\frac{L_{C,K}}{\log2}\right)
     \left(1+\frac{L_{C,K}}{\log3}\right).               \tag{18}
 \end{split}
\]

If the right side of (17) is below one, the nonempty selection case cannot occur, so (18) still gives the asserted universal bound.
It proves (2).
Since log d=K log3+log K+O(1), the stated reformulation in terms of d is valid.

This loss is a genuine coordination cost.
All individual orders o_i are below 5N, hence on the scale d/K, and all ratios o_i/f_i are the same fixed number.
Yet the smallest and largest selected indices already force an exponential 3^t lcm cost, while powers of 2 separately pay for any desired depth.
Bounding order/degree ratios alone does not control either their common divisibility pattern or the product of these two costs.
Equation (18) uses one and the same actual n throughout; no incomes at different exponents have been added.

### 5. Complete Mahler cost: this family is far from near one

The bounded coefficients chosen after (12) give, by the triangle inequality and the coefficient sum of (t+t^{-1})^j,

\[
 \|P_K\|_1\le 2^s+18\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}2^j
                 =19\,2^s-18.
\]

Jensen's formula therefore gives the upper bound in (3).
This is an upper bound for the full original polynomial, with no factor or cofactor omitted.

A lower bound follows from the actual trace polynomial, and also shows why the example does not meet the near-one restriction.
Jensen's formula followed by Parseval and the concavity of log gives

\[
 M(T)\le\|T\|_2\le\sqrt{1+324s}\le s
                         \quad(s\ge400).               \tag{19}
\]

Use the nonnegative real test function on C

\[
 \begin{split}
 \chi(z)&=\min(1,(2-|z|)_+),\\
 g(z)&=\min(1,(|\operatorname{Im}z|-1/2)_+),\\
 \phi(z)&=\chi(z)g(z).
 \end{split}
\]

Both chi and g take values in [0,1] and are globally 1-Lipschitz.
Thus phi is globally 2-Lipschitz, is bounded by one, and is supported in the disk of radius two.
On the two unit-circle arcs with arguments in [pi/3,2pi/3] and [4pi/3,5pi/3], chi=1 and g>=1/4.
Their combined normalized arclength is 1/3, so the circle average of phi is at least 1/12.

Let z_1,...,z_s be the zeros of T.
They are simple by its proved irreducibility.
Applying exactly Pri11-U001 with N=s, A=2, R=2 and (19), for s>=400, gives

\[
 \frac1s\sum_{j=1}^s\phi(z_j)
             \ge\frac1{12}-10\sqrt{\frac{\log s}{s}}.
                                                               \tag{20}
\]

In particular, whenever 10 sqrt(log s/s)<=1/24, at least s/24 of these roots satisfy |Im z_j|>1/2, because phi vanishes outside that condition and is at most one.
This is an explicit sufficient threshold, and it holds for all sufficiently large K by (7).

Each trace root z_j corresponds in (13) to the inverse pair of roots of t^2-z_jt+1.
Let R_j>=1 be the larger modulus.
Writing a root of that modulus as R_j exp(i theta) shows

\[
 |\operatorname{Im}z_j|
       =|(R_j-R_j^{-1})\sin\theta|
       \le R_j-R_j^{-1}.
\]

Thus |Im z_j|>1/2 implies R_j>(1+sqrt(17))/4.
The two roots of that quadratic have moduli R_j and R_j^{-1}, so their complete contribution to m(P_K) is exactly log R_j.
Summing all s pairs, rather than selecting a factor of P_K, now gives

\[
 m(P_K)=\sum_{j=1}^s\log R_j
      \ge\frac{s}{24}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}4
      =\frac{d}{48}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}4.             \tag{21}
\]

This proves the lower bound in (3).
It uses the discrepancy theorem only on an integral simple-zero polynomial with its own checked measure bound; no degree-preservation claim about powered roots, Salem classification or exchange of limiting logarithmic integrals is used.
The construction has many expanding inverse pairs and is not presented as a Salem family.

### 6. Consequence, attribution and author check

The exact failed inference is now narrower and more informative than an unnormalized aggregate warning.
At the fixed good prime 2, even full original root mass, growing residue degrees on the scale d/log d, a common constant order/degree ratio, and exactly minimal stable depths do not force a linear local contribution in any fixed degree-linear exponent window.
A new mechanism would have to use more than those marginal order and degree facts: for example, a joint divisibility pattern forced by genuinely small total measure, or a different arithmetic auxiliary that pays for those patterns without taking their lcm.

Neither such near-one incompatibility nor a new auxiliary is proved here.
The positive linear measure cost (21) expressly keeps this obstruction outside the small-total-measure domain.
Other primes might give large same-time income on P_K.
Nothing in (18) rules that out, and no bound on the complete cyclic norm or the unrestricted Lehmer gap is claimed.
This result also supplies no evidence that the whole arithmetic route is exhausted; the unselected high-degree contribution on actual near-one inputs remains the target.
