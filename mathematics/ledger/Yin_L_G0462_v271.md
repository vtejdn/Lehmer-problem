# Linear tests on the reflected Newton sums of a whole suffix

<a id="affine"></a>
## Coefficients and higher sums on a fixed prefix

Let \(P(X)=1+c_1X+\cdots+c_{2h-1}X^{2h-1}+X^{2h}\) be a monic reciprocal polynomial in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree \(2h\), and take all its alternative completions in \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
Write \(c_0=1,\ c_{2h-i}=c_i\) and \(s_k\) for the sums of the \(k\)-th powers of its roots.
Fix \(s_1,\ldots,s_m\), where \(\lceil h/2\rceil\le m<h\), and choose a base completion \(P\) of this prefix.
The [integral Euler coordinates](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/readme.md#counts) imply that every other permitted first half has sums \(s'_j=s_j+j b_j\) for integers \(b_j\), \(m<j\le h\), and that these choices are independent.
Indeed all proper divisors of \(j\le h\) are at most \(m\), so only the \(j\)-th Euler exponent in the formula for \(s_j\) remains free.
Separate lower and upper bounds on \(s'_j\) cut each \(b_j\) down to an integer interval.

Put \(C(X)=\sum_{i=0}^m c_iX^i\) and
\[
 V_j(X)=\sum_{i=j}^{h}c_{i-j}X^i
                 +\sum_{i=j}^{h-1}c_{i-j}X^{2h-i}.
\]
All these polynomials depend only on the fixed prefix.
The full polynomial \(P'\) belonging to the new first half and its Newton sums satisfy
\[
  P'=P-\sum_{j=m+1}^{h} b_j V_j,\qquad
  s'_k=s_k+k\sum_{j=m+1}^{h} b_j[X^k]\frac{V_j(X)}{C(X)}
                  \quad(1\le k\le2m+1).
\]
The two formulas are exact over the integers and apply to unrestricted sizes of the suffix coordinates.

For the proof, reciprocity gives \(\log P(X)=-\sum_{k\ge1}s_kX^k/k\) as a formal power series at zero, since \(P(X)=\prod_\alpha(1-\alpha X)\).
Taking the exponential of the difference through degree \(h\) retains just \(1-\sum b_jX^j\), because \(2(m+1)>h\).
Multiplication by \(P\) gives the stated coefficient changes through the center.
Reflecting them, with the center counted once, gives \(P'-P=-\sum b_jV_j\) in all degrees.
Now \(\log(P'/P)\equiv-\sum b_jV_j/P\pmod{X^{2m+2}}\), since every \(V_j\) has order at least \(m+1\).
Its denominator can be replaced by \(C\) to this precision because \(P-C\) also has order at least \(m+1\).
Comparing coefficients proves the second formula.

In particular changing one \(s_j\) by \(j\) changes \(s_{h+1}\) by \((h+1)(c_{h-1-j}-c_{h+1-j})\), using zero for negative indices.
Up to degree \(h\) the polynomial \(V_j\) agrees with \(X^jP\), and the coefficient at \(h+1\) explains this difference after division by \(P\).
More generally all required bounds on \(|s'_k|\) for \(h<k\le2m+1\) form a system of simultaneous linear inequalities in the independently bounded integers \(b_j\).
This permits testing many completions together by bounding linear sums over intervals or splitting the suffix into two groups.

## A degree-64 and a degree-66 comparison

Use the [boxes at measure 1.091](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v7/readme.md#bounds).
Fix \(m=20\) and the first twenty sums of \(\Phi_5\Phi_7\Phi_{11}\Phi_{17}\Phi_{23}\).
This is only a source of the prefix; either target degree admits its own reciprocal completions.
For a prime \(q\) the contribution is \(q-1\) if \(q\mid k\) and \(-1\) otherwise.
Since \(s_1=-5\), every completion is free of power substitutions and sign identifies it with precisely one vector outside this prefix.
The [affine calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v7/affine_tail.py) uses \(|s'_k|\le 2h-2+T^k+T^{-k}\) for \(h<k\le41\) with \(T=1091/1000\), and independently rebuilds every tested polynomial by Newton recursion.

| Degree | Complete representatives on the fixed prefix | Pass \(s_{h+1}\) | Pass all reflected tests through \(s_{41}\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 64 | 256 | 3 | 1 |
| 66 | 512 | 1 | 1 |

These are counts on the indicated prefix only.
