### 2. Every actual lattice coset enters any open real box

For each r in O and q>=1, the projection of r+qO onto its two real embeddings is dense in R^2.
Here is an elementary proof with the full actual order retained.

Let Lambda be the Minkowski image of O in V=R^2 x C^s, regarded as a real vector space; it is a full lattice.
Its Euclidean dual lattice consists of the functionals x -> Tr(bx) which are integral on O.
Indeed nondegeneracy of the trace pairing gives the unique b in K for any rational functional on K, and lattice-integrality makes its values on an integral power basis integers.
In the real extension the trace functional has coordinates b_+,b_- and 2Re(sigma_j(b)z_j).
A nonzero such functional cannot vanish on the entire neutral subspace E={0}^2 x C^s: vanishing would force a neutral embedding of b to be zero, hence b=0.
The same argument applies to qLambda and its dual.

It follows that E maps densely to V/qLambda.
For completeness, identify this quotient with R^d/Z^d using a lattice basis, and average the orbit of E over expanding rectangular boxes in any real basis of E.
For a nonconstant torus character, at least one of its coefficients on E is nonzero by the preceding paragraph.
Its box average is a product of one-dimensional sinc integrals and tends to zero.

Continuous functions are uniformly approximated by such polynomials: convolution with the product Fejer kernels gives an explicit approximate identity, as follows from concentration of each one-dimensional nonnegative normalized kernel at zero.
A continuous nonnegative function supported in any nonempty open torus ball and positive on a smaller ball has positive torus integral.

This proves density without an assumed quantitative first-visit bound.

Given a desired hyperbolic point y in R^2, density supplies e in E and lambda in qLambda with r-lambda arbitrarily close to(y,0)+e.
Its first two coordinates therefore approach y, and r-lambda belongs to the original coset r+qO.
This proves the assertion.

### 5. An actual infinite-order character detects what the quotient tests miss

This section does not assume the sufficient full-core description(1).
Every actual returning state necessarily satisfies |x_+|<B, by solving its finite zero-return word backwards.
Let pi be any probability supported on such states, and let pi P denote the one-step original law on all of O, even if a successor leaves D.
Write

    phi_pi(t)=E_pi exp(it x_+),
    kappa(t)=(1+cos t)/2=cos^2(t/2).

Then phi_(pi P)(t)=kappa(t) phi_pi(beta t).
Set

    t=pi_number/[2(B+2)],
    c=cos(tB)>0,
    a=(1-kappa(t))c>0,                             (8)

where pi_number denotes the usual circle constant, not the probability pi.
Since |tx_+|<=tB and beta tB=t(B+1)<pi_number/2, monotonicity of cosine in the absolute argument gives

    Re[phi_pi(t)-phi_(pi P)(t)]
      =E_pi[cos(tx_+)-kappa(t)cos(beta t x_+)]
      >=(1-kappa(t)) E_pi cos(tx_+)
      >=a.                                        (9)

Thus a single actual expanding-embedding Fourier test separates pi from its original next-step law, uniformly over all probabilities on D.
It is an infinite-order character on O: if exp(itx_+) factored through O/qO, the nonzero functional t x_+/(2pi_number) would belong to the dual of qLambda while vanishing on the neutral subspace, contradicting Section2's dual-lattice argument.
It is therefore absent from the finite-character algebra in(3).

There is also a direct information certificate for a genuinely admissible stationary Q as in L201.
Put f(x)=cos(t x_+) on D and let K be the original kernel killed outside D.
The function satisfies c<=f<=1, so log f is bounded.
Every original successor of x in D has expanding absolute value at most beta B+1=B+2.
Its full cosine is therefore nonnegative by the choice of t.
Killing outside D can only decrease its cosine sum, and consequently

    Kf(x) <= kappa(t) cos(beta t x_+)
           <= kappa(t) f(x).                       (10)

For any Q row supported on the permitted successors in D, the elementary log-sum inequality gives

    sum_eta q_x(eta)log[q_x(eta)/p_eta]
      >= E_(Q,x) log f(X_1)-log Kf(x).

There is at least one supported successor, hence Kf(x)>0.
Combining(10) and averaging with its genuine stationary probability yields

    I(Q) >= -log kappa(t),
    h_2(beta) >= -2log cos(pi_number/[4(B+2)])>0.     (11)

The cancellation of the log f coboundary is justified by boundedness, not an unproved finite state entropy or integrability assumption.
The second inequality uses L201's exact infimum over admissible actual-core laws.
The bound is asymptotic to pi_number^2 (beta-1)^2/16 as beta decreases to1.
It is weaker than the already retained linear-in-m bound of [L89](Yin_L_G0089_v1.md#mahler-to-binary-min-entropy), and it is not offered as a rate improvement or a gap theorem.
