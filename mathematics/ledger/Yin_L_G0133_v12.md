## The actual degree and the minimum central coefficient

Let d>=1 and let P in Z[z] be monic, reciprocal of degree 2d and have P(0)=1.
Let epsilon be +1 or -1, and suppose
\[
 p(z)=\varepsilon z^{-d}P(z)>0\qquad(z\in\mathbb T).
 \tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
 p(z)=p_0+\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_j(z^j+z^{-j}),\qquad
 p_j\in\mathbb Z,\quad p_d=\varepsilon.
 \tag{2}
\]
The original degree is 2d and all measures, when mentioned, are total measures.
Write M(P)=exp(m(P)), where m(P) is the total logarithmic Mahler measure.

Put
\[
 A(z)=z^d+\varepsilon,\qquad
 Z=\{\zeta\in\mathbb C:\zeta^d=-\varepsilon\}.
\]
These are d distinct points of the unit circle.
For every integer j with 0<|j|<d, their normalized power sum is zero.
Their d-th and minus-d-th powers are both -epsilon.
Consequently
\[
 \frac1d\sum_{\zeta\in Z}p(\zeta)=p_0-2>0.
 \tag{3}
\]
Because p_0 is an integer,
\[
 \boxed{p_0\ge3.}
 \tag{4}
\]
In particular the absolute value of the middle coefficient of P is at least three.
Thus a monic reciprocal constant-one integer polynomial of height at most two cannot have no circle root: if it had none, its nonvanishing real phase-balanced value would have constant sign, and choosing epsilon accordingly would contradict (4).
This conclusion requires neither irreducibility nor a small-measure hypothesis.
Strict positivity is essential for the strict inequality in (3).

<a id="central-three-positive-integer-product"></a>
## Central coefficient three forces equality on the whole grid

Suppose now that p_0=3.
The d positive real numbers p(zeta), zeta in Z, have arithmetic mean one.
At every such point,
\[
 p(\zeta)=\varepsilon\zeta^{-d}P(\zeta)=-P(\zeta).
 \tag{5}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \prod_{\zeta\in Z}p(\zeta)
   =(-1)^d\operatorname{Res}(A,P)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Here Res(A,P)=product_(A(zeta)=0)P(zeta), since A is monic.
This product's integrality is the standard finite quotient determinant: multiplication by P on Z[z]/(A), in its basis 1,z,...,z^(d-1), is an integer matrix whose complex eigenvalues are the complete list P(zeta).
The sign (-1)^d in (6) is retained; neither individual value nor a chosen orbit has been substituted for the full determinant.

AM--GM gives
\[
 0<\prod_{\zeta\in Z}p(\zeta)
 \le\left(\frac1d\sum_{\zeta\in Z}p(\zeta)\right)^d=1.
\]
The positive integer product is therefore exactly one.
Equality in AM--GM forces every value to equal the mean:
\[
 \boxed{p(\zeta)=1,\qquad P(\zeta)=-1
                    \quad\hbox{for every }\zeta^d=-\varepsilon.}
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular
\[
 \operatorname{Res}(A,P)
 =\operatorname{Res}(P,A)=(-1)^d.
 \tag{8}
\]
The two resultant orders agree because their degree-product is 2d^2, which is even.

<a id="central-three-exact-quotient-and-signs"></a>
## The exact coefficient pairing and quotient

At every root of A, equations (7) and epsilon zeta^d=-1 give P(zeta)-epsilon zeta^d=0.
The roots of A are distinct, and A is monic with integer coefficients.
Polynomial division therefore yields a unique Q in Z[z] with
\[
 \boxed{P(z)=(z^d+\varepsilon)Q(z)+\varepsilon z^d.}
 \tag{9}
\]
Its leading coefficient is one, its degree is d and its constant coefficient is epsilon.

For a polynomial R of specified degree n, write R^*(z)=z^n R(z^{-1}).
The polynomial P-epsilon z^d is reciprocal of degree 2d, whereas
\[
 A^*=\varepsilon A.
\]
Reverse (9), cancel the nonzero factor A and use epsilon^2=1 to obtain
\[
 \boxed{Q^*=\varepsilon Q.}
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus Q is reciprocal when epsilon=+1 and anti-reciprocal when epsilon=-1.

The explicit quotient and the equivalent coefficient pairing are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q(z)&=z^d+\varepsilon+
           \varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}p_jz^j,\\
 p_j&=\varepsilon p_{d-j}\qquad(1\le j<d).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
To see equivalence directly, the coefficients of z^(d+j) in (9) prescribe Q_j=epsilon p_j for 1<=j<d.
The coefficients below z^d then match those of P exactly when p_j=epsilon p_(d-j).
The central coefficient in (9) is Q_0+epsilon Q_d+epsilon=3epsilon, as required by p_0=3.
These are necessary equalities under (1); the equalities alone do not replace strict positivity.

The signs include the following boundary cases.
For d=1 there is no intermediate coefficient condition, and (9) becomes
\[
 P=z^2+3\varepsilon z+1,\qquad Q=z+\varepsilon.
 \tag{12}
\]
Both choices of epsilon give a strictly positive p=3+epsilon(z+z^(-1)).
When epsilon=-1 and d is even, (11) gives p_(d/2)=0.
For epsilon=-1, Q^*=-Q implies Q(1)=0; if d is even it also implies Q(-1)=0.
For epsilon=+1 and odd d, Q^*=Q implies Q(-1)=0.
These forced factors do not assert that every factor of Q is cyclotomic.

There is also an actual unit relation in the original integer quotient.
The class of z in Z[z]/(P) is a unit because P is monic with constant term one.
Equation (9) gives
\[
 A Q=-\varepsilon z^d\pmod P,
\]
so both A and Q are units in that quotient, with explicit inverses
\[
 A^{-1}=-\varepsilon z^{-d}Q,\qquad
 Q^{-1}=-\varepsilon z^{-d}A\pmod P.
 \tag{13}
\]
For any original root alpha, both A(alpha) and Q(alpha) are therefore algebraic integer units.
This is a consequence on the original input, not an omitted norm denominator or a bound on total m(P).

## A concrete consequence for an actual doubled certificate

The equality case does give additional structure to any supplied degree-bounded certificate.
Suppose p_0=3 and
\[
 2p=\sum_{j=1}^r |v_j|^2\quad\hbox{on }\mathbb T,\qquad
 v_j\in\mathbb Z[z],\quad \deg v_j\le d.
 \tag{14}
\]
Let u_j be the ordinary remainder of v_j modulo A=z^d+epsilon, so u_j has degree less than d and integer coefficients.
At the d roots of A, equation (7) gives
\[
 \sum_j|u_j(\zeta)|^2=2.
\]
For exponents 0<=k,l<d, the normalized average of zeta^k conjugate(zeta^l) over this grid is one if k=l and zero otherwise.
Averaging the preceding equality therefore yields the exact coefficient identity
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}
                  \bigl([z^k]u_j\bigr)^2=2.}
 \tag{15}
\]
Thus across all remainder polynomials there are exactly two nonzero coefficients, each +1 or -1.
Writing t_j for the coefficient of z^d in v_j gives the actual form
\[
 v_j=t_j(z^d+\varepsilon)+u_j,\qquad t_j\in\mathbb Z,
 \tag{16}
\]
with the two-unit coefficient condition (15).
The two coefficients may belong to one remainder or to two different remainders; no unrestricted independent choice is asserted.
Every further lag equation in (14) still has to hold.

For an actual multiplier-one certificate the same argument gives total remainder coefficient energy one.
More generally an actual cp certificate gives energy c.
These statements are necessary finite quotient constraints, not existence theorems.

The [L133 v11 term-count result](Yin_L_G0133_v11.md#certificate-term-count-mahler-bound) already shows that below M(P)=3/2, a certificate yielding the adopted integral reflection must reduce to multiplier two and exactly two nonzero summands.
On the additional central-three branch, (15)--(16) constrain that two-row problem.
They do not assert that every small-measure original input has central coefficient three or has any certificate.

## The quotient is not a fixed-pair asymptotic return

Equation (9) is a finite original-input identity with the shift equal to the actual degree d of Q.
It supplies no delay tending to infinity while the coefficient block is fixed.

For example, when epsilon=+1, a reciprocal balanced extension for a fixed degree-d Q is
\[
 P_N(z)=(z^N+1)Q(z)+z^{(N+d)/2},
 \qquad N>0,\quad N\equiv d\pmod2.
 \tag{17}
\]
It has actual degree N+d, is monic reciprocal with constant term one, and returns the original P at N=d.
After replacing z by x^2, this is a three-block specialization with centered block x^(-d)Q(x^2), second torus frequency N and a constant middle term.
A sign change in the second torus variable, together with an overall sign, changes between the plus and minus middle-term conventions without changing the two-variable torus measure.

The [C868 v2 fixed-pair interface](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v2.md#fixed-pair-and-original-target) retains a limit only for a fixed pair and its stated delay domain.
It does not provide a bound uniform in a moving Q at its same-scale index N=d.
For epsilon=-1, Q is anti-reciprocal, so the reciprocal-block hypotheses of that interface also need an actual separate match; they cannot be silently imposed.
No finite-input lower bound for m(P) is extracted from a Lawton limit here.

The [L133 v7 universal central split](Yin_L_G0133_v7.md#universal-central-split-endpoint) is a different exact-return control whose original input occurs at an excluded endpoint with a degree drop.
Equation (9) has actual degree 2d and has no such leading-term cancellation.
The common caution is the unavailable transfer from a fixed-pair asymptotic measure to an arbitrary original finite specialization, not an assertion that the two constructions have the same endpoint defect.
