# Resultant ceilings at the five limiting profiles and the moving exceptional parent

<a id="norm-ceiling"></a>
## The five bounds

Use the [full odd grid and strict chamber](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product), with \(p=2n+1\), \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\) for \(1\le j\le n\), \(B=B_n=\prod_j(t-b_j)\), and \(Q=(t-A)B+C\) for \(A\in\mathbb Z\), \(C\in\mathbb Z[t]\), \(\deg C<n\).
The residues \(\rho_j=Q(b_j)/B'(b_j)\) are strictly positive up to some \(1\le c<n\), strictly negative thereafter, and \(Q(-2)/B(-2)<0\).
Put \(P(z)=z^{n+1}Q(z+z^{-1})\) and \(\Delta=|\operatorname{Res}(B,Q)|\).
For \(p\to\infty\) and whole measures \(1<M(P)\to1\), the [endpoint and profile classification](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#only-left-endpoint) gives angular midpoints \(a=2\cos((2c+1)\pi/p)\to-2\).
Along every subsequence with coefficientwise limit
\[
   P_{*,x}(z)=\frac{(1-xz+z^2)^2}{(1-z)(1-z^2)},
   \qquad x\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\},
\]
one has
\[
  \limsup\Delta^{1/n}\le 2\exp[-1/(4I_x)],\qquad
  I_x=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi [2(t+2)(t-x)^2]^2\,d\theta,\quad t=2\cos\theta .
  \tag{1}
\]

| \(x\) | \(-2\) | \(-1\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(2\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(I_x\) | \(3696\) | \(1048\) | \(176\) | \(24\) | \(112\) |

In particular the upper limit is at most \(2e^{-1/14784}\) over the whole chamber, improving the [previous bound \(2e^{-2^{-22}}\)](Yin_L_G0464_v6.md#norm-ceiling).
Every subsequence approaching the upper limit has a further subsequence with one of the five profiles by the cited classification and its compactness argument.
For the \(x=0\) profile the bound is \(2e^{-1/704}\).
These are necessary conditions on a shrinking sequence, including all factors of \(P\).
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v55.md#profile-ceiling-review) checks the completed-grid moments, the mass at \(2\) and all five constants.
On 2026-11-16 the main researcher proposed the comparison with the endpoint atom and the second-moment estimate for Yin's [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract); the delegated researcher derived the comparison from the limiting Cauchy transform and checked the constants and the equivalence below.

## A comparison with integral moments at every order

Write \(\langle h\rangle=\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi h(2\cos\theta)\,d\theta\) and let \(\mathbb E_p\) average over the \(n\) grid nodes.
Adjoin \(b_0=2\), taking \(\rho_0=0\), and prescribe the positive measure
\[
 \sigma_j=2(b_j-x)^2/p\quad(1\le j\le n),\qquad \sigma_0=(2-x)^2/p.
\]
Individual masses may vanish at \(b_j=x\).
For every nonnegative \(k\), \(\sum_{j=0}^n b_j^k\sigma_j\) is an integer.
Indeed it is the full \(p\)-point average of the integral Laurent polynomial \((z+z^{-1})^k(z+z^{-1}-x)^2\), equal to the sum of its coefficients at exponents divisible by \(p\).
It equals \(\langle u^k(u-x)^2\rangle\) when \(k+2<p\), where \(u=2\cos\theta\) denotes the integration variable.
Likewise every \(\sum_j b_j^k\rho_j\) is integral by expansion of \(C/B\) at infinity.

The actual and prescribed moments of any fixed order eventually agree exactly along the chosen profile.
Indeed, under \(t=z+z^{-1}\), \(Q/B=P(z)(1-z)/(z(1-z^p))\) converges coefficientwise at \(z=0\) to
\[
  R_*(t)=\frac{(t-x)^2}{\sqrt{t^2-4}}
        =t-2x+\left\langle\frac{(u-x)^2}{t-u}\right\rangle ,
  \tag{2}
\]
where \(\sqrt{t^2-4}=z^{-1}-z\) is the formal branch at infinity.
The last identity follows by polynomial division and the arcsine transform \(\langle1/(t-u)\rangle=1/\sqrt{t^2-4}\), whose series follows from \(\langle u^{2m}\rangle=\binom{2m}{m}\) and vanishing odd moments.
Since \(t^{-1}=z/(1+z^2)=z+O(z^3)\) has a compositional inverse, the Laurent coefficients at infinity converge at each fixed order as well.
Their integrality gives eventual equality with the coefficients in (2), including \(A=2x\).

For \(j\ge1\), put
\[
 X_j=p(b_j-a)\rho_j>0,\qquad
 G_j=p(b_j-a)\sigma_j,\qquad f(t)=2(t+2)(t-x)^2 .
\]
Then \(\sup_j|G_j-f(b_j)|\to0\) since \(a\to-2\).
For every fixed polynomial \(V\), the moment agreement just proved handles both \(bV(b)\) and \(V(b)\), so
\[
 \mathbb E_p X_j V(b_j)
 =\mathbb E_p G_j V(b_j)+(p/n)(2-a)\sigma_0 V(2)
 \longrightarrow\langle fV\rangle .
\]
The endpoint term is \(O_V(1/p)\), and the unweighted half-grid means converge to the angular mean by equidistribution.
Positivity, bounded mass from \(V=1\), and polynomial approximation extend the convergence to each continuous \(V\).
The [exact grid product](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_p\log X_j=\frac{\log\Delta+\log(p|B(a)|)}{n}
                    =\frac{\log\Delta}{n}+o(1),
\]
uniformly over the cut since \(1\le |B(a)|\le p\).
Hence the logarithmic resultant rates are bounded above by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and are nonnegative by integrality.

## The first differing integer moment

At each conductor the two measures differ, since \(\rho_n<0\) and \(\sigma_n\ge0\).
The Vandermonde matrix of \(b_0,\ldots,b_n\) is invertible, so the first differing monomial moment has some order \(k\le n\).
Eventual agreement at each fixed order gives \(k\to\infty\).
For \(k\ge2\) let \(T_{k-1}(2\cos\theta)=2\cos((k-1)\theta)\).
The polynomial \((t-a)T_{k-1}(t)\) is monic of degree \(k\), and all its lower-degree terms integrate to zero against \(\rho-\sigma\).
Its integral is therefore a nonzero integer.
As \(\|T_{k-1}\|_{[-2,2]}\le2\), it follows that
\[
 1\le\frac{2n}{p}\mathbb E_p|X_j-G_j|+2(2-a)\sigma_0 .
\]
The estimate of the atom is independent of \(k\).
The uniform approximation of \(G_j\) by \(f(b_j)\) now gives
\[
                 \liminf\mathbb E_p|X_j-f(b_j)|\ge1.            \tag{3}
\]

<a id="second-moment"></a>
## A weighted estimate of the logarithmic deficit

Pass to a further subsequence on which \(D=\lim n^{-1}\log\Delta\) exists, and set \(\delta=\log2-D\).
For fixed \(\epsilon>0\), write \(Y_j=X_j/(f(b_j)+\epsilon)\) and \(\psi(y)=y-1-\log y\).
The preceding convergences give
\[
 \lim\mathbb E_p\psi(Y_j)=
 \left\langle\frac{f}{f+\epsilon}\right\rangle-1+
             \langle\log(f+\epsilon)\rangle-D .
\]
The right side tends to \(\delta\) as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).
Indeed \(f>0\) almost everywhere, and \(\langle\log f\rangle=\log 2\) because \(\langle\log|t-v|\rangle=0\) for \(v=-2,x\), including both endpoints by the unit-circle mean formula.
The logarithmic singularities are integrable, so \(\langle\log(f+\epsilon)\rangle\to\langle\log f\rangle\).
In particular \(\delta\ge0\).

Use \((y-1)^2\le2(1+y)\psi(y)\), obtained by integrating \((y-1)/y\) between \(1\) and \(y\).
Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
 \mathbb E_p|X_j-(f(b_j)+\epsilon)|
 \le\left(2\,\mathbb E_p[(f(b_j)+\epsilon)^2(1+Y_j)]
                  \,\mathbb E_p\psi(Y_j)\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The first expectation in the product tends to \(\langle(f+\epsilon)^2+f(f+\epsilon)\rangle\) by the continuous test with \(V=f+\epsilon\).
Let \(p\to\infty\) first, then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).
In view of (3) this gives \(1\le 2\sqrt{\langle f^2\rangle\delta}\).
Consequently \(D\le\log2-1/(4I_x)\), proving (1) also for the subsequence approaching the upper limit.
The same even-moment formula used in (2) expands \(I_x\) as \(24x^4-128x^3+336x^2-384x+176\), yielding the five values in the table.

## The \(x=0\) profile and growing exceptional parents

Consider sequences in the actual integral chamber above with \(p\to\infty\), retaining the strict sign block and the strict endpoint.
Along such a sequence, matching the fixed \(P_{*,0}\) through indices tending to infinity is equivalent, after discarding finitely many terms, to representing the same \(P\)'s by [exceptional integral perturbations](../claims/Yin_C_G0892_v1.md#statement) with matching indices tending to infinity.
In the latter description \(r=p-2s>2s+3\) is odd, \(m=(r-1)/2\), \(F_s=(1+z^2)^2(1-z^{2s})/(1-z^2)\), and \(Q=t^2 E_{s-1}B_m+H\) with \(H\in\mathbb Z[t]\), \(\deg H\le n-2\), where \(E_j(2\cos\theta)=\sin((j+1)\theta)/\sin\theta\).
The measured polynomial, the conductor, every residue and the endpoint ratio are the same in both descriptions.
The [separate independent finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v55.md#profile-equivalence-review) checks both implications and the use of the fixed-reference whole-measure bound.

In the exceptional direction, let \(L\to\infty\) be the matching indices with \(F_s/(1-z)\).
The [linear prefix bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0892_v1.md#statement) implies \(s\to\infty\).
Since \(F_s/(1-z)=(1-z^{2s})P_{*,0}\), the same lift agrees with \(P_{*,0}\) through \(\min(L,2s-1)\to\infty\).

Conversely, suppose the fixed profile is matched through indices \(\ell\to\infty\).
Take \(s=\lfloor(p-4)/4\rfloor\), so \(s\to\infty\), \(s\ge2\) eventually and \(r=p-2s>2s+3\).
Write \(Q_0=t^2E_{s-1}B_m\).
Its [neutral trace identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0892_v1.md#moments) gives \(z^{n+1}Q_0(z+z^{-1})=(1-z^r)(1-z^{2s})P_{*,0}\).
Thus this neutral lift, \(P\), and \(F_s/(1-z)\) agree through \(L'=\min(\ell,2s-1)\to\infty\).
The polynomial \(H=Q-Q_0\) is integral and nonzero since \(Q_0\) has only nonnegative residues.
Its lift difference starts at index \(n+1-\deg H\), hence \(\deg H\le n-L'\le n-2\) eventually.
This realizes the original \(P\) itself in the exceptional family.

The fixed prefix through degree two gives \(A=0\) and \(\sum_j\rho_j=2\) by (2) at \(x=0\), or directly by expansion of \(Q/B\).
For the equivalence, (2) up to this order requires only those three fixed coefficients.
Thus \(Q-tB_n\) has degree \(n-1\) and leading coefficient two on either sequence eventually.
With \(U=(1+z^2)^2\), \(V=(1-z)(1-z^2)\) and the matching index truncated to \(n+1\) if necessary, the [whole-measure prefix bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v10.md#statement) gives \(1<M(P)\to1\).
This equivalence and (1) restrict possible integer sequences; existence of a sequence with this fixed profile remains open.
