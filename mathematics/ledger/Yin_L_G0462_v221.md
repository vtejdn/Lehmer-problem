# Counting quartic Salem directions by a common power of their real root

<a id="count"></a>
## A common divisor of the first exponents

Let \(g(T)=T^4-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^3-T^4\), and let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers with \(a\) odd and \(r=b/a\).
For \(r>6\) the section \(g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) has a [unique real exterior root](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#real), which we write as \(-\sigma_{a,b}\).
Call a direction Salem if the positive number \(\sigma_{a,b}\) is Salem.
For all sufficiently large real \(R\), uniformly in every odd integer \(q\ge3\),
\[
 \#\{(a,b):q\mid a,\ R\le b/a\le 2R,\ \sigma_{a,b}\text{ is Salem}\}
       \ll \log(2R).                                                   \tag{1}
\]
There is no restriction on the parity of \(b\), and the count combines all first exponents divisible by \(q\).

Write \(-\beta<-1\) for the unique exterior zero of \(B\), and set \(L=\log\beta>0\).
For each direction counted in (1) take \(z=\sigma_{a,b}^{a/q}>1\) and let \(P_z\) be its monic minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\).
The [power and degree formulas](Yin_L_G0462_v216.md#powers) show that \(z\) has the same degree \(d\) as \(-\sigma_{a,b}\).
Indeed \(a/q\) is odd, so this is just a change of sign after the indicated power.
Taking powers preserves the unique exterior root, its inverse and the circle conjugates, hence \(P_z\) is a Salem polynomial.
The uniform [one-exterior-root estimates](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#counts), together with the real pole branch, give
\[
 d=(1+o(1))r,\qquad
 z=\beta^{1/q}\exp\!\big(O(\beta^{-r})/q\big),\qquad
 m(P_z)=\log z=\frac{L+O(\beta^{-r})}{q}.                            \tag{2}
\]
Here \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure and the little-oh tends to zero uniformly as \(R\to\infty\).
Distinct primitive directions give distinct \(P_z\).
In fact equal polynomials have the same unique exterior root \(z\), and \(u=z^q>1\) recovers \(r\) from
\[
                    u^r=|g(-u)/B(-u)|.
\]
The denominator is nonzero by the coprimality of \(g,B\), and \(r\) determines its reduced numerator and denominator.

Choose any finite subset of \(h\ge2\) directions in (1) and form \(Q=\prod_z P_z\), of degree \(D\).
This monic integral polynomial has nonzero integer discriminant.
For any two of its exterior roots \(z_i,z_j\), (2) gives \(|z_i-z_j|\ll\beta^{-R}/q\) and \(z_i z_j-1\gg1/q\).
Thus the ratio \(\delta_{ij}=|z_i-z_j|/(z_i z_j-1)\) is \(O(\beta^{-R})\), and reflecting the pair into the disk by taking reciprocals leaves the ratio unchanged.
The [reflected discriminant inequality (4)](Yin_L_G0462_v216.md#discriminant) applies to \(Q\) since its proof uses only the discriminant product for distinct roots and the disk Vandermonde bound.
Keeping the \(h(h-1)\) ordered exterior pairs and equally many interior pairs gives, for an absolute \(C\),
\[
 2(RL-C)h(h-1)
 \ \le\ D\log D-\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|+2(D-1)m(Q)
 \ \le\ D\log D+2(D-1)m(Q).                                         \tag{3}
\]
Now \(D\le(2+o(1))Rh\) and \(m(Q)\le h(L+O(\beta^{-R}))/q\).
In (3) the last summand is at most \((4/3+o(1))RLh^2\), uniformly since \(q\ge3\).
Subtracting it gives \(h\ll1+\log(2Rh)\), hence \(h\ll\log(2R)\) by absorbing \(\log h\).
This proves (1) for the entire set.
The exclusion of \(q=1\) is essential.
For \(a=1\) and large \(b\), the [quartic circle count](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#phase) gives exactly one exterior root, and its reciprocal irreducible factor has degree tending to infinity by the [primitive degree bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement), hence gives a Salem number after the change of sign.

## A global count and certificates for almost every exponent on a fixed row

Across **all** odd \(a\ge3\) the number of Salem directions with \(R\le b/a\le 2R\) is
\[
                      \ll \left(\frac{\log R}{\log\log R}\right)^4.    \tag{4}
\]
Indeed a Salem input has degree \(d\asymp r\) and the [primitive degree bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) gives \(a\ll(\log b/\log\log b)^3\) since \(b=ar\).
In particular \(\log r=\log b-O(\log\log b)\), so \(a\le A=C(\log R/\log\log R)^3\) for some absolute \(C\).
Each odd \(a\ge3\) has a prime divisor \(q\) in \([3,A]\).
Summing (1) over these primes proves (4) by the elementary bound \(\pi(A)\ll A/\log A\).
For example, the product of the primes in \((n,2n]\) divides \(\binom{2n}{n}\le4^n\), and summing over dyadic intervals gives \(\sum_{p\le A}\log p\ll A\), which yields the prime count on separating \(p\le\sqrt A\).

In particular for every fixed odd \(a\ge3\) there are \(O(\log(2R))\) Salem exponents in the dyadic slope window and \(O_a((\log(2R))^2)\) up to slope \(R\), by dyadic summation.
For \(a=3\) this counts all possible [splits of the nontorsion rational core](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#quartic).
For fixed odd \(a\ge3\) the primitive even exponents in the dyadic window number \(\gg_a R\), and the exponents \(b=6a+c^2\) for odd \(c>0\) coprime to \(a\) number \(\gg_a\sqrt R\).
These bounds follow already by taking \(b\equiv2\bmod 2a\) or \(c\equiv1\bmod 2a\), respectively.
In either family all but \(O(\log(2R))\) exponents have a single good prime witnessing the [odd trace degree exclusion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v400.md#statement).
More precisely, remove the cyclotomic factors of the monic section and write the remainder as \(X^n H(X+X^{-1})\).
By the [extremal prime formula](Yin_L_G0462_v220.md#classes), for every non-Salem input of sufficiently large slope and every prescribed reduced residue class, some \(p\) in that class satisfies
\[
 p\nmid\operatorname{disc}H,\qquad
 \ell(p):=\min\{\deg f:f\mid H\bmod p\text{ monic irreducible of odd degree}\}
           \ \ge\ b/a .                                                \tag{5}
\]
The odd factor always exists on these two families.
The counting estimate alone allows infinitely many Salem directions with unbounded first exponent.

<a id="least-prime"></a>
## The least certificate prime can grow on every fixed row

For every fixed odd \(a\ge3\) there are infinitely many primitive even exponents \(b\) for which (5) holds and its least witness exceeds \(c_a\log b\) for some \(c_a>0\).
To see this take a fixed \(b_0>6a\) divisible by four and coprime to \(a\).
Write \(A_b=-g(X^a)-X^bB(X^a)\).
For \(b\equiv b_0\bmod 4a\) the [complete cyclotomic part](Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#orders) is \(\Phi_4\), so \(A_b=\Phi_4 X^{n_b}H_b(X+X^{-1})\) with \(n_b=(4a+b-2)/2\) odd.
For an integer \(Y\ge2\), let \(t_p\) be the order of \(X\) in the units of \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(A_{b_0})\), for every prime \(p\le Y\).
The unit constant term gives \(t_p\le p^{4a+b_0}\) even when the reduction has repeated factors.
Put \(M=4a\,2^Y\prod_{p\le Y}t_p\), so \(Y\ll\log M\ll_a Y\) after fixing \(b_0\) in terms of \(a\).
For \(b>b_0\) congruent to \(b_0\bmod M\), reducing modulo each \((p,A_{b_0})\) gives \(A_{b_0}\mid A_b\bmod p\).
Cancelling the common cyclotomic polynomial and using the free Laurent basis \(1,X\) over \(\mathbb F_p[X+X^{-1}]\) then gives \(H_{b_0}\mid H_b\bmod p\), as in the [trace copying argument](Yin_L_G0462_v217.md#construction).
At every good \(p\le Y\) the odd degree \(n_{b_0}\) therefore forces \(\ell(p)\le n_{b_0}\).

The interval \(MY^2\le b\le 2MY^2\) contains \(\asymp Y^2\) exponents in this common residue class.
Its slope window has lower end \(R=MY^2/a\), with \(\log(2R)=O_a(Y)\).
By (1) only \(O_a(Y)\) of these exponents can be Salem.
For every remaining exponent (5) has a witness, while \(b/a>n_{b_0}\) for large \(Y\) rules out all primes up to \(Y\).
Here \(\log b\ll_a Y\), proving the lower bound.
