# Gaps between Salem conjugates and torsion grids on the circle

<a id="gaps"></a>
## Sum of the squared gaps

Let \(1<\sigma\le2\) be a Salem number of degree \(d\).
Give the circle circumference one and write \(L_j\) for the lengths of the \(d-2\) successive gaps, including the gap across the origin of angular coordinates, between its neutral conjugates.
Then
\[
                       \sum_j L_j^2\ll\frac{\log(2d)}{d}.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for any partition into \(k\) equal arcs, with arbitrary rotation, at most \(O(k^2\log(2d)/d)\) open arcs contain no neutral conjugate.
Thus for \(k=o(d/\log(2d))\) a proportion \(1-o(1)\) of the cells is occupied, uniformly over the grid rotations.

Let \(f\) be the circular distance to the set of neutral conjugates.
It is a sum of continuous triangular functions \(f_j\) with disjoint interiors of support, one per gap, each having base \(L_j\), height \(L_j/2\) and slopes \(1,-1\).
For a Lipschitz circle function \(v\), put \(E(v)=\sum_k |k|\,|\widehat v(k)|^2\) with frequencies \(e^{2\pi ikt}\).
Parseval in the variable \(t\) gives
\[
 E(v)=\int_0^1\!\int_0^1
          \frac{|v(t+s)-v(t)|^2}{|e^{2\pi i s}-1|^2}\,dt\,ds,
\]
because \(\int_0^1 |(e^{2\pi i k s}-1)/(e^{2\pi i s}-1)|^2\,ds=|k|\) by the finite geometric sum.
For distinct nonnegative tents, the cross term between their differences is nonpositive pointwise since their values at each common argument have product zero.
Hence \(E(f)\le\sum_j E(f_j)\).
A single tent of base \(L\) has \(|\widehat f_j(k)|\ll\min(L^2,k^{-2})\) for \(k\ne0\), by its area and two integrations by parts using the bounded total variation of its slope.
Splitting the defining sum at \(1/L\) gives \(E(f_j)\ll L^2\).

Write \(\nu\) for the empirical probability on the neutral conjugates and \(m\) for arclength probability.
We have \(\nu(f)=0\) and \(m(f)=\sum_j L_j^2/4\).
The [Fourier energy estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#energy) bounds \(\sum_{1\le |k|\le d^4}|\widehat\nu(k)|^2/|k|\) by \(O(\log(2d)/d)\).
The Fourier series of \(f\) is absolutely convergent, and its tail after \(d^4\) has absolute sum \(O(d^{-2})\) by Cauchy–Schwarz and \(\|f'\|_2\le1\).
Pairing the frequencies through \(d^4\) with the preceding estimate yields, for \(X=\sum_j L_j^2\),
\[
               X/4\ll \sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}d}\sqrt X+d^{-2}.
\]
This proves (1).
Every empty open grid cell is contained in the closure of a single neutral gap of length \(L\ge1/k\).
At most \(kL\le k^2 L^2\) such cells can occur there since their interiors are disjoint.
Summation proves the occupancy estimate.

## The largest complete Chebyshev grid

Let \(L=\max_j L_j\) and let \(S\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\).
For \(k\ge1\) put \(D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\).
Call \(D_k\) admissible when its distinct torsion trace roots strictly alternate with some \(k+1\) roots of \(S\), starting and ending with a root of \(S\).
For all sufficiently large \(d\),
\[
          \max\{k:D_k\text{ is admissible}\}\asymp L^{-1}
                         \gg\sqrt{\frac d{\log(2d)}}.                    \tag{2}
\]
One can choose an admissible power of two \(k\asymp L^{-1}\) for which \(D_k\) is irreducible.

On the upper semicircle \(0<t<1/2\), the traces \(2\cos(2\pi t)\) decrease strictly, and the \(k\) nodes are at \(t_j=(2j-1)/(4k)\) for \(1\le j\le k\).
The internal cells have length \(1/(2k)\) and both endpoint cells have length \(1/(4k)\).
Any open interval of length exceeding \(L\) contains a neutral conjugate.
Thus every \(k\) with \(4kL<1\) is admissible by selecting one neutral trace in each of these \(k+1\) cells.
By (1), \(L\to0\) as \(d\to\infty\), so a power of two \(k\asymp L^{-1}\) satisfies the strict inequality.
The polynomial \(D_k\) is monic integral by the trace recurrence.
At a power of two its lift \(z^kD_k(z+z^{-1})=z^{2k}+1\) is irreducible, since translating \(z\) by one gives an Eisenstein polynomial at two.
A factorization of \(D_k\) would give one of its lift.

Conversely, for any admissible \(k\ge2\), each of the \(k-1\) internal cells in the upper semicircle contains a neutral phase, since the exterior trace exceeds every node.
Choose one in each cell.
Successive chosen phases differ by at most \(1/k\).
The first is within \(3/(4k)\) of zero and the last within \(3/(4k)\) of \(1/2\).
Reflect these chosen phases by conjugation.
Every gap in the resulting subset has length at most \(3/(2k)\), including the gaps crossing zero and \(1/2\).
Adding the remaining neutral conjugates only shortens gaps, giving \(k\le3/(2L)\).
This proves (2).

At the larger scales covered by the occupancy estimate, removing individual nodes to eliminate empty cells need not preserve integer coefficients.
For example, when \(k\) is a power of two, the nodes of \(D_k\) form a single Galois orbit, so no nonempty proper subset is the root set of a rational polynomial having only simple roots there and no other roots.
Thus the estimate for almost all cells allows more grid points than the guaranteed degree of a fully interlacing integer polynomial.
