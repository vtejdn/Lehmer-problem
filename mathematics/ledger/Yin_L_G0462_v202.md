# The maximal rank of a shrinking factor with an even number of parent terms

<a id="equality"></a>
## Rigidity when the reciprocal factor attains the vertex bound

Suppose a sequence of univariate specializations of a fixed rational Laurent polynomial with \(2r\) terms, \(r\ge3\), has nontorsion irreducible factors of logarithmic Mahler measures tending to zero.
In the [subtorus reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v193.md#obstruction), the intrinsic support rank of the retained reciprocal factor \(H\) is at most \(r\).
If the rank is \(r\), the entire restricted parent is associated to \(H\), has exactly the vertices of an affine cross-polytope as support, and remains absolutely irreducible after every nonsingular monomial substitution in its intrinsic variables.
The original univariate parents on the retained subsequence are reciprocal up to a Laurent unit, with either sign under reversal.
Consequently a sequence of genuinely nonreciprocal univariate parents can only give ranks at most \(r-1\).
These conclusions also apply to bounded coefficient lengths and \(2r\) terms, on passing to one [fixed coefficient tuple](Yin_L_G0462_v193.md#length).

Work with the first nonzero restricted parent \(L=F(\mathbf X^D)\) on the rational span of the retained primitive exponent directions, before taking the further graph section to remove redundant variables.
The reciprocal factor \(H\) there and its version in the intrinsic support lattice have the same Newton polytope up to affine lattice coordinates.
Write \(N(G)\) for the convex hull of the exponents of a Laurent polynomial \(G\).
The polytope \(N(H)\) is centrally symmetric and \(r\)-dimensional.
It has at least \(2r\) vertices because its opposite vertex pairs span \(r\) dimensions about its center.
Writing \(L=HV\), the [Newton product formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#rank) gives \(N(L)=N(H)+N(V)\).
The normal fan of this sum refines that of \(N(H)\), since the face exposed by each linear functional is the sum of the two exposed faces.
There are at least \(2r\) vertex cones in the refinement and at most \(2r\) terms in \(L\), so equality holds throughout.
In the full ambient dual each old vertex cone therefore contains exactly one new full-dimensional cone, and that closed cone equals the old one because the fan covers the dual space.
In particular the common lineality spaces are equal, so the polytopes have the same span of differences even in a larger ambient torus.
The \(r\) opposite vertex pairs of \(N(H)\) give independent directions, making it an affine cross-polytope.
Thus \(N(L)\) has the same face incidences, and all its support points are vertices.

<a id="isogenies"></a>
## Triangular faces and absolute irreducibility

Every Laurent polynomial with support exactly the vertices of a polytope combinatorially equivalent to an \(r\)-dimensional cross-polytope, \(r\ge3\), is absolutely irreducible, and so are its nonsingular monomial pullbacks in characteristic zero.
Indeed any Newton polytope summand \(S\) determines a vertex \(s_x\) for each vertex \(x\) of the full polytope, by the unique decomposition of an exposed vertex in a Minkowski sum.
Along an oriented edge \(xy\), its summand difference \(s_y-s_x\) is \(\lambda_{xy}(y-x)\) with \(0\le\lambda_{xy}\le1\), because the two exposed summands of that edge are parallel segments or points.
Around a triangular face telescoping shows that its three edge ratios are equal, by independence of two edge directions.
All edges of a cross-polytope of rank at least three are connected by such triangles.
At a vertex any two incident edges with nonopposite other ends lie on a triangle, and for opposite other ends an edge using a third axis connects the two triangles.
The edge graph itself is connected, so there is one ratio \(\lambda\), and \(s_x-\lambda x\) is constant on its vertices.
Every vertex of \(S\) occurs as an exposed summand of a vertex of the full polytope by a perturbation of the exposing functional.
Hence all summands are homothetic copies of the full polytope up to translation, with a point allowed at scale zero.

If both polynomial factors are nonunits, both Newton summands have positive scale and the initial polynomials of both factors on any exposed triangular face are nonunits.
Their product is the face polynomial, a trinomial with two independent exponent differences.
Such a trinomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb C\) in the Laurent ring of the entire ambient torus.
After removing a monomial, complete its two exponent vectors to a rational basis, and clear denominators in the inverse basis map to send them to \(m e_1,m e_2\) by an injective integral lattice map, with \(m>0\).
The image \(c_0+c_1U^m+c_2V^m\) is Eisenstein as a polynomial in \(V\) at any simple root of \(c_0+c_1U^m\), with \(c_0c_1c_2\ne0\).
Gauss's lemma, Laurent localization and adjoining the remaining independent coordinates preserve its irreducibility here.
The injective substitution preserves every nonunit factor, giving the contradiction.
Nonsingular monomial substitutions preserve the support and all face incidences, proving the pullback assertion.
Applying it to \(L\) shows \(V\) is a Laurent unit, so \(L\) itself is reciprocal and is supported at the vertices of a centrally symmetric cross-polytope.

The retained primitive original directions already factor through \(D\), so reciprocity of \(L\) passes to their original univariate parents and persists on restoring the common dilations.
For a fixed multivariate \(F\) whose original intrinsic support rank was exactly \(r\), the map on its rational support-difference span is injective, since the image still has rank \(r\).
The equal sums of opposite exponents of \(L\) therefore lift to equal sums in \(F\), with the same coefficient pairing because all \(2r\) terms survive.
This makes \(F\) reciprocal as well.
A higher-rank fixed parent can acquire reciprocity on the subtorus.
For instance multiplying one vertex term of \(\sum_{i=1}^r(Y_i+Y_i^{-1})\) by a new variable \(Z\) destroys its central symmetry, while \(Z=1\) restores the model.

## The signed pairs and their primitive frequencies

Write the opposite exponents of \(L\) as \(u_i,u_i'\), \(1\le i\le r\), with \(u_i+u_i'=h\) independent of \(i\).
Their coefficients are \(c_i,\epsilon c_i\), with \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) common to all pairs because inversion twice is the identity up to the cancelling monomials.
After squaring the torus coordinates and removing \(\mathbf X^h\), the result is a monomial pullback of
\[
                 G(\mathbf Y)=\sum_{i=1}^r c_i(Y_i+\epsilon Y_i^{-1}).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The exponent vectors \(2u_i-h\) are independent, so this is an isogeny in intrinsic coordinates.
Equivalently, for an original support \(v_j\) any possible matching of its \(2r\) coefficients imposes the linear equations \((v_i+v_{i'}-v_1-v_{1'})\cdot\mathbf w=0\) on the retained directions \(\mathbf w\).
If the multiset of coefficients admits no matching for either sign, or if the projected opposite differences have rank less than \(r\) on every such solution space, only lower ranks are possible.
For integer coefficients a paired tuple has even coefficient length.
In particular, six coefficients in \(\{-1,1\}\) with five of one sign and one of the other always give a lower-rank obstruction if their factors shrink.

For the normalized univariate parents let \(0=e_0<\cdots<e_{2r-1}=D_0\) be the exponents after removing their gcd.
For sufficiently late retained indices all \(2r\) terms are distinct since the directions escape every fixed proper rational hyperplane of their span.
The pairing is sorted reversal, so \(e_j+e_{2r-1-j}=D_0\), with signs and coefficients as in (1).
If \(D_0\) is even the positive centered frequencies \(D_0/2-e_j\) for \(j<r\) have gcd one.
If \(D_0\) is odd, substituting \(X^2\) and removing \(X^{D_0}\) gives the all-odd positive frequencies \(D_0-2e_j\), also with gcd one.
Indeed the gcd of the latter list for either parity is \(\gcd(D_0,2)\), since \(\gcd(D_0,e_1,\ldots,e_{r-1})=1\).
The initial removal of common dilations preserves shrinking by the [bounded radical measure ratio](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement).
A square root of a root of the undoubled factor has measure between one half and one times its measure by the relative-degree height identity.
Thus a maximal-rank shrinking sequence gives primitive specializations of a single (1) with shrinking factors.
The centered directions escape all fixed rational hyperplanes, since the opposite difference vectors are independent in their original span and scaling directions leaves homogeneous relations unchanged.
For this \(G\) all isogeny pullbacks are irreducible by the [face argument](#isogenies).
Hence along those directions the [rational specialization conjecture](../audits/literature/AS18/Yin_U001.md#as18-u001) predicts at most one nontorsion irreducible factor with multiplicity one outside its finitely many exceptional hyperplanes.
A shrinking factor in this bounded-support family would contradict that prediction by the [whole-polynomial gap and multiplicity argument](Yin_L_G0462_v193.md#obstruction).

If the coefficient of the highest frequency \(n\) in a specialization of (1) has magnitude at least the sum of the other magnitudes, all roots lie on the unit circle.
Here take distinct positive frequencies after orienting each pair and combining any coincidences, as holds on the escaping subsequence.
For strict dominance the corresponding real cosine sum takes alternating signs at \(j\pi/n\), and the sine sum at \((j+1/2)\pi/n\), giving all \(2n\) roots on the circle.
Equality follows by increasing the magnitude of the leading pair slightly and using continuity of polynomial roots at fixed degree.
Any monic integral irreducible factor is then cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem, and any primitive nonmonic factor has logarithmic measure at least \(\log2\).

<a id="clustering"></a>
## Two clusters for every flat six-term parent with a shrinking factor

Let \(f\) be any integer polynomial with precisely six distinct exponents \(0=e_0<\cdots<e_5=D\) and coefficients \(\pm1\).
If a nontorsion irreducible factor has positive log measure \(m\to0\), then uniformly
\[
 \frac{e_1}{D},\ \frac{D-e_4}{D}\ll m^2,\qquad
 \frac{e_2}{D},\ \frac{D-e_3}{D}\ll m.                               \tag{2}
\]
In particular the first and last three exponents form two clusters separated by a gap \(D(1-O(m))\), including for nonreciprocal parents.
For the rank-three model with flat coefficients and \(n_1>n_2>n_3>0\), this gives \(n_1-n_2\ll n_1m^2,\ n_1-n_3\ll n_1m\).
Thus both actual three-term blocks have width \(O(n_1m)\), and one is the signed reversal of the other.

The individual factor \(P\) is a monic unit polynomial after normalization, and for \(m<\log\theta\) it is reciprocal by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions), where \(\theta>1\) is the plastic number.
For the top endpoint set \(w=D-e_4\), \(g=e_4-e_3\) and \(d=\deg P\).
The exterior house log \(t=\max_{P(\alpha)=0}\log|\alpha|>0\) satisfies \(wt\le\log6\) by comparison with the leading term.
By reciprocity it bounds the absolute value of every conjugate log modulus.
The [all-root square bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) and the [sparse divisor estimate](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) give \(1/10\le2dmt\) and \(m\gg d/D\), as in the [endpoint estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v188.md#finite).
Thus \(t\gg1/(Dm^2)\) and \(w\ll Dm^2\) from this endpoint alone.

The leading two terms form a shifted signed binomial with width \(w\) and zeros on the unit circle.
The remaining block has length at most four and is separated by \(g\).
If \(g>8w+8\), apply the [two-block capacity inequality before the common relative-gap estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#application), with \(j=\lfloor(g-5w)/2\rfloor\), \(D'=j+3w\), and \(\lambda=(j+3w/2)/D'\).
Here \(j>g/8,\ D'\ge j,\ \lambda\ge1/2\), and \(g-2w>4\) supplies the size condition there with relative gap \((g-2w)/D\).
The inequality reads \(d\ge k\lambda^2D'/(384\log(2\cdot4^{10k}))\) for an integer \(k\ge1\), so \(d\gg g\) and \(g\ll Dm\).
Otherwise \(g\le8w+8\le16w\), giving the same bound for small \(m\).
Reversal of the parent preserves \(P\) up to a unit and exchanges its endpoints, yielding the other two bounds in (2).
For either endpoint there is the additional restriction \(g/D\ll m^2\log(1+4g/w)\) with \(w,g\) measured inward from that end.
Indeed at a maximal exterior root the leading binomial has modulus at least \(\exp(wt)-1\ge wt\) after removing its monomial, so \(wt\exp(gt)\le4\).
The inequality \(u\exp(u)\le v\) for \(u,v\ge0\) implies \(u\le\log(1+v)\), and the lower bound on \(t\) gives the assertion.

## A primitive flat model with two nontorsion factors

For \(C_0=2,\ C_1=Y,\ C_{j+1}=YC_j-C_{j-1}\), the specialization of the plus model with trace \(C_{14}-C_{10}+C_9\) has primitive exponents \(0,4,5,23,24,28\) after translation.
The recurrence gives \(C_{14}-C_{10}+C_9=Y(Y^2-3)AB\), where
\[
 A=Y^5-Y^4-5Y^3+5Y^2+4Y-3,\qquad
 B=Y^6+Y^5-5Y^4-5Y^3+4Y^2+4Y+1 .
\]
The successive remainders of \(B,A\) modulo two are \(Y+1,1\), giving coprimality over \(\mathbb Q\).
Since \(A(-3)=-159,\ A(-2)=1\), the first trace factor has a root below \(-2\).
In fact \(X^5A(X+X^{-1})=X^{10}-X^9+X^7-X^6+X^5-X^4+X^3-X+1\), Lehmer's polynomial evaluated at \(-X\).
Write \(B=1+g\) with \(g=Y(Y+1)^2(Y-1)(Y-2)(Y+2)\).
For \(Y=-x\), \(0\le x\le1\), one has \(|g|=x(1-x)(1-x^2)(4-x^2)<1\).
Outside this interval \(g<0\) only on \((-2,-1)\) and \((1,2)\).
On each of those intervals \((\log|g|)''<0\) by the real linear factors, so \(g=-1\) has at most two solutions counted with multiplicity.
As \(B\) has degree six it has a nonreal root.
Torsion roots have traces \(X+X^{-1}\) in \([-2,2]\), so each of \(A,B\) supplies a nontorsion rational irreducible factor after trace substitution.
The two factors are distinct by coprimality.
This primitive example separates irreducibility of every multivariate pullback from irreducibility of each individual univariate specialization.
