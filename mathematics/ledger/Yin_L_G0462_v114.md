# An integral positive-real-part function at the limit of a delay-two Salem construction

<a id="input"></a>
## Normalizing the sign and the pole

Let \(f_i\) be monic minimal polynomials of Pisot numbers \(\theta_i\), and put \(q_i=|f_i(0)|\).
Suppose the multiplicative measures of the actual polynomials \(z^2 f_i\pm f_i^*\) are strictly greater than one and tend to one.
The [delay and seed bounds](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v27.md#statement) allow extraction at \(q_i=q\in\{1,2\}\) with \(\theta_i\) converging in a bounded interval separated from one.
The seeds may be taken distinct: the measures at finitely many seeds and two signs cannot approach one strictly from above.
Reciprocal seeds up to sign give a cyclotomic multiple of \(f_i\), of measure \(\theta_i\), and can be discarded.
Put \(e_i=\operatorname{sgn} f_i(0)\).
At every delay the choice \(z^N f_i-e_i f_i^*\) has measure at least \(\theta_i\).
Indeed each interior conjugate has modulus at least \(q_i/\theta_i\ge1/\theta_i\), by their product and the unit-disk bound.
There is no real zero in \([0,1/\theta_i]\), as equality at the right end would force reciprocity by irreducibility.
So \(f_i(1/\theta_i)\) has the sign of its constant, the displayed polynomial is negative at \(\theta_i\), and it has a larger positive real root.
This also works for a degree-one seed.
The remaining sign is exactly Boyd's sign \(H_i=z^2 f_i+e_i f_i^*\).

Write \(F_i=e_i f_i/f_i^*\), \(r_i=1/\theta_i\) and \(b_r(z)=(r-z)/(1-rz)\).
The function \(B_i=b_{r_i}F_i\) is a finite Blaschke product up to sign, since it is rational and analytic on the closed disk, and has boundary modulus one.
The actual \(H_i\) has just one simple exterior root \(\alpha_i>1\) and one simple interior root \(\tau_i=1/\alpha_i\).
For the root count, damping the reciprocal summand by \(0<t<1\) gives precisely one exterior root of \(z^2 f_i+t e_i f_i^*\) by circle Rouché.
The limit has at most one, and positive measure excess supplies one.
It is real and positive: an interior root solves \(b_{r_i}(z)=-z^2 B_i(z)\), and a negative real \(-1<z<0\) cannot solve this equation since \(b_{r_i}(z)>|z|>|z|^2\).
The reciprocal symmetry gives the root pair and \(M(H_i)=\alpha_i\to1\).
No zero of \(f_i^*\) is a zero of \(H_i\), by coprimality.
Define \(T_i=\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1}\).

<a id="positive"></a>
## Removing the two off-circle poles before passing to the limit

For one input suppress \(i\) and define
\[
       J(z)=\frac{1-z^2F(z)}{1+z^2F(z)},\qquad
       2K(z)=(T-z-z^{-1})J(z)+z^{-1}-z .
\]
The pole at zero cancels since \(J(0)=1,\ J'(0)=0\).
The other possible disk pole of \(J\) is the simple pole at \(\tau\), canceled by \(T-z-z^{-1}\).
Thus \(K\) is analytic in the disk and \(K(0)=T/2\).

We claim \(K\) has positive real part there.
Let \(w=-z^2F\) and \(h=b_r^{-1}\circ(-z^2B)\), so \(w=(b_r\circ h)/b_r\).
The map \(h\) is a finite Blaschke product of degree at least two, with interior fixed point \(\tau\).
At a circle point \(\xi\) with \(w(\xi)=1\), one has \(h(\xi)=\xi\).
The angular derivative of \(h\) at \(\xi\) is greater than one.
In fact conjugate the map by a disk automorphism taking \(\tau\) to zero.
Angular derivatives at corresponding circle fixed points are unchanged by the chain rule.
The conjugated product has a factor \(z\) and a remaining nonconstant finite Blaschke product, and the angular derivative is the sum of the positive Poisson kernels of its factors.
Consequently the phase derivative of \(w\) at \(\xi\) is \(D b_r(\xi)(D h(\xi)-1)>0\), where \(D\) denotes angular derivative.
The pole of \(J=(1+w)/(1-w)\) there is simple with positive Herglotz weight \(1/Dw(\xi)\), in the convention of the kernel \((\xi+z)/(\xi-z)\).
The factor \(T-\xi-\bar\xi>0\) keeps each weight of \(2K\) positive.
Away from these poles \(K\) has purely imaginary boundary values.
Subtract the Herglotz kernels with these weights: the residual rational function is analytic on the closed disk and has zero real part on the circle and hence throughout the disk.
This proves the claim and gives an actual positive measure for \(K\).

<a id="limit"></a>
## Classification when the measure has a continuous part

On an extracted subsequence the signed Pisot quotients \(F_i\) converge meromorphically on compact subsets of the disk to a rational \(F=A/Q\) in the bounded-pole Pisot class.
Here \(F(0)=q\), \(Q(0)=1\) is the reciprocal polynomial of the limiting Pisot pole parameter, \(A,Q\) are integral, and \(|F|<1\) except at finitely many circle points because the input quotients are distinct.
These are the [compactness and derived-set criteria of Boyd and Mauldin](../audits/literature/BM96/Yin_U001.md#bm96-u001).
The topology implies coefficient convergence near zero, and all coefficients of \(F_i\) and \(F\) there are integers.

Since \(T_i\to2\), the positive-real functions \(K_i\) converge after extraction on compact subdisks to a positive-real function \(K\) with constant one.
Its coefficients are integers.
Indeed \(J_i=1+2z^2 U_i\) with \(U_i\in\mathbb Z[[z]]\), and for each fixed index its Taylor coefficients stabilize by convergence of the integral coefficients of \(F_i\).
In the formula for \(K_i\), substitute \(T=2\) in the limit.
At positive indices the only extra terms from the Taylor constant occur at index one and combine to \(-2z\) before halving.
In particular \(K'(0)=q-1\).
The identity of rational germs becomes
\[
 K=\frac{(1-z)(1+zF)}{1+z^2F},\qquad
 F=\frac{K-1+z}{z(1-z-zK)} .                                    \tag{1}
\]
On the circle, away from finitely many exceptions, \(J=(1-z^2F)/(1+z^2F)\) has strictly positive real part because \(|F|<1\).
As \(2-z-z^{-1}=|1-z|^2\) there, \(K\) has strictly positive boundary real part almost everywhere.
Its representing probability has a nonzero absolutely continuous part, for example on an arc of continuity via the Poisson integral.
The [integral Herglotz classification with a continuous part](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) therefore gives
\[
           K=1,\qquad K=1+\epsilon z^m,\qquad
           K=(1+\epsilon z^m)^{-1}\quad(m\ge1,\ \epsilon=\pm1).
\]
Here only the classification preceding the special seam restrictions in that result is used.
For \(q=2\), the first derivative forces \(K=1+z\) or \((1-z)^{-1}\).
The corresponding quotients are \(2/(1-2z-z^2)\) and \((2-z)/(1-3z+z^2)\).
For \(q=1\) the list comprises \(K=1\) and the indicated nonconstant models with \(m\ge2\).
The constant model gives \(F=1/(1-2z)\), with pole at \(1/2\).

## How the delay-two excess controls the length of the dyadic prefix

Suppose the limiting quotient is \(F_*=1/(1-2z)\).
For each input let \(s\) be its first Taylor discrepancy from \(F_*\), so \(s\to\infty\).
It exists since a seed quotient has boundary modulus one, whereas \(F_*\) has strict modulus loss on an arc.
Set \(t=T-2>0\).
For \(F_*\) one has \(J_*=(1-2z-z^2)/(1-z)^2=1-2z^2/(1-z)^2\).
The actual \(J\) agrees with \(J_*\) through degree \(s+1\) since the first discrepancy of \(F\) is at \(s\).
The integer comparison \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2 F)\) has \(K^0-1=z((1-2z)F-1)/(1+z^2 F)\), which starts at degree \(s+1\).
As \(K=K^0+(t/2)J\) for the actual positive-real function of this input, its coefficients obey
\[
          K(0)=1+t/2,\qquad K_n=-t(n-1) \quad(1\le n\le s).
\]
Writing \(\mu\) for its positive representing measure, nonnegativity of
\(\int|1+v+\cdots+v^s|^2 d\mu/(s+1)\) gives
\[
  0\le 1+\frac t2-t\sum_{n=1}^s (1-n/(s+1))(n-1)
       =1+\frac t2-\frac{t\,s(s-1)}6 .
\]
Thus \((T-2)(s(s-1)-3)\le6\) for every such actual input.
In particular \((\alpha-1)s\) stays bounded on escape.
This inequality alone allows both the prefix length and the degree to grow.
