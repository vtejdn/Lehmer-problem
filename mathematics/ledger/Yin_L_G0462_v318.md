# Recovering two exponent residues after deleting two coefficients

<a id="residues"></a>
## A quadratic equation at reciprocal residue roots

Let \(P\) be the reciprocal minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), and fix nonzero rational integer coefficients in a relation among powers of \(\alpha\).
Suppose a prime \(p\) divides both coefficients erased in the [two-term projection](Yin_L_G0462_v313.md#recovery).
Select two surviving labeled coefficients whose reductions \(a,b\) modulo \(p\) are nonzero, and fix the exponents at every other surviving label.
Choose a root \(\beta\) of \(P\bmod p\) in a finite field and let \(n\) be its multiplicative order.
The reciprocal polynomial also vanishes at \(\beta^{-1}\), and both evaluations of the Laurent relation are valid since its product by a suitable monomial is an integer polynomial divisible by the monic polynomial \(P\).
Write \(U,V\) for the negatives of the fixed surviving sums evaluated at \(\beta,\beta^{-1}\), respectively.
The missing residue powers \(x=\beta^i,y=\beta^j\) then satisfy
\[
 ax+by=U,\qquad a/x+b/y=V.
\]
Substitution of \(y=(U-ax)/b\) gives
\[
 aVx^2+(b^2-a^2-UV)x+aU=0.
\]
Unless \(U=V=0\) and \(a^2=b^2\), the polynomial is nonzero, so there are at most two ordered residue pairs \((i,j)\bmod n\).
In the exceptional case \(y=-(a/b)x\), so there are at most \(n\) ordered residue pairs, and each has \(y/x=\pm1\).
In a prescribed interval of integer span \(W\), each residue has at most \(L=1+\lfloor W/n\rfloor\) representatives, giving respective bounds \(2L^2\) and \(nL^2\) for the number of exponent pairs.

<a id="count"></a>
## A bound for classes up to translation

Assume all \(r\ge5\) original exponents are distinct, occupy a window of span at most \(W\), and occur in a fixed total order of the labeled coefficients, including any marked coefficient.
Assume also that the erased pair has nonzero sum in characteristic zero, as it does in a nondegenerate relation with more than two terms.
If \(n>2W\), the number of full lists up to translation is at most \(2\binom W{r-5}\).
Indeed, among the \(r-4\) fixed surviving labels, translate the smallest exponent to zero.
The fixed order assigns their other \(r-5\) exponents to a subset of \([1,W]\) in at most one way.
Both unknown surviving exponents then lie in \([-W,W]\), in which each residue has at most one representative.
Their actual difference is nonzero and has magnitude at most \(W\), so \(\beta^{2(i-j)}\ne1\) and the exception is impossible.
There are at most two choices for the pair, and the [characteristic-zero recovery](Yin_L_G0462_v313.md#recovery) gives at most one completion for each choice in the fixed order.
For lists marked at zero, each translation class determines the marked representative uniquely.
Application to a shrinking sequence requires a common coefficient divisor and a residue root of sufficiently large order for the tuple and window in question.

## Six distinct residues in the Lehmer computation

In the [Lehmer projection computation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0180/readme.md#projection), every one of the 188 lists passing the residual parity test has six distinct residues modulo 31.
Indeed the [binary quotient calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0180/projection.py) gives \(P\bmod 2\mid X^{31}-1\), and \(P(1)\) is odd, so any residue root has order 31.
The six signed surviving coefficients all reduce to one.
For four nonzero field elements in characteristic two whose sum and inverse sum both vanish, take any partition into pairs.
Their pair sums agree, and their reciprocal pair sums agree.
If the common pair sum vanishes, the two entries of each pair coincide.
Otherwise the reciprocal pair sum equals the pair sum divided by the pair product, so the two pairs have the same product and agree as unordered pairs.
Thus the four entries can always be partitioned into equal pairs.
If two of the six residues coincide, canceling their powers leaves four elements with zero sum and zero inverse sum, so all six would occur in equal pairs.
Among the distinct integers from 0 through 32, only residues 0 and 1 can be repeated modulo 31, each at most twice.
Three equal pairs are therefore impossible.
Given four of the six residues, the remaining two powers have nonzero sum and their product is determined by their reciprocal sum, so at most one unordered pair of residues completes them.
