# The exact two-adic precision of dyadic trace norms is eventually affine on progressions

<a id="valuation"></a>
## Divisibility on every dyadic subsequence of a fixed reciprocal base

Fix a monic reciprocal minimal polynomial \(Q\) of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), of degree \(D=2b\), and put \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
Write \(h\) for the multiplicity of \(x-1\) in \(Q\bmod2\).
For \(J=2^k,\ k\ge2\), define
\[
          V_k=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{2^{k-2}}+\alpha^{-2^{k-2}}),
          \qquad U_k=2^{-h/2}V_k.
\]
Eventually \(U_k\) is an odd signed integer and the odd part \(W_k\) of \(|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{J/2}+1)|\) is \(U_k^2\), by the [trace identity](Yin_L_G0462_v83.md#trace-norm) and the [eventual two-adic valuation](Yin_L_G0462_v84.md#sign).
Let \(\epsilon=(-1)^s\), where \(s\) counts with multiplicity the monic self-reciprocal irreducible factors of \(Q\bmod2\) other than \(x-1\).

There is a positive integer \(T\) depending on \(Q\), and for each residue class \(a\bmod T\) there are integers \(1\le e_a\le2^b\) and \(c_a\) such that for every sufficiently large \(k\equiv a\bmod T\),
\[
                  v_2(W_k-1)=1+v_2(U_k-\epsilon)= e_a k+c_a.            \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(v_2(W_k-1)=O_Q(\log J)\) on the entire sequence, including the orders prescribed after irreducible substitutions \(Q(z^p)\) for primes \(p\) in the [odd-norm criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#criterion).
At fixed base even the largest power of two dividing \(W_k-1\) can only give a lower bound of order \(\log J\) for \(\log W_k\) by divisibility alone.
The criterion requires order \(d\) with \(d\le J<2d\), and the actual \(\log W_k\) on each fixed base does grow on the scale \(Jm(Q)\).
The implied constant and the index from which (1) holds may depend on the base.

<a id="interpolation"></a>
## A finite sum of two-adic exponentials

Fix an embedding of the splitting field of \(Q\) in a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), with \(v(2)=1\).
Choose representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_b\) of the inverse pairs of roots of \(Q\).
Write \(x_i=\zeta_i u_i\) with \(\zeta_i\) the odd-order lift of the residue and \(v(u_i-1)>0\), as in the [Teichmüller factorization](Yin_L_G0462_v84.md#telescoping).
Choose \(n_0\) such that \(v(u_i^{2^{n_0}}-1)\ge2\) for all \(i\), using \(v(y^2-1)\ge\min(2v(y-1),1+v(y-1))\).
Choose \(T\) with \(2^T\equiv1\) modulo each order of \(\zeta_i\), treating order one as imposing no condition.
On a fixed class of \(k\bmod T\), \(\lambda_i=\zeta_i^{2^{k-2}}\) is constant for \(k\ge2\).
Put \(L_i=\log(u_i^{2^{n_0}})\) in the chosen two-adic field.
The usual series \(\log(1+y)=\sum_{m\ge1}(-1)^{m-1}y^m/m\) converges at \(v(y)\ge2\) with value of valuation at least two.
At such arguments the formal exponential and logarithm identities may be evaluated by convergent series, since \(v(m!)\le m-1\) and the exponential terms have valuations tending to infinity.

On the chosen class form the analytic function on \(|t|_2\le1\)
\[
        E_a(t)=2^{-h/2}\prod_{i=1}^b
              \left(\lambda_i\exp(tL_i)+\lambda_i^{-1}\exp(-tL_i)\right).
\]
At \(t=2^{k-2-n_0}\) its value is \(U_k\) for every \(k\ge2+n_0\) in that class, by taking ordinary integral powers after exponentiation.
The [Frobenius-orbit telescoping product](Yin_L_G0462_v84.md#telescoping) gives \(E_a(0)=\epsilon\), since residue-one pairs each contribute two, canceled by \(2^{-h/2}\), and the other orbits have products \(1\) or \(-1\).

The function \(E_a(t)-\epsilon\) is not identically zero.
Indeed the [signed resultant bound (2)](Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) gives \(\log|N(\alpha^{J/2}+1)|=(J/2)m(Q)+O_Q(\log J)\) for all \(J=2^k\).
It applies directly to \(Q\), which has no root of unity.
As the total norm equals \(V_k^2\) in absolute value, \(|U_k|\to\infty\) in the ordinary absolute value along each class.

Expanding \(E_a(t)-\epsilon\) gives at most \(2^b+1\) exponentials \(C_i\exp(\nu_i t)\), counting the subtracted constant.
Combine equal \(\nu_i\) and omit zero coefficients, leaving \(r\) distinct exponents.
If the first \(r\) coefficients vanished, multiplying coefficients of degrees \(0,\ldots,r-1\) by their factorials would give \(\sum_i C_i\nu_i^j=0\) for all \(0\le j<r\).
The characteristic-zero Vandermonde determinant is nonzero, so all \(C_i\) would vanish.
Consequently the first nonzero coefficient \(c\) has degree \(1\le e_a\le r-1\le2^b\).
Factoring out \(t^{e_a}\) leaves an analytic function tending to \(c\ne0\) at zero.
For all sufficiently large \(k\) in the class the ultrametric inequality now gives \(v_2(U_k-\epsilon)=e_a(k-2-n_0)+v(c)\) exactly.
The left side and \(e_a(k-2-n_0)\) are integers, so \(v(c)\) is an integer.
It follows also that \(v_2(U_k+\epsilon)=1\) eventually, proving (1).

<a id="windows"></a>
## Fixed polynomial combinations of neighboring norms

Fix nonnegative shifts \(r_1,\ldots,r_u\) and a rational polynomial \(H\) in \(u\) variables.
On each residue class modulo the same \(T\), the value \(H(U_{k+r_1},\ldots,U_{k+r_u})\) either vanishes at every sufficiently large index in that class or has eventually affine two-adic valuation \(e k+c\) with integers \(e\ge0,c\).
The statement also applies with \(W\) in any of the arguments, since \(W_k=U_k^2\) eventually.
For fixed \(k\equiv a\bmod T\), substitute \(E_{a+r_i}(2^{r_i}t)\) for each argument.
The result is a finite sum of exponentials, analytic at zero.
An identically zero expression gives the first alternative, while its first nonzero coefficient and the same valuation argument give the second, allowing degree zero.
This covers a fixed number of neighboring norms and a fixed polynomial, including exact relations among them.

<a id="slopes"></a>
## Bases exhibiting different slopes

For \(Q=x^2-3x+1\), the [quadratic recurrence](Yin_L_G0462_v83.md#squarefree) gives \(U_k=b_{k-2}\), \(b_0=3,\ b_{j+1}=b_j^2-2\).
Here \(b_1=7\) and \(b_j\equiv-1\bmod8\) for \(j\ge1\).
Thus \(v_2(b_{j+1}+1)=v_2(b_j+1)+v_2(b_j-1)=v_2(b_j+1)+1\).
It follows that \(v_2(W_k-1)=k+1\) at every \(k\ge3\).

If \(Q\bmod2=(x-1)^D\), all \(\lambda_i=1\) and \(T\) may be taken to be one.
Every factor of \(E_0(t)\) is even in \(t\), so the single positive slope in (1) is even.
An example within \(m(Q)\le1\) is \(Q=x^4-2x^3-2x+1\), whose trace polynomial is \(y^2-2y-2\) with roots \(1\pm\sqrt3\).
The positive conjugate greater than two gives one exterior positive root, while \(1-\sqrt3\in(-2,2)\) yields a circle pair.
The relative quadratic is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\), since its discriminant is negative at the latter real embedding.
This gives a Salem unit of degree four.
Its exterior root is less than \(5/2\), since \(1+\sqrt3<5/2+2/5\), and hence its logarithmic Mahler measure is less than one.

For this quartic the slope is exactly two and \(v_2(W_k-1)=2k-2\) for every \(k\ge5\).
To verify it, start with \(t_0=1+\sqrt3,\ t'_0=1-\sqrt3\) and iterate \(x\mapsto x^2-2\) on both, writing \(S_n=t_n+t'_n,\ P_n=t_nt'_n\).
Exponent doubling gives \(P_n=V_{n+2}\).
The integer recurrences \(S_{n+1}=S_n^2-2P_n-4,\ P_{n+1}=(P_n+2)^2-2S_n^2\) from \(S_0=2,P_0=-2\) give \(S_3=772,P_3=-1532\).
Put \(s=S_n/4-1,\ r=P_n/4-1\) for \(n\ge3\).
Their respective next values are \(8s+4s^2-2r\) and \(12r-16s+4r^2-8s^2\).
At \(n=3\), \(v_2(s)=2n,\ v_2(r)=2n+1\).
In the two recurrences the uniquely least-valuation terms are then \(-2r\) and \(12r\), respectively, proving the same formulas by induction.
Thus \(U_k=P_{k-2}/4\) has \(v_2(U_k-1)=2k-3\), as claimed on squaring.
