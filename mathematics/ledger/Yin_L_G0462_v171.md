# A reciprocal family separating constant denominators from two nonconstant rows

<a id="family"></a>
## Simultaneous tests on a single factor

Let \(n>1\) be squarefree with all prime factors at least five, let \(u\ge3\), and put \(k=2u+1\).
Set
\[
 U(Z)=Z^k+Z^u+1,\qquad V(Z)=Z^k+Z^{u+1}+1,\qquad
 E(x)=U(x^n)+xV(x^n),\qquad D=kn+1 .
\]
As \(\min(n,u)\to\infty\), there is an absolute integer \(K\ge1\) and a reciprocal irreducible nontorsion factor \(P\) of \(E\), of degree \(D/K\le d\le D\), with the following properties for every \(p\mid n\) prime.
Writing \(\alpha\) for a root of \(P\) and \(\beta=\alpha^p\), the fields \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) coincide.
The index \(I_p=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\beta]]\) and the least positive integer \(b_p\) with \(b_p\alpha\in\mathbb Z[\beta]\) satisfy
\[
  I_p\mid 3^{n(p-1)/2},\qquad
  v_3(b_p)\ \ge\ \frac{p(n-1)}{nK}-1 .                              \tag{1}
\]
In particular the exponent of the additive order quotient is a multiple of \(b_p\), while \(\log I_p/(pd)=O(1/u)\).
The same lower bound applies to the normalized log resultant divided by \(\log 3\) of every adjacent two-row witness with a constant stripped row and positive stripped degree sum, even when \(P\) divides it only once.
In the [fourth-logarithmic window](#window), witnesses with two nonconstant rows and exact multiplicity two can have normalized log resultant \(o(p)\) on every prescribed pair.

<a id="reciprocity"></a>
## Measuring the whole reciprocal polynomial

Reversal exchanges \(U\) and \(V\), so \(E\) is reciprocal.
Their difference \(V-U=Z^u(Z-1)\) gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|=3\) by evaluation at zero and one.
For the fixed three-variable polynomial
\[
  \mathcal E(z,X,Y)=(1+z)(1+XY^2)+Y(1+zX)
\]
the specialization \((z,X,Y)=(z,z^n,z^{un})\) gives \(E\).
Each fixed nonconstant torus character has zero mean on the specialization for sufficiently large \(n,u\), since \(a+n(b+uc)=0\) with fixed integer exponents forces first \(a=0\), then \(b=c=0\) in that range.
Continuous approximation by trigonometric polynomials applied to \(\log\max(\varepsilon,|\mathcal E|)\), followed by \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), gives \(\limsup m(E)\le m(\mathcal E)\).
Here and below \(m\) is the normalized torus integral of the logarithm of the absolute value.

For \(z=e^{it}\ne-1\) and \(|X|=1\), replacing \(Y\sqrt X\) by \(W\) gives the quadratic \((1+z)(W^2+qW+1)\).
Its middle coefficient \(q=(1+zX)/((1+z)\sqrt X)\) is real and \(|q|\le1/|\cos(t/2)|\), by the half-angle formula.
Integration over \(W\) contributes \(\log|1+z|+\operatorname{arcosh}(\max(1,|q|/2))\).
The first term has mean zero.
Consequently
\[
 m(\mathcal E)\le\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/6}\operatorname{arcosh}
                      \!\left(\frac{1}{2\sin t}\right)dt
 \le \frac{1+\log2}{3}-\frac16+\frac{\sqrt3}{4\pi}<0.54 .
\]
For the second inequality use \(\operatorname{arcosh}(v)\le\log(2v)-1/(4v^2)\) for \(v\ge1\), from \(\sqrt{1-v^{-2}}\le1-1/(2v^2)\) and \(\log(1-s)\le-s\).
On the integration interval \(\sin t\ge3t/\pi\), so the integrand is at most \(\log(\pi/(3t))-\sin^2 t\).
The displayed bound is strictly below \(2\log\theta\), where \(\theta>1.32\) is the positive root of \(x^3-x-1\).
Any nonreciprocal nontorsion factor of \(E\) would occur with its distinct reversal and cost at least \(2\log\theta\) by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
Thus all its nontorsion factors are reciprocal for sufficiently large \(n,u\).

The top block \(x^{kn}(1+x)\) and the remainder \(x^{un}(1+x^{n+1})+1+x\) have coefficient lengths at most four.
The leading block has width one, its only root is \(-1\), and the separation minus twice its width is \(un-3\ge D/4\).
The [separated-block factor bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement) gives an absolute \(c>0\) with every nontorsion factor of degree at least \(cD\) and an absolute positive lower bound on its measure.
Choose \(K\ge1/c\); then there are at most \(K\) such factors, counting multiplicities.

## Interpolation at three

For any \(p\mid n\), the relation \(\alpha=-U(\alpha^n)/V(\alpha^n)\) proves degree preservation since \(U,V\) are coprime.
In characteristic three the \(n-1\) nonidentity elements \(a\) of \(\mu_n\) are simple roots of \(E\).
Indeed \(U(1)=V(1)=3\), \(U'(1)\equiv1\), \(V'(1)\equiv-1\bmod3\), and \(E'(a)=na^{n-1}(1-a)\ne0\) in the residue field.
They lift to distinct simple roots in an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_3\).
Each lift is nontorsion, since the order of a torsion lift would be divisible by some \(p\mid n\) with \(p\ge5\), and its \(p\)-th power would lower the degree by the cyclotomic degree formula.
The degree preservation argument applies to every root of \(E\).
Choose one irreducible \(P\) containing at least \((n-1)/K\) marked lifts and keep this choice for all \(p\mid n\).

Fix one such \(p\) and set \(\nu=n/p\).
The powered marks have at most \(\nu\) different residues, so some residue has \(r\ge p(n-1)/(nK)\) of them.
Let \(c\) be its Teichmüller lift, so \(c^\nu=1\).
For the selected roots \(\alpha_i\), write
\[
  Z_i=\nu c^{-1}(\alpha_i^p-c)/3 .
\]
These are integral, and \((\alpha_i^n-1)/3\equiv Z_i\bmod 3\) by binomial expansion.
Taylor expansion of \(U,V\) at one in \(E(\alpha_i)/3\) now gives
\[
  1+\bar Z_i+\bar\alpha_i(1-\bar Z_i)=0,\qquad
  \bar\alpha_i=(\bar Z_i+1)/(\bar Z_i-1).
\]
In particular the \(\bar Z_i\) are distinct and none equals \(1\) or \(-1\).
For \(r\ge2\) the interpolant of degree \(<r\) taking \(Z_i\) to \(\alpha_i\) has integral coefficients by the unit Vandermonde determinant.
Its leading coefficient is a unit: a reduction \(T\) of degree at most \(r-2\) would give \((Z-1)T=Z+1\) at \(r\) distinct points, hence identically, which fails at one.

For \(b\alpha=W(\beta)\) with \(b\) a nonzero rational integer and \(W\in\mathbb Z[X]\), take the remainder of \(W(c+3cZ/\nu)\) modulo the monic \(\prod_i(Z-Z_i)\).
Its coefficient of \(Z^{r-1}\) is divisible by \(3^{r-1}\), since every term originally of degree at least \(r-1\) carries this power and division takes place over the valuation ring.
The remainder is \(b\) times the preceding interpolant, proving \(v_3(b)\ge r-1\), also trivial if \(r=1\).
After stripping the two row monomials, adjacent residues give more generally \(W(\beta)=b\alpha^{\pm1}\beta^t\) for some \(t\in\mathbb Z\), up to sign, when one row is constant.
The reductions of the right-hand values divided by \(b\) are a nonzero scalar times the same fractional linear function or its inverse, since \(\bar\beta=\bar c\) at every mark in the disk.
The same interpolation proof applies, evaluating the putative polynomial identity at its pole.
For a constant row the absolute stripped resultant equals \(|b|^{\deg W}\) and the positive stripped degree sum is \(\deg W\).
This proves the constant-row assertion.

<a id="index"></a>
## The index on the whole set of roots

Put \(A(X)=U(X^\nu)\), \(B(X)=V(X^\nu)\), so \(E(x)=A(x^p)+xB(x^p)\) and \(|\operatorname{Res}(A,B)|=3^\nu\).
Let \(a_i\) range over all \(D\) complex roots of \(E\), with multiplicity, and write \(\Delta_p(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}x^{p-1-j}y^j\).
At a root \(a_i\), \(E(\zeta a_i)=(\zeta-1)a_iB(a_i^p)\) for every nonidentity \(p\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\).
Expand the product over \(i,\zeta\) also using the monic factorization of \(E\).
The diagonal gives the same factor \(p^D\) in absolute value on both sides because \(|\prod_i a_i|=1\).
Each unordered pair of labels contributes \(|\Delta_p(a_i,a_j)|^2\) after the product over \(\zeta\), including when the labels have equal roots.
It follows that
\[
 \left|\prod_{i<j}\Delta_p(a_i,a_j)\right|^2
   =\left|\prod_i B(a_i^p)\right|^{p-1}
   =3^{n(p-1)} .
\]
The last equality uses \(|\operatorname{Res}(E,B(x^p))|=|\operatorname{Res}(A,B)|^p\) by evaluating \(E\) at the roots of \(B(x^p)\).
Restricting the unordered pairs to one copy of \(P\) gives \(I_p\) in absolute value by the power-basis Vandermonde formula.
The product over the complementary pairs is a rational algebraic integer: use separately the roots of \(P\), the roots of the monic quotient \(E/P\), and the pairs between the two multisets.
This proves the divisibility in (1).

## Placing the prescribed primes in the actual degree window

Fix \(0<\epsilon<1\) and let \(Y\to\infty\).
Take any nonempty set of primes in \([5Y/4,7Y/4]\) with product \(n\le\exp((1-\epsilon)Y^{1/4})\).
Choose \(u\) so that \(D=\exp(Y^{1/4})/3+O(n)\), by rounding the defining linear equation for \(u\).
Both \(n,u\) tend to infinity; since \(D/K\le d\le D\), \(y(d)=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil=Y+O(Y^{3/4})\).
Every prescribed prime therefore belongs to \((y(d),2y(d)]\) for this single \(P\).
For each of them the normalized log resultant of an adjacent constant-row witness divided by \(p\) is at least \((1/K-o(1))\log3\).
In contrast, \(\log I_p/(pd)\to0\) uniformly and \(m(P)\) is bounded, so the [short-denominator estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v378.md#window) supplies witnesses with two nonconstant rows and \(\log H/(Np)\to0\) for all prescribed residue pairs, with ordinary degree below \(p(3d+1)\) and exact multiplicity two.
Every admissible double-root witness in the larger comparison window still pays \(m(R)/p\ge m(P)/3\) for its noncyclotomic cofactor by the [finite cofactor comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement).
The positive individual-factor gap above makes this cost uniform throughout the family.
