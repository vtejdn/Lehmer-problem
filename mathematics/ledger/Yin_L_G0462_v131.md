# Dividing trace-power residues in an integral Salem inverse

<a id="period"></a>
## The integer denominator and its exact period

Let \(P=z^mS(z+z^{-1})\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(1<\sigma\le2\), with \(d=2m\).
Write \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(u=T-2\), and \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) for the other roots of \(S\).
Fix an integer \(b\ge2\).
For \(k\ge1\) define \(D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\), \(g_k=(x+2)(D_k-2)\), and let \(p_k\) be the integer remainder of \(g_k S'\) modulo the monic \(S\).
There is a positive integer \(r_b=r_b(S)\) such that
\[
           p_k/b\in\mathbb Z[x]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_b\mid k .
 \tag{1}
\]
This is also exactly the integrality condition for the [inverse polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#seed)
\[
        f=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}(p_k/b)(z+z^{-1}).
\]
Indeed the map from the polynomial in \(x\) back to the integral antisymmetric term has an integral inverse on its image, by monic division by \(z-1\) and the integral basis of symmetric Laurent polynomials used in the [inverse simplex](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex).

To compute (1), reduce \(g_k S'\) in \((\mathbb Z/b)[x]/S\) and test for zero.
The successful indices are nonempty.
The quadratic extension given by \(z^2-xz+1=0\) is free of rank two over this finite ring and identifies with \((\mathbb Z/b)[z]/P\), since \(z\) is invertible and \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
If \(z^k=1\) there then \(D_k-2=0\) in the base ring.
For \(a\ge1\) define \(C_a(x)=(D_a(x)-2)/(x-2)\in\mathbb Z[x]\).
The composition law gives \(g_{ak}=g_k C_a(D_k)\), so multiples of successful indices succeed.
For coprime \(a,c\), \(C_a\) and \(C_c\) generate the unit ideal in \(\mathbb Z[x]\).
In any algebraically closed field write \(x=w+w^{-1}\).
Then \(C_a(x)=w^{1-a}(1+w+\cdots+w^{a-1})^2\).
A common zero with \(w\ne1\) would have \(w^a=w^c=1\), and at \(w=1\) the two values are \(a^2,c^2\).
Thus the monic polynomials have resultant of absolute value one, with \(C_1=1\) immediate.
For any two successful indices \(k,l\), apply their integral Bézout identity with \(a=k/h,\ c=l/h,\ h=\gcd(k,l)\) at \(D_h\).
Modulo \((b,S)\) it shows \(g_h S'=0\).
The least successful index therefore divides all the others.

<a id="prime"></a>
## Prime divisors and lifting

For a prime \(\ell\), factor \(\bar S=\prod h^{a_h}\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) into monic irreducibles and put \(E_\ell=\prod_{\ell\nmid a_h}h\).
The exact test is \(E_\ell\mid(x+2)(D_k-2)\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), since \(\bar S/\gcd(\bar S,\bar S')=E_\ell\) by separability of the \(h\).
For each factor \(h\mid E_\ell\) other than \(x+2\), take one root \(\xi\) and one root \(w\) of \(w+w^{-1}=\xi\) in a finite extension.
The order of \(w\) is independent of these choices by inversion and Frobenius conjugacy.
Since \(D_k(\xi)-2=w^{-k}(w^k-1)^2\), \(r_\ell\) is exactly the least common multiple of these orders, with empty least common multiple one.
In particular if \(\bar S\) is an \(\ell\)-th power then \(r_\ell=1\).

For a prime power \(\ell^a\), (1) remains an exact test by remainder.
It suffices to test the divisors of \(N\ell^{\lceil a/2\rceil-1}\), where \(N\) is the order of \(z\) in \((\mathbb F_\ell[z]/\bar P)^\times\).
In the integral quotient \(\mathbb Z[z]/P\), \(z^N-1\) belongs to \(\ell(\mathbb Z[z]/P)\), and raising \(z^N\) successively to the \(\ell\)-th power increases the divisibility of its difference from one by at least one power each time, by the binomial theorem.
Squaring the difference gives the required divisibility of \(D_k-2\).
For general \(b\), the periods for its exact prime-power factors combine by least common multiple.
If \(b\) divides all coefficients of \(S'\), every \(k\) succeeds.

For example \(S=x^2-2x-1\) has roots \(1\pm\sqrt2\) and is a Salem trace polynomial.
Indeed \(T^2-4\) is not a square in the real trace field since it is negative at the conjugate of \(T=1+\sqrt2\).
The positive exterior root of \(z^2-Tz+1\) lies below two, and the remaining trace gives two circle conjugates.
Here \(r_2=1\), whereas \(r_4=3\).
Indeed \(S'=2(x-1)\), and after dividing the test at four by two it becomes \((x+1)^2\mid x(x+1)D_k\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
The lifts of \(x=1\) have order three.

<a id="range"></a>
## The range for a branch below two per degree

Along any sequence of Salem numbers \(\sigma_i\to1\), fix \(b\) and form only the inverses above with integral \(p_k/b\).
There are choices giving actual positive-constant Pisot seeds of the same degree with \(\theta-q\to0\) and branch \(j/d\to 2/b\) if and only if there are indices \(k\in r_b(S)\mathbb N\) for which
\[
             d=o(X),\qquad uX=o(d),\qquad X=\sigma^k .               \tag{2}
\]
Here \(q=f(0)\), \(\theta\) is the exterior Pisot root and \(j\) is the total mass of the [integer trace residues](Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#residues).

For sufficiency, the [simplex calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#seed) divides the residues \(b_j=(T-x_j)(-g_k(x_j))\) there by \(b\).
The new constant is \(q=1+[g_k(T)+\sum_{j<m}g_k(x_j)]/b\).
Since \(-16<g_k(x_j)<0\) and \(g_k(T)=(T+2)(X+X^{-1}-2)\), (2) gives \(q\sim4X/b\) and \(q/d\to\infty\).
The endpoint sum is less than \(18m/b\), so the inverse simplex gives a Pisot minimal polynomial and \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\).
The [positive root equation](Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#cost) reads
\[
 \theta-q-u=\frac{u}{\theta-1}
       +\frac1b\sum_{j<m}\frac{(T-x_j)(-g_k(x_j))}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x_j}.
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular \(\theta-q\to0\).
The branch residues at all \(m\) trace roots are \((x-2)g_k(x)/b\), by linearity of the [rational trace calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#residues).

Whenever \(k\to\infty\) and \(\log X=o(d)\), the [joint equidistribution calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#limit) gives normalized mass \(2/b+o(1)\) on the circle traces.
Its test function is \((4-x^2)(2-y)\) with \(y=D_k(x)\), and the limiting measure per degree is \(2/b\) times the semicircle probability.
The same limit holds for \((bd)^{-1}\sum_{j<m}(T-x_j)(-g_k(x_j))\), since \(T-2\to0\) and the \(g_k(x_j)\) are uniformly bounded.
In (2) we have \(k\to\infty\) and \(\log X=o(d)\), since the [Voutier estimate](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) gives \(u\ge(\log\sigma)^2>\tfrac1{16}(\log\log d/\log d)^6\), and \(d\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness.
The exterior branch mass is \(u g_k(T)/b=o(d)\), proving the claimed branch limit.

Conversely suppose the indicated integral seeds are Pisot with \(q\ge2,\ \theta-q\to0,\ j/d\to2/b\).
The [positive lower bound for the excess at fixed \(q\)](Yin_L_G0462_v113.md#nonunit) gives \(q\to\infty\).
Since \(b(q-1)\le g_k(T)\), this forces \(X\to\infty\) and \(k\to\infty\).
The positive exterior residue alone bounds \(u g_k(T)\le bj=O(d)\), so Voutier again gives \(\log X=o(d)\).
The circle mass already contributes \(2/b+o(1)\) per degree, forcing \(uX=o(d)\).
In (3), \(q/(\theta+\theta^{-1}-x_j)\to1\) uniformly in \(j\), whence \(q(\theta-q-u)/d\to2/b\).
The excess tending to zero therefore forces \(q/d\to\infty\), and \(b(q-1)\le g_k(T)\) gives \(d=o(X)\).

A sufficient condition for (2) is \(u\sigma^{r_b(S)}\to0\).
Indeed choose \(h=(u\sigma^{r_b(S)})^{-1/2}\) and take the least multiple of the period with \(\sigma^k\ge dh\).
Then \(X<dh\sigma^{r_b(S)}\), which gives both inequalities.
For example any hypothetical sequence with trace polynomials that are \(\ell\)-th powers modulo a fixed prime \(\ell\) admits branch ratio \(2/\ell\).
No existence of a Salem sequence tending to one is required for this conditional statement.

<a id="obstruction"></a>
## Bounded branch per degree forces repeated trace factors modulo the denominator

For any sequence of these divided Pisot inverses with \(b\) fixed, \(\sigma\to1\) and \(j=O(d)\), the exterior residue implies \(X=O(d/u)\).
Consequently
\[
    k=\frac{\log X}{\log\sigma}
       =O\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^4}{(\log\log d)^3}\right)
\]
by the same explicit height estimate.
For every prime \(\ell\mid b\), \(E_\ell\) divides \((x+2)(D_k-2)\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), a polynomial of degree \(k+1\).
Thus \(\bar S=A^\ell B\) with \(\deg B\le(\ell-1)(k+1)\), by extracting the largest \(\ell\)-th power from each factor multiplicity.
In particular a squarefree trace reduction modulo any prime divisor of \(b\) excludes a bounded branch ratio along such a sequence.
This obstruction concerns division of the specific trace-power remainder.
