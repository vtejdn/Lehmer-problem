# Testing the two signed rows together on a quadratic modular example

<a id="example"></a>
## A small-prime certificate that rejects the degree-131262 pair

Take the two explicit rows at \(p=293\), \(d=131262\) in the [five-term construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v467.md#example).
For these particular rows one can exclude *every* common genuine degree-\(d\) target using the signs together, without knowing the two original quadratic polynomials or expanding a graph of degree \(pb+1\).

For an integral row \(A\) set \(V=\varepsilon Y^bA(1/Y)\), and take the nonzero polynomial
\[
          W=pY(A'V-AV')-AV .
\]
Remove its scalar content and its full power of \(Y\).
If a reciprocal nontorsion unit and its square with distinct degree-\(d\) minima are genuine double factors in both graphs, the [powered-factor identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) places the powered minima in both derivative polynomials.
Genuineness recovers the original algebraic number from its powered value, so the powered minima retain degree \(d\) each.
Their unequal positive measures distinguish them.
Their product is monic with nonzero constant term and still divides both trimmed polynomials by Gauss's lemma.
At every auxiliary prime the degree of the gcd of the two reductions must therefore be at least \(2d\).
The trimmed primitive polynomials each have nonzero reduction.
This test takes the actual rows as inputs.

For the displayed rows, write \(A(Y)=Y^wB(Y^k)\) with \(w=32927,\ k=65631,\ u=-1\).
Up to sign and a nonzero scalar and monomial, the derivative polynomial is \(W_0(Y^k)\), where
\[
      W_0(T)=293T\big(B'(T)B_4^*(T)-B(T)(B_4^*)'(T)\big)+B(T)B_4^*(T).
\]
This follows by differentiating the two substituted rows and using \(293(b-2w-4k)+1=ku\).
The two base polynomials \(W_0\) reduce modulo five to the following lists in increasing order of degree, taking the same displayed sign convention for both:
\[
       (3,2,4,2,3,2,4,2,3),\qquad (3,2,0,2,2,2,0,2,3).
\]
Neither the scalar \(k\) nor the leading and constant coefficients vanish modulo five.
The difference equals \(-T^2(T^4-T^2+1)\) in that field.
On dividing the first polynomial by \(T^4-T^2+1\) and continuing the Euclidean algorithm, the next remainders have coefficient lists
\[
             (1,3,4,4),\quad (0,3,3),\quad (1,3).
\]
The root \(3\) of \(1+3T\) is not a root of \(3T+3T^2\).
Thus the two reductions are coprime.
A polynomial Bézout identity remains a Bézout identity after \(T=Y^k\), so their substituted polynomials have gcd one as well.
This rules out any common genuine pair of minima for these two specified representatives.

The [composite-modulus construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v467.md#digits) can choose different representatives for different products of primes.
A uniform degree count must control all eligible representatives and targets, including the original measure bound.
The small-prime certificate here applies only to the displayed pair.
