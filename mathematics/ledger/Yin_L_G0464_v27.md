# Congruence classes of low trace coordinates

<a id="congruence-counts"></a>
## Pairs of classes and individual classes

Use a sequence in the [full strict integral odd-grid chamber](../claims/Yin_C_G0894_v1.md#statement) with \(p=2n+1\to\infty\), \(1<M(P)\to1\), final negative block of size \(\kappa\), and \(Z=(4-t^2)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_i E_i\).
Write \(\mathbb E\) for the uniform mean on its \(n\) traces, \(V=\mathbb E|Z|\), and \(\beta=2\pi\kappa/p\).
Fix a modulus \(d\ge1\) and \(\epsilon>0\), and put
\[
 \lambda=\min(\epsilon/4,1/8),\qquad
 g=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi\lambda/2}(\sin u/u)^2\,du>0.
\]
For a set \(S\) of residue classes modulo \(d\), let \(N(S)\) count nonzero \(a_i\) with \(0\le i<\epsilon p\) and \(i\bmod d\in S\).
For every \(j\bmod d\),
\[
  \liminf\frac{\kappa}{p}N(\{j,-j-2\})\ \ge\ \frac{g}{32d}.           \tag{1}
\]
In particular (1) applies to the individual class \(j\) whenever \(d\mid 2(j+1)\).
If \(\beta\log p=o(V)\), every individual class satisfies
\[
  \liminf\frac{\kappa}{p}N(\{j\})\ \ge\ \frac{g}{32d}.                \tag{2}
\]
These bounds also count discrepancies from arbitrary reference values constant on each class modulo \(2d\), with the reference values allowed to depend on \(p\).
Thus they apply to the [neutral profiles](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-coordinates), which are constant on each parity except for index zero.
On 2026-11-16 the construction contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained the paired and individual estimates by separating the actual cut from its other images under angle multiplication.
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v66.md#congruence-count-review) checks the class pairing, the additional hypothesis for individual classes and the constants.

## A separator on a fixed endpoint interval

Put \(\theta_j=2\pi j/p\) and \(y=\pi-\theta_j\), so \(t=b_j=-2\cos y\).
The sign of \(Z\) is negative below \(y=\beta\) and positive above it, and the nearest nodes to the cut are at \(\beta\pm\pi/p\).
The [block estimates](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#endpoint-index-exponent) give \(\beta\to0\) and \(\kappa\to\infty\).
The [signed first moments](../claims/Yin_C_G0880_v2.md#moving-cut) give \(\mathbb E Z=O(1)\) and \(\mathbb E|t-a||Z|=O(1)\) for \(a=-2\cos\beta\).
Consequently \(\mathbb E(Zf(t))=O_f(1)\) for each fixed polynomial \(f\), by subtracting \(f(a)\), and \(\mathbb E({\bf1}_{y\ge\eta}|Z|)=O_\eta(1)\) for every fixed \(0<\eta<\pi\).
The [fixed-weight variation gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0880_v2.md#statement) gives \(V\to\infty\).
Set \(G=(-1)^{d-1}E_{d-1}(t)Z=(\sin(dy)/\sin y)Z\).
Since the multiplier is bounded and tends to \(d\) at zero, \(V_G=\mathbb E|G|\) satisfies \(V_G/V\to d\).

Set \(m=\lfloor\lambda p/d\rfloor\), \(\alpha=d\beta\), and apply the [Fejér interval separator](../claims/Yin_C_G0894_v1.md#separator) at angle \(dy\).
It gives the bounded function
\[
 W(y)=1-2\alpha/\pi-\frac4\pi\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}
      (1-r/m)\frac{\sin(r\alpha)}r\cos(rdy),\qquad |W|\le1 .
\]
On \(0<y<\eta<\pi/d\), the folded angle equals \(dy\), and its distance from the cut \(d\beta\) at the nodes is at least \(d\pi/p\).
For the normalized Fejér kernel \(K_m(v)=(2\pi m)^{-1}(\sin(mv/2)/\sin(v/2))^2\), the sign margin there is at least
\[
 \gamma_p=2\left(\int_0^{d\pi/p}K_m-\int_\alpha^\pi K_m\right)\ \longrightarrow\ g .
\]
Indeed the second integral is \(O((m\alpha)^{-1})=o(1)\), and substitution \(u=mv/2\) in the first gives the displayed limit.
Thus \(W G\ge\gamma_p|G|\) at these nodes.
Taking \(0\le s<d\), restricting first to such an interval and then letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) gives
\[
      \liminf V_G^{-1}\mathbb E\big[WG\cos(sy)\big]\ \ge\ g ,          \tag{3}
\]
since \(\cos(sy)\to1\) at zero and the contribution from the complement is \(O_\eta(1)\).

For \(k>d\) let \(D(k)=a_{k+d-1}-a_{k-d-1}\) in the index range used below.
Writing \(t=2\cos\theta\), the sine addition formula gives
\[
          2\cos(k\theta)E_{d-1}(t)=E_{k+d-1}(t)-E_{k-d-1}(t).
\]
The [exact grid quadrature](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-cut-compatibility) gives \(\mathbb E(Z E_i)=(p/n)a_i\) for \(0\le i<n\).
Indeed the weights in that quadrature are \((4-b_j^2)/p\).
It follows that \(\mathbb E[G\cos(ky)]=(-1)^{d-1+k}(p/(2n))D(k)\) when both indices are in range.
For \(3\le r<m\) this applies to \(k=dr\pm s\), since \(dm+2d<n\) eventually.
For each \(r\) for which both differences vanish, the product formula for \(\cos(rdy)\cos(sy)\) makes its contribution in (3) zero.
The constant term and \(r=1,2\) give \(O_d(1)\) in the unnormalized mean by the fixed polynomial moment bound.
Every remaining term has absolute mean at most \(V_G\) times the magnitude of its scalar coefficient, which is at most \(4d\beta/\pi=8d\kappa/p\).
Thus the set \(R\) of \(3\le r<m\) with \(D(dr+s)\ne0\) or \(D(dr-s)\ne0\) satisfies \(\liminf(\kappa/p)|R|\ge g/(8d)\).
Each difference compares indices at distance \(2d\) below \(\epsilon p\) and in the class \(s-1\) or \(-s-1\bmod d\).
For either choice of sign an index occurs for at most two values of \(r\), hence \(|R|\le4N(\{s-1,-s-1\})\).
This proves (1) and the comparison with references modulo \(2d\).

## Selecting positive frequencies in a single class

Keeping the actual cut in both phases gives a sum with only the positive frequencies \(k=dr+s\),
\[
 Y_s(y)=1-2\alpha/\pi-\frac4\pi\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}
       (1-r/m)\frac{\sin((dr+s)\beta)}r\cos((dr+s)y).
\]
For fixed \(d,s\) and small fixed \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \sup_{0\le y\le\eta}|Y_s(y)-W(y)|
       \ \le\ C_{d,s}\eta(1+|\log\eta|)                               \tag{4}
\]
once \(\beta<\eta\).
To check the estimate, put \(S_m(v)+i C_m(v)=\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}(1-r/m)(\sin(rv)+i\cos(rv))/r\) and \(\tau=\operatorname{dist}(v,2\pi\mathbb Z)\).
One has \(|S_m(v)|\le C\) and \(|C_m(v)|\le C(1+\log^+\min(m,1/\tau))\), taking \(1/0=\infty\).
For \(\tau>0\) split at \(1/\tau\), bounding the initial sines by \(r\tau\) and summing the tail by parts using the geometric-sum bound \(O(1/\tau)\) and decreasing weights.
At \(\tau=0\) the sine sum vanishes and the harmonic sum bounds the cosines.
The product-to-sum formula uses \(x=\beta+y,\beta-y\), with \(\sin((dr+s)x)\) in \(Y_s\) in place of \(\sin(drx)\) in \(W\).
Their weighted sum difference at each \(x\) equals \((\cos(sx)-1)S_m(dx)+\sin(sx)C_m(dx)\).
When \(|x|\le2\eta\) and \(\eta\) is sufficiently small, the bounds just proved give (4), including \(x=0\) by continuity.

On the remaining interval one has
\[
       \sup_{\eta\le y\le\pi}
       \left|\frac{\sin(dy)}{\sin y}Y_s(y)\right|
                         \ \le\ C_{d,s,\eta}(1+\beta\log p).            \tag{5}
\]
For \(y\) separated from both endpoints, take \(\tau=\operatorname{dist}(dx,2\pi\mathbb Z)\) with \(x=\beta\pm y\).
Here \(|\sin(dy)|\le d\beta+\tau\), which bounds the possible logarithmic term by \(O(1+\beta\log m)\).
Near \(y=\pi\) the quotient of sines is bounded by \(d\).
If \(d\) is odd, \(dx\) then stays away from \(2\pi\mathbb Z\) on a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood.
If \(d\) is even, the nearest multiple of \(2\pi\) is \(\pm d\pi\), so \(|\sin(sx)|\le (s/d)\tau\) there, including its endpoint value by continuity.
These bounds give (5) for both terms of the product-to-sum formula.

Consequently the contribution to \(\mathbb E(GY_s)\) from \(y\ge\eta\) is \(O_{d,s,\eta}(1+\beta\log p)\).
Under the additional hypothesis of (2), division by \(V_G\), use of (4) and the sign margin, and then \(\eta\downarrow0\) give \(\liminf V_G^{-1}\mathbb E(GY_s)\ge g\).
Only \(r\) with \(D(dr+s)\ne0\) contribute after removal of the three fixed frequencies as before.
The scalar coefficient has magnitude at most \(4(d+s)\beta/\pi\le16d\kappa/p\), and each nonzero difference requires a discrepancy at one of two indices in the class \(s-1\).
Each index occurs at most twice, proving (2) with the stated constant.

The additional hypothesis holds, for example, whenever \(p/\kappa\ge(\log p)^c\) eventually for some fixed \(c>0\).
Indeed with \(\delta=2+a=O(\beta^2)\), the fixed moment estimate gives \(\mathbb E|(2+t)^hZ|\ll_h1+\delta^h V\) by subtracting \((2+a)^h\).
The fixed-weight variation gap for \((2+t)^h\) forces \(\delta^h V\to\infty\) for every fixed \(h\).
Choosing \(h\) with \(c(2h+1)>1\) yields \(\beta\log p=o(V)\).
The unconditional estimate (1) allows one member of a nonsingleton pair to provide all the required changes.
