## Actual input and the changed coverage question

The positive certificate in [C868 v6](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v6.md#integer-reflection-certificate-statement), at its [selected assessment](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v6.md#decision-whole-integral-reflection-review-2026-11-05), cannot cover an irreducible original polynomial having a circle conjugate.
[L133 v14](Yin_L_G0133_v14.md#mod-two-certificate-return-boundary) retains the additional binary obstruction for that positive certificate branch.
Here signs are allowed in the square identity.
This changes its existence and circle-root coverage, but the resulting reflection need not be Euclidean unitary.

The actual cost interface is the [C868 v5 statement](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v5.md#statement), with its [selected rational-price assessment](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v5.md#decision-whole-rational-price-review-2026-11-05).
For each supplied normalized rational matrix germ F(0)=I it gives
\[
 \tau_{\min}(F)=J(F)+N(F),\qquad
 J(F)=\int_{\mathbb T}\sum_i\log^+\sigma_i(F)\,d\mu,\qquad
 N(F)=\sum_{|\alpha|<1}\delta_\alpha(F)\log\frac1{|\alpha|}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The infimum concerns actual complex Schur pairs U=FQ with invertible Q(0), and their full determinant expense.
Rational approximation at that interface does not supply rational-number or integer coefficients for Q.
No rank or degree normalization is used.

The [earlier circle-unitary pole obstruction](Yin_L_G0133_v8.md#power-pullback-circle-pole-rigidity) and [rank-one shear cost](Yin_L_G0133_v10.md#rank-one-shear-exact-cost) remain at their stated scopes.
They neither exclude the present nonunitary construction nor pay its boundary expense.
A bounded local search for signed Hermitian reflections, indefinite reflections and J-unitary versions found no adequate existing account of the specific construction and complete cost calculation below.
The binary-support reflection terminology in C843 concerns a different object.
No exhaustive search or novelty claim is made.

Let P in Z[z] be monic irreducible reciprocal of degree 2d, d>=1, with P(0)=1.
Fix epsilon in {+1,-1} and put
\[
 p(z)=\varepsilon z^{-d}P(z)
   =c+\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k(z^k+z^{-k}),\qquad
 c,p_k\in\mathbb Z,\quad p_d=\varepsilon.
 \tag{2}
\]
Unlike the positive-certificate branch, p may change sign and vanish on the circle.
We have m(P)=integral log|p|, and the original G0001 comparison concerns m(P)>0.
All circle integrals below have normalized Haar measure.
The signature matrix is denoted Sigma to distinguish it from the boundary expense J.

<a id="signed-reflection-general-germ"></a>
## A supplied signed certificate gives an integer involution

Suppose an actual degree-bounded signed identity is supplied:
\[
 2p=v^\star\Sigma v,\qquad
 v=(v_1,\ldots,v_r)^T,\quad v_j\in\mathbb Z[z],\quad
 \deg v_j\le d,\qquad
 \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_r),\ s_j=\pm1.
 \tag{3}
\]
Zero rows can be removed.
For a polynomial column, v^star(z)=v(z^{-1})^T; on the circle it is the conjugate transpose.
Define
\[
 W=I_r-\frac{vv^\star\Sigma}{p}
   =I_r-\varepsilon\frac{z^d v(z)v(z^{-1})^T\Sigma}{P(z)}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The numerator matrix in the second expression is polynomial with integer coefficients, since every exponent is between zero and 2d.
Because P(0)=1, its reciprocal has an integer Taylor series at zero.
Thus W is an actual rational integer germ, with only the original P as a possible finite denominator.

The rank-one product identity (vv^star Sigma)^2=2p vv^star Sigma gives
\[
 W^2=I_r,\qquad \det W=-1,\qquad W^*\Sigma W=\Sigma
       \quad\hbox{on }\mathbb T\hbox{ away from the poles}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The last identity is signature preservation, not Euclidean unitarity.
It follows by conjugating (4) on the circle and canceling the two rank-one cross terms with the quadratic term using (3).

Write a_k=[z^k]v.
The highest lag in (3) is
\[
 a_d^T\Sigma a_0=2\varepsilon,\qquad
 W_0=W(0)=I_r-\varepsilon a_0a_d^T\Sigma,\qquad W_0^2=I_r.
 \tag{6}
\]
In particular W_0 is an invertible integer matrix.
The normalized germ
\[
 F=WW_0
 \tag{7}
\]
has F(0)=I_r and integer Taylor coefficients.
No orthogonality of W_0 is assumed.

<a id="signed-reflection-pole-lengths"></a>
## Every original root remains a simple rank-one pole

Irreducibility in characteristic zero makes every root alpha of P simple.
Every nonzero v_j has degree at most d<2d, so it cannot vanish at alpha.
The same holds at alpha^(-1), which is again a root of the reciprocal P.
Consequently the residue in (4),
\[
 -\frac{\varepsilon\alpha^d}{P'(\alpha)}
       v(\alpha)v(\alpha^{-1})^T\Sigma,
 \tag{8}
\]
is nonzero and has rank one.
There is no original-root cancellation.

For a matrix with a simple pole and residue R, a holomorphic column h lies in its pole-cancellation module exactly when R h(alpha)=0.
The local quotient therefore has dimension rank R.
Applying this observation to (8) gives
\[
 \delta_\alpha(W)=\delta_\alpha(F)=1,\qquad
 N(W)=N(F)=m(P).
 \tag{9}
\]
Multiplication by the constant invertible W_0 preserves the local module colength.
The last equality retains every disk root and uses the monic constant-one product of all root moduli.
The determinant -1 in (5) does not cancel the pole expense.
Circle roots are genuine poles as well; their expense enters the boundary integral rather than the disk sum in (9).

The Euclidean squared norm of the value vector
\[
 E(\zeta)=\|v(\zeta)\|^2
\]
is strictly positive everywhere on the circle.
Indeed a common circle zero of all rows would force p and hence P to vanish there, contradicting the degree argument just used.
This also prevents any hidden common-zero cancellation in the boundary formulas.

<a id="signed-reflection-euclidean-cost"></a>
## Exact Euclidean singular values and the mixed-sign expense

At a circle point where p is nonzero put
\[
 E_\pm=\sum_{s_j=\pm1}|v_j|^2,\qquad
 E=E_++E_-,\qquad
 s=E_+-E_-=2p.
 \tag{10}
\]
The only potentially nonunit singular values of W form a reciprocal pair L,L^(-1), with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L&=\frac{E+\sqrt{E^2-s^2}}{|s|}
    =\frac{(\sqrt{E_+}+\sqrt{E_-})^2}{2|p|},\\
 \log L&=\operatorname{arcosh}\frac{E}{2|p|}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
All other singular values are one.
When the two defining vectors are parallel the pair degenerates to one.

For a direct proof, normalize v and Sigma v to unit vectors.
Their real inner product is t=s/E, with |t|<=1.
On their Euclidean span an orthonormal basis makes W the block
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -1&-2\sqrt{1-t^2}/t\\
 0&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its singular values have product one and the claimed larger value (11); W is the identity on the orthogonal complement.
The case |t|=1 follows directly or by continuity.
This calculation uses the Euclidean metric throughout.

Thus
\[
 J(W)=\int_{\mathbb T}\operatorname{arcosh}
                  \frac{E}{2|p|}\,d\mu.
 \tag{12}
\]
The elementary inequalities log x<=arcosh x<=log(2x), x>=1, imply
\[
 \int\log E-\log2-m(P)
 \le J(W)\le
 \int\log E-m(P).
 \tag{13}
\]
These are finite quantities: E is positive continuous and log|p| is integrable.

If both signature groups contain a nonzero row, (11) and (9) give the exact identity
\[
 J(W)+N(W)
 =2\int\log(\sqrt{E_+}+\sqrt{E_-})\,d\mu-\log2
 \ge\log2.
 \tag{14}
\]
Indeed sqrt(E_+)+sqrt(E_-)>=2(E_+E_-)^(1/4).
In each nonempty group choose one nonzero integer row f.
Then E_group>=|f|^2 and integral log E_group>=2m(f)>=0 by Jensen and its integer leading coefficient.
The logarithms are integrable even if a group has isolated circle zeros.
This proves (14), with no matrix-size factor.

Equation (14) is a bound for the complete unnormalized characteristic J(W)+N(W).
To use (1) for the normalized F, the actual constant W_0 must still be retained.
The same elementary oblique-reflection calculation gives
\[
 \gamma_0=\sum_i\log^+\sigma_i(W_0)
   =\operatorname{arcosh}\frac{\|a_0\|\|a_d\|}{2}.
 \tag{15}
\]
For an invertible matrix A, sum_i log^+ sigma_i(A) equals log max_k ||exterior^k A||, including k=0.
Submultiplicativity of these norms, first in F=WW_0 and then in W=FW_0, gives
\[
 |J(F)-J(W)|\le\gamma_0,\qquad
 \tau_{\min}(F)=J(F)+m(P).
 \tag{16}
\]
In particular (14) alone gives only tau_min(F)>=log2-gamma_0.
When W_0 is orthogonal, gamma_0=0 and its right multiplication preserves every boundary singular value exactly.
No inequality for m(P) follows just from either cost lower bound.

<a id="signed-reflection-circle-log-expense"></a>
## Circle roots have a real logarithmic boundary expense

Let beta=e^(i theta_0) be an original circle root.
Then E(beta)>0 and P'(beta) is nonzero.
Expanding the original P along the circle in (12) gives
\[
 \log L(e^{i\theta})
 =\log\frac1{|\theta-\theta_0|}
     +\log\frac{E(\beta)}{|P'(\beta)|}+o(1).
 \tag{17}
\]
Here |p(e^(i theta))|=|P(e^(i theta))| and
P(e^(i theta))=i beta P'(beta)(theta-theta_0)+O((theta-theta_0)^2).
The final step uses arcosh x=log(2x)+o(1) as x tends to infinity.
Thus every circle root has a genuine but integrable log singularity.
Signature cancellation makes v^*Sigma v vanish there without making the outer product in (8) vanish.
It therefore removes the positive-certificate existence obstruction, not this Euclidean expense.

<a id="universal-rank-three-signed-certificate"></a>
## A fixed three-row certificate covers every original input

Define the actual analytic polynomial
\[
 H(z)=c+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_kz^k,\qquad
 v=(H,1,H-1)^T,\qquad
 \Sigma_3=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1).
 \tag{18}
\]
On the circle Re H=p, so
\[
 |H|^2+1-|H-1|^2=2p.
 \tag{19}
\]
All coefficients are integers, the actual degree of H is d with leading coefficient 2epsilon, and every row has degree at most d.
This is a supplied signed certificate for every original input (2), without any no-circle-root or positivity restriction.

Its positive Euclidean quantity and boundary cost are
\[
 E=2(|H|^2-p+1),\qquad
 J(W_3)=\int\operatorname{arcosh}
                  \frac{|H|^2-p+1}{|p|}\,d\mu.
 \tag{20}
\]
Both signature groups are nonzero.
The origin data are
\[
 a_0=(c,1,c-1)^T,\qquad a_d=(2\varepsilon,0,2\varepsilon)^T,
\]
and therefore
\[
 W_{3,0}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1-2c&0&2c\\
 -2&1&2\\
 2-2c&0&2c-1
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \gamma_0=\operatorname{arcosh}\bigl(2\sqrt{c^2-c+1}\bigr).
 \tag{21}
\]
The actual normalized three-row germ is F_3=W_3 W_(3,0).
Equations (9), (16) and (20) retain its complete original pole cost and the precise constant-normalization comparison.
The certificate's fixed row count does not itself bound that price by a multiple of m(P).

<a id="universal-signed-orthogonal-origin"></a>
## A diagonal-sign construction can also have orthogonal origin

For d>=2 choose one integer k with 1<=k<d and put
\[
 B(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_jz^{d-j}.
\]
Take the two rows z^d+B and z^d-B with signs +1,-1.
If c is nonzero, append 2|c| copies of z^k, each with sign sign(c); if c=0 append none.
The result has r=2+2|c| rows and
\[
 |z^d+B|^2-|z^d-B|^2+2c=2p.
 \tag{22}
\]
Its coefficients are integers and its degree window is exactly the required one.
Since B(0)=epsilon and deg B<d, its endpoint vectors have first two entries (epsilon,-epsilon) and (1,1), with zeros elsewhere.
Equation (6) yields
\[
 W_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\oplus I_{r-2}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Thus W_0 is Euclidean orthogonal, F=WW_0 is normalized, and J(F)=J(W).
For this actual all-input construction in degree at least four,
\[
 E=2(1+|B|^2+|c|),\qquad
 J(F)=\int\operatorname{arcosh}
                   \frac{1+|B|^2+|c|}{|p|}\,d\mu,\qquad
 N(F)=m(P).
 \tag{24}
\]
In particular the mixed-sign lower bound log2 in (14) applies directly to its normalized price.
This shows that a nonorthogonal origin is not the only source of the loss.
The row count here depends on c; the fixed-rank construction (18) and the rank-two construction below have different explicit costs.
No separate treatment of d=1 is needed for universal coverage, because (18) already includes it.

<a id="universal-normalized-rank-two-germ"></a>
## An explicit normalized two-by-two germ and its exact price

Put
\[
 g=H-c=2\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_kz^k,\qquad
 q=\frac1p=\frac{\varepsilon z^d}{P}.
\]
Both are integer germs and g(0)=q(0)=0.
Define directly
\[
 F_2=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1-gq&2g-g^2q\\
 -q&1-gq
 \end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}1&g\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-q&1\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&g\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{25}
\]
This proves F_2(0)=I_2, integer Taylor coefficients and det F_2=1.
There is no new denominator beyond the actual original P.

Its exact relation to the signed certificate can also be made integral.
Set
\[
 {\cal H}_c=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&2c\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 u=(g,1)^T,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
\]
On the circle u^*H_c u=2c+g+\bar g=2p.
Consequently
\[
 W_2=I_2-\frac{uu^\star{\cal H}_c}{p},\qquad
 W_2(0)=D,\qquad F_2=W_2D.
 \tag{26}
\]
The matrix H_c is indefinite, with determinant -1; it is not a positive Gram metric to which the positive-metric arithmetic theorem can be applied.

For the three-row model, let the columns of the integer matrix T_c be
\[
 a=(1,0,1)^T,\qquad b_c=(c,1,c-1)^T,\qquad e=(1,-1,1)^T.
\]
Direct calculation gives
\[
 \det T_c=-1,\qquad
 T_c^T\Sigma_3T_c={\cal H}_c\oplus1,\qquad
 T_c^{-1}W_3T_c=W_2\oplus1.
 \tag{27}
\]
Indeed v=g a+b_c and e is signature-orthogonal to a,b_c.
These identities explain the arithmetic relationship.
They do not assert that this generally nonorthogonal conjugation preserves Euclidean singular values or the old three-row expense.
The price of F_2 is calculated directly in its stated Euclidean coordinates.

For a rank-one oblique reflection I-2xy^*/(y^*x), the two-vector proof of (11) still applies when ||x|| and ||y|| differ: its cosine parameter is (y^*x)/(||x||||y||).
Here x=u, y=H_c u, y^*x=2p, and
\[
 K=\|u\|\|{\cal H}_cu\|
   =\sqrt{(1+|g|^2)(1+|g+2c|^2)}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz makes K/(2|p|)>=1.
The orthogonal right factor D in (26) preserves singular values, so
\[
 J(F_2)=\int\operatorname{arcosh}
      \frac{\sqrt{(1+|g|^2)(1+|g+2c|^2)}}{2|p|}\,d\mu.
 \tag{28}
\]
At every original root alpha, (25) has residue
\[
 -\frac{\varepsilon\alpha^d}{P'(\alpha)}
       \begin{pmatrix}g(\alpha)\\1\end{pmatrix}
       \begin{pmatrix}1&g(\alpha)\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{29}
\]
It is nonzero and rank one, regardless of g(alpha).
The local module argument therefore gives N(F_2)=m(P), including all actual disk roots once.
Equation (1) now applies directly to the normalized two-by-two germ, with no origin-cost subtraction.

Define the concrete analytic-part functional
\[
 {\cal A}(P)=\frac12\int\log(1+|g|^2)\,d\mu
       +\frac12\int\log(1+|g+2c|^2)\,d\mu.
 \tag{30}
\]
From the two elementary bounds on arcosh used in (13),
\[
 \boxed{{\cal A}(P)-\log2
       \le\tau_{\min}(F_2)\le{\cal A}(P).}
 \tag{31}
\]
This retains the original pole expense: the term -integral log|p| in (28) is canceled by the actual N=m(P), rather than omitted.

For every nonzero polynomial f, convexity of x to log(1+e^(2x)) gives
\[
 \int\log(1+|f|^2)\,d\mu
       \ge\log(1+\exp(2m(f))).
\]
Its use at the finitely many possible zeros is legitimate because log|f| is integrable and the displayed positive logarithm is continuous.
Both g and g+2c have actual degree d and leading coefficient 2epsilon.
Their Mahler measures are therefore at least two.
Thus A(P)>=log5, and the actual normalized price satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\tau_{\min}(F_2)\ge\log(5/2).}
 \tag{32}
\]
This is a self-contained lower bound for the supplied realization, not for m(P).

There is also an explicit finite upper bound in the original coefficients.
Putting S=sum_(k=1)^d p_k^2, ordinary Jensen for the concave logarithm gives
\[
 {\cal A}(P)
 \le\frac12\log(1+4S)
       +\frac12\log(1+4c^2+4S).
 \tag{33}
\]
Equations (28)--(33) make this universal candidate's expense testable.
They do not compare A(P) from above with the original total m(P).

<a id="signed-fixed-input-expense-control"></a>
## A fixed original input admits arbitrarily expensive normalized certificates

Fix any signed certificate with d>=2 and choose 1<=k<d.
For a positive integer L append two identical rows Lz^k with opposite signs.
The signed symbol p and original P are unchanged, the degree remains at most d, and only two rows have been added.
The Euclidean quantity becomes
\[
 E_L=E+2L^2.
\]
Both new rows have zero constant and degree-d coefficients, so
\[
 (W_L)_0=W_0\oplus I_2,\qquad
 \gamma_{0,L}=\gamma_0,\qquad N(W_L)=N(F_L)=m(P).
\]
The general residue proof still applies to these actual rows.
Equations (13) and (16) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J(W_L)+m(P)&\ge2\log L,\\
 \tau_{\min}\bigl(F_L=W_L(W_0\oplus I_2)\bigr)
       &\ge2\log L-\gamma_0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{34}
\]
Thus a fixed original polynomial, a fixed support window and a fixed matrix size can have expense tending to infinity merely by choosing these valid signed certificates.
When the starting construction is (22), gamma_0=0 and no normalization loss occurs.

For a concrete positive-measure input with actual circle roots, use the [already retained Salem quartic](Yin_L_G0133_v10.md#circle-root-shear-log-cost)
\[
 P_S=z^4-z^3-z^2-z+1,\qquad
 p=z^2+z^{-2}-z-z^{-1}-1.
\]
Its irreducibility, two circle roots and fixed positive total measure are retained through the C206 input cited there.
For d=2, epsilon=1 and c=-1, the construction (22) is explicitly
\[
 v=(z^2-z+1,\ z^2+z-1,\ z,\ z)^T,\qquad
 \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).
 \tag{35}
\]
Its origin is the orthogonal swap (23).
Appending Lz,Lz with signs +1,-1 yields six rows for every L and gives actual normalized expense at least 2log L.
No root estimate, changing original polynomial, growing rank or added cyclotomic factor is involved.

This control refutes the inference that existence of a signed certificate automatically supplies a uniform bound C m(P) for every such certificate.
It does not refute a bound for the particular canonical F_2, an optimized signed certificate, or another original-input construction.
Those upper comparisons remain unsupplied.

<a id="signed-truncated-log-source-comparison"></a>
## An inspected truncated-log source describes a related price floor

DP23, Dubickas and Pritsker, [Lower bounds for Mahler-type measures of polynomials](https://math.okstate.edu/people/igor/Mahler-type%20measures.pdf), is used here only as a source comparison.
The actual seven-page author PDF was text-read at definition (1.5), Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2, Proposition 1.3 and Corollary 1.4, printed pages 2--4.
The coordinator additionally inspected the relevant proof specialization.
This is not a new whole-source proof audit, and (32) does not depend on it.

For a nonconstant monic complex polynomial R with |R(0)|=1 and real u<=log2, the inspected statements give
\[
 \log M_u(R)=\int\max\{u,\log|R|\}\,d\mu
 \ge\log C_u
 =\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi
       \max\{u,\log|e^{it}-1|\}\,dt>0.
 \tag{36}
\]
For u=0, C_0 is the two-variable Mahler measure M(1+x+y).
This is a truncated-log measure, not the original m(R); cyclotomic examples already separate them.

The model-specific comparisons to (36) are direct.
For F_3 in (21), the vector a=e_1+e_3 satisfies W_(3,0)a=a and v^star Sigma_3 a=1.
Hence
\[
 e_2^TF_3a=-1/p,\qquad
 \|F_3\|_{\rm op}\ge\frac1{\sqrt2|P|}.
\]
Adding its exact pole expense gives
\[
 \tau_{\min}(F_3)\ge\log M_{-\log2/2}(P)\ge\log C_{-\log2/2}>0.
 \tag{37}
\]
For F_2, its lower-left entry is -1/p in unit Euclidean coordinates, so
\[
 \tau_{\min}(F_2)\ge\int\max\{0,\log|P|\}\,d\mu=\log M_0(P).
 \tag{38}
\]
This last source-based floor is weaker than the self-contained bound (32) for this F_2.
Neither comparison supplies an original-measure upper bound or a Lehmer conclusion.

<a id="signed-reflection-return-and-limits"></a>
## What is supplied and what is still missing

Signed integer certificates do supply every original input, including the circle-root domain excluded from the positive certificate.
The resulting rational germs have actual integer coefficients, fixed or explicitly counted matrix size, a correct origin normalization and exactly one local pole charge per original root.
The normalized two-by-two construction additionally has an explicit exact boundary formula and the complete price comparison (31).

The unresolved issue has moved from certificate existence to its Euclidean analytic expense.
Signature preservation is not Euclidean unitarity.
The mixed-sign floor, the normalized rank-two floor and the fixed-input divergence control all concern realization price, not the original Mahler measure.
No actual estimate J(F)+N(F)<=C m(P) on the unrestricted original core has been established.
The control for arbitrary certificates does not exclude a genuinely optimized choice or every general matrix mechanism, and G0001 remains unresolved.
