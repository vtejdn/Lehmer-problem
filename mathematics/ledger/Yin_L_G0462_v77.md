# Three primitive cubic prefixes remain at branch two

<a id="cubic"></a>
## Enumeration up to a sign change of the variable

Consider a sequence of successful six-term positive-constant inputs on branch two with \(q\to\infty\), whose two lower nonconstant offsets have primitive pair \((m,3)\).
After replacing the projected variable \(w=z^{\gcd(d,e)}\) by \(-w\) if necessary and taking a subsequence, the lower polynomial must be one of
\[
                 2-w+w^3,\qquad 2-2w+w^3,\qquad 2+w^2+w^3 .
\]
The rotation is a classification of the projected polynomials and does not require realization of the rotated input at the same offset.
All three cubics are irreducible and have all roots strictly outside the closed unit disk.
The existence of a suitable inverse denominator for any of them remains open.

Indeed the [six-term reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) gives \(A=2+a w^m+b w^3\), \(m=1,2\), with \(|b|=1\), \(1\le |a|\le3\), and no open-disk root.
Rotating fixes the cubic coefficient to \(+1\).
The [quadratic prefix theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v18.md#quadratic) eliminates all reducible cubics here, including cubics with a repeated cyclotomic factor.

When \(m=1\), \(a\ge2\) gives \(A(-1)=1-a<0<A(0)\).
For \(a=1\), \(-1\) is a root, and for \(a=-3\), \(1\) is a root.
This leaves \(a=-1,-2\).
When \(m=2\), \(a\le-2\) gives \(A(-1)=1+a<0<A(0)\), and \(a=-1\) gives a root at \(-1\).
For \(a\ge2\), \(A(-2)=4a-6>0\) gives a real root smaller than \(-2\).
The product of the moduli of its two other roots is then less than one, which again gives an open-disk root.
Only \(a=1\) survives in this case.

Both critical values of each displayed cubic are positive.
For the first two, write \(a=-k\) with \(k=1,2\).
The smaller critical value is \(2-\frac{2k}{3}\sqrt{k/3}>0\).
For the last cubic the critical points are \(-2/3,0\), with respective values \(58/27,2\).
Each therefore has exactly one real root, lying strictly between \(-2\) and \(-1\) by direct evaluation.
Its complex conjugate pair has common modulus \(\sqrt{2/|\alpha|}>1\), where \(\alpha\) is the real root.
The real root is not an integer, so these monic cubics are irreducible over the rationals.
