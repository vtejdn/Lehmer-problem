# Positive Pisot excess for sparse trace lifts of bounded coefficient denominator

<a id="gap"></a>
## The gap and its dependence on the trace lift

For each pair of positive integers \(K,m_{\max}\) there is \(c(K,m_{\max})>0\) with the following property.
Let a positive-constant Pisot input have exterior root \(\theta\), constant \(q\ge2\), branch index \(j\ge1\), trace numerator of degree \(h\) and [trace lift](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(\Pi\).
If \(\Pi\) has at most \(K\) nonzero coefficients and the least common denominator of the coefficients of \(\Pi/j\) is at most \(m_{\max}\), then \(\theta-q\ge c(K,m_{\max})\).
In particular on every fixed branch the number of terms must tend to infinity along any sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\), and \(q\) is bounded for each fixed pair \(K,j\) by the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).

Suppose to the contrary that \(\theta-q\to0\) with these two bounds.
Write \(m\) for the least common denominator and \(F=m\Pi/j\), so \(F(0)=m,\ M(F)=m\), with \(M\) the multiplicative Mahler measure.
Indeed the roots of \(\Pi\) lie on the unit circle.
After extraction \(m\), the term count and the ordered tuple of coefficients of \(F\) are fixed by the [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound).
Use \(B=B^\sharp\) for the actual integer monic reciprocal trace denominator of degree \(2h+2\), with constant one, and \(J\) for its inverse Herglotz series.
The [actual-input representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) gives
\[
 B=(F/m)J,\qquad 1<M(B)\longrightarrow1,\qquad h\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
The symmetric real signed measures \(\omega\) representing \(J\) have mass one, with negative mass \(\alpha\to0\) supported at \(1\).
Their doubled moments are the positive Taylor coefficients of \(J\), whereas its Taylor constant is one.
The [first nonintegral coefficient](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) of \(F/m\) exists and its index \(d\) is \(o(h)\), since the circle supremum is bounded.
This excludes \(m=1\) and permits \(m\ge2\) in the following argument.

<a id="initial"></a>
## Coefficients on every interval at the first nonintegral index

For a polynomial \(Y\) use \(\|Y\|_{\mathrm{coef}}=\sum_n|[z^n]Y|\), extending its coefficient sequence by zero to \(\mathbb Z\).
At scale \(s\) the estimate needed below is an integer multiplier \(P\), varying along the sequence, for which
\[
 \deg P=O(s),\qquad \|P\|_{\mathrm{coef}}=O(1),\qquad
 \sup_{a\in\mathbb Z}\ \sum_{a\le n<a+s}|[z^n]PB|=O(1).
 \tag{1}
\]
The bound is uniform over interval location.
All coefficients of \(B\) are uniformly bounded, since each is a sum of at most \(K\) bounded multiples of a Taylor coefficient of \(J\), whose moments are bounded by \(2\|\omega\|\).

At \(s=d\), (1) holds with \(P=1\) or \(P=1-\epsilon z^u\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
If \(d\) is bounded take \(P=1\).
If \(d\to\infty\), formal integer division gives integral moments \([z^l]J\) for \(1\le l<d\).
The [local integer-prefix theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v68.md#low) gives agreement on \(0\le l\le\kappa d\) for some fixed \(0<\kappa<1/4\) with \(1\), \(1+\epsilon z^u\), or \((1-\epsilon z^u)^{-1}\).
Its moving-index proof uses only integrality below \(d\), \(d\le h\), the negative atom and the \(2h\) distinct charged lifts of the positive part, all supplied by the actual-input representation.
For the polynomial models the doubled model measure has density at most four against Haar probability.
For the reciprocal model with \(u>\kappa d/4\) the moments on the shorter initial interval \(0<l\le\lfloor\kappa d/4\rfloor\) vanish.
In these cases take \(P=1\).
In the remaining reciprocal model take \(P=1-\epsilon z^u\).
Then \(|P|^2\) annihilates its atomic part and leaves a doubled measure of density at most four.
Consequently in each case the signed Gram matrices of the characters \(v^nP(v^{-1})\) in \(2\omega\) have uniformly bounded operator norm on blocks of diameter \(\kappa d/4\), by exact moment matching including the zero index.

For clarity, if \(E\) is the support of \(F\) and \(\lambda_e=[z^e]F/m\), on any interval disjoint from all \([e,e+\deg P]\), \(e\in E\), the coefficients of \(PB\) have the form
\[
    [z^n]PB=2\int v^nP(v^{-1})\,T(v)\,d\omega(v),
    \qquad T(v)=\sum_{e<n}\lambda_e v^{-e}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here \(T\) is fixed on that interval with \(\|T\|_\infty\le\sum_e|\lambda_e|\).
For any fixed \(k\) tested indices, passing to \(2\omega^+\) adds a Gram matrix of norm at most \(2k\alpha\|P\|_\infty^2=o(1)\), and changes each integral by \(o(1)\).
If all \(k\) coefficients were nonzero integers, the sum of the squared positive-part integrals would be at least \(k(1-o(1))^2\).
It is bounded above by the Gram norm times \(2\int |T|^2\,d\omega^+\), which stays bounded independently of \(k\) in the limit.
Choosing \(k\) larger than this bound controls the number of nonzero coefficients on each block.

For the reciprocal model the exceptional intervals have length \(u+1\).
Split their union at the finitely many points \(e,e+u\), retaining these endpoints individually.
On each remaining exceptional piece the tested coefficients are integrals of ordinary characters against a fixed bounded sum of the two translated partial multipliers.
The diameter is less than \(u\), so all nonzero Gram differences vanish by the same model, and the unweighted Gram is twice the identity.
The preceding finite-index test again applies.
Only boundedly many pieces and blocks occur in any interval of length \(d\), and the individual coefficients are bounded.
This proves (1), including its uniformity in location, by the [all-interval argument](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals).
In the base step \(P(0)=M(P)=1\).

## The numerator face between two separated scales

Pass to a subsequence on which the ratios of every two positive lower offsets, \(d\) and \(h\) converge in \([0,\infty]\).
Collect quantities whose ratio tends to a finite positive number in a single class.
Use as successive scales \(s_0,\ldots,s_b\) one representative of each class from that of \(d\) through that of \(h\), with \(s_0=d,\ s_b=h\).
Thus \(s_\ell/s_{\ell+1}\to0\), \(b\) is bounded in terms of \(K\), and all lower offsets strictly between successive scales in the sense of these ratios are absent.
Early offsets smaller than \(d\) are included in every following face.

At a pair of successive scales \(s,S\), let \(A\) be the truncation of \(F\) to the constant and all exponents \(O(s)\).
Its largest exponent \(D\) is comparable to \(s\), its constant is \(m\), and \(A/m\) contains the nonintegral coefficient at \(d\).
All omitted terms have exponent at least \(\eta S\) for a fixed \(\eta>0\), including all reflected upper terms.
Thus \(A/m=F/m\) as series through any fixed smaller fraction of \(S\).
On the full stabilized exponent lattice this truncation is an exposed face by the [separated-scale face argument](Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#intermediate).
The projection of the omitted exponents modulo the early span admits no nonzero nonnegative rational dependence, since any such relation after clearing denominators would evaluate to at least \(\eta S\) on the late side and \(O(s)\) on the early side.
Face Jensen thus bounds the limiting measure of the face by that of the full lattice polynomial, which is \(m\), with the reverse bound given by the constant vertex.
[Lawton specialization](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) now gives \(M(A)\to m\), also for a bounded face degree when the specialization becomes constant.

The [intermediate-row theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#intermediate) supplies a product \(U\) of boundedly many binomials \(1-z^a\), each with \(1\le a=O(s)\), and a fixed \(L\ge2\) such that
\[
             [z^n]UB=0\qquad (Ls\le n\le S/L).
 \tag{3}
\]
Its inputs here are the primitive part of the stabilized \(A\), the constant of that primitive part, its endpoint measure equality, the limiting probability and integrality of \(B\) on the prefix.
The primitive constant \(m/\operatorname{cont}(A)\) is at least two since \(A/m\) has a nonintegral coefficient.
This proof also applies when the original \(j\) varies because the normalized series is \(A/m\) on the entire prefix, with \(m\) and the coefficients of \(A\) fixed after extraction, and the actual-input representation already permits varying \(j\).
The tests in the theorem at base frequencies \(O(s)\) and mixed frequencies \(kt+O(s)\), for fixed \(k>0,\ s/t,t/S\to0\), all lie in the common prefix when positive.
The associated torsion translates in the base marginal give the same cyclotomic product at every such intermediate index, and the [diagonal interval argument](Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#multiplier) gives (3), enlarging \(L\) if needed.

## Propagating the coefficient bound to the next scale

Suppose (1) holds at \(s\) for \(P\).
Define integer polynomials
\[
                    W=PU,\qquad C=WB,\qquad V=WA .
\]
They satisfy \(\deg W,\deg V=O(s)\) and \(\|W\|_{\mathrm{coef}},\|V\|_{\mathrm{coef}}=O(1)\).
By (3), \(C=P(UB)\) vanishes between \(Ls+\deg P\) and \(S/L\).
On its initial interval through \(Ls+\deg P\), the polynomial \(C=U(PB)\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum by (1) on boundedly many translates of intervals of length \(s\).
Consequently for all sufficiently late inputs
\[
                   \sum_{0\le n\le S/L}|[z^n]C|=O(1).
 \tag{4}
\]

For a real polynomial \(T\) write \(T^*(z)=T(z^{-1})\) as a Laurent polynomial, with the same convention for coefficient reflection of series.
If \(\widehat\omega(k)=\int v^k\,d\omega(v)\), then \(J+J^*=2\widehat\omega\) as two-sided coefficient sequences, with zeroth coefficient two.
The identity \(C=(V/m)J\) holds throughout the nonnegative prefix below the first omitted offset of \(F\), and both sides vanish at negative indices.
Shrink \(1/L\) if necessary by enlarging \(L\) in (3) so that \(S/L\) is inside that prefix.
For every \(|k|\le S/(4L)\) one therefore has the exact two-sided identity
\[
       2\int v^k |V(v^{-1})|^2\,d\omega(v)
                =m[z^k]\big(V^*C+VC^*\big).
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed for \(a\) in the support of \(V\) the two coefficients tested are \(C_{k+a}\) and \(C_{a-k}\).
Their nonnegative indices are at most \(|k|+\deg V\le S/(2L)\) eventually, and the negative ones are zero.
Using (4), multiplying by the bounded absolute coefficient sum of \(V\), and summing over both signs in (5), gives
\[
          \sum_{|k|\le S/(4L)}
             \left|2\int v^k |V(v^{-1})|^2\,d\omega(v)\right|=O(1).
 \tag{6}
\]
In particular the signed Gram of \(v^nV(v^{-1})\) has uniformly bounded operator norm on every block of this diameter, by its absolute row and column sums.

Outside the at most \(K\) intervals \([e,e+\deg V]\) around the exponents \(e\) of the full \(F\), equation (2) holds for the coefficients of \(VB\) with multiplier \(V\).
Split any interval of length \(S\) at these exceptional intervals and into blocks of diameter \(S/(4L)\).
On the complement, (6) and the finite-index Bessel estimate in the initial step give boundedly many nonzero integer coefficients per block.
The passage to the positive part still costs \(O(k\alpha\|V\|_\infty^2)=o(1)\) on each fixed \(k\)-point test.
Each exceptional interval has length \(O(s+1)\).
There \(VB=(UA)(PB)\) is a sum of boundedly many bounded multiples of translates of \(PB\), so (1) at \(s\) already bounds its absolute coefficient sum.
All tested coefficients are uniformly bounded because those of \(B\) and \(\|V\|_{\mathrm{coef}}\) are bounded.
This proves (1) at \(S\) with multiplier \(V\), including the full transition intervals wherever they occur.

## The product at the degree scale

Starting at \(d\), apply the preceding step once for each consecutive pair of scales, extracting subsequences at the finitely many applications of the intermediate-row theorem.
At \(h\) the final \(P\) has degree \(O(h)\), so boundedly many intervals of length \(h\) cover the support of \(PB\).
Its absolute coefficient sum, hence also its term count, is bounded by (1).
At every step \(V(0)=mP(0)\) and \(M(V)=M(P)M(A)\), since \(U(0)=M(U)=1\).
Thus the final product has constant \(m^b\), while
\[
           M(PB)>m^b,\qquad M(PB)\longrightarrow m^b .
\]
The strict inequality uses \(M(B)>1\) and the endpoint bound for every factor.
The convergence follows from \(M(A)\to m\) at each step, \(M(B)\to1\) and the base multiplier of measure one.
This contradicts the [bounded-term Mahler gap at an endpoint](../claims/Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#statement) and proves the asserted excess bound.
The constants from this argument depend on the two bounds in the statement through subsequence extraction.
