# The offset in a two-term Chebyshev trace numerator

<a id="fixed"></a>
## Restrictions when the offset stays fixed

In the [inverse construction](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse), let
\[
       C_h(x)=2U_h(x/2)-U_{h-d}(x/2),\qquad 1\le d\le h.
\]
Suppose it gives a sequence with \(q\to\infty\) and \(d\) fixed.
Along a subsequence the [normalized series](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) converge locally on the open disk to
\[
                   K(z)=(2-z^d)\frac{U(z)}{V(z)},
\]
where \(U,V\) are coprime squarefree signed cyclotomic products with constant term one.
Write \(u=\deg U,\ v=\deg V,\ \epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\), and put
\[
          g=\gcd(d,v-u-d),\qquad a=d/g,\qquad b=(v-u-d)/g .
\]
Then \(a\le4\).
All possibilities for the primitive data \((a,b,\epsilon)\) belong to the following table.

| \(a\) | \((b,\epsilon)\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(1\) | \((-3,-),(-2,\pm),(-1,\pm),(0,\pm),(1,\pm)\) |
| \(2\) | \((-1,\pm)\) |
| \(3\) | \((-4,+),(-2,-),(-1,-)\) |
| \(4\) | \((-3,\pm)\) |

In the rows with \(a\ge2\) the whole cyclotomic quotient \(U/V\) is fixed by the listed choice.
With \(w=z^g\) it is respectively

| \((a,b,\epsilon)\) | \(U/V\) |
| --- | --- |
| \((2,-1,\pm)\) | \(1/(1\pm w)\) |
| \((3,-4,+)\) | \(1+w\) |
| \((3,-2,-)\) | \(1/(1-w)\) |
| \((3,-1,-)\) | \(1/(1-w^2)\) |
| \((4,-3,\pm)\) | \(1/(1\pm w)\) |

These are necessary conditions.
For \(a=1\), the odd-multiplicity circle factors are obtained from the [offset-one bracket table](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#phase) by \(z\mapsto z^g\).
Their allocation between \(U\) and \(V\) must be taken over the rationals after this substitution.

## Non-torsion zeros and a phase test on four arcs

Write \(x=z+z^{-1}\), \(B^\sharp=z^{h+1}B(x)\) for the reciprocal lift of the monic trace denominator.
The [trace identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) gives
\[
 K_q(z)B^\sharp(z)
   =(1-z^2)z^h C_h(x)
   =(2-z^d)+z^{2h+2-d}(1-2z^d).
\]
The endpoint condition \(q-1\mid C_h(2)=h+d+1\) gives \(h\to\infty\).
The [integer rational limit argument](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#cyclotomic) applies to \(B^\sharp\to(2-z^d)/K\): every pole of this rational function is torsion.
The [periodicity theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) gives \(K\) an integral numerator and squarefree cyclotomic denominator \(V\) with \(V(0)=1\).
The factor \(z^d-2\) is irreducible by Eisenstein at 2.
It must occur as a whole in the numerator of \(K\), and can occur only once.
Indeed if the numerator had only torsion zeros, its constant two would give \(K\in2\mathbb Z[[z]]\), contradicting \(K\cdot\lim B^\sharp=2-z^d\).
The remaining factor \(U\) has constant one and only torsion zeros, all simple by the Herglotz lower bound in the same limit argument.

The continuous density \(\rho\) of twice the Herglotz measure of \(K\) is the [nonzero](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#radial-limits) nonnegative Laurent polynomial \(K(z)+K(1/z)\) on the circle away from poles.
After reciprocal reversal it is \(U(z)P(z^g)/V(z)\), where
\[
        P(w)=(2-w^a)+\epsilon w^b(2w^a-1)
             =2(1+\epsilon w^{a+b})-w^a-\epsilon w^b .
\]
Every odd-multiplicity circle zero of \(P\) must be torsion, since a nonnegative real density has even order at every zero and \(U,V\) have only torsion zeros.
The [torsion argument](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#phase) applied to \(w^a\) and \((2-w^a)/(2w^a-1)=-\epsilon w^b\) gives \(w^a\in\{1,-1,e^{i\pi/3},e^{-i\pi/3}\}\) at a torsion zero.
Each such value determines \(w^b\) and hence \(w\), since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).

To bound \(a\) and \(b\), parameterize the \(a\) sectors by \(w=\exp(i(2\pi r+t)/a)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\).
The phase of \(w^b(2w^a-1)/(2-w^a)\) is \(2\pi rb/a+f(t)\), where
\[
 f(t)=bt/a+\arg((2e^{it}-1)/(2-e^{it})),
\]
and the continuous argument starts at zero.
Zeros of \(P\) have phase \(0\bmod 2\pi\) for \(\epsilon=-1\) and \(\pi\bmod2\pi\) for \(\epsilon=1\).
Across the \(a\) sectors, the corresponding levels of \(af(t)/(2\pi)\) comprise exactly \(\mathbb Z+\eta\), where \(\eta=0\) for \(\epsilon=-1\) and \(\eta=a/2\) for \(\epsilon=1\).
Write \(s=a+b\).
At \(t=0,\pi/3,\pi,5\pi/3,2\pi\), the continuous arguments of \((2e^{it}-1)/(2-e^{it})\) are \(0,2\pi/3,\pi,4\pi/3,2\pi\), respectively.
Thus the five values of \(af(t)/(2\pi)\) are
\[
           0,\qquad (a+s)/6,\qquad s/2,\qquad (5s-a)/6,\qquad s.       \tag{1}
\]
No consecutive pair may strictly straddle a level of \(\mathbb Z+\eta\).
Otherwise the analytic phase crosses that level with odd order in the open subarc in at least one sector, where no torsion zero is possible.
Odd crossing order of the phase equals the multiplicity of the corresponding zero of \(P\).
Each step in (1) consequently has length at most one.
This gives \(|s|\le4,\ |a+s|\le6,\ |2s-a|\le6\), so \(a\le6\).
At \(a=6\) one would have \(s=0\), contrary to \(\gcd(a,s)=1\).

For clarity, the table of primitive data can be reproduced by letting \(a=1,\dots,5\) and \(s=-4,\dots,4\), keeping \(\gcd(a,s)=1\).
For each adjacent pair \(A,B\) in \(0,a+s,3s,5s-a,6s\), discard the choice if
\[
  6\left(\left\lfloor\frac{\min(A,B)-6\eta}{6}\right\rfloor+1\right)
                           +6\eta <\max(A,B).
\]
With \(b=s-a\) this gives the displayed table and one extra triple \((5,-4,-)\).
For that triple \(P(e^{2it})=-2i e^{it}(2\sin t+\sin 9t)\).
The parenthesis is positive at \(\pi/9\), and it is negative immediately to the left of its zero at \(\pi/6\), since the derivative there is \(\sqrt3\).
It therefore changes sign in \((\pi/9,\pi/6)\), where \(w^5=e^{10it}\) is between arguments \(10\pi/9\) and \(5\pi/3\) and never takes one of the four permitted values.

To recover \(U/V\), the odd-multiplicity torsion roots of \(P(z^g)\) are exactly the roots of \(UV\), by squarefreeness, coprimality and even density multiplicity.
For each displayed triple with \(a\ge2\), the torsion equation gives just \(w=-1\) for the plus sign and \(w=1\) for the minus sign, except at \((3,-1,-)\), which gives both.
Each listed zero has odd order: the phase derivative in the \(w\) angle is \(b+a(3/4)/|w^a-1/2|^2\), nonzero at these points except \(w=-1\) for \((3,-1,-)\).
There the Poisson kernel has a strict quadratic minimum and the phase has a cubic crossing.
The resulting total odd degree in \(z\) is \(g|s|=|v-u|\).
Since this degree equals \(u+v\), all factors go to the numerator for \(s<0\) or to the denominator for \(s>0\), giving the second table.

## The five forms when the offset tends to infinity

Now let \(d\to\infty\), \(1\le d\le h\), and allow either sign in \(C_h=2U_h\pm U_{h-d}\), with the same half-trace arguments as above.
If \(q\to\infty\), every subsequential limit has \(K=2W\) with
\[
               W=1,\qquad W=1\pm z^l,\qquad
               W=(1\pm z^l)^{-1}\qquad(l\ge1).
\]
Indeed the product \(K_qB^\sharp\) tends coefficientwise to 2, since the other three terms have exponents at least \(\min(d,2h+2-d)\to\infty\).
The integer rational limit argument for \(1/W=\lim B^\sharp\) makes all zeros of \(W\) torsion.
As \(K(0)=2\), this gives \(W=U/V\) with the same squarefree coprime cyclotomic conventions, both normalized to one at zero.
Its continuous doubled density away from poles is \((U/V)(1+\epsilon z^{v-u})\), a nonzero nonnegative Laurent polynomial.
If \(u=v\), the bracket is a nonzero constant, so no simple root of \(U\) or \(V\) is possible.
If \(u\ne v\), the bracket has \(|v-u|\) simple circle roots.
As above these are exactly the roots of \(UV\), so \(u+v=|v-u|\).
One factor is constant and the other is the constant-normalized binomial sharing those roots, as asserted.
The argument imposes no relation between the rates of growth of \(d\) and \(h\).
