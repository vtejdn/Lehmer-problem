# Positive angular moments and the negative eigenvalues of reciprocal Newton sums

<a id="angular"></a>
## A second-order radial error for all angular tests

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal real polynomial of degree \(D\) with constant term one and multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(P)\le T\), where \(T\ge1\).
Write \(s_k=\sum_\alpha\alpha^k\) over its roots with multiplicity, with \(s_0=D\), and let \(t_k=\sum_\alpha(\alpha/|\alpha|)^k\).
Both sequences are real.
Reflection \(\alpha\mapsto1/\overline\alpha\) pairs every exterior root with an interior root of the same angle, and fixes each root on the circle.
An exterior root \(e^{a+i\phi}\) and its mate contribute \(2\cosh(ka)e^{ik\phi}\) to \(s_k\), against \(2e^{ik\phi}\) to \(t_k\).
The exterior logarithms \(a>0\) have sum \(\log M(P)\le\log T\).
The function \(x\mapsto2\cosh(kx)-2\) vanishes at zero and has nonnegative increasing derivative for \(x\ge0\), hence is superadditive there.
Consequently
\[
             |s_k-t_k|\le\delta_k:=T^k+T^{-k}-2.                         \tag{1}
\]
At fixed \(k\) this error vanishes quadratically in \(\log T\).
For a single positive real exterior pair the difference in (1) is exactly \(\delta_k\) when \(M(P)=T\).

The angular measure is positive with total mass \(D\).
For any real even nonnegative trigonometric polynomial \(W(\theta)=w_0+2\sum_{k=1}^N w_k\cos(k\theta)\), sum \(W(\arg\alpha-\theta)\) over all roots and use (1).
This gives the necessary inequality
\[
        D w_0+2\sum_{k=1}^N w_k\cos(k\theta)s_k
          \ge-2\sum_{k=1}^N |w_k\cos(k\theta)|\delta_k                  \tag{2}
\]
for every real \(\theta\).
The Fejér choice \(w_0=1,\ w_k=1-k/(N+1)\) is nonnegative by the squared modulus of \(\sum_{j=0}^N e^{ij\theta}\) divided by \(N+1\).
When the test coefficients, \(T\) and \(\cos\theta\) are rational and the Newton sums are integers, every entry of (2) is rational.

## A common budget for all negative eigenvalues

Set \(S_N=(s_{|j-k|})_{0\le j,k\le N}\) and put
\[
       E_N(T)=\frac12\sum_{j=0}^N
            \left(T^{2j-N}+T^{N-2j}-2\right).
\]
The sum of the absolute values of the negative eigenvalues of \(S_N\) is at most \(E_N(T)\).
In particular \(S_N+cI\) is positive definite for every \(c>E_N(T)\), uniformly over the input degree.

Consider the Hermitian matrix contributed by each same-angle pair using signed exponents \(j-k\).
The sum over the pairs and the roots on the circle equals \(S_N\), since reciprocity gives \(s_{-k}=s_k\).
Each unit-circle root gives a positive rank-one matrix.
For the pair \(e^{\pm a+i\phi}\) define \(u_j=e^{ij\phi}\cosh((j-N/2)a)\) and \(v_j=e^{ij\phi}\sinh((j-N/2)a)\).
Its matrix is \(2uu^*-2vv^*\) by the hyperbolic cosine difference identity.
Write \(S_N=B-C\) with \(B,C\) positive semidefinite, where \(C\) sums all the terms \(2vv^*\).
An orthonormal family of negative eigenvectors of \(S_N\) gives a sum of absolute negative eigenvalues bounded by \(\operatorname{tr}C\).
The contribution to this trace from one pair is \(2\sum_j\sinh^2((j-N/2)a)=E_N(e^a)\).
Each summand is nonnegative, increasing and superadditive as a function of \(a\ge0\), by the same argument as for (1).
Summation over the exterior logarithms now gives \(\operatorname{tr}C\le E_N(T)\).
For the polynomial consisting of one real reciprocal pair, \(u^*v=0\) by centering, so the negative eigenvalue for \(a>0, N\ge1\) has absolute value \(E_N(e^a)\).
This proves optimality of the degree-independent bound over real reciprocal polynomials.

There is a refinement for inputs with no exterior root of modulus greater than \(\sqrt T\).
On \([0,\tfrac12\log T]\) convexity and the zero value at the origin give \(E_N(e^a)\le 2aE_N(\sqrt T)/\log T\) when \(T>1\).
The same trace argument then gives the smaller budget \(2E_N(\sqrt T)\).
In particular this applies if every exterior root is nonreal, since its complex conjugate has the same modulus.

## An exclusion missed by each of the five cyclotomic orders

At the [smaller-factor threshold](Yin_L_G0462_v266.md#target) \(T=1089/1000\), take \(N=10\) and \(s_1=\cdots=s_{10}=-6\).
The Fejér test at \(\theta=0\) has left side \(D-60\), while its error is strictly less than \(1647/1000\) by substitution into (2).
This excludes the prefix for both \(D=56\) and \(D=58\).
Its sign mate under \(P(X)\mapsto P(-X)\) is excluded at \(\theta=\pi\).
For the matrix test, the unshifted size-eleven matrix on the constant prefix has eigenvalue \(D-60\) on the constant vector, and \(E_{10}(1089/1000)<167/100\).

Both prefixes survive all five [cyclotomic tests](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v2/readme.md#sieve) through length fifteen, for every admissible extension.
For the negative constant prefix bound each partial score above using its known first ten terms and subsequently the full absolute Newton bounds.
The ratio of this upper estimate to its positive trace-box cap is less than \(4/5\) in both degrees at every one of the five orders and fifteen lengths.
The sign action permutes the tests, so the same conclusion holds on the other prefix.
The [fixed-prefix extension count](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v2/readme.md#extensions) for the positive constant six through length ten applies also to the negative constant six, since negation of all the Newton coordinates preserves their congruences and symmetric intervals.
The nonzero first sum excludes power substitutions, and each sign orbit in the union has exactly one extension on the specified negative side.
Thus this example alone excludes another 262,144 representatives in degree 56 and 524,288 in degree 58.
