# Integer coefficients and radial deficits for Pisot numbers close to their constant coefficient

<a id="second"></a>
## The second coefficient on a fixed branch

Let \(f\) be a Pisot minimal polynomial with positive constant \(q\ge2\), real exterior root \(\theta\), and \(\delta=\theta-q>0\).
Define \(g=f^*\), \(F=f/g=\sum_{n\ge0}u_nz^n\) at zero, \(D=zf+g\), and \(j=u_1-q^2+1\).
The \(u_n\) are integers since \(g(0)=1\).
For each fixed \(j\) and all sufficiently large \(q\), one has
\[
 \begin{split}
 u_2&=q^3+(2j-1)q+a,\qquad a\in\mathbb Z,\quad |a|\le 2j,\\
 \theta-q&=\frac{j}{q}+\frac{a}{q^2}+O_j(q^{-3}).
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{1}
\]
The normalization below gives integer restrictions at every fixed order as well.
Boyd used first-coefficient inequalities in his [interval search](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#negative-norm-cubic).
Here the rational series retaining these higher coefficients is attached to the actual signed delay-one associate.

Write
\[
 b(z)=\frac{1-\theta z}{\theta-z},\qquad B=bF,\qquad
 \epsilon=\frac{\delta}{\theta},\qquad H_q=\frac{1-B}{\epsilon}.
\]
The only pole of \(F\) in the disk is cancelled by \(b\), and \(B\) has unit boundary modulus.
Thus \(|B|\le1\) in the disk, \(B(0)=q/\theta\), and \(H_q\) has positive real part with \(H_q(0)=1\).
By the Schwarz lemma applied to \((H_q-1)/(H_q+1)\) one has \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\) and \(|H_q(z)|\le(1+R)/(1-R)\) on \(|z|\le R<1\).
Direct differentiation gives
\[
                    H_q'(0)=q+\theta^{-1}-j/\delta .                \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(j\ge1\) and \(q\delta=j+O_j(1/q)\) as \(q\to\infty\) for fixed \(j\).

<a id="limit"></a>
## An integer series with positive-real-part limits

Define the rational function
\[
 K_q(z)=\frac{(1-z^2)^2}{z}
       \left(\frac{f(z)}{D(z)}-\frac{q-z}{1-z^2}\right).
\]
The zero at the origin cancels in the numerator, and \(D(0)=1\), so \(K_q\) has integer Taylor coefficients at zero.
Using \(f/D=F/(1+zF)\) gives \(K_q(0)=j\) and \([z]K_q=u_2-q^3-(2j-1)q=a\).
For each \(0<R<1\), all sufficiently large \(q\) on this branch satisfy
\[
         K_q(z)=jH_q(z)+O_{R,j}(q^{-1})\qquad (|z|\le R).           \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(f/D=B/(b+zB)\), with
\[
 W=(\theta-z)(b+zB)=1-z^2-z(\theta-z)\epsilon H_q.
\]
On any fixed closed disk of radius less than one, \(W=1-z^2+O_{R,j}(1/q)\) and is eventually nonzero there.
This proves analyticity on the tested disk even though \(D\) has a zero in the open unit disk for each individual \(q\).
Algebra gives
\[
 K_q=\frac{1-z^2}{W}\left[
        \frac{\delta(1-z^2)}{z}
        +\epsilon(\theta-z)(q-z^{-1})H_q
                              \right].
\]
On each fixed circle \(0<|z|=R<1\), use \(\delta=O_j(1/q)\), \(q\delta=j+O_j(1/q)\), and the bound on \(H_q\).
This gives (3) on the circle and hence its disk by the maximum principle.
Cauchy's formula gives \(a=jH_q'(0)+O_j(1/q)\).
Integrality now implies \(|a|\le2j\) eventually, and solving (2) gives (1).

From any sequence on this fixed branch with \(q\to\infty\), extract a subsequence on which \(H_q\) converges locally uniformly by the displayed disk bounds.
By (3) the functions \(K_q\) converge locally uniformly on each strict disk to \(K=j+\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n\), with positive real part.
Every coefficient eventually equals its limiting integer on this subsequence.
The Herglotz representation gives a positive measure \(\mu\) on the circle of mass \(j\), symmetric under conjugation because the Taylor coefficients are real, with
\[
 K(z)=\int\frac{1+\overline v z}{1-\overline v z}\,d\mu(v).
\]
In particular \(a_1=a\) eventually, and \(a_1^2/j-2j\le a_2\le 2j\) by the variance inequality for \(\Re v\).
At \(a_1=\pm2j\), equality in the first moment forces all of \(\mu\) onto \(v=\pm1\), respectively, fixing every limiting coefficient.

<a id="periodic"></a>
## Periodicity of every limiting coefficient sequence

Put \(A(0)=2j\), \(A(n)=a_{|n|}\) for \(n\ne0\).
This integer sequence on \(\mathbb Z\) is the Fourier–Stieltjes transform of \(2\mu\), with norm \(2j\).
By [Sanders's Corollary 12.4](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes), \(A\) is a sum of a bounded number, depending only on \(j\), of signed coset indicators on \(\mathbb Z\).
Splitting each integer coefficient into repeated signed copies gives this form.
Each of the cosets is a singleton or an arithmetic progression.
It follows that \(A(n)\) is periodic outside a finite set, and \(K\) is rational with no poles except possibly simple poles at roots of unity.
Indeed the nonnegative-index generating series of a progression has denominator \(1-z^h\), and a singleton contributes at most one monomial.
This bounds the number of cosets in the description, without bounding their periods or the locations of the finite exceptions.

The periodic part is the Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a finite atomic measure supported on roots of unity, by the finite Fourier expansion over a common period.
The finite exceptions are the transform of a trigonometric-polynomial density against normalized arc length.
By uniqueness of Fourier coefficients their sum is \(2\mu\), so its atomic and absolutely continuous parts are both positive.
The limiting description applies to coefficients on fixed finite prefixes.
Every actual \(K_q\) on the positive-constant branch has a pole at the interior reciprocal of the exterior root of \(D\) from the [positive delay-one construction](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
Indeed this point lies strictly between zero and one, and \(f\) and \(g\) are coprime since their primitive irreducible polynomials are not associates when \(q>1\).
Thus no actual \(K_q\) equals one of the limiting rational functions.

<a id="weights"></a>
## Where the conjugates lose radial modulus

For each interior conjugate \(\beta_i=\rho_i v_i\) of \(\theta\) write \(\ell_i=-\log\rho_i>0\).
Their sum is \(L=\log(\theta/q)\), and \(\epsilon=1-e^{-L}\).
The probability measure \(\nu_q=L^{-1}\sum_i\ell_i\delta_{v_i}\) weights each direction by its share of this sum.
Along the same subsequence as above, \(\nu_q\) converges weakly to \(\mu/j\).
In contrast, the [unweighted directions](Yin_L_G0462_v34.md#geometry) become uniformly distributed.

To prove the weighted assertion, factor \(B\) as the product of the disk factors \((\rho_i-\overline v_i z)/(1-\rho_i\overline v_i z)\).
The factors have the correct interior zeros, unit boundary modulus and product \(q/\theta\) at zero, fixing the unimodular multiplier.
On \(|z|\le R<1\), put \(w=\overline v_i z\).
For \(L\) small the branch of the logarithm vanishing at \(\ell_i=0\) gives
\[
 \log\frac{e^{-\ell_i}-w}{1-e^{-\ell_i}w}
       =-\ell_i\frac{1+w}{1-w}+O_R(\ell_i^2)
\]
uniformly, by Taylor expansion in \(\ell_i\).
Sum using \(\sum_i\ell_i^2\le L^2\), then exponentiate and divide \(1-B\) by \(\epsilon=L+O(L^2)\).
It follows that \(H_q\) differs on that disk by \(O_R(L)\) from the Herglotz integral of \(\nu_q\).
The limiting Fourier coefficients determine the asserted weak limit on the compact circle.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree needed for a small first coefficient

More generally, if \(q/j\to\infty\), let \(d\) be the degree of the exterior root \(\sigma\) of \(D\).
Equations (2) and [Boyd's trace estimate](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#near-integer-lemma) imply \(\log\sigma\ll\sqrt{j/q}\), since \(2\cosh u-2\ge u^2\).
Eventually \(\sigma\) is Salem, and \(d\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness for bounded Mahler measure.
The [numerical Dobrowolski bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument) gives \(\log\sigma\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) there.
Consequently, writing \(X=(q/j)^{1/6}\to\infty\), one must have \(\log d\gg X\log X\).
Indeed \(\log d/\log\log d\gg X\) first gives \(\log d\gg X\), and substitution gives the extra factor.
The degree of the original Pisot seed is at least \(d-1\) because \(D=zf+g\).

## An exact limiting profile for the negative constant sign

[Boyd's cubic](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#negative-norm-cubic) \(h=z^3-(q-1)z^2-qz-q\) has constant \(-q\).
For \(q\ge2\) it has a unique positive root \(\theta>q\) by Descartes' rule of signs and \(h(q)<0\).
It has no root \(-t\) with \(t\ge0\), because \((q-1)t^2-qt+q>0\) by its negative discriminant.
The other two roots form a conjugate pair of modulus \(\sqrt{q/\theta}<1\), giving irreducibility by the nonzero integer constant argument.
Use the sign-normalized quotient \(F=-h/h^*\) with \(u_0=q\), hence replace \(f/D\) in the definition of \(K_q\) by \(-h/(h^*-zh)\).
Here \(u_1=q^2\), so \(j=1\), while
\[
 h^*-zh=(1+z)(1-z^3),\qquad K_q(z)=\frac{1-z^2}{1+z+z^2}
\]
identically for every \(q\).
This profile has measure equally divided between the primitive cube roots and first coefficient \(a=-1\).
The associated polynomial is entirely cyclotomic, and the two interior conjugates give this limiting radial distribution.
The positive-constant sign still requires a genuine interior pole whose effect escapes every fixed coefficient prefix.
