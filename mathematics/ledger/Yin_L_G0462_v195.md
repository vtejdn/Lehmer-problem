# Three multiplicity layers exclude the symmetric constant-trace exception

<a id="exception"></a>
## The cofactor bound at primes of bounded residue multiplicity

Let \(P\) be a reciprocal nontorsion unit minimal polynomial of even degree \(d\ge4\), and let \(p\ge5\) be a prime preserving the degree after powering a root \(\alpha\) to \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
Let \(Q\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) and \(s\) the largest multiplicity of a root of \(\overline P\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\).
In the [constant-trace pencil](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#vanishing), take \(b\) to be its prescribed least positive denominator.
For **every** admissible member of this pencil the [finite cofactor bound](Yin_L_G0462_v192.md#cost) holds if
\[
                              p\ge 3s+2.                                  \tag{1}
\]
This includes primes dividing the discriminant.
It also suffices that \(p\ge3s\) and some residue root \(\omega\) of multiplicity \(s\) satisfies \(\omega^2\ne-1\).
In the fourth-logarithmic window these criteria give \(m(R_F)/(p\,m(P))\ge1-o(1)\), uniformly as in the finite bound.

Write \(A_e=\mathbb Z[x]/(P^e)\), \(B_e=\mathbb Z[X]/(Q^e)\subset A_e\) with \(X=x^p\), so \(b\) is the additive order of \(x\) modulo \(B_2\).
In these orders the variables are units.
If the exceptional symmetry in [L192](Yin_L_G0462_v192.md#symmetry) holds, let \(B=X^{-d}A\) for the row \(A\), \(C=(B+B^\iota)/2\) with \(\iota:X\mapsto X^{-1}\), and \(E=X\,d/dX\).
Then \(B=C+pEC\), \(C-p^2E^2C\) has value \(b\) at \(\beta\), and \(B(\beta)=b(1-\sigma\alpha)\).
Putting \(q=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) and \(\delta=\alpha-\alpha^{-1}\), these identities and their inverses give
\[
 2C(\beta)=b(2-\sigma q),\qquad
 2p EC(\beta)=-\sigma b\delta,\qquad
 2p^2 E^2C(\beta)=-\sigma b q .
\]
Thus \(2C(x^p)-b(2-\sigma(x+x^{-1}))\) and its first two Euler derivatives vanish at \(\alpha\).
Since \(2C\) is integral, separability and monic division after clearing a monomial yield
\[
 b(x+x^{-1})\in B_3,\qquad (b/p)(x-x^{-1})\in B_2.                   \tag{2}
\]
For the second inclusion differentiate the congruence modulo \(P^3\) once and divide by \(p\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
The resulting powered representative is integral, and \(p\mid b\) by the [denominator calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#vanishing).

<a id="coordinates"></a>
## Denominators in one residue class

Extend \(\mathbb Z_p\) to the ring of integers \(\mathcal O\) of a finite unramified extension over which \(\overline P\) splits.
This preserves the \(p\)-part of the order of any given element modulo \(B_e\), by faithful flatness.
Hensel factorization decomposes both orders by their distinct root residues, with the residue of \(x\) paired with its \(p\)-th power for \(X\).
These labels are in bijection by Frobenius, so the local factors can be tested independently.

In a block of multiplicity \(s_0\), choose the Teichmüller lift \(\omega\) of its nonzero residue.
Write \(Z=x/\omega-1\), \(N=e s_0\).
The block of \(A_e\) has the presentation \(\mathcal O[Z]/((Z^{s_0}+pG(Z))^e)\) for an integral \(G\) of degree less than \(s_0\).
In particular \(Z^N\in p A_e\) in this block.
When \(p>N\), set \(Y=((1+Z)^p-1)/p\), an integral element there.
Expanding the defining relation modulo \(p^2\) and multiplying by \(Z^{p-N}\) gives
\[
               Z^p\equiv-e p Z^{p-s_0}G(Z)\pmod {p^2}.
\]
In the special fiber \(\kappa[Z]/Z^N\), where \(\kappa=\mathcal O/p\), the ordinary binomial coefficients therefore give
\[
       Y\equiv \sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(-1)^{i-1} Z^i/i
                       \pmod{(p,Z^{p-s_0})}.                           \tag{3}
\]
If \(p-s_0\ge2\) and \(N\ge2\), the linear term is one, so \(1,Y,\ldots,Y^{N-1}\) form a basis by reduction modulo \(p\).
As \(X=\omega^p(1+pY)\), the powered suborder in the block is \(\mathcal O[pY]\), with basis \(1,pY,\ldots,p^{N-1}Y^{N-1}\).
Indeed its powers up to degree \(N-1\) are independent and span by the integral characteristic polynomial of \(pY\).
For \(0\le j<\min(N,p-s_0)\), inversion of (3) in the truncated polynomial ring gives the following residues of the \(Y^j\) coordinates:
\[
 [Y^j]x=\bar\omega/j!,\qquad
 [Y^j](x\pm x^{-1})=(\bar\omega\pm(-1)^j\bar\omega^{-1})/j!
                              \quad\text{in }\kappa .                  \tag{4}
\]
Here the first coordinate formula uses \(x=\omega(1+Z)\), and the exponential and logarithm truncate below \(p\).
A unit coefficient at \(j\) requires at least \(p^j\) in any integer denominator that puts the element in the powered order.
Conversely \(p^{N-1}\) suffices for every element in the block.

For \(e=2,\ p\ge3s\), (4) covers every coordinate in every block.
In a block of largest multiplicity the top coefficient of \(x\) is a unit, giving the **exact** exponent \(v_p(b)=2s-1\).
If \(\bar\omega^2\ne-1\) in such a block, \(x-x^{-1}\) also has a unit top coefficient because \(2s-1\) is odd.
The second inclusion in (2) would then force \(v_p(b)\ge2s\).

For \(e=3,\ s\ge2,\ p\ge3s+2\), apply (4) in a largest block at \(j=2s,2s+1\).
Both indices are below \(3s\) and \(p-s\), and at least one coefficient of \(x+x^{-1}\) is a unit because \(2\bar\omega\ne0\).
The first inclusion in (2) again requires \(v_p(b)\ge2s\), proving (1).
For \(s=1\) the good-prime [symmetry exclusion](Yin_L_G0462_v192.md#pencil) already applies.
When \(p\ge4s\), the same calculation determines the full third-layer exponent for the trace in a block: it is \(3s_0-1\) unless \(\bar\omega^2=(-1)^{3s_0}\), in which case it is \(3s_0-2\).
In the latter case the penultimate coefficient is a unit and the last is divisible by \(p\), which proves the upper bound as well.

## An exact rational test at the remaining primes

Let \(H\) be the minimal polynomial of \(q\), of degree \(d/2\), and define \(V_p(t)\) by \(V_p(x+x^{-1})=x^p+x^{-p}\).
Let \(G_0(Y)\in\mathbb Q[Y]\) of degree \(<3d/2\) be the unique polynomial satisfying
\[
                        G_0(V_p(t))\equiv t\pmod{H(t)^3}.               \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(q'=V_p(q)\) generates the same trace field as \(q\): both lie in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and each is fixed by the inverse involution with relative degree two.
Moreover \(V_p'(q)=p(\beta-\beta^{-1})/(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\ne0\).
Consequently substitution gives an injective map from the rational quotient by the cube of the minimal polynomial of \(q'\) to the quotient by \(H^3\), since the order of vanishing at \(q'\) is preserved at \(q\).
Their dimensions are both \(3d/2\), proving existence and uniqueness in (5).

For a symmetric member, \(2C(X)\) is a rational polynomial in \(X+X^{-1}\) of degree at most \(d\).
The triple congruence in (2) and uniqueness in (5) force
\[
  C(X)=\frac b2\big(2-\sigma G_0(X+X^{-1})\big),\qquad
  A(X)=X^d(C(X)+pEC(X)).                                               \tag{6}
\]
Conversely (5) gives the three displayed jet values of \(2C\) at \(\beta\), so the row (6) has the two prescribed jets modulo \(P^2\) and satisfies the required symmetry identically.
An admissible symmetric member exists precisely when \(\deg G_0=d\), the leading coefficient \(M\) of (6) is an integer, and \(G_0-2\sigma\) is squarefree with \(G_0(2),G_0(-2)\ne2\sigma\).
In fact the degree equality gives nonzero endpoint coefficients in (6) by the multipliers \(1\pm pd\); a lower degree loses the endpoints, and a higher degree is impossible for the prescribed row.
With that equality the difference between (6) and the integral representative of degree \(<2d\) from the pencil is \(M Q^2\), since their jets agree and \(X=x^p\) is unramified at the nonzero roots.
Thus integrality is equivalent to \(M\in\mathbb Z\), and primitivity follows from the minimal denominator as in the [pencil construction](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#vanishing).
Finally the reciprocal row is \(X^d(C-pEC)\).
Its common roots with (6) are exactly the nonzero common roots of \(C\) and \(EC\), which gives the stated squarefreeness and endpoint tests.
This decides whether the single candidate occurs for a specified input even outside (1); an unrestricted cost bound if it occurs remains open.

## Nonconstant reduced trace values with a proportional derivative

More generally use the [odd-span trace data](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd) with zero remainder \(D\), and write \(u=U_0(q)\ne0\), where \(\deg U_0<d/2\).
The condition \(v/u\in\mathbb Q\) holds **exactly** for
\[
 U_0(t)=b(2\sigma-t)^j,\quad b\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\quad
 0\le j<d/2,\qquad v=-(2j+1)u.                                       \tag{7}
\]
Indeed \(D=0\) says \(v=-u+2(2\sigma-q)U_0'(q)\).
Proportionality makes \((2\sigma-t)U_0'\) a rational scalar times \(U_0\) as a polynomial, since both have degree below the degree of \(q\).
Expanding in powers of \(2\sigma-t\) shows that just one exponent occurs, proving (7).

For an adjacent shift \(S\equiv e\bmod p\), \(e\in\{1,-1\}\), put \(\ell=(J-e)/(2p)\), \(B=X^{-\ell}A\), as in [L192](Yin_L_G0462_v192.md#cost).
For (7) use \(T_j=(j+1)B-e pEB\).
The value and derivative equations give \(T_j(\beta)=(2j+1)u\) for \(e=1\) and \(T_j(\beta)=-\sigma(2j+1)u\) for \(e=-1\).
Both values are fixed by inversion.
Thus \(Q\mid T_j-T_j^\iota\); whenever the difference is nonzero the two finite cofactor and row bounds of L192 hold with
\[
 L=\log\bigl(2(j+1+p(\ell+1))\bigr)+2\log(1+C_0 n).
\]
This follows from \(|T_j-T_j^\iota|\le2|T_j|\) and the same logarithmic derivative estimate for \(T_j/B\).
In the stated window and cap the extra \(j<d/2\) preserves the uniform coefficient-one lower bound.
For \(S>1\), the left endpoint coefficient of \(T_j\) is nonzero: its multiplier is \(j+1+e p\ell\), positive for \(e=1\) and strictly negative for \(e=-1\) because \(p\ell=(J+1)/2\) and \(J\ge2d\).
Its upper support endpoint is at most \(n-\ell<\ell\), ruling out symmetry.

If \(S=1\) and \(T_j=T_j^\iota\), putting \(C=(B+B^\iota)/2\) gives \(B=C+pEC/(j+1)\).
Here \(2C(\beta)=\sigma b(2\sigma-q)^{j+1}\) and \(T_j=(j+1)C-p^2E^2C/(j+1)\).
Consequently
\[
 p^2(E^2(2C))(\beta)=-(j+1)b(2\sigma-q)^j
                                \bigl(2j+(j+1)\sigma q\bigr).
\]
At \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\), \(2\sigma-q\) is a unit modulo \(p\) in \(\mathbb Z[q]\), since a trace residue \(2\sigma\) would produce a double root \(\sigma\) of \(\overline P\).
If \(p\nmid j+1\), the last linear factor is nonzero modulo \(p\) in the power basis of \(\mathbb Z[q]\), whose rank is at least two.
As \(\mathbb Z[q]\) is a direct summand of \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), this forces \(p^2\mid b\).

These data are realized in the integral pencil with \(n=2d,\ J=2pd+1\) by representing
\[
 b x^{pd}(1-\sigma x)(2\sigma-x-x^{-1})^j\pmod {P^2}
\]
in \(B_2\), taking the least positive integer \(b\) that permits this, and adding \(M Q^2\) to the reduced integral row.
The target has zero signed reversal sum, and its centered Laurent trace representative is precisely \((1-\sigma x)U_0(x+x^{-1})\), giving \(D=0\).
As in the constant pencil, the difference from the reversed row evaluates at \(\beta\) to \(-\sigma b\beta^d(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})(2\sigma-q)^j\ne0\).
Coprimality and nonzero endpoints thus hold outside finitely many \(M\).
A common content divisor \(g\) would make \((b/g)(2\sigma-q)^j\in\mathbb Z[q]\) by comparing the \(\alpha\) coordinates of the original row value; the leading coefficient of the reduced trace polynomial gives \(b/g\in\mathbb Z\), and minimality gives \(g=1\).
At a good prime \(p\) the two-layer quotient has \(p\)-part of exponent \(p\), so \(v_p(b)\le1\).
Hence for \(p\nmid j+1\) every admissible member of these minimal-denominator pencils satisfies the cofactor bound, including the nonconstant cases \(1\le j<d/2\).
