# Boyd width and sparse completions at the nonconstant delay-two profiles

<a id="width"></a>
## The width of a limiting associated function

Let \(F_i=\operatorname{sgn}(f_i(0))f_i/f_i^*\) be Boyd associated functions of distinct nonreciprocal monic Pisot minimal polynomials.
Suppose their Taylor coefficients stabilize at a derived function \(F=A/Q\) with one simple pole \(1/\theta_*\) in the disk.
We use integer polynomials with \(Q(0)=1,\ A(0)>0\), allowing a common cyclotomic factor with constant one.
Suppose that after removal of the common factor the denominator has just the one disk zero and no circle zero.
Write \(\Delta(z)=Q(z)Q(1/z)-A(z)A(1/z)\), which is nonnegative on the circle.
Assume it is nonzero.
Since \(Q(0)=1\), Jensen's formula gives \(M(Q)=\theta_*\), also in the presence of the allowed common factor.
The [width formula of Boyd](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#narrow-branches) then gives
\[
                 w(F)=\theta_*^2 M(1-|F|^2)=M(\Delta).
\]
Here the multiplicative measure of a nonnegative function is the exponential of its mean logarithm.
In particular the width remains the same after removing any common cyclotomic factor.

<a id="completions"></a>
## From width one to a bounded-term multiple of the actual seed

Suppose \(M(\Delta)=1\).
Let \(b\) be the highest exponent of the symmetric Laurent polynomial \(\Delta\).
Its coefficients at \(\pm b\) coincide and have modulus one, by the integer leading coefficient bound for Mahler measure.
A sufficiently late first discrepancy of \(F_i\) from \(F\) has value \(+1\) or \(-1\), and each signed departure determines at most one continuation in Boyd's class by [Lemma 1](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#narrow-branches).
The strict inequality required there is \(1<(1+\sqrt{17})/4\).

For clarity about the reversal, take a large integer \(p\), a sign \(s=\pm1\), and put
\[
            Q_p(z)=Q(z)+s z^p A(1/z),\qquad
            A_p(z)=A(z)+s z^p Q(1/z)=s z^pQ_p(1/z).
\]
These are ordinary integer polynomials once \(p\) exceeds both fixed degrees.
Their constants are respectively \(1,A(0)\), and \(sA_p\) is monic.
The identity
\[
                \frac{A_p}{Q_p}-\frac A Q
                       =\frac{s z^p\Delta(z)}{Q_p Q}
\]
shows that the first discrepancy occurs at \(p-b\), with opposite unit values for the two signs.
They therefore realize every sufficiently late possible signed departure once their quotients are shown to belong to Boyd's class.

To count the interior zeros of \(Q_p\), first multiply its high term by a real factor \(0<r<1\).
If \(A,Q\) have a common cyclotomic factor \(G\), then \(z^{\deg G}G(1/z)=\pm G(z)\), so both terms retain that factor.
After division by \(G\), on the circle the shrunk term is strictly smaller than the original denominator by \(|A|\le |Q|\) and the absence of any remaining circle zero.
Rouché gives exactly one interior zero for the shrunk sum.
Taking \(r\) to one gives at most one strict interior zero of \(Q_p\), counted with multiplicity.
For all sufficiently large \(p\), on a small disk around \(1/\theta_*\) the polynomials \(Q_p,A_p\) converge uniformly to \(Q,A\).
Thus \(Q_p\) has a simple zero there and \(A_p\) has none there.
Reality and uniqueness make this zero real and positive.
On the circle \(A_p\) is a signed reversal of \(Q_p\), so any circle zeros cancel with their multiplicities and the reduced quotient has boundary modulus one.
The quotient has one simple interior pole and integer Taylor coefficients with the prescribed positive constant, as required by [Boyd's characterization](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#narrow-branches).

For every sufficiently late input, \(F_i=A_p/Q_p\) by uniqueness after the first discrepancy.
The \(F_i\) are distinct and have modulus one on the circle whereas \(\Delta\ne0\), so each has such a discrepancy.
Its pole identifies \(\theta_i\) as the sole strictly exterior root of \(A_p\).
Since \(sA_p\) is monic integral with nonzero constant, its other irreducible factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
Consequently \(sA_p=C_i f_i\) with \(C_i\) a product of cyclotomic polynomials, possibly one.
This exhibits the bound \(\#\operatorname{supp}(C_i f_i)\le\#\operatorname{supp} A+\#\operatorname{supp}Q\), including when neither of the two fixed polynomials was reduced.
For any signs \(\sigma_i\) and delays \(N_i\ge1\), the exact identity \(C_i^*=\pm C_i\) makes \(C_i(z^{N_i}f_i-\sigma_i f_i^*)\) a sum of two translates or reversals of \(sA_p\), with the appropriate signs.
Its measure is that of the original associate.
The [fixed-support Mahler gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) therefore excludes a sequence of these measures tending to one strictly from above.

## The binomial and reciprocal delay-two profiles

The [delay-two limit classification](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#limit) expresses a derived Boyd function by a real rational function \(K\) as
\[
                 F=\frac{K-1+z}{z(1-z-zK)} .
\]
For \(K=k/l\), where \(k(0)=l(0)=1\), take
\[
                 A=\frac{k-(1-z)l}{z},\qquad Q=(1-z)l-zk .
\]
On the circle, subtracting the squared moduli gives
\[
                 |Q|^2-|A|^2=2|1-z|^2\operatorname{Re}(k\bar l).
\]
Indeed with \(X=(1-z)l\) the left side is \(|X-zk|^2-|X-k|^2\).
For \(K=1+\epsilon z^m\) or \(K=(1-\epsilon z^m)^{-1}\), \(m\ge1,\ \epsilon=\pm1\), the right side becomes respectively
\[
                |1-z|^2|1+\epsilon z^m|^2,\qquad
                |1-z|^2|1-\epsilon z^m|^2 .
\]
Both have measure one for every fixed \(m\).
The displayed choices \(k,l\) are coprime in each case.
At a common zero of \(A,Q\) one has \(k=(1-z)l\) and \((1-z)^2l=0\), forcing \(z=1\).
Any remaining circle zero of \(Q\) would also be a zero of \(A\) with at least the same multiplicity by \(|A|\le|Q|\), so there is none after reduction.
For an actual derived function the reduced denominator has just its single simple disk pole.
Thus all of these nonconstant profiles with positive \(A(0)\) satisfy the preceding hypotheses, including cancellation at \(1\).

At seed norm two the derivative condition \(K'(0)=1\) selects \(K=1+z\) and \(K=(1-z)^{-1}\) among these profiles.
They give
\[
                \frac{2}{1-2z-z^2},\qquad
                \frac{2-z}{1-3z+z^2},
\]
with defects \(|1-z^2|^2\) and \(|1-z|^4\), respectively.
Their reciprocal completions give seed multiples with at most five terms and associate multiples with at most ten.
At seed norm one the derivative condition \(K'(0)=0\) allows both signs when \(m\ge2\).
The formulas for \(A,Q\) then use at most three and four terms respectively, giving an associate multiple with at most fourteen terms.
The coefficient neighborhood required for uniqueness can depend on the fixed limit profile.
The term bounds and the resulting sparse Mahler gap are independent of \(m\).

For \(K=1\) the same calculation gives \(F=1/(1-2z)\) and \(\Delta=2|1-z|^2\), of width two.
Boyd's width-one conclusion above leaves this constant case open.
