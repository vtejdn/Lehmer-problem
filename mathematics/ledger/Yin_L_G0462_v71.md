# A six-term trace lift with a binomial factor in its initial coefficients

<a id="six-term"></a>
## An absolute gap for arbitrary branch index and common offset

Let a positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomial have constant \(q\ge2\), exterior root \(\theta\) and [trace numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) of degree \(h\) and leading coefficient \(j\ge1\).
Set \(N=2h+2\) and \(w=z^d\), where \(d\) is a positive integer with \(2d\le h\).
There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that \(\theta-q\ge c\) whenever its trace lift has the form
\[
 \Pi=A(z)-z^N A(1/z),\qquad
 A(z)=(1+s w)(j+t w),\qquad s=\pm1,\quad t\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus among the [six-term possibilities](Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) on \(j=2\), the exponent pair \((d,2d)\) with coefficients \((a,b)=(3,1),(-3,1),(1,-1),(-1,-1)\) has \(q\) uniformly bounded over all \(d,h\).
Indeed \(a=2s+t,\ b=st\) for \(t=\pm1\), and the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(q\le 2/c+2\).

Suppose there were a sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\), and pass to a fixed sign \(s\).
The normalized coefficient \(\lambda=t/j\) is bounded by the [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound), since \(\Pi\) has at most six terms, \(M(\Pi)=j\), and its coefficient at \(2d\) is \(st\).
Extract with \(\lambda\to\lambda_0\).
The normalized lifts have bounded circle supremum and an integral prefix through \(d-1\), so \(h\to\infty\) and \(d=o(h)\) by the [varying-branch divisibility theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement).
Write \(B^\sharp=\sum_{l=0}^N b_lz^l\) for the actual lifted denominator, \(J=j/K_q\), and \(\omega\) for its [signed inverse measure](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure).
Then \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J\), \(\omega\) has mass one and sole negative part \(\gamma\delta_1\) with \(\gamma=j/[4(q-1)]\to0\), and \(M(B^\sharp)\to1\) strictly from above by the [delay-one bound](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).

The exact residual factor after division by \(1+s w\) is
\[
       \frac{\Pi}{j(1+s w)}=1+\lambda w-s z^{N-2d}(w+\lambda).
\]
If \(|\lambda_0|>1\), fix \(b\in(1/|\lambda_0|,1)\) and take \(|z|=b^{1/d}\).
The first two terms have modulus at least \(|\lambda|b-1>0\) bounded away from zero, while the reflected term is \(o(1)\) since \(d=o(h)\).
Rouché's theorem gives \(d\) interior zeros of the residual factor, contradicting the circle roots of the actual lift.

<a id="unit-prefix"></a>
## Division by an integral unit on a positive fraction of the degree

Fix \(0<\alpha\le\beta<1,\ M<\infty,\ \eta>0\).
For any actual trace lift with \(\|\Pi/j\|_\infty\le M\), suppose that through degree \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) it agrees with
\[
                 G(z)(1+\lambda z^d),\qquad
 G\in1+z\mathbb Z[[z]],\quad 1\le d\le h,\quad \alpha\le|\lambda|\le\beta.
\]
Then \(\theta-q\) has a positive lower bound depending only on \(\alpha,\beta,M,\eta\).
No size, degree or support bound on \(G\) is needed.
On a hypothetical vanishing-excess sequence we may extract \(\lambda\) with an interior nonzero limit, and the integral prefix below \(d\) again forces \(d=o(h)\).
Since \(G^{-1}\) has integer coefficients, the series \(X=B^\sharp/G\) has integer coefficients \(x_l\) and agrees through the given prefix with \((1+\lambda z^d)J\).

The [interior-ratio moment argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v15.md#interior) applies to these coefficients.
To see its input scope, for \(d\to\infty\) and \(t'=o(d)\) the tests at \(rt'\) and \(d+rt'\) use just the doubled moments of the same \(\omega\) and the integrality of the \(x_l\) on the prefix.
They force a Haar limit for the first coordinate, giving a vanishing initial interval and the Bessel support bound on every \([0,Ld]\) for fixed \(L\).
For bounded \(d\) the latter bound is immediate.
The coefficients in these ranges are uniformly bounded by their moment expressions.
At scales \(d\ll t'\ll h\), the [integer projected rows](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows) test \(x_{kt'+ld}\) for fixed \(k\ge1,\ l\in\mathbb Z\).
The three applicable [interior-ratio profile and extinction results](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v15.md#interior) use this integer prefix, the limiting multiplier and the vanishing negative mass.
Thus the surviving atomic moments at shifts zero and six agree, giving \(x_{t'+6d}=x_{t'}\) eventually.
Extract a fixed \(L\) with \(1/L<\eta\) such that this period holds throughout \(Ld\le t'\le h/L\).

Let \(U=1-z^{6d}\), truncate \(UX\) at \(H=\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q\), and put \(A_0=1+\lambda z^d\).
The period and the local support bound show that \(Q\) has bounded coefficient sum in absolute value and degree at most \((L+6)d\).
The series \(J_*=Q/(UA_0)\) agrees with \(J\) through \(H\).
Its positive coefficients have a Fourier measure of bounded variation by the [congruence-class expansion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#comparison), whose correction counts here are bounded by a constant times \(\sum_{n\ge0}(1+n)\beta^n\).
The exact identity on the *original* denominator is
\[
      A_0 U B^\sharp-Q\Pi/j=A_0 U(\Pi/j)(J-J_*).
\]
The polynomial on the left has degree \(O(h)\), while the right has bounded circle multipliers and a zero Taylor prefix of length comparable to \(h\).
The [two cutoffs](Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) bound its circle \(L^1\) norm.
Since \(Q\Pi/j\) is bounded on the circle and \(|A_0|\ge1-\beta\), the integer polynomial \(UB^\sharp\) has bounded \(L^1\) norm and hence [bounded support](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window).
Its measure contradicts the fixed-support gap.
For our six-term family take \(G=1+s w\), with prefix through \(h\), to rule out \(0<|\lambda_0|<1\).

<a id="short"></a>
## Clearing the denominator coefficients through the second offset

For the remaining limits we need a cyclotomic polynomial product \(W\), up to sign, with at most two terms, such that
\[
                   W P_D\text{ has bounded support},\qquad
                   P_D=\sum_{l=0}^D b_lz^l,\quad D=d\ \text{or}\ 2d.
\]
For bounded \(d\) take \(W=1\).
When \(d\to\infty\), the moments \(m_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega\) equal \(b_l\) for \(1\le l<d\), with \(m_0=2\) and \(J(0)=1\).
As in the [short-prefix lemma](Yin_L_G0462_v68.md#low), a subsequence matches one of the models \(1,\ 1+\epsilon z^u,\ (1-\epsilon z^u)^{-1}\) for \(J\) through \(L_0=\lfloor c_0d\rfloor\), where \(0<c_0<1/4\).
The signed prefix and the \(2h\) distinct positive atoms of the actual measure are precisely the hypotheses used to get those models.
On the two open intervals \((0,d)\) and \((d,2d)\) the coefficient \(b_l\) is \(\int v^l T(v)\,d\omega\), with respective multipliers \(T=2\) and \(T=2(1+(s+\lambda)v^{-d})\).
Both multipliers are uniformly bounded, and the remaining single indices \(0,d,2d\) cost at most three terms.

For the first two models the Gram matrix of any choice of distinct characters in a block of diameter \(L_0\) against \(\omega\) equals that of a probability with density bounded by two.
For any fixed number \(K\) of characters, passage to \(\omega^+\) changes the Gram norm by at most \(K\gamma\) and each integral against \(T\) by at most \(\gamma\|T\|_\infty\).
Bessel's inequality therefore bounds the number of nonzero integer integrals on each block, by testing \(K>2\sup\|T\|_\infty^2+1\).
Partition each open interval separately into \(O(1)\) such blocks to obtain the assertion with \(W=1\).
For the reciprocal model with \(u>L_0/4\) the moments through \(\lfloor L_0/4\rfloor\) agree with Haar probability, so the same proof applies on shorter blocks.

In the remaining reciprocal case put \(W=1-\epsilon z^u\), \(u\le L_0/4\).
When \(l,l-u\) are in the same open interval above, the integer difference \(b_l-\epsilon b_{l-u}\) is the integral of \(v^l W(v^{-1})T(v)\) against \(\omega\).
The Gram matrix of the weighted characters \(v^lW(v^{-1})\) on any block of diameter at most \(L_0/2\) agrees with the model Gram matrix, since all its tested frequencies and their shifts by \(u\) lie in the matched range in absolute value.
The model is half Haar and half uniform on \(v^u=\epsilon\), so weighting by \(|W|^2\) kills the atoms and leaves density at most two.
Against \(\omega^+\) the Gram correction for \(K\) characters has norm at most \(4K\gamma\) and the integral corrections are at most \(2\gamma\|T\|_\infty\).
The same fixed-size Bessel test bounds the number of nonzero differences on each of the boundedly many blocks.
In the strips where \(l\) and \(l-u\) cross a partition point or a truncation endpoint, bound the two original coefficient lists separately, extending \(b_l\) by zero outside \([0,D]\) for this calculation.
Apart from the three individual boundary indices, the contributing lists split into a bounded number of intervals of length at most \(u\), each within one open interval of constant multiplier \(T\) as a function of the index.
Their distinct character differences have absolute value strictly below \(u\), where the model has Haar moments.
The unweighted Bessel test on each such interval bounds its number of nonzero coefficients.
This handles the seam at \(d\) and the ends of the polynomial \(WP_D\).

<a id="fold"></a>
## Folding at the two limiting reciprocal blocks

If \(\lambda_0=0\), put \(F=1+s w,\ D=d,\ \epsilon=s\).
If \(\lambda_0=\tau=\pm1\), put \(F=(1+s w)(1+\tau w),\ D=2d,\ \epsilon=s\tau\).
Thus \(F=\sum f_a z^a\) is integral, has uniformly bounded absolute coefficient sum, and satisfies \(f_{D-a}=\epsilon f_a\).
With \(p=N-D>D\), the difference \(\Pi/j-F(1-\epsilon z^p)\) has absolute coefficient sum \(o(1)\), by expansion at the respective limit.
At each support point of \(\omega\) we have \(\Pi(v)=0\), hence \(|F(v)|\,|1-\epsilon v^p|=o(1)\) uniformly.

Let \(I\) be the remainder of \(B^\sharp\) modulo \(z^p-\epsilon\).
Its coefficient \(I_l\) equals \(b_l+\epsilon b_{D-l}\) for \(0\le l\le D\) and \(b_l\) for \(D<l<p\).
For every \(0\le l<p\), uniformly,
\[
                           I_l=\sum_{a=0}^D f_a m_{l-a}+o(1).
\]
Indeed the coefficient of \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J\) at such \(l\) differs from \([z^l]FJ\) by \(o(1)\) by boundedness of the Taylor coefficients of \(J\).
For \(l\le D\), summing the two truncated convolutions reflects the second using \(f_{D-a}=\epsilon f_a\) and \(m_{-k}=m_k\).
They each supply \(f_l J(0)\), adding to \(f_l m_0\), while all other summands occur once.
For \(D<l<p\) only positive Taylor indices enter.

Round the signed measure weighted by \(2F(v^{-1})\) to the nearest points \(\zeta^p=\epsilon\) in angle, splitting ties equally.
It has uniformly bounded variation and conjugation symmetry.
For \(l<p\) the error of its \(l\)-th moment is \(o(1)\) because \(|v^l-\zeta^l|\le(\pi/2)|1-\epsilon v^p|\) and \(|F(v^{-1})|=|F(v)|\).
On \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\) its transform is within \(o(1)\) of the integer function extended by \(I_{l+p}=\epsilon I_l\).
The [finite-group rounding and coset argument](Yin_L_G0462_v68.md#fold) therefore gives a product \(V\) of boundedly many binomials \(1-z^t\) with \(VI\) of bounded support.
The argument uses the variation bound, integer approximation on both halves of the group and the fact that a coset restricted to the first \(p\) indices is an ordinary finite arithmetic progression.

Finally \(B^\sharp=I+(z^p-\epsilon)z^D P_D(1/z)\).
The reversal \(W^*\) from the short-segment argument makes \(W^*z^D P_D(1/z)\) the reversal of \(WP_D\), with equally many terms.
Consequently \(VW^*B^\sharp\) has bounded support and equals \(B^\sharp\) in Mahler measure, a contradiction to the [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This excludes both remaining limits and proves the uniform six-term assertion.
For \((a,b)=(1,1),(-1,1)\) on \(j=2,\ e=2d\), the displayed integral binomial factorization is unavailable, so those two unit-coefficient branches remain open here.
