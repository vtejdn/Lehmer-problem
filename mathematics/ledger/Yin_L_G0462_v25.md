# Quarter-turn resultants at the degree of the plastic-seed Salem polynomials

<a id="quarter-turn"></a>
## The integer return from the two quarter turns

Use the [height-one subsequence](Yin_L_G0462_v23.md#degree-returns) with \(f=z^3-z-1,\ g=1-z^2-z^3\),
\[
     S(z)=\frac{z^Nf(z)-g(z)}{z-1},\qquad d=\deg S=N+2 ,
\]
where \(N\) runs through sufficiently large multiples of the fixed integer specified there.
These \(S\) are irreducible Salem polynomials.
Set \(Q_i(z)=if(z)-z^2g(z)=z^5+z^4+iz^3-z^2-iz-i\).
Then
\[
   \log|\operatorname{Res}(S,z^d-i)|=d\log M(Q_i)+O(\log d),
   \qquad
   \log|\operatorname{Res}(S,z^{2d}+1)|=2d\log M(Q_i)+O(\log d),        \tag{1}
\]
with \(M(Q_i)>29/20>\sqrt2\).
The full-turn and half-turn absolute resultants at \(d\) are \(N-7\) and \(9\), respectively.
The [uniform bound at exponent \(d\)](Yin_L_G0462_v24.md#arbitrary-phase) gives \(\sup_{|w|=1}|\operatorname{Res}(S,z^d-w)|=o(2^d)\).
Nevertheless their quarter-turn product in (1) is the single integer binomial resultant at exponent \(2d\), for which the elementary cost is \(2^d\).
Consequently
\[
       \liminf\log M(S)\ \ge\ \log M(Q_i)-\tfrac12\log2
                           \ >\ \log\!\left(\frac{29}{20\sqrt2}\right)>0. \tag{2}
\]
On 2026-11-16 the main researcher and proof contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained the quarter-turn comparison and its finite disk bound.

## A phase-uniform upper bound with the original multiplicities

Let \(\alpha\) be any nontorsion algebraic integer unit of degree \(d\), \(P\) its minimal polynomial and \(m=\log M(P)\).
For an integer \(n\ge1\) put \(\beta=\alpha^n,\ D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), and \(h=nm/d\).
For \(|w|=1\) write \(X(w)=d^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^n-w)|\), using \(\log0=-\infty\).
For \(0<h\le e^{-40}\) one has
\[
   \sup_{|w|=1}\max(0,X(w))\le
   \Phi_5(h)=86\sqrt h\,\frac{(\log(1/h))^{5/2}}{(\log\log(1/h))^{3/2}}. \tag{3}
\]
The [existing cyclic estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#statement) already gives (3) at \(w=1\), including its positive-income inverse, and the [degree-linear window theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0391_v1.md#bounded-power-degree-escape) already gives uniform convergence there as \(m\to0\).

For the rotation, each distinct conjugate of \(\beta\) occurs exactly \(d/D\) times among the original powered conjugates.
In the [direct energy calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v2.md#normalized-chord-energy-calculation) rotate the compact test itself, using \(\phi_w(z)=\phi(\bar w z)\).
Its truncated chord is \(|w-z|/\max(1,|z|)\), with cutoff height \(T\ge1\) and the same radial cutoff at radius \(R\ge2\).
Rotation preserves the global Lipschitz constant \(3e^T+T/R\), the Dirichlet integral bound \(D[\phi_w]/(2\pi)\le4T+7\), the unit-circle mean bound \(e^{-T}/2\), and the one-sided exterior error \(1/R\).
Apply the [actual powered-polynomial discrepancy estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v2.md#direct-pri11-energy-application) to the conjugates of \(\beta\) with this rotated test.
The polynomial, its actual degree and its integer discriminant are unchanged.
Thus for each regularization radius \(\rho>0\) the same proof gives
\[
 X(w)\le h+e^{-T}/2+1/R+(3e^T+T/R)\rho
                 +\sqrt{(4T+7)(2h-\log(\rho)/D+4\rho)} .
\]
With \(\ell=\log(1/h)\), the [actual degree bound and substitution](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument) give \(D^{-1}\le2400 h(\ell/\log\ell)^3\), \(T=\ell/2,\ R=e^T,\ \rho=h\), and hence (3) with the same constant.
This upper bound applies also at zero resultants.
For any fixed \(C>0\), it tends to zero uniformly for \(1\le n\le Cd\) as \(m\to0\), by monotonicity of \(\Phi_5\) on its small-height domain.
In particular any fixed positive lower rate for even one phase in that window obstructs a vanishing sequence.

For normalized arclength \(d\mu(w)\), the circle mean formula applied to each factor gives \(\int X\,d\mu=h\).
Thus (3) also gives \(\int|X-h|\,d\mu\le2(\Phi_5(h)-h)\), by equality of the integrals of the positive and negative parts of \(X-h\).

## Computing the rate at \(i\)

For \(y^d=i\), substitution in the displayed formula for \(S\) gives \(y^2(y-1)S(y)=Q_i(y)\).
Taking the product over the \(d\) nodes and reversing the resultant gives
\[
       |\operatorname{Res}(S,z^d-i)|
              =\frac1{|i-1|}\prod_{Q_i(r)=0}|r^d-i| .                  \tag{4}
\]
Exterior roots of \(Q_i\) contribute \(d\log|r|+O(1)\) in the logarithm and interior roots contribute \(O(1)\).
At a fixed nontorsion unit-circle root \(r\), the [fixed algebraic power bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle) gives \(|r^{4d}-1|\gg_r d^{-C_r}\).
Factoring \(r^{4d}-1\) into the four linear factors at \(r^d\) gives \(|r^d-i|\ge |r^{4d}-1|/8\).
For a torsion root the finitely many values \(|r^d-i|\) attained on this subsequence are all nonzero, by the substitution identity and the absence of torsion roots of \(S\).
These estimates prove the first formula in (1).
The real coefficients of \(S\) give equal absolute resultants at \(i\) and \(-i\), and \((z^d-i)(z^d+i)=z^{2d}+1\) proves the second.

As an algebraic check, the polynomial \(Q_i Q_{-i}=f^2+z^4g^2\) is \(z^5T(z+z^{-1})\), with
\[
    T(t)=t^5+2t^4-4t^3-10t^2+t+8,\qquad \operatorname{disc}T=-49856 .
\]
The negative discriminant forces a nonreal trace root, so the reciprocal lift has an exterior root and \(M(Q_i)=M(Q_{-i})>1\).
For the stronger numerical separation in (2), take \(c=(-16+13i)/14\).
In descending powers the coefficients of \(14^5Q_i(c+u/14)\) are
\[
      1,\ -66+65i,\ -26-3236i,\ 37080+33518i,\ -270123+15548i,\ 6394-1091i .
\]
Writing \(Q_i(c+v)=\sum_{j=0}^5 b_jv^j\), squaring the moduli gives \(|b_0|<1/50,\ |b_1|>7,\ |b_2|<19,\ |b_3|<17,\ |b_4|<7,\ b_5=1\).
On \(|v|=1/100\) the linear term dominates the sum of the absolute values of all other terms.
By [Rouché's theorem](../audits/literature/Sha23/Yin_U001.md#rouche-disk), \(Q_i\) has a root of modulus at least \(|c|-1/100>29/20\), using \(|c|^2=425/196\).
This proves the claimed measure bound.
Finally \(|\operatorname{Res}(S,z^{2d}+1)|\le2^d M(S)^{2d}\) by applying the triangle inequality at each of the \(d\) original roots, giving (2).
