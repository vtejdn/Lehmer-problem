# Splitting minimal ternary relations through bounded rational multiples of arbitrary powers

<a id="carriers"></a>
## Partial sums that are single powers with rational coefficients

The [one-exponent splitting test](Yin_L_G0462_v295.md#splitting) uses two minimal supports meeting at an exponent inside the original interval.
Consider instead a signed relation at the chosen nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) whose individual terms are \(c_i\alpha^{e_i}\), with integer exponents and nonzero rational coefficients of multiplicative height at most an integer \(K\ge1\).
For a reduced fraction the height here is the larger of its positive denominator and its absolute numerator.
Allow repeated exponents as separate terms and require that no nonempty proper subsum of the relation vanish.
Call such a relation terminal if no subset of between two and \(r-2\) of its \(r\) terms sums to \(c\alpha^h\), for any integer \(h\) and any nonzero rational \(c\) of height at most \(K\).
The test includes exponents outside the original span, and also exponents already appearing elsewhere in the relation.

Use the finite witness length \(N\), weight ceiling \(T+1\), degree \(d\), and positive logarithmic measure \(m\) of the [ternary support count](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#statement).
Put
\[
 L=N+2(T+1)\left\lceil\frac{d\log((T+1)K^2)}m\right\rceil.
\]
Write \(D_r\) for the number of sorted exponent multisets of terminal relations of weight \(r\) and span at most \(L\), up to translation and division by the gcd of exponent differences; an all-equal multiset gives one shape.
Take any sequence with \(m\to0\) and \(\log K=o(\log(1/m))\), including a slowly increasing \(K\).
Then there are \(r\to\infty\), \(3\le r\le T+1\), such that
\[
                \log D_r\ge(1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m).
\]
In particular a uniform exponential bound for these terminal exponent multisets on record-measure inputs at the displayed lengths would prove a Lehmer gap, even when the allowed height \(K\) increases at the indicated rate.
The relations after splitting have bounded rational coefficients, and need not themselves be support-minimal ternary relations.

<a id="tree"></a>
## Splitting and reconstruction

Start with a minimal ternary relation of weight \(j\le T+1\), choosing any witnessing signs.
It has no vanishing proper subsum.
If a proper sublist of the allowed size sums to \(c\alpha^h\), append \(-c\alpha^h\) to it and append \(c\alpha^h\) to its complementary sublist.
Both children have zero sum, and each remains nondegenerate: a zero proper subsum avoiding the new term belonged to the parent, and for one containing it the complementary zero subsum avoids it.
The size restrictions give children strictly smaller than the parent and of size at least three.

There is a conjugate of modulus at least \(e^{m/d}\) and another of modulus at most \(e^{-m/d}\), since the sums of the positive and negative logarithmic moduli are \(m\) and \(-m\).
At the first conjugate the triangle inequality applied to the partial sum gives \(h\le\max_i e_i+d\log(jK^2)/m\), using \(1/K\le |c|,|c_i|\le K\); the second gives \(h\ge\min_i e_i-d\log(jK^2)/m\).
Along any branch the weight decreases each time, so all exponent spans in the tree are at most \(L\).

Let \(I_r\) count the exponent multisets of these terminal relations up to translation alone, with span at most \(L\) and without coefficient decorations.
The [tree count](Yin_L_G0462_v295.md#trees) gives, for the original translation-only count \(A_j\),
\[
 A_j\le128^j\left(\max_{3\le r\le j} I_r^{1/(r-2)}\right)^{j-2}.
\]
Indeed each node introduces one edge on splitting, with the exponent \(h\) propagated to both children.
For reconstruction each internal node chooses its translated exponent multiset and the incoming position in its sorted list, and then propagates the outgoing positions in order to its children.
Ties may be broken in any order since their exponents coincide; signs and amplitudes are unnecessary to reconstruct the leaf exponents.
The same rooted plane trees, total degree bound and multiplicity of incoming choices apply.

## The cost of deleting amplitudes and dilations

For one normalized exponent multiset that is not all equal, there are at most \((2K^2)^r\) ordered coefficient lists, assigning repeated positions individually.
For each list clear denominators by their product and collect equal powers to obtain an integer polynomial of coefficient norm at most \(r K^{r+1}\) in the sum-of-absolute-values norm.
If it has any nondegenerate realization it cannot vanish identically: vanishing of one entire equal-exponent group would already be a proper vanishing subsum.
For every occurring dilation \(q\ge1\) this polynomial vanishes at \(\alpha^q\).
Their minima are distinct by their houses and each has measure at least \(m\) by the [powered-factor budget](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#dilations).
The sum of their logarithmic measures is at most \(\log(rK^{r+1})\) by the coefficient norm.
The all-equal case has just one translation class, so in the small-measure range we have
\[
 I_r\le D_r(2K^2)^r\frac{\log r+(r+1)\log K}{m}.
\]
For each fixed \(r\), one also has \(I_r\le C_r(2K^2)^r\), independently of \(\alpha,L\).
Indeed for each ordered coefficient list normalize the least exponent to zero and divide by its coefficient, obtaining a nondegenerate unit equation on \(r-1\) coordinates in the product of \(r-1\) copies of \(\langle\alpha\rangle\).
The [uniform unit-equation bound](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess02-u001) for this rank bounds the number of exponent tuples by \(C_r\), including coincident powers.

The [finite deletion lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale) supplies \(A_j\ge(\log2/(32m))^{j-1}\) for some \(j\).
The tree inequality shows that a maximizing weight \(r\) has \(\log I_r\ge(r-2)(\log(1/m)-O(1))\).
Since \(\log K=o(\log(1/m))\), the fixed-weight bound forces \(r\to\infty\).
In the dilation inequality the logarithm of the coefficient-list factor is \(o((r-2)\log(1/m))\), and both \(\log(1/m)\) and \(\log(\log r+(r+1)\log K)\) are also of that smaller order.
This gives the claimed lower bound.
The missing ingredient is a uniform upper bound for the terminal relations for at least one choice of the allowed amplitudes; this calculation only quantifies how much either translations, coefficient choices or dilations can explain.
