# Classification and prefix rigidity at one projected coefficient for fixed branches above two

<a id="prefix"></a>
## Eight possible series on a long integer prefix

Fix an integer \(j\ge3\).
Let \(J\) be the Herglotz transform of a conjugation-invariant real signed circle measure of total mass one, normalized so \(J(0)=1\) and \([w^l]J=2\int v^l\,d\omega(v)\) for \(l>0\).
There exist \(\delta,c>0\) and \(N_0\), depending at most on \(j\), with the following property.
If \(\omega^-(\mathbb T)\le\delta\) and \(B=(1-w/j)J\) has integral coefficients through degree \(N\ge N_0\), then through degree \(\lfloor cN\rfloor\) its coefficients agree with a single row \(B_0\) in the table below.
For \(B=(1+w/j)J\), use \(B_0(-w)\) instead, by reflecting the input through \(w\mapsto-w\).
This extends the [branch-two prefix theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix) with a different profile list.

Write \(K=j/J_0\) for the reciprocal of a limiting probability transform, scaled to \(K(0)=j\), and \(\rho=2\Re K\) for its continuous doubled density away from atoms.
Set \(\lambda(t)=(t+\sqrt{t^2-4})/2\) for \(t\ge2\).
The parameters \(b,\epsilon\) in the table are defined below.

| \(b,\epsilon\) | \(B_0=(j-w)/K\) | \(M(\rho)/j\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(-3,-\) | \(1/(1-w^2)\) | \(\lambda(j)/j\) |
| \(-2,+\) | \(1/(1+w)\) | \(\lambda(j+1)/j\) |
| \(-2,-\) | \(1/(1-w)\) | \(\lambda(j-1)/j\) |
| \(-1,+\) | \(1\) | \(\lambda(2j)/j\) |
| \(-1,-\) | \((1-w)/(1+w)\) | \(1/j\) |
| \(0,+\) | \(1+w\) | \((j-1)/j\) |
| \(0,-\) | \(1-w\) | \((j+1)/j\) |
| \(1,-\) | \(1-w^2\) | \(1\) |

<a id="classification"></a>
## Torsion zeros, phase and measure

To analyze a sequence with \(N\to\infty\) and negative mass tending to zero, take a weakly convergent subsequence, using the uniform total variation bound.
Its limit is a symmetric probability with transform \(J_0\) and \(\Re J_0>0\) in the open disk.
Fixed coefficients of \(B\) converge to those of \(B_0=(1-w/j)J_0\) and eventually equal these integers.
Since \(B_0(0)=1\), \(K=(j-w)/B_0\) has integral coefficients, positive real part and first coefficient congruent to \(-1\bmod j\).
By the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) its reduced rational denominator \(V\) is a squarefree signed cyclotomic product with constant one.
Its numerator is integral and \(\rho\) is a nonnegative Laurent polynomial.
The [integer rational reciprocal argument](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#cyclotomic) shows that \(B_0=(j-w)/K\), analytic in the disk with integer coefficients, has only torsion poles.
Every zero of \(K\) is thus torsion apart from at most a simple zero at \(j\).
If all were torsion, the numerator's constant \(j\) would give \(K\in j\mathbb Z[[w]]\), contrary to its first coefficient.
It follows that \(K=(j-w)U/V\) with \(U\) another signed cyclotomic product normalized at zero and coprime to \(V\).
Its roots are simple by \(\Re K(rv)\ge j(1-r)/(1+r)\) and regularity at each such zero.
Also \(\rho\) is nonzero since \(K(j)=0\) while \(K(1/j)>0\), excluding the rational identity \(K(w)+K(1/w)=0\).

Let \(b=\deg V-\deg U-1\), \(\epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\).
The bracket in the rational identity for \(\rho\) is
\[
       \rho=(U/V)P,\qquad P(w)=(j-w)+\epsilon w^b(jw-1).
\]
All odd-order circle zeros of \(P\) must be torsion because every circle zero of \(\rho\) has even order.
If \(w\) is such a torsion point, then \(t=(j-w)/(jw-1)=-\epsilon w^b\) is torsion and \(jt w-j=t-w\).
The algebraic integer \((t-w)/j\) has all conjugates of modulus at most \(2/j<1\), hence is zero.
Thus \(t=w\) and \(w=\pm1\).
On the circle the phase of \(w^b(jw-1)/(j-w)\) has degree \(b+1\) and derivative \(b+(1-j^{-2})/|w-1/j|^2\) with respect to angle.
For \(b\ge0\) it is strictly increasing; for \(b\le-2\) it is strictly decreasing, because the Poisson term has maximum \((j+1)/(j-1)\le2\), with equality only at an isolated point for \(j=3\).
Thus for these \(b\) there are \(|b+1|\) distinct crossings, all of odd order even if a derivative vanishes.
At most two crossings are allowed, hence \(-3\le b\le1\).
For \(b=-3,1\) with \(\epsilon=1\), neither \(1\) nor \(-1\) is a zero, excluding these choices.

For the remaining cases \(w^{\max(0,-b)}P\) factors as follows, up to overall sign.

| \(b\) | \(\epsilon=1\) | \(\epsilon=-1\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(-3\) | excluded | \((w^2-1)(w^2-jw+1)\) |
| \(-2\) | \((w+1)(w^2-(j+1)w+1)\) | \((w-1)(w^2-(j-1)w+1)\) |
| \(-1\) | \(w^2-2jw+1\) | \(1-w^2\) |
| \(0\) | \((j-1)(1+w)\) | \((j+1)(1-w)\) |
| \(1\) | excluded | \(j(1-w^2)\) |

The squarefree products \(U,V\) distribute exactly the odd-multiplicity torsion factors, with \(\deg V-\deg U=b+1\).
The triple root at \(1\) in \(b=-2,\epsilon=-1,j=3\) therefore supplies one factor to \(U\).
The degree difference specifies the allocation except at \(b=-1,\epsilon=-1\), where \(U=1-w,\ V=1+w\) would give \(\rho=w+w^{-1}-2<0\) on an open arc.
The other choice gives the tabled \(B_0=V/U\).
Since \(M(\rho)=M(P)\) and \(M(w^2-tw+1)=\lambda(t)\), the same factorizations give all eight measures.

<a id="extinction"></a>
## Vanishing of every projected harmonic row

For any listed \(K\), let \(\sigma=2\Re J_0\) away from atoms, so \(\sigma=j\rho/|K|^2\).
Suppose that at regular points \(w\) there are conditional circle probabilities with moments \(c_l(w)\), \(l\ge1\), for which
\[
              H_l=(1-w/j)\sigma c_l\in\mathbb Z[w,w^{-1}].
\]
Then all \(H_l\) vanish.
Indeed they satisfy \(|H_l|\le D=|1-w/j|\sigma\), where \(M(D)=M(\rho)/j\) since \(M(K)=j\).
Apart from \(b=-1,\epsilon=1\), every tabulated measure ratio is at most \((j+1)/j\le4/3<\sqrt2\).
The [separated-block inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes) makes the list of nonzero integer Laurent polynomials bounded by \(D\) finite up to monomial shifts.

In the remaining case \(K=j-w\), set \(x=w+w^{-1}\).
Then \(D^2=(2j-x)^2/(j^2+1-jx)\le4\), with equality only at \(\pm1\), since subtracting the numerator from four times the denominator gives \(4-x^2\).
Its mean is strictly below four, so any nonzero integer Laurent polynomial bounded by \(D\) has at most three terms, all with coefficients \(\pm1\).
For a trinomial with exponents outside arithmetic progression, the three nonzero positive differences are distinct and the fourth moment is \(3^2+6=15>4\cdot3\), contradicting the supremum bound two.
For exponents in arithmetic progression, equal endpoint coefficients permit alignment to give modulus three and opposite endpoints permit a phase giving modulus \(\sqrt5\).
For a binomial the alignment points must all be among \(\{\pm1\}\), bounding its exponent difference by two.
This again leaves finitely many shapes, with \(M(D)=\lambda(2j)/j<2\).

For every case the [prime specialization argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) now applies with the linear factor \(j-w\).
For a nonzero shape \(S\), \(M(S)\le M(D)<2<j\) prevents \(S(j)=0\), after a monomial shift to an ordinary integer polynomial.
The phase ratio \(S(w)(j-w^{-1})/[S(w^{-1})(j-w)]\) therefore has a pole at \(j>1\), precluding its rational squaring identity.
At a sufficiently large prime order common to all the shapes and regular at all primitive nodes, every nonzero specialized moment is nonreal.
Indeed reality would place the phase ratio in the group of prime-order roots; the squaring field automorphism and the failed rational identity exclude this at all but finitely many nodes.
The moments at all conjugate nodes represent probabilities and are Galois compatible since \(\sigma\) and the Laurent rows are rational functions over \(\mathbb Q\).
Their integral-basis coordinate sequences after clearing the fixed denominator are integer Fourier–Stieltjes transforms, hence eventually periodic by the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes).
The mean on multiples of a common period is real nonnegative, forcing that eventual value to be zero and the specialized probability to have zero mass on the corresponding root group.
The averages in its other residue classes then vanish as well, so every late harmonic is zero at the chosen node.
No shape vanishes there, giving only finitely many nonzero rows.
A largest nonzero row \(H_r\) would have \(|c_r|\le1/2\), since the pushforward by the \(r\)-th power has no moments above the first and its density is nonnegative.
Thus \(1\le M(H_r)\le M(D)/2<1\), proving the assertion.

## From extinction to the forced coefficient progression

If the prefix assertion failed, take signed measures with negative mass at most \(1/i\) and \(N_i\ge i\) with no listed profile matching through \(\lfloor N_i/i\rfloor\).
After extraction the limiting transform has a listed \(B_0\) by the classification above.
Its first coefficient discrepancy \(n_i\) must tend to infinity by coefficient stabilization, with \(n_i/N_i\to0\).
For fixed \(l\ge1,\ s\in\mathbb Z\), both \(ln_i+s\) and \(ln_i+s-1\) are eventually inside the integer prefix.
The [two-circle comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v52.md#harmonics) applies to the positive parts of the doubled measures under \(v\mapsto(v,v^{n_i})\).
Discarding the negative measures changes all moments by \(o(1)\); equality on the growing prefix identifies the first conditional moment at each limiting torsion atom, with its whole mass at the extracted phase.
All higher atomic moments therefore agree, and subtraction of the atomic parts leaves the continuous projections with moments \(\sigma c_l\) after reflection in the first circle.
Multiplication by \(1-w/j\) takes their Fourier coefficients to the integral differences at the two displayed indices.
Each bounded density has square-summable coefficients; the integer differences imply each \(H_l\) is Laurent polynomial.
The first discrepancy gives \(H_1\ne0\), and the conditional moment inequalities extend from almost every point to all regular points by continuity of every finite Toeplitz matrix.
This contradicts row extinction and proves the prefix assertion.

For the actual [positive-constant inverse construction](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) with \(C=jU_h\pm U_{h-d}\) in the half-trace variable, let \(B^\sharp=\sum_s b_s z^s\) be the lifted monic trace denominator.
The [signed measure](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) of \(J_q=j/K_q\) has negative mass \(j/[4(q-1)]\).
Pushing it forward by \(v\mapsto v^d\) and using \(B^\sharp=(1\pm z^d/j)J_q+O(z^{2h+2-d})\) gives \(b_{kd}=[w^k](1\pm w/j)\widetilde J_q\) through \(N=\lfloor(2h+1-d)/d\rfloor\), with \(\widetilde J_q\) the projected transform.
Thus for sufficiently large \(q\) and \(N\), at fixed \(j\), the same list forces the progression of every \(d\)-th coefficient through \(k=\lfloor cN\rfloor\).
