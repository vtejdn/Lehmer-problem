# An escaping sparse trace has a coefficient of positive limiting size beyond its first fractional scale

<a id="separation"></a>
## Two separated scales on every positive branch

Consider actual positive-constant Pisot inputs, with normalized trace lifts \(F_i=\Pi_i/j_i\) having at most \(K\) terms and \(\theta_i-q_i\to0\).
Write \(d_i\) for the first nonintegral coefficient index of \(F_i\), and \(h_i\) for the trace degree.
After extraction there are lower support exponents \(e_i\le h_i\) with
\[
                   e_i/d_i\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
                   \liminf_i |[z^{e_i}]F_i|>0 .
\]
This extraction is possible from every subsequence.
In particular the largest positive lower exponent divided by \(d_i\) tends to infinity on the original sequence.
For each fixed \(K\) there are \(A\)-dependent uniform positive lower bounds on \(\theta-q\) for lifts with all lower exponents at most \(A d\), for every fixed \(A\ge1\) for which \(d\) exists.
The term count, the scales and the branch can otherwise vary within the stated hypotheses.

<a id="round"></a>
## The integer polynomial at the first scale

Work on an arbitrary subsequence and extract a fixed ordered support size, limits of all bounded normalized coefficients and limits of the ratios of all positive lower exponents to \(d_i\) in \([0,\infty]\).
Here the uniform height bound and \(d_i=o(h_i)\) are as in the [first fractional scale theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#first).
The terms of \(F_i\) at lower exponents \(O(d_i)\), including its constant, converge coefficientwise at their actual positions to an integer tuple by that theorem.
Use the rounded integer tuple there to form \(A_i\), dropping its zero entries.
Its constant is one and \(k_i=\deg A_i=O(d_i)\).

On the stabilized lattice of the full support the corresponding integer polynomial \(A\) is a face of the frozen numerator, and \(M(A)=1\).
Indeed all omitted exponents divided by \(d_i\) tend to infinity, including the reflected terms.
The [face separation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#faces) uses only the stabilized relations, and the full frozen measure is one by the [uniform logarithmic truncation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#moving).
The constant is an exposed vertex in the face.
If only the constant remains, its measure is one directly.
Lawton now gives \(M(A_i)\to1\) since \(A_i\) is its evaluation on the original stabilized lattice.
Each \(A_i\) is integral, has constant one, and has bounded term count.
The [fixed-support gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) therefore gives \(M(A_i)=1\) eventually.
Every root of such a polynomial is on the circle, since the constant and leading coefficient are nonzero integers and the measure is one.
Reality implies
\[
                 z^{k_i} A_i(1/z)=\sigma_i A_i(z),
                       \qquad \sigma_i=\operatorname{lc}(A_i)\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
This also covers \(A_i=1\) with \(k_i=0\).
Thus the rounded polynomial of the entire first group, before any later scale, is reciprocal up to sign for all sufficiently large indices of this extraction.

<a id="fold"></a>
## The later terms cannot all vanish in normalized size

Suppose all lower exponents with ratio to \(d_i\) tending to infinity have coefficients tending to zero, including when the list is empty.
Put \(N_i=2h_i+2,\ p_i=N_i-k_i\) and drop subscripts on a single input.
The anti-reciprocal lift satisfies
\[
                       \|F-(1-\sigma z^p)A\|_{\rm coef}=o(1),
\]
where here \(A=A_i\) and \(\|\cdot\|_{\rm coef}\) denotes the sum of absolute coefficients.
This follows from the finite term count, rounding error on the first group, vanishing on the remaining lower terms and exact reflection of the lower half.
We have \(k=O(d)=o(h)\), so \(p>k\) eventually.

The [folding argument](Yin_L_G0462_v107.md#fold) now applies with the actual sparse \(A_i\), whose bounded integer coefficients can occur at arbitrary indices through \(k\).
In detail, for the reciprocal integer denominator \(B=B^\sharp\) let \(I\) be its remainder modulo \(z^p-\sigma\).
For \(0\le l<p\) one has \(I_l=2\int v^l A(v^{-1})\,d\omega_i+o(1)\) uniformly, by \(a_{k-r}=\sigma a_r\) and the two Taylor halves of the inverse series, whose sum at zero is two.
The actual inverse measure is supported on the zeros of \(F\), so \(|A(v^{-1})|\,|1-\sigma v^p|=o(1)\) there uniformly.
Projection to the nearest nodes \(\zeta^p=\sigma\) changes these moments by \(o(1)\) for \(0\le l<p\), and the projected measure has bounded variation since \(\|A\|_{\rm coef}=O(1)\).
The [cyclic norm and rounding argument](Yin_L_G0462_v107.md#grid) therefore supplies a bounded-term cyclotomic product \(Q\) such that \(QI\) has boundedly many terms.

It remains to recover the initial portion \(P_k\) of \(B\) through \(k\).
All Taylor coefficients of the inverse series below \(d\) are integral by triangular division using the actual numerator below its first fractional index and the integer \(B\).
The [all-location initial estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial) therefore gives a multiplier \(U=1\) or \(1-\epsilon z^u\) of degree \(O(d)\) such that \(UB\) has bounded support cardinality on every interval of length \(d\).
This bounds the support of \(UP_k\) through \(k=O(d)\).
If \(U\ne1\), the remaining coefficients occur in the next \(u\) positions and are the translate of one length-\(u\) block of the unfiltered \(B\), padded by zeros below index zero.
Exactly as at the [edge of a truncation](Yin_L_G0462_v107.md#lower), nonzero moment differences of order less than \(u\) vanish in the matched initial reciprocal model.
Split the block at the boundedly many support points of \(F\).
Bessel's inequality for the ordinary characters on each remaining piece, uniform bounds on the partial sums of \(F\) and integrality of the tested coefficients bound their count.
Thus \(UP_k\) has bounded term count also when \(k<u\).

By reciprocity \(B=I+(z^p-\sigma)z^kP_k(1/z)\).
Multiplying by \(Q\) and the reversed multiplier of \(U\) gives a nonzero bounded-term integer polynomial of measure \(M(B)>1\) tending to one, contradicting the fixed-support gap.
At least one later coefficient limit is nonzero.
This proves the assertions by subsequence extraction, including the uniform lower bound for each \(K,A\).
