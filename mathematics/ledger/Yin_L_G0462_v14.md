# Short transvection words and a constant-Lehmer conjugacy control

<a id="scope"></a>
## Question and retained inputs

This exploration for Yin on 10 November 2026 asks whether adding transvections changes the integer supply mechanism for the [unrestricted G0001 question](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
It uses the exact positive lattice, two fixed isotropic vectors, even-norm transvections and accepted uniform gap of [C885 v2](../claims/Yin_C_G0885_v2.md#decision-positive-lattice-two-transvection-gap).
The calculations below are direct conjugacy applications and a known-value control, checked by the author from the retained formulas.
The [separate independent finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v4.md#short-word-conjugacy-control-review) checks the complete application and control, without a new general gap estimate.
No sampler, root computation or matrix-word enumeration was run.

The [preceding comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#coverage-and-first-test) retains the full expanding-root bound, the first failed scaling, and the actual Coxeter and fixed-support applicability boundaries.
Those inputs are unchanged.
In particular the canonical Coxeter theorem is not automatically a theorem about every integral isometry, and unrecognized representation coverage does not establish escape.

<a id="short-word-reduction"></a>
## Combining and cyclically reducing at most three factors

Write \(E_v=T_{e,v}\) and \(F_w=T_{f,w}\), with the positive pairing and lattice of C885 v2.
For permitted positive-lattice vectors \(u,v\), direct substitution in the transvection formula gives
\[
E_uE_v=E_{u+v},\qquad F_uF_v=F_{u+v},\qquad
E_u^{-1}=E_{-u},\qquad F_u^{-1}=F_{-u}.
\]
For example, if \(s=(e,x)\), the right factor \(E_v\) preserves \(s\) and changes \((u,x)\) by \(s(u,v)\) before \(E_u\) is applied.
The resulting coefficient of \(e\) is
\[
 -(u+v,x)-\tfrac12\bigl((u,u)+2(u,v)+(v,v)\bigr)s,
\]
which is the coefficient in \(E_{u+v}x\).
Even norms are preserved by addition, because the cross term is twice an integer.

For an integral positive isometry \(R\) extended to fix \(e,f\), covariance of the same formula gives
\[
 E_uR=R E_{R^{-1}u},\qquad F_uR=R F_{R^{-1}u}.
\]
The transformed vectors remain integral and retain their norms.
Thus intervening positive isometries may all be moved to the left, replacing them by their product and preserving the number of transvection factors.

For an alternating three-factor word, conjugation by its final factor gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_u\bigl(R E_vF_wE_u\bigr)E_{-u}
   &=E_uR E_vF_w\\
   &=R E_{R^{-1}u+v}F_w.                         \tag{1}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The conjugating matrix and its inverse are integral lattice isometries.
Consequently the entire characteristic polynomial, with all roots and multiplicities, is unchanged.
The new first vector has even norm by the addition calculation.

The analogous identity with \(e,f\) exchanged handles \(R F_vE_wF_u\).
The integral isometry exchanging \(e\) and \(f\) conjugates a two-factor word in the opposite order to the order in C885 v2.
Adjacent equal-direction factors combine, and zero factors disappear.
A one-factor word is neutral by the zero-vector case of that theorem.

It follows directly that every word containing at most three transvections at these two fixed cusps, with any intervening integral positive isometries, has the same uniform positive total-logarithmic gap as C885 v2.
This is a transfer by exact integer conjugacy, not an estimate depending on the displayed parameter sizes.
It does not cover transvections based at arbitrary other isotropic vectors.
After these specific reductions, an alternating four-factor word is the first possible unresolved length; this is not a claim that every such word is irreducible under all group relations.

## An explicit two-factor realization of the known Lehmer core

Take \(H=I_9\), index the positive standard basis so that \(Rb_j=b_{j+1}\) modulo nine, and let \(a=b_0+b_1\).
Put \(B=R E_aF_a\).
Then \((a,a)=2\), so this is an allowed input.

More generally, for a cycle of length \(g\ge3\) and that same consecutive pair, the exact cyclic correlation numerator is
\[
U_{a,a}(z)=z^{g-1}+z+2,\qquad q(z)=z^g-1.
\]
Indeed the correlations are one at lags \(1,g-1\), two at lag \(g\), and zero at all other lags from one through \(g\).
In the [complete determinant formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0885_v1.md#argument), all four correlation resolvents equal \(V=U_{a,a}/q\), the two half-norms are one, and the mixed pairing is two.
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_B
 &=q\bigl((z+1+V)(z-1+zV)-z(1+V)^2\bigr)\\
 &=q(z^2-z-1)+(z^2-1)U_{a,a}\\
 &=z^{g+2}-z^g-z^{g-1}+z^3+z^2-1.                \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
At \(g=9\) this factors exactly as
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_B(z)&=(z-1)L(z),\\
L(z)&=z^{10}+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is precisely the polynomial in the [accepted McM02 source statement](../audits/literature/McM02/readme.md#canonical-coxeter-gap-input), and it equals \(F(-z)\) for the [existing J156 canonical core](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0156/primitive_core_sampling.md#exact-observed-minima).
Thus the whole output has the already known Lehmer measure.
The extra eigenvalue one adds no Mahler cost.
This is an exact identity and an input control, not a new small value or a new numerical certification.

<a id="unbounded-parameters"></a>
## Unbounded three-factor parameters with exactly the same positive measure

For every positive integer \(N\), conjugate that fixed \(B\) by \(E_{Na}\):
\[
 B_N=E_{Na}B E_{-Na}
     =R E_{N R^{-1}a+a}F_aE_{-Na}.               \tag{3}
\]
All displayed vectors are integral with even norms.
Since \((a,R^{-1}a)=1\), their squared norms, in order, are
\[
                  2N^2+2N+2,\qquad 2,\qquad 2N^2.
\]
In particular the sum of the three squared norms tends to infinity.

Nevertheless (3) is an exact integral conjugacy, so
\[
                  P_{B_N}=(z-1)L(z),\qquad M(P_{B_N})=M(L)
\]
for every \(N\).
The two large parameter vectors cancel in the reduction (1): its new first vector is exactly \(a\).
No degree, irreducible core or complete measure changes.

This control refutes an attempted inference from unbounded displayed word energy alone to large complete Mahler measure for words with three factors.
It does not contradict C885 v2's explicit large-norm bound, which concerns the two vectors of its precise two-factor representation.
It also shows why an optimizer may find arbitrarily large integer parameters and many successful target occurrences while recovering only one known polynomial.

## Consequence for a longer-word investigation

A new finite word test must first distinguish actual characteristic polynomials and measures from the conjugate controls above.
The three-factor extension supplies no new shrinking-measure mechanism, even over a changing positive lattice.
A four-factor or longer candidate would still need complete spectral control, the actual integral parameter conditions and the separate Coxeter-applicability check.
The existence of a longer expression, or a generation theorem for a larger lattice group, does not by itself provide any of these inputs.

The first unresolved analytic question is whether an explicit genuinely longer family can keep its full exterior cost small while avoiding reduction to an already controlled family.
No general four-factor gap, representation theorem for arbitrary Salem polynomials, or positive-to-zero family is established here.
