# Matrix height-gap transfer: exact dyadic noise and fixed Laurent compactness

<a id="scope-and-strategic-assessment"></a>
## Scope and strategic assessment

This bounded 2026-11-05 exploration tests a fixed-matrix-dimension route to the unrestricted [G0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
The intended bridge would construct, from every original non-torsion algebraic integer unit alpha, a finite matrix set F_alpha in one fixed dimension such that its generated group is not virtually solvable and its normalized all-place joint spectral height is at most C m(alpha), with C independent of the original degree.
The matrix_height_return agent developed and checked the calculations for human author Yin.
These are ordinary author exploration and source-applicability checks, without a formal review, independent review, Claim assessment or proof of a uniform Mahler gap.
No mathematical program, numerical experiment or solver is used.

The explicit two-by-two Hadamard conjugate pair below automatically generates a Zariski-dense subgroup of SL_2, but its normalized spectral height tends to (log 2)/2 whenever the original absolute height tends to zero.
The surviving cost comes from actual joint spectral radii at places over 2, including after arbitrary common changes of basis.
Its proposed height-to-total-Mahler comparison is equivalent to the positive-gap problem.
A separate fixed-family argument shows that integer Laurent matrices whose specializations generate relatively compact groups everywhere on the unit circle cannot supply a non-virtually-solvable specialization.
The test supplies no new covering construction or additional arithmetic income.

The original input remains arbitrary in degree and integer coefficients.
[C231's all-power norm descent](../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#all-positive-power-existence-descent) permits a further reciprocal power-stable endpoint reduction in the small-total-measure regime, at its retained SELF and external-input scope.
Neither that reduction nor the present calculation reduces unrestricted inputs to Salem numbers.
The explicit pair calculation applies to every non-torsion algebraic integer unit, without reciprocity or power stability.

## Original source scope and height conventions

The inspected Bre11 source is Emmanuel Breuillard's published [A height gap theorem for finite subsets of GL_d(overline Q) and nonamenable subgroups](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v174-n2-p07-p.pdf), Annals of Mathematics 174 (2011), 1057--1110, DOI 10.4007/annals.2011.174.2.7.
The actual journal PDF, rather than a different author-site draft, was read online.
The selected passages are the definitions and introductory statements on printed pages 1058--1061, Section 2.2 on pages 1071--1072, Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 on pages 1074--1075, and the closed-locus explanation in Section 7.3 on page 1105.
The main height-gap proof and the original structural inputs used in the source's zero-height and bounded-index arguments were not audited.
No local original-file acquisition or whole-paper review is claimed.

For a finite nonempty F subset GL_r(K), with K a number field, write

    R_v(F)=lim_n max_(w in F^n) ||w||_v^(1/n),
    h_mat(F)=[K:Q]^(-1) sum_v n_v log^+ ||F||_v,
    h_hat(F)=[K:Q]^(-1) sum_v n_v log^+ R_v(F).

The source uses the Euclidean operator norm at infinite places and the sup operator norm at finite places, with |p|_v=p^(-1) and n_v=[K_v:Q_p] at a finite place over p.
At infinite places n_v is 1 or 2 according as the completion is real or complex.
Proposition 2.13 gives h_hat(F)=lim_n h_mat(F^n)/n and invariance under common conjugation.
The height does not change when K is replaced by a containing number field.
This is a normalized matrix height; it is not the original total logarithmic Mahler measure.

The exact unrestricted input used here is Theorem 3.1 on page 1074: for each fixed r there is epsilon(r)>0 such that h_hat(F)>=epsilon(r) whenever the group generated by F is not virtually solvable.
The introductory Theorem 1.2 has an additional strong-irreducibility hypothesis, so it is not silently substituted for the complete statement.
Proposition 3.2 identifies h_hat(F)=0 with the generated group being virtually unipotent.
The source states on page 1059 that, for fixed r and fixed tuple size, the tuples generating virtually solvable groups form a closed algebraic subvariety of GL_r^k.
Section 7.3 on page 1105 explains the pair case using the dimension-dependent bound for the index of a triangularizable subgroup.
These are accepted external statements at this inspection scope; the underlying bounded-index theorem is not independently re-proved here.

A common change of basis at a fixed place changes the norm of every word by one condition factor independent of word length.
Dividing its logarithm by n removes it in R_v.
In particular, if all generators at one infinite place preserve one positive-definite Hermitian form, their local joint spectral radius is 1 even when the form is badly conditioned.
Different infinite places may use different common forms.
For the SL_2 pair below, the finite-place joint radius detects whether a common invariant local lattice exists; denominators in a displayed basis alone do not determine that growth.

The nearest existing source boundary is [BV20-U003](../audits/literature/BV20/Yin_U003.md#bv20-u003), which distinguishes the non-virtually-solvable uniform-Tits branch from the solvable affine family.
A uniform Tits theorem cannot directly supply a gap for that affine family.
[C852](../claims/Yin_C_G0852_v1.md#fixed-rational-function-height-gap) already charges fixed nonmonomial rational scalar maps on independent small-height inputs.
The actual unit, own-field and finite-place restrictions in [C866v6](../claims/Yin_C_G0866_v6.md#all-place-normalization) are not waived by changing to matrices.
The calculation below instead measures the joint growth of two matrices over the same original field.

<a id="same-field-hadamard-pair"></a>
## Same-field Hadamard pair and automatic Zariski density

Let alpha be a non-torsion algebraic integer unit, K=Q(alpha), d=[K:Q], and

    h=h_abs(alpha)=m(alpha)/d,
    u=alpha^(-1), t=alpha+alpha^(-1), s=alpha-alpha^(-1),
    A=diag(alpha,alpha^(-1)),
    P=[[1,1],[-1,1]],
    B=P A P^(-1)=(1/2)[[t,-s],[-s,t]],
    F_alpha={A,B}.                                      (H1)

Both matrices lie in SL_2(K); no extension by sqrt(2) is needed.
The notation P/sqrt(2) below is used only as a complex unitary change of basis.
Since alpha is non-torsion, the Zariski closure of its powers in G_m is all of G_m: a proper closed subset of G_m is finite, while those powers are distinct.
Consequently the Zariski closure G of the group generated by F_alpha contains both the diagonal torus T and P T P^(-1).

The Lie algebra of G contains

    H=diag(1,-1),
    X=P H P^(-1)=[[0,-1],[-1,0]],
    [H,X]=[[0,-2],[2,0]].

These three matrices span sl_2 in characteristic zero.
Thus G has dimension at least 3, and G=SL_2 because it is a closed subgroup of the irreducible three-dimensional algebraic group SL_2.
The construction therefore supplies the required non-virtually-solvable group automatically for every stated original input.
The remaining issue is its actual normalized height, rather than group generation.

<a id="complete-local-joint-radius"></a>
## Complete local joint-radius calculation

At an infinite place, put a_v=max(|alpha|_v,|alpha|_v^(-1)).
The matrix P/sqrt(2) is unitary, so ||A||_v=||B||_v=a_v.
Submultiplicativity gives R_v(F_alpha)<=a_v, while the words A^n give the reverse inequality.
Hence R_v(F_alpha)=a_v exactly.
Because alpha is a unit, the product formula gives

    d^(-1) sum_(v infinite) n_v log R_v(F_alpha)
      =d^(-1) sum_(v infinite) n_v |log |alpha|_v|
      =2h.                                             (H2)

At a finite place not over 2, alpha and its inverse are units and 2 is invertible.
Thus A,B lie in SL_2(O_v), all words have norm at most 1, and determinant 1 forces R_v(F_alpha)=1.

Now let v divide 2, so |2|_v=1/2.
Set

    E=[[1,0],[0,0]],
    Q=(1/2)[[1,-1],[-1,1]].

Then

    A=u I+s E, B=u I+s Q,
    E^2=E, Q^2=Q, E Q E=E/2, Q E Q=Q/2.              (H3)

The v-norms are ||E||_v=1 and ||Q||_v=||E Q||_v=||Q E||_v=2.
For k>=1, an arbitrary product of k letters from {E,Q} first reduces, by idempotence, to an alternating product of length 1<=ell<=k.
For odd ell=2j+1 it is 2^(-j)E or 2^(-j)Q, and for even ell=2j it is 2^(-(j-1))E Q or 2^(-(j-1))Q E.
Therefore every such product has norm at most

    2^(ceil(ell/2))<=2 (sqrt(2))^k.                    (H4)

The empty product has norm 1.
In a length-n word in A and B, expand each factor using H3.
A term choosing k projection letters has a scalar of v-absolute value |s|_v^k, because u is a unit.
The ultrametric triangle inequality introduces no factor for the number of expansion terms.
Taking the maximum over k=0,...,n and then n-th roots gives

    R_v(F_alpha)<=max(1,sqrt(2)|s|_v).                 (H5)

A direct multiplication also gives

    tr(A B)=t^2/2, det(A B)=1.                         (H6)

If |t|_v>1/sqrt(2), then |tr(A B)|_v>1.
For the two eigenvalues lambda,lambda^(-1), the ultrametric triangle inequality and their product 1 show that one has absolute value |tr(A B)|_v and the other its reciprocal.
Thus the spectral radius of A B is 2|t|_v^2.
Repeating the word A B yields R_v(F_alpha)>=sqrt(2)|t|_v.
Since s=t-2u, one has |s|_v=|t|_v above that threshold, and |s|_v<=1/sqrt(2) at or below it.
Together with H5 and determinant 1, this proves

    R_v(F_alpha)=max(1,sqrt(2)|t|_v) for every v|2.     (H7)

All finite places and all original embeddings have now been included.
Combining H2 and H7 gives the exact common-field formula

    h_hat(F_alpha)
      =2h+d^(-1) sum_(v|2) n_v
                   ((log 2)/2+log|t|_v)_+.            (A)

The displayed-basis denominators have not been substituted for their invariant joint spectral cost.

<a id="local-lattice-versus-entry-integrality"></a>
## Common local lattices versus entry integrality

For a place v|2, use integer ord_v and ramification index e_v, so log|z|_v=-(ord_v(z)/e_v)log 2.
The entries of B in H1 are integral exactly when ord_v(t)>=e_v.
Indeed B is integral if and only if both t/2 and s/2 are integral, and s=t-2u makes these conditions equivalent.

By contrast, H7 gives R_v(F_alpha)=1 exactly when ord_v(t)>=e_v/2.
This condition also exactly characterizes the existence of a common invariant O_v-lattice for the generated group.
For sufficiency, the inverse generators satisfy

    A^(-1)=alpha I-s E, B^(-1)=alpha I-s Q.

The same H3--H5 word expansion therefore bounds every group word, including inverses, by 2 whenever |s|_v<=1/sqrt(2).
The O_v-span of all translates of O_v^2 is then a full lattice: it contains O_v^2 and lies in pi^(-N)O_v^2 for a fixed integer N, and submodules of the latter finite free module over the local discrete valuation ring are finitely generated.
This lattice is invariant under the group.
Conversely, a common invariant lattice gives a basis in which all words have norm at most 1, so the basis-invariant local joint radius is 1.

The common-lattice requirement is therefore weaker than entry integrality in the displayed basis.
No assertion is made that independently chosen local lattices glue to one globally free O_K-module or one integral matrix basis over O_K.
Formula A already computes the normalized height without that additional global assertion.

<a id="dyadic-height-floor-and-cyclic-norm-cost"></a>
## Dyadic height floor and the paid original cyclic-norm bound

Because alpha is non-torsion, t is nonzero.
It is an algebraic integer, and every finite |t|_v is at most 1.
The product formula therefore gives

    sum_(v|2) n_v(-log|t|_v)
      <=sum_(v finite) n_v(-log|t|_v)
      =log |Norm_(K/Q)(t)|.                            (H8)

For each v|2, (c+log|t|_v)_+ is at least c+log|t|_v and at most c, where c=(log 2)/2.
Also sum_(v|2)n_v=d.
Applying these facts to A yields

    2h+(log 2)/2-log|Norm_(K/Q)(t)|/d
      <=h_hat(F_alpha)<=2h+(log 2)/2.                  (H9)

For n>=1 let C_n=|Norm_(K/Q)(alpha^n-1)|, a positive integer.
The unit norm of alpha and the identity alpha^4-1=(alpha^2-1)(alpha^2+1) imply

    |Norm_(K/Q)(t)|
      =|Norm_(K/Q)(alpha^2+1)|=C_4/C_2<=C_4.           (H10)

The selected [C147v5 full cyclic-norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#statement) gives, whenever 0<nh<=exp(-40),

    log C_n/d<=Phi_5(nh),
    Phi_5(x)=86 sqrt(x) log(1/x)^(5/2)
                         /[log log(1/x)]^(3/2),
    Phi_5(x)->0 as x->0.

Only n=4 is used.
Its original-field multiplicities, powered-degree allowance and retained external Dobrowolski/source-review limits remain those of C147v5.
No singular logarithmic limit is inferred merely from weak equidistribution.
If 4h<=exp(-40), H9--H10 give the fully paid comparison

    2h+(log 2)/2-Phi_5(4h)
      <=h_hat(F_alpha)<=2h+(log 2)/2.                  (B)

In particular, along every sequence of non-torsion algebraic integer units with h_abs(alpha)->0, one has h_hat(F_alpha)->(log 2)/2.
This statement concerns the actual original absolute height.
A hypothetical sequence with positive total m(alpha)->0 would satisfy that hypothesis, but its existence is not asserted.

<a id="odd-torsion-control"></a>
## Exact odd-order torsion control

The local calculations H2--H7 also apply to a root of unity alpha=zeta of odd order N>1.
For every v|2, its reduction cannot be 1.
Otherwise the equality 1+zeta+...+zeta^(N-1)=0 would reduce to the nonzero residue N=1 in characteristic 2.
If t=zeta+zeta^(-1) reduced to zero, then zeta^2 would reduce to 1, which in characteristic 2 would force zeta to reduce to 1.
Hence |t|_v=1 at every v|2.

All infinite-place contributions vanish, and formula A gives the exact control

    m(zeta)=0, h_hat(F_zeta)=(log 2)/2.                (H11)

This is not a counterexample to a comparison restricted to non-torsion inputs.
It identifies a fixed nonzero arithmetic background already present on infinitely many cyclotomic specializations, despite their simultaneous archimedean unitarity.
The non-torsion density argument above is not applied to zeta.

<a id="cheap-comparison-is-the-original-gap"></a>
## The cheap comparison is equivalent to the original gap

For this explicit F_alpha, consider the proposed uniform statement

    there exists C>0 such that h_hat(F_alpha)<=C m(alpha)
    for every non-torsion algebraic integer unit alpha. (H12)

If H12 holds, automatic Zariski density and Bre11 Theorem 3.1 give m(alpha)>=epsilon(2)/C.
Conversely, if m(alpha)>=delta>0 for every such unit, then h<=m(alpha) and H9 give

    h_hat(F_alpha)<=(2+(log 2)/(2delta))m(alpha).

Thus H12 is equivalent to a positive total-Mahler gap on non-torsion units.
This unit gap is equivalent to the unrestricted positive-gap contract, by multiplicativity of Mahler measure under primitive irreducible factorization.
After changing the sign of a factor if necessary, a nonmonic factor has leading coefficient at least 2 and hence measure at least 2.
A monic irreducible factor other than x whose root is not a unit has measure at least the absolute value of its nonzero constant coefficient and hence at least 2.
Powers of x and cyclotomic factors contribute zero logarithmic measure.
Consequently a unit gap delta yields the unrestricted gap min(delta,log 2), while the unrestricted gap directly includes units.

The explicit construction has therefore paid automatic non-virtually-solvable generation but has not made H12 independently accessible.
A uniform C cannot be obtained from its norm upper bound by dropping the fixed additive term.
Formula B identifies the full dyadic obstruction without claiming that H12 is false or that a vanishing-measure sequence exists.

<a id="fixed-integer-laurent-compactness"></a>
## Fixed integer Laurent families with joint compactness on the circle

First consider U(t) in M_r(Z[t,t^(-1)]) such that U(z) is unitary for every |z|=1 in the standard Hermitian form.
Write U_ij(t)=sum_k c_ijk t^k.
Integrating the squared norm of row i on the circle and using character orthogonality gives

    sum_(j,k) c_ijk^2=1.

The integer coefficients therefore give exactly one nonzero coefficient, equal to +1 or -1, in each row.
Orthogonality of distinct rows forces their occupied columns to be distinct.
Hence U is a signed Laurent-monomial permutation matrix.
A finite family of such matrices lies in the diagonal-torus normalizer, an extension of an abelian group by a finite permutation group, and generates a virtually abelian group.

There is a stronger fixed-family conclusion which allows varying Hermitian forms.
Let r,k be fixed and F_1(t),...,F_k(t) belong to GL_r(Z[t,t^(-1)]).
Assume that for every z in S^1 the subgroup of GL_r(C) generated by F_1(z),...,F_k(z) is relatively compact.
Then for every nonzero algebraic alpha the specialized tuple generates a virtually solvable group.

To prove this, specialize first at any root of unity zeta and use K=Q(zeta).
Every infinite embedding sends zeta to another point of S^1, so the assumed relative compactness gives local joint radius at most 1.
The matrix entries and inverse entries are integral at every finite place, because the Laurent matrices are invertible over Z[t,t^(-1)] and zeta is a unit.
Thus all finite-place joint radii are at most 1 as well, and h_hat(F(zeta))=0.
Bre11 Proposition 3.2, or the contrapositive of Theorem 3.1, implies that this specialized group is virtually solvable.

Let V_(r,k) be the fixed closed virtually-solvable locus from the source scope above.
The Laurent tuple defines a regular morphism G_m->GL_r^k.
Its inverse image of V_(r,k) is closed and contains every root of unity.
An infinite subset of G_m is Zariski dense, since a nonzero Laurent polynomial in one variable has only finitely many roots.
The inverse image is therefore all of G_m, proving the assertion for every algebraic alpha.

No common standard form is required here.
The common positive-definite form witnessing compactness may depend on z, and neither its continuity nor a uniform condition bound is assumed.
No generic irreducibility, trace-pairing reconstruction, semisimple quotient or unexamined radical case is used.
The conclusion is confined to one fixed integral Laurent tuple that is jointly relatively compact on the entire circle.
It does not cover input-dependent tuples, rational-coefficient tuples with nonzero finite-place cost, or compactness only at an input's actual finite collection of conjugates.

## Comparison and reopening boundary

[C67](../claims/Yin_C_G0067_v1.md#statement) treats rational Laurent paraunitary matrices whose values lie in the original order, under its actual isolated-support and row-window hypotheses.
It allows the rational matrix and the input to vary while retaining that exact algebraic-order and width structure.
The fixed-family compactness argument above has no such support or window assumptions, but instead requires integer Laurent coefficients in one fixed tuple and joint compactness at every point of the circle.
Neither statement is substituted for the other's hypotheses.

[L377's paraunitary-lifting exploration](Yin_L_G0377_v1.md#paraunitary-lift-reuse-and-domain) concerns prescribed quotient residues, rational lifting and full covariance/denominator costs.
[L21's matrix realization](Yin_L_G0021_v1.md#symmetric-entropy-lift) and [L27's matrix-compression comparison](Yin_L_G0027_v1.md#boundedness-equivalence) concern integer symmetric realizations and their total scalar entropy.
Those objects do not already supply the present all-place joint spectral calculation, and their realization gaps are not removed by it.
C852's fixed rational scalar barrier remains consistent with the dyadic background, while the local-lattice calculation shows why merely restating entrywise half-integrality would charge the wrong invariant condition.

The bounded search first inspected these local source and construction boundaries, then searched for Breuillard's precise height-gap statement and read the published original at the passages specified above.
It stopped after the actual source and direct pair calculation answered the discriminating questions.
There is no claim of an exhaustive literature search or novelty.
The retained finding is an unsuccessful transfer mechanism with precise paid costs, not a new covering route.

An input-dependent integral tuple that is jointly compact only at the original neutral embeddings is not excluded by the fixed-family argument.
No such tuple with automatic non-virtually-solvable generation and uniformly cheap original-measure return has been supplied here.
Reopening this matrix route would require a concrete new construction or arithmetic source controlling those simultaneous properties and the actual finite-place radii, rather than assuming a cheap second generator or varying the same fixed rotation without a changed mechanism.
