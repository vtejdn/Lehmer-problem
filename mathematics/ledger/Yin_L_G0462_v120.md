# Two possible limiting distributions at a binomial departure from the Pisot function at two

<a id="window"></a>
## Integral moments through every fixed multiple of the first departure

Consider an escaping sequence of positive delay-two measures as in the [dyadic concentration theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v28.md#statement).
Write \(n\) for the first discrepancy, \(\alpha=M(z^2 f+e f^*)>1\), \(T=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), and \(t=T-2\).
Use the [positive-real function](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#positive) \(K\), the disk germ \(J=(1-z^2F)/(1+z^2F)\), and the integer series \(K^0=K-tJ/2\) of the [prefix bound](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#energy).
Thus \(K(0)=1+t/2,\ K_j=-t(j-1)\) for \(1\le j\le n\), and \(t=O(n^{-2})\).
For each fixed \(C>0\) we have
\[
 \sup_{0\le j\le Cn}|K_j-K^0_j|=O_C(n^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \#\{0\le j\le Cn:K_j^0\ne0\}=O_C(1).
\]

Take the conjugation-invariant positive measure \(\mu\) on the circle with \(K_j=2\int v^j\,d\mu\) for \(j>0\) and \(\mu(\mathbb T)=K(0)\).
The Gram matrix of \(1,v,\ldots,v^{n-1}\) in \(L^2(\mu)\) has diagonal \(1+t/2\) and the sum of the absolute off-diagonal entries in any row is \(O(t n^2)=O(1)\).
Its operator norm is uniformly bounded.
This bounds the norm squared of the analysis map \(g\mapsto(\langle g,v^j\rangle)_{0\le j<n}\).
Testing on any monomial gives a uniform bound for the sum of \(|K_j|^2\) on every block of \(n\) consecutive positive indices.
In particular \(\sum_{0\le j\le Cn}|K_j|=O_C(\sqrt n)\).
The defining identity for \(K\) gives
\[
             J=\frac{1-z^2-2zK}{1-Tz+z^2}.
\]
Through degree \(Cn\) each coefficient of the reciprocal denominator is \(O_C(n)\), since its \(j\)-th coefficient is \(\sum_{a=0}^j\alpha^{j-2a}\) and \(n\log\alpha=O(1)\).
Convolution gives \(tJ_j=O_C(n^{-1/2})\), proving the first estimate.
Every nonzero integer \(K_j^0\) then has \(|K_j|\ge1/2\) for large \(n\), so the block square bound proves the second.

## The conditional circle moments

Push \(\mu\) forward by \(v\mapsto(x,y)=(\bar v,v^{n+1})\) and take a weak limit \(\Omega\) along a subsequence on which the [centered function](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#departure) tends to \(R/2\).
This is a probability with normalized arclength as first marginal, since every fixed positive coefficient of \(K\) tends to zero.
For \(l\ge1\) and \(r\in\mathbb Z\) the Fourier coefficient \(2\int x^{-r}y^l\,d\Omega\) is the limit of \(K_{l(n+1)+r}\).
It is an integer by the preceding window estimate.
Writing \(P_l(x)=2\mathbb E[y^l\mid x]\) gives a function of circle norm at most two with all Fourier coefficients integral.
Parseval makes it an integer Laurent polynomial, and the [norm bound for three terms](Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#norm) gives at most two terms.
When two terms occur their coefficients have modulus one.

In the notation of the [centered quotient](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#schur) write \(V\) for the actual rational function before passage to the limit.
Solving for \(F\) gives \(F=(1+(z-2)z^n V)/(1-2z+z^{n+1}V)\).
Substitution in the integer comparison yields
\[
                  K^0=\frac{1-z^{n+1}V}{1+z^{n+1}V}.
\]
At index \(n+1+r\) for fixed \(r<0\) the coefficient is zero for large \(n\).
For \(r\ge0\) fixed it equals \(-2[z^r]V\) for large \(n\).
Therefore \(P_1=-R\) with the indicated Fourier convention.

## Two conditional laws in the binomial case

Suppose \(P_1(x)=a+b x^k\) with \(a,b=\pm1,\ k\ge1\).
Put \(Y=y/a\) and \(w=(b/a)x^k\), so that \(\mathbb E[Y\mid x]=(1+w)/2\).
Then the joint probability \(\Omega\) has one of the following two conditional laws for almost every \(x\).

1. \(Y\) takes the values \(1,w\) with equal probabilities.
2. \(Y^2=w\) almost surely. For either measurable choice \(u^2=w\) on the circle, the conditional probability of \(Y=u\) is \((1+\operatorname{Re}u)/2\), and the other value is \(-u\).

To prove this, put \(L(x)=\mathbb E[Y^2\mid x]=P_2(x)/2\) and \(m=(1+w)/2\).
The variance bound \(|L-m^2|\le1-|m|^2\) holds almost everywhere by averaging \((Y-m)^2\).
It holds everywhere for these Laurent polynomials.
At any \(x_0\) with \(w(x_0)=1\), it forces \(L(x_0)=1\), and \(L-m^2\) vanishes to at least second order in the angle.
In particular the angular derivative of \(L\) there is \(ik\).
If \(P_2\) is a monomial its coefficient must have absolute value two and these value and derivative conditions give \(L=w\).
Otherwise \(L=(c x^p+d x^q)/2\), \(c,d=\pm1,\ p<q\).
Both monomials equal one at each of the \(k\) points with \(w=1\), so \(k\mid p,q\).
The derivative gives \(p+q=2k\).
Taking two conditionally independent copies of \(Y\) and using \(1-\cos(2h)\le4(1-\cos h)\) gives \(1-|L|^2\le4(1-|m|^2)\).
Near \(x_0\), comparison of the quadratic coefficients gives \((q-p)^2\le4k^2\).
The only unequal multiples of \(k\) with this sum and separation are \(0,2k\), so \(L=(1+w^2)/2\).

If \(L=w\), the unit random variable \(Y^2/w\) has expectation one conditionally, forcing \(Y^2=w\), and the first conditional moment determines the displayed weights.
If \(L=(1+w^2)/2\), choose \(u^2=w\) and put \(Z=Y/u\).
Its conditional mean is \(\operatorname{Re}u\), and the real part of its second moment is \(\operatorname{Re}(u^2)\).
Thus \(\operatorname{Re}Z\) has zero conditional variance and equals \(\operatorname{Re}u\) almost surely.
On the circle this gives \(Y\in\{1,w\}\), with equal weights by the first moment wherever \(w\ne1\).

Both conclusions concern a joint limit of measures of the positive-excess inputs.
They impose a distinction visible at index \(2(n+1)\) and its fixed offsets: the limiting second row is \(2w\) or \(1+w^2\).
Neither conditional law by itself supplies a sequence of such inputs.
