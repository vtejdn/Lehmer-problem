# A lower bound for the primitive height of double contacts at a Salem unit and its square

<a id="rate"></a>
## The height at minimum width grows with the Salem degree

Let \(\delta\) be a Salem number of degree \(d=2s\ge4\), and let \(A_n\) be the primitive integral signed row at formal width \(m=2d\) giving double contact at the conjugates of \(\delta,\delta^2\).
Use the [signed matrix and its minimum-width kernel](Yin_L_G0462_v276.md#upper) at either fixed sign.
The row exists uniquely up to sign at every sufficiently large exponent \(n\).
Its rate satisfies
\[
  (4d-5)\log\delta\ \le\
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A_n\|_1}{n}\
  \le\ (6d-4)\log\delta .
\]
In particular the fixed-seed limits are bounded below by \(2(4d-5)/(\log(3d))^3\), uniformly over Salem seeds of degree \(d\), by [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) applied to \(\log M(\delta)=\log\delta\).
This lower bound tends to infinity with \(d\).
The exponent at which the norm approaches its limit can depend on the seed.

Choose inverse-pair representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\) for the conjugates of \(\delta\), let \(K\) be the splitting field, and use independent variables \(z_i\) for \(x_i^n\) and \(N\) for \(n\).
Write \(C\) for the tuple of signed maximal minors before division, \(F\) for their gcd in \(K[N,\mathbf z]\), and \(Q=C/F\).
At each site \(y=z_j^k\), \(k=1,2\), the label is \(\varepsilon x_j^k\).
The [coordinate-width formula](Yin_L_G0462_v276.md#upper) expresses the height rate as \((\log\delta)/d\) times the sum of the \(s\) joint coordinate widths of \(Q\).
A joint width is the largest exponent minus the smallest exponent of the indicated variable across all nonzero entries, with \(N\) treated as a coefficient variable.
We give a lower bound of \(6+16(s-1)\) for each width.

<a id="isolated"></a>
## An isolated collision while the other site moves

Fix \(i\), write \(z=z_i\), and fix generic nonzero complex values of the other \(z_j\).
For each \(j\ne i\) select the points
\[
   z^2=z_j^{\pm1},\qquad z=z_j^{\pm2},\qquad z=-z_j^{\pm1}.
\]
There are respectively four, two and two distinct points per \(j\).
At each selected \(z_0\), precisely one moving site \(y=z^k\) meets a fixed site or its inverse \(w=z_j^{\pm l}\), with \(k,l\in\{1,2\}\).
For the third group, the meeting sites are the two squares.
Their inverses meet at \(w^{-1}\ne w\).
These conditions and pairwise distinctness of all selected points hold generically, for instance by taking the other \(z_j\) algebraically independent over \(K\).
Indeed the exponent relations displayed in each group force only the indicated meeting and its inverse, and in the third group the minus sign separates the two ordinary sites.

There is no sequence \(N\ne0,\ (N,z)\to(0,z_0)\) on which all the raw minors vanish.
We verify the effect of the additional moving site in the [isolated-collision calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v253.md#collision).
Keep the remaining \(m-2\) rows, omitting the two for the moving site \(y\), and regard them as functions of \(N,z\).
Their \(N=0\) conditions are evaluations at \(m-2\) distinct nodes at \(z_0\).
An invertible minor gives a three-dimensional kernel with a basis of polynomials analytic jointly in \(N,z\).
Choose it to equal \(H_0,ZH_0,Z^2H_0\) at \((0,z_0)\), where \(H_0\) is the monic reciprocal product of the factors for these \(m-2\) nodes.
Both \(w,w^{-1}\) are simple zeros of \(H_0\).

For this basis \((A_\nu)\) introduce an independent Taylor variable \(\xi\), write \(Y=w e^\xi\), and set
\[
 u(\xi)=Y^{-m/2}(A_\nu(Y))_\nu,\qquad
 v(\xi)=Y^{m/2}(A_\nu(Y^{-1}))_\nu .
\]
In these expressions \((N,z)\) is held fixed during differentiation in \(\xi\).
Put \(\theta=\log(y/w)\), \(c=\varepsilon x_i^k\), and let \(b\) be the fixed label \(\varepsilon x_j^l\) or its inverse according to the orientation of \(w\).
Reversing orientation exchanges the two evaluations and preserves the pair of equations with the inverted label, as in the cited calculation.
The [independence of the Salem representatives](Yin_L_G0462_v276.md#upper) gives \(b\ne c\) and \(bc\ne1\).
The two moving rows after centering are \(f=u(\theta)+cv(\theta)\) and \(g=N(u'(\theta)+cv'(\theta))+cv(\theta)\).
The fixed rows have label \(b\) and argument zero.
With \(u_j=u^{(j)}(0)\) and \(v_j=v^{(j)}(0)\), they give the exact identities
\[
       u_0=N(u_1+bv_1),\qquad v_0=-N(u_1+bv_1)/b.
\]

At \((N,z)=(0,z_0)\), the two triples \(u,v\) are the same scalar \(Y^{1-m/2}H_0(Y)\) times \((Y^{-1},1,Y)\) and \((Y,1,Y^{-1})\), respectively.
Since this scalar has a simple zero at \(w\), the row vectors \(u_1,v_1\) there are independent.
Use the moving basis \(u_1,v_1,e\) with a fixed third vector \(e\) completing the limiting basis.
The third-coordinate projection annihilates \(u_1,v_1\) exactly for each \((N,z)\).
Its limit is a nonzero multiple of \((1,-(w+w^{-1}),1)\), under which the first derivatives of both displayed triples have the same nonzero image up to that multiple, \(w-w^{-1}\).
The projections of \(u_2,v_2\) therefore tend to the same nonzero number \(\alpha\).
Analyticity in \((N,z,\xi)\) gives bounded Taylor remainders uniformly as \((N,z)\to(0,z_0)\).

Put \(a=1-c/b\).
In the first two coordinates the expansions of \(f,g\) start with \((Na+\theta,Nba+c\theta)\) and \((Na,c\theta)\), each with error \(O(|N\theta|+|\theta|^2)\).
The determinant in those coordinates therefore forces
\[
        c\theta^2-ba^2N^2=O((|N|+|\theta|)^3)
\]
along any putative sequence of rank loss.
It follows that \(|\theta/N|\) is bounded above and away from zero, since either extreme contradicts the equation after division by the square of the larger modulus.
Pass to a subsequence with \(\theta/N\to t=q-q^{-1}=a q\) for a choice \(q^2=b/c\).
The first coordinate of \(f/N\) tends to \(a(1+q)\ne0\), so dependence would give \(g=\lambda f\) with \(\lambda\to1/(1+q)\).
By the projection identities, the third coordinates of \(f,g\) are \(\alpha(1+c)\theta^2/2+o(N^2)\) and \(\alpha(1+c)N\theta+c\alpha\theta^2/2+o(N^2)\), respectively.
The third coordinate of \(g-\lambda f\), divided by \(\alpha N^2t\), tends to \((1+q)(c+1/q)/2\), which is nonzero by \(b\ne c\) and \(bc\ne1\).
This excludes rank loss even with the other site varying with \(z\).

## Zeros that survive cancellation at formal exponent zero

For the fixed other variables write \(\mathcal Y\) for the \(m\) ordered evaluation nodes \(z_j^{\pm1},z_j^{\pm2}\), including those depending on \(z\).
Set \(P(Z)=\prod_{t\in\mathcal Y}(Z-t)\), and let \(\Delta=\prod_{t<t'}(t'-t)\) use this chosen order.
At \(N=0\), subtracting the derivative row from the value at each site changes that row pair into evaluations at the site and its inverse, with a nonzero constant times a Laurent monomial multiplying the inverse evaluation.
The tuple of signed minors of the resulting evaluation matrix is \(\pm\Delta\) times the coefficient vector of \(P\), since both span its kernel at distinct nodes and the leading coordinate is the ordinary Vandermonde determinant up to sign.
The identity extends to the Laurent polynomial ring by agreement at generic \(z\).
Consequently
\[
             C(0,z)=U(z)\Delta(z)\,([Z^l]P(Z))_{l=0}^m
\]
for some nonzero constant times a Laurent monomial \(U\).

The polynomial \(F(0,z)\) is not identically zero on this slice, since the leading raw minor is generically nonzero.
At a selected point \(z_0\) one has \(F(0,z_0)\ne0\).
Indeed if \(F(0,z_0)=0\), choose a small disk around this isolated zero with \(F(0,z)\) nonzero on the boundary.
By Rouché's theorem \(F(N,z)\) has a zero in that disk for all sufficiently small \(N\), and shrinking the disk yields forbidden simultaneous zeros of \(C\) approaching \((0,z_0)\) with \(N\ne0\).
At \(z_0\) exactly two factors of \(\Delta\) vanish, one at the meeting \(w\), the other at its inverse.
Each vanishes simply because the moving map \(z\mapsto z^k\) and its inverse have nonzero derivatives at \(z_0\), while the colliding counterpart is constant.
Thus \(\Delta\) vanishes to order two there.

Now \(Q(0,z)=S(z)([Z^l]P(Z))_{l=0}^m\), with \(S=U\Delta/F(0,z)\).
Since \(P\) is monic, \(S\) is the leading coordinate of \(Q(0,z)\), so it is a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
It retains a double zero at each of the \(8(s-1)\) selected nonzero points.
After removing a power of \(z\), the resulting polynomial with nonzero constant term has the same exponent width and degree at least \(16(s-1)\).
The coefficient tuple of \(P\) itself has joint \(z\)-width six.
Indeed its \(z\)-dependent portion is \((Z-z)(Z-z^{-1})(Z-z^2)(Z-z^{-2})\), whose extreme powers are \(z^{\pm3}\), each with coefficient \(Z^2\).
Multiplication by the remaining monic polynomial independent of \(z\) preserves both extreme powers.
Maximum and minimum exponents add under multiplication by a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
The joint width of \(Q(0,z)\) is therefore at least \(6+16(s-1)\), and specializing \(N\) and the other variables can only decrease the joint width from the original tuple.
Summing this lower bound over the \(s=d/2\) coordinates gives \((4d-5)\log\delta\) in the height formula.
The exact rate still depends on possible common divisors outside these isolated collisions.
