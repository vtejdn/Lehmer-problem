### 2. The exact inverse-Gram Laurent identity

For a finite Laurent polynomial H(t)=sum_h h_h t^h with real coefficients write ||H||_W=sum_h|h_h|; this is only the absolute coefficient sum, defined here.
Direct coefficient convolution gives ||H_1H_2||_W<=||H_1||_W||H_2||_W.

Let v_N(t)=(1,t,...,t^(N-1))^T and v_k(t) similarly, and define

    K_R(t)=v_N(t^-1)^T R v_N(t)
          =d+sum_(h=1)^(N-1)a_h(t^h+t^-h),
    B_k(t)=v_k(t^-1)^T G^-1 v_k(t),
    H_P(t)=P(t)P(t^-1).

**Proposition 1.** As an exact rational Laurent-polynomial identity,

    K_R(t)=N-H_P(t) B_k(t).                          (3)

The constant coefficient of H_P B_k is k.
Moreover

    kappa_R=(||H_P B_k||_W-k)/d.                    (4)

*Proof.* The column construction gives A^T v_N(t)=P(t)v_k(t) and v_N(t^-1)^T A=P(t^-1)v_k(t^-1)^T.
Substitute these two identities in R=I-A G^-1 A^T; the identity contribution is N.
Taking constant coefficients gives constant K_R=trace R=d, hence constant H_P B_k=N-d=k.
Its nonconstant coefficients are exactly -a_h at both signed frequencies.
Since k>0, taking absolute coefficient sums proves(4).

On |t|=1, B_k(t)>0 because G^-1 is positive definite and v_k(t) is nonzero; its complex evaluation is v_k(t)^*G^-1v_k(t).
Thus(3) retains the actual squared modulus |P(t)|^2, including every unit-circle zero and its contact with K_R=N.
If P is reciprocal, P(t^-1)=t^-d P(t), so the cleared identity t^(N-1)(N-K_R(t)) has P(t)^2 as an exact factor.
This follows algebraically from(3), with the remaining shifted B_k a genuine polynomial because its exponents lie between-(k-1) and k-1.

### 3. A constructive dimension-free finite-Gram criterion

                                                               (5)

    kappa_R <= (k/d) [(1+gamma)/(1-theta_k)-1]
             = (k/d)(gamma+theta_k)/(1-theta_k).      (6)

*Proof.* For any real vector x of length k the triangle inequality gives

    sum_i |(Gx)_i|
      >=B sum_i |x_i|-sum_j(sum_(i!=j)|G_(i,j)|)|x_j|
