# Which six-term unit-coefficient lifts give a valid interlacing numerator

<a id="simple"></a>
## A common-root test for the two early unit coefficients

The [six-term necessity](Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) leaves two coefficients of modulus one when the reduced exponents are unbounded.
Write
\[
  N=2h+2,\quad 1\le d<e\le h,\quad A(z)=2+a z^d+b z^e,\quad
  \Pi(z)=A(z)-z^N A(1/z),\quad a,b\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
All roots of \(\Pi\) are on the unit circle.
The trace polynomial
\[
 C(x)=2U_h(x/2)+aU_{h-d}(x/2)+bU_{h-e}(x/2)
\]
has \(h\) distinct roots in \((-2,2)\) if and only if there is no simultaneous solution
\[
                      z^d=-a,\qquad z^e=-b,\qquad z^N=-1 .
\]
In particular every coprime pair \((d,e)\) supplies a valid numerator for all \(h\ge e\).
The remaining [inverse test](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) asks for a monic integer denominator \(B\) with the required residues and endpoints.

Indeed the triangle inequality gives \(A(z)\ne0\) for \(|z|<1\).
Equality on the circle identifies the common roots of the first two binomials in the displayed test, and \(zA'(z)=-(d+e)\) at each, so they are simple.
Let \(G\) be the product of their linear factors, normalized to constant one, and set \(E=A/G\).
The common roots are stable under conjugation and inversion and form a finite union of Galois orbits of roots of unity.
Thus \(G\) is an integral product of signed cyclotomic polynomials and \(G(1/z)=\epsilon z^{-\deg G}G(z)\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Every zero of \(E\) has modulus strictly greater than one.
After canceling \(G\), the rational function
\[
                   W(z)=z^N A(1/z)/A(z)
                      =\epsilon z^{N-e} E^*(z)/E(z),\qquad
                   E^*(z)=z^{\deg E}E(1/z),
\]
is a finite Blaschke product of degree \(N-\deg G\).
This follows by pairing each exterior zero of \(E\) with its reciprocal conjugate in \(E^*\), along with the \(N-e\) zeros at zero.
Each factor contributes strictly positive angular derivative on the circle, and their sum integrates to \(2\pi(N-\deg G)\).
Hence \(W=1\) has exactly \(N-\deg G\) distinct circle solutions.
Together with the roots of \(G\) these account for all roots of \(\Pi=GE(1-W)\), counting multiplicities.

At a common root \(\zeta\), the limit of \(A(1/z)/A(z)\) is \(-\zeta^{-2}A'(\zeta^{-1})/A'(\zeta)=-1\) by the derivative formula above.
Therefore a double root occurs precisely when \(\zeta^N=-1\).
Both \(1,-1\) are roots of \(\Pi\) since \(N\) is even and neither can be double.
The identity \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\) now proves the simple-root test, since away from the endpoints \(z\mapsto z+z^{-1}\) has nonzero derivative and pairs inverse roots into one interior real trace.
When \(d,e\) are coprime, the power equations give \(\zeta^{\gcd(2d,2e)}=1\), so only \(1,-1\) could be common roots.

## The first sub-Lehmer parameter tests

Put \(m=h+1\).
The endpoints are
\[
 C(2)=(2+a+b)m-ad-be,\qquad
 (-1)^h C(-2)=2m+a(-1)^d(m-d)+b(-1)^e(m-e).
\]
For each \(h\), the largest possible positive endpoint is \(4m-3\), achieved at \(a=b=1,\ (d,e)=(1,2)\).
The inverse construction needs \(q-1\mid C(2)\), so \(h\ge19\) is necessary to reach \(q\ge77\).
Such a constant would give an exterior trace \(T\) with \(T-2\le2/(q-1)\le1/38\), since the [inverse trace function](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#endpoints) has \(F(2)=-2/(q-1)\) and \(F'\ge1\) above two.
This is strictly below the [trace excess of Lehmer's number](Yin_L_G0462_v37.md#pruning).
At \(h=19\) only the two positive signs can attain \(C(2)\ge76\).
Indeed each \(m-d,m-e\) lies strictly between zero and \(m\), and with any negative sign the sum is less than \(3m\).
The two positive signs require \(d+e\le4\), leaving precisely \((1,2,q=78)\) and \((1,3,q=77)\).
Both offsets are coprime, so the simple-root test verifies the numerator before searching the integer denominator.
In each case the divisor in the inverse construction must be one.
