# A sparse Mahler gap above the modulus of an endpoint coefficient

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## One extremal coefficient

For integers \(K,m\ge1\) there is \(\varepsilon(K,m)>0\) with the following property.
Let \(P\) be a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial in one variable with at most \(K\) terms and with coefficient of modulus \(m\) at its least or greatest exponent.
Then its multiplicative Mahler measure satisfies
\[
                  M(P)=m\qquad\text{or}\qquad M(P)\ge m+\varepsilon(K,m).
\]
The other endpoint, the exponents and the degree span are unrestricted.
The same constant works for an integer Laurent polynomial in any finite number of variables with a coefficient of modulus \(m\) at a vertex of its Newton polytope.

On 2026-11-16 agent-S for Yin obtained the endpoint argument while examining whether a bounded-term multiple in the opposite-sign eight-term trace case could be divided by its specialized reciprocal factor.
The argument uses the equality case of the Mahler lower bound under torus translations of a fixed polynomial.
In [Dobrowolski–Smyth, Theorem 2, p. 2 and Section 4, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.04376), the set of logarithmic measures of bounded-term integer Laurent polynomials is closed, and zero is isolated in that set.
Here their [coefficient-height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives finite coefficient possibilities, and the endpoint condition gives isolation from above at each fixed \(m\).

<a id="translations"></a>
## Torus translations at a fixed specialization

Write \(m_{\log}(F)=\log M(F)\) for the integral of \(\log|F|\) against normalized Haar measure on its torus.
Reversing exponents and removing a monomial preserve \(M\), so use the highest coefficient of modulus \(m\) and take the least exponent to be zero.
A monomial satisfies equality.
If the one-variable assertion fails there are polynomials \(P_i\) with \(M(P_i)>m\) tending to \(m\).
After extraction they have \(k\ge2\) nonzero terms and one fixed tuple of integer coefficients \(a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1}\) in order of increasing exponents, with \(|a_{k-1}|=m\).
Indeed the height is at most \(2^{k-2}M(P_i)\) by the sparse height inequality.
Call the distinct positive exponents \(\mathbf n_i=(n_{1,i},\ldots,n_{k-1,i})\).

By a countable diagonal extraction each fixed integral relation on \(\mathbf n_i\) either holds eventually or fails eventually.
The subgroup \(R\) of eventual relations is saturated, and \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z^{k-1}/R\) is free of positive rank.
After its finite basis of relations vanishes, evaluation at \(\mathbf n_i\) descends to a homomorphism \(\lambda_i:\Gamma\to\mathbb Z\).
In a fixed basis of \(\Gamma\) the shortest nonzero sup-norm relation on the evaluation vector tends to infinity, allowing infinity if its kernel is zero.
Otherwise a bounded kernel vector would repeat on a subsequence, contrary to the stabilized relations.
Let \(v_0=0\) and \(v_j\) for \(1\le j<k\) be the images of the standard generators.
For the fixed Laurent polynomial \(F=\sum_j a_j X^{v_j}\) on \(\widehat\Gamma\), the specialization by \(\lambda_i\) equals \(P_i\).
The \(v_j\) are distinct since their evaluations are distinct.
[Lawton's specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) now gives \(m_{\log}(F)=\log m\).

Fix any one of these \(i\).
For each character \(X\in\widehat\Gamma\) form the polynomial
\[
               P_{i,X}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} a_j X^{v_j}z^{\lambda_i(v_j)} .
\]
Its degree is fixed for this \(i\), and the leading coefficient has modulus \(m\) at every \(X\).
The one-variable root product gives \(M(P_{i,X})\ge m\).
The function \(X\mapsto m_{\log}(P_{i,X})\) is continuous by continuity of the root multiset in fixed degree with nonvanishing leading coefficient.
For each \(|z|=1\) multiplication of \(X\) by the character \(v\mapsto z^{\lambda_i(v)}\) preserves Haar probability.
Since \(\log|F|\) is integrable for a nonzero Laurent polynomial, this translation identity also gives integrability of the absolute logarithm on the product torus.
Fubini thus gives
\[
                 \int_{\widehat\Gamma}m_{\log}(P_{i,X})\,dX
                      =m_{\log}(F)=\log m .
\]
The continuous integrand is bounded below by its mean, so it equals \(\log m\) at every \(X\).
In particular the trivial character gives \(M(P_i)=m\), contradicting strict excess.
The phase-translation step also works for a fixed finite tuple of complex coefficients, with constants then depending on that tuple.
More generally, local finiteness of the allowed complex coefficients in bounded disks replaces integrality in the coefficient extraction, with dependence on that set.

<a id="vertex"></a>
## A vertex of a multivariable polynomial

For a Laurent polynomial \(Q\) of fixed positive ambient dimension \(s\), let \(v\) be the vertex at which the coefficient has modulus \(m\).
Choose an integral linear functional \(\ell\) uniquely maximized at \(v\) on the support, by strict separation and rational approximation.
In lattice coordinates take the specialization vectors
\[
                   \mathbf b(t)=t^s\ell+(1,t,\ldots,t^{s-1}),
                          \qquad t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
The final exponent belongs to \(v\) for all large \(t\).
For every nonzero integral vector \(w\), \(w\cdot\mathbf b(t)\) is a nonzero polynomial in \(t\), since the coefficients below degree \(s\) already give \(w\).
Consequently all support terms are eventually distinct and the shortest nonzero relation tends to infinity.
By Lawton the measures of the specialized univariate polynomials converge to \(M(Q)\).
They have the same endpoint modulus \(m\) and at most \(K\) terms, so they belong to the closed set \(\{m\}\cup[m+\varepsilon(K,m),\infty)\).
This proves the asserted dimension-independent gap.

<a id="product"></a>
## Application to a bounded-support multiple of the reciprocal trace

Suppose \(A_i,D_i\in\mathbb Z[z]\), \(M(A_i)=|\operatorname{lc}(A_i)|=m\) is fixed, and \(|\operatorname{lc}(D_i)|=1\).
If \(A_iD_i\) has at most \(K\) terms, the endpoint gap and multiplicativity give
\[
                  M(D_i)=1\qquad\text{or}\qquad
                  M(D_i)\ge 1+\varepsilon(K,m)/m .
\]
Only the product needs a term bound.
In particular a uniform circle \(L^1\) bound on the integral product suffices by the [integer support estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window).

For the opposite-sign eight-term return take \(A_i=2(1-z^{d+e})+a(z^d-z^e)\), \(a=\pm1\), and \(D_i=U_iV_iB^\sharp\) from the [denominator comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v96.md#return).
The [trace factorization](Yin_L_G0462_v94.md#opposite) gives \(\Pi=A_i(1+z^{N-d-e})\).
The polynomial \(\Pi\) has all zeros on the unit circle and leading coefficient of modulus two, so \(M(A_i)=|\operatorname{lc}(A_i)|=2\).
Also \(D_i\) has leading coefficient of modulus one and \(M(D_i)=M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one on an escaping sequence, since \(U_i,V_i\) are products of cyclotomic binomials up to sign.
One can retain the full factor \(A_i\) on both sides of the comparison.
With \(Q_0\) the truncation used there and \(R_i=2/A_i\), it reads
\[
        A_iD_i-Q_0\Pi
            = A_i(\Pi/2)\bigl(U_iV_i J_i-Q_0R_i\bigr).
\]
The two series on the right have the long common prefix and bounded positive-index representing variation established in that comparison.
The two polynomial multipliers have uniformly bounded suprema, and the left polynomial has degree \(O(h)\).
Thus the [two cutoffs](Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) give bounded circle norm of the difference and then of \(A_iD_i\), since \(Q_0\Pi\) is uniformly bounded.
The product gap at \(m=2\) excludes this return already with the reciprocal factor present.
The separate argument of L462v96 uses exact cancellation to obtain a bounded-term multiple at endpoint one.
