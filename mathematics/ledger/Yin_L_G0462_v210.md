# Real exterior roots at every slope and an explicit quartic splitting threshold

<a id="real"></a>
## Real exterior roots for all positive exponents

Fix integers \(s\ge3\) and \(a,b\ge1\).
Write \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), \(F(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\), and \(r=b/a\).
There is no positive real exterior root of \(F\).
The negative real exterior roots are simple, and their number is one in exactly the following cases and zero otherwise:

| Exponents and seed | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Odd \(a\), even \(s\), even \(b\) | Every \(r>0\). |
| Odd \(a\), even \(s\), odd \(b\) | \(r>s+2\). |
| Odd \(a\), odd \(s\), even \(b\) | \(r<s-2\). |

For \(u>1\) we have \(B(u)<0\) and \(g(u)+B(u)=-u-u^{s-1}<0\), ruling out a positive exterior root.
For negative \(X\) with even \(a\), put \(u=|X|^a>1\).
If \(b\) is even the same inequality applies; if \(b\) is odd then \(g(u)-u^rB(u)>g(u)-B(u)=2u^s+u^{s-1}-u-2>0\).

Now take \(a\) odd and \(u=|X|^a>1\).
For even \(s\) write \(G=u^s+u-1\) and \(V=u^s-u^{s-1}-1\).
The equation for a negative root is \(u^rV=(-1)^bG\).
There is a unique \(\beta_s>1\) with \(V(\beta_s)=0\), since \(V\) increases strictly for \(u\ge1\).
If \(b\) is even, \(u>\beta_s\) and the right comparison is \(u^r=G/V=1+(u^{s-1}+u)/V\).
Both \(V/u^{s-1}=u-1-u^{1-s}\) and \(V/u=u^{s-1}-u^{s-2}-u^{-1}\) have positive derivative for \(u>1\).
Thus \(G/V\) decreases strictly from infinity to one, giving one simple solution.
If \(b\) is odd, any solution lies below \(\beta_s\).
The identity
\[
   G+u^{s+2}V=(u-1)(u^s-1)(u^{s+1}-1)
\]
excludes solutions there when \(r\le s+2\).
For \(r>s+2\), \(G+u^rV\) vanishes at one with negative derivative \(s+2-r\), and is positive at \(\beta_s\).
Its powers in decreasing order have signs \(+,-,-,+,+,-\).
After substituting \(u=y^a\), Descartes' rule bounds the positive roots by three, counted with multiplicity.
Besides one and the root above one, their reciprocal symmetry gives the inverse root below one.
This proves simplicity and uniqueness.

For odd \(s\), \(g(-u)=-(u^s-u+1)<0\) and \(B(-u)=u^s-u^{s-1}+1>0\).
Odd \(b\) gives no solution.
For even \(b\) put \(A=u^s-u+1\), \(D=u^s-u^{s-1}+1\).
The identity
\[
      u^{s-2}D-A=(u-1)(u^{s-2}-1)(u^{s-1}-1)
\]
excludes \(r\ge s-2\).
For \(r<s-2\), \(u^rD-A\) has derivative \(r-(s-2)<0\) at one and is positive for large \(u\).
Its largest term has positive sign, then come the two negative terms of exponents \(r+s-1,s\), the two positive terms of exponents \(r,1\), and the negative constant.
Coinciding powers in either same-sign pair do not increase the number of sign changes.
The same three-root count applies, since the expression changes by the factor \(-u^{-r-s}\) under inversion.
This completes the classification for every \(s,a,b\), including common dilations.

## The complete exterior count for the quartic seed at slope six

For \(s=4\) and \(b\ge6a\), \(F\) has exactly \(b+2a\) simple roots on the unit circle and exactly \(a\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity.
Set \(t=z+z^{-1}\) on \(|z|=1\).
The two reciprocal polynomials \(g,B\) have no common root: a common root of them would satisfy \(z^3=-z\) by addition and would be one of \(0,\pm i\), none of which is a root of \(g\).
In particular \(g\) has no circle root.
The phase derivative of \(g/B\) on the circle, with respect to \(\arg z\), is
\[
  \frac{z(g'B-gB')}{gB}=-\frac{t^3+2}{D(t)},\qquad
                  D(t)=|g(z)|^2=-t^4-t^3+4t^2+4t+1 .
\]
It is at most six.
Indeed it is nonpositive for \(t\ge-\sqrt[3]2\), whereas on the rest of \([-2,2]\)
\[
     6D(t)+t^3+2=(t+2)(-6t^3+7t^2+10t+4)\ \ge0 .
\]
For \(v=-t>0\) the second factor is \(6v^3+7v^2-10v+4>0\), since the quadratic has negative discriminant.
Equality of the phase derivative with six requires \(z=-1\).

The [quartic pole calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#quartic) gives one zero of \(g\) and three of \(B\) inside the circle.
Thus \(g/B\) has winding number \(-2\).
The phase of \(z^b B(z^a)/g(z^a)\) increases by \(2\pi(b+2a)\) on a full turn.
Its derivative is strictly positive for \(b>6a\).
For \(b=6a\) it remains positive except at \(z^a=-1\), where the ratio equals \(1\) and the section equation requires \(-1\).
Consequently there are exactly \(b+2a\) simple solutions of the section equation on the circle in both cases.
Reciprocity and total degree \(4a+b\) give the exterior count.

## A Salem test for every primitive quartic section with \(a=3,\ b>18\)

For \(a=3\), the [real count](#real) and the [phase bound](#phase) give exactly one negative exterior root \(-\sigma\) and one nonreal conjugate pair whenever \(b>18\).
No coprimality is needed for these counts.
When \(b\) is odd, \(\sigma^3<\beta_4<7/5<(9/8)^3\).
When \(b\) is even, compare \(u^{b/3}\) with the decreasing \(G/V\) on \(u>\beta_4\).
At \(u=2\) the ratio is \(17/7<2^{b/3}\), so \(\sigma<2^{1/3}<\theta\), with \(\theta^3=\theta+1\) for the least Pisot number \(\theta\).
For even \(b\ge26\), taking \(w=9/8\) and \(u=w^3\) instead gives \(w^{26}V(u)>G(u)\): after clearing positive denominators this reads
\[
        9^{26}(9^{12}-9^9 8^3-8^{12})
                 >8^{26}(9^{12}+9^3 8^9-8^{12}).
\]
Thus \(\sigma<9/8\) also in that range.

Every nontorsion rational irreducible factor receives an exterior root, by the unit endpoints and Kronecker's theorem.
A factorization into two nontorsion factors therefore isolates \(-\sigma\), since the nonreal pair is inseparable under rational factorization.
The factor containing only this exterior root has multiplicative Mahler measure \(\sigma<\theta\), so is reciprocal by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001).
It cannot be quadratic: its rational integer trace after sign change would be \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) strictly between two and three.
The sign change therefore gives a Salem number.
Conversely if \(\sigma\) is Salem its factor cannot contain the nonreal exterior pair and the core splits.
All exterior roots are simple, so in the split case there are exactly two nontorsion factors with multiplicity one.
For all primitive \(b>18\) this makes the [previous asymptotic criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#quartic) effective.
Any split at odd \(b>18\) or even \(b\ge26\) gives a Salem number below \(9/8\).
Whether any such split occurs remains open.
