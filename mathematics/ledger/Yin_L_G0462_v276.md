# A fourth-order cofactor divisor for a Salem unit and its square

<a id="center"></a>
## The common zero at the inversion fixed point

Let \(\delta\) be a Salem number of degree \(d\ge4\), and choose one conjugate \(x_i\) from each inverse pair.
Use the [signed value and Euler derivative matrix](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#extra) with width \(m=2d\), all columns \(0,\ldots,m\), formal exponent \(N\), and sign \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
For each \(i\) its two sites are \(z_i,z_i^2\), with respective labels \(\varepsilon x_i,\varepsilon x_i^2\).
Thus the entries in column \(l\) at a site \(y\) with label \(c\) are \(y^l+cy^{m-l}\) and \(Nly^l+c(1+N(m-l))y^{m-l}\).
The variables \(z_i\) are independent over the splitting field \(K\).
In the polynomial gcd of the maximal minors in \(K[N,\mathbf z]\), each \(z_i-1\) occurs with multiplicity exactly four.
Moreover, for fixed generic values of the other \(z_j\), there is no sequence of simultaneous zeros of the maximal minors with \(N\ne0,\ z_i\ne1\) and \((N,z_i)\to(0,1)\).
The [quadratic computation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/versions/v4/readme.md#certificate) previously exhibited the common divisor in a family of degree-two examples.

Fix \(i\), put \(x=x_i\), and fix the remaining variables so that their \(m-4\) sites and inverse sites \(z_j^{\pm1},z_j^{\pm2}\), counted individually, are distinct and avoid \(1,-1\).
At \(N=0\) the kernel of the remaining \(m-4\) rows consists of \(H(Z)B(Z)\), where \(H\) is the monic product of the linear factors for these points and \(\deg B\le4\).
These rows have full rank near \(N=0\), so their five-dimensional kernel has a basis analytic in \(N\), by solving at a fixed invertible minor.
For a polynomial \(A\) in this kernel write \(v(t)=e^{-mt/2}A(e^t)\).
The five jets \(v^{(j)}(0)\), \(0\le j\le4\), are independent at \(N=0\) because \(H(1)\ne0\) and \(e^t-1\) has a simple zero.
They remain independent for small \(N\).
Choose the analytic kernel basis dual to these jets, so its \(j\)-th function is \(t^j/j!+O(t^5)\) with derivative \(jt^{j-1}/j!+O(t^4)\), uniformly near \(N=0\), with zero leading derivative for \(j=0\).
Eliminating an invertible minor in the other rows reduces the ideal of full maximal minors locally to the ideal of four-row minors on this kernel.
Indeed analytic invertible row and column operations give the direct sum of an identity matrix and the four-by-five restriction, and preserve the ideal of maximal minors by multilinearity.

Put \(\theta=\log z_i\) near \(1\).
At \(k=1,2\), set \(c_k=\varepsilon x^k,\ a_k=(1-c_k)/(1+c_k),\ r_k=(1-a_k^2)/2\).
After dividing out the centering exponential and subtracting \(Nm/2\) times the value row from the derivative row, these rows are
\[
 f_k=v(k\theta)+c_kv(-k\theta),\qquad
 g_k=N\big(v'(k\theta)-c_kv'(-k\theta)\big)+c_kv(-k\theta).
\]
Here \(v'\) differentiates with respect to the site logarithm, so the derivative row has the same \(N\) for both sites.
Write \(F_k=f_k/(1+c_k)\) and \(G_k=(g_k-c_k f_k/(1+c_k))/(1+c_k)\).
For \(N\theta\ne0\) multiply column \(j\) by \(j!/\theta^j\) and each \(G_k\) by \(\theta/N\).
If \(t=\theta/N\) is bounded, the entries differ by \(O((1+|t|)|\theta|^{5-j})\) from those in the array
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &F_k&G_k\ \text{after scaling}\\ \hline
 j\ \mathrm{even}& k^j & j k^{j-1}\\
 j\ \mathrm{odd}&a_k k^j &j a_k k^{j-1}-t r_k k^j .
\end{array}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
These bounds follow from the two uniform jet remainders.

<a id="order"></a>
## Multiplicity four

Hold \(N\) fixed and nonzero sufficiently close to zero.
Undoing the column and row scalings in a four-column minor gives a factor proportional to \(N^2\theta^{\sum j-2}\).
The sum of four distinct indices in \(0,\ldots,4\) is at least six, so every minor vanishes to order at least four.
Write \(a=a_1,\ b=a_2,\ r=r_1,\ R=r_2\).
For columns \(0,1,2,3\) the determinant of (1) at \(t=0\), up to sign, is
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
  2b-a&3&8b-a\\ a&2&3a\\ b&4&12b
 \end{pmatrix}=32b^2-41ab+8a^2.
\]
This follows by subtracting \(F_1\) from \(F_2\) and expanding in column zero.
For \(T=x+x^{-1}\) the ratio \(b/a\) is \((T+2)/T\) at the positive sign and \(T/(T+2)\) at the negative sign.
The determinant divided by \(a^2\) is correspondingly
\[
  \frac{-T^2+46T+128}{T^2}
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \frac{-T^2-50T+32}{(T+2)^2}.
\]
The positive-sign numerator is positive on \([-2,2]\), which contains the trace of a unit-circle Salem conjugate.
The negative-sign numerator is negative at the exterior trace \(\delta+\delta^{-1}>2\).
All denominators are nonzero since the Salem conjugates are nontorsion.
Testing at the indicated embeddings proves nonvanishing over \(K\).
Thus the four-column minor has order exactly four.
The generic choices of the other variables range over a nonempty Zariski-open set, and for each such choice the order holds for every sufficiently small nonzero \(N\).
Polynomiality then gives exact common multiplicity four along \(z_i=1\) in the original multivariate tuple.

## Excluding simultaneous zeros approaching this collision

If \(N,\theta\to0\) with \(N\theta\ne0\) and \(t=\theta/N\) bounded, take a subsequence with \(t\) convergent.
The even columns \(0,2,4\) in (1) have rank three.
In row order \(F_1,G_1,F_2,G_2\), their unique annihilating combination up to scale is \((1,3/4,-1,3/8)\).
For this combination also to vanish on the odd columns \(1,3\) in the limit would require
\[
  14a-13b=6t(r+R),\qquad 13a-14b=3t(r+4R),
\]
where \(t\) now denotes the subsequential limit.
Eliminating \(t\) gives the necessary equation
\[
                         (5b-4a)r+(10a-8b)R=0.                 \tag{2}
\]
At the positive sign \(b=2a/(1+a^2)\) and the left side of (2) equals
\[
  \frac{2a^3(1-a^2)(6-3a^2-a^4)}{(1+a^2)^3}.
\]
It is nonzero at the embedding \(x=\delta\), where \(-1<a<0\).
At the negative sign \(b=(1+a^2)/(2a)\), and the left side is \((1-a^2)/(2a^3)\), also nonzero.
The limit matrix has rank four at every finite \(t\).

If \(|\theta/N|\to\infty\), retain the column scalings and use the rows \(F_k,G_k\) without the derivative-row scaling.
The terms containing \(N/\theta\) then tend to zero.
On even columns the limiting \(G_k\) vanishes and on odd columns it is \(-r_k k^j\).
Because \(r_k\ne0\), the limiting rows at each site span the two evaluations of monomials at \(k,-k\).
The Vandermonde minor on columns \(0,1,2,3\) at \(1,-1,2,-2\) is invertible.
Both cases exclude a sequence of off-divisor common zeros approaching \((0,1)\).

<a id="other"></a>
## Distinct first-order separations at a simultaneous pair collision

At a generic collision \(z_i=z_j\), the logarithmic separations of the ordinary sites and of their squares are \(\eta=\log(z_i/z_j)\) and \(2\eta\).
For an isolated collision of sites with labels \(c,d\), the [leading determinant of the two restrictions](Yin_L_G0462_v253.md#collision) vanishes at scaled separation \(\pm(q-q^{-1})\), where \(q^2=d/c\).
For the base and square collisions take \(q^2=x_j/x_i\).
The two isolated leading determinants cannot both vanish at these prescribed separations, since that would give
\[
     2(q-q^{-1})=\pm(q^2-q^{-2}).
\]
The distinct representatives have \(q^2\ne1\), so division gives \(q+q^{-1}=\pm2\), a contradiction.
The same comparison applies at \(z_i=z_j^{-1}\) after inverting the second representative and its square, as in the cited collision calculation.
It remains to analyze the full restrictions when the base and square collisions occur together, as well as the other possible limits of a common divisor.
These unresolved collisions can affect the lower bound for the primitive height.

## An upper bound for the primitive height at minimum width

Put \(h=\log\delta\) and specialize \(N=n,\ z_j=x_j^n\).
For each sign let \(A_n\) be the primitive integral generator of the signed kernel at width \(m=2d\).
At all sufficiently large positive integers \(n\) it exists and is genuine at every site by the [minimum-support theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#statement).
Indeed the \(x_j\) are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, since an embedding sending \(x_j\) to \(\delta\) sends every other representative to the unit circle.
Thus the \(2d\) roots \(x_j^{\pm1},x_j^{\pm2}\) are distinct with nontorsion pairwise quotients, and they form the roots of the two distinct minima of \(\delta,\delta^2\).
We obtain
\[
                  \log\|A_n\|_1\le n(6d-4)h+O_\delta(\log(2n)).
\]

For an embedding \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), give \(z_j\) weight \(u_j=\log|\sigma(x_j)|\).
Exactly one \(u_i\) is nonzero, and \(u_i=\pm h\).
The two sites of this representative have weights \(u_i,2u_i\).
In a determinant term, a column \(l\) assigned to a site of weight \(w\) contributes at most \((m/2)w+|l-m/2|\,|w|\).
The two rows of the heavier site have absolute weight \(2h\), and the next two have absolute weight \(h\).
The four largest column distances from \(m/2\) are \(m/2,m/2,m/2-1,m/2-1\).
Thus the [column rearrangement bound](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support) gives an upper weight of \(3mu_i+(3m-2)h\) for every raw minor, including after the square substitution.

Divide all signed minors by \(B=\prod_j(z_j-1)^4\), using the [multiplicity calculation](#order), and denote the resulting tuple by \(Q\).
The maximum weight of a product of two nonzero polynomials over \(K[N]\) is the sum of their maximum weights, since their top-weight parts have nonzero product.
As \(B\) has maximum weight \(4\max(u_i,0)\), every nonzero entry of \(Q\) has maximum weight at most
\[
                      3mu_i+(3m-2)h-4\max(u_i,0).
\]
The evaluation \(B(\mathbf x^n)\) is nonzero, so \(Q(n,\mathbf x^n)\) represents the rational kernel line.
After multiplying \(Q\) by a fixed integer to clear coefficient denominators, its evaluated coordinates are algebraic integers and each finite-place log maximum is nonpositive.
At the embedding \(\sigma\), the triangle inequality bounds the log maximum by \(n\) times the displayed weight bound plus \(O_\delta(\log(2n))\), since the coefficients of the monomials in \(\mathbf z\) are fixed polynomials in \(N\).
The average of \(u_i=\sum_j\log|\sigma(x_j)|\) over all embeddings is zero by the product formula for the units \(x_j\).
Its absolute value is always \(h\), so \(\max(u_i,0)\) averages to \(h/2\).
Combining the places bounds the absolute logarithmic projective height by \(n(3m-4)h+O_\delta(\log(2n))\).
This height equals the log maximum coefficient of the primitive integral generator, and its one-norm is at most \(m+1\) times the maximum.
This gives the stated bound and in particular \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log\|A_n\|_1\le(6d-4)\log\delta\) for both signs.
The exact loss from this upper rate can be expressed using the remaining polynomial gcd.
Let \(D\) be the gcd of the entries of \(Q\) in \(K[N,\mathbf z]\), and let \(w_i\) be the difference between the largest and smallest \(z_i\)-exponents in \(D\).
Then
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A_n\|_1}{n}
       =\left(6d-4-\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^{d/2}w_i\right)\log\delta .
\]

To verify this identity, first give the independent \(z_j\) arbitrary real weights \(u_j\) such that all \(d\) numbers \(|u_j|,2|u_j|\) are nonzero and distinct.
Before the square substitution, assign the formal sites the corresponding weights \(u_j,2u_j\).
In the minor with column \(m/2\) omitted, the [extremal term calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support) assigns the columns in unique complementary pairs to the sites in decreasing order of their absolute weight.
The two orderings in each pair give a nonzero two-by-two determinant, so a unique exponent vector attains the bound and its coefficient is the signed product of the labels.
Substituting \(z_j,z_j^2\) at the two sites preserves this extreme term, because any other exponent vector mapping to the same monomial would have the same weight.
The maximum weight of the center minor is a continuous function of the direction, as is the column rearrangement bound.
Equality extends from these generic directions to the coordinate directions \(u=\pm e_i\).
In the positive direction the maximum of the raw minors is \(3m+(3m-2)=6m-2\), and in the negative direction it is \(-3m+(3m-2)=-2\).
Thus their joint \(z_i\)-exponents range from \(2\) to \(6m-2\).
Division by \(B\) reduces the width \(6m-4\) by four, and division by \(D\) reduces it by \(w_i\), using weight additivity in each of the two directions.
Multiplying the tuple by one monomial translates its exponents and preserves all these widths.

The chosen Salem representatives generate the group of all the roots freely, so the [fixed-support height formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) applies on the full sequence of exponents.
For each \(i\), a fraction \(1/d\) of the embeddings send \(x_i\) to \(\delta\), and another fraction \(1/d\) send it to \(\delta^{-1}\), by transitivity of the Galois group on the conjugates.
At each such embedding all the other \(z_j\) have weight zero.
Consequently the rate is \(h/d\) times the sum of the reduced coordinate widths \(6m-8-w_i\), as asserted.
The upper rate is attained exactly when \(D\) is a monomial in the \(z_i\) times a polynomial in \(N\).
Whether this holds for every Salem seed and both signs remains open.
