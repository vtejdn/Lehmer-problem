# Small modular factors of high-slope reciprocal cubic sections

<a id="linear"></a>
## A binary linear equation for an irreducible family

Let \(g(T)=T^3-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\), and write \(A_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\).
Bars denote reduction modulo two in this section.
For each integer \(r\ge1\) put
\[
              t=2^r,\qquad k=2^t,\qquad q=2^k,\qquad
              H=2^{t^2-1}-1.
\]
As \(r\to\infty\), \(H=q^{o(1)}\).
For \((a,b)=(1,q+1)\) the cyclotomic part is exactly \(\Phi_8\), by the [complete cubic torsion table](Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#cyclotomic).
Indeed \(q+1\equiv1\bmod8\) and \(k\) is a multiple of four, giving \(q+1\equiv17\bmod30\).
Here \(q+4\) is the full degree, so the remaining monic factor product \(P=-A_{1,q+1}/\Phi_8\) has degree \(q\).
For large \(r\), it is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) by the [exterior-pair partition](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition), since there is only one pair to allocate.
It is reciprocal, and its trace polynomial of degree \(q/2\) is consequently irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Nevertheless its reduction modulo two is squarefree and every irreducible trace degree divides \(H\).

To see this, set \(f=\bar g/\bar B\), \(D(x)=f(x)/x\), and \(w(x)=1/(x+1)\).
The identity \(\bar g+x\bar B=(x+1)^4\) gives
\[
                 w(D(x))=\frac{x\bar B(x)}{(x+1)^4}
                          =w(x)^4+w(x)^2+w(x)+1 .
\]
The section equation at \(x\ne1\) is \(D(x)=x^q\).
More explicitly,
\[
       \bar A_{1,q+1}(x)=(x+1)^{q+4}
                 \bigl(w^q+w^4+w^2+w+1\bigr),\qquad w=1/(x+1).
\]
The polynomial in \(w\) is separable of degree \(q\), with neither zero nor one as a root.
It gives \(q\) distinct nonzero roots \(x\ne1\).
The remaining root \(x=1\) has multiplicity four and is exactly the reduction of \(\Phi_8\).

Let \(S(v)=v^2\) on \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\), and put \(E(Z)=Z^k+Z^2+Z+1\), so \(E(S)w=1\).
If \(E(u)=0\), then \(S^t u+Su+u=1\), since \(k=2^t\).
Thus \((S+1)(S^t+S+1)u=0\).
Every root \(z\) of \(Z^t+Z+1\) satisfies \(z^t=z+1\) and \(z^{t^2}=z\), since \(t\) is a power of two.
These roots are simple, nonzero and distinct from one.
They therefore lie in \(\mathbb F_{t^2}^{\times}\), and
\[
            (Z+1)(Z^t+Z+1)\mid Z^{t^2-1}-1.
\]
This implies \(S^{t^2-1}u=u\), placing all the roots of \(E\) in \(\mathbb F_{2^{t^2-1}}\).
Since \(E(0)=1\) and \(E'=1\), we have \(Z^H-1=E(Z)J(Z)\) in \(\mathbb F_2[Z]\).
Both sides have a simple zero at one, and differentiation there gives \(J(1)=1\).
For each of the \(q\) solutions it follows that
\[
                 (S^H-1)w=J(S)1=1,\qquad x^{2^H}=x^{-1}.
\]
Indeed changing \(w\) to \(w+1\) takes \(x=1+1/w\) to its inverse.
All trace roots \(x+x^{-1}\) belong to \(\mathbb F_{2^H}\).
The inverse pairs are disjoint and have two elements since \(x\ne1\), giving \(q/2\) distinct trace roots and proving the degree assertion.
Finally \(\log H/\log q<(t^2-1)/2^t\to0\).

<a id="growing"></a>
## An unbounded number of small residue orbits after both exponents grow

With the same parameters take
\[
                    a=q,\qquad b=q(q+1)+2^H+1 .
\]
Then \(a\) is a power of two, \(b\) is odd and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
Both \(a\) and \(b/a\) tend to infinity.
The reduction of the nontorsion trace polynomial in this family has at least \((a-2)/2\) distinct roots whose degrees over \(\mathbb F_2\) divide \(H=a^{o(1)}\).
Consequently these roots occupy at least \((a-2)/(2H)\) different irreducible factors modulo two.

In fact every one of the \(q\) roots \(x\ne1\) from the preceding section has \(x^{2^H+1}=1\).
The element \(x^a\) still belongs to that root set by Frobenius.
Now \(x^b=(x^a)^{q+1}\), so \(x\) is a root of \(\bar A_{a,b}\).
All roots of this last reduction are simple because its derivative is \(X^{b-1}\bar B(X^a)\), the constant term is a unit, and \(\bar g,\bar B\) are coprime by \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\).
As \(a\) is even, only \(\Phi_2,\Phi_6,\Phi_{10}\) can be removed in characteristic zero.
Their binary roots have respective orders \(1,3,5\).
Order one is absent from the protected set, and order five is absent since \(H\) is odd and \(5\nmid2^H+1\).
Removing possible cube roots consequently discards at most two of its \(q\) distinct elements.
Both the protected set and the torsion removal are invariant under inversion.
The [reciprocity and simplicity at high slope](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition) therefore give the asserted trace roots after projection, without counting either member of an inverse pair twice.
These small orbits are a subset of the new reduction and supply no allocation of exterior roots to rational factors.

<a id="moduli"></a>
## A fixed trace divisor appears modulo every integer up to a logarithmic cutoff

Fix a positive integer \(b_0\equiv13\bmod120\), and put \(P_0=-A_{1,b_0}=X^{n_0}H_0(X+X^{-1})\), where \(n_0=(b_0+3)/2\).
Both exponents are odd, and neither the \(\Phi_8\) nor the \(\Phi_{30}\) condition in the torsion table holds.
Thus \(P_0\) is monic reciprocal, has unit constant and is entirely nontorsion.
For an integer \(Y\ge2\), let \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,Y)\) and take \(t_Y\) to be the multiplicative order of \(X\) in
\[
                   ((\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)[X]/(P_0))^\times .
\]
This order exists because \(P_0(0)=1\), and \(t_Y\le L^{2n_0}\) by monicity.
Put \(M=120\,2^Y t_Y\) and choose either of the two directions
\[
        (a,b)=(1,b_0+M),\qquad
        (a,b)=(1+b_0 M,\ b_0+Ma).
\]
Both are primitive, since in the second \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(1+b_0M,b_0)=1\).
Both have \(b/a\to\infty\), with \(a\to\infty\) in the second.
In either direction \(a\equiv1,\ b\equiv b_0\bmod120\), so the entire parent \(-A_{a,b}=X^nH_{a,b}(X+X^{-1})\) is again reciprocal and torsion-free.

For every integer modulus \(2\le m\le Y\) one has
\[
                H_0(Y')\mid H_{a,b}(Y')\quad
                         \text{in }(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)[Y'] .
\]
Indeed \(a\equiv1,\ b\equiv b_0\bmod t_Y\), so \(X^a=X\) and \(X^b=X^{b_0}\) in the displayed finite ring.
Hence \(P_0\) divides \(-A_{a,b}\) modulo \(L\).
For \(R=\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\), the ring \(R[X,X^{-1}]\) is free over \(R[Y']\), \(Y'=X+X^{-1}\), on \(1,X\).
This follows from the monic presentation \(X^2-Y'X+1=0\), in which \(Y'-X=X^{-1}\).
After removal of the monomial units, membership of \(H_{a,b}(Y')\) in \(H_0(Y')R[X,X^{-1}]\) thus implies divisibility already in \(R[Y']\).
Reduction to \(m\mid L\) proves the assertion at all the stated moduli, including all bad primes and prime powers.

For fixed \(b_0\) the cutoff satisfies \(Y\gg_{b_0}\log(a+b)\).
Indeed \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2j)\) divides \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,j)\binom{2j}{j}\).
For each prime whose highest power increases, that power lies in \((j,2j]\) and contributes one to the valuation of the binomial coefficient by the factorial formula.
Iteration along powers of two and \(\binom{2j}{j}\le4^j\) give \(\log L\le 2Y\log4\).
The bound on \(t_Y\) now gives \(\log M=O_{b_0}(Y)\), and the exponents are polynomially bounded in \(M\).

Taking \(b_0=13\) gives \(n_0=8\).
For large \(Y\), \(H_{a,b}\) has no rational factor of degree eight in either family, since such a factor would produce a rational factor of the parent of degree at most sixteen, contrary to the [uniform degree growth](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement).
Yet at every prime \(\ell\le Y\) the same monic \(H_0\) and its monic complement provide a division into degrees eight and \(n-8\).
In particular with \(n\ge16\) no such prime can have a trace factor of degree \(n\) or \(n-1\), as required by the [single-long-cycle test](Yin_L_G0462_v190.md#criterion).
In the first family rational irreducibility holds for all sufficiently large \(Y\) by the one-pair partition, so a witnessing prime exists and its least possible value exceeds \(Y\).
In the second family the modular exclusions hold for both exponents growing, irrespective of whether the rational parent is irreducible.
Choosing a larger fixed \(b_0\equiv13\bmod120\) gives the same conclusion with the prescribed trace degree \(n_0=(b_0+3)/2\).

## An Eisenstein family with full exterior allocation on a different graph

For \(g_1(U)=2U-1,\ B_1(U)=2-U\), the reciprocal section up to sign is
\[
             G_{a,b}(X)=X^{a+b}-2X^a-2X^b+1 .
\]
If \(a+b=2^j\) and \(a\) is odd, then \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(G_{a,b}(X+1)\) is Eisenstein at two.
Its reduction is \(X^{2^j}\), and its constant is \(G_{a,b}(1)=-2\).
Thus every exterior root belongs to one rational factor, including along choices \(a\to\infty\), \(a=o(2^j)\).
For those choices its logarithmic measure tends to \(\log2\).

In fact \(T=X^a\) identifies the roots with those of \(T^b(2-T)^a-(1-2T)^a\), by Bézout in \(a,b\) and nonvanishing of both graph coefficients at a root.
On the circle the map \((1-2T)/(2-T)\) has winding number one and phase derivative \((1-1/4)/|T-1/2|^2\le3\).
For \(r=b/a>3\), the winding equation therefore gives exactly \(b-a\) simple circle roots, and reciprocity leaves \(a\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity.
On \(|T-2|=1/2\) one has \(|T|\ge3/2\), so for large \(r\) Rouché gives \(a\) projected roots inside that disk.
Each satisfies \(|T-2|\le6(3/2)^{-r}\) there.
These roots exhaust the exterior set, each contributing \(a^{-1}\log|T|\), which proves the stated limit.
