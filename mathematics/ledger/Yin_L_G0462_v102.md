# Salem automorphisms whose periodic groups agree at every divisor of the degree minus two

<a id="plateau"></a>
## Two positive Salem families

For all sufficiently large multiples \(J\) of \(12600\) there are two monic reciprocal Salem minimal polynomials of degree \(d=J+2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_J(z)&=\frac{z^J f(z)+f^*(z)}{z+1},
       &f(z)&=z^3-z-1,\\
 Q_J(z)&=\frac{z^J g(z)-g^*(z)}{z-1},
       &g(z)&=z^3-2z^2-z+1,
\end{aligned}
\qquad u^*(z)=z^3u(1/z).
\]
The first has coefficient height one, the second height two.
Their exterior roots tend to the positive Pisot roots of \(f,g\), respectively, and each positive power of each Salem number generates its original number field.
Both limiting logarithmic Mahler measures lie strictly between zero and one.

For a polynomial in this pair let \(T\) be its integral companion automorphism on the real torus \(\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d\), and write \(F_n(T)=\ker(T^n-I)\).
For every \(n\mid J\) the actual fixed subgroups and the actions on them are as follows.

| Polynomial | Divisor \(n\mid J\) | Fixed subgroup | Action of \(T\) on it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(P_J\) | Odd | Trivial | Identity |
| \(P_J\) | Even | \(F_2(T)=\ker(T+I)\), cyclic of order \(J+1\) | Negation |
| \(Q_J\) | Every divisor | \(F_1(T)\), cyclic of order \(J+1\) | Identity |

In both families the [single common integer modulus](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) satisfies \(G_p=1\) at every odd prime \(p\mid J\).
For \(Q_J\) the primitive resultant is one even at order two and at every composite order \(n>1\) dividing \(J\).
The [earlier Salem norm construction](Yin_L_G0462_v28.md#cyclic) has two possible norm sizes distinguished by a prescribed power of two.
Here the second family gives one fixed subgroup with trivial action on the full divisor set, and the first family has trivial fixed group at every odd divisor with limiting measure the plastic number.
The [degree-plus-one family](Yin_L_G0462_v90.md#degree-plus-one) addresses a single prime immediately above the degree.

## The cyclotomic factors and the Salem degrees

The identity \(f+f^*=-z(z+1)\) holds, and the plastic seed \(f\) has just one root outside the unit circle as in the [plastic construction](Yin_L_G0462_v82.md#family).
For the plus sign used here the [six-term rank bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0270_v1.md#rank-two-cyclotomic-bound) gives cyclotomic orders dividing \(360\).
Indeed this is its original pair of columns evaluated at \((J,1)\).
As \(360\mid J\), any torsion zero of \(z^Jf+f^*\) must be \(-1\), and its derivative there is \(J+1\).
The quotient is therefore integral, has no torsion zeros and satisfies \(P_J(-1)=J+1,\ P_J(1)=-1\).

For the second seed \(g-g^*=-z(z-1)\).
The signs at \(-1,0,1,2,5/2\) locate its three roots in \((-1,0),(0,1),(2,5/2)\).
In particular it has no circle zero.
We bound the cyclotomic orders of \(z^Jg-g^*\) using the [rational minimal-relation bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0262_v2.md#minimal-vanishing-order).
At a torsion root of order \(t\), partition the eight terms into minimal vanishing blocks.
Their formal support consists of two columns \(\{0,1\}\times\{0,1,2,3\}\), evaluated at \((J,1)\).
Let \(\Lambda\) be generated by differences of support vectors within each block.
For every \((a,b)\in\Lambda\) one has \(t\mid210(aJ+b)\), since \(210\) is the product of the primes at most eight.
If the differences all had zero first coordinate, each column would sum to zero and \(g\) would have a torsion zero.
If they lay on one line of nonzero first-coordinate direction, each block would pair one term from each column.
Orienting all pairs from the first column to the second gives a single translation of \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) onto itself, with zero vertical displacement.
The matched coefficient magnitudes at height one are different, so that pair cannot vanish.
Thus two independent differences have a determinant \(D\) with \(1\le |D|\le6\), since horizontal differences have absolute value at most one and vertical differences at most three.
Their lattice contains \((0,D)\), whence \(t\mid 210 |D|\mid12600\).
Our choice of \(J\) gives \(z^J=1\) at every possible torsion zero, and \(g-g^*\) leaves only \(1\).
The derivative there is \(Jg(1)+(g-g^*)'(1)=-J-1\).
Thus \(Q_J\) is integral, has no torsion zeros and \(Q_J(1)=-J-1\).

For either seed \(u\), on the unit circle one has \(|u^*|=|u|>0\).
Rouché at \(z^Ju\pm t u^*\), \(0<t<1\), followed by \(t\uparrow1\), gives at most one exterior zero counting multiplicity for the numerator.
At fixed real points just below and just above the exterior root \(\theta>1\) of \(u\), with both points greater than one, the dominant term \(z^Ju\) changes sign as \(J\) grows.
Consequently there is exactly one exterior root, it is positive and tends to \(\theta\).
Reciprocity or anti-reciprocity gives its inverse as the sole interior zero.
The monic irreducible factor containing the exterior root must contain this inverse too, since its constant has modulus one and no other root can balance their product.
Every remaining monic irreducible factor would then be cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
Removing the one simple torsion zero above thus gives the Salem minimal polynomial, of exact degree \(J+2\) in both cases.
The sole conjugate of maximum modulus is the exterior root \(\alpha\), so every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) fixing \(\alpha^r\) fixes \(\alpha\), for every \(r\ge1\).

For \(P_J\), the exact expression
\[
              P_J(z)=-z+f(z)\frac{z^J-1}{z+1}
\]
has coefficients \(1,-1,0\) at the bottom three indices, their reversals at the top, and alternating signs of modulus one at indices \(3,\ldots,J-1\), by \(f(-1)=-1\).
For \(Q_J\) one has
\[
              Q_J(z)=-z+g(z)(1+z+\cdots+z^{J-1}).
\]
The coefficients at the bottom three indices are \(1,-1,-2\), with their reversals at the top and constant \(-1\) at indices \(3,\ldots,J-1\), by \(g(1)=-1\).

## Units in the integral order and the full fixed groups

For a root \(\alpha\) of \(Q_J\), the displayed geometric sum multiplied by \(g(\alpha)\) is \(\alpha\), a unit in \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Thus each \(\Phi_u(\alpha)\) for \(u\mid J,\ u>1\), is itself a unit in this integral order.
In particular the two ideals \((\alpha^n-1)\) and \((\alpha-1)\) coincide there for every \(n\mid J\).
For a root of \(P_J\), the first displayed expression instead says that \(f(\alpha)(\alpha^J-1)/(\alpha+1)=\alpha\).
Every \(\Phi_u(\alpha)\) for \(u\mid J,\ u\ne2\), including \(u=1\), is a unit in this case.
Hence \(\alpha^n-1\) is a unit for odd \(n\mid J\) and is an associate of \(\alpha+1\) for even \(n\mid J\).

Realize the companion matrix as multiplication by \(\alpha\) on the power basis of \(\mathcal O\).
An association in \(\mathcal O\) gives two matrices differing by a commuting integral automorphism, hence identical kernels on the real torus and also after reduction modulo any positive integer.
The cokernel of multiplication by \(\alpha-1\) for \(Q_J\) is \(\mathcal O/(\alpha-1)\simeq\mathbb Z/(Q_J(1))\).
The cokernel of multiplication by \(\alpha+1\) for \(P_J\) is likewise \(\mathbb Z/(P_J(-1))\).
Both have order \(J+1\), so Smith normal form gives the cyclic torus kernels in the table.
Modulo any integer \(m\ge1\), their corresponding nontrivial-case kernels are cyclic of order \(\gcd(m,J+1)\), and the actions are still identity or negation as displayed.
In particular the nonfixed points of \(F_J(T)\) for \(P_J\) have exact period two because \(J+1\) is odd, while every point of \(F_J(T)\) for \(Q_J\) is fixed.

For an odd prime \(p\mid J\) the norm of the unit \(\Phi_p(\alpha)\) has absolute value one for both inputs.
The reciprocal trace resultant in C899v5 squares to that primitive resultant, and the common integer modulus divides the trace resultant.
This proves \(G_p(P_J)=G_p(Q_J)=1\).

## Excluding every prime in a logarithmic search window at once

Take \(J=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,y)\) for integers \(y\) tending to infinity, so \(12600\mid J\) eventually.
Every \(n\le y\) divides \(d-2\) in these same examples.
In particular all odd primes up to \(y\) have trivial common modulus, and \(Q_J\) admits no torus point of exact period \(2,\ldots,y\).
The size of the window satisfies \(\log(d-2)=\sum_{p^k\le y}\log p\asymp y\) by the [Chebyshev estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units).
Partial summation of the classical prime number theorem \(\pi(y)\sim y/\log y\) gives \(\sum_{p\le y}\log p\sim y\).
Prime powers of exponent at least two add \(O(\sqrt y\log^2 y)\), so \(\log(d-2)\sim y\).
Thus for every fixed \(0<c<1\), orders \(p\le c\log d\) cannot guarantee even one \(G_p>1\) once the degree varies.
These families still have positive limiting logarithmic Mahler measures, and the [fixed-base all-prime asymptotic](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) concerns the subsequent limit in \(p\) for each single polynomial.
