# Integer representatives of small degree for the trace residues of a delay-one inverse

<a id="weights"></a>
## The endpoint identity for actual residues

Suppose \(\sigma_i\to1\) are Salem numbers with monic minimal polynomials \(P_i\) of degrees \(d_i=2m_i\to\infty\).
At each input let \(f\) be a degree-\(d\) monic Pisot minimal polynomial with positive integer constant \(q\ge2\) and \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\).
Suppress the input subscript when possible.
Write \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}=2+u,\ P=z^mS(z+z^{-1})\), and let \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) be its interior traces.
Use the [positive trace rational function](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(\mathcal R=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m} w_\ell/(x-x_\ell)\), where \(x_m=T,\ w_\ell>0\) and \(\sum w_\ell=j\) is the seed branch.
Evaluating at \(z=1,\ x=2\) gives exactly
\[
         1-q=\mathcal R(2)=\sum_{\ell<m}\frac{w_\ell}{2-x_\ell}
                                      -\frac{w_m}{u}.
\]
Here \(zf+f^*\) is nonzero at one.

Let \(b\ge1\) be an integer that may vary with the input and suppose \(b w_\ell=W(x_\ell)\) for an integer polynomial \(W\) of degree \(K\).
Assume
\[
              bj/d=O(1),\qquad K\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Then eventually \(W(2)=0\), \(W\) is nonnegative everywhere on \([-2,2]\), and the number of terms and absolute coefficient sum of \(U(z)=W(z+z^{-1})\) are bounded along the sequence.
Writing \(B=[z^0]U\), one has
\[
           B\in 2\mathbb Z,\qquad B\ge4,\qquad
           \frac{bj}{d}=\frac B2+\frac{W(T)}d+o(1).
\]
Only positivity at the conjugate traces is assumed.

<a id="sampling"></a>
## From positivity at the nodes to bounded Fourier support

Let \(\nu\) be the probability on the \(d-2\) unit-circle conjugates, and let \(m_{\mathbb T}\) denote circle arclength probability.
Put \(\varepsilon_d=\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\).
The [uniform sampling estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#sampling) gives relative error \(O(K\varepsilon_d)\) in sampling \(|U|\), uniformly in its coefficients.
Here \(\int|U|\,d\nu=2(bj-W(T))/(d-2)\) is bounded because all the conjugate values are positive.
Thus \(\int |U|\,dm_{\mathbb T}=O(1)\).
The [integer Fourier support inequality](../audits/literature/JKS24/Yin_U001.md#jks24-u001) bounds this integral below by \(\log(1+\#\operatorname{supp}U)/52\).
Consequently the term count is bounded.
Each coefficient is an integer bounded by the same \(L^1\) norm, so the coefficient absolute sum is bounded as well.
In particular \(|dU(e^{it})/dt|\le C K\) with a constant uniform on the sequence.

We claim that along any subsequence with a negative circle value for every \(U\) there would be a contradiction.
Every point of the circle lies within \(O(\varepsilon_d)\) of a node by the [local arc discrepancy estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#arcs), so \(\min U\ge-O(K\varepsilon_d)=-o(1)\).
Extract a fixed number of terms, fixed integer coefficients, and stabilized relations among the positive exponents \(b_1,\ldots,b_s\).
This last extraction decides, for every \(h\in\mathbb Z^s\), whether \(\sum h_t b_t\) is eventually zero or eventually nonzero.
Let \(\Lambda\) be the group of eventual zero relations, and \(G=\{y\in\mathbb T^s:y^h=1\ (h\in\Lambda)\}\).
The pushforwards of arclength by \(z\mapsto(z^{b_1},\ldots,z^{b_s})\) converge to Haar measure on \(G\), since their integrals on each character converge to \(1_\Lambda(h)\).
This includes the case of no positive exponents by taking the trivial group.
The fixed real Laurent polynomial representing \(U\) must therefore be nonnegative throughout \(G\), by the minimum estimate and full support of Haar measure there.
A finite basis of \(\Lambda\) holds at all sufficiently late inputs, so the entire parametrizing circle lies in \(G\) at those inputs.
Hence \(U\) itself is everywhere nonnegative then, a contradiction.
This proves eventual global positivity, even though the inputs only specified signs at finitely many traces.

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## The value at trace two is forced to vanish

The [explicit degree bound for the Salem height](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) gives \(u\ge(\log\sigma)^2\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^6\).
Thus \(\varepsilon_d=o(u)\).
Choose an angular radius \(\eta>0\) so that \(\varepsilon_d/\eta\to0\) and \(\eta/\min(u,1/(K+1))\to0\).
The geometric mean of \(\varepsilon_d\) and \(\min(u,1/(K+1))\) is one such choice.
If \(W(2)>0\) then \(W(2)\ge1\).
The derivative bound gives \(U(e^{it})\ge1/2\) for \(|t|\le\eta\) at all sufficiently large such inputs.
The angular count gives at least \(c d\eta\) interior traces in this arc after pairing conjugates, and each satisfies \(2-x_\ell\le\eta^2\).
The endpoint identity multiplied by \(b\), together with \(q\ge2\), yields \(W(T)/d\ge c' u/\eta\to\infty\), contrary to \(W(T)\le b j=O(d)\).
Since global positivity excludes \(W(2)<0\), equality \(W(2)=0\) follows eventually.

The [sharp circle-mean bound](Yin_L_G0462_v130.md#mean) now applies and gives the claimed parity and lower bound on \(B\).
The uniform sampling estimate also gives \(2(bj-W(T))/(d-2)=B+o(1)\), since \(\int|U|\) is bounded and \(K\varepsilon_d\to0\).
This proves the branch formula.
In particular \(\liminf b j/d\ge2\), also without a prior boundedness assumption by restricting to any bounded subsequence.
At \(b=1\) the controlled inverse at zero scaled exterior mass [attains two](Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#limit) using \(K=k+2=O(\log d/\log\sigma)\) in its logarithmically bounded choice of target, so it satisfies the degree hypothesis by the same height bound.
At \(b j/d\to2\), eventually \(B=4\) and \(W(T)/d\to0\).
These conclusions apply to every integer representative meeting the degree condition, without restricting its initial size, support or signs between nodes.
