### 1. Whole-order correlations and exact support

Let alpha be any algebraic unit, including a root of unity; O=Z[alpha] is its actual additive order, isomorphic to Z^d.
Use exactly the original difference digits eta=0,+1,-1 of probabilities1/2,1/4,1/4 and X_(j+1)=alpha X_j+eta_j.
Let D be the actual zero-to-zero core, K its original killed kernel, p_n=K^n(0,0), p_0=1, h=lim -(log p_n)/n, R=exp h, and

    U(z)=sum_(n>=0) p_n z^n=1/(1-F(z)),  0<z<R.       (1)

Here F counts all complete original first returns.
It has F(z)<1 on this interval.
Let nu_n be the actual fair-binary sum law on O and let

    mu_n(x)=sum_y nu_n(y)nu_n(y-x).
    A_z(x)=U(z)^(-1) sum_(n>=0) z^n mu_n(x),
    B_z(x)=U(z)^(-1) sum_(n>=0) z^n mu_n(alpha^n x),
    sigma_z(x)=A_z(x)B_z(x).                            (2)

Reversing the independent chronological digits identifies mu_n with the full n-step displacement law.
Its symmetry and Cauchy--Schwarz give 0<=mu_n(x)<=p_n, exactly as in L173.
Thus both series converge absolutely for every x, with values in[0,1], and A_z(0)=B_z(0)=sigma_z(0)=1.
The n=0 terms are delta_0 and are not discarded.

A function f on O is positive definite here when every finite quadratic form sum_(i,j)c_i conjugate(c_j) f(x_i-x_j) is nonnegative.
Each mu_n is positive definite: substitute its autocorrelation and write that form as the squared norm of a finite linear combination of translates of nu_n.
Precomposing with x -> alpha^n x preserves this property.
Positive sums, their pointwise convergent limits and division by the positive number U preserve it.
The product of two such functions also has the property.
Indeed for a fixed finite set their matrices are Gram matrices, and the entrywise product is the Gram matrix of tensor products of the Gram vectors.
A Gram representation exists by diagonalizing the finite positive semidefinite matrix.
This is the elementary finite tensor proof of the usual Schur product fact, not an assertion that an ordinary product of arbitrary positive matrices is positive.
Consequently

    A_z, B_z and sigma_z are positive definite on O.     (3)

Moreover sigma_z is positive precisely on D.
Positivity of A_z(x) means that some original word goes from0 to x.
Positivity of B_z(x), by symmetry of mu_n, means that some original word goes from x to0: its displacement is -alpha^n x.
Both may use different lengths.
Their concatenation places x on a closed original word, and conversely each core state has both paths.
All their weights are strictly positive.
Therefore

    sigma_z(x)>0 iff x belongs to D.                    (4)

This support assertion concerns the product on the WHOLE order.
It does not assume that every forward reachable state is returnable, or that the actual core equals a full geometric strip.

### 2. Exact occupation identity, including the zero correction

For x in D minus{0}, let H_z(x) be the sum of z-weighted original first-hit paths from x to0, and let L_z(x) be the sum of z-weighted paths from0 to x which have no further visit to0 before their endpoint.
L_z counts all possible visits to x before the first return; it is not a first-hit-to-x distribution.
Set H_z(0)=L_z(0)=1.

Every ambient path between two core endpoints stays in the core, since fixed paths from0 and back to0 complete it.
Decomposition at the first hit to0 and at the last visit to0 respectively gives

    sum_(n>=0) z^n K^n(x,0)=H_z(x)U(z),
    sum_(n>=0) z^n K^n(0,x)=U(z)L_z(x).                (5)

The same endpoint probabilities are mu_n(alpha^n x) and mu_n(x), using the digit symmetry in the first case.
Thus B_z=H_z and A_z=L_z on D.
Both functions are at most one; there is no divergence hidden in either path sum.
In particular sigma_z=H_z L_z there.

For a complete first-return word w, write p(w) for its original digit probability and tau=|w|.
The canonical chain Q_z from L201 has complete excursion law

    q_z(w)=p(w)z^tau/F(z),  mu_z=zF'(z)/F(z).          (6)

The mean is finite for z<R.
Its stationary probability pi_z is the expected departure occupation in a complete excursion divided by this mean.
At a nonzero x, a distinguished visit splits a first-return word uniquely into a prefix counted by L_z(x) and a first-hit suffix counted by H_z(x).
This bijection also works when the same x occurs several times: distinguishing its visit counts each one once.
Nonnegative summation therefore gives unnormalized visit weight L_z(x)H_z(x).
At zero, every complete excursion has exactly one departure visit and its unnormalized weight is F(z).
Hence, extending pi_z by zero outside D,

    zF'(z) pi_z(x)=sigma_z(x), x!=0,
    zF'(z) pi_z(0)=F(z),
    sigma_z=zF'(z)pi_z+(1-F(z))delta_0.               (7)

In particular sigma_z is summable and

    sum_x sigma_z(x)=T(z):=zF'(z)+1-F(z)>=1.          (8)

The inequality also follows from 1+sum_(n>=1)(n-1)f_n z^n.
Both F and zF' are positive.
Thus

    omega_z=sigma_z/T(z)
       =[zF'(z)/T(z)]pi_z+[(1-F(z))/T(z)]delta_0      (9)

is a positive-definite probability on O.
This is the exact correction; pi_z itself is not identified with the normalized product in (2).
The normalization uses the complete mean and every original first-return word, not an independent digit model.

### 3. Fourier positivity and all finite additive quotients

Choose the actual power basis of O and identify its additive group with Z^d.
For a summable real symmetric function f, write

    f_hat(theta)=sum_(x in O) f(x) exp(2pi i <x,theta>),
    theta in (R/Z)^d.

A positive-definite summable f has f_hat>=0.
A short direct proof suffices here: apply its finite quadratic-form condition to the box {0,...,N-1}^d and coefficients exp(2pi i <x,theta>), then divide by N^d.
The result is the nonnegative sum of f(v) times the exponential and the overlap factor product_j(1-|v_j|/N)_+.
Absolute summability permits dominated convergence as N tends to infinity; the limit is f_hat or f_hat(-theta), equal for the present real symmetric f.
No measure representation or unproved limit exchange is used.

Apply this to (7)--(9).
For all theta and0<z<R,

    pi_z_hat(theta)>=-(1-F(z))/(zF'(z)),
    omega_z_hat(theta)>=0.                            (10)

The characteristic functions are real because digit sign reversal makes every function in(2), and hence pi_z, even.
Formula(10) is a complete, possibly nonsharp lower bound; it need not assert that pi_z_hat is itself nonnegative.

Let J be any finite-index additive subgroup of O, with N=[O:J], and put bar pi_z(c)=sum_(x in c) pi_z(x) for c in O/J; define bar sigma_z similarly.
Summability makes this a genuine probability marginal.
Its finite Fourier coefficients are the values of the whole-order transform at the characters of O/J, and are nonnegative for bar sigma_z.
Finite character inversion gives

    bar sigma_z(0)-bar sigma_z(c)
      =N^(-1) sum_chi sigma_z_hat(chi)[1-Re chi(c)]>=0.
                                                               (11)

All sums and signs are exact.
Combining with(7) gives, for every nonzero coset c,

    bar pi_z(c)-bar pi_z(0)
                       <=(1-F(z))/(zF'(z)).          (12)

There is also the bound bar omega_z(0)>=1/N: the trivial-character term in Fourier inversion is1/N and all other terms are nonnegative.
This can equivalently be proved by the finite Gram condition and character orthogonality.
Ideals of O are permitted J, but primality, maximal-order replacement and an index-one power basis are not assumed.
The result concerns the true occupation marginal, not a new Markov law obtained by reducing and renormalizing paths.

### 4. Critical limits and an attaining law

On each core state H_z and L_z are increasing nonnegative power series bounded by one.
Their limits at R therefore exist and are finite; set sigma_R(x)=H_R(x)L_R(x) on D and zero outside.
The whole-order positive-definite property passes to the limit in every finite quadratic form, and sigma_R(0)=1.
Monotone convergence on D and(8) show

    sum_x sigma_R(x)=1+R F'(R)-F(R),                 (13)

with infinity allowed.

In the recurrent critical case F(R)=1, this sigma_R is the unnormalized critical excursion occupation measure, normalized to mass one at zero.
The same distinguished-visit argument proves this directly from the probability word law p(w)R^|w|, even if its mean is infinite.
Its total mass is R F'(R).
Thus it is summable exactly in the finite-mean case, and the L201/L232 attainment criterion gives

    pi_*=sigma_R/[R F'(R)],
    pi_*(0)=1/[R F'(R)],
    pi_* is positive definite on the whole O,
    pi_*_hat(theta)>=0 for every theta.              (14)

This statement includes every actually attaining unit, not just finite cores or reciprocal inputs.
It does not assume existence of a critical probability on an infinite core.
The null-recurrent occupation measure remains positive definite but is not normalized to a nonexistent stationary probability.

Every finite additive quotient of an attaining pi_* consequently satisfies

    bar pi_*(0)>=bar pi_*(c) for all c,
    bar pi_*(0)>=1/[O:J].                             (15)

L232 already proves that zero is a largest individual atom, strictly so at positive measure.
The new conclusion (14)--(15) is the full additive positive-definite/Fourier constraint and its simultaneous residue-coset version.
It is stronger than merely comparing individual atoms, and no independence between different quotients is asserted.

For example, every three states0,x,y give the exact determinant condition for the normalized values a=pi_*(x)/pi_*(0), b=pi_*(y)/pi_*(0), c=pi_*(x-y)/pi_*(0):

    1+2abc-a^2-b^2-c^2>=0.                           (16)

If x-y is outside the actual core, c=0 and a^2+b^2<=1.
More generally, if distinct nonzero x_1,...,x_k lie in D while x_i-x_j does not for i!=j, their Gram matrix has block form [[1,v^T],[v,I]], so sum_i pi_*(x_i)^2<=pi_*(0)^2.
This follows by evaluating its quadratic form on(1,-v), and needs no invertibility assumption.
These are compatibility tests for an asserted critical occupation law, not a new uniform bound on R.
In fact the unnormalized sigma_z satisfies the same finite tests throughout z<R; exceeding a finite path-sum test bounds R from above and therefore bounds h from above, not below.
That direction cannot be used as a positive-gap proof.

If F(R)<1 but F'(R)<infinity, (13) is finite and (9) has the corresponding corrected endpoint probability.
It is NOT the uncorrected canonical endpoint law.
If the derivative is infinite, normalization in(9) may escape; no limiting probability is claimed.
When F(z) tends to1, or when zF'(z) tends to infinity, the possible negative Fourier part allowed by(10) tends to zero.
This statement about transforms does not imply tightness or attainment.

### 5. An actual control showing why the critical range matters

Reuse L5's complete golden-ratio core, now in the chronological original convention:

    alpha=phi, u=phi-1,
    D={0,1,u,-1,-u};
    0->0 has weight1/2, 0->+/-1 each1/4;
    1->u has weight1/4;
    u->1 has weight1/2 and u->0 has weight1/4;
    the remaining two rows follow by sign reversal.

These transitions also follow directly from phi*u=1 and phi=1+u.
All five states communicate through0 and no additional core state is supplied.
A nontrivial first-return word has an initial sign choice, then alternating1,u or their negatives, and finally its return to0.
Each extra two-cycle has weight1/8.
Therefore the COMPLETE first-return series is

    F(z)=z/2+z^3/[32(1-z^2/8)],
    R_f=sqrt8.                                      (17)

The critical root R of F(R)=1 lies strictly between1 and2: F(1)=1/2+1/28<1, while F(2)=3/2>1.

Thus this actual positive-measure input attains critically, consistently with L206.
The postcritical canonical laws of L212 are nevertheless defined for all0<z<R_f, by normalizing the complete word law, even when F(z)>1.

At z=2, direct differentiation gives

    F(2)=3/2,  2F'(2)=7/2,  mu_2=7/3,
    pi_2(0)=3/7,  pi_2(+/-1)=pi_2(+/-u)=1/7.         (18)

The four equal nonzero masses follow because a positive-sign excursion visits1 and u equally often, a negative-sign excursion does likewise, and both signs have the same total weight.
Their sum is1-pi_2(0)=4/7.

Thus

    pi_2_hat(chi)=3/7-4/7=-1/7<0.                   (19)

Indeed reducing modulo the additive subgroup on which the two basis coordinates have even sum gives masses3/7 in the zero coset and4/7 in the nonzero coset.

The law in(18) is finite and already Markovian; no numerical approximation or program is a premise.
