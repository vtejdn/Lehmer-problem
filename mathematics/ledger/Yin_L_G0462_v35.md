# The two endpoint resultants of a reciprocal polynomial and the loss in its half-degree encoding

<a id="middle-span"></a>
## An exact-degree estimate using the span of the middle coefficients

Write \(m=\log M\) and \(C_k(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^k-1)|\).
For every monic reciprocal integer polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d=2n\) with nonzero middle part there is a unique \(B\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(s<n\) satisfying
\[
                 P(z)=z^n\{z^n+z^{-n}-B(z+z^{-1})\}.
\]
Indeed the monic polynomials \(T_i\) defined by \(T_i(z+z^{-1})=z^i+z^{-i}\) for \(i\ge1\), together with \(1\), give the symmetric Laurent expansion in integral coefficients.
Suppose \(s\ge1\) and \(P\) has no torsion roots.
Put \(U_\epsilon(z)=z^s[B(z+z^{-1})-2\epsilon]\) for \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Then \(M(U_+U_-)>1\), and, writing \(m_0=m(U_+)+m(U_-)\), one has
\[
     \log C_d(P)=n m_0
                  +O\!\left((1+m_0)s^3\log(2s)\log(2d)\right).      \tag{1}
\]
In particular a fixed nonconstant middle pattern gives exponential growth of the actual integer at the exponent \(d\) as \(n\) grows through admissible values.
If \(P\) varies with at most \(t\) nonzero terms and \(s^3\log(2s)\log(2d)=o(d)\), then \(\log C_d(P)/d\ge c_t>0\) eventually, with unrestricted coefficient heights.
If the leading coefficient of \(B\) has absolute value at least two, then \(m_0\ge2\log2\) gives a positive rate under the same span condition with unrestricted term count.
These calculations compare the exact-degree integer test and the [canonical auxiliary phase test](Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#half-degree) for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
The proof-side contributor derived the endpoint criterion and the two families below on 2026-11-16.

At the \(n\) solutions of \(z^n=\epsilon\) one has \(|P(z)|=|U_\epsilon(z)|\).
The product over both signs is \(C_d(P)\).
For each root \(v\) of \(U_\epsilon\), the [unrestricted-height two-logarithm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v2.md#circle) with the [signed-distance reduction](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v3.md#distances) gives
\[
 \log|v^n-\epsilon|=n\log^+|v|+
          O\!\left((1+m(U_\epsilon))s^2\log(2s)\log(2d)\right).
\]
Indeed each root has degree at most \(2s\) and its primitive minimal polynomial has measure at most \(M(U_\epsilon)\), by factorization over the integers.
Invert \(v\) if necessary and use \(|v^n-\epsilon|\ge |v^{2n}-1|/2\) inside the closed disk for a nontorsion root.
For a root of unity the order is \(O(s^2)\), and \(v^n\ne\epsilon\) by the hypothesis on \(P\).
Reversing the resultant multiplies the product over the \(2s\) roots, with multiplicity, by the \(n\)-th power of the absolute leading coefficient of \(U_\epsilon\).
This gives (1) including the nonmonic case.
For the bounded-term conclusion \(U_\epsilon\) has at most \(t+1\) terms, so the [fixed-support measure gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) applies to whichever endpoint polynomial has positive logarithmic measure.

<a id="endpoint-rigidity"></a>
## Only a signed Chebyshev polynomial can lose both endpoints

Suppose \(M(U_+)=M(U_-)=1\).
Their common leading coefficient is a unit, and replacing \(B\) by \(-B\) if necessary makes it monic.
Kronecker's theorem and reciprocity imply that both \(B-2\) and \(B+2\) have all their roots in \([-2,2]\).
By Rolle's theorem the roots \(\xi_1\le\cdots\le\xi_{s-1}\) of \(B'\), ordered with multiplicity, interlace the roots of each of these two monic polynomials.
Include \(\xi_0=-2\) and \(\xi_s=2\).
For \(0\le i\le s\), both \((-1)^{s-i}(B(\xi_i)-2)\) and \((-1)^{s-i}(B(\xi_i)+2)\) are nonnegative, by the number of real linear factors of each sign.
Thus \((-1)^{s-i} B(\xi_i)\ge2\), which also forces consecutive \(\xi_i\) to be distinct.
Since \(|T_s|\le2\) on \([-2,2]\), the polynomial \(D=B-T_s\) satisfies \((-1)^{s-i}D(\xi_i)\ge0\).
Its degree is less than \(s\), so Lagrange interpolation gives
\[
                   \sum_{i=0}^s\frac{D(\xi_i)}
                                  {\prod_{k\ne i}(\xi_i-\xi_k)}=0.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative, so all \(s+1\) values vanish and \(B=T_s\).
Restoring the sign gives \(B=\epsilon T_s\), in which case \(P=(z^{n-s}-\epsilon)(z^{n+s}-\epsilon)\).
This proves the positive endpoint assertion.
For the omitted degree-zero case \(B=c\), a positive measure requires \(|c|>2\), and \(C_d(P)=|c^2-4|^n\) directly.
If the middle part vanishes, \(P=z^{2n}+1\) has only torsion roots.

<a id="factor-test"></a>
## An obstruction from small-degree factors even at full middle span

Suppose \(P\) is irreducible of degree \(d=2n\), with \(M(P)>1\).
For each sign let \(F_\epsilon\) be any integer polynomial dividing \(U_\epsilon\) modulo \(z^n-\epsilon\), meaning that \(U_\epsilon\equiv F_\epsilon G_\epsilon\bmod (z^n-\epsilon)\) with \(G_\epsilon\in\mathbb Z[z]\).
The choice \(F_\epsilon=1\) is allowed.
For \(u\ge1\) let \(H_u(F)\) be the logarithm of the positive content of \(F\) plus the sum of \(m(A)\) over its primitive irreducible factors \(A\) of degree at most \(u\), with multiplicity.
There is no bound on the degree of \(F_\epsilon\), on \(s<n\), on coefficient heights, or on the number of factors.
With an absolute \(C>0\) set \(\delta=C u^3\log^4(2u)\log(2d)/n\).
For \(\delta<1\) one has
\[
      \frac{\log C_d(P)}d\ \ge\
          \frac{1-\delta}{2}
              \big(H_u(F_+)+H_u(F_-)\big).                            \tag{3}
\]

In fact the same distance calculation for a primitive positive-measure factor \(A\) of degree \(t\le u\) gives
\[
 \frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(A,z^n-\epsilon)|}{n}
     \ge m(A)-\frac{C' t^3\log(2t)\log(2d)}{n}(1+m(A)).
\]
No root of \(F_\epsilon G_\epsilon\) is a sampling node, by the nonzero values of \(P\).
The [Dobrowolski degree estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#transfer) gives \(m(A)\ge c/\log^3(2t)\) when \(A\) is monic up to sign; otherwise \(m(A)\ge\log2\).
Thus the right side is at least \((1-\delta)m(A)\).
At these nodes the product of all the absolute resultants, including \(G_\epsilon\), is the original endpoint norm and every factor is a positive integer.
Dropping the other factors costs nothing, and the content contributes exactly \(n\) times its logarithm, proving (3).

For any sequence of positive irreducible reciprocal inputs with \(M(P)\to1\), the [original-unit degree-window estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0391_v1.md#bounded-power-degree-escape) gives \(\log C_d(P)/d\to0\).
Consequently for fixed \(0<\eta<1/3\) the sum in (3) tends to zero uniformly over both chosen modular divisors for \(u=\lfloor d^{1/3-\eta}\rfloor\).
In particular their contents must both be one eventually, and the degree range eventually excludes primitive factors with absolute leading coefficient greater than one and nonreciprocal irreducible factors of positive logarithmic measure.
For the latter exclusion, a primitive monic nonreciprocal factor with nonzero constant obeys the [nonreciprocal measure bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-reciprocal-number), and the factor \(z\) has measure one.
Equation (3) still allows high-degree factors and small-measure reciprocal factors.

<a id="five-terms"></a>
## Both half-degree choices overestimate the original rate for a five-term family

Fix an odd integer \(a\ge9\), set \(b=2\), take \(n=3^k\ge9\), and put
\[
 P_n(z)=z^{2n}-az^n-b(z^{n+1}+z^{n-1})+1,\qquad d=2n.
\]
This primitive support has differences generating \(\mathbb Z\).
The polynomial is irreducible, every power of its dominant positive root has degree \(d\), and its coefficient sum bounds its measure uniformly.
For each fixed \(j\ge1\) and each coprime choice \(e=\pm1\) in the [canonical half-degree encoding](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v1.md#universal-half-degree-delay-encoding), put \(V_j=f_e^j-(-z^n f_e^*)^j\).
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
    \lim_{k\to\infty}m(V_j)&=j\log\kappa_e,\qquad
       \kappa_e=\frac{a+b+e+\sqrt{(a+b+e)^2+4eb}}2>a,\\
    \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log C_{jd}(P_n)}d
       &=\frac12\sum_{w^{2j}=1}\int_{|z|=1}
                     \log\big(a+b(z+z^{-1})-w-w^{-1}\big)\,d\mu(z)
         <j\log a ,
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{2}
\]
where \(\mu\) is normalized arclength.
In particular an \(o(d)\) error between \(d\,m(V_j)\) and \(\log C_{jd}\) fails at every fixed multiplier, including the seventh.
The exact-degree estimate (1) applies to this family with \(s=1\).

Modulo two, \(P_n=z^{2n}+z^n+1=\Phi_{3^{k+1}}\).
The order of \(2\bmod 3^{k+1}\) is \(2\cdot3^k\), since \(v_3(4^{3^h}-1)=h+1\) by induction using the binomial theorem and \(2\bmod3\) has order two.
The Frobenius orbit of a primitive root thus has size \(2n\), proving irreducibility over \(\mathbb F_2\) and over \(\mathbb Q\).
For \(z=e^{t+i\phi}\), \(t>0\), the root equation \(z^n+z^{-n}=a+b(z+z^{-1})\) gives
\[
 \frac{(a+2b\cosh(t)\cos\phi)^2}{4\cosh^2(nt)}
       +\frac{b^2\sinh^2(t)(1-\cos^2\phi)}{\sinh^2(nt)}=1.
\]
The left side is strictly convex in \(\cos\phi\) because \(\tanh(nt)>\tanh(t)\), and its unique maximum on \([-1,1]\) is at \(1\) because \(a,b>0\).
The positive ratio \((a+2b\cosh t)/(2\cosh nt)\) decreases strictly from more than one to zero, since both \(1/\cosh(nt)\) and \(\cosh(t)/\cosh(nt)\) decrease strictly.
It equals one at a unique \(t_0>0\).
Thus \(e^{t_0}\) is the unique root of maximum modulus.
No other conjugate can have the same positive integral power as \(e^{t_0}\), so every such power generates its field by separability.

At \(z^n=w\) with \(w^{2j}=1\), the absolute value of \(P_n(z)\) is the positive expression \(a+b(z+z^{-1})-w-w^{-1}\).
For every \(w\) the \(n\)-point averages of its logarithm converge by continuity, giving the second limit in (2).
Its strict inequality follows from strict concavity of the logarithm and the joint mean \(a\) over \(w,z\).

Write \(y=z^n\), \(c=a+b+e\), and \(g=z^{n+1}f_e(1/z)\).
The canonical formula gives \(f_e=(1+z)y+e-cz-bz^2\).
Each choice is coprime to its reversal since \(f_e(-1)=a+2e\ne0\) and the only possible common factor is \(z+1\).
Up to sign and a Laurent monomial, \(V_j\) is the specialization at \(y=z^n\) of the product over \(w^j=1\) of
\[
 H_w(z,y)=w(e-cz-bz^2)+(1+w)(1+z)y+L_e(z)y^2,\qquad
 L_e(z)=ez-c-bz^{-1}.
\]
On \(|z|=1\) the constant coefficient of \(H_w\) is \(wz\,\overline{L_e(z)}\).
After dividing by a square root of \(wz\), the endpoint coefficients are conjugates and the middle coefficient is real of absolute value at most four.
Since \(|L_e|\ge c-b-1\ge a-2>2\), the quadratic in \(y\) has both roots on the unit circle by solving its real cosine equation.
Integration in \(y\) thus gives \(\log|L_e(z)|\).
The roots of \(z L_e\) are the roots of \(e z^2-c z-b\), one of modulus \(\kappa_e\) and the other of modulus \(b/\kappa_e<1\).
For \(e=1\), \(\kappa_e>c>a\).
For \(e=-1\) the moduli are the roots of \(X^2-cX+b\), and \(a^2-ca+b=b-a(b-1)<0\) puts \(a\) strictly between them.
Jensen's formula now gives \(\int\log|L_e|\,d\mu=\log\kappa_e\).
Applying [Lawton's specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) to the fixed product, clearing any monomial powers, gives its two-variable measure as the limit under \((z,y)=(z,z^n)\), since the smallest relation norm is \(n\).
This proves the first limit in (2).

## Unit resultants at both the original degree and twice that degree

For a family with exact vanishing logarithms, let
\[
 Q(y)=y^{38}-y^{34}-y^{29}+y^{25}-y^{22}+y^{20}-y^{19}
           +y^{18}-y^{16}+y^{13}-y^9-y^4+1.
\]
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), the polynomial \(P(z)=Q(z^p)\) is irreducible of degree \(d=38p\), with \(C_d(P)=C_{2d}(P)=1\).
For the coprime canonical choice \(e=1\), the auxiliary logarithms satisfy \(\liminf_{p\to\infty}m(V_j)\ge j\log2\) at every fixed \(j\ge1\).
Here taking a \(p\)-th power drops the field degree, while the five-term family in (2) preserves it.
The [Salem divisor construction](Yin_L_G0462_v28.md#degree-scale) instead supplies a growing list of exact unit norms at exponents dividing \(d-2\).

The trace polynomial of \(Q\) is \(J=T_{19}-T_{15}-T_{10}+T_6-T_3+T_1-1\), with \(Q(y)=y^{19}J(y+y^{-1})\).
Modulo three, \(X^{3^{19}}-X\equiv0\bmod J\), whereas \(J(0),J(1),J(-1)\) are \(-1,8,-4\).
As \(19\) is prime, these checks prove irreducibility of \(J\bmod3\).
Its real roots include one in \((0,1)\) and one in \((2,3)\), since \(J(2)=-1,\ J(3)=85528484\).
For a root \(u\) in the first interval the element \(u^2-4\) is negative in this real embedding, so it is not a square in \(\mathbb Q(u)\).
Thus \(Q\) is irreducible of degree \(38\), and the second interval gives a conjugate outside the unit circle.
In increasing order \(s=1,2,4,19,38,76\), the integer resultants \(\operatorname{Res}(Q,\Phi_s)\) are respectively \(-1,1,1,1,1,1\).
The following exact computations reproduce the Frobenius remainder and the six integers.

~~~python
from sympy import Poly, cyclotomic_poly, symbols

x = symbols("x")
T = [2, x]
for i in range(2, 20):
    T.append((x*T[-1] - T[-2]).expand())
J = Poly(T[19] - T[15] - T[10] + T[6] - T[3] + T[1] - 1, x, modulus=3)
X = Poly(x, x, modulus=3)
r = X
for i in range(19):
    r = (r**3).rem(J)
assert r == X
Q = Poly(x**38 - x**34 - x**29 + x**25 - x**22 + x**20 - x**19
         + x**18 - x**16 + x**13 - x**9 - x**4 + 1, x)
assert [Q.resultant(Poly(cyclotomic_poly(s, x), x))
        for s in (1, 2, 4, 19, 38, 76)] == [-1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1]
~~~

For a root \(\alpha\) of \(Q\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
If \(X^p-\alpha\) were reducible over \(K\), a root \(\gamma\) of a proper irreducible factor of degree \(h<p\) would have \(N_{K(\gamma)/K}(\gamma)^p=\alpha^h\).
As \(p\) and \(h\) are coprime this would make \(\alpha\) a \(p\)-th power of a unit in \(K\).
The possible units have degree at most 38 and absolute logarithmic height \(h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)/p\).
The elementary monic coefficient bounds give only finitely many such units even with the upper bound \(h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)\), and a nontorsion unit cannot have two different powers equal to \(\alpha\).
This proves the asserted irreducibility for large \(p\).
Each root of \(Q\) then has \(p\) preimages among the roots of \(P\), so \(C_{jd}(P)=C_{38j}(Q)^p\), giving the two unit values.

For the last auxiliary comparison write \(h=19,\ q_i=[y^i]Q,\ y=z^p\) and \(U(y)=\sum_{i=1}^h q_{h-i}y^i\).
The half-degree polynomial is \(f_e=(1+z)U(y)+e+(q_h-e)z\), so \(f_1(-1)=2-q_h=3\).
In the phase \(w f_e+z^{hp}f_e^*\), the constant and linear coefficients in \(z\), treating \(y\) independently, are
\[
 \begin{split}
 A_w(y)&=w(U(y)+e)+y^{2h}(U(y^{-1})+q_h-e),\\
 B_w(y)&=w(U(y)+q_h-e)+y^{2h}(U(y^{-1})+e).
 \end{split}
\]
On the unit circle \(A_w=w y^{2h}\overline{B_w}\), so the Mahler measure in the \(z\) variable is \(|B_w(y)|\) almost everywhere.
At \(e=1\), \(|B_w(0)|=|q_h-1|=2\), giving \(m(B_w)\ge\log2\).
The same specialization theorem applied to the product over \(w^j=1\) proves the auxiliary bound.
These examples establish the need to control the original sampled values in the general half-degree encoding, and (1) provides that control when the actual middle span is sufficiently small.
