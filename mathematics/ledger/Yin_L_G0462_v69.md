# Testing one linear order after removing arbitrarily few degree-dependent exceptions

<a id="single"></a>
## A single primitive or full return in the original degree window

Let \(s(d)\) be any positive function tending to infinity with \(s(d)=o(d)\), with arbitrarily slow divergence allowed.
For \(d\ge2\) let \(p_d\) be its largest prime divisor.
Use the integer orders in \([d,2d]\), deleting the multiples of \(p_d\) only when \(d/p_d\le s(d)\).
At most \(\lfloor s(d)\rfloor+1\) orders are deleted and the choice depends on the degree alone.
Write \(X_j(P)=\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|/\varphi(j)\) and \(Z_j(P)=\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^j-1)|/j\).
For either statistic \(W=X,Z\), the following are equivalent.

1. The positive logarithmic Mahler measures of nontorsion algebraic units have an absolute positive lower bound.
2. There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that for the minimal polynomial \(P\) of every sufficiently large-degree nontorsion algebraic unit, at least one retained composite order has \(W_j(P)\ge c\).

Indeed the [linear-window lower estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#scale) gives a fixed positive proportion of composite orders with \(\log D_j\gg d\,m(P)\).
Here \(D_j=|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|\), the full resultant dominates \(D_j\), and both normalizing denominators are at most \(2d\).
Deleting \(o(d)\) orders leaves witnesses under the first condition.
Conversely fix the monic minimal polynomial \(Q\) of any nontorsion unit of degree \(D\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), the [irreducible power substitutions](Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) \(Q(z^p)\) have degree \(d=Dp\), largest prime divisor \(p\), and measure \(m(Q)\).
Eventually \(s(Dp)\ge D\), so only \(j\) coprime to \(p\) remain.
At every such order \(W_j(Q(z^p))=W_j(Q)\) exactly by the permutation of the primitive or full root set.
For fixed \(Q\), the [fixed-polynomial resultant estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) gives \(W_j(Q)\to m(Q)\) as \(j\to\infty\).
Taking the single witnesses along these substitutions proves \(m(Q)\ge c\).
Allowing prime orders as additional retained choices gives the same equivalence.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## An arbitrarily sparse set of test degrees

It is enough for the deletion rule and the lower-bound test above to be imposed only in a degree set chosen in advance of the input polynomials, which may be arbitrarily sparse in the following sense.
Choose positive integers \(D_n\) in which every integer occurs infinitely often, for instance \(D_n=1+v_2(n)\).
Choose any increasing sequence of size thresholds \(H_n\).
Recursively choose primes \(p_n>D_n\) sufficiently large that \(d_n=D_n p_n>\max(H_n,2d_{n-1})\), with \(d_0=0\), and \(s(d_n)\ge D_n\).
At \(d_n\) exclude the multiples of \(p_n\) and ask for one retained order as above just for the nontorsion units of that degree.
The forward argument works at these degrees.
For each fixed \(Q\), the subsequence with \(D_n=\deg Q\) still contains infinitely many irreducible substitutions at unbounded primes, proving the converse.

The deletion has a specific arithmetic purpose.
At a discarded order \(j=kp\) in the power-substitution family the sampled value is the original value \(W_k(Q)\), with \(D\le k\le2D\), rather than a sample with order growing to infinity.
The argument gives no converse for a single witness free to choose those exceptional orders.
The existence of the absolute lower bound for the retained witnesses remains open.
