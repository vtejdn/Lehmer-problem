# Integral harmonic densities and real trace prefixes with roots on the circle

<a id="harmonic"></a>
## A nonnegative density with an integral outer coefficient

Let \(S\in\mathbb R[w]\) have \(S(0)=1\), no root in the open unit disk and at least one nonintegral coefficient.
Let \(\mu\) be a circle probability whose Herglotz series \(J=1+2\sum_{r>0}\widehat\mu(r)w^r\) satisfies \(SJ\in\mathbb Z[[w]]\), where \(\widehat\mu(r)=\int w^{-r}\,d\mu(w)\).
If \(f\ge0\) is integrable, \(f\,dm\le\mu\) for normalized circle Haar measure \(m\), and \(H=2Sf\) is an integer Laurent polynomial almost everywhere, then \(f=0\) almost everywhere.

Suppose \(f\ne0\).
The rational function \(H/(2S)\) is real almost everywhere on the circle and so equals its conjugate reciprocal as a rational function.
Any pole at a nonzero point outside the circle would force a pole in the open punctured disk, where \(S\) has no zero.
A genuine circle pole contradicts absolute integrability.
Thus \(f\) is a real-valued Laurent polynomial, and its continuous representative is nonnegative.
If it is a positive constant, \(2f=H(0)\) is an integer in \((0,2]\).
The value one gives \(H=S\), contradicting nonintegrality, and the value two exhausts \(\mu\) as Haar so that \(J=1\), again impossible.

Otherwise put \(L=2f\) and let \(k>0\) be its largest frequency.
Its lowest frequency is \(-k\) by reality.
Write \(s_n\) for the leading coefficient of \(S\).
The root product gives \(0<|s_n|\le1\).
Since \(H=SL\) has nonzero integer coefficient \(s_n L_k\) at its largest exponent, \(|L_k|\ge1\), and \(L_k\) is real.
Averaging \(L\) over the \(k\)-th roots of unity retains only \(L_0+2\Re(L_k w^k)\).
Nonnegativity gives \(L_0\ge2|L_k|\), while \(f\,dm\le\mu\) gives \(L_0\le2\).
It follows that \(L_0=2,\ L_k=\epsilon=\pm1\), and \(f\,dm=\mu\).
At every root of \(w^k=-\epsilon\) the average vanishes, so \(L\) itself vanishes there by nonnegativity.
Each zero is at least double, because the real angular derivative of a nonnegative differentiable function vanishes at its zeros.
These \(k\) double zeros account for the full degree of \(w^k L\).
Comparing leading coefficients gives \(L=2+\epsilon(w^k+w^{-k})\).
Consequently \(J=1+\epsilon w^k\) has an integral formal inverse, and \(S=(SJ)/J\) would be integral.
This proves the assertion.

## Mixed harmonics for an arbitrary stable one-variable prefix

Suppose \(S_i\to S\) in bounded degree, with constant coefficients one and the center \(S\) as above.
On an escaping sequence of actual positive-constant Pisot inputs suppose \(\|\Pi/j\|_\infty\) stays bounded and an initial interval of length \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\), \(\eta>0\) fixed, of the normalized lift agrees with \(G_i(z)S_i(z^{g_i})\) for \(1\le g_i\le h,\ G_i\in 1+z\mathbb Z[[z]]\).
Take any limiting probability \(\mu\) in the coordinate \(w=\bar v^{g_i}\) of the [signed inverse measures](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality).
The [real fractional coefficient argument](Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#real-seam) gives \(g_i=o(h)\) by the first nondivisible index and bounded absolute sums through \(Cg_i\), for each \(C\), for the integral series \(X_i=B_i^\sharp/G_i\).
This argument uses the first nonintegral coefficient of \(S\) and convergence in bounded degree.
In particular it does not require strict exteriority of the roots.
Its base tests give integral \(SJ\) for the Herglotz series of \(\mu\).

For \(g_i/t_i,t_i/h\to0\), take any further joint limit \(\Omega\) with the extra coordinate \(u=v^{t_i}\).
The [finite-range transform and separated slopes](Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#slopes) give an integer Laurent polynomial \(H=2Sf\) for every grouped graph with nonnegative projected density \(f\,dm\le\mu\).
Indeed the zero layer of \(2|S|^2\Omega\) is \(S^*(SJ)+S(SJ)^*\), with finite coefficient range, and its other layers use the bounded integer transform of the projection of \(2S u^k\Omega\) for \(k>0\) and reflection.
Finite interpolation followed by the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) decomposes the weighted measure into finitely many torsion translates with trigonometric polynomial densities.
Division by \(2|S|^2\) away from its zeros recovers the positive components there.
The omitted base points are Haar-null and carry zero in every row weighted by \(S\).
On a component \(u^b=\zeta w^a\), with \(b>0\), a multiple \(k\) of \(b\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\) gives \(u^k=w^{ka/b}\) identically, for slopes of either sign or zero.
Thus every sheet contributes its actual projected mass density, and coincident slopes add with their original nonnegative weights.
On high common multiples the full-torus component contributes zero, since its trigonometric polynomial density before division has finite degree in \(u\).
The full-dimensional density of the \(k\)-th weighted row is then \(\sum_\sigma w^{k\sigma}2S f_\sigma\), with \(f_\sigma\,dm\le\mu\) the sum of all projected measures at slope \(\sigma\).
Its Laurent coefficients are integers by the integer transform of the row.
Shifting to one slope and using Riemann–Lebesgue decay for every other \(2Sf_{\sigma'}\) gives integrality of each coefficient of \(2Sf_\sigma\), and decay of its own coefficients makes it Laurent polynomial.
The [outer coefficient argument](#harmonic) forces every such \(f\) to vanish.
At a last nonzero absolutely continuous harmonic the [conditional positivity bound](Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#return) gives \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(S)M(f_0)<1\), where \(f_0\) is the density of the absolutely continuous part of \(\mu\).
Here \(M(S)=1\) by its constant coefficient and absence of interior roots, and \(M(f_0)<1\) since \(SJ\) integral excludes a Haar marginal.
All positive rows are therefore carried by the finite torsion atom set from the weighted base marginal.
A binomial \(1-z^{Tg_i}\) annihilating this set gives, after extraction, exact vanishing of its product with \(X_i\) on \(Lg_i\le l\le h/L\) for some fixed \(L\), by the same moving-index and integrality argument.

## When every root of the center has modulus one

Under the same hypotheses, suppose now that all roots of \(S\) have modulus one.
Then \(\mu\) itself is supported on the circle zeros of \(S\) and finitely many roots of unity.
To see this, the zero-layer decomposition just used gives \(2|S|^2\mu\) as a trigonometric polynomial density plus finitely many torsion masses.
Away from the zeros, division shows that the absolutely continuous density \(f_0\) of \(\mu\) is a Laurent rational function with possible poles in the punctured plane only at the roots of \(S\), since all roots of \(S^*\) are there too.
All such poles are removable by integrability on the circle, leaving \(f_0\) Laurent polynomial.
The remaining measure is a sum of atoms at the claimed finite set.

Write \(n=\deg S\) and use \(S(w)=\epsilon w^n S(1/w)\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\), from real coefficients, constant one and the unit-circle roots.
The Herglotz transform of the density \(f_0\) is polynomial, and each atom contributes a rational term with a simple pole at its support point.
Hence \(V=SJ\) is rational with only simple finite poles at torsion points, since \(S\) cancels the poles at its own zeros.
Take \(T\ge1\) divisible by the orders of the torsion atoms.
Then \(V=Q(w)/(1-w^T)\) for an integer polynomial \(Q\), using the integrality of the Taylor coefficients of \(V\).
For almost every point on the circle, \(J+J^*=2f_0\), since each atomic Herglotz kernel has zero real part there and the polynomial part recovers the density.
Consequently
\[
                  2S f_0=V+\epsilon w^n V^*
\]
is a Laurent polynomial and also a quotient of an integer Laurent polynomial by \(1-w^T\).
Monic division after clearing Laurent units makes it integral Laurent.
The [density lemma](#harmonic) with \(f=f_0\) proves \(f_0=0\).

For example, when \(S=1+a w+w^2,\ -2<a<2,\ a\notin\mathbb Z\), the equal probability masses on its two roots do satisfy the marginal integrality condition, with \(J=(1-w^2)/S\).
The contradiction for actual full lifts uses the denominator and both ends of its support.

## A uniform neighborhood gap for actual full lower polynomials

Fix an integer \(K\ge\deg S\) and a nonintegral real polynomial \(S\) with constant one and all roots on the unit circle.
There are \(\varepsilon,c>0\) such that the following holds on every positive-constant Pisot branch, in either degree parity.
Suppose the actual normalized lift has the form
\[
     \Pi/j=S_i(z^g)-z^N S_i(z^{-g}),\qquad N=2h+2,\qquad
     1\le g\le h,\qquad S_i(0)=1,\qquad \deg S_i\le K ,
\]
with \(rg\le h\) for each nonconstant exponent \(r\) present in \(S_i\).
If the maximum coefficient distance from \(S_i\) to \(S\), after padding through \(K\), is less than \(\varepsilon\), then \(\theta-q\ge c\).
In particular this gives a whole coefficient neighborhood at every quadratic center \(1+a w+w^2\) specified above, with arbitrary real \(a\notin\mathbb Z\) in the open trace interval.
The perturbations need not keep either quadratic reciprocity or the exact degree of the center.

Otherwise extract such actual inputs with \(S_i\to S\) and \(\theta-q\to0\).
The bounded degree and coefficients bound the circle supremum, and the actual prefix through \(h\) has \(G_i=1\), so the preceding results apply.
Choose \(T\) annihilating the torsion atoms of a fixed limiting marginal and put \(W_i=1-z^{Tg}\).
Its full middle coefficients satisfy, for \((K+T)g<l<N-Kg\),
\[
  [z^l]W_iB_i^\sharp
       =2\int v^l (1-v^{-Tg}) S_i(v^{-g})\,d\omega_i(v).
\]
Indeed every lower term and its translate is active, every reflected term is absent, and all Taylor indices of the actual Herglotz series are strictly positive.
The absolute values tend to zero uniformly over this entire interval, since the positive parts pushed forward in \(w=\bar v^g\) converge to \(\mu\), the negative masses tend to zero, and the limiting continuous modulus \(|(1-w^T)S(w)|\) vanishes on the support of \(\mu\) by the [atomic conclusion](#circle).
Integrality gives exact simultaneous vanishing in the full middle.
The absolute coefficient sum of \(B_i^\sharp\), hence also of its difference \(W_iB_i^\sharp\), through \((K+T)g\) is bounded by the [short interval estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#real-seam).
Since \(B_i^\sharp\) is reciprocal of degree \(N\), its binomial multiple is anti-reciprocal of degree \(N+Tg\).
Reflection carries the entire remaining interval \(l\ge N-Kg\) into the bounded initial range.
Thus this integer polynomial has bounded support, while its Mahler measure \(M(B_i^\sharp)>1\) tends to one by the actual inverse estimates.
The [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) gives the contradiction.
The same conclusion holds uniformly on a neighborhood of any compact family of the stated centers for fixed \(K\), by a finite cover.
