# The first possible sparse numerator has two lower terms at sublinear indices

<a id="four"></a>
## Four terms do not suffice on the second branch

Let \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\) be the trace lift for a positive-constant Pisot input on branch \(j=2\).
There is an absolute bound for the constant \(q\) whenever \(\Pi\) has at most five nonzero terms.
More generally, if \(q\to\infty\) and the number of terms is bounded, then at least two distinct nonconstant terms have indices \(o(h)\), in the sense that the second smallest strictly positive support index divided by \(h\) tends to zero.

Indeed put \(N=2h+2\), so \(\Pi\) is anti-reciprocal of degree \(N\), its constant coefficient is two and its midpoint coefficient is zero.
It has even support size, and all its roots are on the unit circle.
Each of its coefficients is uniformly bounded by the [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound), since \(M(\Pi)=2\).
The [first odd index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#statement) is \(o(h)\).
If the second strictly positive support index were at least \(\eta h\) on a subsequence, the first term beyond the constant would therefore be \(a z^d\) at \(d=o(h)\), with fixed odd \(a\) after extraction.
The choices \(a=\pm1\) contradict the [arbitrary-offset bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#statement) using a shorter positive fractional prefix.
For \(|a|\ge3\), take \(r^d=9/10\).
On the circle of radius \(r\), \(|2+a z^d|\ge 7/10\), whereas the sum of all the remaining terms is uniformly \(O(r^{\eta h})=o(1)\).
Rouché's theorem puts \(d\) zeros of \(\Pi\) inside that circle since \(2/|a|<9/10\), a contradiction.
When the lift has at most four terms its support has at most one index strictly between zero and \(h+1\), giving the asserted bound.

<a id="six"></a>
## Six terms with or without a bounded frequency relation

For an escaping sequence with six terms the lift has the exact form
\[
       \Pi(z)=A(z)-z^N A(z^{-1}),\qquad A(z)=2+a z^d+b z^e,\quad
                   0<d<e<h+1 .
\]
Here \(e=o(h)\), both coefficients are nonzero and at least one is odd.
After extraction take \(a,b\) constant, write \(g=\gcd(d,e)\), \(m=d/g,\ n=e/g\).
On any subsequence with \(n\to\infty\) one necessarily has
\[
                             |a|=|b|=1.
\]
If the primitive pair \((m,n)\) stays fixed instead, then \(P(x)=2+a x^m+b x^n\) has no roots in the open unit disk, and \(|b|=1,\ |a|\le3\).

For the first assertion apply [Lawton's specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) to
\[
 F(x,y,t)=2+a x+b y-t(2+a x^{-1}+b y^{-1})
\]
with exponent vector \((d,e,N)\), after clearing Laurent monomials.
There are no bounded nonzero relations along the subsequence.
Indeed \(e=o(N)\) forces a bounded relation to have third coefficient zero eventually, and a nonzero relation between \(d,e\) has maximum absolute coefficient at least \(n\).
Since \(M(\Pi)=2\), the specialization limit yields \(M(F)=2\).
On the torus the two coefficients in \(t\) are conjugates, so integrating its logarithm in \(t\) gives \(M(2+a x+b y)=2\).
Integration in \(y\) gives the circle mean of \(\log\max(|2+a x|,|b|)\).
It is at least \(\log\max(2,|a|)\).
Equality at \(\log 2\) requires \(|a|\le2\) and \(|b|\le\min_{|x|=1}|2+a x|=2-|a|\), since any strict inequality in the opposite direction holds on an open arc.
Nonzero integral coefficients therefore have modulus one.

For fixed \(m,n\) use the same argument with Laurent polynomial \(P(x)-tP(x^{-1})\) and vector \((g,N)\).
Because \(g=o(N)\), its shortest relation tends to infinity even if \(g\) stays bounded.
Thus \(M(P)=2\), its constant coefficient, so no zero is in the open unit disk.
The highest coefficient satisfies \(|b|\le2\).
If \(|a|>2+|b|\), the middle term strictly dominates the others on the circle and forces \(m>0\) interior zeros.
It remains to exclude \(|b|=2\).
In this case equality with the leading coefficient forces every zero of \(P\) onto the circle.
Real reciprocity then gives \(P(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(b)x^n P(1/x)\), and its middle term forces \(b=2\) and \(n=2m\).
Coprimality gives \(m=1,\ n=2\), and the quadratic unit-circle roots give \(|a|\le4\).
Then exactly
\[
       \Pi(z)=(2+a z^g+2z^{2g})(1-z^{N-2g}),\quad
       C(x)=(2X_g(x)+a)U_{h-g}(x/2),
       \qquad X_g(2\cos t)=2\cos(gt).
\]
At least one coefficient of the lift is odd, so \(a\) is odd.
The second factor of \(C\) is monic, and the first is an integral multiple of the entire primitive nonmonic part of \(C\), by unique factorization and its leading coefficient two.
It takes the nonzero value \(4+a\) at 2 and has interval supremum at most eight.
This contradicts the [trace multiplier bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) since \(q\to\infty\) on the fixed branch gives \(\theta-q\to0\).
Thus \(|b|=1\), and the middle-term bound gives \(|a|\le3\).
These conditions constrain the six-term case without asserting that any of its remaining trinomials admits the required integer denominator.
