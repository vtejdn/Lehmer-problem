# Genuine neutral mass and a half-integral inverse at square constants

<a id="genuine"></a>
## Mass carried by the neutral conjugates of the Salem number

Consider actual Pisot inputs of even degree \(d\), constant \(q=d^2\), root \(\theta\), and excess \(\delta=\theta-q\to0\).
The full reciprocal return has an exterior root \(\sigma\to1\), eventually Salem, with trace \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}=2+u\).
There are \(h=d/2-1\) neutral traces, of which \(h_0\) belong to the Salem minimal polynomial and \(k=h-h_0\) belong to cyclotomic factors.
Take \(v_i=b_i\) for the inverse residues or \(v_i=c_i\) for the branch residues in [the full-return identities](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches).
Write \(S=\sum_i v_i\), \(M=\sum_{\rm Salem}v_i\), \(p=h_0/h\) and \(G=h(\prod_i v_i)^{1/h}\).
The determinant estimate gives \(\liminf G\ge1/2\) for either choice of residues. [Full neutral product](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#limits)

Applying the arithmetic-geometric mean separately to the Salem and cyclotomic nodes gives
\[
    G\le (M/p)^p\left((S-M)/(1-p)\right)^{1-p},\qquad
    M\ge p G^{1/p}\left((1-p)/S\right)^{(1-p)/p}.                   \tag{1}
\]
When \(p=1\) this means \(M=S\ge G\).
The same inequalities apply to any subset of the nodes, even if membership depends on the entire return.
In particular if \(S\) stays bounded and \(\liminf p>0\), then \(\liminf M>0\).

The bound \(S\le\sum_i b_i<4q+2T=O(d^2)\) holds without a bounded-mass hypothesis.
Thus whenever \(\limsup k\log d/d\le C<\infty\), we have
\[
                             \liminf M\ge \tfrac12 e^{-4C}.           \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(h=d/2-1\) implies \(1-p\to0\) and \(\limsup(1-p)\log S\le4C\), while \((1-p)\log(1-p)\to0\).
This proves (2) from (1), also along return subsequences with no cyclotomic factors.
In particular \(k=o(d/\log d)\) forces both genuine masses to have liminf at least \(1/2\).
Either genuine mass tending to zero requires \(k\log d/d\to\infty\).

If \(S\to1/2\), all individual weights approach the uniform weights in the aggregate sense
\[
                            \sum_i |v_i-S/h|\longrightarrow0 .       \tag{3}
\]
For any subset containing fraction \(p'\) of the nodes and fraction \(s'\) of the mass, the first inequality in (1) gives
\[
    p'\log(p'/s')+(1-p')\log((1-p')/(1-s'))\le\log(S/G).
\]
The left side is at least \(2(p'-s')^2\), by integrating its derivative \((s'-p')/[s'(1-s')]\) from \(p'\) to \(s'\).
The limiting endpoint cases follow by continuity when finite.
Use the subset \(v_i\ge S/h\), on which \(2S(s'-p')\) equals the left side of (3).
Since \(G\le S\) and \(\liminf G\ge1/2\), the right side of the preceding logarithmic inequality tends to zero.
This proves (3), including the consequence \(M-p/2\to0\).

## Branch mass one half on a Chebyshev grid

Fix a Salem number \(\sigma\) with monic trace minimal polynomial \(Q_0(x)=(x-T)H_0(x)\) of degree \(h_0+1\).
Assume
\[
                              x^2\mid\overline Q_0(x)
                              \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[x].        \tag{4}
\]
There are actual Pisot minimal polynomials with square constant \(q=d^2\), degrees \(d\to\infty\), and full return equal to the Salem minimal polynomial times \(z^{2k}+1\), where \(d=2h_0+2+2k\).
Their total genuine inverse and branch masses tend to zero.
Their total neutral branch mass tends to \(1/2\) and the branch measures tend weakly to \(\rho/2\), with \(d\rho(x)=dx/(\pi\sqrt{4-x^2})\).
They satisfy \(\theta-q=u+O_T(1/k)\).
For a sequence of Salem numbers satisfying (4) and tending to one, the choices can therefore yield actual \(\delta\to0\) and branch mass tending to \(1/2\).

Take \(k\) divisible by four and put \(B=C_k\), where \(C_k(2\cos t)=2\cos(kt)\).
Write \(V_j(2\cos t)=\sin((j+1)t)/\sin t\) and define
\[
             E=\frac{V_{k-1}-(k/2)B}{2-x},\qquad D=E/2 .
\]
The numerator vanishes at \(x=2\), since \(V_{k-1}(2)=k\) and \(B(2)=2\).
Thus \(E\in\mathbb Z[x]\) has degree \(k-1\) and leading coefficient \(k/2\).
At a root \(y\) of \(B\), differentiation gives \(B'(y)=kV_{k-1}(y)\), hence
\[
                            \frac{D(y)}{B'(y)}=\frac1{2k(2-y)}.      \tag{5}
\]
The full inverse must have integral coefficients.
The identity \(C_k=xV_{k-1}-2V_{k-2}\) and \(4\mid k\) give \(\overline B=x^2\overline E\) in \(\mathbb F_2[x]\).
Choose \(J\in\mathbb Z[x]\) lifting \(\overline Q_0/x^2\), with \(\deg J\le h_0-1\).
For \(R_0\in J/2+\mathbb Z[x]\) the polynomial
\[
                                  R=B R_0+Q_0 D                     \tag{6}
\]
is integral, since \(BJ+Q_0E\) has even coefficients.
Conversely this divisibility condition on \(\overline Q_0\) is necessary for this particular \(D\) and a correction in a half-integral old coefficient lattice, since \(\overline E\ne0\) and \(\overline B=x^2\overline E\).

Take \(q_0=q+k/4\) and require \(R_0\) to have degree \(h_0\) and leading coefficient \(1-q_0\).
Then (6) has leading coefficient \(1-q\).
For every \(\varepsilon>0\) there are arbitrarily large \(k\in4\mathbb Z\) and such \(R_0\) with old residues \(b_i=-R_0(x_i)/H_0'(x_i)>0\) satisfying
\[
                         \sum_i b_i<\varepsilon,\qquad
                         \sum_i b_i/(2+x_i)<\varepsilon .           \tag{7}
\]
Indeed write \(e\) for the \(h_0\) lower coefficients of \(H_0\) in descending order.
As in the [square-constant lattice argument](Yin_L_G0462_v170.md#squares), the entries of \(e\) together with \(1\) are rationally independent, and interpolation at the \(x_i\) sends the lower coefficients of \(R_0-(1-q_0)H_0\) invertibly to the residue vector.
Modulo \(\mathbb Z^{h_0}\) these lower coefficients equal \((q_0-1)e\) translated by the fixed coefficient vector of \(J/2\).
On \(k=4v\), \(q_0=(2h_0+2+8v)^2+v\) has integer quadratic coefficient \(64\).
Every nonzero integer character of the coefficient vector has irrational quadratic coefficient, so the quadratic equidistribution proof there applies with the fixed translation.
The open set coming from the strict inequalities (7) therefore receives arbitrarily late visits.

## Stability, excess and the loss in inverse mass

The enlarged trace polynomial is \(Q=Q_0 B\), with \(H=H_0B\).
Its neutral traces are simple since the old ones come from nontorsion circle roots and the grid ones from torsion roots.
At the old traces (6) leaves exactly the residues in (7).
At each new trace (5) gives
\[
                    c_y=\frac1{2k},\qquad
                    b_y=\frac1{2k}\left(1+\frac{u}{2-y}\right).
\]
Logarithmic differentiation at \(2\) gives \(\sum_y(2-y)^{-1}=B'(2)/B(2)=k^2/2\), and the grid symmetry gives the same sum at the negative endpoint.
Therefore
\[
       \sum_y c_y=\frac12,\qquad
       \sum_y b_y=\frac12+\frac{uk}{4},\qquad
       \sum_y \frac{b_y}{2+y}=\frac k4+\frac{uk}{8}.
\]
For sufficiently large \(k\) the last expression plus (7) is less than \(q+T/2\).
The [strict inverse criterion and excess formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return) now give the asserted actual monic irreducible Pisot polynomial at \(q=d^2\).
They also give
\[
   0<\theta-q-u=
       \frac{u}{\theta-1}+\sum_x\frac{b_x}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x}
         \le \frac{u}{q-1}+\frac{1/2+uk/4+\varepsilon}{q-2}.
\]
The old branch masses are smaller than the respective inverse masses.
Taking \(\varepsilon\to0\) and midpoint averages at \(y_j=2\cos((2j-1)\pi/(2k))\) proves the claimed mass limit and weak convergence.
At a varying Salem input one can choose \(k\) after the input and the tolerance, imposing any further lower cutoff.
In particular \(uk\to\infty\) is possible even on a shrinking sequence, so the branch optimum can coexist with unbounded inverse mass.

There is an unconditional fixed input satisfying (4).
The polynomial \(Q_0=x^2-2x-2\) has one root \(T=1+\sqrt3>2\) and the conjugate \(1-\sqrt3\in(-2,2)\).
The quadratic \(z^2-Tz+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\), since its discriminant becomes negative at the other real embedding.
It defines a degree-four Salem number and \(\overline Q_0=x^2\).
For this fixed input the limiting excess is \(T-2>0\).
The construction at shrinking excess requires the stated sequence of Salem inputs, and whether inverse total mass \(1/2\) is attainable in a shrinking sequence remains open here.
