# Exact growth of the least coefficient cost at the degree of a Salem seed

<a id="width"></a>
## The signed cofactor vector has width twice the degree in every node

Let \(P\) be a Salem minimum of degree \(e=2s\), with exterior root \(\rho\), and put \(h=\log\rho\).
For all sufficiently large positive integers \(p\) there exist coprime integral rows of degrees at most \(e\) with
\[
 P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p),\qquad UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0,
 \qquad A^\iota(Y)=A(1/Y).
\]
The least possible \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) over these pairs satisfies
\[
                       \log H=eph+o_P(p).
\]
The proof gives the same rate for the one-norm of the primitive generator of each of the two signed kernel lines.

Choose one root \(x_j\) from each inverse pair and work in the splitting field \(L\).
Fix one sign \(\varepsilon\) and write \(c_j=\varepsilon x_j\).
No \(c_j\) is \(0,1,-1\), and for \(i\ne j\) neither \(c_i=c_j\) nor \(c_ic_j=1\) holds.
Introduce independent variables \(N,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s\).
For degree bound \(e\), let \(A^*(Z)=Z^eA(1/Z)\).
Use the \(e\)-by-\((e+1)\) matrix with two rows at each \(j\) and column indices \(0\le d\le e\), whose entries are
\[
 Y_j^d+c_jY_j^{e-d},\qquad
 NdY_j^d+c_j(1+N(e-d))Y_j^{e-d}.
\]
These are the signed value and Euler derivative at \(X=x_j\) after \(N=p,\ Y_j=x_j^p\).
Let \(C_d\) be \((-1)^d\) times the determinant after deleting column \(d\).
After division of all \(C_d\) by their polynomial gcd, the width of their joint support in every \(Y_i\) is exactly \(2e\), where width is maximum exponent minus minimum exponent.

Fix \(i\), put \(y=Y_i,\ c=c_i\), and temporarily choose complex values of the other \(Y_j\) such that the \(e-2\) nodes \(Y_j^{\pm1}\), \(j\ne i\), are distinct, nonzero and different from \(1,-1\).
At \(N=0\) the remaining \(e-2\) rows require a polynomial to vanish at these \(e-2\) nodes.
Their kernel is the three-dimensional space \(H_0(Z)\langle1,Z,Z^2\rangle\), where \(H_0(Z)=\prod_{j\ne i}(Z-Y_j)(Z-Y_j^{-1})\).
An invertible minor of the \(e-2\) rows stays invertible for \(N\) in a complex disk about zero.
Solving using that minor gives an analytic basis \(A_1,A_2,A_3\) of their kernel near zero, chosen to specialize to \(H_0,ZH_0,Z^2H_0\).
Full row rank of the entire matrix is equivalent to the last two functionals having independent restrictions to these three columns.
Analytic bases and all their \(y\)-derivatives used below are bounded on smaller closed neighborhoods of the finitely many nonzero points in question.
Every Taylor remainder in the local variable is consequently uniform as \(N\to0\).

The two moving rows each have degree at most \(e\) in \(y\), so every maximal minor has degree at most \(2e\).
At \(N=0\) their constant terms as functionals on a polynomial \(A\) are \(a_0+ca_e,ca_e\), while the coefficients of \(y^e\) are \(a_e+ca_0,ca_0\).
The functionals \(a_0,a_e\) are independent on \(H_0\langle1,Z,Z^2\rangle\), because \(H_0\) is monic with constant one.
Hence some maximal minor has a nonzero constant term and some has a nonzero \(y^{2e}\) coefficient already at \(N=0\).
At finite nonzero \(y\) and \(N=0\) the restrictions have rank two except at \(y=\pm1\) and \(y=Y_j^{\pm1}\), \(j\ne i\).
Indeed away from the zeros of \(H_0\) they test the two quadratic values at \(y,y^{-1}\).

If there were common zeros in the \(y\)-line for arbitrarily small nonzero \(N\), choose a sequence of them with \(N\to0\) and pass to a convergent subsequence in the complex projective line.
Homogenize all minors to degree \(2e\).
The nonzero endpoint coefficients exclude limiting points zero and infinity.
The remaining candidates are the finitely many points analyzed in the next two sections.

<a id="simple"></a>
## The two fixed points of inversion

Near \(y=\xi\in\{1,-1\}\) write \(y=\xi e^\theta\) and put
\[
 v(\theta)=y^{-s}(A_1(y),A_2(y),A_3(y)).
\]
Apart from a nonzero scalar and a multiple of the first row, the two restrictions to the kernel basis are
\[
 f=v(\theta)+c v(-\theta),\qquad
 g=N\big(v'(\theta)-c v'(-\theta)\big)+c v(-\theta).
\]
Use the moving basis \(v(0),v'(0),v''(0)\) of row vectors.
It is invertible at \(N=0\), since multiplication by the nonvanishing scalar \(y^{-(s-1)}H_0(y)\) preserves the invertibility of the first three jets of \((y^{-1},1,y)\) at \(\xi\).
It remains invertible with bounded inverse for small \(N\).

Since the first coordinate of \(f\) tends to \(1+c\ne0\), dependence requires \(g=\lambda f\).
The first coordinate gives \(\lambda=c/(1+c)+O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3)\).
The second coordinate of this equality gives
\[
 0=(1-c)N-\frac{2c}{1+c}\theta+O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3).
\]
Thus \(\theta/N\to (1-c^2)/(2c)\ne0\).
In the third coordinate the terms \(c\theta^2/2\) cancel, leaving
\[
 0=(1+c)N\theta+O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3).
\]
Division by \(N^2\) contradicts the nonzero limit.

<a id="collision"></a>
## Collision with a distinct node or its inverse

Near \(w=Y_j\), \(j\ne i\), put \(d=c_j,\ y=w e^\theta\), and set
\[
 u(\theta)=y^{-s}(A_l(y))_{l=1}^3,\qquad
 v(\theta)=y^s(A_l(1/y))_{l=1}^3 .
\]
Primes in this section denote ordinary derivatives in \(\theta\), including the derivative of \(1/y\) inside \(v\).
The two restrictions at the moving node are
\[
 f=u+c v,\qquad g=N(u'+c v')+c v .
\]
The fixed rows at \(w\) give the identical equations with parameter \(d\) at \(\theta=0\).
Writing \(u_j,v_j\) in the following calculation for the \(j\)-th derivatives at zero, they give the exact identities
\[
                     u_0=N(u_1+d v_1),\qquad
                     v_0=-N(u_1+d v_1)/d .
\]
At \(w=Y_j^{-1}\) the same equations apply with \(d=c_j^{-1}\).
To see this, interchange \(u,v\) and reverse the sign of the log parameter in the fixed equations at \(Y_j\).
The new derivative row is the negative of the old derivative row divided by \(c_j\), plus the new value row.
For either choice \(d\ne c\) and \(cd\ne1\).

At \(N=0\), \(u(\theta),v(\theta)\) are respectively a common scalar \(B(\theta)=y^{-(s-1)}H_0(y)\) times
\((y^{-1},1,y)\) and \((y,1,y^{-1})\).
The equality of the common scalars uses the reciprocity of \(H_0\).
Here \(B(0)=0,\ B'(0)\ne0\) and \(w\ne\pm1\), so \(u_1,v_1\) are independent.
Complete \(u_1,v_1\) to a basis using one fixed vector independent of them at \(N=0\).
Its inverse is bounded and analytic near zero.
The projection onto its third coordinate annihilates the *moving* \(u_1,v_1\) exactly.
At \(N=0\) this projection is a nonzero scalar multiple of the functional \((1,-(w+w^{-1}),1)\).
It sends the derivatives of both displayed triples to \(w-w^{-1}\), up to that same scalar.
Consequently the projections of \(u_2,v_2\) tend to the same nonzero number \(\alpha\), since each contains twice \(B'(0)\) times the corresponding first derivative and a multiple of the original triple.

Let \(a=1-c/d\ne0\).
In the first two coordinates the leading parts of \(f,g\) are respectively
\[
          (Na+\theta,\,Nda+c\theta),\qquad (Na,\,c\theta).
\]
The errors are \(O(|N||\theta|+|\theta|^2)\).
Their two-by-two determinant, if zero, gives
\[
                c\theta^2-da^2 N^2=O((|N|+|\theta|)^3).
\]
Along any putative sequence of zeros, \(|\theta/N|\) is therefore bounded above and bounded away from zero.
After taking a subsequence \(\theta/N\to t=a q=q-1/q\), with \(q^2=d/c\).
Here \(q\ne0,\pm1\).
The first coordinate of \(f/N\) tends to \(a(1+q)\ne0\), so proportionality \(g=\lambda f\) requires \(\lambda\to1/(1+q)\).

In the third coordinate the exact zero projections of \(u_1,v_1\) and the formulas for \(u_0,v_0\) remove all constant and linear terms of \(f\).
There \(f=\alpha(1+c)\theta^2/2+o(N^2)\), and \(g=\alpha(1+c)N\theta+c\alpha\theta^2/2+o(N^2)\) along the subsequence.
The third coordinate of \(g-\lambda f\), divided by \(\alpha N^2t\), tends to
\[
 (1+c)+\frac{t}{2}\left(c-\frac{1+c}{1+q}\right)
             =\frac{(1+q)(c+1/q)}{2}.
\]
It is nonzero because \(q\ne-1\) and \(cq=-1\) would imply \(cd=1\).
This contradicts proportionality at every candidate collision.

For each allowed fixed choice of the other \(Y_j\), compactness now shows that all sufficiently small nonzero \(N\) give maximal minors with no common zero in \(y\).
If a common polynomial factor in \(L[N,\mathbf Y]\) had positive \(Y_i\)-degree, one could choose the other nodes subject to the preceding restrictions with its leading \(Y_i\)-coefficient still a nonzero polynomial in \(N\).
Specializing any sufficiently small \(N\) outside its finite zero set would then give a nonconstant common factor of the specialized minors, an impossibility over \(\mathbb C\).
Applying this argument to each \(i\) puts the whole common factor in \(L[N]\).
Dividing it out leaves every coordinate width equal to \(2e\) by the nonzero endpoint coefficients proved above.

<a id="rate"></a>
## Projective height of the signed lines and exact cost of a genuine pair

Divide the cofactor tuple by its common polynomial gcd and scale it over \(L\) to have coefficients integral over \(\mathbb Z\).
Its remaining entries have no common nonconstant polynomial factor.
The conjugates \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\) are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion.
Indeed there is an embedding sending any prescribed \(x_i\) to \(\rho\), with all other representatives on the circle.
After \(N=p,Y_j=x_j^p\), every nonzero polynomial in the parameters gives a nondegenerate exponential polynomial, by collecting equal \(\mathbf Y\)-exponents with their polynomial coefficients in \(p\).
At a fixed embedding, division by the \(p\)-th power of a base of maximal modulus and the [local exponential estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) show that the log modulus equals \(p\) times the largest log modulus among the bases, up to \(o_P(p)\).
The upper bound comes directly from the triangle inequality.
This also gives eventual nonvanishing of any fixed nonzero parameter polynomial.

The common finite valuation of the specialized tuple has normalized log norm \(o_P(p)\).
To verify this when no entry is constant, take one nonzero entry \(F\) and an integral linear combination \(G\) of all the entries which avoids each of the finitely many irreducible factors of \(F\).
For each factor the forbidden coefficient vectors form a proper subspace, so such a choice exists over the integers.
The common valuation of the entries is at most the minimum for \(F,G\).
Use [Xiao's outside-place gcd theorem](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) for that coprime pair at \((p,x_1^p,\ldots,x_s^p)\) with only the archimedean places allowed.
The finite height in the almost-unit condition is \(\log p\), the point height is at least \(ph/e\), and the sum of its coordinate heights is \(O_P(p)\).
For each fixed positive tolerance the exceptional closed subset is avoided eventually, by applying the preceding nonvanishing result to a nonzero polynomial vanishing on that subset.
Letting the tolerance tend to zero gives the asserted sublinear loss.
A nonzero constant entry or a constant chosen \(G\) gives the same conclusion from its fixed integral norm.

At each embedding sending \(x_i\) to \(\rho\) or \(\rho^{-1}\), only the \(i\)-th exponent of a monomial contributes to its log modulus.
In the first case the archimedean log maximum of the tuple is \(ph\) times its maximal \(i\)-th exponent up to \(o_P(p)\).
In the second it is \(-ph\) times its minimal \(i\)-th exponent up to the same error.
These \(2s=e\) embedding classes partition the embeddings of \(L\) with equal weights \(1/e\).
The projective height of the tuple, obtained by averaging the archimedean maxima and subtracting the finite common valuation sum, is therefore
\[
                    \frac{ph}{e}\sum_{i=1}^s 2e+o_P(p)=eph+o_P(p).
\]

For every sufficiently large \(p\) the specialized matrix has rank \(e\), since some maximal minor is generically nonzero.
Its kernel consists of the polynomials \(A\) with \(P^2\mid A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A^*(X^p)\).
Indeed the value and derivative conditions at the \(s\) representatives propagate to their inverses by signed reversal about \(ep+1\), also over \(L\).
Divisibility by \(P^2\) is a rational linear system on the coefficients of \(A\), so the one-dimensional kernel has a primitive integral generator.
Its coefficient maximum has logarithm equal to the absolute projective height just obtained, and its one-norm differs by a factor between one and \(e+1\).

At \(N=0\) the full cofactor kernel for either sign is spanned by the monic reciprocal polynomial \(\prod_j(Z-Y_j)(Z-Y_j^{-1})\) for distinct nonzero nodes away from the endpoints.
Both raw cofactor vectors there are nonzero and have equal nonzero constant and leading coefficients.
Consequently \(C_{+,0}C_{-,e}+C_{+,e}C_{-,0}\) is a nonzero parameter polynomial.
It remains nonzero for all sufficiently large \(p\).
The [equal-width kernel formula](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) applies since all powered conjugates are distinct.
It gives genuine coprime pairs at each of these exponents and the exact minimum \(\frac{\kappa}{2}\max(\|A+C\|_1,\|A-C\|_1)\), where \(A,C\) are the two primitive signed generators and \(\kappa\in\{1,2\}\).
The maximum is between \(\max(\|A\|_1,\|C\|_1)\) and twice that quantity by the triangle inequality.
This proves the claimed asymptotic for the least cost before imposing a cutoff.
