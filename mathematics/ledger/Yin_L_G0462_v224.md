# Irreducible quartic sections with no small Salem exclusion prime

<a id="progression"></a>
## Irreducibility along the progression preserving a cubic

Write \(P_{a,b}=-g(X^a)-X^bB(X^a)\) for \(g(T)=T^4-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^3-T^4\).
For primitive positive \(a,b\), denote the monic cyclotomic-free part by \(X^nH_{a,b}(X+X^{-1})\).
Use \(r=b/a\), and call \(p\) good if \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}H_{a,b}\).
In the two [odd trace degree families](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v400.md#statement), write \(-\sigma\) for the negative exterior root and \(\ell(p)\) for the least odd irreducible factor degree of \(H_{a,b}\bmod p\).
For every fixed \(\alpha\in[1,3)\) there are primitive directions with
\[
 a\asymp(\log b)^\alpha,\qquad r\to\infty,\qquad
 H_{a,b}\ \text{irreducible over }\mathbb Q,\qquad
 p_{\rm least}\gg\log b ,
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(p_{\rm least}\) is the least good \(p\) with \(\ell(p)\ge r\).
On these directions \(\sigma\) is not Salem, the unique odd rational degree is \(n\), and even simultaneous reduction modulo every integer up to a constant times \(\log b\) retains a common false degree-three divisor.

For an integer \(Y\to\infty\), take \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,Y)\) and \(M=4\cdot2^Yt\) from the [fixed cubic construction](Yin_L_G0462_v223.md#least), where \(t\) is the order of \(X\) modulo \(L\) and \(P_{1,2}\).
Thus \(\log M\asymp Y\).
Choose a prime \(a\asymp Y^\alpha\) coprime to \(M\).
For \(\alpha>1\) the prime interval argument there applies directly.
For \(\alpha=1\), use its bounds \(c x\le\vartheta(x)\le Cx\) at \(x=Y,DY\), where \(\vartheta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p\).
Choosing \(D\) large enough makes \(\vartheta(DY)-\vartheta(Y)>\log M\), which provides \(Y<a\le DY\).

Put \(K=\lceil a^2Y^2\rceil,\ b=2a+kM\) and take \(K\le k\le2K\) with \(a\nmid k\).
All these \(b\) are \(2\bmod4\) and coprime to \(a\), so the parent has no cyclotomic part by the [quartic torsion table](Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#orders).
In the [transported cubic algebra](Yin_L_G0462_v223.md#transport) the same generator \(z\) satisfies \(z^M=1\), so the same monic cubic over \(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\) divides every \(H_{a,b}\bmod L\) in the interval.
At each prime \(p\le Y\) it contributes an odd irreducible factor of degree at most three.

Take \(R=2+KM/a\).
Every selected \(b/a\) is in \([R,2R]\), \(\log(2aR)\asymp Y\), and \(a\sqrt{\log(2R)/R}\to0\) since \(M\ge2^Y\) and \(a\) grows at most polynomially in \(Y\).
The [cross-direction count](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#statement) bounds the number of reducible cores in this entire slope interval by \(O(a^2Y)\).
Each selected \(k\) yields a different primitive direction, and there are \(\gg K\) admissible \(k\).
Thus all but an \(O(1/Y)\) fraction have irreducible core.
On those directions the core has all \(a>1\) exterior roots by [quartic pole allocation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation) and the [unique exterior pole](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#quartic).
This proves the non-Salem conclusion, and irreducibility of the core implies irreducibility of \(H_{a,b}\) by lifting any trace factorization.
The [one-prime criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v220.md#maximum) now guarantees a certificate, whereas every prime up to \(Y\) retains the cubic and \(3<r\).
Since \(\log b\asymp Y\), this proves (1).

## Delaying all primes with a fixed or arbitrarily slowly growing first exponent

For every fixed \(B_0>0\), the same lower bound \(p_{\rm least}\gg_{B_0}\log b\) can be obtained for each choice of
\[
             a=83^{\,2h+1}\le Y^{B_0},\qquad h\ge0 .
\]
The core is again irreducible and the result holds for all but an \(O_{B_0}(1/Y)\) fraction of the parameters below.
Thus \(a\) can be fixed at 83, can grow arbitrarily slowly along such powers, or can be chosen comparable to \((\log b)^\alpha\) for any fixed \(\alpha>0\).
Here each \(p\le Y\) retains an odd irreducible factor of degree at most five.

For \(Y\ge83\) let \(C\) be the product of primes \(5\le p\le Y,\ p\ne83\), so \(\log C\asymp Y\) by the preceding bounds on \(\vartheta\).
With \(K=\lceil a^2Y^2\rceil\), take \(K\le k\le4K/3\) prime to \(2\cdot3\cdot83\) and set
\[
                      c=kC,\qquad b=6a+c^2 .
\]
These are distinct primitive square-offset directions.
Because \(a\) is polynomially bounded in \(Y\), \(\log b\asymp_{B_0}Y\).
With \(R=6+(KC)^2/a\), all slopes are in \([R,2R]\) and the first-exponent hypothesis of C401 again holds uniformly.
The fixed coprimality condition leaves \(\gg K\) parameters, so at most an \(O_{B_0}(1/Y)\) fraction have reducible core.
For each irreducible core the allocation and Salem conclusions of the preceding section apply.

For every prime \(p\mid C\), the [endpoint formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v400.md#unitary) gives \(p\mid H_{a,b}(-2)\).
Indeed \(c^2\) contributes valuation at least two, and dividing by the cyclotomic endpoint factors removes at most one at five and none at the other primes of \(C\).
It remains to treat \(2,3,83\), including the effect of removing actual cyclotomic factors before reduction.
We have \(a\equiv11\bmod24,\ c^2\equiv1\bmod24,\ b\equiv19\bmod24\).
Hence \(\Phi_{24}\) and \(\Phi_{18}\) are absent, and the only possible factors removed are \(\Phi_2,\Phi_{10}\).

* At two choose a primitive cube root \(x\) in \(\mathbb F_4\).
  For \(u=x^a=x^2\), the seed gives \(g(u)=1,\ B(u)=u\), and \(x^b=x\), so this is a section root.
  The removed factors have only orders one and five modulo two, and \(x+x^{-1}=1\) gives a linear trace factor.
* At three put \(P_0=P_{1,6}=X^{10}+X^9-X^6-X^4+X+1\).
  Its reduction factors as \((X^5-X-1)(X^5+X^4-1)\).
  Modulo \(X^5-X-1\) over \(\mathbb F_3\), the powers \(X^9,X^{27},X^{81}\) are \(X^4+X+1,\ X^4+X^2+1,\ X^4-X^3-X^2+X+1\), respectively.
  Multiplying these by \(X^3X\) gives \(X^{121}=1\).
  Thus choose a root \(u\ne1\) of this quintic with order dividing \(121\) in \(\mathbb F_{3^5}\), and choose \(x\in\langle u\rangle\) with \(x^a=u\) since \(11\nmid a\).
  Now \(121\mid c^2\), so \(x^b=u^6\) and \(P_0(u)=0\) supplies a section root.
  The order of \(x\) is 11 or 121 and survives both cyclotomic removals.
  Its trace belongs to \(\mathbb F_{3^5}\), giving degree one or five.
* At 83 the residue \(x=7\) has \(x^{41}=1,\ x\ne1\) and \(P_{1,6}(x)=0\), by reduction of the displayed polynomial.
  Since \(a\) is a power of 83, \(x^a=x\), and \(41\mid c\) gives \(x^b=x^6\).
  Its order 41 survives the removals and the trace is already rational over \(\mathbb F_{83}\).

This accounts for every prime up to \(Y\), independently of the factorization over \(\mathbb Q\).
The degree bounds apply even at bad primes as modular assertions.
For the irreducible cores all good primes attaining \(\ell(p)\ge r\) therefore exceed \(Y\), as claimed.
The square-offset construction supplies a potentially different small odd degree at each prime.

## Full-degree witnesses and the half-degree window

For the irreducible cores in either construction, \(\max_p\ell(p)=n\) over good primes, so the largest possible degree of a modular irreducible factor is also \(n\).
Indeed \(n\) is odd, and attaining \(\ell(p)=n\) is exactly irreducible reduction of \(H_{a,b}\).
Every \(p\le Y\) has an odd factor of degree at most five, hence has no degree-\(n\) irreducible reduction for large \(Y\).
The least good prime attaining either full-degree maximum also exceeds \(Y\).

There is an obstruction to copying the [nearest-degree construction](Yin_L_G0462_v223.md#half) on these logarithmic first-exponent scales.
For its seed \(P_{1,b_*}\) with \(b_*=2j-4\), let \(t\) be the order of \(X\) modulo \(L\) and this seed of degree \(2j\).
Reduction modulo two gives \(P_{1,b_*}\bmod2\mid X^t-1\), so \(t\ge2j\).
Even under inverse-power transport, a choice \(b=b_*a+kM,\ k\ge1,\ t\mid M\) with \(j\) a nearest integer to \(r/2\) must have \(3\le kM/a\le5\).
It follows that \(a\ge2j/5\) and \(b=ar=O(a^2)\), incompatible with \(a\le(\log b)^{B_0}\) as \(b\to\infty\).
This excludes that specific period construction.
Whether a different construction can retain a common modular divisor at the nearest predicted Salem trace degree throughout the height range of (1) remains open.
