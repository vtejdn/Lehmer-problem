# Nine limiting series for a Chebyshev trace numerator of leading coefficient two

<a id="profiles"></a>
## Possible limits of the inverse construction

In the [inverse construction](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) take \(C_h(x)=2U_h(x/2)-U_{h-1}(x/2)\), \(h\ge1\), where \(U_n(\cos t)=\sin((n+1)t)/\sin t\).
For a sequence of successful constructions with \(q\to\infty\), every subsequential limit of the [normalized series](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) has the form \(K=(2-z)U_0/V\) in the following table.
Here \(U_0\) denotes a numerator factor, \(\Phi_6=z^2-z+1\), and \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
The limiting Herglotz measure \(\mu\) has mass two, \(2\mu\) has continuous density \(\rho\) relative to normalized arc length, and \(M(\rho)\) is the multiplicative Mahler measure in \(z\).

| \(U_0\) | \(V\) | \(\rho\) | \(M(\rho)\) | Atoms of \(\mu\) and their masses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(1-z^2\) | \(1\) | \((x-2)^2(x+2)\) | \(1\) | none |
| \(1+z\) | \(1\) | \((3-x)(x+2)\) | \((3+\sqrt5)/2\) | none |
| \(\Phi_6\) | \(1-z\) | \((x-1)^2\) | \(1\) | \(1\) of mass \(1/2\) |
| \(1\) | \(1\) | \(4-x\) | \(2+\sqrt3\) | none |
| \(1+z\) | \(1-z\) | \(x+2\) | \(1\) | \(1\) of mass \(1\) |
| \(1\) | \(1+z\) | \(1\) | \(1\) | \(-1\) of mass \(3/2\) |
| \(1\) | \(1-z\) | \(3\) | \(3\) | \(1\) of mass \(1/2\) |
| \(1\) | \(\Phi_6\) | \(2\) | \(2\) | each primitive sixth root of mass \(1/2\) |
| \(1\) | \(1-z^2\) | \(2\) | \(2\) | \(1\) of mass \(1/4\) and \(-1\) of mass \(3/4\) |

The list gives necessary conditions only.
For instance, the [restriction at \(-1\)](Yin_L_G0462_v40.md#statement) already excludes the first and fifth rows.
For every fixed Taylor order, all sufficiently large \(q\) among successful constructions must match the prefix of one of these nine series, by subsequential compactness and integrality.

## Integrality of the limiting reciprocal

Since \(q-1\mid C_h(2)=h+2\), the degrees \(h\) tend to infinity.
Put \(B^\#(z)=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})\) for the monic integer denominator \(B\) in the inverse construction.
The [trace identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) and the geometric sum for \(z^hU_h(x/2)\) give the exact polynomial identity
\[
       K_q(z)B^\#(z)=(1-z^2)z^h C_h(x)=2-z+z^{2h+1}(1-2z).
\]
In particular \(K_q'(0)=-1-2[z]B^\#\) is odd.
Every sequence has a [locally convergent subsequence](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) on each strict disk, and its limit \(K\) has strictly positive real part with \(K(0)=2\).
The [eventual periodicity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) of its integer coefficients gives a reduced rational representation \(K=U/V\), with \(U,V\) integral and \(V(0)=1\) a signed squarefree cyclotomic product.
Indeed one may start with denominator \(1-z^d\) for an eventual period \(d\) and cancel common factors.
The limiting reciprocal \(A=(2-z)/K\) is rational, analytic in the open disk, and its Taylor coefficients are integers as coefficientwise limits of the \(B^\#\).

Every pole of \(A\) is a root of unity.
To see this, take the rational vector space spanned by the left shifts of its Taylor coefficient sequence.
Rationality gives a linear recurrence beyond a finite prefix, hence finite dimension.
Finitely many coordinates together are injective on this space.
The integer span of the shifts embeds through those coordinates into \(\mathbb Z^d\) for some \(d\), and is therefore a finitely generated free group.
The shift preserves it, so in a lattice basis its matrix \(S\) is integral.
Evaluating the first coordinate of the iterates recovers the coefficients of \(A\), whose denominator thus divides \(\det(I-zS)\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
The reciprocal of each pole is an algebraic integer, and all its conjugates are reciprocals of poles because \(A\) is rational over \(\mathbb Q\).
They have modulus at most one by analyticity, so Kronecker's theorem gives the assertion.

It follows that every zero of \(U\) is torsion except possibly one simple zero at \(2\).
If \(U\) had only torsion zeros, its constant \(2\) would make it twice an integral cyclotomic product, giving even Taylor coefficients for \(K\) and contradicting its odd first coefficient.
We obtain \(U=(2-z)U_0\), where \(U_0(0)=1\) and \(U_0\) is a signed cyclotomic product.
Each of its zeros \(\xi\) is simple.
Indeed \(K\) is analytic at \(\xi\) and \(\Re K(r\xi)\ge2(1-r)/(1+r)\) by the Herglotz representation, excluding vanishing of order at least two.

## A phase bound and the finite factor calculation

Set \(u=\deg U_0,\ v=\deg V,\ k=v-u-1\) and \(\epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U_0)\operatorname{lc}(V)\in\{1,-1\}\).
On the circle away from poles, cyclotomic reciprocity gives
\[
       \rho(z)=K(z)+K(1/z)
         =\frac{U_0(z)}{V(z)}
              \big[(2-z)+\epsilon z^k(2z-1)\big].
\]
This nonnegative real Laurent polynomial extends over the poles by the [Fourier decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) and is nonzero by the [strict moment inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#radial-limits).
Every circle zero of \(\rho\) has even order.
In particular every simple circle zero of the bracket must be torsion, since \(U_0,V\) have only torsion zeros.

If a torsion point \(\xi\) zeros the bracket, then \(\eta=(2-\xi)/(2\xi-1)=-\epsilon\xi^k\) is torsion and \(2(\xi\eta-1)=\eta-\xi\).
Thus \((\eta-\xi)/2\) is an algebraic integer with all conjugates of modulus at most one.
Kronecker's theorem makes it zero or a root of unity.
In the first case \(\eta=\xi\) and \(\xi=\pm1\).
In the second case \(|\eta-\xi|=2\), so \(\eta=-\xi\) and \(\Phi_6(\xi)=0\).
Of these possible points, \(1\) is a root exactly for \(\epsilon=-1\), and \(-1\) exactly for \(\epsilon(-1)^k=1\).
The primitive sixth roots occur exactly for \(k\equiv1\bmod6\) with \(\epsilon=1\), or \(k\equiv4\bmod6\) with \(\epsilon=-1\).

For \(k\ge0\), the function \(z^k(2z-1)/(2-z)\) has winding number \(k+1\) and angular derivative of its argument \(k+P(t)>0\), where \(P(t)=(1-1/4)/|e^{it}-1/2|^2\).
There are therefore \(k+1\) simple circle zeros of the bracket.
For \(k=-m\) with \(m\ge4\), use \(z^m(2-z)/(2z-1)\), of winding number \(m-1\) and phase derivative \(m-P(t)>0\), since \(P(t)\le3\).
These counts first give \(-5\le k\le3\), and the explicit torsion conditions then exclude \(k=-5,-4,2,3\) for both signs.
For \(k=-3,\epsilon=1\), the bracket times \(z^3\) is \(z^2(-x^2+2x+2)\).
The simple root \(x=1-\sqrt3\) lies in \((-2,2)\) and differs from each allowed torsion trace \(2,-2,1\).

The remaining brackets, multiplied by \(z^{\max(0,-k)}\) and up to nonzero real constants, factor as follows.

| \(k\) | \(\epsilon=1\) | \(\epsilon=-1\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(-3\) | excluded | \((z-1)^3(z+1)\) |
| \(-2\) | \((z+1)(z^2-3z+1)\) | \((z-1)\Phi_6\) |
| \(-1\) | \(z^2-4z+1\) | \((z-1)(z+1)\) |
| \(0\) | \(1+z\) | \(1-z\) |
| \(1\) | \(\Phi_6\) | \(1-z^2\) |

The coprime products \(U_0,V\) are squarefree.
By even multiplicity in \(\rho\), their product contains exactly the torsion roots of odd bracket multiplicity, each once.
Any root assigned to \(V\) must divide the bracket because \(\rho\) has no pole.
Distribute the factors over \(\mathbb Q\) with \(v-u=k+1\) and constant terms one.
This gives the nine rows in order, with the additional choice \(U_0=1-z,V=1+z\) for \(k=-1,\epsilon=-1\) discarded because its density \(x-2\) is negative on the open arc.
The displayed density identity evaluates each row, and the mass at a simple pole \(\xi\) is \(-(2-\xi)U_0(\xi)/(2\xi V'(\xi))\) by the Herglotz kernel residue.
For \(a>2\), \(M(a-x)=(a+\sqrt{a^2-4})/2\) by the two roots of \(z^2-az+1\), and all monic factors with only unit roots have measure one.
These evaluations give the values in the table.
