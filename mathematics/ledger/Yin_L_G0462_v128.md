# Neither limiting circle distribution survives the next delay-two coefficient discrepancy

<a id="first"></a>
## The first departure from the circle distribution at the preceding scale

Suppose the delay-two Mahler measures of actual Pisot inputs tend to one from above.
Use the [signed concentration theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v29.md#statement), pass to one discrepancy sign \(a=\pm1\), and write \(p=n+1\to\infty\).
Let \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2F)\), and write \(h_0=2,\ h_j=[z^{|j|}]K^0\) for \(j\ne0\).
For the positive measure \(\mu\) attached to the input extract a weak limit \(\nu\) of \(v^p\).
The [two possible laws](Yin_L_G0462_v123.md#laws) give the following two comparison germs, respectively,
\[
             H=1+a z^p,\qquad H=(1-a z^p)^{-1}.
\]
The model is chosen along the extracted subsequence.
It is analytic in the open disk, whereas \(K^0\) has a genuine pole at the interior root of the matched delay polynomial.
Thus a first positive coefficient discrepancy \(q\) between \(K^0\) and \(H\) exists for each input.
The agreement through any fixed multiple of \(p\) from [the first-scale coefficient comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v125.md#window) gives \(q/p\to\infty\).

By the [long-prefix bound](Yin_L_G0462_v124.md#window), the signed integer data now approximate doubled Fourier moments uniformly through every fixed multiple of the new scale,
\[
        \sup_{|j|\le Cq}\left|h_j-2\int v^j\,d\mu\right|\longrightarrow0
             \qquad(C>0).
\]
The extension across zero uses conjugation symmetry and \(\mu(\mathbb T)\to1\).
In particular \(h_q\) is a bounded integer.
Extract its value, the value of \([z^q]H\), and a joint limit of \((x,y)=(v^p,v^q)\); the two coefficient values are distinct.
The first marginal of that joint limit \(\Omega\) is the preceding \(\nu\).
For every \(r\in\mathbb Z\) let \(m_r=2\int x^r y\,d\Omega\), a real integer given by the limit of \(h_{q+rp}\).
For each fixed \(r<0\) one has \(0<q+rp<q\) eventually, so these moments come from \(H\) exactly.
The moment \(m_0\) comes from the actual discrepant coefficient.

<a id="continuous"></a>
## The arclength law with one harmonic

In the first case \(\nu=(1+a\operatorname{Re}x)\,dm(x)\), where \(m\) is normalized arclength.
The measure obtained by weighting \(\Omega\) by \(2y\) and projecting to \(x\) has density \(Q\) satisfying
\[
                |Q(x)|\le 2+a(x+x^{-1})=|1+ax|^2 .
\]
Its Fourier coefficients, up to reversal of index, are the integers \(m_r\).
The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma makes \(Q\) an integer Laurent polynomial.
At \(x=-a\) it vanishes to order at least two by the displayed inequality.
Division in \(\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}]\) by \(2+a(x+x^{-1})\) gives an integer Laurent polynomial of circle norm at most one.
By Parseval the quotient is zero or a signed monomial.
Here \(m_0=h_q\ne0\), since \([z^q]H=0\).
Consequently \(Q=c x^b(2+a(x+x^{-1}))\) for some \(b\in\mathbb Z,\ c=\pm1\).
It follows that \(y=c x^b\) almost surely, since its conditional mean has modulus one at almost every \(x\).
Thus \(\int|1-c v^{q-bp}|^2d\mu\to0\).
The exponent is positive and \(O(q)\) for large inputs as \(b\) is fixed and \(q/p\to\infty\).
This contradicts the [cyclotomic recurrence exclusion](Yin_L_G0462_v127.md#annihilator) at scale \(q\).

<a id="mixed"></a>
## Half arclength and half a point mass

Now \(\nu=(m+\delta_a)/2\).
Decompose \(\Omega\) into the half-mass on \(x=a\) and the complementary half with \(x\)-marginal \(m/2\).
There is a constant \(A=\mathbb E[y\mid x=a]\), \(|A|\le1\), and a function \(B\in L^\infty(m)\) with \(\|B\|_\infty\le1\) such that
\[
                    m_r=a^r A+\int x^r B(x)\,dm(x).
\]
Here \(B\) is the conditional expectation of \(y\) for the complementary probability.
Set \(e=[z^q]H\), constant on this extraction.
If \(p\nmid q\) then \(e=0\) and \([z^{q+rp}]H=0\) whenever \(q+rp>0\).
If \(p\mid q\), the corresponding coefficient is \(e a^r\) with \(e=a^{q/p}\).
In either case \(m_r=e a^r\) for every negative integer \(r\).
The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma along \(r\to-\infty\) gives \(A=e\).
All Fourier coefficients of \(B\) are then integers, and its constant coefficient is \(m_0-e\ne0\).
Parseval and \(\|B\|_\infty\le1\) force \(B=c\) almost everywhere with \(c=\pm1\).
Thus \(y=c\) almost surely on the continuous component, and the entire limit is supported on
\[
                        (x-a)(y-c)=0 .
\]
It follows that \(\int |(1-a v^p)(1-c v^q)|^2d\mu\to0\).
The product has degree \(p+q=O(q)\) and coefficient absolute sum at most four, so the [same recurrence and pole contradiction](Yin_L_G0462_v127.md#inverse) applies.
These two exclusions exhaust the possible marginal laws and hence rule out a sequence of delay-two measures tending to one strictly from above.
