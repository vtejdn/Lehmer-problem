# One pair of integer polynomials with repeated reciprocal factors at several primes

<a id="common"></a>
## Finite coefficient minima for a set of primes

Let \(S\) be a nonempty finite set of primes, write \(r=|S|\), \(s=\sum_{p\in S}p\), and let \(b\) be a positive integer.
Take the minimum \(M(S,b)\) of the initial candidate \(2^s\) and the costs \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\le 2^s\) of the following pairs.
The two nonzero integral polynomials \(U,V\) have degree at most \(b\), are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), and have \(D=UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), with \(A^\iota(Y)=A(1/Y)\).
For **every** \(p\in S\), the graph \(U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\) must have a repeated monic reciprocal irreducible factor which is the minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit.
The same \(U,V\) serve all primes, although the qualifying irreducible factors may differ.
The [single-prime factor test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#factor) decides these conditions for each candidate using polynomial factorizations of degree at most \(2b\).

Write \(\lambda\) for the unrestricted Lehmer infimum in logarithmic measure.
For any sequence of such sets \(S_j\) and budgets \(b_j\) with \(\min S_j\to\infty\) and \(b_j/|S_j|\to\infty\), put \(s_j=\sum_{p\in S_j}p\).
Then
\[
 \lambda\le\liminf_j\frac{\log M(S_j,b_j)}{s_j}
 \le\limsup_j\frac{\log M(S_j,b_j)}{s_j}
 \le\min\{\log2,2\lambda\}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\lambda=0\) if and only if for each integer \(k\ge2\) all sufficiently large \(j\) admit a common qualifying pair of cost at most \(2^{\lfloor s_j/k\rfloor}\).
Equivalently a positive gap holds if and only if for some \(k\ge2\) infinitely many sets in the prescribed sequence admit no such pair.
The upper estimate will use the same seed factor at all the primes of each set.

<a id="tangencies"></a>
## Distinct tangencies and the sum of the measures

Fix a qualifying pair and let \(P\) range through all its distinct qualifying factors at each \(p\).
For a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\) put \(z=\alpha^p\) and \(R=-U/V\).
Coprimality gives \(V(z)\ne0\), and the vanishing of the graph and its derivative gives
\[
                    R(z)=\alpha,\qquad pzR'(z)=R(z).
\]
Indeed the graph is \(V(x^p)(x-R(x^p))\).
It follows that \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and the minimum \(Q_{p,P}\) of \(z\) has the same degree as \(P\) and measure \(p\,m(P)\).
At a given \(z\) the two nonzero values \(R(z)\) and \(zR'(z)\) determine \(p\) uniquely, and \(R(z)\) then determines the root \(\alpha\).
Consequently distinct pairs \((p,P)\) give distinct \(Q_{p,P}\), since sharing a powered minimum would allow the same conjugate \(z\) to be used for both pairs.
By the [reciprocal elimination identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity), each \(Q_{p,P}^2\) divides \(D\) in the integral Laurent ring.
Their product divides \(D\), so nonnegativity of the remaining integral measure and the circle bound \(|D|\le H^2\) give
\[
         \sum_{p,P}p\,m(P)\le\tfrac12 m(D)\le\log H,\qquad
         \sum_{p,P}\deg P\le b.                                     \tag{2}
\]
The degree estimate uses the exponent span of \(D\), at most \(2b\).
Taking one factor at each prime gives \(s\lambda\le\log H\).
The initial candidate also satisfies this bound since \(\lambda\le\log2\).

<a id="construction"></a>
## Interpolation of the same seed at all the primes

Fix a monic reciprocal minimum \(P\) of a nontorsion unit with conjugates \(\alpha_i\), degree \(e\) and measure \(h>0\).
Assume each prime in \(S\) preserves the degree of \(P\) under powering.
For fixed \(P\) only finitely many primes can fail this hypothesis, by the [powering argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers).
Within each prime the nodes \(z=\alpha_i^p\) are distinct.
The powered minima for different primes have respective measures \(ph\) and are therefore distinct and coprime.
Let \(N=re\), let \(Q(Y)=\prod_z(Y-z)\) over all these nodes, and set \(A_z(Y)=Q(Y)/(Y-z)\) and \(L=\prod_{p\in S}p\).
The monic reciprocal \(Q\) has constant term one.
The [single-prime construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#rows) prescribes individual derivatives.
For the common graph we prescribe the value and first derivative of the quotient at every node.
Form
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_0(Y)&=L Y\sum_z A_z(Y)^2,\\
 U_0(Y)&=-\sum_{z=\alpha_i^p}\alpha_i
                 [LY+(L/p)(Y-z)]A_z(Y)^2 .
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{3}
\]
The coefficients are algebraic integers fixed by every automorphism of the splitting field, hence rational integers.
At \(z=\alpha_i^p\) the summands for all other nodes vanish to order at least two, and \(V_0(z)=LzQ'(z)^2\ne0\).
Consequently modulo \((Y-z)^2\) the quotient \(-U_0/V_0\) equals
\[
            \alpha_i\left(1+\frac{Y-z}{pY}\right).
\]
Its value at \(z\) is \(\alpha_i\) and its ordinary derivative there is \(\alpha_i/(pz)\).

Put
\[
       U=YU_0,\qquad V=YV_0+Q^2(1+cY+Y^2).
\]
At every node the quotient \(-U/V\) has the same value and first derivative as \(-U_0/V_0\).
Neither \(U\) nor \(V\) vanishes there, and \(V(0)=1\).
At each nonzero root \(w\) of \(U\) the slope \(wQ(w)^2\) in the parameter \(c\) is nonzero.
Since \(\deg U\le2N\), some \(c\in\{0,\ldots,2N\}\) avoids all common roots.
The polynomial \(V\) is monic of degree \(2N+2\), whereas \(U\) is divisible by \(Y\) and has smaller span.
The outer coefficient in \(VV^\iota\) therefore gives \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\).
Applying the two [tangency equations](#tangencies) gives \(P^2\mid U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\) simultaneously for all \(p\in S\).

To bound the one-norms set \(\Lambda=\prod_z(1+|z|)\le2^N\exp(hs)\).
One has \(\|Q\|_1\le\Lambda\) and \(\|A_z\|_1\le\Lambda/(1+|z|)\).
Since \(|\alpha_i|\le\max(1,|z|)\), the weight \(|\alpha_i|(1+1/p+|z|/p)\) is at most \(2(1+|z|)^2\).
Thus \(\|U_0\|_1\le2LN\Lambda^2\), \(\|V_0\|_1\le LN\Lambda^2\), and the endpoint and coprimality correction costs at most \((2N+2)\Lambda^2\).
In particular the common qualifying cost satisfies
\[
                \log H\le 2hs+2N\log2+\log(5LN),
             \qquad \max(\deg U,\deg V)\le2N+2.                       \tag{4}
\]

<a id="gap"></a>
## The limit at a vanishing gap

For any fixed seed, eventually every prime of \(S_j\) preserves its degree and \(2e|S_j|+2\le b_j\).
Writing \(u_j=\min S_j\), we have \(N/s_j\le e/u_j\) and \((\log L)/s_j\le\sup_{x\ge u_j}(\log x)/x\).
Also \(\log(5N)/s_j\to0\), uniformly in the size of \(S_j\) for this fixed \(e\).
Thus (4) gives \(\limsup (\log M)/s_j\le2h\), using the initial candidate if the row exceeds the height cutoff.
The [reduction to reciprocal unit minima](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) permits \(h\) to approach \(\lambda\).
Together with (2) this proves (1).
If \(\lambda=0\), the normalized minima tend to zero, which eventually places a genuine pair inside the asserted integer cutoff for each fixed \(k\).
If \(\lambda>0\), choose \(k\) with \((\log2)/k<\lambda\) and use the pointwise lower bound.
For singleton sets the [single-prime limit](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) identifies the exact constant \(\lambda\).
Whether the upper factor two in (1) can be removed for arbitrary growing sets remains open here.
