# Long strings of unit cyclic norms at powers of an original Salem number

<a id="degree-scale"></a>
## The exponents and the original degrees

Let \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), and write \(C_n(\alpha)=|\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^n-1)|\).
For each positive integer \(K\), choose a power \(s=2^h\ge\max(4,K)\), put \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(16s,1,\ldots,K)\), and take arbitrarily large odd \(e\) subject to the [divisibility condition below](#torsion).
There is an irreducible Salem number \(1<\alpha<\phi\) of degree exactly \(d=Le+2\) such that
\[
  C_n(\alpha)=
  \begin{cases}
      1,&2s\nmid n,\\
      (1+(d-2)/s)^s,&2s\mid n
  \end{cases}
                  \qquad\text{for every }n\mid(d-2).                 \tag{1}
\]
Every positive power of \(\alpha\) has degree \(d\).
In particular
\[
             C_{je}(\alpha)=C_{(d-2)/(16j)}(\alpha)=1
                            \qquad(1\le j\le K).                    \tag{2}
\]
Thus even the complete norm at each of the indicated degree-dependent exponents is a unit in absolute value.

One can take \(K\to\infty\), \(e=d^{1-o(1)}\), and \(\alpha\to\phi\) with
\(\max_{n\mid(d-2)}\log C_n(\alpha)/d\to0\).
For fixed \(K\) the index \(e\) is exactly \((d-2)/L\), whereas along the growing lists \(e/d\to0\).
Every powered unit \(\beta=\alpha^e\) is an actual \(e\)-th power in its degree-\(d\) field, with the first \(K\) cyclic norms all equal to one.
The construction leaves open the cyclic norms at \(d,2d,\ldots\) and an exponent chosen freely from a linear window for each input.
The measures of the original polynomials tend to \(\phi>1\).

<a id="seed"></a>
## A Pisot polynomial with a prescribed cyclotomic sum

Take \(F(z)=z^2-z-1,\ A(z)=z^2-1\) and set
\[
        f_s=z^sF+A,\qquad
        g_s=z^{s+2}f_s(1/z),\qquad D_N=z^Nf_s+g_s,\qquad N=Le.
\]
On the unit circle \(|F|^2-|A|^2=1\), since \(F/z=z-z^{-1}-1\) and \(A/z=z-z^{-1}\).
Rouché's theorem gives \(s+1\) zeros of \(f_s\) inside the open disk and one outside, counted with multiplicity.
Its value at 1 is negative and at \(\phi\) is \(\phi^2-1>0\), so its exterior root \(\theta_s\) belongs to \((1,\phi)\).
It is irreducible since any monic integral factor supported on the strictly interior roots would have a nonzero constant term of absolute value less than one.
For every fixed \(1<t<\phi\), \(t^{-s}f_s(t)\to F(t)<0\), so \(\theta_s\to\phi\) as \(s\to\infty\).
Direct reversal gives
\[
                             f_s+g_s=-z(1+z^s).                        \tag{3}
\]

For each fixed \(s\), the monic reciprocal \(D_N\) has precisely one simple root outside the unit circle for all sufficiently large \(N\), and this positive root tends to \(\theta_s\).
Indeed \(|g_s|=|f_s|>0\) on the circle, so for \(0<\lambda<1\) the polynomials \(z^Nf_s+\lambda g_s\) have just one exterior root by Rouché.
Continuity of the roots as \(\lambda\) increases to one bounds the number strictly outside for \(D_N\) by one.
For any \(1<u<\theta_s<v\le\phi\), \(D_N(u)<0<D_N(v)\) for large \(N\), which locates the root and proves the assertions.
Reciprocity gives exactly one strictly interior root, the inverse of this same number.

## Removing exactly the factor of order \(2s\)

It suffices to require that \(e\) be an odd multiple of every odd integer at most \(32(s+2)^2\).
We adapt the [three-conjugate torsion sieve](Yin_L_G0462_v24.md#all-delays) to \(G=-g_s/f_s=1-z+O(z^2)\) at zero.
A torsion root \(\zeta\) of \(D_N\) satisfies \(\zeta^N=G(\zeta)\).
According as its order \(t\) is odd, twice an odd integer, or divisible by four, one of \(\zeta^2,-\zeta^2,-\zeta\) is a conjugate of \(\zeta\).
The corresponding exponents are \(2,t/2+2,t/2+1\), respectively, each coprime to \(t\) in its case.
Writing the conjugate as \(\xi\zeta^k\), the evenness of \(N\) gives \(G(\xi\zeta^k)=G(\zeta)^k\).
For \(k=2\), the left side lacks a linear term at zero, while the right side has linear coefficient \(-2\).
For \(k=1,\xi=-1\), the linear coefficients have opposite signs.
Clearing denominators therefore gives a nonzero integral polynomial of degree at most \(2k(s+2)\le4(s+2)\) vanishing at \(\zeta\).
Hence \(\varphi(t)\le4(s+2)\).

The elementary bound \(t\le2\varphi(t)^2\) follows multiplicatively from the prime-power formula, for which \(\varphi(q^v)^2/q^v\ge1\) for odd primes and is at least \(1/2\) for \(q=2\).
The odd part of \(t\) thus divides \(e\).
If \(2^u\mid t\) with \(u\ge1\), then \(2^{u-1}\le\varphi(t)\), giving \(2^u\le8(s+2)\le16s\).
Thus \(t\mid N\).
By (3) the torsion roots are exactly the \(s\) roots of \(1+z^s\), all of order \(2s\).
At each one, \(f_s(\zeta)=\zeta\), \((f_s+g_s)'(\zeta)=s\), and
\[
                             D_N'(\zeta)=N+s .
\]
They are all simple.

The monic quotient \(S=D_N/(1+z^s)\) is integral of degree \(N+2\).
The monic irreducible factor containing the exterior root must contain its inverse, since its constant term is a unit and all the other available roots have modulus one.
Every remaining factor would then have all its roots on the unit circle and would be cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem, contrary to the removal of every torsion root.
Thus \(S\) is the minimal polynomial of the exterior root \(\alpha\) and its other \(d-2\) roots, besides \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\), lie on the unit circle.
To see that all powers keep the degree, a conjugate above \(\alpha^j\) in the norm tower must have modulus \(\alpha\), which occurs at just one conjugate of \(\alpha\).
The extension \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\) therefore has degree one for each \(j\ge1\).

## The complete integer norms on every divisor

For \(n\mid N\) and \(\zeta^n=1\), equation (3) gives \(S(\zeta)=-\zeta\) whenever \(\zeta^s\ne-1\).
At each root \(\zeta^s=-1\) the derivative formula gives \(|S(\zeta)|=(N+s)/s\).
The \(n\)-th roots of unity contain either all \(s\) exceptional roots or none according as \(2s\mid n\) or \(2s\nmid n\).
Multiplying over \(\zeta^n=1\) proves (1) for the original irreducible factor.

Every \(je\) with \(1\le j\le K\) divides \(N\), and its power of two is at most \(s\) because \(e\) is odd.
Also \(16j\mid N\) for \(1\le j\le K\) and \(v_2(N)=h+4\), so \(v_2(N/(16j))=h-v_2(j)\le h\).
This proves (2).
For the joint limit take \(s\) to be the least allowed power of two, then choose each admissible odd \(e\) so large that \(\log e\ge K\log L\), \(s\log(1+N/s)/d<1/K\), and \(|\alpha-\theta_s|<1/K\) with \(\alpha<\phi\).
All three requirements follow for fixed \(K,s\) as \(e\) grows on the specified progression.
They give the asserted simultaneous limits.

## Relation to exceptional units

Zaïmi's [fixed-list theorem](../audits/literature/Zai25/Yin_U001.md#fixed-list) already supplies infinitely many Salem degrees for any prescribed finite list of exceptional powers, with infinitely many examples in each indicated degree.
In particular one may select examples of arbitrarily large absolute height at one of those fixed degrees by the elementary coefficient bound for monic polynomials of bounded degree and measure.
Thus the arithmetic of an isolated powered unit with positive absolute height and arbitrarily long unit prefixes does not by itself force a positive norm return at a bounded multiple.
Formula (1) gives the complete norms at exponents determined by the original Salem degree, with a common bound on the original measures.
The [original-unit income criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0391_v1.md#bounded-power-degree-escape) still permits other exponents, and this construction does not meet the hypothesis of measures tending to one.
