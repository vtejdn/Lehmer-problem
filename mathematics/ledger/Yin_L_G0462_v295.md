# Small Mahler measure forces many minimal supports that resist splitting at one exponent

<a id="splitting"></a>
## A finite interval and a single intermediate exponent

Fix a nontorsion algebraic unit with minimum \(P\) and a length \(N\).
Use the [minimal ternary supports and their translation and dilation normalization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#statement).
Say that a minimal support \(S\subset[0,N)\) splits if two minimal supports \(S_1,S_2\subset[\min S,\max S]\) intersect in exactly one exponent \(h\) and satisfy \(S=(S_1\cup S_2)\setminus\{h\}\).
The signs of their relations can always be chosen to cancel at \(h\).
The interval restriction makes splitting intrinsic to a translation class and keeps all intermediate supports within the original observation window.
Write \(I_r\) for the number of nonsplitting supports of size \(r\) and span less than \(N\) up to translation, and \(D_r\) for the number after additionally dividing by the exponent gcd.
A normalized shape is counted whenever it has an actual nonsplitting realization.

For the length \(N\) and weight ceiling \(T+1\) in the [finite-scale bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale), any sequence of inputs with log Mahler measure \(m\to0\) has weights \(r\to\infty\), \(r\le T+1\), satisfying
\[
              \log D_r\ \ge\ (1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m).
\]
Consequently an exponential bound restricted to the nonsplitting patterns at these lengths on record-measure inputs would suffice for a Lehmer gap.

<a id="trees"></a>
## Counting by trees of exponent sets

Let \(A_j\) count all size-\(j\) minimal supports of span less than \(N\) up to translation.
Put \(H_j=\max_{3\le r\le j} I_r^{1/(r-2)}\).
If \(A_j>0\), repeated splitting terminates since both children have at least three terms and are strictly smaller than their parent.
It gives a tree with one internal vertex for each final nonsplitting support and one leaf for each surviving exponent.
For precision, start with the star of the original support, with one incident edge for each exponent.
On splitting a vertex, distribute its old edges to the two new vertices according to their exponent labels and connect the two vertices by a new edge labelled \(h\).
At each vertex the incident labels are exactly its support, even if a label is reused elsewhere in the tree.
The \(j\) leaves keep their original labels.
If the internal degrees are \(r_1,\ldots,r_s\), the tree degree identity gives \(\sum_i(r_i-2)=j-2\), so \(s\le j-2\) and \(\sum_i r_i\le3j-6\).

Root at the leaf of smallest surviving exponent, translate it to zero and order each vertex's children by increasing exponent.
The number of plane rooted trees on at most \(2j-2\) vertices is at most \(4^{2j}\), by summing the Catalan bounds.
At a vertex of degree \(r_i\) choose one of the \(I_{r_i}\) translation classes and the index of the incoming edge among its ordered exponents.
The known incoming exponent fixes its translate and the order fixes all outgoing labels.
Thus a description determines all surviving exponents, while a support can admit several descriptions.
Using \(r_i\le 2^{r_i}\) gives
\[
                         A_j\ \le\ 128^j H_j^{j-2}.
\]
This bound uses no uniqueness of the decomposition.

The [translation count at the witness length](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale) supplies \(3\le j\le T+1\) with \(A_j\ge(\log 2/(32m))^{j-1}\).
Writing \(L=\log(1/m)\), the tree estimate gives \(\log H_j\ge L-O(1)\), uniformly for \(m\to0\).
Take a maximizing degree \(r\).
The [fixed-weight unit-equation bound](Yin_L_G0462_v290.md#shapes) bounds \(I_r\le A_r\) uniformly in the input at each fixed degree, so \(r\to\infty\).
The [dilation budget](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#dilations) gives \(I_r\le D_r\,2^{r-2}\log(r)/m\).
Consequently \(\log D_r\ge(r-2)(L-O(1))-L-\log\log r\), which proves the assertion.

For the [golden ratio](Yin_L_G0462_v294.md#supports), all minimal supports of weight greater than three do split.
Indeed the weight-\(\ell+2\) support for \(\ell\ge2\) is obtained from its predecessor and the consecutive triple ending at \(2\ell\) by cancelling their common exponent \(2\ell-2\).
There is only one nonsplitting shape, the consecutive triple.
For general inputs a bound for the number of nonsplitting shapes remains open.
