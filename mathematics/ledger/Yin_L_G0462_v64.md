# A uniform Pisot gap when the endpoint coefficients dominate the trace lift

<a id="dichotomy"></a>
## A lower bound on the total size of the interior coefficients

Let \(q\ge2\) be the positive constant of a Pisot minimal polynomial, \(\theta\) its exterior root, \(j\ge1\) its branch index and \(C\) its [trace numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) of degree \(h\).
Write \(N=2h+2\) and
\[
  \Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})=j(1-z^N)+E,\qquad
  \varepsilon=\frac1j\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}|[z^k]E|.
\]
There are absolute constants \(\varepsilon_0,c>0\) such that \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\) implies \(\theta-q\ge c\).
Thus along any sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\), the sum of the absolute interior coefficients exceeds \(\varepsilon_0j\) eventually, regardless of their number or positions.
In the small-perturbation range one also has \(q\le j/c+2\).
The main researcher proposed rounding the inverse measure to the \(N\)-th roots and clearing cyclic cosets by binomials.
The following argument gives the uniform error and the ordinary polynomial identity needed to apply the fixed-support gap.

Set \(\delta=\theta-q\), \(B^\sharp=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})=\sum_{k=0}^{N} b_kz^k\) and \(J_q=j/K_q\).
The [signed inverse measure](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) \(\omega_q\) is real and conjugation invariant, has mass one and has negative part \(\gamma\delta_1\), where \(\gamma=j/[4(q-1)]\).
Its support consists of lifts of roots of \(C\) and endpoint atoms at \(1,-1\), all zeros of \(\Pi\).
The exact [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(j\le\delta(q+3)\), hence \(\gamma\le5\delta/4\).
It suffices to consider \(\delta\le1/5\), so \(2\|\omega_q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2(1+2\gamma)\le3\).

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Approximation on the full cyclic group

Assume \(\varepsilon<1\).
Every support point \(v\) satisfies \(|1-v^N|=|E(v)|/j\le\varepsilon\).
Choose its nearest \(N\)-th root \(\xi(v)\), which is unique because a tie would give \(v^N=-1\).
The assignment commutes with conjugation.
Writing \(v=\xi(v)e^{it}\) with \(|Nt|\le\pi\) gives
\[
      |Nt|\le \frac{\pi}{2}|1-v^N|
               \le\frac{\pi}{2}\varepsilon .
\]
Consequently \(|v^n-\xi(v)^n|\le(\pi/2)\varepsilon\) simultaneously for \(0\le n<N\).
The real function \(F(n)=2\int \xi(v)^n\,d\omega_q(v)\) on \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) has Fourier algebra norm at most \(2\|\omega_q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le3\), since the doubled signed weights collected at the grid points are its Fourier coefficients in the [normalized convention](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#finite-abelian-fourier-algebra-norm).

Let \(I(n)\) be the coefficient of \(B^\sharp\) in residue class \(n\) modulo \(N\), summing coefficients with that residue.
Its endpoint coefficients are \(b_0=b_N=1\), so \(I(0)=2=F(0)\).
For \(0<n<N\) one has \(I(n)=b_n\).
In \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J_q\) the term \(-z^N J_q\) does not enter these indices.
The positive Taylor coefficients \(j_n=[z^n]J_q=2\int v^n\,d\omega_q(v)\) have modulus at most three and \(j_0=1\).
The convolution with \(E/j\) therefore contributes at most \(3\varepsilon\) to each \(b_n\), and rounding the argument contributes at most \(3(\pi/2)\varepsilon\) to \(j_n\).
This proves the uniform bound
\[
                   \|I-F\|_\infty\le3(1+\pi/2)\varepsilon .
\]

Take \(\varepsilon_0>0\) small enough that \(\varepsilon_0<1\) and \(3(1+\pi/2)\varepsilon_0\le e_3\), where \(e_3\) is the fixed threshold in the [finite-group rounding lemma](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#rounding).
Its finite iteration of [Sanders's near-integer estimate](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#near-integer-iteration) applies to any finite abelian group, including the even cyclic group used here.
For \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\) it expresses the integer function \(I\) as a sum of at most \(S_0\) signed coset indicators, with \(S_0\) absolute.
Equivalently, the lemma bounds \(\|I\|_A\) and [Sanders's integer theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001) supplies the same qualitative conclusion.

<a id="clearing"></a>
## Lifting the cosets to an ordinary cyclotomic multiple

Write the signed list as \((s_i,r_i,a_i)_{1\le i\le S}\), where \(s_i=\pm1\), \(a_i\mid N\), \(0\le r_i<a_i\), and the coset is \(n\equiv r_i\pmod{a_i}\).
Its representatives in \([0,N-1]\) have generating polynomial \(z^{r_i}(1-z^N)/(1-z^{a_i})\).
In particular this represents the whole group for \(a_i=1\) and a singleton for \(a_i=N\).
Restoring the two separate endpoint coefficients of \(B^\sharp\) gives the ordinary polynomial identity
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B^\sharp+1-z^N
     &=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}I(n)z^n
       =\sum_{i=1}^S s_i z^{r_i}\frac{1-z^N}{1-z^{a_i}},\\
 D B^\sharp
     &=(1-z^N)
         \left(\sum_{i=1}^S s_i z^{r_i}
                  \frac{D}{1-z^{a_i}}-D\right),
 \qquad D=\prod_{i=1}^S(1-z^{a_i}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(1\le S\le S_0\) since \(I(0)=2\).
The last expression has at most \(2(S2^{S-1}+2^S)\le(S_0+2)2^{S_0}\) nonzero terms, independently of the exponents and the group size.
The integer polynomial \(D\) has Mahler measure one, so \(M(DB^\sharp)=M(B^\sharp)>1\).
The actual delay-one associate \(zf+f^*\) of the Pisot minimal polynomial \(f\) differs from \(B^\sharp\) only by a possible factor \(z+1\).
The [fixed-support gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) therefore supplies a single \(\lambda>0\) with \(\log M(B^\sharp)\ge\lambda\) for all inputs satisfying both \(\delta\le1/5\) and \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\).
The [delay-one estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) gives
\[
 \delta\ \ge\ \frac29\bigl(e^\lambda+e^{-\lambda}-2\bigr).
\]
Taking \(c\) to be the smaller of this number and \(1/5\) proves the dichotomy.
The derivative identity \(q=j/\delta-\theta^{-1}+H_q'(0)\) with \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\) gives the claimed bound on \(q\).

<a id="two-terms"></a>
## Two Chebyshev terms with large leading coefficient

For an actual input with \(C(x)=jU_h(x/2)+aU_{h-d}(x/2)\), \(1\le d\le h\), the interior part of the lift is exactly \(E=a(z^d-z^{N-d})\).
Its two exponents are distinct, so \(\varepsilon=2|a|/j\).
Hence \(2|a|\le\varepsilon_0j\) implies the same absolute excess bound and \(q\le j/c+2\) for all offsets and both degree parities.
In particular for the unit perturbation \(a=\pm1\) this holds simultaneously for every \(j\ge\lceil 2/\varepsilon_0\rceil\).
An escaping sequence of two-term inputs would therefore require \(|a|/j>\varepsilon_0/2\) eventually.
