# Adjacent central perturbations and the fixed-support whole-measure boundary

Two adjacent perturbations give a genuine second coefficient direction, and their equal-amplitude line cancels the endpoint change exactly.
This does not yet supply a surviving low-measure construction.
More decisively, the complete lift has a cyclotomic multiple with at most twenty nonzero terms, so the already accepted fixed-support gap excludes positive total measures tending to one throughout this two-term family.
This exploration for Yin's [unrestricted Lehmer contract](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract), developed on 2026-11-09, retains exact algebra and one conductor-11 control.
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1112_v1.md#adjacent-support-review) covers the complete argument, source applicability, conditional prefix combination and the exact controls, with its stated source-inspection limits.
No parameter scan or new numerical computation is claimed by this exploration; the review's bounded reproduction of the already identified survivor is recorded separately there.

<a id="family"></a>
## Two adjacent perturbation indices

Use the neutral trace, full grid and coordinate conventions in [C884v1](../claims/Yin_C_G0884_v1.md#inputs).
Let \(p=2n+1=r+s\), with \(r=2m\) even and \(s\) odd, and choose
\[
                     3\le L<\min(r,s),\qquad L\le n-2.
\]
Put \(k=n-L-1\ge1\), take arbitrary integers \(A,C\), and define
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q&=Q^{(0)}_{r,s,0}
       -t\bigl(A B_k+C B_{k-1}\bigr),\\
 P(z)&=z^{n+1}Q(z+z^{-1}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{1}
\]
The perturbation indices are \(L\) and \(L+1\).
Their lifts start at coefficient indices \(L+1\) and \(L+2\), so the common exact profile prefix through \(L\) is preserved.
Only \(L<\min(r,s)\) is needed for that common prefix.
If both individual index labels are required to lie below the neutral block lengths, add \(L+1<\min(r,s)\); all the conclusions below still apply, and the chosen small control satisfies this stronger condition.

By the [all-\(r\) coordinate identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0884_v1.md#all-r-pole-values),
\[
 h(b_j)=b_j^2E_{m-1}(b_j)^2
           -b_j\bigl(AE_L(b_j)+CE_{L+1}(b_j)\bigr).
 \tag{2}
\]
The pointwise expression in parentheses is now a linear combination of adjacent sine frequencies.
It is not covered simply by substituting one amplitude into [C884v3](../claims/Yin_C_G0884_v3.md#statement).
For fixed neutral block and indices, \(B_k\) and \(B_{k-1}\) are linearly independent by degree.
Thus two nonzero coefficients do not reduce to a single term with that same neutral block; changing the neutral block can nevertheless absorb special coefficient lines, as the exact control below shows.

<a id="endpoint-and-profile"></a>
## Endpoint cancellation and profile cancellation are different conditions

The exact endpoint and profile derivative are
\[
 \begin{split}
 P(-1)&=4m+2(-1)^L(A-C),\\
 Q'(0)&=-A B_k(0)-C B_{k-1}(0).
 \end{split}
 \tag{3}
\]
The neutral trace has a factor \(t^2\).
Moreover
\[
 B_j(0)=(-1)^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor},\qquad
 \frac{B_k(0)}{B_{k-1}(0)}=(-1)^{k+1}.
\]
Therefore the original profile double factor is retained exactly on the line
\[
                              C=(-1)^k A.
 \tag{4}
\]
Under the original strict chamber and endpoint, that line has complete measure one by [L462v11's double-profile-factor argument](Yin_L_G0462_v11.md#double-factor-obstruction).
It cannot supply a positive shell.

The equal-amplitude line \(C=A\) has the separate advantage
\[
                              P(-1)=4m.
\]
The elementary recurrence gives
\[
                B_k+B_{k-1}=(t+2)E_{k-1},
\]
so its perturbation is \(-A t(t+2)E_{k-1}\).
For even \(k\), this endpoint cancellation is also the neutral double-profile line (4).
For odd \(k\) and \(A\ne0\), the profile root is simple:
\[
                       Q'(0)=-2A(-1)^{(k-1)/2}\ne0.
\]
Thus odd \(k\) offers a distinct algebraic freedom: endpoint preservation together with a broken double profile factor.
A simple profile root does not itself establish nonneutrality or the required grid signs.

At the last pole, writing \(u=\pi/p\), the exact sign is
\[
 \begin{split}
 h(b_n)
 ={}&\frac{4\cos^2u\,\sin^2(mu)}{\sin^2u}\\
 &+\frac{2(-1)^L\cos u}{\sin u}
       \left(A\sin((L+1)u)-C\sin((L+2)u)\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{5}
\]
The second sine is larger than the first because \(L+2\le n\).
Consequently equal amplitudes do not cancel the last-pole effect; they cancel only the endpoint.
This is the precise candidate cancellation mechanism, before any claim that the whole chamber survives.

<a id="complete-lift"></a>
## A twenty-term cyclotomic completion preserves the complete measure

The lift identity
\[
                    z^jB_j(z+z^{-1})
                       =\frac{1-z^{2j+1}}{1-z}
\]
gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 P(z)=P^{(0)}(z)
  -\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}\bigl[
    A z^{L+1}(1-z^{p-2L-2})
   +C z^{L+2}(1-z^{p-2L-4})\bigr].
 \end{split}
\]
Multiply by the fixed cyclotomic product \((1-z)^2(1+z)\).
The resulting nonzero integer polynomial is exactly
\[
 \begin{split}
 F(z)
 :={}&(1-z)^2(1+z)P(z)\\
 ={}&(1+z^2)^2(1-z^r)(1-z^s)\\
 &-(1-z^4)\bigl[
    A z^{L+1}(1-z^{p-2L-2})
   +C z^{L+2}(1-z^{p-2L-4})\bigr].
 \end{split}
 \tag{6}
\]
All exponents here are nonnegative; the binomial exponents in the second line of perturbations are positive because \(L\le n-2\).
The neutral product contributes at most twelve nonzero terms, and each perturbation contributes at most four.
Therefore \(F\) has at most twenty nonzero coefficients, even when the block lengths, degree and amplitudes are unbounded.
Coincident exponents or cancellations only reduce that number.
The polynomial has constant coefficient one and is nonzero.

The multiplier has complete multiplicative Mahler measure one.
Thus, with every original factor and multiplicity retained,
\[
                         M(F)=M(P),\qquad m(F)=m(P).
 \tag{7}
\]
This is a whole-measure identity, rather than a small residual factor being selected.

The accepted source input is [DS17-U001](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001), specifically Dobrowolski--Smyth, arXiv:1606.04376v2, Theorem 2 and the univariate consequence retained there.
For each fixed positive integer \(K\), every nonzero integer polynomial with at most \(K\) nonzero coefficients has total logarithmic measure zero or at least some \(\eta_K>0\).
The source imposes no degree, exponent, coefficient-height or irreducibility bound.
Applying its \(K=20\) consequence to the actual \(F\) in (6) proves
\[
                          m(P)=0\quad\hbox{or}\quad
                          m(P)\ge\eta_{20}>0.
 \tag{8}
\]
The calculation instantiates the sparse-completion mechanism recorded in [C221](../claims/Yin_C_G0221_v1.md#statement) and [L19](Yin_L_G0019_v1.md#cyclotomic-support-escape), with the new exact completion (6).
The gap in (8) applies directly to \(F\) under DS17, so no irreducibility hypothesis is imported from C221's original formulation.
The accepted source-inspection limits remain those of DS17-U001; no original-foundation proof audit or effective value of \(\eta_{20}\) is asserted.
The [versioned primary source page](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04376v2) also states the fixed-monomial isolation conclusion; the actual premise reused here is the accepted local theorem scope.

Thus this entire two-adjacent-term family cannot provide a sequence of positive total measures tending to one.
The conclusion does not require the strict pole signs or endpoint.
It supplies no numerical claim that \(e^{\eta_{20}}\) exceeds \(6/5\), so it does not exclude finite inputs with \(1<M(P)<1.2\).

<a id="fixed-number-of-terms"></a>
## The same boundary holds for any fixed number of central terms

More generally, assume \(3\le L<\min(r,s)\) and \(L\le n-1\).
For a fixed integer \(K\ge1\), take integer coefficients \(A_i\) and indices \(L\le\ell_i\le n-1\), and set
\[
              Q=Q^{(0)}-t\sum_{i=1}^{K}A_iB_{n-\ell_i-1}.
\]
The common prefix through \(L\) is retained when \(L<\min(r,s)\).
The same calculation gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 (1-z)^2(1+z)P
  ={}&(1+z^2)^2(1-z^r)(1-z^s)\\
    &-(1-z^4)\sum_{i=1}^{K}
              A_i z^{\ell_i+1}(1-z^{p-2\ell_i-2}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{9}
\]
There are at most \(12+4K\) nonzero terms and the complete measure is unchanged.
Repeated indices may be combined; vanishing coefficients and exponent collisions cannot increase the support.
For every fixed \(K\), DS17 therefore gives
\[
                          m(P)=0\quad\hbox{or}\quad
                          m(P)\ge\eta_{12+4K}>0.
\]
Moving from two terms to any other fixed number does not remove this obstruction.
Within this precise neutral-block presentation, a positive-to-one sequence would need the number of effective perturbation terms to become unbounded, and its actual cyclotomic-completion support must escape every fixed bound.
Unbounded formal term count alone is not sufficient if cancellations leave bounded actual support.
No effective law for \(\eta_K\), or existence mechanism when \(K\) grows, is inferred.

## One exact conductor-11 control

Take
\[
                    p=11,\ n=5,\ r=6,\ s=5,\ L=3,\ k=1.
\]
Both \(L\) and \(L+1\) lie below \(\min(r,s)=5\).
The proposed equal-amplitude cancellation line has
\[
              A=C=-H,\qquad Q=Q^{(0)}_{6,5,0}+Ht(t+2).
\]
Its endpoint is always \(P(-1)=12\), and \(Q'(0)=2H\), so \(H\ne0\) breaks the double profile factor.
The actual grid values are
\[
                h(t)=t^2(t^2-1)^2+HtB_4(t)
                \quad\text{at each pole}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The following exact check excludes every real \(H\), without a scan.

Write \(u=\pi/11\) and \(c=\cos u\).
At the last pole \(t=-2c\),
\[
                         tB_4(t)=-4c\sin(u/2)<0.
\]
Last negativity therefore requires
\[
                 H>
                 \frac{c(4c^2-1)^2}{\sin(u/2)}>1,
\]
where \(c>\sqrt3/2\) suffices for the last inequality.
At \(b_2=2\cos(4u)\), one has \(0<b_2<1\) and
\[
                         B_4(b_2)=-2\cos(2u)<-1.
\]
Hence
\[
            \frac{b_2^2(b_2^2-1)^2}{-b_2B_4(b_2)}
              <b_2<1.
\]
Any \(H\) making the last node negative therefore makes node 2 negative.
At node 4, \(b_4<0\) and
\[
               B_4(b_4)=\frac{\sin(36u)}{\sin(4u)}
                        =-\frac{\sin(3u)}{\sin(4u)}<0.
\]
Thus the perturbation and neutral contribution at node 4 give a strictly positive value.
The negative node 2 followed by positive node 4 violates every strict terminal negative block.
This endpoint-preserving, simple-profile-root line fails its first genuine chamber test.

The same small domain also shows why two nonzero coefficients can rediscover an excluded single-term input after a neutral-block change.
Exact cancellation of the neutral products gives
\[
                       Q^{(0)}_{6,5,0}-Q^{(0)}_{4,7,0}=t^2.
\]
Since \(B_1=t+1\) and \(B_0=1\), every point on \(C=-1\) satisfies
\[
 Q^{(0)}_{6,5,0}-t(A B_1-B_0)
       =Q^{(0)}_{4,7,0}-(A-1)tB_1.
 \tag{11}
\]
In particular \((A,C)=(4,-1)\) is exactly the old conductor-11 survivor, not a new output.
Its [already retained complete-measure enclosure](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0157/readme.md#survivor-measures) is
\[
                  2.759883858<M(P)<2.759883859,
\]
so this two-coefficient representation supplies no \(M<1.2\) improvement.
The measure is reused for the identical polynomial; no new root computation is performed.

## The first gap for the original objective

For a genuine two-term candidate, the [whole-measure prefix theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#statement) would give
\[
 1\le M(P)\le
       (8\lfloor L/2\rfloor+8)^{2/\lfloor L/2\rfloor}
\]
once the original strict signs, endpoint and exact common prefix hold.
Nonneutrality is an additional requirement; breaking the double profile factor alone does not prove \(M(P)>1\).
At \(L=140\), this upper bound is \(568^{1/35}<6/5\), so a certified nonneutral integer chamber point at that length would be a finite target success.

However, (8) prevents that positive upper-bound argument from continuing to arbitrarily large \(L\) with only two perturbation terms.
For sufficiently large \(L\), every such chamber point must instead have \(M(P)=1\), since the prefix upper bound tends to one while (8) has a fixed positive logarithmic gap.
The same conclusion holds separately for every fixed number \(K\) of terms.

The distinct endpoint-cancellation direction is therefore a finite-search diagnostic, not a mechanism for refuting the unrestricted positive-gap conjecture.
No new integer input with \(1<M<1.2\) has been produced by this bounded investigation.
The concrete mathematical change needed to reopen a positive-to-one construction within this presentation is unbounded effective perturbation support; the first missing result there is simultaneous integer realization, strict chamber, endpoint and nonneutrality under the whole-measure prefix bound.
