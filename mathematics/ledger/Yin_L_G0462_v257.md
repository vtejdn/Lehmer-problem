# The exact cutoff by exponent residue at the degree of a reciprocal unit seed

<a id="progressions"></a>
## Powered nodes on each exponent progression

Fix a monic reciprocal irreducible minimum \(P\) of a nontorsion algebraic unit, of degree \(e=2s\), with splitting field \(L\subset\mathbb C\) of degree \(d_L\).
Choose one representative \(x_i\) of each inverse pair of roots.
At width \(e\) consider integral rows \(U,V\) of degrees at most \(e\), coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), with \(UU^\iota\ne VV^\iota\) and \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\), where \(A^\iota(Y)=A(1/Y)\).
Write \(H_p(P)\) for their least cost \(\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) when such pairs exist.
For this fixed seed and width, attainment of the coefficient cutoff \(H_p(P)\le2^p\) is eventually constant on each of finitely many exponent residue classes.
The description below includes multiplicative relations and torsion among the ratios of distinct conjugates.

Let \(G\subset L^\times\) be generated by the \(x_i\), and let \(M\ge1\) annihilate its torsion subgroup, meaning \(\zeta^M=1\) there.
The group \(G^M\) is free of positive rank \(r\).
Choose a basis \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r\) of ordinary algebraic units and write \(x_i^M=\boldsymbol\gamma^{\mathbf m_i}\), using the notation \(\mathbf T^{\mathbf m}=\prod_k T_k^{m_k}\).
On \(p=a+Mn\), \(0\le a<M\), define the Laurent monomials
\[
                 Y_i(\mathbf T)=x_i^a\mathbf T^{\mathbf m_i}.
\]
At \(T_k=\gamma_k^n\) these are \(x_i^p\).
The powered nodes coincide for some positive \(p\) in the progression precisely when two of the \(e\) monomials \(Y_i^{\pm1}\) are identical.
Indeed if two distinct roots \(x,x'\) have \(x^p=(x')^p\), their ratio is torsion in \(G\), so \((x/x')^M=(x/x')^a=1\).
Their exponents in the \(\gamma\)-basis agree as do their constants.
Conversely identical monomials give coincidence at every positive exponent in that class.
In this case there are no qualifying pairs.
For any such pair coprimality gives \(V(x^p)\ne0\), and every conjugate satisfies \(x=-U(x^p)/V(x^p)\), incompatible with a common powered value of two different roots. [Field recovery](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower)

Assume the \(e\) monomials are distinct.
Put the \(Y_i(\mathbf T)\) into the [two signed matrices](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#directions), using a free parameter \(N\) for the exponent.
For sign \(\varepsilon\), their two entries at node \(i\) in column \(0\le j\le e\) are
\[
  Y_i^j+c_iY_i^{e-j},\qquad NjY_i^j+c_i(1+N(e-j))Y_i^{e-j},
  \qquad c_i=\varepsilon x_i .
\]
The matrices live in \(L[N,T_1^{\pm1},\ldots,T_r^{\pm1}]\).
The physical substitution is \(N=a+Mn\), an invertible affine change of parameter over \(L\).
Setting \(N=0\) tests a polynomial identity with \(\mathbf T\) still free.
At generic \(\mathbf T\) all \(e\) nodes are different because the monomials are distinct.
At \(N=0\) the two rows at each node are equivalent to vanishing at \(Y_i\) and \(Y_i^{-1}\).
Thus both kernels are generated there by the monic reciprocal polynomial \(\prod_i(Z-Y_i)(Z-Y_i^{-1})\).
In particular both matrices have full generic rank, and their raw signed maximal-minor vectors \(C_{\varepsilon,j}\) satisfy
\[
              C_{+,0}C_{-,e}+C_{+,e}C_{-,0}\not\equiv0.
\]
Indeed at \(N=0\) each kernel vector has equal nonzero constant and leading coefficients.

<a id="height"></a>
## Exact projective heights on the torus generated by the roots

For each sign take the gcd of the entries of its cofactor vector **after** the displayed monomial substitution.
A concrete polynomial procedure multiplies the vector by a common Laurent monomial to clear negative exponents and divides out its polynomial gcd in \(L[N,T_1,\ldots,T_r]\).
Scale all coordinates by the same nonzero element of \(L\) to make their coefficients algebraic integers.
Use \(S_{\varepsilon,a}\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\) for the union of the \(\mathbf T\)-exponents in these reduced entries with nonzero coefficients in \(L[N]\), after collecting all equal exponent vectors.
Specializing dependent nodes can merge distinct terms and introduce new common factors, so this reduction is performed separately on each progression and for each sign.
Translations of the support from a common Laurent monomial make zero contribution to the average below, by the product formula for units.

For each embedding \(\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) put
\[
 \mathbf w_\sigma=(\log|\sigma\gamma_1|,\ldots,\log|\sigma\gamma_r|),\qquad
 R_{\varepsilon,a}=
 \frac1{M d_L}\sum_\sigma\max_{\mathbf u\in S_{\varepsilon,a}}
                                      \langle\mathbf u,\mathbf w_\sigma\rangle .
\]
The log one-norm of the primitive integral generator of the signed kernel, divided by \(p\), tends to \(R_{\varepsilon,a}\) along this class.
To prove this, each nonzero specialized parameter polynomial becomes
\(\sum_{\mathbf u} b_{\mathbf u}(a+Mn)(\boldsymbol\gamma^{\mathbf u})^n\).
The affine substitution preserves nonzero coefficient polynomials, and the distinct bases have nontorsion ratios.
At each embedding, divide by the \(n\)-th power of any base of largest modulus.
The [local estimate for a nondegenerate recurrence](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) and the triangle inequality give the log modulus as \(n\) times that largest base's log modulus plus \(o_P(n)\).
This proves eventual nonvanishing for each nonzero polynomial used here, including the raw rank minors, the mixed edge expression and any common factor removed.
In particular ties of maximal archimedean weight are allowed.

The common finite valuation of each reduced tuple has normalized log norm \(o_P(n)\).
If a nonzero coordinate is constant its norm suffices.
Otherwise choose one nonzero coordinate and an integral linear combination of the tuple avoiding all irreducible factors of that coordinate.
Such choices exist since each forbidden condition gives a proper linear subspace.
The common finite divisor of the tuple is bounded by that of this coprime pair.
Use [Xiao's estimate outside the archimedean places](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) at \((a+Mn,\gamma_1^n,\ldots,\gamma_r^n)\), treating a constant member directly.
The finite cost in the almost-unit condition is \(\log(a+Mn)\), the point height is at least \(n h_{\rm W}(\gamma_1)>0\) for \(n>0\), and the sum of coordinate heights is \(O_P(n)\), with \(h_{\rm W}\) denoting absolute logarithmic height.
For each fixed positive tolerance the exceptional closed set contains only finitely many of the points, by eventual nonvanishing applied to a nonzero polynomial vanishing on the set.
Let the tolerance tend to zero to obtain the assertion.
Here all constants and limits concern the fixed seed and chosen progression data.

Averaging the archimedean log maxima and subtracting this finite common divisor gives \(pR_{\varepsilon,a}+o_P(p)\) for the projective height, since \(n=(p-a)/M\).
The signed equations at the chosen roots imply those at their inverses by formal reversal, so the specialized line is defined by the rational remainder equations modulo \(P^2\).
It has a primitive integral generator whose log coefficient maximum is precisely its absolute projective height.
Passing to the one-norm adds at most \(\log(e+1)\).
The powered roots are distinct and the mixed edge expression is eventually nonzero, so the [exact two-line minimum formula](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) gives genuine coprime pairs at all sufficiently large exponents in this class.
Its minimum is between one half and twice the larger primitive one-norm, including the parity choice.
Consequently
\[
            \lim_{\substack{p\to\infty\\p\equiv a\pmod M}}
                     p^{-1}\log H_p(P)=R_a:=\max(R_{+,a},R_{-,a}).
\]

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## A strict and decidable comparison on each progression

For every sign and embedding choose one maximizing vector \(\mathbf u_{\varepsilon,\sigma}\).
The equality
\[
 \exp(2Md_L R_{\varepsilon,a})
       =\prod_\sigma\big|\sigma(\boldsymbol\gamma^{\mathbf u_{\varepsilon,\sigma}})\big|^2
\]
expresses this positive real number as an algebraic unit.
Each squared absolute value is the product of an algebraic unit with its complex conjugate.
Hence \(R_a\ne\log2\), since \(2^{2Md_L}\) is a rational nonunit integer and the maximum equals one of the two rates.
In every noncolliding class, all sufficiently large \(p\) qualify at \(H_p(P)\le2^p\) if \(R_a<\log2\), and all sufficiently large \(p\) fail if \(R_a>\log2\).
Together with the colliding classes, this describes an eventually periodic qualification set for the fixed seed at its own degree.
Restricting to odd prime exponents takes the corresponding subsequence of that set.

For an exact computation, sufficient input is a splitting-field representation with the conjugates, together with a certified integral basis of the full relation lattice
\[
          \mathcal R=\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^s:\mathbf x^{\mathbf v}
                                      \text{ is a root of unity}\}.
\]
This lattice is saturated since a power of a torsion-free nonidentity class in \(G/G_{\rm tors}\) cannot be the identity.
Complete its basis to an integral basis of \(\mathbb Z^s\).
The units corresponding to the relation basis generate \(G_{\rm tors}\), so the least common multiple of their orders is an admissible \(M\), with \(M=1\) if the list is empty.
The \(M\)-th powers of the units corresponding to the complementary basis vectors furnish \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r\).
Integer basis change then provides all \(\mathbf m_i\).
Equivalently one can supply these group data directly with their completeness established.
The remaining finite operations compare monomials, form determinants, compute polynomial gcds, and compare the positive real algebraic numbers \(|\sigma(\boldsymbol\gamma^{\mathbf u})|^2\) for the support maxima and their products for the threshold.
The result determines the eventual yes-or-no answers, without giving numerical values of the first exponents after which they hold.
For a fixed wider row bound \(w\ge e\) on a noncolliding progression, the \(e\)-by-\((w+1)\) signed matrix still has rank \(e\) at \(N=0\) over \(L(\mathbf T)\), by evaluation at the same \(e\) distinct nodes.
Its signed maximal minors in complementary column order give the Plücker coordinates of the kernel, which is again rational after specialization by reversal about \(wp+1\).
The primitive integer representative of these coordinates represents the saturated lattice of integral signed rows.
The same support procedure gives each lattice's log covolume rate, since the Euclidean norm of its primitive Plücker vector is its covolume and differs from the maximum coordinate by a bounded factor for fixed \(w\).
When the lattice rank exceeds one, its shortest-vector rate requires information beyond this covolume.
The [unrestricted Lehmer coefficient criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) allows wider rows and varying seeds.
The progression computation provides no uniform onset or lower rate across those choices.
