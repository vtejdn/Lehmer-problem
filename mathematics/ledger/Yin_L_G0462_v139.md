# The dyadic denominator of the resolvent for a leading-two trace interlacer

<a id="local"></a>
## The unique local factor outside the dyadic unit disk

Let \(S\) be the monic trace polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma\), of degree \(m\), with largest root \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\).
Let \(C=\sum_{i=0}^{g}c_i x^i\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be a primitive strict interlacer of degree \(g=m-1\), with leading coefficient two and all its roots in \([-2,2]\).
The [torsion-node degree obstruction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#records) allows an unbounded-degree primitive nonmonic factor in this case.
Normalize \(|2|_2=1/2\) and put \(b=g-\deg(\bar C)\), where the reduction is modulo two.
Primitivity gives \(1\le b\le g\).
Precisely \(b\) roots of \(C\) in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) have modulus \(2^{1/b}\), and all the other roots have modulus at most one.
The \(b\) roots constitute one irreducible factor over \(\mathbb Q_2\), defining a totally ramified extension of degree \(b\).

Indeed at a root \(y\) of modulus \(r>1\), the only possible largest terms of \(C(y)\) are \(2y^g\) and \(c_{g-b}y^{g-b}\).
Their moduli must agree, so \(r^b=2\).
The dyadic Gauss norm of \(C\) is one and equals \(|2|_2\prod_{C(y)=0}\max(1,|y|_2)\), by multiplicativity of the polynomial Gauss norm on the linear factors.
Thus there are exactly \(b\) such roots.
The valuations of \(1,y,\ldots,y^{b-1}\) are distinct modulo \(\mathbb Z\), implying linear independence over \(\mathbb Q_2\).
All conjugates of \(y\) have the same modulus, so its local degree is \(b\) and its ramification index is \(b\).
In \(C^\sharp(z)=z^g C(z+z^{-1})\) the \(b\) polar trace roots give \(b\) circle-coordinate roots of each dyadic modulus \(2^{1/b}\) and \(2^{-1/b}\), by the quadratic equation \(z+z^{-1}=y\).
Every other root of the lift has dyadic modulus one.

<a id="moments"></a>
## Exact denominators and bounded real moments

Put \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\), and \(U=2P/C^\sharp\), expanded at zero.
For every integer \(n\ge0\),
\[
        2^{\lfloor n/b\rfloor}[z^n]U\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
        2^j[z^{jb}]U\ \text{is odd}\quad(j\ge0).
 \tag{1}
\]
The constant of \(C^\sharp\) is two.
Its first odd coefficient is at degree \(b\), since the least Laurent exponent of \(\bar C(z+z^{-1})\) is \(-\deg\bar C\).
Write \(C^\sharp=2V+z^b W\) with \(V,W\) integral, \(V(0)=1\) and \(W(0)\) odd.
Then
\[
      U=\sum_{h\ge0}2^{-h}(-1)^h z^{bh} W^h P/V^{h+1}.
\]
Every factor following \(2^{-h}z^{bh}\) has integral Taylor coefficients.
At degree \(jb\) the summand \(h=j\) contributes an odd numerator at denominator \(2^j\) since \(P(0)=1\), proving (1).

The probability weights \(C(x_l)/(2S'(x_l))\) at the roots \(x_l\) of \(S\) are positive by strict interlacing and sum to one by partial fractions.
Write \(A\) for their mean and \(B\) for their variance.
Inverting their Stieltjes transform gives
\[
   2S(x)/C(x)=x-A-\sum_{C(y)=0}\frac{\rho_y}{x-y},
   \qquad \rho_y=-2S(y)/C'(y)>0,\qquad\sum_y\rho_y=B .
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the expansion of the reciprocal transform at infinity gives polynomial part \(x-A\) and coefficient \(-B\) at \(1/x\), and the signs at the simple poles follow from interlacing.
Since all \(x_l\) are in \([-2,T]\), averaging \((T-x_l)(x_l+2)\ge0\) yields \(B\le(T-A)(A+2)\le(T+2)^2/4\).
For \(D_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\) and \(n\ge1\) define \(F_n=\sum_y\rho_y D_n(y)\).
The real bound \(|F_n|\le2B\) holds for every \(n\).
The geometric-series identity used in the [torsion-node proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#poles) gives
\[
  (1-z^2)U=(1-z^2)(1+z^2-Az)-z^2\left(B+\sum_{n\ge1}F_nz^n\right).
 \tag{3}
\]
Multiplication by \(1-z^2\) preserves the odd numerator in (1) at positive multiples \(jb\), since a contribution two degrees earlier has strictly smaller denominator there.
Consequently \(F_{jb-2}\) has reduced denominator exactly \(2^j\) whenever \(jb>4\).
In particular these bounded real moments are neither integral after multiplication by a fixed scalar nor periodic.

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## A target-dependent local valuation and discriminant bound

At each polar dyadic trace root \(y\), the formula in (2) gives
\[
          |\rho_y|_2\ge |y|_2^2,
\]
with equality precisely for odd \(b\).
Indeed \(S(y)/y^m\) reduces to one in the residue field, by monicity and \(|y|_2>1\).
In \(C(y)/(2y^g)=0\) only the leading term and the term at degree \(g-b\) are units, and their reductions are opposite.
It follows that \(C'(y)/(2y^{g-1})\) is integral locally and reduces to \(g-(g-b)=b\) in characteristic two.
Substitution into (2) proves both cases, using \(m=g+1\).

Over the complex embeddings the product of all positive residues is exactly
\[
       \prod_y\rho_y
       =2^{g-3}\frac{|\operatorname{Res}(C,S)|}{|\operatorname{disc} C|}.
\]
Here \(|\prod_y S(y)|=|\operatorname{Res}(C,S)|/2^m\) and \(|\operatorname{disc} C|=2^{g-2}|\prod_y C'(y)|\).
The arithmetic–geometric mean bound on (2) therefore gives
\[
       |\operatorname{disc} C|\ge 2^{g-3}(g/B)^g
                         |\operatorname{Res}(C,S)| .
\]
If \(\bar C\) is constant and \(g\) is odd, then \(b=g\) and every trace root is polar.
The derivative calculation above yields \(v_2(C'(y))=1/g\) at every root, so \(v_2(\operatorname{disc} C)=g-1\).
Also \(v_2(\operatorname{Res}(C,S))=m+g(-m/g)=0\).
Thus the odd part of the polynomial discriminant is at least \((g/B)^g|\operatorname{Res}(C,S)|/4\).
For bounded \(T\) this forces the \(g\)-th root of that odd part to grow at least linearly with \(g\).

<a id="shift"></a>
## Two adjacent Chebyshev terms

For \(k\ge2\) take \(C=2D_k+D_{k-1}\), so \(g=k,\ b=1\) and
\[
          C^\sharp=(2+z)+z^{2k-1}(1+2z).
\]
On the circle the zeros satisfy \(z^{2k-1}(z+1/2)/(1+z/2)=-1\).
The left side is a finite Blaschke product of degree \(2k\), with strictly positive circle phase derivative, so \(C^\sharp\) has \(2k\) simple unit-circle roots.
Neither endpoint is a root.
In fact \(C\) strictly interlaces the monic \(D_{k+1}\): at \(\cos((k+1)v)=0,\ 0<v<\pi\), its value is \(4\sin((k+1)v)\sin v(1+\cos v)\), of alternating sign.

The monic target \(S=D_{k+1}-C\) still strictly interlaces \(C\).
At the roots of \(C\) its signs agree with those of \(D_{k+1}\).
At the left endpoint \(S(-2)\) has the sign of \(D_{k+1}(-2)\), since \(C(-2)=2(-1)^k\).
Its rightmost root lies in \((2,3)\), since \(S(2)=-4\) and \(S(3)=D_k(3)-2D_{k-1}(3)>0\).
The last inequality follows by the recurrence \(D_{k+1}(3)=3D_k(3)-D_{k-1}(3)\) and \(D_2(3)>2D_1(3)>0\).
Thus the reciprocal lift has one Salem factor, since a rational exterior trace in \((2,3)\) cannot be an algebraic integer, and every other factor is cyclotomic.
At \(k=2\), \(S=x^3-2x^2-4x+4\) has no rational root and is irreducible.
Its reciprocal lift is a Salem minimal polynomial of degree six, since \(T^2-4\) is negative at the two interior embeddings.
Its moments already have reduced denominators \(2^{n+2}\) for every \(n\ge3\), by (3).
This gives an actual Salem target for which the exact bounded integer Fourier sequence of the torsion-node argument is unavailable.
The calculation does not exclude other methods for this displaced set of nodes.
