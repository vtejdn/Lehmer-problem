# The first possible two-value prime in the fixed-budget signed test

<a id="exclusion"></a>
## At most one value at every prime through 97

In the [fixed-budget test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement) one has \(N_p\le1\) for every prime \(p\le97\), for either prescribed sign.
At \(p=101\), a row with two different positive measures would have exactly two qualifying factors, of degrees \((56,56)\) or \((56,58)\).
Each would have measure strictly below Lehmer's.

Recall \(a=\max(2,\lfloor p^{1/8}\rfloor)\), \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\), and \(B=(\log2)/4\).
For the row \(A\), set \(U=A,\ V=\varepsilon Y^b A(1/Y)\) and \(H=\|A\|_1\).
For one representative \(P_i\) of each of two different qualifying measures, let \(Q_i\) be the minimum of the \(p\)-th power of a root.
They have the original degrees \(d_i\) and logarithmic measures \(p\,m(P_i)\).
The [summed derivative divisibility](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) puts \((Q_1Q_2)^{a-1}\) into \(W=pY(U'V-UV')-UV\), of degree at most \(2b\).
In particular \((a-1)(d_1+d_2)\le2b\).
Both \(P_i\) are monic reciprocal unit minima of even degree, by the [small-budget reduction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper).

The term \(u_iv_jY^{i+j}\) in \(W\) carries the multiplier \(p(i-j)-1\), of absolute value at most \(pb+1\).
Hence \(\|W\|_1\le(pb+1)H^2\).
Using the defining strict budget with \(2pb+1\) improves the measure sum to
\[
   m(P_1)+m(P_2)
       < B-\frac{\log((2pb+1)/(pb+1))}{(a-1)p}.                         \tag{1}
\]
Here and below \(m=\log M\).

For every prime \(p\le101\) one has \(a=2,\ pb\ge4,\ d_i\le2b\le114\).
The last inequality follows from \(56^8<101^7<57^8\).
Let \(h_L=\log\rho\) for the exterior root of the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial \(L(X)=X^{10}+X^9-X^7-X^6-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1\).
If either \(m(P_i)\ge h_L\), the other measure would be strictly less than
\[
    B-h_L-\frac{\log(9/5)}{101}
    <\frac7{40}-\frac{14}{87}-\frac4{707}
    =\frac{20723}{2460360}<\frac1{108}.                                \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(40^{10}L(47/40)=-330254204731271<0\), so \(\rho>47/40\) by uniqueness of the exterior root.
Use \(\log x\ge2(x-1)/(x+1)\) for \(x\ge1\) at \(47/40\) and \(9/5\).
The first four exponential series terms at \(7/10\) already give \(e^{7/10}>2\).
But [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(m(P)>2/(\log(3d))^3>1/108\) at every \(2\le d\le114\), since \(3d\le342<(8/3)^6<e^6\).
This contradicts (2).
Thus both distinct measures are strictly below \(h_L\).

By the [first assertion of Mossinghoff–Rhin–Wu, Theorem 1](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree), each \(d_i\ge56\).
This assertion applies to all integer polynomials in the specified measure interval.
Its companion per-degree table calls an input primitive when it has no nontrivial power substitution.
If \(P(X)=T(X^k)\) is irreducible then \(T\) is irreducible and has the same measure, so passage to the primitive polynomial in that sense never increases the degree.
For \(p\le97\), \(54^8<97^7<55^8\) gives \(2b\le110<112\), a contradiction.
At 101 the even degrees and \(d_1+d_2\le114\) leave precisely the two pairs stated as possibilities.
Neither factor can arise by a nontrivial power substitution, since its lower-degree core would still have positive measure below \(h_L\).
Any third distinct qualifying factor, even one repeating a measure, would contribute its degree to the same product-divisibility bound.
There is no room in the \((56,58)\) case.
The remaining two degrees in the \((56,56)\) case cannot contain an admissible minimum, since a nontorsion reciprocal quadratic unit has \(M\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2>2^{1/4}\).

## Both possible factors at 101 must have repeated roots on reduction

Suppose such a row at \(p=101,\ b=57\) exists.
The integer cutoff gives \(H\le58\), since
\[
  ((2\cdot101\cdot57+1)58^2)^4<2^{101}
             <((2\cdot101\cdot57+1)59^2)^4 .
\]
In particular \(\bar A\ne0\) over \(\mathbb F_{101}\).
Write \(F=P_1P_2,\ D=\deg F\) and use bars for reduction modulo 101.
Since \(F^2\) divides the graph \(G\), each irreducible divisor of \(\bar F\) divides \(\bar G,\bar G'\).
In this characteristic \(\bar G=\bar U^p+X\bar V^p\) and \(\bar G'=\bar V^p\).
Thus \(\operatorname{rad}(\bar F)\mid\gcd(\bar U,\bar V)\), and \(\deg\operatorname{rad}(\bar F)\le57\).
In particular the total loss of distinct roots under reduction is at least 55 for \(D=112\) and at least 57 for \(D=114\), including collisions of roots from different factors.
Multiplication by \(F'\) on \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(F)\) has determinant equal up to sign to \(\operatorname{disc}F\).
Its nullity modulo \(p\) is \(\deg\gcd(\bar F,\bar F')\ge D-\deg\operatorname{rad}(\bar F)\), so the corresponding power of \(p\) divides the discriminant by integer Smith normal form.

Each \(\operatorname{Res}(P_i,A)\) is a nonzero integer.
Indeed if \(P_i\mid A\), then reciprocity gives the same divisor in \(A_b^*\), and dividing the graph by \(P_i(X^p)\) yields the signed graph for \(A/P_i\) at width \(b-d_i\le1\).
For a nonzero \(A\) this divisibility case requires \(d_i\le b\).
At all the originally qualifying roots \(\alpha_j\) the removed divisor is nonzero because \(A(\alpha_j^p)\ne0\).
The smaller graph therefore retains both qualifying factors, contradicting its derivative degree bound \(2(b-d_i)\le2\).

Let \(s_i=\deg\operatorname{rad}(\bar P_i)\).
The nullity modulo 101 of multiplication by \(A\) on \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(P_i)\) is at least \(s_i\), since \(\operatorname{rad}(\bar P_i)\mid\bar A\).
The determinant of this multiplication is \(\operatorname{Res}(P_i,A)\), while the root-product bound is \(H^{d_i}M(P_i)^{57}\).
We obtain
\[
    101^{s_i}\le|\operatorname{Res}(P_i,A)|
            \le58^{d_i}M(P_i)^{57},\qquad
    101^{s_1+s_2}<58^D 2^{57/4}.                                      \tag{3}
\]
For \(d=56,58\), the integer comparison \(58^{4d}2^{57}<101^{4(d-4)}\) yields \(d_i-s_i\ge5\) individually, using \(M(P_i)<2^{1/4}\).
For \(D=112,114\), \(58^{4D}2^{57}<101^{4(D-11)}\) gives \((d_1-s_1)+(d_2-s_2)\ge12\).
As before each polynomial discriminant is divisible by \(101^{d_i-s_i}\).
Both discriminants are therefore divisible by \(101^5\), and their product is divisible by \(101^{12}\).
These necessary conditions leave \(N_{101}\ge2\) undecided.
