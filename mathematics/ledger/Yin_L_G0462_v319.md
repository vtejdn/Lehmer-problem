# Joint prime tests on circle logarithms and a cyclotomic comparison

<a id="tails"></a>
## Growth of the raw tail at large primes

On 2026-11-20 the G0001 inquiry compared the [prime substitution bounds](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v618.md#statement) across different primes at a single input.
Let \(P\) be a nonconstant monic reciprocal integer polynomial with constant term one, put \(L(t)=\log|P(e^{it})|\) and use normalized circle measure.
Zeros at finitely many points cause only integrable logarithmic singularities.
Write \(m=m(P)\), and let \(\theta\) be the positive root of \(X^3-X-1\).
The [two-polynomial separation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v615.md#linear) with \(P,1\) gives \(\|L\|_1\ge 2\log\theta-m\).
Since every positive integer dilation preserves circle measure, the reverse triangle inequality gives for every integer \(p\ge2\)
\[
 \int (|L(pt)-pL(t)|-a_p)_+\,d\mu
 \ \ge\ (p-1)\|L\|_1-a_p,\qquad a_p=2\operatorname{arsinh}p .
\]
Thus along any sequence with \(m\to0\) and \(p\to\infty\) the lower limit of the raw tail divided by \(p\) is at least \(2\log\theta\), even without a condition on \(pm\).
Adding these large-prime lower bounds alone cannot distinguish an obstruction specific to hyperbolic shrinking inputs.

<a id="cyclotomic"></a>
## One measure-zero input passing the simultaneous lower tests

Consider \(P_0=(1+X)^{10}\) with logarithm \(L_0\).
On \(|t|\le\pi/3\), \(|1+e^{it}|\ge\sqrt3\), and \(\log|1+e^{it}|\) has mean zero.
It follows that \(\|L_0\|_1\ge (10/3)\log3\).
Concavity of \(\operatorname{arsinh}\) gives \(a_p\le (p-1)a_2\) for \(p\ge2\), since \(\operatorname{arsinh}(p)/p\le\operatorname{arsinh}(2)/2\) and \(p/2\le p-1\).
The preceding estimate bounds the tail from below by \((p-1)((10/3)\log3-a_2)\).
On the same arc \(|(1-e^{it})/(1+e^{it})|\le1/\sqrt3\), so the antipodal excess above \(a_2\) has integral at least \((5\log3-a_2)/3\).
Both lower bounds exceed \(c=\log(11/4)/22\).
For \(b\ge0\), the function \(J_p(b)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(p\cosh(b/2))-a_p\) has \(J_p(0)=0\) and derivative in \([0,1]\), so \(J_p(c)\le c\).
This single cyclotomic power satisfies all the numerical prime and antipodal tail requirements for \(m=0\).
It has a unit-circle root and repeated factors, leaving the irreducible hyperbolic problem open.

<a id="joint"></a>
## Cancellations between integer dilations

For a nonzero integer vector \((b_j)_{1\le j\le N}\) the same linear-pencil estimate gives
\[
       \left\|\sum_{j=1}^N b_j L(jt)\right\|_1
                \ \ge\ 2\log\theta-m\sum_{j=1}^N|b_j|.
\]
Indeed split the positive and negative powers of \(P(X^j)\) into two polynomials \(U,V\) with constant term one, allowing the empty product.
Each is reciprocal and the products are distinct.
To see this, let \(s\) be the first nonconstant exponent of \(P\), let \(j_0\) be the smallest index with \(b_{j_0}\ne0\), and expand \(U/V\) at zero.
Its coefficient at degree \(sj_0\) is \(b_{j_0}[X^s]P\ne0\).
Thus \(U\ne\pm V^*\), and the sum of their measures is \(m\sum_j|b_j|\).
For coefficients summing to zero the linear combination also has mean zero.
The bound applies to cyclotomic inputs as well.
A simultaneous test seeking a contradiction must use information beyond these separated logarithms, such as the absence of unit-circle poles of \(1/P\) and the [energy of its integral multiples](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#statement).
