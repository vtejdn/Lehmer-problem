# A height-one Salem polynomial with trivial return at the prime immediately after its degree

<a id="degree-plus-one"></a>
## An explicit polynomial of exact degree \(p-1\)

For every prime \(p\equiv-1\bmod360\), set \(N=p-3\), \(f(z)=z^3-z-1,\ f^*(z)=1-z^2-z^3\), and put
\[
                      P_p(z)=\frac{z^Nf(z)-f^*(z)}{z-1}.
\]
This is an irreducible Salem polynomial of degree \(d=p-1\) and coefficient height one.
Its Mahler measure tends from below to the plastic number \(\theta_0>1\), and every positive power of its exterior root generates the original number field.
At the first prime greater than its degree it satisfies
\[
        |\operatorname{Res}(P_p,\Phi_p)|=1,\qquad
        |\operatorname{Res}(P_p,z^p-1)|=p-10 .
\]
In particular the [largest common integer modulus](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) at this order is exactly one.
The primes \(p\) can also be chosen so that every rational prime in either class \(\pm1\bmod p\) exceeds \(c p\log p\) for an absolute \(c>0\).

<a id="irreducible"></a>
## The cyclotomic part on this progression

Write \(H=z^N f-f^*\).
As in the [plastic-seed construction](Yin_L_G0462_v82.md#family), on \(|z|=1\) the polynomials \(f,f^*\) have the same positive modulus.
Rouché applied to \(z^N f-t f^*\) for \(0<t<1\), followed by \(t\to1\), shows that \(H\) has at most one exterior zero counting multiplicity.
Here \(H(1)=0,\ H'(1)=7-N=10-p<0,\ H(\theta_0)>0\).
Thus \(H\) has exactly one exterior root \(\alpha\), with \(1<\alpha<\theta_0\).
For every fixed \(1<u<\theta_0\) one has \(H(u)<0\) for large \(p\), proving convergence to \(\theta_0\).
Antireciprocity gives exactly one interior zero, and the unit constant term of the minimal factor of \(\alpha\) forces it to contain that interior zero.
The remaining factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.

The six formal exponent vectors of \(H\) are the same as in the [two-column rank calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v82.md#family).
That argument uses their two transverse differences, the unequal coordinate sums of the columns and coprimality of \(N,1\).
It therefore gives orders dividing one of \(30,6,12,18,24,36\) also for the present even \(N\).
All those orders divide 360.
Since \(N\equiv-4\bmod360\), at every possible cyclotomic zero \(z\) one has
\[
 z^4 H(z)=f(z)-z^4f^*(z)
       =(z-1)z^3g(z+z^{-1}),\qquad g(t)=t^3+2t^2-t-3 .
\]
The cubic \(g\) has no rational root among \(\pm1,\pm3\), and its discriminant is \(-31\).
It is irreducible and has nonreal conjugates, whereas a trace \(z+z^{-1}\) of a root of unity is totally real.
Hence 1 is the sole cyclotomic zero of \(H\), and its nonzero derivative makes it simple.
This proves irreducibility and the claimed Salem degree.
From the constant term upward the coefficients of \(P_p\) are \(1,1,0\), then \(-1\) from exponent \(3\) through \(p-4\), and then \(0,1,1\).
For any positive integer \(r\), embeddings over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^r)\) take \(\alpha\) to a conjugate of modulus \(\alpha\); uniqueness of the exterior conjugate gives the asserted equality of fields.

## The prime-order products

For primitive \(p\)-th roots \(z\),
\[
           P_p(z)=z^{-3}(z+1)\frac{z^5-1}{z-1}.
\]
Indeed \(z^3H(z)=f(z)-z^3 f^*(z)=(z+1)(z^5-1)\).
The fifth-power map permutes these roots and \(\Phi_p(-1)=1\).
Multiplication over them gives the first resultant, and including \(P_p(1)=10-p\) gives the second.
For the optional prime-size condition use the [prime order construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v4.md#orders) with its first modulus replaced by \(\operatorname{lcm}(360,M)\).
It still has logarithm comparable to \(y\), and the other two large prime moduli are coprime to it.
The same least-prime estimate and the same exclusion of every candidate \(ip\pm1\) with \(i\le y\) give infinitely many primes with both requirements.
Thus even these prime order and prime-size conditions allow trivial resultants when the base varies at degree \(p-1\).
