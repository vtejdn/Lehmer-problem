# Trace norms and split or inert primes in the prescribed dyadic resultant

<a id="trace-norm"></a>
## The odd square for reciprocal bases

Use the [prescribed dyadic-order family](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#statement).
Thus \(Q\) is the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), of degree \(D\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(0<m(Q)\le1\).
The polynomial \(Q(z^p)\) is irreducible for the selected prime \(p\equiv1\bmod4\), \(d=Dp\), \(D\le\log(d)/(4\log\log(3d))\), \(J=2^{\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}\), and \(B=J/2^{\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor}\).
Write \(W\) for the odd part of \(|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{J/2}+1)|\), and take \(d\) sufficiently large.
For every such base,
\[
                              W\equiv1\pmod J .
\]
Indeed at every contributing odd prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) the residue of \(\alpha\) has order \(J\), so \(N\mathfrak q\equiv1\bmod J\).
The odd ideal product, with its full valuations, has norm \(W\) by the [norm and transport calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#transport).

Suppose now that \(Q\) is reciprocal.
Put \(\beta=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
The map \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\) is a nonidentity field automorphism of \(K\) fixing \(\beta\), and \(\alpha\) solves \(x^2-\beta x+1=0\).
Thus \([K:F]=2\), \(D=2n\), and the monic minimal polynomial \(T\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of \(\beta\) has degree \(n\) with \(Q(z)=z^nT(z+z^{-1})\).
This trace field is totally real for a Salem base.
Let \(w\) be the odd part of \(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{J/4}+\alpha^{-J/4})|\).
The summed powers belong to \(F\) by invariance under the involution, and
\[
                    W=w^2,\qquad h=2\,\operatorname{ord}_{x=0}(\bar T(x)),
\]
where \(h\) is the multiplicity of \(1\) in \(Q\bmod2\).
Indeed \(\alpha^{J/2}+1=\alpha^{J/4}(\alpha^{J/4}+\alpha^{-J/4})\), and the prefactor is a unit of absolute norm one.
Taking the absolute norm of an element of \(F\) from \(K\) squares its absolute norm from \(F\).
The multiplicity formula follows from \(z+z^{-1}=(z-1)^2/z\) in characteristic two.

<a id="split"></a>
## Exact residue degrees in the two fields

Let \(\ell\mid w\), let \(\mathfrak q\) be a prime of \(K\) over \(\ell\) contributing to the norm, and let \(\mathfrak r=\mathfrak q\cap\mathcal O_F\).
By [good reduction](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#norm), \(\ell\nmid\operatorname{disc}Q\), and also \(\ell\nmid\operatorname{disc}T\).
For the latter assertion, the factorization \(Q(z)=\prod_{T(t)=0}(z^2-tz+1)\) gives
\[
                 \operatorname{disc}Q=(\operatorname{disc}T)^2
                                          \prod_{T(t)=0}(t^2-4).
\]
Each cross resultant of two quadratic factors is \((t-t')^2\), and the last product is an integer.
Consequently the indices of \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) and \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\) in their respective rings of integers are both prime to \(\ell\), by the discriminant-index formula.
Reduction at these primes therefore generates the full residue fields by \(\bar\alpha\) and \(\bar\beta\), respectively.
Both fields are unramified at \(\ell\).

Write \(f=[\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak q:\mathbb F_\ell]\), \(g=[\mathcal O_F/\mathfrak r:\mathbb F_\ell]\).
The Frobenius orbits give
\[
       f=\operatorname{ord}_J(\ell),\qquad
       g=\min\{k\ge1:\ell^k\equiv 1\text{ or }-1\pmod J\}.
\]
In fact \(\bar\alpha\) has order \(J\), and two nonzero field elements \(u,v\) have \(u+u^{-1}=v+v^{-1}\) exactly when \(v=u\) or \(v=u^{-1}\).
The residue of \(\beta\) is the sum at this primitive \(\bar\alpha\).
Conversely, over every odd \(\mathfrak r\) dividing \(\alpha^{J/4}+\alpha^{-J/4}\), both roots of the quadratic equation have order \(J\), since their \(J/4\)-th powers sum with their inverses to zero and hence their \(J/2\)-th powers are \(-1\).
These conclusions concern primes contributing to the norms.

The subgroup generated by an odd \(\ell\bmod J\) contains \(-1\) precisely when \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod J\).
Indeed \(\ell^k\equiv-1\bmod J\) requires odd \(k\) by reduction modulo four, and odd powering is an automorphism of the abelian \(2\)-group \((\mathbb Z/J\mathbb Z)^\times\) fixing \(-1\).
It follows that for \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod J\) one has \(g=1,f=2\), and each matching trace prime is inert in \(K/F\).
At all other contributing primes \(f=g\), and each matching trace prime splits since the quadratic extension is unramified there.
In particular \(f=g\le n\) in the split case.
Applying the [dyadic valuation formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#norm) to \(J\mid\ell^f-1\) now gives
\[
                 \ell\equiv\pm1\pmod{2B},\qquad
                 2B=J/2^{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}.
\]
The same congruence holds directly in the inert case since \(2B\mid J\).

<a id="squarefree"></a>
## The squarefree kernel and the sign modulo the whole order

Each valuation \(v_\ell(w)\) is a sum of the trace-prime valuations weighted by their common residue degree \(g\), and hence is a multiple of \(g\).
This \(g\) is a power of two and equals one precisely when \(\ell\equiv\pm1\bmod J\).
It follows that every prime dividing the squarefree kernel of \(w\), the product of its primes of odd valuation, satisfies the stronger congruence \(\ell\equiv\pm1\bmod J\).
For the norm itself one has
\[
 w\equiv (-1)^{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\equiv-1\bmod J}v_\ell(w)}\pmod J,
              \qquad W\equiv1\pmod{2J}.
\]
Indeed \(\ell^g\equiv1\bmod J\) in the split case, and the inert case contributes its indicated sign.
Squaring either residue gives one modulo \(2J\).
Thus \(W\ge(J-1)^2\) as soon as \(W>1\), which holds eventually by the [uniform norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#norm) and its degree-dependent Mahler bound.
The congruence gives a lower bound of order \(\log J\) for \(\log W\), whereas the [unrestricted-gap criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#criterion) requires order \(d\).

The negative prime class can occur at every dyadic scale for one fixed base.
Take \(Q(z)=z^2-3z+1\), with \(0<m(Q)=\log((3+\sqrt5)/2)<1\).
For \(J=2^k,\ k\ge3\), its positive trace norm is \(b_{k-2}\), where \(b_0=3,\ b_{s+1}=b_s^2-2\).
This follows by successive doubling of exponents, and each \(b_s\) is odd.
Since \(b_1=7\), induction gives \(b_s\equiv-1\bmod 2^{s+2}\) for every \(s\ge1\).
For each admissible lifted degree with that ceiling, \(w=b_{k-2}\) therefore has a prime \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod J\) to odd exponent.
This holds for every \(k\ge3\) on the base norm itself, since any odd prime divisor \(\ell\) of \(b_{k-2}\) gives a root of the quadratic of order \(J\) in \(\mathbb F_{\ell^2}\), whose Frobenius is itself or its inverse.
Thus each such divisor is \(1\) or \(-1\bmod J\), and their product with valuations is \(-1\bmod J\).
The \(b_s\) for \(s\ge1\) are pairwise coprime, since the later iterates modulo \(b_s\) take the values \(-2,2,2,\ldots\).
