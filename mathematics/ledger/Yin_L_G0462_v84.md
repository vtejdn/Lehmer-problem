# The signed dyadic trace norm and an inert prime for one circle pair

<a id="sign"></a>
## The residue of the signed odd part

Let \(Q\) be the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and put \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), \(D=\deg Q\).
Inversion gives \([K:F]=2\), and the conjugates for the norm from \(F\) are indexed by the inverse pairs of roots of \(Q\), as in the [dyadic trace identity](Yin_L_G0462_v83.md#trace-norm).
For \(k\ge2\) write \(J=2^k,\ n=k-2\), and set
\[
         V_k=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{2^n}+\alpha^{-2^n}).
\]
This is a nonzero signed integer by nontorsion.
In \(Q\bmod 2\) let \(h\) be the multiplicity of \(X-1\), and let \(s\) count, with multiplicity, the monic self-reciprocal irreducible factors other than \(X-1\).
Then \(h\) is even, and for all \(k\ge 3+\lceil\log_2 D\rceil\),
\[
           v_2(V_k)=h/2,\qquad
           2^{-h/2}V_k\equiv(-1)^s\pmod{2^k}.                         \tag{1}
\]
The threshold depends only on the degree.

<a id="telescoping"></a>
## Teichmüller lifts and the sign of an inversion orbit

Embed a splitting field in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\), normalizing the valuation by \(v(2)=1\).
For each root \(x\) let \(\zeta_x\) be the odd-order root of unity in \(\mathbb Q_2(x)\) lifting its residue.
Indeed, in its residue field of size \(2^f\), each nonzero residue is a simple root of \(X^{2^f-1}-1\), so Hensel lifting gives existence and uniqueness.
Writing \(x=\zeta_x u\), the ramification index is at most \(D\), hence \(v(u-1)\ge1/D\).
If \(v(y-1)=t>0\), then \(v(y^2-1)\ge\min(2t,t+1)\) by expanding \(y=1+(y-1)\).
The lower bound doubles up to one and increases by one per step thereafter, so \(v(u^{2^n}-1)\ge2\) at the stated threshold and tends to infinity.

Exactly \(h\) roots of \(Q\), counted in the characteristic-zero splitting field, reduce to one since reduction retains their multiplicities.
They come in complete inverse pairs, with no fixed point since \(x=\pm1\) would be torsion.
For each such pair, \(y=x^{2^n}\) satisfies
\[
      \frac{y+y^{-1}}{2}=1+\frac{(y-1)^2}{2y}\equiv1\pmod4 .
\]
For any other pair its factor is congruent modulo four to \(\zeta_x^{2^n}+\zeta_x^{-2^n}\).
This is a 2-adic unit since distinct inverse residues in characteristic two have nonzero sum.

To multiply these latter terms group the nontrivial inverse residue pairs by Frobenius orbits.
Choose an odd-order lift \(\zeta\) in one orbit and let \(f\) be the first positive index for which \(\zeta^{2^f}=\zeta\) or \(\zeta^{-1}\).
With \(z=\zeta^{2^n}\), all denominator differences below are nonzero, and
\[
   \prod_{i=0}^{f-1}(z^{2^i}+z^{-2^i})
       =\prod_{i=0}^{f-1}
          \frac{z^{2^{i+1}}-z^{-2^{i+1}}}{z^{2^i}-z^{-2^i}}
       =\frac{z^{2^f}-z^{-2^f}}{z-z^{-1}} .
\]
The result is \(-1\) exactly when Frobenius first returns the oriented root as its inverse.
This is the case of a single self-reciprocal irreducible factor, since its ordinary orbit then has length \(2f\).
Otherwise there are two distinct reciprocal irreducibles of equal multiplicity, and the product is \(+1\).
Each unordered pair of nontrivial inverse residues has as many characteristic-zero pairs above it as the multiplicity of either residue.
The product, with these multiplicities, is therefore \((-1)^s\), independently of \(n\).
Combining the residue-one and nontrivial factors proves the valuation and the signed congruence modulo four, and even \(2^{-h/2}V_k\to(-1)^s\) in \(\mathbb Z_2\).
For \(Q=X^2-4X+1\), one has \(h=2,\ s=0\) and \(V_3/2=7\), so the sign modulo four can differ at smaller indices.

<a id="inert"></a>
## The full modulus and the prime field residue class

The [trace-prime norm calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v83.md#squarefree) gives the following local argument for arbitrary fixed bases, even before good reduction of the full polynomial.
For an odd prime \(\mathfrak r\) of \(F\) dividing \(\alpha^{2^n}+\alpha^{-2^n}\), write \(q_{\mathfrak r}=\ell^g\) for its residue cardinality.
At a prime of \(K\) above it the residue \(a\) of \(\alpha\) satisfies \(a^{J/2}=-1\), so its order is exactly \(J\).
The quadratic \(X^2-(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})X+1\) reduces with distinct roots \(a,a^{-1}\) because \(J\ge4\).
Frobenius of the trace residue field fixes \(a\) or interchanges it with \(a^{-1}\), giving \(q_{\mathfrak r}\equiv1\) or \(-1\bmod J\), respectively.
In the second case the reduced quadratic is irreducible and separable, so the local quadratic extension is unramified and inert.
In the first case the two simple roots lift by Hensel and the prime splits.
If \(\ell^g\equiv-1\bmod J\), then \(g\) is odd by reduction modulo four, and odd powering is an automorphism of \((\mathbb Z/J\mathbb Z)^\times\), so \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod J\).

Write \(w_k=2^{-v_2(V_k)}|V_k|\).
Factoring the principal ideal in \(F\) gives \(w_k=\prod_{\mathfrak r}q_{\mathfrak r}^{v_{\mathfrak r}(\alpha^{2^n}+\alpha^{-2^n})}\) over the odd contributors.
Thus \(w_k\equiv\pm1\bmod J\), and the sign is \((-1)^{\sum_{\ell\equiv-1\bmod J}v_\ell(w_k)}\).
Indeed primes outside that class contribute \(+1\), and in that class the norm contains \(\ell\) raised to its full rational exponent.
The signed odd part also belongs to \(\{1,-1\}\bmod J\), whose two elements differ modulo four.
The signed congruence already proved modulo four therefore proves (1) modulo \(J\).
In particular \(w_k\equiv-1\bmod4\) forces some rational prime \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod J\) to occur to odd exponent.
At least one contributing trace prime over this \(\ell\) has odd \(g\) and odd local valuation, and is inert in \(K/F\).
For a fixed base at all sufficiently large \(k\), every contributing odd \(\ell\) avoids \(\operatorname{disc}Q\), since the order \(J\) is at most \(\ell^D-1\) and there are only finitely many bad primes.
At those indices each matching inert trace prime has residue degree one over \(\ell\) and its extension has degree two, by the [Frobenius orbit calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v83.md#split).

<a id="one-pair"></a>
## Recurrence of the negative class with one circle pair

Suppose \(Q\) has exactly one nonreal conjugate pair on the unit circle, and write one member as \(\xi=e^{2\pi i x}\), \(0<x<1\).
Then \(x\) is irrational.
For \(k\ge3\) each pair of real inverse roots contributes positively to \(V_k\), since \(2^n\) is even.
Each nonreal inverse pair off the circle has a distinct complex conjugate inverse pair, and their two factors multiply to a positive squared modulus.
The sign of \(V_k\) is therefore the sign of \(\cos(2\pi 2^n x)\).
By (1), attaining the sign \(-(-1)^s\) forces \(w_k\equiv-1\bmod J\).
If \(Q\) has no circle pair instead, then \(V_k>0\) for all \(k\ge3\), so odd \(s\) forces an inert prime to odd rational exponent at every index in (1).

For the single pair write \(x=.b_1b_2\ldots\) in binary.
The cosine is positive exactly when \(b_{n+1}=b_{n+2}\), and negative exactly when the digits differ, since the two zeros are the quarter points.
Eventual equality would give a constant tail and eventual inequality an alternating tail, both impossible for an irrational number.
Thus both signs occur infinitely often and infinitely many \(k\) supply an inert prime to odd exponent in \(w_k\).
The rational primes obtained are unbounded since \(\ell+1\ge 2^k\).
In the full odd norm from \(K\), which equals \(w_k^2\), the corresponding rational valuation is twice the odd exponent.

Write \(M(Q)\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure of the fixed base.
If \(L\) consecutive signs starting at \(n\) agree, the first \(L+1\) digits of \(\{2^n x\}\) are either constant or alternating.
In the first case the distance to \(0\) or \(1\) is at most \(2^{-(L+1)}\).
In the second the distance to \(1/3\) or \(2/3\) is at most \(2^{-(L+1)}\), by their alternating expansions.
Consequently \(|1-\xi^{a2^n}|\le6\pi\,2^{-(L+1)}\) with \(a=1\) or \(3\), respectively.
The [Baker–Wüstholz distance bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle) applies to both powers since \(\xi\) is nontorsion.
Its two-logarithm constant is \(O(D^4\log(2D))\), the two modified heights are \(O((1+\log M(Q))/D)\) and \(\pi/D\), and \(\log(2a2^n)=O(n+1)\).
Comparison gives
\[
           L\ll D^2\log(2D)(1+\log M(Q))(n+1).
\]
In particular for some \(C_Q\) every interval \([K_0,C_Q K_0]\) of integer indices with \(K_0\) sufficiently large contains a \(k\) giving an inert prime to odd exponent.
