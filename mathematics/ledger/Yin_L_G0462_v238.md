# Counting factors that miss an exterior pole across all primitive directions

<a id="global"></a>
## A logarithmic bound on the total exceptional exterior count

Fix \(s\ge3\), put \(g(T)=T^s-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), and consider the sections \(g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\) for coprime positive \(a,b\).
Let \(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_t\) be the exterior zeros of \(B\), set \(I=\sum_j\log|\tau_j|\), and give \(\tau_j\) weight \(\omega_j=(\log|\tau_j|)/I\).
For large \(r=b/a\), the [pole-lift estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#poles) associates each exterior root \(x\) to one of the \(a\)-th roots \(\xi\) of a pole, with \(x=\xi\exp(u/a)\), \(|u|\ll_s|\tau_j|^{-r}\).
For a nontorsion monic rational irreducible factor \(P\), let \(k(P)>0\) be its number of exterior roots and \(h(P)\) the sum of their pole weights.

Across **all** primitive directions with \(R\le b/a\le 2R\), the sum of \(k(P)\) over factors that miss at least one exterior pole is \(O_s(\log(2R))\).
Here missing \(\tau_j\) means that no exterior root of \(P\) has \(a\)-th power near \(\tau_j\).
There is no restriction on \(a\) or on the number of factors in any individual section.

More generally fix an integer \(q\ge1\), restrict to \(q\mid a\), and project \(x\) to \(x^{a/q}\).
Each \(q\)-th root \(\lambda\) of \(\tau_j\) has weight \(\omega_\lambda=\omega_j\).
Write \(k_\lambda(P)\) for the number of exterior roots at this projected lift, so \(\sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda=q\) and \(\sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda k_\lambda(P)=h(P)\).
For fixed \(0<\delta\le1\), one has
\[
 \sum_{\substack{(a,b)=1,\ q\mid a,\ R\le b/a\le2R\\
       P:\ \max_\lambda|qk_\lambda(P)/h(P)-1|\ge\delta}} k(P)
             \ \ll_{s,q,\delta}\ \log(2R).                              \tag{1}
\]
The choice \(q=\delta=1\) includes every factor missing a pole.
For each fixed \(q\), every factor with \(k(P)<qt\) misses a projected lift, so the same bound applies to their total exterior count over all the indicated directions.

For example, at every fixed even \(s\ge6\) the [pole count](Yin_L_G0462_v230.md#poles) gives \(t\ge3\).
Thus the number of primitive directions in the entire dyadic window for which the negative real exterior root generates a Salem number after sign change is \(O_s(\log(2R))\), including first exponent one.
Such a factor has \(k(P)=1\) by the [real-root criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#real).
For the quartic seed there is just one pole, but (1) applies to all first exponents divisible by any fixed \(q\ge2\) and every factor with fewer than \(q\) exterior roots.
It includes factors without a real exterior root, beyond the [common-power Salem count](Yin_L_G0462_v221.md#count).

## One squarefree product for different first and second exponents

For an exterior root \(x\), put \(y=x^{a/q}\) and \(v=y^q=x^a\).
The section equation gives \(x^b=-g(v)/B(v)\), with both values nonzero since \(g,B\) are coprime and \(x\ne0\).
Bézout for \(a,b\) therefore recovers \(x\) from \(v\).
In particular \(\mathbb Q(x)=\mathbb Q(v)=\mathbb Q(y)\), and \(P\) maps to a monic irreducible polynomial of the same degree whose roots are exactly the projected conjugates without repetition.
The projected polynomial determines its original direction and factor.
Indeed, from any of its exterior roots \(y\) one recovers
\[
                      r=\frac{\log|g(y^q)/B(y^q)|}{\log|y^q|}.
\]
The reduced fraction determines \(a,b\), and the same Bézout formula then gives \(x\) itself.
Consequently projected polynomials are pairwise distinct even when both exponents vary or several selected factors come from one section.
They are reciprocal by the [large-slope reciprocity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#poles) and by preservation of all conjugates under projection.

Choose a finite collection of distinct factors from directions in \(\rho\le b/a\le(1+\eta)\rho\) inside \([R,2R]\), \(0\le\eta\le1\), and form the squarefree product of their projected polynomials.
Write \(D\) for its degree, \(W=\sum_P h(P)\), \(N_\lambda=\sum_P k_\lambda(P)\), and \(\varepsilon_R=\sqrt{\log(2R)/R}\).
The individual degree and measure estimates in [C401](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#poles) give
\[
 D\asymp_s\rho W,\qquad D\le(1+\eta)\rho W(1+O_s(\varepsilon_R)),
 \qquad m(\text{product})=\frac{IW}{q}(1+O_s(e^{-c_sR})).
\]
Indeed taking the \(a/q\)-th power multiplies each positive logarithmic modulus by \(a/q\).
The projection of \(\xi\exp(u/a)\) is \(\lambda\exp(u/q)\), so two exterior roots at the same \(\lambda\) have distance \(O_s(|\tau_j|^{-\rho}/q)\), and their product of moduli exceeds one by \(\gg_s1/q\).
Their reflected disk distance ratio is thus \(O_s(|\tau_j|^{-\rho})\), including pairs from the same projected irreducible polynomial.
Reciprocity gives an interior pair of equal ratio for every exterior pair.
The [reflected discriminant bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#collision) applied to this squarefree product yields
\[
 2\sum_\lambda(\rho I\omega_\lambda-C_s)N_\lambda(N_\lambda-1)
                 \le D\log D+2(D-1)m(\text{product}).
\]
The discriminant is a nonzero integer, and all collision constants are independent of \(q\).
Since \(\omega_\lambda\ge\min_j\omega_j>0\), division by \(2\rho I\) with relative error \(O_s(1/R)\), and \(\sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda N_\lambda=W\), give
\[
 \sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda(N_\lambda-W/q)^2
      \ll_s (\eta+\varepsilon_R)W^2/q+W\log(2D).                        \tag{2}
\]

For (1), condition on one lift and one sign realizing the defect, assigning each qualifying factor to just one such choice.
Then \(|N_\lambda-W/q|\ge\delta W/q\), so the left side of (2) is at least \((\min_j\omega_j)\delta^2 W^2/q^2\).
Split the slope window into a bounded number depending on \(s,q,\delta\) of intervals with \(\eta\) sufficiently small, and take \(R\) large enough to absorb the \(\varepsilon_R\) term as well.
In each group \(W\ll_{s,q,\delta}\log(2D)\), and \(D\ll_s RW\) implies \(W\ll_{s,q,\delta}\log(2R)\) by absorbing \(\log W\).
There are only \(2qt\) choices of lift and sign.
The bound holds for every finite subcollection, and \(h(P)\ge(\min_j\omega_j)k(P)\), proving (1) for the entire collection.

## Every factor in growing first-exponent families

For any individual \(P\), (2) with \(\eta=0,\rho=R=r\) gives, uniformly in \(q\mid a\),
\[
 \max_\lambda\left|\frac{q k_\lambda(P)}{h(P)}-1\right|^2
    \ll_s q\sqrt{\frac{\log(2r)}r}
              +\frac{q^2\log(2\deg P)}{h(P)}.                           \tag{3}
\]
The [primitive degree bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) applies to this fixed rank-two graph, irreducible by coprimality of \(g,B\) and linearity in its second variable.
Since \(\deg P\asymp_s r h(P)\) and \(\deg P\le sa+b\), (3) is bounded by
\[
          O_s\!\left(q\sqrt{\frac{\log(2r)}r}+
               \frac{q^2(\log b)^4}{a(\log\log b)^3}\right)              \tag{4}
\]
for sufficiently large \(r\).
Thus when the two terms tend to zero every actual factor occupies all \(qt\) projected lifts with \(k_\lambda(P)=(1+o_s(1))h(P)/q\) uniformly over its lifts and over the factors.
For \(q=1\), any factor missing a pole must in fact have
\[
                     a\ll_s\frac{(\log(2r))^4}{(\log\log(3r))^3}.
\]
Indeed (1) with \(R=r\) gives \(h(P)\ll_s\log(2r)\), and the primitive degree bound gives \(a\ll_s \log(2r)(\log b/\log\log b)^3\).
It follows first that \(\log a=O_s(\log\log b)\), so \(\log r=\log b-\log a\sim\log b\), proving the assertion.

An explicit primitive family outside the first-exponent range of the [density-one theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#statement) is
\[
 a=2^{2j},\qquad b=a^2+1,\qquad q=2^{\,j-\lceil 2\log_2 j\rceil}
\]
for sufficiently large integers \(j\).
Here \(r\sim a\), \(q\asymp\sqrt a/(\log a)^2\), and (4) tends to zero, its second term being \(O_s((\log\log a)^{-3})\).
Every nontorsion rational factor therefore receives exterior roots over **every** one of these \(qt\) projected lifts, with the relative count in (3) tending uniformly to zero.
The estimates allow distinct balanced factors, including ones with \(h(P)=o(a)\), so this example settles their distribution across the specified lifts while leaving full irreducibility and an individual positive measure gap open.

An agent for Yin developed the common projection for arbitrary exterior allocations and the global count of factors with unbalanced projected lifts.
