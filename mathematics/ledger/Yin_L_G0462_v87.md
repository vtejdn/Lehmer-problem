# Uniform Pisot excess for six-term branch-two trace lifts

<a id="statement"></a>
## All integer coefficients and both offsets

On the positive-constant Pisot branch \(j=2\), every actual trace numerator
\[
       C(x)=2U_h(x/2)+aU_{h-d}(x/2)+bU_{h-e}(x/2),
       \qquad 1\le d<e\le h,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb Z ,
\]
has \(q\) bounded by an absolute constant and \(\theta-q\) bounded below by an absolute positive constant.
Here \(U_n\) denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind and both parities of the original polynomial are allowed.
Equivalently the exclusion covers all branch-two trace lifts with at most seven nonzero terms, since the lift is anti-reciprocal with zero midpoint and each lower offset has exactly one reflected partner.
The [four-term theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v15.md#statement) covers degenerate coefficients and smaller degrees.
The [fixed-branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) makes \(q\to\infty\) equivalent to vanishing excess along a sequence on this branch.

Suppose a six-term sequence has \(q\to\infty\).
We first exclude an unbounded primitive pair by extraction with \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\), and then exclude a fixed primitive pair without a degree cutoff.
On the unbounded-pair subsequence the [six-term reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) gives \(e=o(h)\) and, after extraction, two signs \(a,b\) as the nonconstant lower coefficients.
Write \(A(x,y)=2+a x+b y,\ S=A/2\).
Through degree \(h\) the normalized trace lift \(\Pi/2\), where \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\), agrees with \(S(z^d,z^e)\).
Let \(\omega_i\) be its [inverse signed measure](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure), of mass one and negative mass tending to zero, and let \(\mu\) be any limiting probability of \((\bar v^d,\bar v^e)\) under these measures.

The two-dimensional part of every intermediate mixed row vanishes.
The remaining part is supported on a fixed finite union of torsion translates of proper subtori, which determine a cyclotomic multiplier of the actual denominator.
When \(e/d\to\infty\), the second fractional coefficient restricts the first-coordinate Herglotz transform to two explicit possibilities and gives the local support needed at scale \(e\).

## An integer Fourier transform on three circles

Use the Fourier convention \(\widehat\nu(r,s)=\int x^{-r}y^{-s}\,d\nu\).
For each nonzero \(g=(r,s)\in\mathbb Z^2\), \(rd+se\ne0\) eventually by the unbounded reduced upper offset.
Extract its eventual sign simultaneously for all \(g\), giving a translation-invariant order on \(\mathbb Z^2\) with both coordinate vectors positive.
Define a coefficient function \(J(g)\) by \(2\widehat\mu(g)\) on positive indices, one at zero and zero on negative indices.
The coefficients of \(B=SJ\) are integers on the full lattice, where the product denotes finite convolution.
Indeed at positive \(g\) its coefficient is the limit of \([z^{rd+se}]B^\sharp\), from \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\) with \(J_i\) the signed Herglotz transform, and the index is \(O_g(e)=o(h)\).
At zero or negative indices the assertion follows directly from the order.
For coefficient functions write \(P^*(g)=\overline{P(-g)}\).
Since \(J+J^*=2\widehat\mu\), the Fourier coefficients of \(|A|^2\mu\) are \(A^*B+AB^*\), all integers.
In particular \(\mu\) cannot be Haar probability on the two-torus, since then \(J\) is supported only at zero and \(B=S\) has two half-integer coefficients.

For any sequence of positive integers \(t=t_i\) with \(e/t\to0,\ t/h\to0\), take any further weak limit \(\Omega\) of the laws of \((x,y,u)=(\bar v^d,\bar v^e,v^t)\).
It projects to \(\mu\).
For \(k>0\) let \(\nu_k\) be the projection of \(A(x,y)u^k\,d\Omega\) onto \((x,y)\).
All its Fourier coefficients are integers, being limits of \([z^{kt+rd+se}]B^\sharp\).
Here all three Taylor indices are strictly positive and within the prefix.
It follows that the Fourier coefficients of \(\Lambda=|A|^2\Omega\) are integers on every layer with \(k>0\) by multiplying by \(A^*\), on the zero layer by the preceding calculation, and on negative layers by conjugation.
The norm of this positive measure is at most sixteen.

Apply the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) to these measures on the duals of \(\mathbb Z^2,\mathbb Z^3\).
The inverse transform of a coset indicator \(1_{g+H}\) is a character times normalized Haar measure on \(H^\perp\).
Integer combinations give a finite decomposition into Haar measures on torsion translates of connected subtori with trigonometric polynomial densities, by splitting each closed subgroup into its finitely many components.
For a positive measure the combined density on each distinct such component is nonnegative, by mutual singularity of the different component measures.
For any integer Fourier transform, its full-dimensional density is an integer Laurent polynomial, since only singleton cosets contribute to it.
In particular this applies to the full-dimensional density \(H_k\) of \(\nu_k\).
The decomposition of \(|A|^2\mu\) also shows that its marginal \(\mu\) has only full-dimensional, circle and point components, since division is valid away from the sole torus zero \((-a,-b)\).
Its point components are at torsion points.

## Excluding surfaces projecting onto the full base

Among the components of \(\Lambda\) whose projections have two-dimensional Haar measure, a proper connected subtorus translate has an equation
\[
                u^m=\zeta x^p y^r,\qquad m>0 ,
\]
with \(\zeta\) a root of unity and \(m,p,r\) integers.
Indeed its annihilator has rank one with nonzero \(u\)-coefficient, whereas the only component of dimension three is the full torus.
Group these surfaces by their slopes \((p/m,r/m)\).
After division by \(|A|^2\), let \(f_j\ge0\) be the projected density for slope \(j\).
It is integrable, since the original measure \(\Omega\) is finite and each such surface projects Haar measure to Haar measure.
The missing fiber above \((-a,-b)\) has singular projection and does not enter the densities.
The full-torus term has trigonometric polynomial density before division by \(|A|^2\), so its conditional \(u\)-moments vanish above a finite order, also after division.

Take \(k\) tending to infinity through multiples of every \(m\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\) in the finite list.
The full-dimensional part of \(\nu_k\) then has density
\[
                   H_k=\sum_j x^{k p_j/m_j}y^{k r_j/m_j} A f_j .
\]
Each fixed Fourier coefficient of \(A f_j\) is a limit of integer coefficients of \(H_k\), by shifting to slope \(j\) and using Riemann–Lebesgue decay of the other \(L^1\) functions at the diverging slope differences.
Since the integer coefficients of \(A f_j\) themselves tend to zero, \(P_j=A f_j\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.
Reality of \(f_j\) gives \(P_j A^*=P_j^* A\).
The primitive linear polynomial \(A\) is irreducible in the integer Laurent ring, and its triangular exponent set is not a translate of its negative, so \(A\) and \(A^*\) are relatively prime there.
Hence \(A\mid P_j\) and \(f_j\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.
Any nonzero such nonnegative density has positive integer mean, here at most the total probability one.
Mean one would leave the entire marginal equal to \(f_j\) times Haar measure.
Every nonconstant Fourier coefficient then has modulus strictly less than one by strict triangle inequality for an absolutely continuous probability, so integrality forces \(f_j=1\).
This contradicts the exclusion of Haar marginal.
All the surface densities \(f_j\) therefore vanish.

Only the full-torus term can now contribute to \(H_k\), so the positive rows vanish above a finite order.
Let \(f\) be the two-dimensional density of \(\mu\).
Where \(f>0\), write \(H_k=A f c_k\) for the conditional moments of the last coordinate.
At a largest nonzero positive row, pushing these probabilities forward by the \(k\)-th power leaves only the first harmonic, giving \(|c_k|\le1/2\) by nonnegativity of its density.
Jensen's inequality gives \(M(f)<1\), since its integral is at most one and equality would require the excluded Haar marginal.
Here \(M(f)=\exp\int\log f\) on the two-torus, allowing zero for divergent negative integral.
Also \(M(A)=2\) by successive one-circle Jensen integrals, first fixing \(y\) and using \(|2+b y|\ge1\).
A nonzero integer Laurent polynomial has measure at least one by the same Jensen inequality applied successively to its leading coefficients.
Thus \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(A)M(f)/2<1\), a contradiction.
This proves extinction of the two-dimensional part of every positive mixed row.

<a id="singular"></a>
## A cyclotomic multiplier for all the remaining components

For each singular component of \(\mu\) choose a nonzero integer character \((r,s)\) with \(x^r y^s=1\) on the component.
This is possible on each torsion translate of a proper connected subtorus by multiplying a nonzero annihilating character by the order of the translation.
The extra possible point \((-a,-b)\) also has this property.
Put \(U_i=\prod(1-z^{|rd+se|})\) for this fixed finite list of characters, with empty product one.
Each exponent is eventually nonzero and \(O(e)\).
For every \(e/t,t/h\to0\), every further joint limit above the same marginal has \(\nu_1\) supported on this union, since its full-dimensional part vanishes and \(\nu_1\) is dominated in variation by \(4\mu\).
Consequently \([z^t]U_i B^\sharp\) tends to zero and eventually equals zero by integrality.
Indeed the finitely many shifts are Fourier coefficients of \(\nu_1\), combined with the product of \(1-x^{\pm r}y^{\pm s}\), whose signs are fixed by the extracted order and which vanishes on the union.
The conclusion also holds with any further difference \(W_i=1-z^{2(e-d)}\), since its shift is \(O(e)\).
By a diagonal choice of counterexample indices if necessary, there is a fixed \(L\ge2\) such that \([z^t]U_i W_i B^\sharp=0\) for all \(Le\le t\le h/L\) eventually.

## Two sharp first marginals when the upper offset dominates

Suppose \(e/d\to\infty\).
In the first coordinate rotate \(w=-a\bar v^d\) so that the first prefix factor is \(S_1(w)=1-w/2\), and take \(u=v^e\).
The first marginal has transform \(J\) with \(S_1J\) integral, by testing each fixed multiple of \(d\) before \(e\).
The [nine-profile classification](Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix) gives \(J=V/(S_1U)\) from the [explicit list](Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#profiles).
Its atoms have orders dividing six, and the remaining density is continuous after removing the atom kernels.
The continuous mass is positive in each row, as its tabulated nonzero density for \(2/J\) gives \(\Re J>0\) on an open arc.

Let \(\chi\) be the projection of \(2S_1(w)u\) times the joint probability onto \(w\).
At each integer \(l\) the exact coefficient at \(e+ld\), multiplied by \((-a)^l\), yields
\[
                   \widehat\chi(l)+(b/2)[w^l]J\in\mathbb Z ,
\]
with the Taylor coefficient zero for \(l<0\) and one at zero.
The atomic Fourier coefficients of \(\chi\) are periodic of period six and its other coefficients tend to zero by domination by the marginal.
Along negative multiples of six the limit is therefore an integer.
Along positive multiples the added term tends to \(b\) times the atomic mass of the first marginal, since positive Taylor coefficients are doubled moments.
The two limits differ by an integer and the atomic mass is strictly less than one, so that mass vanishes.
This forces \(U=1\) since every simple pole of \(J\) on the circle is an atom.

Now \(H=\chi_{\rm dens}+(b/2)J\) has only finitely many nonzero integer Fourier coefficients by Riemann–Lebesgue, so it is an integer Laurent polynomial.
Here \(J=V/S_1\) is evaluated on the circle and \(|\chi_{\rm dens}|\le |S_1|\sigma,\ \sigma=2\Re J\).
At each root \(\zeta\) of \(V\) the nonnegative smooth density \(\sigma\) vanishes to order at least two.
Thus \((2-w)H-bV\) has at least a double zero at every such root and \(V\mid H\) by integral Laurent division, since \(V\) is squarefree and monic up to sign.
Dividing the difference by \(V\) and evaluating gives \((2-\zeta)(H/V)(\zeta)=b\).
The rows left with \(U=1\) have \(V=1,1\pm w,1-w^2,\Phi_6\), and the norms of \(2-\zeta\) at \(\zeta=-1\) or a primitive sixth root are both three.
Thus \(V=1\) or \(1-w\), proving the two claimed marginals after undoing the rotation.
For \(d\ll t\ll e\) the [single-offset mixed-row extinction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows) uses only the coefficients at fixed \(kt+ld<e\), the first profile, and the vanishing negative mass.
It leaves only first-marginal atoms, now absent, giving \([z^t]B^\sharp=0\) eventually.
Diagonal extraction gives vanishing throughout \(L_1d\le t\le e/L_1\) for a fixed \(L_1\).
The [local Bessel bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#scales) gives bounded support through \(L_1d\), also directly for bounded \(d\).
The coefficients are bounded by the moment identity, so their absolute sum through \(\lfloor e/L_1\rfloor\) is bounded.

Both possibilities occur for the positive-measure and prefix conditions alone.
Put \(X=1+a z^d,\ Y=1+b z^e\).
The functions \(J_i=2/(X+Y)\) and \(J_i=2/(1/X+1/Y)\) have positive real part in the disk, are real symmetric and have constant one.
Multiplication by \(S_i=(X+Y)/2\) gives the integer polynomials \(1,XY\), respectively.
Below \(e\) their sampled transforms at multiples of \(d\) agree with \(1/(1+a w/2)\) and \((1+a w)/(1+a w/2)\).
An actual Pisot input additionally requires the full reciprocal denominator and its strict Mahler limit.

## Local support on the scale of the second offset

Put \(p=2(e-d)\), \(W=1-z^p\), and \(S_i=S(z^d,z^e)\).
On the extracted sequence we claim that \(WB^\sharp\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum through \(Ce\) for every fixed \(C\), and that the coefficients \(j_l=[z^l]J_i\) satisfy \(\sum_{0\le l\le p}|j_l|=O(1)\).
For the reciprocal of \(S_i\) we will use the bound
\[
              \sum_{l\le Ce,\ l\ge0}|[z^l]1/S_i|\le2(1+\lfloor C\rfloor).
\]
Indeed expanding in \(b z^e/2\) over \(1+a z^d/2\) leaves at most \(1+\lfloor C\rfloor\) powers, and the term of order \(m\) has total absolute coefficient sum at most \(2^{-m}(1-1/2)^{-m-1}=2\).

First take \(e/d\) bounded.
The [two-offset local support theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) bounds the term count of \(WB^\sharp\) on each interval of length \(d\), and the moment formula bounds its coefficients.
If \((e-d)/d\) is bounded away from zero, the same result holds for \(B^\sharp\), so the displayed bound and \(J_i=B^\sharp/S_i\) through \(h\) bound the initial sum of the \(j_l\) through \(p\).
In the remaining case pass to \(e-d=o(d)\), so \(p<d\) and \(j_l=[z^l]B^\sharp\) through \(p\).
If the first positive nonzero coefficient below \(d\) is absent or stays above a fixed fraction of \(d\), this initial sum is one eventually.
Otherwise pass to a first nonzero index \(u=o(d)\).
The [divisor and model calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#mixed) gives \(e-d=u\) or \(2u\), and through a fixed positive fraction of \(d\) the inverse series \(J_i\) agrees with \(1\pm z^u\) or \(1/(1\pm z^u)\).
There are at most five nonzero terms through \(p\le4u\).
These alternatives can be taken after extraction throughout the contradiction argument.

For \(e/d\to\infty\), the preceding hierarchy proof gives a bounded coefficient sum for \(B^\sharp\) through \(e/L_1\).
In that range \(J_i=B^\sharp/(1+a z^d/2)\), so \(\sum_{l\le e/L_1}|j_l|=O(1)\) for the nonnegative Taylor indices.
On a block of diameter at most \(e/L_1\), the Gram matrix of any distinct characters against the signed measure has bounded norm by its absolute row sums, since the nonzero difference moments equal \(j_{|l|}/2\).
For \(K\) characters the positive-part correction has norm \(o(1)K\).
Away from the six lift offsets, each coefficient of \(B^\sharp\) is a signed moment against twice the partial sum of \(\Pi/2\) at the active offsets, with a fixed bounded circle multiplier between consecutive offsets.
The [fixed-size Bessel test](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) thus bounds the number of nonzero integer coefficients in each block, by testing \(K\) larger than the fixed Gram and multiplier bound.
Splitting at the offsets and into blocks of this diameter proves bounded absolute coefficient sum for \(B^\sharp\) on each interval of length \(e\), uniformly in its location, and hence also for \(WB^\sharp\).
Multiplication by \(1/S_i\) and the displayed initial sum estimate now give \(\sum_{0\le l\le p}|j_l|=O(1)\) also here.

## Comparing the actual denominator without dividing by a circle zero

Set \(H=\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) with \(L\) from the intermediate-scale vanishing, and truncate \(U_i W B^\sharp\) through \(H\) to get \(Q_0\).
It has degree \(O_L(e)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum by the vanishing and the local support of \(WB^\sharp\), since \(U_i\) has a fixed number of monomials counting multiplicity.
The comparison \(J_*=Q_0/S_i\) agrees with \(U_iW J_i\) through \(H\).

We verify bounded variation for their positive coefficients, including the moving shifts in \(U_i\).
The series \(1/S_i\) has positive real part in the open disk and constant one, so twice its symmetric representing probability has its Taylor coefficients at positive indices.
Shifting this two-sided Fourier sequence by \(l=O(e)\) to represent \(z^l/S_i\) costs the incorrect constant and at most the absolute sum of the Taylor coefficients through \(l\), using conjugation at negative indices.
Each correction is a singleton Fourier coefficient represented by a monomial density.
The preceding estimate on \(1/S_i\) and the coefficient sum of \(Q_0\) give bounded variation for \(J_*\).

For the other series write \(m_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega_i\), so \(m_{\pm l}=j_l\) for \(l>0\) and \(m_0=2\).
The sequence \(m_l-m_{l-p}\) has a representing measure of bounded variation.
At \(l\ge0\) it agrees with \([z^l]WJ_i\) except through \(p\), where the absolute corrections cost \(O(1+\sum_{0\le l\le p}|j_l|)=O(1)\).
Correct precisely those nonnegative indices by monomial densities.
At every remaining negative index \(-r,\ r>0\), the resulting coefficient equals \(j_r-j_{r+p}=-[z^{r+p}]WJ_i\).
Through \(Ce\), the nonnegative coefficients of \(WJ_i=(WB^\sharp)/S_i\) have bounded absolute sum for every fixed \(C\) by the local estimates and prefix agreement.
Thus each shift \(l=O(e)\) from \(U_i\) needs only bounded total correction from the negative indices, giving the required representation of \(U_iWJ_i\).

Apply the [two-cutoff estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#cutoff) to
\[
        S_i U_i W B^\sharp-Q_0\Pi/2
                 = S_i(\Pi/2)(U_iWJ_i-J_*) .
\]
The polynomial on the left has degree \(O(h)\), the difference vanishes through \(H\) and its positive coefficients have a bounded-variation measure, and \(S_i,\Pi/2,Q_0\) have bounded circle supremum.
This gives \(\|S_i U_i W B^\sharp\|_1=O(1)\).
On the circle set \(X=a z^d,\ Y=b z^e\).
Then
\[
     |W|^2\le4|X-Y|^2=4(4-|X+Y|^2)\le16|2+X+Y|=32|S_i|.
\]
Consequently the integer polynomial \(U_i W^3 B^\sharp\) has bounded circle \(L^1\) norm and hence [bounded support](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window).
All factors of \(U_i W^3\) are nonzero cyclotomic binomials up to sign, so this polynomial has measure \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one, contrary to the [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This excludes the proposed unbounded reduced pairs.

## A fixed primitive pair of arbitrary degree

It remains to take \(d=mg,\ e=ng\) with fixed coprime \(1\le m<n\).
The [fixed-pair reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) gives \(g=o(h)\) and, after extraction, a fixed integral \(P(w)=2+a w^m+b w^n\) with \(|a|\le3,\ |b|=1\) and no zero in the open disk.
Its leading coefficient is a unit.
Factor \(P=EF\) in \(\mathbb Z[w]\), normalizing the irreducible factors to positive constant term.
There is exactly one irreducible factor \(F\) of constant two, occurring once, and all other factors have constant one and unit leading coefficient.
Their root moduli are all one by the product formula and absence of interior zeros, so they are cyclotomic up to sign by Kronecker's theorem.
For a unit-circle zero \(\zeta\) of \(F\), the reciprocal \(\zeta^{-1}=\bar\zeta\) would also be a zero by real conjugation.
Irreducibility would then make its reversal a scalar multiple of \(F\), the scalar being \(\pm1\) by reversing twice, contrary to constant two and unit leading coefficient.
Thus \(F\) has all roots strictly exterior, and \(E\) contains all the circle zeros with their multiplicities.
In particular all circle zeros of \(P\) are torsion.

Take a limiting probability \(\mu\) of \(w=\bar v^g\).
With the ordinary positive order on \(\mathbb Z\), define \(J\) by its positive coefficients \(2\widehat\mu(l)\), constant one and zero negative coefficients.
Then \(B=(P/2)J\) has integer coefficients by testing the actual denominator at \(lg\) for each fixed \(l\ge0\).
The marginal cannot be Haar since the leading coefficient \(b/2\) of \(P/2\) is not an integer.
For \(g/t,t/h\to0\), let \(\Omega\) be a joint limit with \(u=v^t\).
The projection of \(P(w)u^k\,d\Omega\) at every positive \(k\) has integer Fourier coefficients given by the limits of \([z^{kt+lg}]B^\sharp\), since all three Taylor indices are positive and \(o(h)\).
The zero layer of \(|P|^2\Omega\) has integer transform \(P^*B+PB^*\) by \(J+J^*=2\widehat\mu\).
The other layers are integral by multiplication by \(P^*\) on the positive side and conjugation on the negative side.

The same coset decomposition now applies on one and two circles.
The proper components projecting onto the full base have equations \(u^j=\zeta w^r,\ j>0\).
Vertical components and any mass lost where \(P=0\) project to torsion points.
Group the nonvertical components by slope \(r/j\).
Along large common multiples \(k\) of the \(j\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\), the full-dimensional projected density of \(P(w)u^k\,d\Omega\) is the sum of \(w^{kr/j} P(w) f_{r/j}(w)\), with \(f_{r/j}\) the nonnegative projected density of \(\Omega\) on that slope.
Division by \(|P|^2\) does not change the finite harmonic support of the full two-torus term in the \(u\)-coordinate.
The translated Fourier-coefficient and decay argument from the [surface calculation](#extinction) makes \(P f_{r/j}\) an integer Laurent polynomial for each slope.

Here is the divisibility step even when \(P\) shares cyclotomic factors with its reversal.
For each such density put \(H=P f_{r/j}\).
The rational function \(H/P\) equals its conjugate reciprocal as a rational identity since it is real almost everywhere on the circle.
Any pole in \(\mathbb C^\times\) must therefore be both a zero of \(P\) and the reciprocal conjugate of a zero of \(P\), so it must be on the circle by absence of interior zeros.
A genuine circle pole would contradict absolute integrability of the density.
Thus \(H/P\) is Laurent polynomial, and its coefficients are integers by division using the unit leading coefficient of \(P\), after clearing monomials.
The positive integer mean of a nonzero such density would force the entire marginal to be Haar, as in the two-base-variable case.
All these slopes vanish.
The remaining absolutely continuous rows vanish above a finite harmonic.
At a last nonzero row their integer Laurent density \(H_k\) would satisfy \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(P)M(f)/2<1\), where \(f\) is the continuous-part density relative to Haar, \(M(f)<1\), and \(M(P)=2\) by its constant and its root moduli.
Here the factor \(1/2\) again follows by the \(k\)-th power pushforward on the conditional circle probabilities.
Thus every positive row is supported on the marginal's finitely many torsion atoms, including the possible additional points where \(P=0\).
Choosing \(T\ge1\) divisible by their orders, we obtain \([z^t]\bigl(1-z^{Tg}\bigr)B^\sharp=0\) eventually for every such intermediate scale.
By diagonal extraction this holds throughout \(L g\le t\le h/L\) for some fixed \(L\).

## The first fractional coefficient for a fixed pair and the denominator comparison

The coefficient sum of \(B^\sharp\) in absolute value is bounded through \(C g\) for each fixed \(C\).
For bounded \(g\) this follows from the moment bounds.
For \(g\to\infty\) let \(D_0\) be the first fractional offset of \(P(z^g)/2\), either \(mg\) or \(ng\).
The coefficients \(j_l\) of the actual inverse series are integral for \(0\le l<D_0\) by recursive division of the integer denominator by its integral unit-constant prefix.
For positive \(t'=o(g)\) test the coefficient identity at \(rt'\) and \(D_0+rt'\) for each fixed integer \(r\), using positive \(r\) for the first index.
Every earlier nonconstant offset has integral multiplier and contributes an integer from an index strictly between zero and \(D_0\).
Every later offset is absent, since the offsets differ by at least \(g\).
The multiplier at \(D_0\) equals an integer plus a signed half, with the zeroth Taylor coefficient one when \(r=0\).
Consequently the doubled mixed moments satisfy exactly the [fractional-seam congruences](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#seam), and the [first marginal must be Haar](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar).
Hence \(j_{t'}\) eventually vanishes for each such choice of \(t'\), by integrality.
Diagonal extraction gives a fixed \(\kappa>0\) with zero moments throughout \(1\le l\le\kappa g\) eventually.
On intervals of this diameter the signed character Gram is the identity, and the bounded partial multipliers and the [Bessel argument](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#sparse) prove the local coefficient bound after splitting at the finitely many offsets.

Write \(V_i=1-z^{Tg}\) and truncate \(V_i B^\sharp\) at \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q_0\) of degree \(O(g)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
Put \(F_i=F(z^g)/2,\ E_i=E(z^g)\).
Then \(Q_0/F_i\) agrees with \(E_i V_i J_i\) through the truncation point, since \(\Pi/2\) agrees with \(E_i F_i\) through \(h\).
The Taylor coefficients of \(2/F\) are absolutely summable by strict exteriority, so those of \(Q_0/F_i\) have uniformly bounded absolute sum.
They can be represented at positive indices by a bounded-variation measure using monomial densities.
The coefficients of \(J_i\) through any \(C g\) have bounded absolute sum by the local bound for \(B^\sharp\) and the first \(1+\lfloor C\rfloor\) fixed Taylor coefficients of \(2/P\).
The symmetric two-sided moments of \(J_i\) thus allow every shift in \(E_i V_i\), all of size \(O(g)\), with bounded initial correction just as in the [comparison above](#return).
This gives a bounded-variation representation at positive indices for \(E_i V_i J_i\).
Retaining the entire cyclotomic factor in the polynomial comparison gives
\[
   E_iF_i V_i B^\sharp-Q_0\Pi/2
           =F_i(\Pi/2)\bigl(E_i V_i J_i-Q_0/F_i\bigr).
\]
The two-cutoff estimate bounds \(\|E_iF_i V_i B^\sharp\|_1\) by the bounded multipliers, the zero prefix of positive fractional length and the degree \(O(h)\).
The circle minimum of \(F_i\) is bounded below by a positive constant, so the integer polynomial \(E_i V_i B^\sharp\) has bounded \(L^1\) norm and bounded support.
Since \(M(E_i V_i)=1\), its measure is again strictly greater than one and tends to one, contradicting the fixed-support gap.
This excludes every fixed primitive pair, completing the proof.
