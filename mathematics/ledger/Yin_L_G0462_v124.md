# Positive moments through a fixed multiple of a second matching length

<a id="fejer"></a>
## The model prefix bounds the delay-two excess

Consider a sequence of positive delay-two excesses tending to zero as in the [signed first-discrepancy theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v29.md#statement).
Write \(p=n+1\), where \(n\to\infty\) is the first discrepancy of the signed seed quotient, and let \(a=\pm1\) be its sign.
Let \(\alpha>1\) be the multiplicative Mahler measure of the matched delay polynomial and put \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2\).
Use the [positive-real function and its representing measure](Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#positive), with \(K_0=1+t/2\) and \(K_j=2\int v^j\,d\mu\) for \(j>0\).
Suppose that the integer comparison series \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2F)\) agrees through degree \(q-1\) with one of
\[
             H=1+a z^p,\qquad H=(1-a z^p)^{-1},
\]
where \(q/p\to\infty\).
These are the two models associated to the [possible power marginals](Yin_L_G0462_v123.md#laws).
The coefficient agreement is a hypothesis on the entire prefix.
Then \(t=O(q^{-2})\), uniformly over the two models and both signs.

Write \(H_k=[z^k]H\) and, for integers \(s\ge1\), put
\[
               A(s)=s+\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}(s-k)H_k .
\]
The identities \(K=K^0+tJ/2\) and \((1-z)^2J=1-z^2-2zK^0\) give
\[
            K_j=H_j-t\bigl(A(j)-1\bigr)\qquad(1\le j<q).
\]
Expanding the nonnegative integral of \(|1+v+\cdots+v^{q-1}|^2\) now gives
\[
       t\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}(q-j)(A(j)-1)-q/2\right)\le A(q).
\]

In the polynomial case \(A(s)=s+a(s-p)_+\), where \(x_+=\max(x,0)\).
For the reciprocal model with \(a=1\), one has \(A(s)=\sum_{m\ge0}(s-mp)_+\).
In particular \(A(s)\) is comparable to \(s^2/p\) uniformly for \(s\ge2p\), as seen by retaining \(0\le m\le s/(2p)\) for the lower bound and counting all terms for the upper bound.
For the reciprocal model with \(a=-1\), the same sum has alternating signs.
For \(s=2lp+r\), \(l\ge0,\ 0\le r<2p\), pairing consecutive terms gives \(A(s)=lp+\min(r,p)\), hence \(s/2\le A(s)\le s\).
All four cases have \(A(s)\ge1\) for \(s\ge1\) and, uniformly for \(q/2\le j\le3q/4\), \(A(j)-1\ge c A(q)\) for some absolute \(c>0\) eventually.
In particular the polynomial with negative sign has \(A(j)=A(q)=p\) on this interval and \(p\to\infty\).
Summing over this interval gives \(\sum_{j<q}(q-j)(A(j)-1)\ge c' q^2 A(q)\), where the sum starts at \(j=1\) and \(c'>0\) is absolute.
Subtracting \(q/2\) leaves at least half this lower bound for large inputs, which proves the excess estimate.

## The integer comparison approximates all moments on the longer window

For every fixed \(C>0\),
\[
       \sup_{0\le j\le Cq}|K_j-K^0_j|=O_C(p^{-1/2}).
\]
Indeed the [Gram estimate at the first discrepancy](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#window) bounds the sum of \(|K_j|^2\) over every block of \(p-1\) consecutive positive indices by an absolute constant.
Covering the interval \(1\le j\le Cq\) by these blocks and applying Cauchy–Schwarz on each gives \(\sum_{0\le j\le Cq}|K_j|=O_C(q/\sqrt p)\).
Since \(t=2\cosh(\log\alpha)-2\ge(\log\alpha)^2\), the excess estimate gives \(q\log\alpha=O(1)\).
In the identity
\[
           J=\frac{1-z^2-2zK}{1-(2+t)z+z^2},
\]
the reciprocal denominator has \(j\)-th coefficient \(\sum_{h=0}^j\alpha^{j-2h}=O_C(q)\) for \(0\le j\le Cq\).
Convolution with the numerator gives \(J_j=O_C(q^2/\sqrt p)\) throughout the interval.
Multiplying by \(t/2\) proves the asserted uniform approximation.
In particular, if the bilateral coefficients are defined by \(h_0=2\) and \(h_j=K^0_{|j|}\) for \(j\ne0\), then \(h_j-2\int v^j\,d\mu\to0\) uniformly for \(|j|\le Cq\), by conjugation invariance and \(2\mu(\mathbb T)=2+t\).
