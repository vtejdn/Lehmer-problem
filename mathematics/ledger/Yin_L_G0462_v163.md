# The contribution of a single conjugate to the powered-order exponent

<a id="comparison"></a>
## The error is bounded by the original order

Let \(\alpha\) be an algebraic unit with monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\), and suppose \(\alpha^p\) retains this degree and has minimal polynomial \(Q\).
Write \(O=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), \(\overline O\) for the full ring of integers of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(a=\exp(\overline O/O)\), and \(s_p=\exp(O/\mathbb Z[\alpha^p])\).
At a prime \(\ell\) fix a normalized valuation in a splitting field and, with \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) for the conjugates \(\alpha_i\), define
\[
 w_\ell=\left\lceil\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}
       \big(v_\ell(\beta_i-\beta_j)-v_\ell(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)\big)
            \right\rceil .
\]
Each summand is nonnegative by the geometric sum formula, and all choices of the place above \(\ell\) give the same maximum.
Then
\[
   w_\ell\ \le\ v_\ell(s_p)\ \le\ w_\ell+v_\ell(a),\qquad
   \prod_\ell\ell^{w_\ell}\mid s_p\mid a\prod_\ell\ell^{w_\ell}.          \tag{1}
\]
In particular, outside the primes where the original order is nonmaximal the local formula is exact, including at ramified primes where that order is already maximal.
For a fixed input the discrepancy in the logarithm of the exponent is at most \(\log a\), independently of the powering prime.

Indeed set \(D=Q'(\alpha^p)/P'(\alpha)\).
The [conductor identity](Yin_L_G0462_v162.md#conductor) says that \(s_p\) is the least positive denominator of \(1/D\) in \(O\).
In the maximal order localized at \(\ell\), integrality of an element is equivalent to nonnegative valuation in every field factor over \(\ell\).
These conditions can be read at all embeddings into the chosen splitting field, so the smallest \(t\ge0\) with \(\ell^t/D\in\overline O\otimes\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}\) is precisely \(w_\ell\), by the products of differences in the derivatives.
The inclusions \(a\overline O\subset O\subset\overline O\) then place the local denominator exponent in \(O\) between \(w_\ell\) and \(w_\ell+v_\ell(a)\).
The product is finite by the [power-index identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) and the nonnegativity of the summands.
This proves (1).

## Which collisions affect the choice of a prime

At \(\ell\ne p\) pairs in the same original residue have valuation difference zero, since the geometric sum reduces to \(p\alpha_i^{p-1}\).
For pairs at different original residues the whole powered distance contributes.
At every \(\ell\) where \(O\) is maximal, (1) therefore counts the greatest sum incident to a single conjugate, rounding up if necessary.
If \(\ell\ne p\) and \(\ell\nmid\operatorname{disc}(P)\), the splitting extension is unramified because the simple roots lift from a finite residue field extension, so the individual distances are already integral.
Distinct isolated pairs can increase the total power index additively while the exponent at the same prime only records the largest incident sum.
For varying inputs the error allowance at the remaining primes depends on the original order; it cannot be discarded using the fixed-input bound alone.
