# Exact residue-order returns after removing a prescribed set of rational primes

<a id="deletion"></a>
## A size and cardinality bound for the removed primes

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit, of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(0<m=m(P)\le1\).
Suppose that at most \(\eta d\) of its roots lie on the unit circle, with \(0\le\eta\le1\), allowing \(\eta=1\) for arbitrary inputs.
Fix \(A\ge1,\ \delta>0\), and
\[
             \max(1/2,3\delta)<\beta<\exp(-\eta/(2A)).
\]
For a finite set \(S\) of rational primes put \(H=|S|\), \(L_S=\sum_{\ell\in S}\log\ell\).
There are constants \(c_0,c_1,c_2>0\) depending only on these fixed parameters with the following property.
For all sufficiently large \(d\), every \(S\) satisfying
\[
                    (H+1)^2L_S\le c_0 d^\beta\log d                     \tag{1}
\]
leaves at least \(c_1d\) composite orders \(Ad\le j\le2Ad\) for which
\[
 \ell\mid D_j\ \Longrightarrow\ \ell>d^\delta,\quad \ell\notin S,
 \qquad
 T_j=(1+o(1))\log D_j,\qquad \log D_j\ge c_2d\,m .
\]
Here \(D_j=|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|\) and \(T_j=\sum_{\ell>d^\delta,\,\ell\nmid j}v_\ell(D_j)\log\ell\) is the [exact-order contribution](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#statement).
The relative error is uniform also in \(S\).
In particular \(S\) can be chosen after seeing every resultant.

The ideal obtained by discarding all prime ideals above \(S\) from the [first-return product](Yin_L_G0462_v78.md#valuation), with this window and cutoff, retains log norm at least \(c_3d^2m\).
Indeed its log norm is the sum of the exact-order contributions outside \(S\), and the displayed orders alone supply the bound.
For \(A=\eta=1,\ \delta=1/5\), any fixed \(3/5<\beta<e^{-1/2}\) works.

<a id="orders"></a>
## Orders with a large prime factor before the additional deletion

Choose \(\beta<\alpha<1\) such that \(A\log(\alpha/\beta)>\eta/2\).
The [large-prime-factor count](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v1.md#orders) gives \(Ad(\log(\alpha/\beta)+o(1))\) orders in this window divisible by a prime \(d^\beta<r\le d^\alpha\).
Each such order is composite and its \(r\) is unique since \(\beta>1/2\).
Choose \(\gamma>\delta\) with \(2\gamma+\delta<\beta\).
The [small-characteristic exclusions](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v1.md#residues) remove only \(o(d)\) of these orders before the geometric restriction.
Specifically the rejected \(r\) divide products of \(\ell^k-1\) with \(\ell\le d^\delta\), \(k\le d^\gamma\), of total logarithm \(O(d^{2\gamma+\delta})\).
For the other \(r\), any such characteristic occurring in \(D_j\) uses a factor of \(P\bmod\ell\) of degree greater than \(d^\gamma\), and each factor accounts for at most two orders per dyadic window.
This leaves \(D_j\) free of primes at most \(d^\delta\).

The [two-sided primitive estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v73.md#circle) loses at most \(\eta d/2+o(d)\) orders at \(Y=Ad\).
The strict choice of \(\alpha\) thus leaves a positive proportion.
Discarding the orders with \(\varphi(j)<a d\) for sufficiently small fixed \(a>0\) costs at most \(O_A(a d)\) by the bounded mean of \(j/\varphi(j)\).
On the rest \(\log D_j\gg d m\).
The [discriminant and valuation bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#discriminant) removes the contribution of \(\ell>d^\delta\) dividing \(j\) at a cost \(o(d m)\) outside \(o(d)\) orders.
This local bound uses \(m\le1,\ \delta>0\), independently of \(\eta\) and the choice of distinguished prime.
Consequently we start with at least \(b d\) orders satisfying all the desired conclusions except avoidance of \(S\), for a fixed \(b>0\).
Every such order still has its distinguished \(r\).

<a id="budget"></a>
## Excluding the prescribed characteristics

Put \(K=\lceil C(H+1)\rceil\), with \(C\ge1\) fixed large enough that \(2/C<b/4\).
Discard the orders whose distinguished prime \(r\) divides the integer
\[
                  \prod_{\ell\in S}\ell\prod_{\ell\in S}
                                      \prod_{k=1}^{K}(\ell^k-1).
\]
Its logarithm is at most \((1+K(K+1)/2)L_S\).
Every discarded prime is greater than \(d^\beta\), and has at most \(O_A(d^{1-\beta})\) multiples in the window.
By (1) the discarded orders therefore number at most \(O_{A,\beta,C}(c_0d)\), at most \(b d/4\) upon fixing \(c_0\) sufficiently small.

For a surviving \(r\) and every \(\ell\in S\), one has \(r\ne\ell\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_r(\ell)>K\).
Suppose \(\ell\mid D_j\), and set \(u=j/\ell^{v_\ell(j)}\).
The reduction of \(\Phi_j\) has roots of order \(u\), so an irreducible factor of \(P\bmod\ell\) of degree \(f\) has roots of that order.
As \(r\mid u\mid\ell^f-1\), this factor costs \(f>K\) of the degree.
For fixed \(\ell\) at most \(d/K\) distinct factors have this size, even if the reduction has multiplicities.
The possible indices for any one such factor are \(u\ell^a\) with \(a\ge0\), of which at most two lie in the closed dyadic interval.
Over all \(\ell\in S\) this removes at most \(2Hd/K\le 2d/C\) further orders.
At least \(b d/2\) original orders remain, proving the assertion.

<a id="radical"></a>
## A lower bound using each rational prime only once

Let \(R\) be the product of the distinct rational primes appearing in the first-return ideal at this cutoff and window.
Write \(H=\omega(R)\), counting each rational prime once irrespective of its residue degrees, number of prime ideals or valuations.
Then
\[
       (H+1)^2\log R>c_0d^\beta\log d,\qquad
                         \log R\gg d^{\beta/3}\log d .                  \tag{2}
\]
Indeed taking \(S\) to be its entire prime support would leave log norm zero, so (1) fails.
In particular \(H\ge1\), and \(\log R\ge H\delta\log d\) gives \((H+1)^2\log R\le4(\log R)^3/(\delta\log d)^2\).
This proves the second bound.
The largest individual logarithm in the support is therefore at least \(c_0d^\beta\log d/[H(H+1)^2]\).

There is also a choice of distinct characteristics at distinct large-return orders when individual prime sizes are bounded.
Suppose every rational prime appearing in this ideal has logarithm at most \(B\).
One can select at least \(\lfloor c_4(d^\beta\log d/B)^{1/3}\rfloor\) different good composite orders with a different exact-order prime at each, for a fixed \(c_4>0\).
Take \(S\) to consist of the primes already selected.
With fewer than the asserted number selected, \((H+1)^2L_S\le(H+1)^2HB\) meets (1) by choosing \(c_4\) small.
The theorem leaves linearly many good orders avoiding those primes altogether, each with \(T_j>0\).
Their number exceeds the number already selected, since \(B\ge\delta\log d\) and \(\beta<1\).
Choosing a fresh order and one contributing prime proves the claim by induction.
The lower bound \(c_2dm\) concerns the whole resultant at each selected order and allows contributions from several primes.
