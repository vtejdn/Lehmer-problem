# Half mass on complete Chebyshev grids forces the ordinary midpoint weights

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Positive weights constant on each complete fiber

Let \(Q_0\) be the monic trace minimal polynomial of a fixed Salem number \(\sigma\), and let \(s=\deg Q_0\).
Use \(C_j(2\cos t)=2\cos(jt)\) and \(V_j(2\cos t)=\sin((j+1)t)/\sin t\).
Consider a full return with trace polynomial \(Q_0(x)P(C_m(x))\), where \(P\) is monic integral of degree \(N\ge1\), all its roots \(b\) are simple and in \((-2,2)\), and the new and old traces are disjoint.
Assume \(m>2s\) and an integral inverse polynomial \(R\) gives a strictly positive branch weight \(w_b/m\) at each of the \(m\) preimages of \(b\).
Weights can differ between fibers and \(P,N\) may depend on \(m\).
Then the mass \(S=\sum_b w_b\) on the new grid is a positive half-integer.
If \(S=1/2\), then \(P=C_N\), the grid is the zero set of \(C_{mN}\), and all its weights equal \(1/(2mN)\).

Let \(D\) have degree \(<N\) and interpolate \(D(b)=w_b P'(b)\).
Its leading coefficient in degree \(N-1\) is \(S\) by Lagrange interpolation.
By the [branch residue formula](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches) and \(C_m'=mV_{m-1}\), the weights require
\[
       (2-x)R(x)\equiv Q_0(x)V_{m-1}(x)D(C_m(x))\pmod {P(C_m(x))}.       \tag{1}
\]
Use \(y=C_m(x)\).
Over \(\mathbb Z[y]/P\) the quotient by \(C_m(x)-y\) has the integral basis \(V_0(x),\ldots,V_{m-1}(x)\), by monicity and triangular change from the power basis.
Over the reals the same basis identifies each coefficient uniquely.
Write \(Q_0=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^s a_j C_j\) with integers \(a_j\) and \(a_s=1\).
By the trigonometric definitions,
\[
 Q_0V_{m-1}=a_0V_{m-1}
       +\sum_{j=1}^s a_j\bigl(yV_{j-1}+2V_{m-j-1}\bigr).
\]
The high and low basis indices are disjoint since \(m>2s\).
The coefficients of \(V_{m-s-1}\) and \(V_{s-1}\) in (1) therefore give integral classes \(2D(y)\) and \(yD(y)\) modulo \(P(y)\).
Since \(\deg D<N\), \(G=2D\) itself is integral and \(2S\) is a positive integer.
When \(S=1/2\), \(G\) is monic of degree \(N-1\) and \(yG-P\) has even coefficients by the second class condition.
The residues \(G(b)/P'(b)=2w_b\) are positive and sum to one.

<a id="interlacing"></a>
## The interlacing pair specified by the parity condition

Suppose \(P,G\) are monic as above, \(P\equiv yG\bmod2\), and all residues of \(G/P\) are positive.
Then \(P=C_N,\ G=V_{N-1}\).
For \(N=1\), \(G=1\) and the unique root of \(P\) is an even integer strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), hence zero.
For \(N\ge2\), the partial fractions of \(G/P\) give strict interlacing.
Starting with \(P_0=P,\ P_1=G\), real Euclidean division gives
\[
    P_{j-1}=(y-d_j)P_j-e_j P_{j+1}\quad(1\le j<N),\qquad
    P_{N-1}=y-d_N,\quad P_N=1 .
\]
Here each \(P_j\) is monic of degree \(N-j\) and \(e_j>0\).
Indeed the negative remainder has alternating nonzero signs at the roots of \(P_j\) and is positive at its largest root, by the interlacing with \(P_{j-1}\).
It consequently has full degree \(N-j-1\), positive leading coefficient, and roots strictly interlacing those of \(P_j\), proving the assertion inductively.
The symmetric tridiagonal matrix \(J\) with diagonal \(d_j\) and adjacent entries \(\sqrt{e_j}\) has characteristic polynomial \(P\), by expansion of the trailing principal determinants.
Thus \(2I\pm J\) are positive definite.

The parity condition gives \(d_1\) even, and \(|d_1|<2\) yields \(d_1=0\).
It also gives \(e_1\) even, as the leading coefficient of \(yG-P\).
The upper left entry of \(J^2\) is \(e_1<4\) since all eigenvalues of \(J\) lie in \((-2,2)\), so \(e_1=2\) and \(P_2=(yG-P)/2\) is integral.
Then \(d_2\) is integral by monic division, including the terminal case \(N=2\).
The order-two leading minors of \(2I\pm J\) equal \(2(2\pm d_2)-2>0\).
Hence \(d_2=0\), and the leading minors of orders one and two are all equal to two.
At each successive order \(j\ge3\), if both preceding minors are two, positivity gives
\[
                      2(2\pm d_j)-2e_{j-1}>0 .
\]
The positive \(e_{j-1}\) is an integer, since it is the leading coefficient of the negative remainder on dividing \(P_{j-2}\) by \(P_{j-1}\), both already integral.
The two inequalities give \(e_{j-1}<2\), hence \(e_{j-1}=1\).
The remainder then makes \(P_j\) integral, so \(d_j\) is integral by the next monic quotient or the terminal linear polynomial.
The same inequalities force \(d_j=0\), and the two new minors again equal two.
This proves the induction.
The leading characteristic determinants now start with \(y,\ y^2-2\) and satisfy the recurrence of \(C_j\), giving \(P=C_N\).
Removing the first row and column leaves the ordinary path recurrence with initial polynomials \(1,y\), giving \(G=V_{N-1}\).
As \(C_N'=N V_{N-1}\), the residues give \(w_b=1/(2N)\), and \(C_N(C_m)=C_{mN}\) proves the assertion about the entire grid.

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## The remaining central congruence prevents equality for every grid size

Put \(A=Q_0(0)\) and assume \(A\) is even.
For the zeros of \(C_k\), \(k\ge2\), an integral polynomial can interpolate uniform weights \(1/(2k)\) precisely when
\[
                 J_0(2)(k-1)+(A/2)k\equiv0\pmod2,\qquad
                 J_0(x)=\frac{Q_0(x)-A}{x}.                            \tag{2}
\]
This criterion concerns the interpolation on the new nodes alone.
Indeed (1) specializes to \((2-x)R\equiv Q_0V_{k-1}/2\bmod C_k\).
Using \(xV_{k-1}=C_k+2V_{k-2}\), its right side has the integral representative \(S_k=J_0 V_{k-2}+(A/2)V_{k-1}\).
Evaluation at two gives the necessity of \(2\mid S_k(2)\), since \(C_k(2)=2\) and the monic divisor gives an integral quotient.
Conversely \((S_k-S_k(2)C_k/2)/(2-x)\) is an integral interpolant whenever \(S_k(2)\) is even.
The values \(V_j(2)=j+1\) give (2).
For the remaining case \(A\equiv2\bmod4\) and \(Q'_0(0)\) odd, \(J_0(2)\equiv Q'_0(0)\) is odd and (2) fails for every \(k\).
Consequently **every complete-fiber grid above has mass at least one** for this fixed input, even when its positive weights vary across the base points.

<a id="independent-grids"></a>
## Unrelated midpoint grid sizes

Suppose instead that all the new nodes are the disjoint zero sets of \(C_{k_1},\ldots,C_{k_l}\) with distinct \(s<k_1<\cdots<k_l\), and that each set carries constant positive weights \(u_i/k_i\).
Then \(2u_i\) is a positive integer for each \(i\), without a common divisor condition on the \(k_i\).
The [integer moment tests](Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#moments) give \(\int Q_0 W\,d\nu\in\mathbb Z\) for every integral test \(W\).
Old traces contribute zero.
On the zeros of \(C_k\), the uniform probability mean of \(C_n\), \(n>0\), is zero unless \(2k\mid n\), when it is \(2(-1)^{n/(2k)}\), by summing the midpoint cosines.
For group \(i\) take \(W=C_{2k_i-s}\).
In \(Q_0W\) the frequencies run from \(2k_i-2s>0\) through \(2k_i\), and the top coefficient is one by monicity.
The larger groups contribute zero and the current group contributes \(-2u_i\).
Each earlier group contributes an integer once \(2u_j\in\mathbb Z\) for \(j<i\).
Induction proves the assertion and bounds total new mass below by \(l/2\).
When \(l=1\) the excluded parity in (2) again raises the lower bound to one.

<a id="nonuniform"></a>
## A separation estimate without restrictions on individual midpoint weights

For the fixed input \(A\equiv2\bmod4,\ Q'_0(0)\) odd, let \(\mu\) be the actual branch measure restricted to a single new grid of zeros of \(C_k\), \(k\ge2\), with arbitrary positive weights \(c_y\).
Set \(\mu_k^0=(2k)^{-1}\sum_y\delta_y\) and \(M=\max_{[-2,2]}|Q_0|\).
Then
\[
                    \sum_y|c_y-1/(2k)|\ \ge\ \frac1{2M}.              \tag{3}
\]
In fact \(\mu_k^0\) passes all the same integer tests \(\int Q_0 W\) as \(\mu\).
The constant coefficient of \(Q_0W\) in the basis \(1,C_1,C_2,\ldots\) is even since \(C_j(0)\) is even and \(Q_0(0)\) is even.
All other basis elements have even integral midpoint means.
If the left side of (3) were smaller than \(1/(2M)\), every integer difference tested against \(Q_0 C_j\) would have absolute value below one, since \(\|C_j\|_\infty=2\).
The same holds for \(Q_0\) alone.
The differences therefore vanish for all polynomial tests after multiplication by \(Q_0\).
Since \(Q_0\) is nonzero at each new node, interpolation forces \(\mu=\mu_k^0\), contrary to (2).

If actual full branch masses on these single grids tend to \(1/2\) as \(k\to\infty\) at the square constants, then necessarily
\[
                               \log\sigma\ge \frac1{2M^2}.               \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the [unique limiting measure](Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#fixed) is nonatomic, so the mass on the fixed finite set of old traces tends to zero.
The grid mass \(S=\sum_y c_y\) consequently tends to \(1/2\).
Writing \(G_k=k(\prod_y c_y)^{1/k}\), the branch formula, \(\prod_y(2-y)=2\), and the [midpoint discriminant](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v374.md#lower) give
\[
 G_k^k=\frac{2^{2-k}|\operatorname{Res}(C_k,R)|}
                   {|\operatorname{Res}(C_k,Q_0)|},
 \qquad \liminf G_k\ge\frac1{2\sigma}.
\]
The numerator resultant is a nonzero integer by positivity, and the denominator is at most \(2^{s-1}(\sigma^k+\sigma^{-k})\) by evaluating \(C_k\) at the old traces.
The relative entropy from uniform probability to \(c_y/S\) equals \(\log(S/G_k)\).
The [finite entropy inequality](Yin_L_G0462_v179.md#genuine) and the triangle inequality bound the left side of (3) by \(S\sqrt{2\log(S/G_k)}+|S-1/2|\).
Taking limits gives (4).
Estimate (3) allows oscillation of the individual weights even when their measures converge weakly to the target.
Realization on arbitrary nonuniform grids for all fixed inputs of the remaining parity is still open.
