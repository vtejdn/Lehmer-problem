# Uniform Mahler measures for the phase polynomials of Pisot units

<a id="uniform-phase"></a>
## One multiplier for a range of Pisot seeds

Fix \(1<\theta_0\le\Theta<\infty\) and an integer \(K\ge0\).
Let \(f\) vary over the minimal polynomials of real Pisot units \(\theta\in[\theta_0,\Theta]\) for which \(f\) and its reversal \(g(z)=z^{\deg f}f(1/z)\) are relatively prime.
Thus \(f\) is monic, \(f(0)=\pm1\), and all roots of \(f\) other than \(\theta\) lie in the open unit disk.
For an integer \(k\ge-K\) set \(k_+=\max(k,0)\) and \(k_-=\max(-k,0)\).
For either sign \(\sigma\) and every positive integer \(j\) put
\[
 V_j(z)=(z^{k_+}f(z))^j-(\sigma z^{k_-}g(z))^j .
\]
These are nonzero integral polynomials since \(f/g\) has a pole at \(1/\theta\).
With \(m=\log M\), one has
\[
             \frac{m(V_j)}j-\log\theta\ \longrightarrow\ 0
                  \quad (j\longrightarrow\infty)                       \tag{1}
\]
uniformly in \(f,k,\sigma\).
There is no bound on the seed degree or the positive offsets.
In particular every sufficiently large fixed multiplier \(j\) gives \(M(V_j)>2\) with a common strict margin.
The [fourth multiplier for the plastic seed](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v2.md#statement) gives an explicit optimal multiplier in that family.
Here the seed also varies, and the passage to an actual cyclic resultant has the separate quantifiers given [below](#delay-transfer).
The strategy-discovery contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained the uniform estimate on 2026-11-16.

<a id="analytic-normalization"></a>
## Removing the unique pole in the disk

Set \(a=1/\theta\), \(b_a(z)=(z-a)/(1-az)\), and \(B=b_a f/g\).
The only zero of \(g\) in the disk is its simple root \(a\).
The rational function \(B\) extends across this point and across the unit circle, where \(|B|=1\) by reversal.
The maximum principle gives \(|B|\le1\) in the disk.
Write \(A=z^{k_+}B\), \(C=\sigma z^{k_-}b_a\), \(F_j=A^j-C^j\), and use \(dt/(2\pi)\) on the unit circle for all boundary integrals.
The equality \(|F_j|=|V_j|/|g|^j\) on that circle, together with \(m(g)=m(f)=\log\theta\), gives
\[
       m(V_j)=j\log\theta+\int\log|F_j|,\qquad \|F_j\|_\infty\le2 .
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular (2) gives the upper bound in (1).

For the possible zero offset, write \(f/g=c_0+c_1z+\cdots\) at zero.
One has \(c_0=\pm1\), \(c_1\in\mathbb Z\), and \(c_1\ne0\).
Indeed, \(c_1=0\) would give \(B(0)=-ac_0\) and \(B'(0)=(1-a^2)c_0\).
The disk automorphism \(c_0 b_a\) has the same value and derivative there.
Composing \(B\) with its inverse gives a disk map fixing zero with derivative one, which is the identity by Schwarz's lemma.
This would make \(f/g=c_0\) constant.

<a id="uniform-proof"></a>
## The lower bound

If the lower bound in (1) failed, there would be \(\epsilon>0\), multipliers \(j_s\to\infty\) and allowed data indexed by \(s\) such that
\[
       \int\log|A_s^{j_s}-C_s^{j_s}|\le-\epsilon j_s .
\]
Extract a subsequence on which \(a_s\to a\in[1/\Theta,1/\theta_0]\), the sign and \(c_{0,s}\) are fixed, and \(B_s\) converges locally uniformly in the open disk by normality of bounded analytic functions.
Since \(k_s\ge-K\), after extraction it is constant or tends to positive infinity.
The functions \(A_s,C_s\) converge locally uniformly to \(A,C\), where \(C\) is a finite Blaschke product up to sign.
For \(k_s\to+\infty\) the limits are \(A=0,\ C=\sigma b_a\).
For a fixed nonzero \(k\), precisely one limit vanishes at zero, the other having modulus \(a>0\) there.
For \(k=0\), if \(|A|\) and \(|C|\) agreed throughout the disk, the open mapping theorem would give \(A=c_0 b_a\) from the values at zero.
But
\[
       |B_s'(0)-c_0 b_{a_s}'(0)|=a_s|c_{1,s}|\ge1/\Theta ,
\]
contradicting convergence of the derivatives to those of \(c_0 b_a\).
In all cases the set of points with \(|A|\ne|C|\) is dense, since equality of moduli on any open set would imply equality everywhere.

Define a finite positive measure on the unit circle by
\[
 d\nu_s=\frac{\log2-\log|A_s^{j_s}-C_s^{j_s}|}{j_s}\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}.
\]
Boundary zeros have integrable logarithms because each difference is a nonzero rational function analytic across the circle.
The measures have total mass at least \(\epsilon\).
Writing \(P_z\) for the Poisson kernel, subharmonicity gives in the open disk
\[
 \frac1{j_s}\log|A_s(z)^{j_s}-C_s(z)^{j_s}|
       \ \le\ \frac{\log2}{j_s}-\int P_z\,d\nu_s .                     \tag{3}
\]
For example one may first apply the Poisson upper bound to the continuous subharmonic function \(\log(|A_s^{j_s}-C_s^{j_s}|+\delta)\) and then decrease \(\delta\) to zero.
At every point with \(|A(z)|\ne|C(z)|\), the left side tends to \(\log\max(|A(z)|,|C(z)|)\).
Indeed the ratio of the smaller to the larger modulus stays below a constant strictly less than one there, so its \(j_s\)-th power tends to zero.
Fixing one such point in (3) bounds the total masses from above, because \(P_z\) has positive minimum on the circle.
Pass to a weakly convergent subsequence with limit \(\nu\), still of mass at least \(\epsilon\).
At the dense set of points of unequal modulus, (3) implies
\[
                        |C(z)|\le\exp\left(-\int P_z\,d\nu\right).
\]
Both sides are continuous in the disk, so the inequality holds throughout.
The finite Blaschke product \(C\) extends across the circle with modulus one there.
Hence \(\log|C(re^{it})|\to0\) uniformly in \(t\) as \(r\to1\).
Integrating the logarithm of the inequality over each such circle bounds its left side by \(-\nu(\mathbb T)\), since the angular mean of the Poisson kernel is one.
This contradicts \(\nu(\mathbb T)\ge\epsilon\), proving (1).

<a id="explicit-positive"></a>
## An explicit estimate at positive offsets

For \(k\ge1\) put \(b=(a+1/2)/(1+a/2)>1/2\).
At \(z=-1/2\) one has \(|A(z)|\le1/2\) and \(|C(z)|=b\).
The Poisson kernel there has minimum \(1/3\).
Using \(\log|F_j|\le\log2\) in its Poisson upper bound gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 m(V_j)&\ge j\log\theta+3\log|F_j(-1/2)|-2\log2\\
       &\ge j\log(b^3/a)+3\log(1-(2b)^{-j})-2\log2 .
 \end{split}
\]
The coefficient of \(j\) is positive for every \(a<1\), since
\((2a+1)^3-a(a+2)^3=(1-a)^3(1+a)>0\).
On the specified compact interval of \(a\)'s this yields an effective common positive slope and a uniformly bounded error.

## What transfers to the Salem polynomials

For each fixed eligible seed consider \(D_N=z^N f-\sigma g\) and the actual minimal polynomial \(S_N\) of its unique exterior root for sufficiently large \(N\).
The [circle argument](Yin_L_G0462_v24.md#all-delays) applies: comparison with \(z^Nf\) on the circle first after multiplying \(\sigma g\) by \(0<t<1\), then passage to \(t=1\), gives at most one exterior root.
At two fixed positive real arguments bracketing \(\theta\) and greater than one the signs of \(D_N\) eventually equal those of \(f\), so a unique simple exterior root tends to \(\theta\).
Reciprocity up to sign supplies its inverse inside the disk.
Both belong to the same irreducible factor by the absolute constant term, and all remaining factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.

For completeness the phase transfer uses only finitely many torsion roots for this fixed seed.
At a root of unity \(\zeta\) of \(D_N\), let \(G=g/f\).
According to the order of \(\zeta\) modulo four, a conjugate is \(\xi\zeta^h\) with \((\xi,h)=(1,2),(-1,2),(-1,1)\), as in the [torsion argument](Yin_L_G0462_v23.md#irreducible).
Consequently \(G(\xi\zeta^h)=\xi^N\sigma^{h-1}G(\zeta)^h\).
Each of the finitely many corresponding rational identities in the indeterminate \(z\) fails, since \(G(0)\ne0\) and \(G'(0)\ne0\) by the coefficient calculation above.
For \(h=2\) only the right side has a linear term, and for \(h=1,\xi=-1\) either the constants or the linear terms differ.
Thus the possible torsion roots form a finite set.
They are simple for large \(N\), since multiplicity would imply \(N=\zeta G'(\zeta)/G(\zeta)\).
In particular \(D_N=H_NS_N\) with \(H_N\) one of finitely many products of cyclotomic factors, \(d=\deg S_N\to\infty\), and \(k=N-d=\deg H_N-\deg f\).

For fixed \(f,j\), the [phase product proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v2.md#transfer) now gives
\[
      \log|\operatorname{Res}(S_N,z^{jd}-1)|=d\,m(V_j)+O_{f,j}(\log d).
\]
Indeed its phase polynomial at \(w^j=1\) is \(w z^{k_+} f-\sigma z^{k_-}g\), equal to \(z^{k_-}D_N\) at the nodes \(z^d=w\).
There are finitely many such polynomials for fixed \(f,j\), including both signs and the possible \(H_N\).
At a common torsion root \(\eta\), \(|D_N'(\eta)/H_N'(\eta)|\asymp_f d\).
The products at the other nodes use the same [lower bound for powers of a fixed algebraic number on the circle](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle) as the cited argument.
These are the finiteness and root estimates used in that transfer.

Choose, for example, any sufficiently large \(j\) that makes \(m(V_j)>2\log2\) in (1) uniformly on \(k\ge-K\).
For each fixed seed the resulting cyclic norm then exceeds \(2^d\) with an exponential margin for all sufficiently large delays whose actual offset satisfies that bound.
The multiplier depends only on \(\theta_0,\Theta,K\), while the threshold delay in this conclusion may depend on the seed.
The proof gives no transfer error uniform over diagonally varying seeds and delays.
For offsets tending to negative infinity the comparison functions \(z^{k_-}b_a\) converge to zero in the open disk, so the final integration against a nonzero finite Blaschke product no longer applies.
