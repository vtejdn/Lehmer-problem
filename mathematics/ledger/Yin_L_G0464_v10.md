# Positive-unit squares and exact moment ellipsoids at powers of three

Agent-S derived and self-checked this reduction on 2026-11-08 for Yin's [strict small-shell question](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
Fix \(p=3^m\), \(m\ge2\), \(n=(p-1)/2\), the [full odd trace grid](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product) \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\), and its monic denominator \(B\).
Let \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[t]/(B)\).
Fix the [integral representative](Yin_L_G0464_v8.md#integral-sign-representative) \(h_0\) of a strict nonempty negative-to-positive cut and write \(\Delta=|\operatorname{Res}(B,h_0)|\).
The allowed variations are exactly \(h=h_0u\) for units \(u\in\mathcal O^\times\) positive at every node.
They preserve the full principal ideal, all signs and the resultant.
For the lift use \(D=B_{n-1}\), \(C=Dh\bmod B\), \(Q=(t-a)B+C\), \(P=z^{n+1}Q(z+z^{-1})\), so \(r_j=\nu_jh(b_j)\), \(\nu_j=(4-b_j^2)/p\).
The [arcsine moment identity](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#prefix-cut) for a matching profile \(x\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\) gives \(a=2x\), \(K=\sum r_j=x^2+2\), and \(L=\sum b_jr_j=-4x\).
These moment values follow by integrating \(1,t\) against \((t-x)^2\) times the arcsine probability measure, whose moments of orders \(0,1,2,3\) are \(1,0,2,0\).
They are eventual necessary conditions for a positive shrinking sequence, with \(x\) chosen along its profile subsequence.

<a id="positive-squares"></a>
## All totally positive units are squares in the original order

The signature image of \(\mathcal O^\times\) consists exactly of the bit vectors having the same sign parity on all \(m\) conductor layers by the [unit-signature criterion](Yin_L_G0464_v8.md#arbitrary-input-lower-bound).
The \(m-1\) independent parity relations give image dimension \(n-m+1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
We check the square classes in the same order, allowing for the intersections between layers.

Take the homomorphism \(u\mapsto(\log|u(b_j)|)_j\).
Every layer product is \(\pm1\), as it is a field norm of a unit, so the logarithms lie in a space of real dimension \(n-m\).
The evaluation image of \(\mathcal O\) is a full lattice in \(\mathbb R^n\), by the nonsingular Vandermonde matrix of its monomial basis.
On a bounded set of logarithms all evaluations are bounded, so the logarithmic image has finite intersection with each compact set.
As a discrete subgroup of a real vector space it is free abelian of rank at most \(n-m\).

An element in the kernel of the logarithms restricts to a constant sign on each layer, since an element of its real trace field whose square is one is \(1\) or \(-1\).
Each layer trace polynomial is \((t-2)^{3^{\ell-1}}\) modulo three by the [layer congruence](Yin_L_G0464_v8.md#arbitrary-input-lower-bound).
Thus for a common integral representative of \(u\), each constant sign agrees with \(u(2)\) modulo three, and hence all those signs agree.
The injective evaluation map now makes this kernel precisely \(\{1,-1\}\).
Lifting a free basis of the logarithmic image gives \(|\mathcal O^\times/(\mathcal O^\times)^2|\le2^{n-m+1}\).
The signature map kills all squares and already has that many images, so equality holds, the free rank is \(n-m\), and the totally positive units are exactly the squares.
Every such unit has exactly two square roots in \(\mathcal O^\times\), differing by a global sign.

<a id="moment-ellipsoid"></a>
## An integer-vector test for the prescribed pair

Use the monic integral basis \(E_i(t)=U_i(t/2)\), \(0\le i<n\), and represent \(v(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}v_i E_i(t)\) by its coefficient column \(\mathbf v\).
The [odd-grid quadrature](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product) is exact through degree \(2n-2\), so the \(E_i\) are orthonormal for \(\sum\nu_j f(b_j)g(b_j)\) by semicircle orthogonality.
Multiplication by \(t\) in this basis has a real symmetric integer matrix \(J\) with off-diagonals \(1\), diagonal entries zero except for the last entry \(-1\), and all other entries zero.
This follows from the recurrence for \(E_i\) and \(B=E_n+E_{n-1}\).
The evaluation matrix with rows \((\sqrt{\nu_j}E_i(b_j))_i\) is orthogonal, and diagonalizes \(J\) with eigenvalues \(b_j\).
Set \(H=h_0(J)\).
For \(h=h_0v^2\) the two moments are
\[
                \mathbf v^T H\mathbf v=K,\qquad
                \mathbf v^T JH\mathbf v=L .
\]
The condition \(|\det v(J)|=1\) is equivalent to \(v\) being a unit of \(\mathcal O\), since \(v(J)\) is its integral multiplication matrix.
For the reverse implication, invertibility of that matrix over \(\mathbb Z\) produces an element mapped to \(1\).
This unit test is necessary because the quadratic equations also accept nonunit vectors in principle.

Choose any separator \(c\) strictly inside the prescribed sign gap.
Then \(G_c=(J-cI)H\) is positive definite with eigenvalues \((b_j-c)h_0(b_j)>0\), and
\[
                  \det G_c=|B(c)|\Delta,\qquad
                  \mathbf v^T G_c\mathbf v=L-cK .
\]
For a rational separator \(c=d/e\) in lowest terms with \(e>0\), use the positive definite integral matrix \(G=(eJ-dI)H\).
Its determinant is \(e^n|B(d/e)|\Delta\).
The exact search consists of all \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n\) on \(\mathbf v^TG\mathbf v=eL-dK\), retaining those with \(\mathbf v^TH\mathbf v=K\) and \(|\det v(J)|=1\), identified in pairs \(\{\mathbf v,-\mathbf v\}\).
It is finite even before imposing an endpoint or a whole-measure bound.
For example, with target \(S=eL-dK>0\), the coordinate limits \(|v_i|^2\le S(G^{-1})_{ii}\) follow from Cauchy-Schwarz in the \(G\) inner product, and every quantity on the right is rational.
A nonpositive target has no unit solution.
Conversely every positive-unit variation hitting the moments occurs, by the square-class result and \(e>0\).

Changing \(h_0\) within the same positive-unit orbit replaces it by \(h_0w^2\) for a unit \(w\).
The matrices \(H,JH,G\) then change by simultaneous integral unimodular congruence, via multiplication by \(w(J)\).
No reduction to independent logarithmic coordinates or assumption of independent signs is needed.

For the angular midpoint \(c\) the [odd-grid evaluation](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product) gives \(|B(c)|\ge1\).
The target \(S_0=L-cK\), when positive, is at most \(20\) over all five \(x\).
The volume in the original square-root lattice coordinates therefore satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{vol}\{\mathbf y:\mathbf y^TG_c\mathbf y\le S_0\}
 \le\frac{(2\pi \mathrm e S_0/n)^{n/2}}{\sqrt{|B(c)|\Delta}}
 \le(40\pi \mathrm e/n)^{n/2}.
\]
Indeed one can majorize the indicator by \(\exp(\alpha S_0-\alpha\mathbf y^TG_c\mathbf y)\), integrate the Gaussian, and take \(\alpha=n/(2S_0)\).
This upper bound is below one already for \(m\ge6\), when \(n\ge364\).
The mean number of lattice points in a uniformly translated solid ellipsoid equals its volume by integration over a fundamental cube.
Even that mean tends to zero here, while the actual problem uses the unshifted lattice, the equality surface, a unit determinant and a second moment.

## Returning to the whole measure and to positive mass

For each retained class recover \(h,Q,P\) and test \(P(-1)>0\), the strict left endpoint.
At every same-sign gap and in the interval from \(-2\) to the least pole the [original root matching](Yin_L_G0464_v1.md#positive-resolvent) gives at least \(n-1\) distinct trace roots in \((-2,2)\).
Each contributes two unit-circle roots of \(P\), leaving at most two exterior roots after reciprocal pairing.
Consequently for the usual companion matrix \(A_P\),
\[
                        M(P)=\rho(\mathop{\bigwedge}\nolimits^2 A_P).
\]
Over \(\mathbb C\) triangularization shows that the eigenvalues of this second exterior power are all pairwise products of roots of \(P\) at distinct indices, including multiplicities.
Its spectral radius is the product of the two largest root radii, and the second radius is at least one because of the matched unit-circle roots.
Thus the equality also covers a single exterior root, two real exterior roots of opposite signs, and \(M(P)=1\).
Direct root isolation of \(P\) or of the exterior-power characteristic polynomial decides comparison with any specified rational bound, including equality by exact algebraic comparison.
The two moment equations by themselves have no such root-location conclusion.

For a fixed conductor, cut, \(h_0,x\) and \(R>1\), let \(s\) be the number of retained classes that also pass the endpoint and the original \(1<M(P)<R\) test.
The positive-unit group is free of rank \(n-m\).
In a multiplicative basis, choose its exponents uniformly in \([-N,N]^{n-m}\cap\mathbb Z^{n-m}\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\) the event probability is exactly \(s/(2N+1)^{n-m}\), because the eligible units form a fixed finite set and \(h_0\) has no zero component.
At one conductor even allowing every exponent cannot give positive measures tending to one under the two moment constraints.
If a law instead gives positive mass to every allowed conductor, profile and positive unit, the event has positive probability exactly when an eligible class passes the strict whole-measure test at some conductor.
For arbitrarily small radii one needs such classes along unbounded conductors.
The [fixed-resultant sign construction](Yin_L_G0464_v8.md#small-norm-terminal-block), finite ellipsoid test and exponent-box formula do not ensure those classes exist.
