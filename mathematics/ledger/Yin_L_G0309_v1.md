### 2. Ordinary PC already permits a nonzero-endpoint cover

    T_w(x)=beta^k x+sum_(i=1)^k eta_i beta^(k-i),
    c_w=(sum_(i=1)^k eta_i zeta_j^(-i))_(j=1)^s.

Its neutral endpoint is A^k(z+c_w).
For concatenation w followed by v,

    c_(wv)=c_w+A^(-k)c_v.                            (1)

This follows term by term from the inverse powers and includes any intervening zero digits.
We assume precisely the already named condition

    (PC) For every x in[1/2,1] and real unit u in C^s,
         some original word w has |T_w(x)|<1
         and u dot c_w<0.                            (2)

No arbitrary negative amplitude or compact termination is included in this assumption.

Consider the fixed original exit v=(1,-1) at real zero.
It has endpoint beta-1 in(0,1) and neutral displacement

    e=(zeta_j^(-1)-zeta_j^(-2))_j.                    (3)

Each e_j is nonzero.
The whole orbit {A^(-h)e:h>=0} has zero in the interior of its convex hull after FINITELY many orbit points are taken.
Here is an elementary proof including the full joint scope.
For a real unit u, write

    f_h=u dot A^(-h)e
       =Re sum_j conjugate(u_j)e_j zeta_j^(-h).

All 2s numbers zeta_j and conjugate(zeta_j) are distinct and different from1; they are also different from-1.

    average_(h<N) f_h ->0,
    average_(h<N) f_h^2 ->(1/2)sum_j |u_j|^2|e_j|^2>0.

Indeed in the squared expansion the only constant-frequency terms pair a selected frequency with its own conjugate.
