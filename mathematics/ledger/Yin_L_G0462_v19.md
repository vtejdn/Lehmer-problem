# No finite reflection enlargement of the natural D4 block action

<a id="scope"></a>
## The tested absorption mechanism

On 2026-11-10, the main agent and an ordinary proof collaborator for Yin tested whether the actual D4 block symmetries could be absorbed by enlarging the positive reflection group on the same positive real space.
The [previous coordinate-mixing example](Yin_L_G0462_v18.md#overlarge-coordinate-group-obstruction) excludes one excessively large proposed generating set.
The argument below instead excludes every finite real reflection group containing the actual natural block action, without adjoining extra coordinate permutations to that action first.
This strengthens that particular feasibility conclusion; it does not modify the earlier fixed argument or its review scope.

For every integer \(r\ge2\), put
\[
V_r=(D_4^{\oplus r})\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R
       \cong\mathbb R^{4r},\qquad
G_r=O(D_4)^r\rtimes S_r,
\]
with its natural action by blockwise lattice isometries and permutations of the equal orthogonal blocks.
There is no finite subgroup \(W\subseteq O(V_r)\), generated by orthogonal reflections in real hyperplanes, such that \(G_r\subseteq W\).
For \(r=1\), the conclusion fails: \(O(D_4)\) itself is the finite reflection group \(W(F_4)\), as explicitly generated in [C886v4](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v4.md#d4-positive-group).

The assertion concerns the same positive space with its natural representation.
It does not exclude a larger positive ambient representation, additional neutral factors, a radical quotient, a Lorentzian canonical action, or a method that avoids containing the entire positive symmetry group.
In particular, it does not rule out a different way to improve the [actual word-dependent permutation cost](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v4.md#d4-permutation-cost).

## The natural block action forces an irreducible containing group

Let \(X\subseteq V_r\) be a \(G_r\)-invariant real subspace.
Every individual coordinate sign change \(\varepsilon_i\) belongs to the signed-permutation subgroup in its D4 block.
Consequently the coordinate projection
\[
\pi_i=(I-\varepsilon_i)/2
\]
preserves \(X\).
If an element of \(X\) has a nonzero \(i\)-th coordinate, then \(X\) contains the entire \(i\)-th coordinate axis.
Since every vector is the sum of its coordinate projections, \(X\) is the span of some collection of coordinate axes.
Permutations within blocks and permutations of the blocks act transitively on all \(4r\) axes.
Thus \(X\) is either zero or all of \(V_r\), proving irreducibility.

Suppose \(W\subseteq O(V_r)\) is a finite reflection group containing \(G_r\).
Every \(W\)-invariant subspace is \(G_r\)-invariant, so \(W\) is irreducible too.
Its reflecting normals span \(V_r\): otherwise their nonzero orthogonal complement would be fixed by \(W\) and hence by \(G_r\), contrary to irreducibility and the nontrivial coordinate sign changes.
Normalize both choices of sign of every reflecting normal to squared norm two.
They form a finite root system spanning \(V_r\), stable under the reflections of \(W\), and generate the actual group \(W\).
The accepted simple-root and diagram correspondence therefore applies in rank \(4r\), and irreducibility makes its elliptic Coxeter diagram connected.

The complete classification list now leaves only
\[
A_{4r},\quad B_{4r},\quad D_{4r},
\]
and, when \(r=2\), the additional possibility \(E_8\).
All other exceptional irreducible finite types have rank at most seven, and the dihedral family has rank two.
The order calculations below contradict \(|G_r|\mid |W|\) in every remaining case.

<a id="order-divisibility"></a>
## Prime-power divisibility excludes every possible type

The block action is faithful, with independent block factors and their permutation action, so
\[
|G_r|=1152^r r!.
\]
For a prime \(p\), let \(v_p\) be its additive integer valuation and \(s_p(n)\) the sum of the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\).
Counting the factors divisible by \(p^j\) in \(n!\) gives
\[
v_p(n!)=\sum_{j\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac n{p^j}\right\rfloor
       =\frac{n-s_p(n)}{p-1}.                           \tag{1}
\]
For the last equality, writing \(n=\sum_{i\ge0}a_i p^i\) makes the sum equal to \(\sum_{i\ge1}a_i(1+p+\cdots+p^{i-1})\).
In particular,
\[
v_2(|G_r|)=7r+v_2(r!)=8r-s_2(r).                        \tag{2}
\]

The classical coordinate models give
\[
|W(A_n)|=(n+1)!,\quad
|W(B_n)|=2^n n!,\quad
|W(D_n)|=2^{n-1}n!.
\]
For \(A_{4r}\), using \(s_2(4r+1)=s_2(r)+1\),
\[
v_2(|W(A_{4r})|)=4r+1-s_2(4r+1)
                   =4r-s_2(r)<8r-s_2(r).
\]
This is insufficient for the divisibility demanded by (2).
For \(D_{4r}\), using \(s_2(4r)=s_2(r)\),
\[
v_2(|W(D_{4r})|)=4r-1+4r-s_2(4r)
                   =8r-1-s_2(r),
\]
which is exactly one less than the required value.

For \(B_{4r}\), the binary valuation equals (2), so the ternary valuation supplies the obstruction.
Since the factor \(2^{4r}\) contributes no factor of three, (1) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_3(|W(B_{4r})|)-v_3(r!)
 &=v_3((4r)!)-v_3(r!)\\
 &=\frac{3r-s_3(4r)+s_3(r)}2
 \le\frac{4r-1}{2}<2r.
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{3}
\]
Here \(s_3(r)\le r\) follows directly from its base-three expansion and \(s_3(4r)\ge1\) because \(r>0\).
But \(v_3(|G_r|)=2r+v_3(r!)\), contradicting (3).

Finally, when \(r=2\), equation (2) gives
\[
v_2(|G_2|)=16-s_2(2)=15,
\qquad v_2(|W(E_8)|)=14.
\]
The remaining exceptional possibility is excluded as well.
This proves the claimed failure for every \(r\ge2\).

## Consequences for the attempted absorption

The original proposed simplification was to replace the actual positive block group by a finite positive reflection group on the same space, then reuse its positive reflections to absorb block permutations without a power.
The order obstruction rules out that replacement for every number of blocks \(r\ge2\), independently of the choice of finite reflection generators.
It leaves the existing radical canonical cover and its exact \(d\)-power estimate intact.
It also leaves every method that changes the positive representation, increases the ambient dimension or constructs the indefinite word directly outside this failure statement.
There is no conclusion here about the existence of small Mahler measures or a uniform gap for the unrestricted [G0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).

The proof developer checked the irreducibility, every valuation formula, the exceptional rank and the distinction between the actual group and the earlier enlarged coordinate group.
No computation or numerical group-order test was used.
The [separately arranged independent finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v17.md#finite-reflection-enlargement-review) checks this complete strengthened argument, every classification interface and the stated limitations.
