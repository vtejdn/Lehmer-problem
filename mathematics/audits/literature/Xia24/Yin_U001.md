# Almost-unit gcd bounds and polynomial-coefficient power sums

<a id="almost"></a>
## Theorem 3.3 outside a prescribed set of places

Use the [43-page arXiv v3](readme.md#edition).
Definition 1.2 on pp. 1–2 and §§2.1–2.2 on pp. 7–9 fix the following conventions.
Let \(k\) be a number field, \(S\) a finite set of its places containing the archimedean places, and \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)\in(k^\times)^d\).
The absolute values include the local degree divided by \([k:\mathbb Q]\), so their product on \(k^\times\) is one.
Write \(\lambda_v(u)=\log\max(1,|u_1|_v,\ldots,|u_d|_v)\), \(h(u)=\sum_v\lambda_v(u)\), and \(u^{-1}=(u_i^{-1})_i\).
The point is an almost \((S,\delta)\)-unit if \(\sum_{v\notin S}(\lambda_v(u)+\lambda_v(u^{-1}))\le\delta h(u)\).
For place sets \(A\) write
\[
  G_A(x,y)=-\sum_{v\in A}\min(0,\log\max(|x|_v,|y|_v)).
\]
At a finite place of ideal \(\mathfrak p\) its summand is \(\max(0,\min(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}x,\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}y))\log N\mathfrak p/[k:\mathbb Q]\).
At integral arguments the finite-place sum is therefore the normalized log norm of the gcd ideal.

Theorem 3.3 on p. 13 gives, for fixed nonconstant coprime \(f,g\in k[X_1,\ldots,X_d]\) and \(0<\delta<1\), a proper Zariski closed subset of the torus outside which every such almost unit satisfies
\[
 G_{M_k\setminus S}(f(u),g(u))
   <2(d^2\deg f+d\deg g)\sqrt{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^d h(u_i).
\]
The exceptional subset may depend on \(\delta,f,g,S,k\).
There is no condition on the values at the origin for this outside-\(S\) estimate. [Theorem 3.3](../../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=13)

## Lemma 4.1 and the local exponential rate

Write a nonzero algebraic recurrence as \(F(n)=\sum_j c_j(n)\alpha_j^n\) with distinct nonzero algebraic bases and nonzero polynomials \(c_j\) over a number field.
Nondegeneracy means that the ratio of any two distinct bases is not a root of unity.
For such a recurrence, Lemma 4.1 on pp. 23–24 says that, at a fixed absolute value for which at least one base has modulus at least one, \(-\log|F(n)|<\epsilon n\) for all but finitely many \(n\), for each \(0<\epsilon<1\). [Lemma 4.1](../../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=23)

At any fixed complex embedding choose a base \(\alpha_*\) of largest modulus and apply the lemma to \(F(n)/\alpha_*^n\).
The normalized bases include one and keep their pairwise ratios.
The triangle inequality bounds the logarithm of the normalized sum above by \(O_F(\log(2n))\).
Consequently \(\log|F(n)|=n\max_j\log|\alpha_j|+o_F(n)\), including ties at the maximum and the case in which the original bases all have modulus below one.
The polynomial coefficients can vary with \(n\) as specified in \(F\), and finitely many exceptional zero terms have no bearing on this limit.
The same argument applies at a fixed finite place, with polynomial values bounded above there by a fixed constant at ordinary positive integers \(n\).
The source proof applies the Subspace Theorem to the evaluated polynomial-coefficient terms and uses the nondegenerate zero-set theorem to remove its exceptional hyperplanes.

## Theorem 4.2 for two recurrences evaluated at the same index

Take two nonzero algebraic recurrences \(F(n),G(n)\) with fixed polynomial coefficients and bases in \(k^\times\).
Let \(S_0\) consist of those places at which **all** bases occurring in the two recurrences have absolute value strictly less than one.
Theorem 4.2 on p. 24 puts all but finitely many indices satisfying \(G_{M_k\setminus S_0}(F(n),G(n))>\epsilon n\) in a finite union of arithmetic progressions on which the two restricted recurrences have a nontrivial common factor.
In its coprime, torsion-free case the theorem gives only finitely many such indices for every \(\epsilon>0\). [Theorem 4.2 and proof](../../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=24)

More precisely, take multiplicatively independent \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r\in k^\times\) and nonzero coprime \(f,g\in k[T,X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_r^{\pm1}]\).
At \(T=n,\ X_i=\gamma_i^n\), group terms by their Laurent exponent vectors to form \(F(n),G(n)\).
Distinct exponent vectors give distinct bases whose ratios have infinite order.
The subgroup generated by the occurring bases is torsion-free.
Coprimality in the displayed ring implies coprimality in the recurrence ring for that subgroup, since a nonunit Laurent polynomial there remains a nonunit after inclusion in the displayed ring.
Theorem 4.2 thus gives \(G_{M_k\setminus S_0}(F(n),G(n))=o(n)\) over all positive integers tending to infinity and hence along prime indices.
If all \(\gamma_i\) are ordinary algebraic units, every finite place lies outside \(S_0\), so this controls the entire finite-place gcd.
For general \(S\)-units it always controls the contribution outside \(S\).
One can check coprimality over \(k(T)\) instead if common polynomial content in \(T\) is removed first by Gauss's lemma.
Restoring that fixed content contributes at most \(O(\log(2n))\) at finite places, by its height at \(n\), outside its finitely many integer zeros.

The proof on pp. 25–26 strips Laurent monomials and applies the almost-unit bound to the points \((n,\gamma_1^n,\ldots,\gamma_r^n)\) in a torus with one extra coordinate.
For \(r\ge1\) their height grows at least linearly, while the outside-\(S\) height in Definition 1.2 is \(O(\log(2n))\).
A nonzero polynomial cutting out a hypersurface containing the exceptional set evaluates to a nonzero nondegenerate recurrence after like exponent vectors are collected.
The Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem then gives only finitely many exceptional indices.
This is the extra argument that permits a pointwise asymptotic conclusion from Theorem 3.3.
Shared Laurent factors in the exponential variables require separate analysis, as do specializations with different indices or bases depending on \(n\).

The inspection on 2026-11-18 checked the conventions, the three displayed source results, and the exceptional-set and local-growth steps used for these applications.
The coefficient normalization, finite-place interpretation and Laurent-ring application above were checked from those statements.
The paper's proof of Theorem 3.3 and its underlying Subspace Theorem were accepted as external inputs at statement scope.

## Fixed hyperplanes and almost-unit equations

On 2026-11-19 I checked Theorem 2.1 on p. 8 and Lemma 2.11 on p. 11 in the same author preprint, together with the height and almost-unit conventions above.
Both results are used at statement scope.

For a number field \(k\), a finite set \(S\) of places and \(s\ge2\), fix at each \(v\in S\) a basis \(L_{1,v},\ldots,L_{s,v}\) of linear forms over \(k\) in \(s\) variables.
Write \(\|x\|_v=\max_i|x_i|_v\) for the place-normalized projective coordinates and \(h([x])=\sum_v\log\|x\|_v\).
At any fixed positive excess over \(s\,h([x])\) proportional to \(h([x])\), the points with
\[
  \sum_{v\in S}\sum_{i=1}^s\log\!\left(\frac{\|x\|_v}{|L_{i,v}(x)|_v}\right)
       \ge (s+\epsilon)h([x])+O(1)
\]
lie in a finite union of proper projective hyperplanes.
This is the number-field version of Schmidt's Subspace Theorem stated by Xiao. [Theorem 2.1](../../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=8)

For \(p\) nonzero coordinates, fix \(0<\delta<1/(p(p+1))\).
Among almost \((S,\delta)\)-units, in the sense of Definition 1.2 with \(S\) containing all archimedean places, there are only finitely many solutions of \(y_1+\cdots+y_p=1\) for which no nonempty proper subsum of the left side vanishes.
Here almost-unit height is measured on the affine tuple, including the coordinate \(1\) in the local maxima.
Xiao gives this result as a special case of Evertse's theorem and refers to that work for the proof. [Lemma 2.11](../../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=11)
