# Mahler measure with a prescribed number of real exterior conjugates

<a id="ber99-u001"></a>
## Bertin's \(\nu\)-Salem numbers and Theorem 4

On printed page 6 of the [published contribution](readme.md#edition), a \(\nu\)-Salem number is a reciprocal algebraic integer with exactly \(\nu\ge1\) conjugates outside the unit circle and at least one conjugate on the circle.
In this terminology *totally real* means that every exterior conjugate is real, and *totally positive* refers to positivity of all the exterior conjugates.
Reciprocity gives \(\nu\) interior conjugates, so the degree has the form \(d=2\nu+2u\), with \(u\) the number of pairs on the unit circle.

Let \(M\) be the multiplicative Mahler measure, the product of the moduli of the exterior conjugates, and write \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
Theorem 4 states that \(M\ge C(\nu,u)=\phi^{\,\nu\,2^{-u/\nu}}\) when all the exterior conjugates are real.
When all are positive it gives \(M\ge C(\nu,u)^2\).
Thus for two real exterior conjugates the bound on \(\log M\) is \(2(\log\phi)\,2^{-(d-4)/4}\), doubled under the positivity hypothesis.
This decreases exponentially in \(d\), while [Voutier's lower bound](../Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) is of order \((\log\log d/\log d)^3\).

The definition, the theorem and the low-degree corollary were inspected on the printed page, including the exponent of \(2\) in the denominator.
The argument from the earlier Corollary 0 and the computational tables used in the low-degree corollary were outside this inspection.
