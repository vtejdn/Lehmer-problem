# Corner coefficients and edge roots of the eigenvalue polynomial

Section 1 of the [published paper](readme.md) starts with a compact three-manifold with one torus boundary and a peripheral basis.
The two coordinates are simultaneous eigenvalues of the peripheral matrices in an \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)\) representation.
The primitive integral \(A\)-polynomial defines the union of the one-dimensional eigenvalue images, up to sign and normalization by monomials.

Theorem 2.5 on pp. 234–235 gives coefficient \(+1\) or \(-1\) at every vertex of its Newton polygon.
Theorem 2.9 on p. 237 states that every root of every knot \(A\)-polynomial edge polynomial is a root of unity.
In its proof, vertex coefficients give monicity up to sign, Lemma 2.7 puts every conjugate on the unit circle via a peripheral volume identity, and Lemma 2.8 applies Kronecker's argument.
Theorem 2.6 on pp. 235–236 proves that a core eigenvalue is an algebraic unit for an irreducible filled representation nontrivial on the boundary torus and a non-boundary filling slope.
The proof uses the eigenvalue's minimal polynomial as a divisor of the specialized reciprocal \(A\)-polynomial.
The statements and these deductions were inspected, without a separate proof audit of the valuation and volume inputs.
For the formulation for one-cusped hyperbolic manifolds in dilation coordinates see [Garoufalidis–Jeon, Theorem 2.2](../GJ24/Yin_U001.md#newton).
