# Simultaneous exceptional powers of Salem units

<a id="fixed-list"></a>
## Prescribed exponents

In the [September 2023 preprint, version 2](readme.md#editions), Toufik Zaïmi's Theorem 2 on p. 2 gives the following result.
For every positive integer \(n\), infinitely many even degrees each admit infinitely many Salem numbers \(\alpha\) for which \(\alpha^n-1\) is a unit.
The paragraph immediately following the theorem, across pp. 2–3, explicitly deduces the same conclusion simultaneously for a prescribed finite list of positive exponents by using their product.
Each \(\alpha^{n_j}-1\) divides the unit \(\alpha^{\prod n_i}-1\) in the ring of integers.
On p. 1 the author identifies the construction as an extension of a construction of Gross and McMullen.

On the published first page, printed p. 2157, Theorem 1.1 asserts existence in every degree \(2t\) with \(t\ge(n+3)/2\) for odd \(n\).
For even \(n\), it gives existence for all sufficiently large odd \(t\) depending on \(n\) and rules out even \(t\).
The existence assertions give infinitely many Salem numbers of each indicated degree.
The printed theorem and preprint Theorem 2 have different formulations and numbering.

The selected fixed-list statements place no uniform upper bound on the Salem numbers and relate no exponent to the degree by an equality.
This source note records statement inspection on 2026-11-16, using the retained preprint and the publisher's first-page preview.
The proofs and the remaining pages of the published edition were not audited for this comparison.
