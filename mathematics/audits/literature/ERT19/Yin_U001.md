# ERT19: the integral companion and Lorentzian trace Gram construction

<a id="ert19-u001"></a>
## Inspected scope and conventions

Use the [arXiv v3 edition](readme.md#arxiv-v3).
The directly read passages are Theorem 1.1 and its converse on printed/PDF pages 1--2, the conventions in Sections 2.1--2.3, and Theorem 6.3 with its companion/Gram construction on pages 11--13.
Pages 12--13 were also checked visually in the retained PDF for the companion convention, Toeplitz entries, the factor of two and the final positive stabilization.
The construction's displayed matrix steps were inspected; this is not an independent full-source proof audit or an audit of the foundational arithmetic-lattice results.

The source permits degree-two Salem numbers, unlike the [degree-at-least-four convention in the existing Smy08 entry](../Smy08/Yin_U003.md#smy08-salem-number).
In this unit a Salem number is a real algebraic integer $\lambda>1$, conjugate to $\lambda^{-1}$, with all remaining conjugates on the unit circle.
Its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb Q$ is denoted $p$, of even degree $d$.

<a id="companion-trace-gram"></a>
## Exact rational-field construction

For
\[
p(x)=x^d+a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0,
\qquad a_0=1,
\]
the source uses the column companion $C$: $Ce_j=e_{j+1}$ for $0\le j<d-1$, and $Ce_{d-1}=-(a_0,\ldots,a_{d-1})^{\mathsf T}$.
Writing $r_1,\ldots,r_d$ for the roots, its symmetric coefficient matrix $A$ is Toeplitz with
\[
A_{ij}=\frac12\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_k^{i-j},\qquad 0\le i,j<d.
\]
The inverse-paired roots make this equal to the formula with exponent $|i-j|$.
The proof gives $A=W^{\mathsf T}JW$, $J=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1)$, and $C=W^{-1}MW$, where $M$ has rotation blocks and one real hyperbolic block with eigenvalues $\lambda,\lambda^{-1}$.
Newton's identities show that $2A$ is integral.

**Definition (trace Gram matrix, the source construction in an integral bilinear normalization).**
Put
\[
G=2A,\qquad
G_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q(\lambda)/\mathbb Q}(\lambda^{i-j}),
\qquad L_p=(\mathbb Z^d,G).
\]
Thus the source construction, in this notation, supplies
\[
C^{\mathsf T}GC=G,\qquad
\operatorname{signature}(G)=(d-1,1),\qquad
G_{ii}=d,\qquad
\chi_C=p.
\]
Here $G$, rather than $A$, is the integral bilinear Gram matrix.
Its diagonal is even, so $L_p$ is an even integral lattice; the source's quadratic form is $f(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax=\tfrac12x^{\mathsf T}Gx$.
The difference between an integral-valued quadratic form and an integral coefficient matrix must not be suppressed when testing an integral $U$-summand.
The complete characteristic measure is exactly $M(\chi_C)=\lambda$; there are no extra noncyclotomic factors to discard.

## The broader source statement and the missing integral bridge

Theorem 6.3 allows $K\subseteq\mathbb Q(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})$ and $n\ge2$ with $\deg_K\lambda\le n+1$, and realizes translation length $\log\lambda$ in a classical arithmetic hyperbolic lattice over $K$.
Its proof first constructs the minimal-degree companion and adds positive identity directions when needed.
The $K=\mathbb Q$ construction above is the selected interface; it does not require raising $C$ to a power.
For orientation on the reverse correspondence, Theorem 1.1 imposes either even hyperbolic dimension or membership in the subgroup generated by squares.
The paper's $O'(f,R)$ denotes preservation of the negative cone's components, not GHS09's rational spinor kernel.

These statements do not assert that $L_p$ contains a unimodular integral hyperbolic plane, or that $C$ has an integral decomposition into prescribed Eichler generators.
Those are separate questions even though the rational form has Lorentzian signature.
A successful integer basis change preserves the characteristic polynomial; a rational basis change alone does not certify an integer matrix on the chosen lattice.
The selected finite investigation must retain the specific $G$, rather than silently substitute a rescaled form or another invariant lattice.
Passing to $C^k$ changes the complete logarithmic measure to $k\log\lambda$, while adjoining an identity block preserves it but changes the representation problem.
Neither operation answers the stated no-power, no-added-hyperbolic-plane test.

The source begins with an already supplied Salem polynomial.
Its realization theorem is not a method for finding new small Salem numbers, and no conclusion about the unrestricted positive Mahler gap is adopted here.
