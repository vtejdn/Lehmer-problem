## Reu96-U002: Rational Tensor Series, Transductions, and Theorem 3.1

- Kind: precise series setting, realization interface, and star-height characterization.
- Locators: Section 3 opening and Theorem 3.1, p. 98; converse discussion and Figures 3.1-3.2, p. 99; transductions and Lemma 3.2, pp. 100-101; theorem assembly, p. 105.
- Scope: the theorem holds for a division ring $D$ with a central subfield $k$ and a finite set $X$.
  It does not require $k$ to be the full center or infinite, and does not require $[D:k]=\infty$.
  Those additional hypotheses in Theorem 2.2 must not be imported into Theorem 3.1.

### Series Algebra and Star Height

Use the free tensor ring $D_k\langle X\rangle$ from [U001](Yin_U001.md#reu96-u001): it is the free product of $D$ and $k\langle X\rangle$ over the central field $k$.
Variables need not commute with $D$.
In particular, a coefficient placed between two letters cannot in general be moved to the left.

The tensor series algebra $D_k\langle\!\langle X\rangle\!\rangle$ is its completion for the ideal generated by $X$.
A homogeneous component has finite tensor support; infinitely many degrees are allowed.
If $(u_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}$ is a $k$-basis of $D$, a series has a unique expansion in monomials
\[
u_{\alpha_0}x_1u_{\alpha_1}\cdots x_nu_{\alpha_n}.
\]
For each degree $n$ the expansion is finite, even when $A$ is infinite.
All convergence in this unit is formal, in the $X$-adic topology; no Hilbert-space or Banach norm is asserted.

Write $\operatorname{ct}(f)\in D$ for the constant term.
A series is invertible precisely when its constant term is nonzero.
A proper series has constant term zero and admits the formal star
\[
f^*=\sum_{m\ge0}f^m=(1-f)^{-1}.
\]
Here the superscript star is the geometric-series operation, not an adjoint, conjugation, or word reversal.

A rational series is obtained from $D_k\langle X\rangle$ by sums, products, and inversions of invertible series, equivalently by sums, products, and stars of proper expressions.
For example, writing an invertible series $a=c-p$ with $c=\operatorname{ct}(a)\ne0$ and $p$ proper gives
\[
a^{-1}=(c^{-1}p)^*c^{-1};
\]
the factor order matters.
Star height is the minimum nesting depth of stars in such expressions, with polynomials and elements of $D$ at height zero.

### Left Transductions and the Minimal State Space

For $\alpha\in A$ and $x\in X$, let $T_{\alpha,x}$ denote the source's left transduction $(u_\alpha x)^{-1}$.
It deletes the initial pair $u_\alpha x$ from a basis monomial when that pair matches, and returns zero otherwise.
Thus, for $n\ge1$,
\[
T_{\alpha,x}
(u_{\alpha_0}x_1u_{\alpha_1}\cdots x_nu_{\alpha_n})
=
\begin{cases}
u_{\alpha_1}x_2\cdots x_nu_{\alpha_n},
 &\alpha_0=\alpha,\ x_1=x,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Every constant is sent to zero.
These maps are continuous and $k$-linear; they are generally not $D$-linear.

For arbitrary series $g,h$, the product identity is
\[
T_{\alpha,x}(gh)
=T_{\alpha,x}(g)h+
T_{\alpha,x}(\operatorname{ct}(g)h).
\]
Split $g$ into its constant and proper parts to check the formula.
It is incorrect to replace the second term by $\operatorname{ct}(g)T_{\alpha,x}(h)$ without additional justification.
This distinction drives the requirement to control $T_{\alpha,x}(d v)$ for every $d\in D$ below.

The source imports from its reference [10], Cohn-Reutenauer, *A Normal Form in Free Fields*, the following interfaces: a series is rational if and only if it has a finite linear representation
\[
f=\lambda(I-M)^{-1}\gamma=\lambda M^*\gamma,
\quad
\lambda\in D^{1\times n},\quad\gamma\in D^{n\times1},
\]
where every entry of $M$ is homogeneous of $X$-degree one; equivalently it lies in a finite-dimensional left $D$-space stable under all left transductions.
The least such space containing $f$ is $L(f)$.
These are stated in Reu96 on pp. 98 and 100.

The separate [CR94-U001 primary-interface review](../CR94/Yin_U001.md#cr94-u001) inspected [CR94](../../../literature/pdf/CR94.pdf), the paper cited as Reu96's reference [10]: the setting and transductions on p. 519, Proposition 2.3 and Lemma 2.6 on pp. 520-521, and Corollary 2.7 on p. 521.
That review checked exact statements and applicability to finite $X$ and central $k\subseteq Z(D)$; it imposes no infinitude hypothesis here.
These established interfaces are accepted as external inputs at that recorded scope.
Their original proofs were not independently audited.
The primary checks were performed by the Section 4 reconstruction agent, not by this unit's local Section 3 reviewer.

The notation $L(f)$ includes closure under left $D$-linear combinations as well as transductions.
It must not be replaced by a residual span justified by a false $D$-linearity assumption.

### Lemma 3.2: From a Height Basis to a Minimal Representation

Let $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ be a left $D$-basis of $L(f)$, with the source's chosen order.
Write
\[
f=\sum_i\lambda_i f_i,\qquad
T_{\alpha,x}(f_i)=\sum_j d_{\alpha,x,i,j}f_j,\qquad
\gamma_i=\operatorname{ct}(f_i),
\]
and define
\[
m_{ij}=\sum_{\alpha,x}u_\alpha x\,d_{\alpha,x,i,j}.
\]
The entries are degree-one tensor polynomials.
Finiteness of the sum is part of the rational realization interface: from any finite representation of a rational $f_i$, its leading degree-one coefficients involve only finitely many initial $u_\alpha x$ pairs.
Multiplying initial coefficients by fixed elements of $D$ preserves finite support in the $k$-basis.
Thus only finitely many $T_{\alpha,x}(f_i)$ can be nonzero.

The column $F=(f_1,\ldots,f_n)^{\mathsf T}$ satisfies
\[
F=\gamma+MF,\qquad F=(I-M)^{-1}\gamma,
\]
by decomposing each series into its constant term and initial-pair transductions.
This gives the asserted representation.

Minimality can be checked directly once the representation interfaces above are available.
For any representation of $f$ of dimension $m$, its state column $G=(I-N)^{-1}\eta$ has a left $D$-span stable under transductions: the relation $G=\eta+NG$ and the degree-one entries of $N$ show that each $T_{\alpha,x}(dG_i)$ is a left $D$-combination of the $G_j$.
Its span contains $f$, so
\[
\dim_D L(f)\leq\dim_D\operatorname{span}_D\{G_1,\ldots,G_m\}\leq m.
\]
The basis construction therefore has minimum dimension.
This is a local reconstruction of the minimality mechanism stated via [10], not an audit of that paper's full theory.

Suppose the ordered basis is a height basis of height at most $\ell$, in the exact sense of [U003](Yin_U003.md#reu96-u003).
Put $h(i)=h(f_i)$ and
\[
n(i)=\min\{j>i:h(j)\ge h(i)\},
\]
with $n(i)=\infty$ if the set is empty.
If $h(i)=0$, the height rule forces $d_{\alpha,x,i,j}=0$ for $j\ge i$.
If $h(i)\ge1$, it forces those coefficients to vanish for $j\ge n(i)$.
Consequently $I-M$, rather than $M$, has the height structure of Section 2: at height zero its diagonal is $1$ and all entries strictly to the right vanish; at positive height the cutoff is $j\ge n(i)$.

The zero series can be handled separately, by the empty representation if that convention is used, or by $(0,1,0)$ at height zero.
The source does not spell out the empty-state convention; it is not a substantive exception to the height bound.

### Theorem 3.1 and Its Two Directions

The exact statement is
\[
\operatorname{starheight}(f)\le\ell
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f\text{ has a minimal representation }(\lambda,I-M,\gamma)
\text{ whose }I-M\text{ has height structure }\le\ell.
\]
There is no separate canonical-invertibility hypothesis here.
Since $M$ is proper, $I-M$ has identity constant matrix; eliminating states produces Schur pivots of constant term $1$, so the source's prescribed elimination is well defined in the series ring.

For the forward direction, U003 reconstructs Lemmas 3.3-3.6: a star expression of height at most $\ell$ produces a finite invariant state space with a height family; redundant vectors may be removed without increasing height; the critical-support construction transfers the bound to its invariant subspace $L(f)$.
Lemma 3.2 then supplies a minimal height-structured representation.
Choosing an arbitrary basis of $L(f)$ or merely minimizing the state dimension does not by itself preserve the required zero pattern.

For the converse, p. 99 gives a graph sketch and also points to the elimination proof underlying Lemma 4.2.
The latter has been reconstructed in [U004](Yin_U004.md#reu96-u004) and supplies the exact star version in this proper-matrix setting.
In a unique highest-height block, write
\[
I-M=\begin{pmatrix}a&R\\ C&N\end{pmatrix},\qquad
q=a-RN^{-1}C.
\]
The lower block inverse has star height at most one less.
Since $R,C$ are proper and $a,N$ have constant terms $1,I$ respectively,
\[
q^{-1}=(1-q)^*,\qquad
1-q=(1-a)+RN^{-1}C
\]
is an expression of the lower star height and is proper.
One new star yields the required height bound.
At height zero the matrix is unit lower triangular and its inverse is polynomial; multiple highest-height blocks combine by sums and products without another star.
Thus every inverse entry, and then $\lambda(I-M)^{-1}\gamma$, has star height at most $\ell$.

The diagrams on p. 99 were checked with the directed edge convention $i\to j$ for $m_{ij}\ne0$.
Their displayed $(1,1)$ entries are, respectively,
\[
\left(m_{11}+m_{13}m_{31}+m_{13}m_{32}m_{21}
+m_{12}m_{21}\right)^*,
\qquad
\left(m_{11}+m_{12}m_{22}^*m_{21}\right)^*.
\]
The first enumerates the possible first returns to vertex $1$; the second permits any number of loops at vertex $2$ before return.
The products' order is correct over noncommuting coefficients.
These examples illustrate depths one and two; a general equivalence with graph loop complexity, attributed to reference [12], was not independently audited here.
The elimination argument supplies the converse needed for this unit.
