### Ambient Rings and Expressions

Let $k$ be a commutative field and $X$ a finite set of noncommuting variables.
The free associative algebra $k\langle X\rangle$ has a universal division ring of fractions, denoted here by $F_k(X)$.

More generally, let $D$ be a division ring and $k\subseteq Z(D)$ a central subfield.
Write

\[
R=D *_k k\langle X\rangle,\qquad F_D(X)=\operatorname{Frac}_{\mathrm{univ}}(R).
\]

This is the source's free tensor ring: variables commute with $k$, but no commutation with arbitrary elements of $D$ is imposed.
Its degree-$m$ part is a direct sum, indexed by words of length $m$ in $X$, of copies of $D^{\otimes_k(m+1)}$.
Thus coefficients on both sides of variables matter; $axb$ cannot generally be replaced by $abx$.

The formal series ring is the product of these homogeneous parts over $m\geq0$.
Each homogeneous component is an algebraic tensor element, hence a finite sum of simple tensors.
All convergence in the series units means formal convergence in increasing $X$-degree.
No norm, probability measure, state, trace, or analytic operator realization is assumed.

For a specified embedding $R\subseteq F$, define

\[
R_0=R,\qquad
R_{j+1}=\text{the subring generated by }R_j
\text{ and }\{a^{-1}:0\ne a\in R_j\}.
\]

The inversion height $h_R(f)$ is the least $j$ with $f\in R_j$.
Equivalently it is the least nested scalar-inversion depth among valid rational expressions for $f$ over $R$.
All elements of $D$ have height zero.
A displayed expression of depth $j$ proves only $h_R(f)\leq j$; an equality needs a separate lower bound.
The ambient embedding and base ring are part of this definition.

For a proper formal series $s$ (constant term zero), $s^*=(1-s)^{-1}=\sum_{m\geq0}s^m$.
Star height minimizes nested stars in expressions built from tensor polynomials by sums, products, and stars of proper expressions.
A free-field inverse of a nonzero element and a formal star are not interchangeable without checking the constant term.

### Full Matrices and Linear Representations

A square $n\times n$ matrix over $R$ is full if it admits no factorization through $r<n$ columns and rows over $R$.
The universal free-field construction in this setting inverts full square matrices.
This correspondence and existence of the field are retained foundational inputs from Reu96's account, not independently proved here.

A linear representation of $f\in F_D(X)$ is

\[
f=\lambda M^{-1}\gamma,\qquad
\lambda\in D^{1\times n},\quad
\gamma\in D^{n\times1},
\]

where $M$ is full and its entries have $X$-degree at most one.
It is minimal if no representation of smaller dimension represents $f$.
The two families used to test minimality are the entries of $M^{-1}\gamma$ (left $D$-independence) and of $\lambda M^{-1}$ (right $D$-independence).

For invertible constant matrices $P,Q$ the transformation

\[
(\lambda,M,\gamma)\longmapsto(\lambda P,QMP,Q\gamma)
\]

preserves the represented element because $(QMP)^{-1}=P^{-1}M^{-1}Q^{-1}$.
The nontrivial assertion that all minimal representations of the same element are related this way is an external normal-form theorem.
Its precise hypotheses and primary statement check are in [CR94-U001](../CR94/Yin_U001.md#cr94-u001).
Do not replace the two-sided transformation by a similarity unless the representation has been normalized to identity constant matrix.

For series, a proper linear representation uses $M=I-N$, with $N$ homogeneous of degree one.
Minimality in this class must be connected to free-field minimality by the imported criteria; equality of the two notions is not a definition.

### Ordered Canonical Invertibility

The source calls a system canonically solvable if elimination uses its fixed index order: eliminate the last unknown by the last equation, then repeat in the remaining system, with every pivot nonzero and no row or column permutations.
A matrix is canonically invertible if these steps succeed.

For a last pivot $d\ne0$, the remaining Schur matrix has entries $a_{ij}-b_i d^{-1}c_j$, in this order.
Invertibility alone is insufficient: $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ is invertible but its last pivot is zero.

### Height Structures

For an ordered index set $I=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ a height structure at most $\ell$ is a map $h:I\to\{0,\ldots,\ell\}$ satisfying:

- If $h(i)=0$, then $m_{ii}=1$ and $m_{ij}=0$ for $j>i$.
- If $h(i)>0$, put $n(i)=\min\{j>i:h(j)\geq h(i)\}$, with $n(i)=\infty$ when the set is empty.
  Then $m_{ij}=0$ for all $j\geq n(i)$.

The next index is **strictly later**, while the height comparison and final zero cutoff are **weak** inequalities.
For $\ell=0$, the matrix is unit lower triangular.
The introductory informal reference to strict triangularity must be read through these precise conditions: $M$ is unit lower triangular, and $M-I$ is strictly lower triangular.

A height structure depends on the ordered representation.
Arbitrary changes of basis or index permutations need not preserve it.
Theorem 2.2 asserts the existence of a suitable minimal representative, not a property of every minimal representative.

## Formal-Series Closure Terminology

The source's rational tensor series are the least subring containing tensor polynomials and closed under scalar inverses that exist in the formal-series ring.
In that ring, invertibility means invertibility of the constant term, hence a nonzero constant term over \(D\).

This is the general scalar division-closure construction applied to the tensor-polynomial inclusion in the formal-series ring.
It is not a new definition for every \(D,k,X\), and the word “rational” does not by itself assert equality with matrix rational closure in an arbitrary ambient algebra.
The general Div/Rat distinction is housed in [MSY23-U003](../MSY23/Yin_U003.md#definitions-47-and-410-two-closures-in-one-ambient-algebra); the [MSY23 convention account](../MSY23/Yin_U002.md#fixed-ambient-closure-conventions) records the notation conversion.

The source's star is a formal-series operation and is unrelated to a Hilbert-space adjoint.
All convergence here is formal degree convergence.
No trace, probability law, operator-norm closure or affiliated-operator domain is part of these definitions.

## Rank, Height, and Coefficient Hypotheses

The introduction distinguishes inversion **height**, representation **depth** using polynomial systems, and representation **rank** using affine linear systems (printed p. 95 / PDF p. 3).
The exact class matters when a minimum is taken.
These complexity quantities are not polynomial inner rank or MSY23 analytic rank; the latter's unnormalized trace convention is checked [separately](../MSY23/Yin_U002.md#analytic-rank-normalization).

For the general tensor-series section, \(k\) is a central subfield of \(D\), and \(X\) is finite.
The free-field discussion of Section 2 uses \(k=Z(D)\); the stronger height characterization imposes that \(k\) be infinite and \([D:k]=\infty\).
The [existing hypothesis map](#scope-map-and-reuse-limits) must accompany use of that theorem.
These extra hypotheses are not imported into every series definition, and the theorem is not applied directly with \(D=k=\mathbb C\).

The primary visual checks confirm two previously recorded definition-level repairs:

- In the height-basis discussion, the maximum is over the chosen ordered basis, not all vectors of its ambient vector space.
- The next-index set in printed (3.8), p. 104, must use \(j>i\), not the displayed \(j\ge i\); including \(i\) itself defeats the intended next-index condition.
  The [existing U003 exact rules](Yin_U003.md#exact-height-rules) retain this repair.

These are confirmations of existing corrections, not new results.
The known representation, stable-space, and height equivalences remain at their accepted source interfaces; this definition task does not reprove them or allocate project Claims.
