# Minimum degree under rational powers and torsion translations

<a id="fm13-u002"></a>
<a id="rational-line-degree"></a>
## The fractional lattice in Proposition 3.2

In Fili and Miner's notation, \(f=f_\beta\ne0\) represents an algebraic number modulo roots of unity, and \(K_f\) is its minimal field.
Put \(\delta=[K_f:\mathbf Q]\).
Every representative of a nonzero rational multiple of \(f\) generates a field containing \(K_f\).
The quantity \(d(f)\) is the minimum degree of a representative, taken over all torsion translations.
A nonzero class is representable when it has a representative in its minimal field. [Selected edition](readme.md) · [Pages 19–21, Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.3](../../../literature/pdf/FM13--author-2013.pdf#page=19)

The rational multiples of \(f\) representable in \(K_f\), including zero, form \((\ell/n)\mathbf Z\) with coprime positive integers \(\ell,n\).
For nonzero coprime integers \(r,s\) with \(s>0\), Proposition 3.2 gives
\[
              d\big((r/s)f\big)
                  =\frac{\ell s\,\delta}{\gcd(\ell,r)\gcd(n,s)} .
\]
In particular \(d(f)=\ell\delta\), and \(d(f/g)=\ell g\delta/\gcd(n,g)\) at every integer \(g\ge1\).
Thus minimizing the measure of an arbitrarily translated \(g\)-th root of \(\beta\) gives \(h(\beta)d(f/g)/g\), directly from the published degree formula and invariance of absolute height under torsion.
The proof in Section 3.1 first identifies the cyclic rational lattice, then takes the relative norm of a representative to its minimal field.
In the normalization where the lattice is \(\mathbf Z\), the norm forces the degree over the minimal field to be divisible by the denominator \(s\).
A root of \(X^s-u^r\), for a representative \(u\) of the generator, realizes this degree.
This inspection checked the proposition, its normalization and its norm argument in the selected May 2013 text. [Proposition 3.2, page 20](../../../literature/pdf/FM13--author-2013.pdf#page=20) · [Lemmas 3.5–3.7, pages 21–22](../../../literature/pdf/FM13--author-2013.pdf#page=21)
