# Multiplicative orders above the square-root scale at almost all primes

<a id="rank-one-orders"></a>
## The unconditional fixed-rank bound

Theorem 2.1 on printed p. 211 of the [published edition](readme.md#edition) concerns the reduction \(\Gamma_p\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times\) of a group generated by a fixed number \(r\) of multiplicatively independent nonzero integers.
The assumptions on p. 207 exclude squares and \(\pm1\) as generators, and the primes under consideration do not divide the generators.
For \(r=1\) and generator \(a\), the cardinality \(|\Gamma_p|\) is the multiplicative order \(\operatorname{ord}_p(a)\).
Put \(\tau=(1-\log 2)/2\), with natural logarithms.
For each function \(\psi(p)\) tending steadily to infinity, Theorem 2.1 gives
\[
  |\Gamma_p|\ \ge\ p^{r/(r+1)}
       \exp\!\left(\frac{(\log p)^\tau}
                         {\exp(\psi(p)\sqrt{\log\log p})}\right)
\]
outside a set of primes of relative natural density zero.

For \(L=\log\log p\) take \(\psi(p)=L^{1/4}\) at all sufficiently large \(p\).
The exponent in the outer exponential is \(\exp(\tau L-L^{3/4})\), which grows faster than \(L\).
Consequently for each fixed \(K>0\) the rank-one estimate implies \(\operatorname{ord}_p(a)>\sqrt p\,(\log p)^{K+1}\) for almost all primes.
For each of \(a=2,3,5\) the rank-one hypotheses hold.
The union of the three exceptional sets has relative density zero.
For \(p\in[x,2x]\) and all sufficiently large \(x\), the three orders on the complementary set exceed \(\sqrt{2x}(\log x)^K\), since the factor \(\log x\) absorbs \(\sqrt2\).
The three conclusions follow by separate rank-one applications and do not require independence among the exceptional sets.

This inspection checked the definitions on p. 207, Theorem 2.1 on p. 211, and the distinction from the GRH-dependent Theorem 2.3 on p. 212.
The unconditional theorem is accepted as an established input at this fixed-rank scope.
The journal proof and its analytic estimates were not re-audited.
