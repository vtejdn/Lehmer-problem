# Density of sets with no progression of a fixed length

On p. 199 of the [journal article](readme.md), Szemerédi defines \(r_k(N)\) as the largest cardinality of a subset of \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\) containing no arithmetic progression of \(k\) distinct terms.
The unnumbered corollary on p. 244 states that for each \(\varepsilon>0\) and fixed \(k\), every subset of \([0,N)\) of size greater than \(\varepsilon N\) contains such a progression once \(N\) is sufficiently large.
Its threshold may depend on both \(k\) and \(\varepsilon\).
Translation identifies this interval with the one in the definition, yielding \(r_k(N)/N\to0\) as \(N\to\infty\) for each fixed \(k\ge2\), in particular for \(k=11\).
The common difference may be any positive integer.
I checked the original interval convention, strict density inequality and order of quantifiers against the [printed corollary](../../../literature/pdf/Sze75--journal.pdf#page=24).
The project uses the qualitative finite-interval statement.
