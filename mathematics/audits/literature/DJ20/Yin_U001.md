### Exact source statement

Let alpha>1 be a genuine Salem number of degree d=2t>=4.
Label its distinct rational conjugates x_1,...,x_(2t) so that x_1=alpha, x_2=alpha^-1 and x_(2j)=x_(2j-1)^-1 for every j.
For j>=2 the two members of the pair are complex conjugates on the unit circle.
For ANY rational numbers c_1,...,c_(2t) and ANY totally real algebraic number y, the identity

    y=sum_(i=1)^(2t) c_i x_i

forces

    c_(2j-1)=c_(2j), 1<=j<=t.                       (1)

The conclusion concerns the particular coefficients in every such representation, even when the conjugates have rational additive relations.
Zero coefficients and y=0 are allowed.
Neither positivity, integrality, a normal trace field nor a small-measure hypothesis is assumed.
The requirement is that y be TOTALLY real, not merely real in the displayed embedding.

### Complete proof check

Suppose c_(2j-1)!=c_(2j), and apply a Galois automorphism carrying x_(2j-1) to alpha; it necessarily carries x_(2j) to alpha^-1.
Complex conjugate the resulting equation and add the two equations.
All neutral inverse pairs become rational multiples of their pair traces.
Rearrangement expresses 2(c_(2j)-c_(2j-1))alpha as a rational linear combination of the pair traces and the conjugate of y.
Every pair trace is totally real: its conjugates are alpha+alpha^-1>2 and the neutral pair traces in (-2,2).
Totally real algebraic numbers form a field, so the rearranged right side is totally real.
The nonzero rational multiple of alpha on the left has a nonreal conjugate, a contradiction.
This checks the full printed argument, including the nonzero coefficient and the use of total reality after conjugation.
No auxiliary theorem from the rest of the paper is needed.

### Direct specialization to the rational convex hull

Let L be the splitting field of alpha, and let F be the field generated by all pair traces

    b_j=x_(2j-1)+x_(2j), 1<=j<=t.

Thus F is the normal closure of Q(alpha+alpha^-1) inside L and is totally real.
For a finite set X in L, conv_Q(X) means finite convex combinations with nonnegative rational coefficients whose sum is1; zero weights can be omitted.
It is a subset of the Q-vector space L, with the SAME rational coefficients at every embedding.
Write L_tr for the set of elements of L that are totally real over Q.
Then (1), with q_j=2c_(2j-1), gives the exact set identity

    conv_Q{x_1,...,x_(2t)} intersect L_tr
       =(1/2) conv_Q{b_1,...,b_t}.                   (2)

For the converse, assign coefficient q_j/2 to each member of pair j.
This proves both inclusions and preserves the coefficient sum exactly.
Consequently the totally real algebraic-integer outputs are exactly

    O_F intersect [(1/2) conv_Q{b_1,...,b_t}].        (3)

Indeed every element on the right of (2) lies in F, and integrality over Z is independent of whether it is tested in F or L.
The factor1/2 must not be removed: the traces b_j are algebraic integers, but b_j/2 need not be.
This is an exact reduction of an integral-point question, not an existence theorem for a nonzero point of (3).
It gives no restriction on a nonreal algebraic-integer output outside L_tr.
The field F here is the normal closure of the actual degree-t trace field; no claim that the latter is already normal is made.
