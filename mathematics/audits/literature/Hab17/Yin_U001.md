# The effective point-height estimate on the open anomalous locus

Let \(X\subset\mathbb G_m^n\) be an irreducible closed subvariety over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), let \(r=\dim X\), and let \(s\ge0\) be an integer.
Remove every irreducible \(Y\subseteq X\) contained in a coset \(H\) and satisfying \(\dim Y\ge\max(1,s+\dim H-n+1)\), obtaining \(X^{\mathrm{oa},[s]}\).
Write \((\mathbb G_m^n)^{[s]}\) for the union of the algebraic subgroups of codimension at least \(s\), allowing disconnected subgroups.
Theorem 11 takes \(C=(600n^7(2n)^{n^2})^r\) and produces a closed \(Z\subseteq X\) whose complement contains \(X^{\mathrm{oa},[s]}\).
For every \(p\in(X\setminus Z)(\overline{\mathbb Q})\cap(\mathbb G_m^n)^{[s]}\) it gives
\[
 h(p)\le(2\deg X)^C(1+h(X)).
\]
Each component of \(Z\) has degree at most \((2\deg X)^C\) and height at most \((2\deg X)^C(1+h(X))\), and their number is at most \((2\deg X)^C\). [Section 2, pp. 5–6](../../../literature/pdf/Hab17--workshop.pdf#page=5)

For the standard torus embedding, the point height \(h\) uses the Euclidean norm at infinite places.
The sup-norm logarithmic projective height \(h_s\) satisfies \(h_s\le h\le h_s+\tfrac12\log(n+1)\), so the same upper bound applies to \(h_s\). [Section 3.2, pp. 9–10](../../../literature/pdf/Hab17--workshop.pdf#page=9)

For a curve in a three-torus take \((n,r,s)=(3,1,2)\).
A positive-dimensional anomalous subvariety must then be the curve itself contained in a coset of dimension at most one, hence equal to a one-dimensional coset.
A point on a one-dimensional torsion coset belongs to the algebraic subgroup generated by the underlying subtorus and the translating torsion point, which still has dimension one.
Consequently the estimate applies on the complement of all positive-dimensional contained cosets at such points.
Its right side involves the component degree and projective variety height, and passing to the heights of defining equations uses the arithmetic Bézout input identified in the [ASZ17 application](../ASZ17/Yin_U002.md#height).
The inspection covered the definitions, the complete Theorem 11 statement and the point-height comparison in the [workshop edition](readme.md#edition).
The proof of Theorem 11 and the arithmetic Bézout theorem were not independently audited.
