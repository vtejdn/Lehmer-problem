## Definitions

The text on **p. 220**, after IV.6.4, calls O_a={r in R:ra=0} the **order ideal** of a (the annihilator ideal of that element).
The torsion submodule A_t consists of a annihilated by some nonzero scalar.
A is torsion if A=A_t and torsion-free if A_t=0.
Over a PID an element has order r when O_a=(r); r is determined only up to multiplication by a unit.
A cyclic module generated by such an element is isomorphic to R/(r).

For a prime p, **IV.6.7, p. 222**, uses A(p) for the elements whose order ideal is a power of (p), including zero, whose order ideal is R.
These are the p-primary components.
Choose one representative from each associate class of primes for a decomposition: associate primes define the same component, not two distinct summands.

**IV.6.10, p. 224**, uses rA for the image of scalar multiplication and A[r] for its kernel.
The text after **IV.6.12, p. 225**, calls the nonzero nonunit cyclic orders r_1 dividing ... dividing r_t **invariant factors**, and the prime-power cyclic orders **elementary divisors**.
Their uniqueness is properly a uniqueness of principal ideals, with the indicated ordering/multiplicity, rather than a choice of scalar representatives.
The free rank is another invariant.

## Main Proof Chain

| Result and locator | Content and checked mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| IV.6.1, pp. 218–219 | A submodule G of a free PID module F is free, with rank no larger. Well-order a basis of F and filter by the spans of preceding basis vectors. Each successive G quotient embeds in R, hence is zero or free of rank one and splits. Chosen lifts are independent by the largest-index term of any finite relation. They span by transfinite induction; at a limit, finite support places an element in an earlier stage. |
| IV.6.2–6.3, pp. 219–220 | Submodules of an n-generated module are generated by at most n elements, using the preimage in a free cover and IV.6.1. A unitary PID module is projective iff free: it is a summand, hence a submodule, of a free module in one direction, and IV.3.2 applies in the other. |
| IV.6.4, p. 220 | Order ideals are ideals, torsion is a submodule over a domain, and Ra is isomorphic to R/O_a. Products of nonzero annihilators give closure under addition. If p^i annihilates a over a PID, O_a=(p^j) for some 0≤j≤i; exact order means smaller nonnegative powers do not annihilate it. The source's factorization argument supplies the last assertions. |
| IV.6.5, p. 221 | A finitely generated torsion-free PID module is free. Choose a maximal independent subset of a finite generating set. Each remaining generator has a nonzero scalar multiple in its span; multiply these finitely many scalars to get nonzero r with rA in that free span. Multiplication by r is injective by torsion-freeness, and IV.6.1 applies. Finite generation is essential. |
| IV.6.6, p. 221 | A finitely generated A decomposes as A_t direct-sum F, with F finite free and isomorphic to A/A_t. The quotient is torsion-free since a nonzero annihilator of ra also annihilates a after multiplication by r. IV.6.5 makes the quotient projective and splits the sequence. The complement is not canonical. |
| IV.6.7, p. 222 | A torsion module is the direct sum of its prime-primary components, with only finitely many nonzero components if finitely generated. Factor an element's annihilator; Bezout coefficients express it as a sum of primary pieces. Coprime powers annihilating an intersection force that intersection to be zero. Finite supports of a finite generating set prove finiteness. Prime indexing is by associate classes as specified above. |
| IV.6.8, pp. 222–223 | If p^n A=0, p^(n-1) A is nonzero and a has order p^n, then Ra is a direct summand. First construct b outside Ra with pb=0 and Ra intersect Rb zero when A differs from Ra. Then use a maximal submodule C disjoint from Ra; if A/C is larger than the image of Ra, the first construction enlarges C, a contradiction. The zero-coefficient case of the first construction is completed below. |
| IV.6.9, p. 223 | A finitely generated p-primary module is a finite direct sum of cyclic modules with orders p^(n_1), ..., p^(n_k), n_1≥...≥n_k≥1. Split off a generator of maximal order using IV.6.8; the complement is generated by at most one fewer generators, so induction applies. The zero module has the empty decomposition. |
| IV.6.10, p. 224 | Scalar images/kernels and torsion/primary parts are invariant under module isomorphisms and commute with direct sums. A[p] is a vector space over R/(p). For R/(p^n), its p-kernel is one-dimensional over R/(p), and p^m times that module is R/(p^(n-m)) for 0≤m<n. Direct generators and their annihilator ideals verify these identities. |
| IV.6.11, p. 224 | R/(product p_i^(n_i)) is the sum of R/(p_i^(n_i)) for pairwise nonassociate primes. For coprime s,t, the source's map (x+(s),y+(t)) to tx+sy+(st) is an isomorphism: Bezout gives surjectivity, and reducing modulo s and t gives injectivity. Iterate. |
| IV.6.12, p. 225 | Every finitely generated PID module is a finite free module plus finite cyclic torsion summands, uniquely classified either by invariant-factor ideals or by elementary-divisor ideals. Existence follows from IV.6.6–6.9 and IV.6.11. The source sketches a transfer from Chapter II; the invariant-count verification below makes the needed uniqueness argument explicit without treating unread earlier proof text as inspected. |
| IV.6.13, p. 226 | Two finitely generated PID modules are isomorphic iff their free ranks and invariant factors, or equivalently their elementary divisors, agree. Match the classified summands for sufficiency; necessity is the uniqueness just established. Scalar representatives are compared up to units. |

## Completions of Compressed Proof Steps

In IV.6.8(i), take c outside Ra and the least positive j for which p^j c lies in Ra.
The source writes p^j c=ra and factors r into a unit-coprime part times a power of p.
If **r=0**, take b=p^(j-1)c directly.
It is nonzero, lies outside Ra, and is killed by p.
If a nonzero sb were in Ra, p cannot divide s; a Bezout identity for s and p^n would imply b is in Ra, a contradiction.
Thus Ra intersects Rb trivially.
If r is nonzero, the printed factorization is legitimate; the order p^n of a forces the p-exponent of r to be at least j, and the source's adjusted vector b has the same properties.
This fills a missing case, without changing the lemma.

For IV.6.12, let T=A_t.
The free rank is the basis rank of A/T, hence invariant.
For each prime class p and integer k≥1, the intrinsic number

\[
c_{p,k}=\dim_{R/(p)}\bigl(p^{k-1}T(p)\bigr)[p]
\]

counts the cyclic p-primary summands of exponent at least k, by IV.6.10.
Thus c_(p,k)-c_(p,k+1) determines the number with exponent exactly k.
This proves uniqueness of elementary-divisor ideals.
To form invariant factors, sort the exponents for each p in nondecreasing order and pad with leading zeros to the maximum number of primary summands.
Multiply prime powers columnwise and use IV.6.11 to combine coprime cyclic factors.
This gives the divisibility chain, and reversing that process proves uniqueness.
If T=0 there are no torsion factors.
For a field R, every module in the finite-generation scope is already finite-dimensional free, so the empty prime set causes no problem.

These are checks of the source's standard proof chain, not new mathematical claims or priority assertions.
The wording “all primes” in IV.6.7 must be read through the associate-class qualification: for example p and -p in Z cannot label two copies of the same nonzero component in a direct sum.
The later uniqueness discussion on p. 225 explicitly acknowledges units and associate primes.

## Use Boundary

These results are retained reference material, without a newly assigned current Claim dependency.
They may inform a one-variable module argument over a polynomial PID if that argument actually supplies the PID and finite-generation hypotheses.
They cannot be transplanted to a multivariable free associative algebra or to an arbitrary ambient affiliated ring merely by changing notation.
Infinite torsion-free modules need not be free; the finite-generation condition in IV.6.5 remains essential.
Exercises and cross-chapter foundations outside the exact checks above remain outside this proof audit.
