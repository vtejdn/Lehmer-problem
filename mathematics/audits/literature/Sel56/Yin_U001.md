# Irreducibility of \(x^n-x-1\)

For every integer \(n\ge2\), the polynomial \(x^n-x-1\) is irreducible in \(\mathbb Q[x]\).
In particular, the assertion covers composite degrees as well as prime degrees. [Theorem 1, §3, printed p. 289](../../../literature/pdf/Sel56.pdf#page=3)

Selmer fixes the field of rational numbers in §1 on printed page 287 and defines \(f_1(x)=x^n-x-1\) in (1.5) on printed page 288.
The inspection checked the field, the signs, the quadratic starting case and the first assertion of Theorem 1 against the rendered journal pages on 2026-11-18.
The theorem is accepted at statement level for \(f_1\).
Its proof and the separate conclusion for \(x^n+x+1\) were outside this inspection. [Inspected edition](readme.md#edition)
