## Conventions and exact main statements

Use the entropy $h$, finite-set entropy $H$, and directed flow-generation conventions of [DGB16-U001](Yin_U001.md#dgb16-u001).
In particular, $[G]^{<\omega}$ means **all nonempty finite subsets**, with no global requirement to contain zero or to be a subgroup.
The notation $V(\phi,F)$ denotes the subgroup generated by all forward iterates of $F$.

<a id="dgb16-mahler-measure"></a>
For a nonzero primitive polynomial $f(t)=a_0+\cdots+a_nt^n\in\mathbb Z[t]$, with roots counted with multiplicity, set

\[
m(f)=\log|a_n|+\sum_{|\alpha_i|>1}\log|\alpha_i|.
\]

For an algebraic number $\alpha$, take its monic minimal polynomial $g\in\mathbb Q[t]$ and the least positive integer $s$ with $sg\in\mathbb Z[t]$; then $m(\alpha)=m(sg)$.
Sign changes of an integer polynomial leave $m$ unchanged.
Zero roots contribute zero to $m$ and cannot be silently excluded.

The two infima in the introduction are

\[
\mathfrak L=\inf\{m(f)>0:f\in\mathbb Z[t]\text{ primitive}\},\qquad
\varepsilon=\inf\{h(\phi)>0:G\text{ abelian},\ \phi\in\operatorname{End}(G)\}.
\]

**Theorem 1.5** asserts

\[
\varepsilon=\inf\{h(\phi)>0:n\geq1,\ \phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Q^n)\}=\mathfrak L.
\]

The source's restriction to monic integer polynomials in the definition of the same infimum is legitimate: a nonmonic primitive polynomial with leading coefficient of absolute value at least $2$ has $m(f)\geq\log2$, while the monic polynomial $t-2$ has measure $\log2$.
Factorization into primitive irreducible factors and additivity of $m$ identify the same infimum with positive Mahler measures of algebraic integers, as in Problem 7.3.
This concerns the infimum, not equality of the sets of all values.

<a id="dgb16-entropy-realization"></a>
For $h(\phi)<\infty$, Definition 1.6 says that **$h(\phi)$ realizes** when there is a nonempty finite subset $F\subseteq G$ such that $H(\phi,F)=h(\phi)$.
This is attainment for that particular endomorphism, not merely existence of another flow having the same numerical entropy.

**Theorem 1.7** asserts the equivalence between $\mathfrak L>0$ and realization for **every** abelian group $G$ and **every** $\phi\in\operatorname{End}(G)$ of finite entropy.
It is an equivalence, not a solution to Lehmer's problem.
Zero entropy is included, with $F=\{0\}$ always witnessing attainment.

## Theorem 1.5: proof inspection

The proof at p. 647 reduces the positive entropy infimum in three steps.

1. The torsion restriction either has zero entropy or contributes at least $\log2$, because torsion entropy takes finite values in $\log\mathbb N_{>0}$.
   In the zero case, addition identifies the whole entropy with that of the torsion-free quotient.
   Discarding the other case cannot raise the desired infimum beyond the rational family, because multiplication by $2$ on $\mathbb Q$ already has entropy $\log2$.
2. For a torsion-free group, any infinite-rank cyclic orbit group forces entropy $\infty$.
   Otherwise every subgroup generated by finitely many orbits has finite rank, and their entropies have supremum $h(\phi)$.
   If this supremum is positive, at least one such restriction has positive entropy.
   Passing to the divisible hull preserves entropy and yields $\mathbb Q^n$.
3. For an endomorphism of $\mathbb Q^n$, quotient by the stabilized kernel $\ker_\infty\phi$.
   Proposition 5.8 preserves entropy, and the induced injective endomorphism of a finite-dimensional rational space is automatically surjective.
   Positive entropy rules out the zero-dimensional quotient.

The Algebraic Yuzvinski Formula then identifies the rational family with the required positive Mahler infimum.
In the converse polynomial-to-automorphism direction, first remove all powers of $t$ from a primitive polynomial of positive measure.
This leaves its measure unchanged and makes its constant coefficient nonzero.
The companion matrix is then invertible over $\mathbb Q$.
The normalization correction to Remark 7.5 below is necessary when writing that step explicitly.

No invalid subtraction of infinite entropies occurs in this reduction.
The actual Addition Theorem and its external torsion inputs retain the boundaries of [DGB16-U002](Yin_U002.md#dgb16-u002).

## Exact auxiliary interfaces used in the realization proof

**Theorem 7.4, as cited from source reference [11].**
Write $E_{\rm alg}(G)=\{h(\phi):\phi\in\operatorname{End}(G)\}$ and let $E_{\rm alg}$ be the union over all abelian groups.
The equivalent assertions are:

1. $E_{\rm alg}=[0,\infty]$;
2. $\mathfrak L=0$;
3. $\inf((\bigcup_{n\geq1}E_{\rm alg}(\mathbb Z^n))\setminus\{0\})=0$.

If $\mathfrak L>0$, the source additionally asserts $E_{\rm alg}\subseteq\mathcal A\cup\{\infty\}$, where $\mathcal A=\{\log|\alpha|:\alpha\text{ algebraic},\ |\alpha|\geq1\}$ is countable and dense in $[0,\infty)$.
The inclusion must then be proper because $E_{\rm alg}$ has a positive gap above zero.
DGB16 supplies no proof of Theorem 7.4; the exact original primary passage in [11] and its proof were not inspected in this unit.
Its role in Theorem 1.7 is specifically $\mathfrak L=0\Rightarrow E_{\rm alg}=[0,\infty]$.

**Corollary 7.8.**
For torsion-free $G$ and finite-entropy $\phi$, attainment on $G$ is equivalent to attainment on its divisible hull $D(G)$.
A realizing finite set in $D(G)$ can be multiplied by one positive integer into $G$; torsion-freeness makes that multiplication injective on all trajectories, so their cardinalities and $H$ are unchanged.
This implication was checked in both directions.

**Lemma 7.9.**
Suppose $h(\phi)<\infty$, $V_0=0\leq V_1\leq\cdots$ are invariant, and $h(\phi)=\sup_nh(\phi|_{V_n})$.
If one fixed $\eta>0$ satisfies

\[
h(\bar\phi_n)\in\{0\}\cup[\eta,\infty]
\quad\text{for every }n\geq1,
\qquad \bar\phi_n\in\operatorname{End}(V_n/V_{n-1}),
\]

then $h(\phi|_{V_n})$ eventually equals $h(\phi)$.
Addition identifies successive differences with nonnegative quotient entropies.
At most $h(\phi)/\eta$ differences can be positive, so the increasing sequence stabilizes at its supremum.
The finite-entropy hypothesis justifies subtraction and excludes infinite quotient values.
The gap assumption is on **successive quotients**, not merely on the values of restrictions.

**Proposition 7.10.**
Every finite-entropy endomorphism realizes when $G$ is torsion, or when $G$ is torsion-free of finite rank.
In the first case, choose an increasing sequence of finitely generated flows approaching $h$, apply Lemma 7.9 with $\eta=\log2$, and replace its finite flow-generating set $F$ by the finite subgroup $F'=\langle F\rangle$.
The valid finite-subgroup version of Lemma 7.7(a) gives attainment.
To check this interface directly, if $G=T(\phi,F')$, every finite set $E\subseteq G$ lies in some $T_k(\phi,F')$, and

\[
T_n(\phi,E)\subseteq T_{n+k-1}(\phi,F').
\]

Dividing logarithms by $n$ gives $H(\phi,E)\leq H(\phi,F')$ and hence $h(\phi)=H(\phi,F')$.
This is an elementary check of the required corrected interface, not acceptance of the false arbitrary-set statement.

For the second case, Corollary 7.8 reduces to $\mathbb Q^n$.
The inspected primary [GBV15-U001](../GBV15/Yin_U001.md#gbv15-u001), arXiv:1111.1287v3, Theorem 4.17, p. 23, states

\[
h_A(\phi)=H_A(\phi,E_m)\quad(m\in\mathcal N(\phi)),
\]

where $\mathcal N(\phi)$ is a cofinal subset of the positive integers.
This supplies nonempty finite realizing sets for every endomorphism of $\mathbb Q^n$.
The statement and its displayed argument on pp. 23–24 were read; upstream p-adic estimates were not re-proved.
DGB16 cites the published paper's Theorem 4.18, while the retained preprint has Theorem 4.17; no claim of verified numbering correspondence between editions is made.
Rank zero is immediate separately.

## Theorem 1.7: checked mechanism and local corrections

For the contrapositive of universal realization implying $\mathfrak L>0$, the source uses Theorem 7.4 to choose flows with specified small entropies.
With the corrected indexing $h(\phi_n)=2^{-(n+1)}$ for $n\geq0$, the direct-sum flow satisfies

\[
h\Bigl(\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\phi_n\Bigr)=\sum_{n\geq0}2^{-(n+1)}=1.
\]

Every finite subset has support in a finite initial sum, whose entropy is strictly less than $1$.
Therefore no finite subset realizes the total.
Direct-sum continuity and finite-product addition justify both the total and the upper bound.
This proves the required contradiction conditional on the stated Theorem 7.4 interface.

Conversely, assume $\mathfrak L>0$ and $h(\phi)<\infty$.
An approximating chain of finitely generated flows and Lemma 7.9 with $\eta=\mathfrak L$ yield one such subgroup with the full entropy.
It suffices to find a realizing finite set there.
Finite entropy forces each cyclic orbit group to have finite rank; finitely many generators therefore make the whole group finite rank.
Lemma 4.3(b) splits it as $K\times t(G)$ with $K$ torsion-free of finite rank and bounded torsion part.
The endomorphism is a skew product with finite skew image, by the argument of Example 5.2/Proposition 5.4.

Proposition 7.10 supplies realizing sets $F_1\subseteq K$ and $F_2\subseteq t(G)$.
Replace the latter by

\[
F_2'=\langle F_2\cup s_\phi(K)\rangle.
\]

This is a finite subgroup, since the ambient group is abelian torsion and the indicated union is finite.
Monotonicity and the upper bound by $h(\phi_2)$ show it still realizes $\phi_2$.
Proposition 5.3(a) now applies with all its hypotheses, giving equality of every finite trajectory for the skew and direct products.
Thus $F_1\times F_2'$ realizes $h(\phi_1)+h(\phi_2)=h(\phi)$.
The existence conclusion is preserved by these explicit repairs.

### P2 — Theorem 7.1 and Corollary 7.2 omit the zero-root exception (p. 646)

Theorem 7.1 asserts that a monic integer polynomial with a root that is not a root of unity has some root of modulus greater than one.
The polynomial $f(t)=t$ is a counterexample: its only root is $0$, which is not a root of unity, and $m(t)=0$.
The same polynomial disproves the literal equivalence in Corollary 7.2; $\alpha=0$ disproves its algebraic-integer consequence.

The valid Kronecker form permits every root to be either zero or a root of unity when all root moduli are at most one.
Equivalently, for primitive integer polynomials,

\[
m(f)=0\iff f=\pm t^r\prod_j\Phi_j(t)^{e_j}
\]

with nonnegative integer exponents and finitely many nonzero $e_j$.
For algebraic integers, the zero set is $\{0\}$ together with the roots of unity.
This is the necessary reuse correction, not a claim that the original Kronecker paper [29] was checked here.
Alternatively, require a nonzero constant coefficient and a nonzero algebraic number in the printed statements.

The forward proof of Corollary 7.2 has a second local defect: “all roots are roots of $t^m-1$” implies divisibility only after accounting for multiplicities.
For example, $(t-1)^2$ does not divide any $t^m-1$ in characteristic zero.
Use divisibility by $(t^m-1)^r$ with $r$ at least the largest root multiplicity, then Gauss's lemma gives leading coefficient $\pm1$.
In the reverse direction, $m(f)=0$ gives leading coefficient of absolute value $1$, not necessarily the positive monic sign; multiply by $-1$ if necessary.
These corrections do not alter positive-measure infima in Theorem 1.5.

### P2 — Lemma 7.7(a) and (b) are false for their stated arbitrary finite sets (p. 649)

Let $G=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{(\mathbb N)}$, let $\phi$ be the right shift, and let $F=\{e_0\}$.
Then $G=V(\phi,F)$ and each $T_n(\phi,F)$ is a singleton, so $H(\phi,F)=0$, whereas $h(\phi)=\log2$.
This meets the printed hypotheses and disproves both (a) and (b).

Even imposing $0\in F$ does not repair (a): for $G=(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^{(\mathbb N)}$ and $F=\{0,e_0\}$, one has $|T_n(\phi,F)|=2^n$, hence $H(\phi,F)=\log2<\log3=h(\phi)$.
For (a), require $F$ to be a finite subgroup, or replace $F$ in its conclusion by $\langle F\rangle$.
The proof of Proposition 7.10(a) already makes precisely this replacement, so its invocation has a valid interface as checked above.

For (b), the singleton example shows that at least an appropriate generating-set condition involving zero/differences is missing.
A proposed version with $0\in F$ requires checking the exact Pinsker result in reference [8]; that original statement and proof were not verified here.
Do not use (b) as printed, and do not silently declare the proposed repair accepted.
No step in the printed proofs of Theorems 1.5 or 1.7 needs Lemma 7.7(b).

### P2 — Companion-matrix and denominator normalizations are misstated (Remarks 7.5–7.6, p. 648)

For the displayed matrix $C(f)$ with last column $-a_i/a_n$, its monic characteristic polynomial is $f/a_n$, not $f$.
For a primitive $f$ with $a_n\ne0$, the Algebraic Yuzvinski Formula gives $h(C(f))=m(f)$ after the primitive integer rescaling.
For example, $f=2t-1$ gives $C(f)=[1/2]$ and characteristic polynomial $t-1/2$.

Remark 7.5 phrases the construction for arbitrary integer $f$, whereas the paper introduced $m$ for primitive polynomials.
Under the usual extension to all nonzero polynomials, a nonprimitive polynomial contributes its content to $m$ while the companion matrix is unchanged: $f=2t-2$ gives $C(f)=[1]$, hence entropy $0$, but $m(f)=\log2$.
Require primitive $f$ for that matrix construction; otherwise the exact output is $m(f/\operatorname{content}(f))$ with an irrelevant sign normalization.
This does not disprove a separate existence assertion with a different construction; it disproves the asserted equality for the displayed companion matrix.

In Remark 7.6, the monic minimal polynomial $g\in\mathbb Q[t]$ really is the characteristic polynomial of multiplication by $\alpha$ on $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$.
Its entropy is $m(sg)$, not the unscaled polynomial measure $m(g)$ if $s>1$.
At $\alpha=1/2$, $g=t-1/2$ has usual polynomial measure $0$, while $h(\mu_{1/2})=\log2=m(2t-1)$.
The final asserted identity $h(\alpha)=m(\alpha)$ is correct under the stated algebraic-number normalization; repair the intermediate expression to $m(sg)$.

### P3 — Geometric-series indexing and a missing subgroup enlargement (pp. 650–651)

The converse proof writes $h(\phi_n)=2^{-n}$ for $n\in\mathbb N$, uses $G_0$, and asserts $\sum_{n\geq0}2^{-n}=1$ and finite partial sums less than $1$.
The sum is actually $2$.
Replacing $2^{-n}$ by $2^{-(n+1)}$ repairs all displayed inequalities.
Equally, retaining the coefficients and replacing the target bound by $2$ gives the same nonattainment example.
The theorem's implication survives.

The forward proof chooses $F_2$ as a finite subset containing the skew image, but Proposition 5.3(a) requires a finite subgroup.
The explicit enlargement $F_2'$ above supplies that hypothesis while preserving attainment.
Thus this is an omitted local justification, not an obstruction to Theorem 1.7.
