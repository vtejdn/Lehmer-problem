# Witt extension and indices of finite classical spaces

<a id="tay92-u001"></a>
<a id="witt-extension"></a>
## Linear isometries on arbitrary subspaces

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field carrying an alternating form, a Hermitian form for a nontrivial involution, or a quadratic form \(Q\).
Write \(\beta\) for the specified sesquilinear form or, in the quadratic case, the polar form \(\beta(x,y)=Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)\).
Taylor writes \(R=\operatorname{rad}V=V^\perp=\{x:\beta(x,V)=0\}\) for the ambient radical.
A linear isometry \(f:U\to V\) on a subspace is an injective map linear over the original field that preserves the restricted sesquilinear form, or preserves \(Q\) itself in the quadratic case.
When the characteristic is two, preserving the polar form alone need not preserve \(Q\).
Taylor calls \(Q\) nondegenerate if \(R\cap Q^{-1}(0)=\{0\}\). [Forms, isometries and radicals, pp. 54–56](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=62)

Theorem 7.4 gives the precise extension criterion
\[
 f\text{ extends to a linear isometry of }V
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(U\cap R)=f(U)\cap R .
\]
The restricted form on \(U\) may be degenerate.
For \(R=0\) the condition holds for every subspace isometry, including one between totally singular subspaces.
When \(Q\) is nondegenerate in Taylor's convention, any totally singular subspace meets \(R\) trivially, so the condition also holds for isometries between two such subspaces.
For a general subspace of an odd-dimensional quadratic space over a finite field of characteristic two, \(R\) is a line on which \(Q\) vanishes only at zero, and the displayed condition still applies.
For example, trivial intersection of both \(U\) and \(f(U)\) with that line suffices. [Theorem 7.4 and proof, pp. 57–58](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=65) · [Degenerate polar forms, p. 143](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=151)

The extension lies in the group of linear isometries of \(V\).
In the quadratic case Taylor denotes that group by \(O(V,Q)\) and defines \(\Omega(V,Q)\) to be its derived subgroup.
Thus commutators of two elements of \(O(V,Q)\) have image in \(P\Omega(V,Q)\).
The extension theorem itself imposes no derived-subgroup or determinant condition on a single extension. [Orthogonal groups, pp. 136–137](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=144)

<a id="orthogonal-index"></a>
## The index of the orthogonal derived subgroup in large dimension

For a nondegenerate quadratic space of dimension \(n\ge7\) over \(\mathbb F_q\), one has \([O(V,Q):\Omega(V,Q)]\le4\).
First suppose the polar form is nondegenerate.
Corollary 11.42 states that the linear orthogonal group is generated by reflections, with the single exception \(O^+(4,2)\) in its scope.
By Theorem 11.11 the reflection associated to a nonsingular vector \(v\) is the involution \(x\mapsto x-\beta(x,v)v/Q(v)\), which depends only on the line of \(v\).
If \(Q(v)\) and \(Q(w)\) belong to the same square class, a scalar multiple of \(w\) has quadratic value \(Q(v)\).
Witt extension on the two lines then gives an orthogonal transformation conjugating their reflections.
There are at most two nonzero square classes in the finite field, so the abelianization is generated by at most two elements of order dividing two.
This proves the bound and gives at most two in even characteristic when the polar form is nondegenerate. [Reflection formula, pp. 144–145](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=153) · [Corollary 11.42, p. 159](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=167)

In odd dimension \(n=2m+1\) and even characteristic, Theorem 11.9 instead identifies \(O(V,Q)\) with \(\operatorname{Sp}(2m,q)\).
In characteristic two this symplectic group has trivial scalar center, and Theorem 8.8 gives nonabelian simplicity for \(m\ge3\).
Its derived subgroup is then the whole group. [Theorem 11.9, pp. 143–144](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=151) · [Theorem 8.8, p. 74](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=82)

## Witt indices over finite fields

A totally isotropic subspace \(U\) for \(\beta\) satisfies \(\beta(U,U)=0\).
A totally singular subspace for \(Q\) satisfies \(Q(U)=0\).
The Witt index is the common dimension of the maximal totally isotropic subspaces for an alternating or Hermitian form, and of the maximal totally singular subspaces for a quadratic form. [Definitions, pp. 56 and 59](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=64)

| Nondegenerate space | Witt index | Source in the corrected edition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alternating form of dimension \(2m\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) | \(m\) | The symplectic basis gives \(m\) orthogonal hyperbolic pairs. [Symplectic bases, p. 69](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=77) |
| Hermitian form of dimension \(n\) over \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), with involution \(a\mapsto a^q\) | \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) | Corollary 10.3 and hyperbolic splitting leave anisotropic dimension zero or one. [Hyperbolic pairs, pp. 116–117](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=124) |
| Quadratic form of dimension \(2m\) over \(\mathbb F_q\), plus or minus type | \(m\) for plus type, \(m-1\) for minus type | The anisotropic remainder in (11.3) has dimension zero or two, respectively. [Finite fields, pp. 138–139](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=146) |
| Quadratic form of dimension \(2m+1\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) | \(m\) | Theorem 11.2 and (11.3) leave an anisotropic line. [Finite fields, pp. 138–139](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=146) |

Here \(q\) may be even or odd.
The group convention \(U(n,q)\) uses the cardinality of the fixed field, while the module dimension is measured over \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\).
In the last row with \(q\) even, the anisotropic line is the polar radical and the induced form on its quotient is nondegenerate alternating. [Unitary convention, p. 117](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=125) · [Odd-dimensional orthogonal space in characteristic two, p. 143](../../../literature/pdf/Tay92--april2021.pdf#page=151)
