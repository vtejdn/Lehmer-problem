# The rational specialization conjecture and its function-field analogue

<a id="as18-u001"></a>
## Removing cyclotomic factors under monomial specialization

The [published edition](readme.md#journal), §2, formulates Schinzel's conjecture for an irreducible noncyclotomic rational Laurent polynomial \(F\) in \(n\) variables.
Conjecture 2.1 gives a finite set of nonzero integer vectors and a finite set of nonsingular integral \(n\times n\) matrices.
For each direction \(\mathbf a\) orthogonal to none of the listed vectors, some listed \(M\) and integral \(\mathbf b\) satisfy \(\mathbf a=M\mathbf b\).
Factor \(F(\mathbf X^M)\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
The conjecture asserts that specializing its factors at \(t^{\mathbf b}\) and removing their cyclotomic parts gives the irreducible factorization of the cyclotomic-free part of \(F(t^{\mathbf a})\), with multiplicities and up to Laurent units.
Example 2.2 illustrates the need to remove cyclotomic factors even when every isogeny pullback is irreducible.

The authors attribute the conjecture to Schinzel and identify his proved cases in one variable and in the non-self-inversive case.
Their Theorem 2.4 uses a nonconstant irreducible Laurent polynomial over \(\mathbb C(z)\) and removes factors constant in \(z\), up to scalar.
Theorem 1.3 retains the free variable \(z\) in its bivariate output.
The toric Bertini comparison starts with a dominant map onto the torus of the same dimension as its source.
The [conditional cubic specialization](../../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v185.md#schinzel) uses Conjecture 2.1 at that expressly conjectural scope.
The statements, alternatives and definition of constant part were checked in the published edition, especially printed pages 364–366.
The proof of the function-field analogue and Schinzel's original arguments were outside this inspection.
