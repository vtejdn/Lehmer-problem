# Alternating sections inside point stabilizers

<a id="bs22-u001"></a>
<a id="stabilizers"></a>
## Theorems 1 and 5

For an integer \(d\ge5\), the authors denote by \(\Gamma_d\) the class of finite groups with no section isomorphic to \(A_d\).
For \(G\le\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)\) and \(\Delta\subseteq\Omega\), let \(G_{(\Delta)}\) be its pointwise stabilizer, the subgroup fixing every point of \(\Delta\).
Theorem 1 gives an absolute linear function \(f(d)\) such that \(b(G)\le f(d)\) if \(G\) is finite primitive and its point stabilizers belong to \(\Gamma_d\).
Transitivity makes all one-point stabilizers conjugate.
Writing \(n=|\Omega|\ge2\), if \(\log|G|/\log n>f(d)\), each point stabilizer therefore has \(A_d\) as a section, since \(b(G)\ge\log|G|/\log n\). [Author manuscript, Theorem 1, p. 1, and stabilizer notation, p. 2](../../../literature/pdf/BS22--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=1) · [Base-size lower bound](../LS99/Yin_U001.md#base)

Theorem 5 treats a non-affine primitive group.
For integers \(1\le c\le n\) and \(d\ge5\), it gives an absolute function \(g(c,d)\), linear in \(c\) and \(d\), such that \(b(G)\le g(c,d)\) whenever \(G_{(\Delta)}\in\Gamma_d\) for every \(c\)-element subset \(\Delta\).
Thus \(\log|G|/\log n>g(c,d)\) forces an \(A_d\) section in the pointwise stabilizer of at least one such subset.
When \(c>1\), different subsets can lie in different \(G\)-orbits, so this deduction specifies existence.
The theorem statements give no numerical coefficients for \(f\) or \(g\).
This inspection covers the two statements, the proofs' stated scopes in §§2.2 and 4, and the elementary deductions from base size.
