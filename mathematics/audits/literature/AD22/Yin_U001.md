# Power fields and the Galois closures of roots of lacunary polynomials

<a id="ad22-u001"></a>

## Conjugate groups, power fields and Proposition 2.4

Fix a nonzero algebraic number \(\alpha\) with distinct conjugates \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_s\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
The Section 2 notation defines \(M_\alpha=\langle\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_s\rangle\) inside \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}\), \(r(\alpha)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}(M_\alpha\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q)\), and \(e(\alpha)\) as the exponent of the finite torsion subgroup of \(M_\alpha\).
Thus \(e(\alpha)\) is the least positive integer annihilating that subgroup under powering and equals one when the subgroup is trivial.
Write \(F=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\), the maximal abelian extension of \(\mathbb Q\) in the chosen algebraic closure, and let \(D=[N:F]\) for the normal closure \(N\) of \(F(\alpha)/F\).
Proposition 2.4 gives an effective absolute \(C\ge1\) such that every nontorsion \(\alpha\) satisfying \(F(\alpha^{e(\alpha)})=F(\alpha)\) obeys
\[
h(\alpha)\ \ge\ (16D)^{-C/(\log\log(16D))^{1/3}}.
\]
Here \(h\) denotes the absolute logarithmic Weil height and the logarithms are natural. [Author manuscript, pp. 4–6](../../../literature/pdf/AD22--author.pdf#page=5)

The rank step in the proof can be performed inside \(N\).
The manuscript initially takes the rank of the group generated by all rational conjugates, while its use of Delsinne's height bound needs independent conjugates whose joint relative degree is bounded by \(D\).
For that step take the subgroup generated by the conjugates over \(F\), of rank \(r_F\).
Powering by \(e(\alpha)\) kills its torsion and its image is free of rank \(r_F\).
The powers of the relative conjugates generate \(N\) as a field over \(F\) because \(F(\alpha^{e(\alpha)})=F(\alpha)\).
Hence \(\operatorname{Gal}(N/F)\) acts faithfully on this free group, and injectivity of reduction modulo \(3\) on finite subgroups of \(\mathrm{GL}_{r_F}(\mathbb Z)\), as used in Lemma 2.2, gives \(D\le3^{r_F^2}\).
One may therefore choose \(n\) independent relative conjugates with \(n\asymp(\log\log(16D))^{1/3}\) for large \(D\).
Their joint relative degree is at most \(D\) and each has height \(h(\alpha)\).
In the notation of Delsinne's theorem, \(\log c_2(n)\) and \(\kappa_2(n)\) are bounded above by \(\exp(O(n^2\log(2n)))\).
Thus \(\log c_2(n)+\kappa_2(n)\log\log(16D)=o(\log(16D))\), and the product-of-heights bound gives \(-\log h(\alpha)\ll\log(16D)/n\).
For bounded \(D\) its one-coordinate version supplies a positive effective lower bound.
This verifies that the asserted degree can be used even when \(\alpha\) has fewer conjugates over \(F\) than over \(\mathbb Q\). [Delsinne's published statement and constants](../Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001)

Lemma 3.1 gives a sufficient test for the power-field equality at every positive integer exponent \(a\).
If the monic rational minimal polynomial \(P\) of \(\alpha\) belongs to no \(\mathbb Q[X^\delta]\) with \(\delta>1\), then \(F(\alpha^a)=F(\alpha)\).
Indeed, set \(E=F(\alpha^a)\cap\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(\delta=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):E]\).
All conjugates of \(\alpha\) over \(E\) have the same \(a\)-th power, so their product is \(\zeta\alpha^\delta\) for a root of unity \(\zeta\).
This product belongs to \(E\), while \(\zeta\in F\cap\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subset E\), giving \(\alpha^\delta\in E\).
The product of \(X^\delta-\sigma(\alpha^\delta)\) over all rational embeddings of \(E\) is monic rational of degree \(\deg P\) and vanishes at \(\alpha\).
It equals \(P\), forcing \(\delta=1\) by hypothesis.
The converse fails for the source's example \(\alpha=\sqrt2\). [Author manuscript, pp. 6–7](../../../literature/pdf/AD22--author.pdf#page=7)

## Proposition 3.3 for a root of an ordered sparse relation

Let \(k\ge1\), let \(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_k\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}\), and let the integer exponents satisfy \(w=m_0>m_1>\cdots>m_k=0\).
Set \(h_*=k(h(\gamma_0:\cdots:\gamma_k)+\log k)\), using the absolute logarithmic projective height of the coefficient point.
For coefficients in a number field \(L\), this height is the sum of the \(\log\max_i|\gamma_i|_v\) with the usual local degree weights divided by \([L:\mathbb Q]\).
Suppose that \(\sum_{i=0}^k\gamma_i\alpha^{m_i}=0\), that every sum \(\sum_{i=0}^j\gamma_i\alpha^{m_i}\) for \(0\le j<k\) is nonzero, that \(\alpha\) is nontorsion and that \(F(\alpha^{e(\alpha)})=F(\alpha)\).
There is an effective absolute \(c>0\) such that for \(w\ge h_*/c\)
\[
[N:F]\ \ge\ (w/h_*)^{\,c(\log\log(w/h_*))^{1/3}} .
\]
The constant may be decreased so that the ratio is in the range of the displayed logarithms.
The expression \(h_*\) is positive here, since its vanishing would force \(k=1\) and a coefficient ratio of height zero, making every root of the binomial torsion.
The integer \(w\) is the width of the displayed relation, even when the rational degree of \(\alpha\) is smaller.
The proposition permits reducible relations and puts no coprimality condition on these exponents.
The manuscript's extra subscript on the last \(\alpha\) in hypothesis 1 has been read as the same root \(\alpha\) throughout, as in the defining equation and the following proof. [Author manuscript, p. 8](../../../literature/pdf/AD22--author.pdf#page=9)

For each cut \(j<k\), the nonvanishing partial sum equals the negative of the tail.
After dividing it by \(\alpha^{m_j}\), the prefix bounds it at places with \(|\alpha|_v\le1\), and the tail supplies the additional factor \(|\alpha|_v^{m_{j+1}-m_j}\) at places with \(|\alpha|_v>1\).
The number of terms in the applicable sum costs at most \(\max(j+1,k-j)\) at the archimedean places and one elsewhere.
The product formula gives \((m_j-m_{j+1})h(\alpha)\le h(\gamma_0:\cdots:\gamma_k)+\log\max(j+1,k-j)\).
Summing yields \(w h(\alpha)\le h_*\).
With \(T=w/h_*\), Proposition 2.4 gives \(\log(16D)\ge C^{-1}\log(T)(\log\log(16D))^{1/3}\).
Since \(D\ge1\), the last factor has a positive absolute lower bound.
Thus \(\log(16D)\gg\log T\), and for sufficiently large \(T\) one has \(\log\log(16D)\gg\log\log T\).
Substitution in the same inequality gives the asserted power of \(T\).

The inspection covered the Section 2 definitions, the relative-degree step and height estimate of Proposition 2.4, the norm proof of Lemma 3.1 and the proof of Proposition 3.3 in the [identified edition](readme.md#edition).
These statements are accepted for their stated hypotheses, using the existing source check of Delsinne's product bound.
The original proof of Delsinne's theorem and the rest of the Amoroso–David article were outside this inspection.
