## Probability Spaces and Basic Operator Terminology

Definition 1.1, printed pp. 3-4 / PDF pp. 20-21: a noncommutative probability space is a unital complex algebra with a unital linear functional.
A trace satisfies $\varphi(ab)=\varphi(ba)$; a star-probability space additionally has a star-algebra and positive functional.
Definition 1.3, printed p. 5 / PDF p. 22, defines faithfulness by $\varphi(a^*a)=0\Rightarrow a=0$.
Definition 1.1(3) gives the usual selfadjoint, unitary and normal conditions.
In the project, the von Neumann algebra, faithful normal tracial state and chosen tuple are stated in each consumer; these hypotheses are not new indexed definitions for each model.

## Free Independence

Definition 5.3, printed pp. 64-65 / PDF pp. 81-82: unital subalgebras $(A_i)$ are free when $\varphi(a_1\cdots a_k)=0$ for centered $a_j\in A_{i(j)}$ with neighboring indices distinct.
Sets are free when their generated unital algebras are free.
Star-freeness uses the generated star-algebras.
This distinction matters when a holomorphic subalgebra is used later; it does not change the initial algebra in a rationality statement automatically.

## Haar Unitary

Definition 1.12(1), printed p. 11 / PDF p. 28: $u$ is unitary and $\varphi(u^k)=0$ for every nonzero integer $k$.
The source's finite-order $p$-Haar variant is not required for the current Claims and is not separately registered.

<a id="semicircular"></a>
## Semicircular Element

Definition 2.16 and Remark 2.17(2), printed p. 29 / PDF p. 46: a selfadjoint element is semicircular of radius $r>0$ when its law has density $2(\pi r^2)^{-1}\sqrt{r^2-t^2}$ on $[-r,r]$.
Standard semicircular means radius 2 and variance 1.
Different radii are parameter choices.
Circular/Fock realizations retain their explicit scaling in the consuming model.

## Free Cumulants

Definition 11.3, printed p. 175 / PDF p. 192, defines $\kappa_\pi=\sum_{\sigma\leq\pi}\varphi_\sigma\mu(\sigma,\pi)$ on the lattice of noncrossing partitions and $\kappa_n=\kappa_{1_n}$, with arguments distributed in block order.
The usual moment-cumulant inversion is an established source identity, not a new project Claim.

<a id="r-diagonal"></a>
## R-Diagonal Element

Notation 15.2 and Definition 15.3, printed pp. 240-241 / PDF pp. 257-258: all star-cumulants vanish except those of even length alternating between $a$ and $a^*$.
Odd-length sequences are explicitly declared nonalternating.
The two determining sequences are $\kappa_{2n}(a,a^*,\ldots,a,a^*)$ and $\kappa_{2n}(a^*,a,\ldots,a^*,a)$; in the tracial case they coincide.
This definition does not make $a$ normal.
Yin_C_F0017 uses the cumulant property, while Yin_C_F0019 constructs its R-diagonal element from a separate normal generating pair.
The Haar-product and injective polar results remain the accepted results in NS06-U001; their proofs are not reconstructed here.

## Exact finite matrix adaptation

Fix a common positive integer \(m\) and positive integers \(n_1,\ldots,n_d\).
Let all entries of the matrices \(C_j=(c^{(j)}_{a\alpha})\in M_{m,n_j}(\mathcal N)\) form one standard free circular family: \(\tau(c^*c)=1\).
Define

\[
Y_j=\frac1m C_jC_j^*\in M_m(\mathcal N),\qquad
\tau_m=\operatorname{tr}_m\otimes\tau.
\tag{N1}
\]

The resulting \(Y_j\) are freely independent free Poisson elements of rates \(n_j/m\) and jump one.
Here is the exact verification, including their joint law.

Write \(y^{(j)}_{ab}=m^{-1}\sum_\alpha c^{(j)}_{a\alpha}(c^{(j)}_{b\alpha})^*\).
Theorem 11.12 applied to a list of \(r\) such products has only pairing contributions.
The join condition permits only

\[
\{(1,2r),(2,3),(4,5),\ldots,(2r-2,2r-1)\}.
\]

For this pairing, covariance forces all colors \(j\) equal, all column indices \(\alpha\) equal, and \(b_\ell=a_{\ell+1}\), with \(a_{r+1}=a_1\).
Thus

\[
\kappa_r\bigl(y^{(j_1)}_{a_1b_1},\ldots,y^{(j_r)}_{a_rb_r}\bigr)
=\begin{cases}
n_j m^{-r}\prod_{\ell=1}^r\delta_{b_\ell,a_{\ell+1}},
 &j_1=\cdots=j_r=j,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{N2}
\]

For \(r=1\), this says \(\tau(y^{(j)}_{ab})=(n_j/m)\delta_{ab}\), so the scaling in (N1) is fixed even before computing higher moments.

For an independent check of the passage from entries to the **scalar matrix law**, take an abstract free family \(x_j\) of free Poisson elements with rates \(n_j/m\), freely adjoin matrix units \(e_{ab}\) of trace \(\delta_{ab}/m\), and set \(p=e_{11}\).
Theorem 14.18 says that the entries \(e_{1a}x_je_{b1}\), in the corner trace \(\tau_p=m\tau\), have precisely the joint cumulants (N2): its factor is \(m^{1-r}\kappa_r(x_j)=n_jm^{-r}\).
The map

\[
x\longmapsto(e_{1a}xe_{b1})_{a,b=1}^m
\]

is the trace-preserving unital star-isomorphism from the ambient algebra to its matrix realization over \(p\).
Equality of all joint entry moments then gives equality of the scalar joint matrix moments of this reconstructed family and (N1).
This proves both the rates and freeness of \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_d\); matching their separate marginal laws alone would not prove freeness.
Since the matrices are bounded, their moments determine the required joint tracial law.

Every finite list of positive rational rates admits the common representation \(\lambda_j=n_j/m\).
No limit of matrix sizes is used.
In particular rates below one and their nonzero kernel projections are included.
Replacing a positive irrational rate by rational approximants does not establish any rational-operator boundedness theorem at that rate.
