# Two powers of independent local units

<a id="bl96-u001"></a>
## The residue order in the valuation bound

On November 21, 2026, the source inspection covered the definitions and Theorem 3 on pages 2–4 of the [author edition](readme.md).
The valuation and height conventions were checked against section 2, and the parameters and inequality against the displayed theorem.
The theorem is accepted as a published input within these hypotheses. Its proof was outside the scope of the inspection.

Fix algebraic numbers \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) in an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_p\), with normalized valuation \(v(p)=1\).
Write \(K_v=\mathbb Q_p(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\), let \(f\) be its residue degree, and put \(D=[\mathbb Q(\alpha_1,\alpha_2):\mathbb Q]/f\).
A local unit has valuation zero, and a principal unit is a unit \(x\) with \(v(x-1)>0\).
For local units \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\), the parameter \(g\) is the least positive integer such that both \(\alpha_1^g\) and \(\alpha_2^g\) are principal units.
Thus \(g\) is the order of the subgroup generated by their two reductions in the cyclic multiplicative group of the residue field, and \(g\mid p^f-1\).

Assume that the two units are multiplicatively independent, so an equality \(\alpha_1^{r}\alpha_2^{s}=1\) with integral \(r,s\) forces \(r=s=0\).
For positive integers \(b_1,b_2\), write \(\Lambda=\alpha_1^{b_1}-\alpha_2^{b_2}\).
Using the absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h\), choose \(L_i=\log A_i\ge\max\{h(\alpha_i),(\log p)/D\}\) and set \(b'=b_1/(D L_2)+b_2/(D L_1)\).
With natural logarithms, Theorem 3 gives
\[
 v(\Lambda)\le \frac{24pgD^4 L_1 L_2}{(p-1)(\log p)^4}
 \max\{\log b'+\log\log p+0.4,\,10(\log p)/D,\,10\}^2.
\]
The theorem allows ramified local fields and uses the actual residue order \(g\) before its replacement by \(p^f-1\) in Corollary 1.
