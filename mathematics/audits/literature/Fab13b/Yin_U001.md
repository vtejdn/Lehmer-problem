# Computing local and directional degrees by reduction

Faber states the algebraic reduction formula for a nonconstant rational map \(\varphi\) over an algebraically closed complete field with a nontrivial non-Archimedean absolute value.
At a type II point \(x\) of the Berkovich projective line, choose separate source and target coordinates taking \(x\) and \(\varphi(x)\), respectively, to the Gauss point of the closed unit disk.
The resulting map has nonconstant reduction, whose rational degree is the local degree of \(\varphi\) at \(x\).
The source coordinate identifies tangent directions at \(x\) with points of the residue projective line.
The directional multiplicity at such a point is the ordinary local multiplicity of the reduced rational function there. [Proposition 3.13, preprint p. 13](../../../literature/pdf/Fab13b.pdf#page=13)

The inspection checked the hypotheses, both coordinate changes and both multiplicity statements against the [identified edition](readme.md#edition).
Faber attributes the formula to Rivera-Letelier and cites Baker and Rumely's Theorem 9.42.
The underlying proof was outside this inspection.
For the [two-row disk calculation](../../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#paths), the target coordinate is fixed throughout so that the original residue labels remain comparable along a path.
Source normalization alone can therefore leave the row ratio constant after reduction.
