## The exact external theorem

Let K be a field of characteristic zero, let n>=1, let Gamma be a subgroup of (K*)^n of finite multiplicative rank r, and fix a_1,...,a_n in K*.
For the equation

    a_1 x_1+...+a_n x_n=1,      x in Gamma,                 (1)

a solution is nondegenerate when

    sum_(i in I) a_i x_i !=0
        for every nonempty I subset {1,...,n}.             (2)

The full subset already sums to one, so one may equivalently test the nonempty proper subsets.
Theorem 1.1 states

    #{nondegenerate solutions of (1)}
        <= exp((6n)^(3n)(r+1)).                            (3)

Here exp has the ordinary natural base.
The rank is the rank of the group of tuples under coordinatewise multiplication.
The source allows a finite-rank group that need not itself be finitely generated: a finitely generated subgroup of the same rank may have torsion quotient.
There is no hypothesis that the variables x_i be distinct, that their exponents be distinct, or that the field have a specified number-field degree.
Coefficients are fixed nonzero field elements, and the bound is uniform in those coefficients.

The introduction states the theorem with K algebraically closed.
The advertised arbitrary characteristic-zero-field form follows by viewing the same Gamma and coefficients in an algebraic closure of K.
The embedding is injective, preserves the rank, and introduces no new solutions inside that fixed Gamma.
There is no need to embed an arbitrary field into C.

The rank warning appears explicitly on printed p.810: if the individual variables range over one scalar group Gamma' subset K* of rank r', the applicable tuple group (Gamma')^n has rank n r'.
Thus a scalar rank-one hypothesis does not make (3)'s r equal to one for independently varying coordinates.

<a id="ess-normalized-power-shapes"></a>
## Direct specialization to fixed-sign vanishing relations

Let alpha in K* be nontorsion, meaning that it is not a root of unity.
Fix an integer k>=2 and a fixed ordered sign pattern epsilon_1,...,epsilon_k in {-1,1}.
Consider ordered integer exponent tuples e=(e_1,...,e_k) satisfying

    sum_(i=1)^k epsilon_i alpha^(e_i)=0.                   (4)

Call (4) minimal when no nonempty proper subset of its k labeled terms has sum zero.
Repeated exponents are allowed.
Two exponent tuples have the same normalized shape if one is obtained from the other by adding the same integer to all k coordinates.
Equivalently, a shape is specified by

    (e_1-e_k,...,e_(k-1)-e_k).                            (5)

Dividing (4) by -epsilon_k alpha^(e_k) gives (1) with

    n=k-1,
    a_i=-epsilon_i/epsilon_k in {-1,1},
    x_i=alpha^(e_i-e_k),
    Gamma=<alpha>^(k-1).                                 (6)

This Gamma has rank exactly k-1.
It is generated by the k-1 tuples having alpha in one coordinate and one in every other coordinate.
If a product of integer powers of these generators is the identity tuple, each coordinate gives alpha^(u_i)=1.
Nontorsion forces every u_i=0.
This proves multiplicative independence, without using the number-field degree or a unit-rank formula.

Minimality of (4) is exactly the nondegeneracy condition after normalization.
A vanishing subset that omits the anchored kth term is a vanishing subsum on the left of (1).
If a vanishing proper subset contains that term, its nonempty complement omits the anchor and also vanishes, since the full sum is zero.
These two possibilities prove both directions of the equivalence.

Since alpha is nontorsion, its distinct integer powers are distinct.
Thus normalized exponent shapes in (5) are in bijection with the corresponding tuple solutions in (6).
Theorem 1.1 therefore supplies the explicit uniform bound

    number of minimal ordered shapes for a fixed sign pattern
        <= B_k := exp(k [6(k-1)]^(3(k-1))).                (7)

For k=2 one may use the sharper elementary bound B_2=1: opposite signs force equal exponents; equal signs would require a power of alpha to equal -1, contrary to nontorsion.
A one-term block cannot vanish.

Equation (7) does not include a factor 2^k, because the sign pattern is already fixed.
A union over all sign patterns could be bounded separately by 2^k B_k if required.
It counts labeled ordered shapes; it does not identify permutations of terms.
Coalescing repeated exponents is unnecessary and would change the coefficients to nonzero integers or introduce cancellations that must be handled explicitly.

If every e_i is restricted to {0,...,N-1}, each normalized shape has at most N representatives, because e_k determines every other coordinate.
Hence (7) gives at most N B_k such minimal ordered tuples in a length-N window.
This is a bound on the number of shapes or their translates, not a bound on the magnitudes of their exponent gaps.

No algebraicity, integrality or unit assumption on alpha is needed for this specialization; those conditions come from G0001's ambient problem.
The source's phrase “unit equation” must not be read as requiring all variables to be units of the maximal order of a prescribed number field.
Its actual domain is the stated multiplicative group.

## What this supplies, and what it does not supply, to high moments

An arbitrary equality between two r-term lists of powers can be degenerate.
For example, the four-term identity alpha^a-alpha^a+alpha^b-alpha^b=0 has infinitely many normalized shapes as a-b varies, even though each of its two minimal blocks has one shape.
Applying (7) to the full degenerate relation would be invalid.
One must first partition the labeled terms into minimal zero-sum blocks and count the choices of such a partition and the independent translations of its blocks.

This bounded source unit supplies (7), the nondegeneracy equivalence and the exact rank accounting to the [complete current block-counting argument C_G276](../../../claims/Yin_C_G0276_v1.md#uniform-fixed-moment-energy), following the [current complete high-moment interface C_G275](../../../claims/Yin_C_G0275_v1.md#faithful-high-moment-arithmetic-interface).

Their [current block-energy finding](../../reviews/Yin_R_G0534_v1.md#current-block-energy-finding) and [current Fourier finding](../../reviews/Yin_R_G0533_v1.md#current-primitive-fourier-finding) are self-reviewed.
The block-counting argument and its actual review scope remain in their own record.
In particular, no uniform estimate of the form A_(N,r)<=(C r N)^r for r proportional to N follows merely by writing down (7).
The explicit B_k grows extremely quickly with k; a fixed-order asymptotic and an estimate uniform in growing order are different assertions.
There is no claim that this source alone proves an entropy gap.
