# Voutier's explicit lower bounds for logarithmic Mahler measure

<a id="height"></a>
## Theorem and Corollary 2

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero algebraic number of degree \(d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\ge2\) which is not a root of unity.
For its primitive integer minimal polynomial \(a_d\prod_{i=1}^d(X-\alpha_i)\) with \(a_d>0\), use \(M(\alpha)=a_d\prod_i\max(1,|\alpha_i|)\) and \(h(\alpha)=d^{-1}\log M(\alpha)\).
The unnumbered Theorem gives
\[
 d\,h(\alpha)=\log M(\alpha)>
 \frac14\left(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d}\right)^3 .
\]
Corollary 2 also gives \(d\,h(\alpha)>2/(\log(3d))^3\) throughout the same range.
All logarithms are natural.
At \(d=2\) the first right-hand side is negative and the corollary gives a strictly positive bound.
Both statements apply to arbitrary algebraic numbers under the displayed hypotheses, including algebraic numbers that are not integers.
They use the degree of the individual number and the Mahler measure of its minimal polynomial. [Author manuscript, pp. 1, 3–4](../../../literature/pdf/Vou96--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=3) · [Mahler and absolute height conventions](../Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization)

The direct inspection checked the hypotheses, strict inequalities, factors, parentheses and height normalization against Section 1 of the [identified author edition](readme.md#edition).
Voutier uses the Cantor–Straus determinant method to obtain an effective height estimate, and his discussion of Corollary 2 also uses low-degree information and Smyth's result.
The height bounds are accepted as established external inputs at statement scope.
The determinant proof and numerical parameter verification were not independently audited.
