# The first Frobenius correction in Voloch's plane curve argument

<a id="vol99-u001"></a>
## Intersecting the curve with its correction

For an integral plane polynomial \(f\) over an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\), let \(\sigma\) be the lift of residue Frobenius on coefficients and \(f_0\) the reduction.
On printed pages 479–480 Voloch writes the condition at prime-to-\(p\) roots of unity modulo \(p^2\) as two equations in the residue coordinates.
One equation is \(f_0=0\); the other is the reduction of \((f^\sigma(X^p,Y^p)-f(X,Y)^p)/p\).
Its degree is at most \(p\deg f\).
On the curve, its two logarithmic partial derivatives equal the \(p\)-th powers of \(X(f_0)_X\) and \(Y(f_0)_Y\).
If the curve divides the correction, their ratio where defined gives a constant logarithmic slope in \(\mathbb F_p\).
The lemma on pages 480–481 uses coefficient comparison to exclude this under the paper's degree and support hypotheses. [Published edition](readme.md)

The theorem on page 479 bounds by \(p(\deg f)^2\) the number of torsion or zero coordinate pairs where the valuation exceeds one, assuming irreducible nonbinomial reduction of the same degree and \(p>(\deg f)^2+2\).
The portion used here is the correction and its differentiation for prime-to-\(p\) coordinates.
The application to nontorsion units in a number field also needs a separate ramification count for a primitive monomial section and an archimedean estimate.
