<a id="bcp82-u001"></a>

## The exponent in the 1982 result

The authors' abstract fixes \(c_0\) and considers a primitive permutation group \(G\) of degree \(n\).
Among the composition factors of \(G\), each alternating simple group must have degree at most \(c_0\) and each classical simple group must have dimension at most \(c_0\).
There is then a constant \(c(c_0)\) such that \(|G|\le n^{c(c_0)}\).
The abstract specifies no numerical value or growth rate for \(c(c_0)\).
This inspection covers the authors' abstract only. [Authors' abstract at St Andrews](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/on-the-orders-of-primitive-groups-with-restricted-nonabelian-comp/) · [Publisher's article](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(82)90323-4)

Liebeck and Shalev's 2003 survey states the consequence for a primitive group with no \(A_d\) section and reports an exponent \(O(d\log d)\) obtainable from the 1982 proof.
Immediately after Theorem 3.1 they credit an unpublished proof of the linear exponent to Pyber.
Their own 1999 Theorem 1.4 proves the linear bound for the size of a base, which also proves the linear order exponent. [Survey, §3, p. 3](../../../literature/pdf/LS03--author.pdf#page=3) · [Liebeck–Shalev base-size theorem](../LS99/Yin_U001.md#base)
