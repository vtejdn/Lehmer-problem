## Accepted input and exact domain

Let beta>1 be the real expanding root of

    P(t)=t^4-2t^3+t^2-2t+1.

Proposition 4.23 states that the monoid generated by x -> beta*x and x -> beta*x+1 is not strongly automatic.
Definition 3.2 defines strong automaticity by regularity of its relation language.
Lemma 4.4 identifies the trimmed relation states with

    {x : x=A(beta)=beta^(-1) B(beta^(-1)),
          A,B in {-1,0,1}[t]}.

The result is accepted for the original alphabet {0,1} at this one beta.
The proof supplies an infinite state domain using descent in the neutral complex embedding and a finite residual check.
Its established conclusion is an accepted mathematical input, while the stated source proof-inspection limits remain in force.

## Local normalization and use

For the original difference recurrence X_(j+1)=beta X_j+eta_j, eta_j in {-1,0,1}, the set of states reachable from zero and admitting a finite continuation to zero is exactly the displayed set.
The negative sign from solving the continuation equation is absorbed by the symmetric alphabet.
Thus it is the D of [L G0157](../../../ledger/Yin_L_G0157_v1.md#global-prime-support-finite-core-equivalence), with no digit enlargement or field-embedding projection.

The source's nonregularity also transfers directly: any ternary word is a digitwise difference of two binary words.
If the ternary zero language had a finite automaton, replacing each ternary transition by the binary digit pairs giving that difference would recognize the binary relation language.
Equal affine maps have equal length because their slopes are beta^n, so the identity-padding convention creates no extra relations.
Reading the opposite composition convention only reverses words and does not alter finite recognizability.

The precise G0001 deduction, its elementary degree check, and new finite tests belong to [D G0008](../../../ledger/Yin_L_G0354_v1.md#mercat-core-consequence).
This supplies an actual fixed Salem control for L G0157.
It does not establish the same assertion for every Salem number or for Lehmer's number, nor a bound for any weighted return series.
