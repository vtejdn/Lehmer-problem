## Checked mechanism

The introduction and Sections 1-2, printed pp.535-540, were read; the mathematical argument on pp.537-540 was inspected directly from page images.
The definition of a small set requires orbit averages of its indicator to tend to zero uniformly over starting points.
For closed sets the proposition on p.538 proves equivalence with nullity for every invariant probability measure.
The proof uses weak limits of empirical measures and closed-set approximation; nullity for only one measure, or the same assertion for an arbitrary nonclosed set, is insufficient.
The complete capacity/SBP conventions are retained at [Lin99-U001](../Lin99/Yin_U001.md#orbit-capacity-and-small-boundary).

Section 2 assumes a nonperiodic point and a dense supply of radii whose spheres are small.
It builds a continuous Cantor-type function by successively restricting where finer values can occur.
At each scale the exceptional orbit frequency is uniformly bounded, and the integers controlling those frequencies are chosen so the total word-count cost remains below the prescribed entropy budget.
The construction is explicitly continuous but generally not Lipschitz (p.540).
This supplies the rare-event and summable-correction idea behind the later quotient formulation; it supplies neither a linear observation nor a bounded polynomial degree.

## Additional reading and limits

Sections 3-5, pp.541-545, were read for the hypotheses and contrast between continuous, differentiable and one-sided-shift factors.
The transversality results and their external differentiable-topology foundations were not independently audited.

That statement was read as a regularity contrast; this account does not establish a new uniformly quantified bound, audit all of its dynamical foundations, or transfer it to arithmetic flows.
Section 5's one-sided interval-shift factor mechanism was read; it does not concern the finite-alphabet finite-entropy calibration used in the G0001 comparison.

The relevant strategy lesson is the need to account for both the amount of exceptional orbit activity and the complexity or regularity of the observation used to encode it.
