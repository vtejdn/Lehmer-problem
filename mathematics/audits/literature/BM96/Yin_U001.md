# Compactness and isolated points in the class of Pisot rational functions

On 2026-11-16 a research agent for Yin inspected the opening discussion and definition on p. 1 and the first two paragraphs of p. 2 of the [author's typescript](readme.md#editions).
The set of Pisot numbers is closed in the real line and consists of algebraic integers greater than one with every other conjugate in the open unit disk.
For a Pisot number \(\theta\) with monic minimal polynomial \(P\), put \(Q(z)=z^{\deg P}P(1/z)\).
The class consists of the quotients \(F=A/Q\) for \(A\in\mathbb Z[z]\) with \(A(0)A(1/\theta)\ne0\) and \(|A|\le|Q|\) on the unit circle.
Each quotient has one pole in the open unit disk, a simple pole at \(1/\theta\).
For bounded pole parameters \(\theta\), sequences have subsequences converging meromorphically on compact subsets of the disk to a member of the class, and the pole parameter varies continuously.
Boyd and Mauldin cite Theorem 2.2.1 of their reference [BD] for compactness.
They recall the Dufresnoy–Pisot characterization of the first derived set by strict boundary inequality except at finitely many points.
Consequently a member is isolated precisely when it has modulus one on the entire circle, in which case its numerator is \(\pm P\).
The characterization has no restriction \(\theta<2\).
The inspected text states these compactness and isolation results and attributes their proofs to earlier sources.
