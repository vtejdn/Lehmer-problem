# Algebraic linear relations between two p-adic logarithms

<a id="fp15-u001"></a>
## The logarithm line of an algebraic point on a two-dimensional torus

Fix an embedding of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) in \(\mathbb C_p\).
[Theorem 2.2 of the preprint](readme.md#preprint), p. 2, concerns a positive-dimensional commutative algebraic group \(G/\overline{\mathbb Q}\) and a nonzero \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\)-linear subspace \(V\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}(G)\).
For an algebraic point \(\gamma\) in the logarithm domain, suppose its logarithm is nonzero and belongs to \(V\otimes\mathbb C_p\).
Then a positive-dimensional algebraic subgroup \(H\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) contains \(\gamma\) and satisfies \(\operatorname{Lie}(H)\subseteq V\).
Section 3.1 defines the logarithm domain by convergence of a subsequence of positive powers to the identity and identifies the kernel there with the torsion points.

Take \(G=\mathbb G_m^2\) and algebraic principal units \(a,b\).
Their \(p^j\)-th powers tend to one, and their logarithm vector is \((\log a,\log b)\).
If this vector is nonzero and lies on an algebraic line \(V\), the theorem gives \(\operatorname{Lie}(H^\circ)=V\) by dimension.
The connected one-dimensional subtorus \(H^\circ\) is the kernel of a primitive character \((x,y)\mapsto x^m y^n\).
Its tangent equation gives \(m\log a+n\log b=0\) with coprime integers \(m,n\).
In particular, if \(\log a=\lambda\log b\) with algebraic \(\lambda\) and \(\log b\ne0\), then \(m\ne0\) and \(\lambda=-n/m\) is rational.
If both logarithms vanish, both units are roots of unity.
For algebraic units \(U,V_0\) with finite residue field, dividing each by the Teichmüller lift of its residue gives algebraic principal units, since the lifts are roots of unity.
The implication is qualitative and supplies no bound for the valuation of a nonzero logarithmic form.
The source inspection covers the theorem statement and its logarithm domain.
