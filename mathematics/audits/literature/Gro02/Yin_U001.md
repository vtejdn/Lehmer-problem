<a id="coxeter-decomposition"></a>
## Coxeter decomposition versus canonical Coxeter representation

**Definition (Section 4, preprint page 12).**
For an even unimodular lattice $L$ with isometry $t$, a Coxeter decomposition is an internal direct sum $L=L_1+L_2$ of abelian groups, not necessarily orthogonal, such that each $L_i$ is positive definite and
\[
2L_i^\vee=L_i.
\]
Here the dual uses the full bilinear form restricted to the half-lattice.
Equivalently, that restricted form takes even values and one half of it is a unimodular positive pairing.
If $\operatorname{pr}_i$ denotes the corresponding orthogonal projection into $L_i\otimes\mathbb Q$, the involutions are
\[
\sigma_i=1-2\operatorname{pr}_i,\qquad t=\sigma_1\sigma_2.
\]
Their minus-one eigenspaces are the half-lattices.

Page 13 permits orthogonal root bases after extension to $\mathbb R$ but explicitly does not guarantee that their cross inner products lie in the canonical interval $[-2,0]$.
Nor does an orthogonal real basis provide an integral orthogonal norm-two basis of each half-lattice.
Thus this arithmetic definition does not identify $t$ with a word in the integral root-reflection subgroup or in the canonical geometric representation of a Coxeter system.
The degree-18 example demonstrates that distinction within the source itself.
Its $\mathbb Z\oplus E_8$ half is understood with one half of the full pairing, rather than replacing it by $\mathbb Z^9$ with a standard integral orthonormal basis.

<a id="trace-multiplier-interface"></a>
## Exact ideal and multiplier formulas

The source writes $A_K=\mathcal O_K$ and $A_k=\mathcal O_k$, with $K=\mathbb Q[\alpha]$, $k=\mathbb Q[\alpha+\alpha^{-1}]$, and involution $\bar\alpha=\alpha^{-1}$.
Its pair $(L,c)$ satisfies, on preprint page 5,
\[
L\bar L(c)=\mathcal O_K^\vee,
\qquad \langle a,b\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(ca\bar b),
\]
where $c\in k^\times$ is positive at the real places of $k$ becoming complex in $K$.
Equivalent pairs satisfy $L'=(\gamma)L$ and $c'=c/(\gamma\bar\gamma)$, as in equation (1.10).

For the Coxeter construction, Lemma 5.1 on page 14 introduces
\[
y=\alpha+\alpha^{-1},\quad
\delta=\alpha-\alpha^{-1},\quad
\gamma=1+\alpha^{-1},\quad
\mu=1-\alpha^{-1},\quad
\epsilon=\delta\bar\delta=4-y^2.
\]
The displayed elements $\alpha,\delta,\gamma,\mu$ are units, and $\epsilon$ is positive at the real places becoming complex and negative at the split real places.
The symbol $\gamma$ here is the specific unit of Lemma 5.1; the equivalence formula above allows an arbitrary nonzero field element with the same source notation.

The converse construction on page 15 starts from a fractional $\mathcal O_k$-ideal $M_k$ and a totally positive $d\in k^\times$ satisfying
\[
M_k^2(d)=\mathcal O_k^\vee.
\]
It sets
\[
M=M_k\otimes_{\mathcal O_k}\mathcal O_K,\qquad
c=d/\epsilon,\qquad
\sigma_1(a)=\bar a,\qquad
\sigma_2(a)=\alpha^{-1}\bar a.
\]
Here $\sigma_2=\sigma_1t$ and $t$ is multiplication by $\alpha$.
Page 16 gives
\[
L_1=M_k\delta,\qquad L_2=M_k\mu,\qquad d^*=c\mu\bar\mu,
\]
with full restricted pairings
\[
\langle m\delta,m'\delta\rangle=2\operatorname{Tr}_{k/\mathbb Q}(dmm'),\qquad
\langle m\mu,m'\mu\rangle=2\operatorname{Tr}_{k/\mathbb Q}(d^*mm').
\]
The positive unimodular pairings on the halves are the trace pairings without this factor two.

<a id="explicit-construction-limit"></a>
## What the exact example does not specify

The examples identify $H$, the maximal-order and class-number assertions, the full lattice type and the two half-lattice types.
They do not display an $H$-specific $M_k$, $d$, $c$, an integral basis, or a Gram/isometry matrix.
The formulas above leave an arithmetic choice satisfying the ideal equation and the signs; they are not an explicit numerical multiplier or a bounded unit-search prescription for $H$.
In particular, $c$ is a trace-pairing multiplier and is not asserted to be an algebraic-integer unit.
The [McM02 diagram construction](../McM02/Yin_U002.md#figure-seven-recipe) is a more direct existing entrance for a finite exact representation check, while its integral quotient and fixed-cusp generator questions remain explicit.
