# The common divisor of two independent unit differences

<a id="s-unit-gcd"></a>
## Proposition 2 and its finite-place consequence

Let \(L\) be a number field of degree \(n\) and \(S\) a finite set of places of \(L\) containing all archimedean places.
Its \(S\)-units are the elements of \(L^\times\) of valuation zero at every finite place outside \(S\).
Write \(h\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(\log^-x=\min(0,\log x)\) for \(x>0\).
The authors normalize the absolute values so that \(h(x)=\sum_v\log\max(1,|x|_v)\).
At a finite prime \(\mathfrak p\), this means \(|x|_{\mathfrak p}=(N\mathfrak p)^{-\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)/n}\), where the valuation is the ideal exponent.
See [CZ05, arXiv v2](readme.md#arxiv-v2), equation (1.3) on printed p. 2 and the conventions at the beginning of Section 2 on p. 3.

Fix \(\epsilon>0\).
Proposition 2, printed p. 7, states that apart from finitely many exceptional pairs, every pair of \(S\)-units satisfying (2.8) belongs to one of finitely many one-dimensional subgroups given by a multiplicative relation.
Consequently all but finitely many multiplicatively independent pairs \(u,v\) satisfy
\[
  -\sum_{w\in M_L}\log^-\max\{|u-1|_w,|v-1|_w\}
       \le \epsilon\max\{h(u),h(v)\}.
\]
Multiplicative independence here excludes \(u^a v^b=1\) for every nonzero \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\).

For ordinary units \(u,v\) the finite-place portion of the left side equals
\[
  \frac1n\sum_{\mathfrak p}
    \min\{\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(u-1),
           \operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(v-1)\}\log N\mathfrak p
     =\frac1n\log N\big((u-1,v-1)\big).
\]
Here \((u-1,v-1)\) is the integral ideal generated by the two differences and \(N\) is the absolute ideal norm.
The archimedean summands are nonnegative after the displayed minus sign, so the same upper bound applies to this normalized logarithmic gcd.
I checked the statement of Proposition 2, the places and height conventions, and this conversion in the preprint.
I accept the proposition for this use without an independent review of its proof.
The exceptional-pair statement supplies no cutoff uniform as the field varies.
