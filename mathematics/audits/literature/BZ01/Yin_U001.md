# Dimension of the canonical Newton polygon

Boyer and Zhang use “hyperbolic knot exterior” for a compact connected orientable three-manifold with torus boundary whose interior has a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric.
In Section 4 of the [paper](readme.md), a curve of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-characters containing a discrete faithful character is a norm curve.
Every nonzero peripheral class has nonconstant trace there.
Section 9 constructs the rational canonical curve from the orbit of such a curve under complex field automorphisms and sign twists.
The canonical polynomial in Section 10 is the product of the polynomials of its constituent curves in peripheral eigenvalues.

Proposition 10.1 identifies the canonical Culler–Shalen norm polygon and the Newton polygon of this canonical polynomial as dual polygons.
In particular the latter is two-dimensional, since the former is a ball of a genuine norm on the real plane \(H_1(\partial M;\mathbb R)\).
Corollary 8.3 gives the corresponding duality for the polynomial of one norm curve under Proposition 8.2.
The manifold convention, the norm-curve condition, the construction of the canonical curve and the stated dualities were inspected.
Their proofs were not independently reviewed.
The use of eigenvalues in this paper and the use of squared eigenvalues in [Garoufalidis and Jeon](../GJ24/Yin_U001.md#filling-polynomial) require keeping the two peripheral coordinate conventions explicit.
