# Primitive permutation groups of small minimal degree

<a id="efk16-u001"></a>
## The Liebeck–Saxl comparison

Let \(G\le S_n\) be primitive and let \(\mu(G)\) be the minimum number of points moved by a nonidentity element.
If \(\mu(G)<n/3\), there exist positive integers \(m,k,t\), with \(m\ge5\), such that the permutation domain can be identified with the ordered \(t\)-tuples of \(k\)-subsets of an \(m\)-element set.
In that identification \(n=\binom{m}{k}^{t}\) and \(A_m^{t}\le G\le S_m\wr S_t\), where the base group acts separately in each coordinate and the top group permutes coordinates.
The case \(k=t=1\) gives the natural action.

This is Theorem 7.2 on page 31 of the [author manuscript](readme.md#edition), which attributes the statement to Liebeck and Saxl.
Cameron and Cohen identify the original as Theorem 2 at the foot of page 137 and give the dichotomy at the top of page 138.
Their printed statement reuses the symbol for permutation rank in the product formula, whereas Theorem 7.2 explicitly quantifies the coordinate count independently.
The author manuscript gives the direct power as the lower inclusion.
The two reproductions supply the theorem statement for comparison with the project's [natural actions on conjugate labels](../../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).
The original 1991 proof was outside this inspection.
