## Sharp evaluation, the Gram determinant and the quadratic example

Review date (UTC) 2026-11-20T19:08.
An agent working for Sheng Yin independently inspected the fixed claim and its cited comparison, without participating in proof development or receiving development discussion, author explanations or prior review conclusions.
The argument establishes all the assertions of [the statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#statement).

Reflection preserves the boundary modulus factor by factor, including multiplicity.
The exterior factors contribute \(e^m\) to the modulus of the constant coefficient of \(W\), while the reflected factors contribute \(e^{-m}\) to the modulus of the leading coefficient, since the product of all root moduli is one.
Thus \(|b|=e^{-2m}<1\).
For \(\deg A<d\), \(A/W^*\) has only strictly negative Fourier modes on the circle because its poles are interior and it vanishes at infinity.
This gives \(\langle A/W,B\rangle=0\).
Evaluation of \(A/W\) at zero against \(1-\bar b B\) then yields the claimed squared norm bound, with denominator \(e^{2m}-e^{-2m}\).
The two leading coefficients of \(W-\bar b W^*\) cancel and its constant coefficient is \(e^m(1-|b|^2)\), so scaling attains equality for each prescribed nonzero lowest coefficient.
Translation by a Laurent monomial leaves the energy unchanged.
For integral coefficients \(|a|\ge1\), and \(2\sinh(2m)\sim4m\).
The cited unrestricted-width comparison applies to irreducible \(P\) and integral nondivisible numerators, with the same logarithmic measure and Haar normalization.

In descending monomial order each Gram--Schmidt residual has lowest coefficient one and width at most \(d-1\).
Its squared norm is at least \(c(m)\), and the product of all \(d\) squared norms is \(\det G_d\).
Conjugation symmetry of the weight makes this the squared covolume of the real lattice with the stated integer coordinates.
The inequality \(\gamma_d\ge1\) gives the asserted behavior of the Hermite upper bound even when \(d\) varies.

The Laurent ring is free over \(\mathbb Z[X^q,X^{-q}]\) with basis \(1,\dots,X^{q-1}\), which gives the componentwise divisibility criterion, including negative exponents.
Pullback around the circle by \(X\mapsto X^q\) preserves Haar measure, and the distinct residue classes are orthogonal after division by \(P(X^q)\).
Every nondivisible numerator therefore has at least one component contributing \(\mathcal E(P)\) or more, while the single class of multiples of \(q\) realizes the original infimum as an infimum.

For \(q=2^j\), the translated polynomial reduces to \(X^{2q}\) modulo two and its constant coefficient is \(-6\), so Eisenstein and translation give irreducibility for every \(j\ge0\).
The two reciprocal roots of \(Q\) yield the stated root moduli and the fixed measure \(\log(4+\sqrt{15})\).
On the circle \(|Q(e^{it})|=8-2\cos t\).
Differentiating the average of \((s-2\cos t)^{-1}\) at \(s=8\) gives the monomial energy \(8/60^{3/2}\), with differentiation justified by the positive lower bound for the denominator near \(8\).
Every other nonzero integral numerator has squared coefficient norm at least two and hence energy at least \(2/100>8/60^{3/2}\).
A Laurent monomial with unit coefficient is a unit, so it attains the infimum among numerators indivisible by \(Q\).
This establishes the fixed positive energy throughout the substitution family.
