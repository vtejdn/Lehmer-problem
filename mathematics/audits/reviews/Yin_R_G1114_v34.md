## Finding

The normalized series, fixed-branch second coefficient, periodicity up to finite exceptions, radial probability limit, growing-degree requirement and signed cubic example all hold as stated.
The classification describes subsequential limits and supplies no positive-constant realization of any individual limiting function.

**Normalization and coefficients.**
The one interior pole of \(f/f^*\) is at \(1/\theta\), and \(b\) cancels it while preserving unit boundary modulus.
The resulting disk map has value \(q/\theta<1\) at zero, making \(H_q(0)=1\) and \(\Re H_q>0\).
Schwarz's lemma for its Cayley transform gives the stated derivative and disk bounds.
Differentiating \(bF\) gives \(H_q'(0)=q+\theta^{-1}-j/\delta\).
Thus \(j=\delta(q+\theta^{-1}-H_q'(0))>0\) even at \(q=2\), and \(q\delta=j+O_j(q^{-1})\) for fixed \(j\).

Expanding \(F/(1+zF)\) to order two and then applying the normalization gives \(K_q(0)=j\) and \([z]K_q=u_2-q^3-(2j-1)q\).
Both are integers because division by \(D(0)=1\) preserves integral power series and the numerator divided by \(z\) has vanishing constant.
Substitution of \(B=1-\epsilon H_q\) gives the displayed formula with \(W=1-z^2-z(\theta-z)\epsilon H_q\).
On a fixed strict disk \(1-z^2\) stays bounded away from zero and \(W-(1-z^2)=O_{R,j}(q^{-1})\).
On its boundary the apparent \(z^{-1}\) terms cost only \(O_{R,j}(q^{-1})\) after the leading \(\delta q H_q\) term is separated.
Analyticity throughout the disk then transfers the error to the center and Cauchy's estimate to the first derivative.
Since \(a=jH'_q(0)+O_j(q^{-1})\) is integral, \(|a|\le2j\) eventually.
Expanding \(\delta=j/(q+\theta^{-1}-H'_q(0))\) gives precisely \(j/q+a/q^2+O_j(q^{-3})\).
I also checked the derivative, the two coefficient identities, the rational expression for \(K_q\), and the signed cubic identities below by exact symbolic expansion.

**Limiting measure and periodicity.**
The disk bounds give local compactness, and the limit of \(K_q\) has positive real part since its real part is nonnegative and its constant is \(j>0\).
Convergence by Cauchy's formula and discreteness of the integers give eventual stabilization of each fixed coefficient on the chosen subsequence.
The Herglotz kernel gives \(a_k=2\int\bar v^k\,d\mu\) for \(k\ge1\), whereas the total mass of \(\mu\) is \(j\).
Reality of the series gives symmetry of the measure by uniqueness.
Writing \(X=\Re v\) under \(\mu/j\) yields \(a_1=2j\mathbb E X,\ a_2=2j(2\mathbb E X^2-1)\), proving both bounds and the endpoint concentration.

I checked equation (12.2) and Corollary 12.4 of [Sanders's published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00041-020-09732-y).
At zero the integer function must have value \(2j\) to be the transform of \(2\mu\), and its Fourier–Stieltjes norm is then \(2j\).
Discrete \(\mathbb Z\) meets the locally compact abelian hypothesis.
The bound on the number of subgroups and on each integer coefficient sum bounds the total number of signed cosets in terms of \(j\).
A coset of the trivial subgroup contributes a singleton and each other coset is a congruence class.
After finitely many exceptions the sequence is periodic, with simple root-of-unity poles as the only possible poles of its ordinary generating function.
Changing the constant from \(2j\) to \(j\) in \(K\) affects only its polynomial part.
On the full two-sided sequence, finite Fourier inversion gives the torsion-supported atomic part and the finite exceptions give a trigonometric-polynomial density.
Uniqueness of the Fourier transform followed by the uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition gives positivity of each part.
This use of the source is at statement and applicability depth.

For every actual positive-constant seed, \(D\) vanishes at the interior reciprocal of its exterior root.
The distinct irreducible polynomials \(f,f^*\) are relatively prime, so \(f\) cannot vanish there.
The term subtracted in \(K_q\) is analytic there and its multiplicative factor is nonzero.
This gives a genuine interior pole even though the compact convergence on a fixed branch eventually excludes poles from any prescribed strict disk.

**Weights, degree and the signed example.**
The product of the stated disk factors has the interior zeros of \(B\), unit boundary modulus and positive value \(\prod_i\rho_i=q/\theta\) at zero, fixing the phase.
On \(|z|\le R<1\) the logarithms used in the expansion are analytic for \(\ell_i\) sufficiently small uniformly in the directions.
Their first derivative at zero is the negative Herglotz kernel, and the total quadratic remainder is \(O_R(\sum_i\ell_i^2)\le O_R(L^2)\).
Since \(1-e^{-L}=L+O(L^2)\), this identifies the limit transform of the probability weights with \(K/j\).
Uniqueness and compactness give weak convergence to \(\mu/j\).
On this branch \(\delta\to0\), so the unweighted angular theorem applies separately.

In the varying-\(j\) statement \(q/j\to\infty\) implies \(q\to\infty\), and the derivative bound gives \(\delta\le j/(q-2+\theta^{-1})=O(j/q)\).
Boyd's positive-sign bound then gives \((\log\sigma)^2\ll j/q\).
The quadratic output is eventually excluded, so \(\sigma\) is Salem and its degree tends to infinity by finiteness of bounded-degree algebraic integers of bounded measure.
The numerical Dobrowolski input applies to the total logarithmic measure \(\log\sigma\) of this nontorsion algebraic integer.
Combining the two inequalities gives \(\log d/\log\log d\gg(q/j)^{1/6}=X\).
The first consequence \(\log d\gg X\) implies \(\log\log d\gg\log X\), giving \(\log d\gg X\log X\).
The degree comparison uses exactly the extra degree of \(zf+f^*\).

I checked Lemma 5, equations (38)–(40), and the sign of the cubic following them on printed p. 1256 of [Boyd's journal article](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1978-0491587-8).
For the cubic \(h\) the identity \(h(\theta)=(\theta-q)\theta(\theta+1)-q=0\) gives \(\delta\to0\).
The negative discriminant \(q(4-3q)\) of the stated quadratic excludes nonpositive real roots, so the interior pair has common modulus \(\sqrt{q/\theta}<1\), proving irreducibility by the integer constant argument.
Its sum is \(-1-\delta\) by the trace and its product tends to one, so its directions tend to the two primitive cube roots with equal radial weights.
The replacement \(F=-h/h^*\) gives \(u_1=q^2\), \(h^*-zh=(1+z)(1-z^3)\), and \(K_q=(1-z^2)/(1+z+z^2)\).
This last rational function is exactly the Herglotz transform of the equal-mass pair, with linear coefficient \(-1\).
The entirely cyclotomic associate in this example uses the negative constant sign.
No correction was identified, and the external proof of the Fourier–Stieltjes theorem and the original Dobrowolski proof were outside this review.
