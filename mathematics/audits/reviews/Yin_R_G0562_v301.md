**Parents and quotient actions.**
Complex conjugation fixes the real inverse pairs pointwise and inverts the neutral representatives.
Consequently the projection onto the exterior labels and its Galois translates preserve the rational relation space.
Saturation gives the corresponding decomposition of integral relations, and the minimal support of the exterior circuit forces its parent to be the entire exterior set.
Transitivity then gives one primitive signed relation per parent.
On the exterior parent that relation takes a real torsion value, hence a sign, and transitivity transports this sign to every parent.
After orienting the representatives its exponent vector is all ones, so every lift of a parent permutation uses one common inversion sign there.
Transitivity on individual roots supplies both prescribed images \(x_j^{\pm1}\) for an individual \(x_i\).
The source part must accompany its chosen label to the target part because the partitions are equivariant.
This proves transitivity of the signed products even when a sign and a permutation cannot be prescribed independently.

**Descending along a partition.**
For at least three parts, a difference between distinct signed part indicators has a nonconstant restriction on a parent.
For different parts of the same parent, the untouched third part forces any putative constant to vanish.
Thus all \(2nk\) values are distinct even modulo torsion and form the full orbit of an algebraic unit, closed under inversion.
Only the exterior parent contributes to the measure.
Lifting coefficients on its parts to coefficients on its labels gives the line of constant vectors as the rational nullspace of the \(s_A\).
This verifies nonvanishing, the exterior circuit on the \(|s_A|\), and the bound by the sum of \(|t_i|\).
The same lift on all parents identifies the complete new relation lattice.
Since each parent has its own distinct labels, the preimage of its stabilizer under restriction to the new splitting field is exactly the old stabilizer.
Surjectivity of restriction therefore gives the claimed action on parts.
Any nontrivial block in that action would lift to a larger proper block, verifying primitivity for a maximal choice.

With two parts, squaring makes complementary products actual inverses for either value of \(\omega\).
The nonconstant-indicator test excludes both a self-inverse product and collisions across parents.
The resulting orbit has two values per parent.
Its only exterior value is positive, with logarithm \(2|s_A|\le\sum_i|t_i|\).
When \(\omega=1\) the complement already gives the unsquared inverse, and rational sign normalization preserves the orbit size and measure.
The bound then improves to \(|s_A|\le \tfrac12\sum_i|t_i|\).
With a neutral parent the positive exterior value and its inverse have unit-circle conjugates and give the stated Salem number.
With just one parent their integral trace exceeds two, giving the lower bound \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
Both quotient constructions strictly reduce degree, which proves the least-degree consequence.

**The primitive action on four labels.**

They are independent since the original rank is three.

Hence the orbit takes both values at each matching and every parent has six distinct values.

After choosing \(t_1=\max_i|t_i|\) by relabeling and simultaneous sign change, all \(t_1+t_j\) are positive and sum to \(2t_1\).
