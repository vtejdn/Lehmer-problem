### Cuts, heights and degree

For each nonvanishing initial sum \(C\), the resultant with the monic minimal polynomial is a nonzero integer.
Applying the lower-degree estimate outside the unit circle and the complementary higher-degree estimate inside gives \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,C)|\le d\log L-(a_{j+1}-a_j)m\).
Unit norm is what makes the two sums of the log moduli equal \(m\) and \(-m\).
The consecutive gaps sum to the full width if all cuts survive, and to the width of each block separately after subtracting successive zero sums and removing the initial monomial.
Effective support is at most the actual number of terms, so the v186 lower bound applies to each normalized block as well as to the original relation.
A vanishing cut with at most five terms would make the unit a root of a monomial or binomial, and with at most seven terms it would make the unit a root of a polynomial with at most three terms.
The binomial forces torsion, and v181 excludes the latter possibility strictly below \(\log\theta\).
In the six-term example the first three terms form the fixed irreducible unit cubic for every integer \(u>3\).

The product-formula proof at a single gap and the summation of the gap bounds agree with the cited source passages. [Lenstra, Proposition 2.3](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1999a/art.pdf#page=4) and [Amoroso and David, proof of Proposition 3.3](https://www.rivmat.unipr.it/wwwprotetto/2022-13-1/Riv_Parma_13-1-2022_02.pdf#page=8)
Voutier's main theorem applies to the nontorsion unit at its own degree \(d\), with \(dh(\alpha)=m\).
Its statement is used as an established external input. [Voutier, main theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.3110)
For \(d\ge16\), \(\log\log d/\log d\) is decreasing, so \(d\le D\) gives the required lower estimate in terms of \(D\).
For smaller \(d\), \(\sum_i\log^2|\alpha_i|\le(2m)^2\) and C855 v2 give \(m\ge1/\sqrt{40d}\).
The extremal terms of \(P\) bound each \(|\log|x||\) by \(\log L/b\), and a reciprocal factor allows either end gap to bound every conjugate by inversion.
Thus \(1/(10d)\le 2m\log L/b\), which combined with the summed gap bound yields precisely (2).

### Residual orders and the unique lift

After monic normalization, every rational irreducible factor of the five-term parent has algebraic units as its roots.
Its squarefree reduction has exactly orders \(3t\) for \(t\mid n\) regardless of \(l\).
Counting even incomplete levels gives \(d\le 2t_*\tau(n)\), while the degree estimate gives \(d\gg n\lambda(n)\).
For two prime divisors the geometric sum over complementary divisors at least \(y=n/t_*\) is \(O((1+\log y)/y)\).
Inverting \(y/(1+\log y)\ll1/\lambda(n)\) gives (4), and a bound on \(5^i7^j\) bounds both exponents of the same complementary divisor.
At period \(3n\) the entire reduction of \(S\) is in the gcd, so v166 at one doubling gives the factor \(d/(6n)\) multiplying \(\log 2\).

The selected antichain has degree \(O(A\sqrt n)=o(n\lambda(n))\), excluding every noncyclotomic constituent in a rational lift, also for a subset of its levels.
For an odd part \(3t\), the reduction of \(\Phi_{2^j3t}\) has repeated roots when \(j\ge2\).
The trace equation at order \(3t\) would require an element of order four in a group of odd order.
At order \(6t\) it instead requires \(\zeta^l\) of order six, equivalent to \(t\mid l\) and \(\gcd(l,6)=1\) since \(t\) is prime to six.
Squarefreeness then gives exactly one permissible cyclotomic factor per represented level, with multiplicity one.
The least common multiple of the selected \(t_a\) is \(n\) for even \(A\) and \(n/5\) for odd \(A\).
Within \(0<l<n\), only the first multiple of \(n/5\) in the odd case is coprime to six, and all the asserted \(\Phi_{6t_a}\) divide the parent at that offset.
This proves existence and uniqueness for the prescribed reduction.
