### Lifting the occupied classes

The reciprocity input applies to the entire integral polynomial with nonzero constant term, so its support is invariant under \(i\mapsto\deg P-i\) even when \(P\) is reducible.
Modulo the odd prime \(p\) this involution has one fixed residue \(c\).
After translation the occupied set consists of exactly \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) pairs, with zero present precisely for odd \(k\).
Two of the \(p\) fractional-part vectors fall in the same half-open box because \(B^t\le p-1\).
Subtracting their indices gives a nonzero unit modulo \(p\), and subtracting coordinates gives the required signed representatives of size at most \(p/B\).
For one pair, the inverse of its representative sends it to \(1\).
Distinct occupied residues stay distinct throughout.
The inverse \(b\) makes each base shift integral, and multiplying individual rows by Laurent units preserves their gcd.
Substitution of \(X=x^p,\ Y=x^b\) returns the initial polynomial after multiplication by \(x^c\).
A common Laurent nonunit in the original rows would factor \(P\) in the Laurent ring, with the quotient still supported in at least two residue classes.
Since \(P(0)\ne0\), irreducibility in the polynomial ring excludes such a factorization.
