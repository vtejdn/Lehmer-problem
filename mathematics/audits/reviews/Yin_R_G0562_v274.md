# Independent inversions and the quadratic orientation fields

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T17:15 UTC a reviewer outside proof development inspected the complete [C345 v274 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#decision-signed-orientations-relative-gap-submitted) for Yin.
The review used the fixed proof, the cited block and rank statements and the published Schinzel inequalities.
The alternatives, the descent for a least-degree witness and both relative measure bounds hold under their stated hypotheses.

### Lifts and the intrinsic orientations

The trace splitting field fixes precisely the permutation of inverse pairs, since \(y+y^{-1}\) determines the unordered roots of \(X^2-(y+y^{-1})X+1\).
C345 v270 gives \(b\ge9\) and independent natural alternating permutation factors on the blocks.
If a kernel sign is nonconstant on a block, rotating three coordinates with both signs changes exactly two entries.
The commutator with any lift of that rotation is therefore the double flip on those entries, with no action outside the triple.
Conjugation under the alternating factor and transitivity on blocks give the even signs supported on every individual block, all in \(G'\).
They form a subgroup of the trace kernel of order \(2^{u(b-1)}\).
Subtracting the image of a putative relation under a double flip meeting its support gives a nonzero relation supported on at most two labels.
This violates the whole-block support theorem, hence all representatives are independent.

In the other case a kernel vector consists of one constant per block.
Every lift \(h\) of a localized three-cycle has \(h^3\) in this elementary abelian kernel, so \(h^4\) has order three, projects to that cycle and kills any sign on a fixed label.
Two lifts supported on the same three pairs differ by a kernel vector supported there, necessarily zero since \(b>3\).
For the unique lifts \(g_i,g_j\) above \((1\,2\,i),(1\,2\,j)\), their product induces \((1\,i)(2\,j)\).
Its square has support in the four pairs and trivial permutation, hence is the identity because \(b>4\).
Following the positive root above \(1\) through the two applications gives sign \(a_j b_i c_i=a_j a_i\), as \(a_i b_i c_i=1\).
Thus the transports from \(1\) to \(2\) agree, and the transports from \(2\) to each \(i\) orient both \(b\)-element root sets consistently.
The resulting generators act as pure permutations in those orientations and generate the natural alternating group there, fixing every root outside the block.
That subgroup supplies the unique supported lift of each three-cycle on the block and is generated by all such lifts.
Its two orbits are consequently intrinsic and are carried to the corresponding orbits by every Galois conjugation.
Root transitivity gives transitivity on all \(2u\) orbits.
The bound \(2^u\) on the kernel in this case is strictly smaller than \(2^{v-u}\) since \(v=ub\) with \(b\ge9\).

### Polynomial and degree descent

The selection stabilizer contains the root stabilizer and is contained with index two in the underlying block stabilizer.
Galois correspondence gives \(E\subset K\subset\mathbb Q(x)\) and degrees \(u,2,b\) successively from \(\mathbb Q\) to \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
The elementary symmetric functions of the selected \(b\) roots are algebraic integers.
An automorphism fixes all of them exactly when it preserves their set, so the coefficients generate \(K\).
All \(2u\) images of the monic polynomial over the embeddings of \(K\) have disjoint root sets exhausting the rational conjugates of \(x\).
The norm polynomial is therefore the rational minimal polynomial, each root counted once.
The nontrivial automorphism of \(K/E\) lifts to a block stabilizer exchanging the two selections, so it sends \(f\) to the reciprocal normalized by \(f(0)\) and the product \(\beta\) to \(\beta^{-1}\).

The selected product is a unit in \(K\) of degree at most \(2u<d\).
Its absolute logarithmic height is at most \(b h(x)\), whence its total measure is at most \(2ub h(x)=m\).
If \(\beta\) had infinite order, its positive measure would therefore give an actual competing number of smaller degree in \((0,m]\).
At a least-degree witness it must be torsion.
The \(u\) conjugate blocks then give relations independent by disjoint support, and C345 v270 supplies the matching rank lower bound \(v-u\).

### Complex conjugation and the relative bounds

For \(K\) totally real or CM, each embedding intertwines its canonical conjugation \(\kappa\) with ordinary conjugation.
The monic conjugate reciprocal of \(f\) is \(g=(f^\tau)^\kappa\).
Since its coefficients generate \(K\), \(g=f\) holds exactly when \(\kappa\tau=1\), equivalently \(\kappa=\tau\).
I inspected Schinzel's 1975 Corollary 1′ on printed p. 329 and the hypothesis and second branch of the 1973 Theorem 2 on printed pp. 385–386 in the journal scans.
The conjugation in the polynomial condition is coefficientwise.
In Corollary 1′ the content ideal and leading-coefficient ideal are both the unit ideal for the monic integral \(f\).
The product is over all embeddings, with no halving for complex pairs.
For \(\kappa\ne\tau\) the logarithmic sum equals \(m\) by the norm calculation and is at least \([K:\mathbb Q]\log\varphi/2=u\log\varphi\).
If \(f(0)\) is torsion, the equality of endpoint moduli and the ideal equality in Theorem 2 hold, giving \([K:\mathbb Q]\log((1+\sqrt{17})/4)\) in the same normalization. [1975 addendum](../literature/Sch75/Yin_U001.md#sch75-monic-integral-gap) · [1973 theorem](../literature/Sch73/Yin_U001.md#theorem-2)

The separate coefficient argument reaches the latter constant.
The common constant coefficient \(f(0)=g(0)\) is a unit, so the first discrepancy \(cX^j\) in \(f/g-1\) has nonzero integral coefficient.
At each embedding the interior roots and reflected exterior roots give the zeros of the two finite Blaschke products, after the unit-circle factors cancel.
Both products can be normalized to have value \(A=\exp(-m_\sigma)\) at zero because \(|\sigma f(0)|=1\).
Their quotient equals \(f^\sigma/g^\sigma\), including its phase, by their common value at zero.
Averaging over \(j\)-th roots of unity and applying Schwarz–Pick in the variable \(X^j\) bounds each \(j\)-th coefficient by \(1-A^2\).
The coefficient of the difference of the two products is \(A\sigma(c)\), giving \(|\sigma(c)|\le4\sinh(m_\sigma)\).
The nonzero norm of \(c\) forces every \(m_\sigma>0\), and concavity of \(\log\sinh\) yields \(m/[K:\mathbb Q]\ge\operatorname{arsinh}(1/4)=\log((1+\sqrt{17})/4)\).

If \(\kappa=\tau\), then \(K\) must be CM because \(\tau\ne1\).
The identity \(\beta\kappa(\beta)=1\) gives modulus one at every embedding, so the unit \(\beta\) is torsion.
For a single block the orientation field \(K\) is quadratic, and a real quadratic field would give \(m\ge\log\varphi\).
The imaginary quadratic possibility rules out a real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(x)\), proving the final assertion at the stated bounds.
