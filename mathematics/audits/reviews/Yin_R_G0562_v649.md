## Counting zeros on each dyadic residue class

At 2026-11-20T10:19 UTC a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the complete [occupied-exponent claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v516.md#statement).
I found the finite bound and its full-interval consequence proved under their stated hypotheses.

With the valuation normalized at 2, the positive value group starts at \(1/a\), and the residue order divides \(2^f-1\).
While the valuation of the difference from 1 is below 1, squaring at least doubles it, and after reaching 1 the next squaring brings it to at least 2.
This includes the unramified case and allows cancellation when the two terms of the binomial expansion have equal valuation.
On the resulting disk the logarithm preserves the valuation and the exponential inverts it, so a zero logarithm would force \(\alpha^h=1\).

Irrationality of the unit prevents cancellation at a shared exponent of the rational rows.
The Vandermonde determinant on the distinct exponents is nonzero in the completion even if distinct exponents coincide modulo 2.
Its invertibility guarantees one finite valuation among the first \(s\) moments, and the monic integral recurrence propagates their minimum as a lower bound without dividing by any exponent differences.
This also handles a constant monomial and arbitrarily large exponents.
Writing \(q=v(\theta)\), the tail estimate at its first index \(2s-2\) exceeds \(M+(s-1)q\) by at least \((s-1)(q-2)+1\), uniformly for \(s\ge2\).
Later indices increase this lower bound and the coefficients tend to zero.
At a zero in the closed unit disk, the coefficient of index \(N-1\) in the quotient equals \(A_N\) plus terms of strictly smaller absolute value, and all higher quotient coefficients have strictly smaller absolute value.
Induction counts distinct zeros, including the integer zero when present.
Distinct nonnegative exponents in each residue class give distinct integer arguments, with no primality or spacing condition.
Finally \(2^{\lceil\log_2 a\rceil}\le2a\) gives the asserted global constant even when \(a=1\).

A fixed common qualifying factor allows one root to be chosen across every graph.
For the full-interval row test its repeated occurrence gives \(re\le b=rj\) by the degree inequality for tangencies, independently of the row height.
The multiplier cap gives \(\log(e(2^e-1))=O((\log L)^{1/4})\), and the prime number theorem gives \(r\sim L/\log L\).
The resulting support lower bound applies to the union, so its larger row has at least half that support.
The finite classwise bound itself requires only simple incidence.
Even unit coefficients on a support of the permitted width cost at most \(b+1\) in one row; the logarithm of this count is \(O(\log L)\), whereas the normalizing sum is at least \(Lr\).
Thus counting occupied exponents alone cannot force a positive normalized logarithmic cost.
