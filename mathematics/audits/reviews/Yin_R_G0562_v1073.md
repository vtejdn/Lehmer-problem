## Finite tests, coherent root towers and the obstruction from \(4+\sqrt{15}\)

At 2026-11-24T20:09 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v17.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v17.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings on v17.
The total-measure comparison, eventual stabilization in the fixed-exponent tower, and exclusion of every torsion translate throughout the indicated divisible group all follow.

For an admissible nontorsion unit \(\beta\) and \(\zeta\in\Sigma\) of order \(b\), the twist \(\beta/\zeta\) is an exceptional nontorsion unit of the same absolute height.
Its degree is at most \(\varphi(b)[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), by passing to the compositum with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\).
Taking the least \(\varphi(b)\) gives the lower inequality in (1).
For the reverse direction, choose a common multiple \(N\) of the orders in the prescribed finite set.
Every solution \(\beta^N=\alpha\) over an exceptional nontorsion \(\alpha\) is a nontorsion unit, since both \(\beta\) and its inverse satisfy monic equations over the algebraic integers.
All \(N\) integral differences have product \(\alpha-1\), a unit, so each difference has integral inverse.
The height of \(\beta\) is \(h(\alpha)/N\) and its relative degree over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) is at most \(N\), giving (2) without an irreducibility requirement.
The golden ratio supplies a starting point since its difference from one is its inverse.
This proves nonemptiness, equality for a prescribed point \(1\) or \(-1\), and the vanishing equivalence.
The same construction works for separately chosen finite sets at each index with positive starting measures tending to zero.
For a full primitive-root orbit, integrality of every difference and the product formula for \(\Phi_s(\beta)\) give the asserted unit equivalence.
Its absolute norm from \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) is the absolute resultant of the two monic polynomials, giving the integer test.

In a tower with fixed \(N\), \(\beta_j^{N^j}=\alpha\) and \(K_j\subseteq K_{j+1}\).
The relative norm to the original field is a unit with \(v_j^{N^j}=\alpha^{d_j}\), so its height is the positive quantity \(d_j h(\alpha)/N^j\) for nontorsion \(\alpha\).
The original field has bounded degree, and a height bound permits only finitely many integral minima there by their coefficient bounds.
Its nontorsion unit heights have a common positive lower bound, hence so do the ratios \(d_j/N^j\).
At each deficient step that ratio decreases by a factor at most \((N-1)/N\), while every other step leaves it unchanged.
Infinitely many deficient steps would force a zero limit.
Consequently the degree is exactly \(N\) at every sufficiently late step of the given tower and the total measures stabilize there by (2).
When \(\alpha\) is exceptional, the \(N^j\)-th root product gives all the asserted tests at level \(j\).
The argument keeps \(N\) and the original unit fixed, and the stabilized value can differ from the original one.

In \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{15}]\) the unit norm cannot be \(-1\) by reduction modulo three.
For \(x+y\sqrt{15}>1\) of norm one the conjugate is its positive inverse, giving \(y\ge1\) and \(x^2=1+15y^2\ge16\) with \(x>0\).
This proves minimality of \(\alpha=4+\sqrt{15}\).
Its positive square root \(\delta=(\sqrt{10}+\sqrt6)/2\) lies in a biquadratic extension.
If \(u=\delta^{1/p}>0\) belonged to the abelian closure for a prime \(p\), it would lie in a finite abelian Galois field, and complex conjugation would fix every conjugate of \(u\).
All its conjugates over \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{15})\) would then lie in \(\{u,-u\}\), since \(u^{2p}=\alpha\).
Separability gives \(u^2\in K\), a unit strictly between \(1\) and \(\alpha\), a contradiction.

Write a nontorsion element of the group as \(\xi\delta^{r/s}\) with \((r,s)=1,\ s>0,\ r\ne0\).
The finite splitting extension over the abelian closure has Galois group embedded in \(\mu_s\), since the roots of unity are fixed.
If the image has order \(d\), then \(\delta^{r/(s/d)}\) is fixed.
Bézout's identity would put \(\delta^{1/(s/d)}\) in the abelian closure, also for \(r<0\).
Any prime divisor of \(s/d>1\) would contradict the preceding exclusion, so the orbit has exactly \(s\) elements.
Their difference product at one is \(1-\xi^s\delta^r\).
If the original difference were a unit, this product of conjugate units would be a unit in any number field containing it.
In a finite abelian field containing \(\delta,\xi\), restriction to \(K\) gives equal numbers of embeddings with modulus \(R=\alpha^{|r|/2}\) and \(R^{-1}\) for \(\delta^r\).
All embeddings send \(\xi^s\) to points of modulus one.
The reverse triangle inequality therefore bounds the geometric mean of the absolute conjugate values of \(1-\xi^s\delta^r\) below by \(\sqrt{R+R^{-1}-2}\ge\sqrt{\sqrt{10}-2}>1\).
This contradicts absolute norm one.
Dividing \(\beta\) by any root of unity preserves the group and nontorsion, so the result at one excludes every prescribed torsion point individually.
The starting unit has fixed positive measure, and this obstruction leaves open how the exceptional-unit infimum relates to the unrestricted Lehmer infimum.
