# Neutral residues of a varying full Pisot return

<a id="neutral-residues"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T13:12 UTC a separate reviewer for Sheng Yin checked all four sections of the [fixed argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches) and the inputs used in them.
The reviewer had no part in the proof development and received no development discussion, author explanation or previous finding about the target.
Equations (1)–(10) hold with the stated constants, including for simple reducible returns and for an exterior trace tending to two.

**The branch and the minimal degree.**
The circle root count for \(zf+f^*\) equals the winding number of \(zf/f^*\) at the level \(-1\), so all circle roots are simple.
Removing the root \(-1\) leaves simple neutral pairs, together with \(\sigma,\sigma^{-1}\).
An irreducible factor containing \(\sigma^{-1}\) must contain \(\sigma\), since its nonzero integer constant would otherwise have modulus less than one.
This gives \(M(\sigma)=\sigma\) at its own degree \(2\le D\le d\), also when \(D=2\).
For the integral inverse \(R\), a zero residue would leave a circle root of \(f\), and a negative \(b_i\) would move one pair outward on the path from the starting quadratic times the neutral factor.
Positivity at \(-1\) throughout that path and the nonzero displacements exclude any subsequent crossing.
This verifies the necessity of the strict residue signs and of the endpoint inequality for the full return.
Substitution of \(\theta\) gives the excess in (1).
For \(F=f/f^*\), direct substitution gives
\[
 (1-z^2)\frac{F}{1+zF}-(q-z)=1-q-(x-2)R/Q .
\]
At zero the left side is \(jz+O(z^2)\), and \(z=x^{-1}+O(x^{-3})\) on this branch.
Expanding \(R/H\) on the right gives \(j=(q-1)(T-2)+\sum b_i\), and taking residues gives \(c_i=b_i(2-x_i)/(T-x_i)\).
These calculations use the branch formula without requiring irreducibility of \(Q\).

I checked Voutier’s Theorem and Corollary 2 in the [retained author edition, pp. 3–4](../../literature/pdf/Vou96--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=3) at statement scope.
The nonzero algebraic number \(\sigma>1\) has \(\log M(\sigma)=\log\sigma>0\).
Squaring the corollary and using \(2(\cosh t-1)\ge t^2\) gives (3) and the strict lower bound on \(D\).
The main theorem has a positive right side for \(D\ge4\), with squared constant \(1/16\).
Monotonicity of \(\log\log D/\log D\) above \(e^e\), and the uniform positive lower bound from (3) below degree 16, justify (4) for all sufficiently large even \(d\).
Multiplication by \(d^2\) gives divergence for the square-constant infima, since \(d^\varepsilon(\log\log d/\log d)^6\to\infty\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\).
The original proof of Voutier’s height bounds lies outside this inspection.

**The determinant and the endpoint.**
The product formula for the nonzero integer resultant uses \(R(T)<0\) and \(\prod_i|H'(x_i)|=\Delta^2\).
The endpoint inequality gives \(s<4q+2T\), whence \(|R(T)|/(T+2)^h\le q-1+(4q+2T)/(T+2)\le2q+1\).
Hadamard’s column bound in the monic cosine basis and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give (6), including \(h=1\).
For bounded \(\sigma\), the full-associate discriminant bounds the regularized Fourier energy of its \(n=d-1\) neutral roots by \(O(n\log(2n))\), as seen before replacing \(\log\sigma\) by its estimate in terms of the excess.
Dividing the fixed-frequency bound by \(n^2\) gives vanishing normalized moments.
Deleting one endpoint changes the normalized integrals by \(O(1/n)\) for bounded test functions, and conjugate pairing pushes Haar measure to the arcsine law.
Away from an endpoint interval of angular length \(\varepsilon\) at each end, the squared row norm divided by \(h\) tends uniformly to \(2\).
It is everywhere at most \(4\), and the limiting exceptional fraction is \(2\varepsilon/\pi\).
Taking the row product and sending \(\varepsilon\) to zero gives \(\limsup\Delta^{2/h}/h\le2\).

For each fixed lower truncation of \(\log(T-x)\), the truncated sums over all nodes and over all but any one node differ by \(O(1/h)\) after normalization, uniformly over omissions.
The truncated functions converge uniformly at fixed truncation height.
Removing the truncation in the arcsine integral gives \(\log\sigma_*\) as an upper bound for both limsups, including the integrable endpoint logarithm at \(\sigma_*=1\).
In the formula for \(R(T)\) the coefficients \(q-1\) and \(s\) have subexponential upper bounds since \(\log q=o(d)\).
Thus the resultant costs at most a factor \(\sigma_*\) from \(|R(T)|^{1/h}\) in bounding the geometric mean from below.
Passing to \(c_i\) costs at most a second factor \(\sigma_*\), because \(H(2)=|Q(2)|/(T-2)\ge1/(T-2)\) and \(T\) stays bounded.
This proves (7) also at \(\sigma_*=1\).
For \(q\to\infty\) the two-point bound gives \(\theta=q+O(1)\), so both \(q/(\theta-1)\) and \(q/(\theta+\theta^{-1}+2)\) tend to one.
Applying (1) and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality now gives (8).
The stronger \(1/2\) additive term at fixed \(\sigma_*>1\) uses the separately specified grid resultant.

**Limits of the residue measures.**
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality applied to \(w(x_i)v_i\) multiplies (7) by the limiting geometric mean of the positive continuous \(w\), proving (9).
In a finite weak limit \(\nu\), approximation in \(L^1(\rho+\nu)\) with the test values clipped to a compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\) controls the logarithms as well.
For \(a=\nu(E)\), \(b=S-a\) and \(0<p=\rho(E)<1\), the inequalities \(ta+b\ge c_*t^p\) force \(a,b>0\).
The minimum of \((ta+b)t^{-p}\) occurs at \(t=pb/((1-p)a)\) and gives (10).
For \(p=1\), division by \(t\) and passage to infinity give \(a\ge c_*\).
These cases imply \(\rho\ll\nu\), excluding a purely atomic finite limit.
When \(S=c_*\), the two-sided derivative at the constant test gives \(\int v\,d\nu=c_*\int v\,d\rho\) for every continuous real \(v\).
Only the weak-limit conclusions require bounded total mass for the chosen residue family.
Existence of a shrinking exterior trace remains open.
