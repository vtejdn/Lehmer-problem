## Overlapping arcs yield the stated sum over all chosen roots

At 2026-11-24T19:38 an agent outside the development of this proof independently checked all four fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on this argument.

For \(|w|=1\), \(|w-z|=|z|\,|w-1/\bar z|\), so reflection of exterior roots with their full multiplicities preserves the whole boundary modulus and its maximum and logarithmic integral.
Unreflected roots at zero cause no difficulty and contribute no chosen arc.
When inverse-reflected images coincide, the multiplicities in the new polynomial are the sums of the originals.
Thus any simultaneous selection permitted in the statement is still a valid root multiset after reflection.

For \(|\lambda|>H_{\mathbb T}\), Rouché on the unit circle places all \(D\) roots of \(G-\lambda X^D\) strictly inside.
For \(|X|\ge1\) the real part of each \(X/(X-z_j)\) is positive, since \(|z_j/X|<1\).
Their sum precludes any zero of the derivative in this region.
If \(|G'(X)|>D H_{\mathbb T}|X|^{D-1}\) at an exterior point, choosing \(\lambda=G'(X)/(D X^{D-1})\) would give precisely such a zero.
Taking boundary limits and the disk maximum principle gives the first derivative estimate.
Applying it successively to the derivative polynomials gives the full falling factorial, including the top derivative.

For distinct nodes consisting of \(r\) chosen zeros and the evaluation point \(w\), Newton interpolation identifies \(G(w)/\prod_{j=1}^r(w-y_j)\) with the divided difference.
The identity for repeated zeros follows from Hermite interpolation, since the required earlier derivatives vanish up to the chosen multiplicities.
If \(w\) itself is a chosen zero, (3) holds there directly, or by continuity of the product identity.
On \(X^j\) with \(j\ge r\), the integrated \(r\)-th derivative over the simplex gives the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree \(j-r\) in all \(r+1\) nodes with coefficient one at every monomial.
This verifies the integral formula for complex nodes as well as for repeated nodes.
Their convex combinations stay in the closed disk, and its derivative bound times simplex volume \(1/r!\) gives the factor \(\binom Dr\) in (3).

Each positive arc half-length \(L_z\) is below one radian because \(v_z\le D\) and \(t>0\).
Angular displacement bounds the chord to the radial projection, so a covered chosen zero lies within \(e^{-1-t}v_z/D\) of the point on the circle.
At a single angle the total \(r\) of the chosen multiplicities of all covering arcs is at most \(D\).
Using all of these copies in (3), \(\binom Dr\le(\mathrm eD/r)^r\) gives \(e^{-tr}\prod(v_z/r)^{v_z}\le e^{-tr}\).
The empty cover gives the trivial modulus bound.
Therefore the logarithmic deficit is at least \(t\) times the sum of the covering multiplicities at every angle with nonzero boundary value.
It remains integrable across the finite set of boundary zeros.
Each summand integrates to \(t v_z L_z/\pi\) with normalized circle measure, regardless of overlap, proving (1).

For primitive squarefree \(P\) of degree \(d\) and nonzero constant term, each of its \(d\) distinct roots can be selected with multiplicity \(a\) in \(F\).
Jensen's root formula and the product of the roots give \(\sum|\log|z||=2h-\log|b_0b_d|\le2h\), including nonmonic \(P\).
Also \(\delta_z=1-\exp(-|\log|z||)\le|\log|z||\).
With \(t=1\), dropping the positive part only decreases the lower bound and gives deficit at least \(a^2d/(\pi\mathrm e^2D)-2ah/\pi\).
The integral cofactor supplied by Gauss's lemma has nonnegative total measure.
Consequently \(m(F)\ge ah\), and \(H_{\mathbb T}\le\|F\|_1\) proves (2) with coefficient \(1-2/\pi>0\).

For both signs of the fixed-exponent graph, including \(q=2\), the residue classes zero and one are disjoint.
Reversal at formal width \(B\) preserves the coefficient one-norm, so the graph has norm exactly \(2\|A\|_1\) and degree at most \(qB+1\).
The primitive minimum of a genuine root divides the integral graph to multiplicity at least \(a\) by separability.
Dropping the nonnegative measure term in (2) gives the stated cap \(\pi\mathrm e^2(qB+1)\log(2\|A\|_1)/a^2\).
With fixed \(q\), \(B/a\to\infty\) and \(\log\|A\|_1=o(a)\), this is \(o(B/a)\).
The same calculation gives \(D/(ad)\ge1/(\pi\mathrm e^2Ch)\) at positive \(h\) under the asserted upper coefficient rate.

In the degree-free infimum each admissible root has a primitive minimal polynomial with strictly positive total measure.
This follows from its leading coefficient in the nonmonic case and from Kronecker's theorem for a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer in the monic case.
Divisibility thus gives \(\log\|F\|_1/a\ge m(P)\ge\lambda\) for every admissible \(F,a\).
For the reverse bound, fix any reciprocal nontorsion unit and apply the full-residue coefficient-rate theorem with fixed \(q\) and an allowed schedule, for example \(B(a)=a^2\).
The resulting admissible integral polynomials have normalized log norms tending to that unit's total measure.
Taking the infimum over such units gives \(\lambda\) by the reciprocal reduction.
This order of the two infima requires no uniform convergence in the unit.
Thus an absolute finite multiplicity constant is equivalent to a positive unrestricted Lehmer gap, while (2) supplies it under the stated bound on \(D/(a\deg P)\).
