## Integral inclusions at both periods

The two row values generate an ideal contained in \((F)\) throughout the sum period and in \((F,F')\) throughout the difference period.
Both inclusions preserve the stated multiplicities.
The finite orders in the original row coordinate follow when the exponents are coprime and the respective row quotient is finite.

**Integral cancellation.**
Over \(\mathbb Q[X]\) the factors of \(X^p-X^q\) are \(X\) and cyclotomic factors, all coprime to \(F\).
Hence the divided difference \(D_+\) is divisible by the full \(F\), and monic division gives an integral quotient.
On the difference period \(x^p=x^q\), so \(D_+\) specializes to \(U'(x^p)+xV'(x^p)\).
The derivative of the graph at \(p\) therefore puts \(V(x^p)\) in \((F,F')\), and the graph itself puts \(U(x^p)\) there.

On the sum period the decisive identity takes place first in the integral Laurent ring.
Reciprocity preserves \((F)\) under inversion, so the difference of the graphs at \(X\) and \(X^{-1}\) belongs to that ideal.
Its explicit quotient by \(X-X^{-1}\) is \(E=L D_-+V(X^{-q})\), since \((X^p-X^{-q})/(X-X^{-1})=X^{1-q}(X^s-1)/(X^2-1)\).
The latter is an integral Laurent polynomial for even \(s\).
The factors \(X-1\) and \(X+1\) are coprime to \(F\) over \(\mathbb Q\), so \(F\) divides the quotient \(E\) with all its multiplicities there.
Clearing Laurent powers and dividing by the monic polynomial \(F\) gives the same divisibility integrally.
The two integral divided differences agree modulo \(X^2-1\) because \(X^q=X^{-q}\) in that quotient.
It follows that \(L(D_--D_+)\) vanishes modulo \(X^s-1\), where \(E-LD_+\) represents \(V(x^p)\).
This proves the sum inclusion over \(\mathbb Z\) before any factorization of the period, even at primes dividing \(2sF(1)F(-1)\).
Evaluation at either sign preserves the row argument since \(p\) is odd.
Every smaller divisor period and every individual cyclotomic factor ring receives the inclusions by its quotient map.

**Orders.**
When \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), exponentiation by \(p\) permutes the cyclic basis for each period and each divisor period.
The row quotient therefore surjects onto the indicated target in the \(X\)-coordinate.
Multiplication by \(F\) on \(A_s\) diagonalizes over \(\mathbb C\) with diagonal \(F(\zeta)\) at all \(s\)-th roots.
Its absolute determinant is the claimed nonzero resultant, giving the full order even when separate cyclotomic resultants share primes.
At the difference period \((P^k,(P^k)')=P^{k-1}(P,kP')\), and multiplication by \(P^{k-1}\) embeds \(A_r\) in itself with index \(C_r(P)^{k-1}\).
The remaining index is exactly \(\lvert A_r/(P,kP')\rvert\), including when \(k=1\).
For coprime rows over \(\mathbb Q\), a rational Bézout identity gives full rank to the joined multiplication matrices, and their maximal minors give the lattice index by Smith normal form.

**Example and growth.**
For \(P=X^2-3X+1\), let \(t_j=a^j+a^{-j}\) at the larger root.
Then \(t_3=3^3-3\cdot3=18\), \(t_6=t_3^2-2=322\) and \(t_{12}=t_6^2-2=103682\), so \(C_{12}(P)=t_{12}-2=103680\).
The conjugate powers \(a^j\) and \(a^{-j}\) are distinct for \(j=5,7\), which supplies the full-degree hypothesis for the cited interpolation and gives actual coprime rows with both squared graph factors.
This example concerns those algebraic conditions, without a coefficient cutoff.
The prime \(5\) divides both \(C_{12}(P)\) and \(P(-1)=5\), explaining its exclusion from the older auxiliary moduli.
