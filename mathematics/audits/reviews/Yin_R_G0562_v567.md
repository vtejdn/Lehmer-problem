## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-19T02:33 UTC a reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v435.md#statement) and its complete argument.
The reviewer had previously checked the cited two-exponent Möbius obstruction and recovery lemma, had no part in developing the present bound and received no development dialogue, author explanation or previous review of this claim.
The contact multiplicities, count of infinite critical orbits and application of Epstein's theorem give (1) for every fixed pair of rows and positive exponent difference.

### Fixed-point order and the excluded degrees

At \(c,\beta=c^n,z=c^{n+k}\), genuine contact makes the denominators in \(f(\beta),g(z)\) nonzero.
Both rational functions take value \(c\), so they are holomorphic and nonvanishing near the respective nodes.
Substituting \(F(X^n)\) for \(X^{n+k}\) into \(g\) changes its germ at \(c\) by \(O((X-c)^t)\), where \(t\) is the smaller contact order.
This gives \(f(Y)-g(F(Y))=O((Y-\beta)^t)\) since the local map \(X\mapsto X^n\) is unramified.
The identity \(T(Y)=Y(f(Y)/g(F(Y)))^k\) implies the same lower bound on the order of \(T(Y)-Y\).
Indeed multiplication by \(Y\) and division by \(g(F(Y))\) contribute local units, and the quotient \((v^k-1)/(v-1)\) has value \(k\) at one.
Thus each node has multiplier one and contributes at least \(t-1\) to the sum of parabolic multiplicities once \(\deg T>1\).

Differentiating the graph equations at the nodes gives the stated nonzero logarithmic derivatives \((n+k)/n\) for \(F\) and \(n/(n+k)\) for \(J\).
This excludes degree zero for either map.
If \(\deg T=1\), both maps must be Möbius.
The [argument for \(F\)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v433.md#identity) requires only its degree, rational coefficients, commutation with inversion and the known value and logarithmic derivative at \(\beta\).
Clearing the derivative equation gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two, since a Möbius monomial has logarithmic derivative \(1\) or \(-1\).
Consequently \(c=f(\beta)\) is a quadratic nontorsion unit, admitting an expanding real conjugate.
The inversion symmetry of \(F\) there forces the absolute logarithmic derivative to be \(\sinh((n+k)x)/\sinh(nx)>(n+k)/n\) with \(x=\log|c|>0\).
Thus a nonidentity Möbius map with a parabolic fixed point is excluded as well as the identity.
The power-field calculation \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(c)\) gives \(D_c\) distinct powered nodes per orbit.
An equality of transported nodes recovers the same \(c\) by \(f\), and nontorsion then recovers the same \(n\).

### Counting critical values in the alternating orbits

Let \(L=F\) or \(J\), with associated row ratio \(u=f\) or \(g\), and \(E=\{0,\infty\}\).
On \(\mathbb C^\times\) the order of \(L\) at a point is \(k\operatorname{ord}u\) or \(-k\operatorname{ord}u\).
There are \(r(u)+2-e_L\) distinct points mapping to \(E\), and at each \(dL/L\) has a simple pole with residue the nonzero signed local order.
At any other point the image is nonzero and finite, so the differential has a zero of order exactly \(\deg_p L-1\), using a local coordinate also at infinity.
Subtracting two from the number of poles gives \(r(u)-e_L\) such zeros with multiplicity.
In particular this bounds the number of distinct critical values outside \(E\), including those arising at source endpoints.
Possible cancellation at \(0,\infty\) for \(k=1\) is included because \(e_L\) is computed from the reduced map's actual endpoint values.

For a critical point \(p\) of \(T\), consider the sequence \(p,F(p),J(F(p)),\ldots\) in the alternating labeled spheres.
Its even-indexed points are precisely the \(T\)-orbit.
The local degrees multiply, so this sequence visits either an \(F\)-critical value in the second sphere or a \(J\)-critical value in the first.
If two such paths reach the same point in the same labeled sphere, then the \(T\)-orbits meet, applying \(J\) once more when that common point is in the second sphere.
This allows at most \(r(f)-e_F+r(g)-e_J\) distinct infinite tails represented by off-endpoint critical values.
For a path reaching an endpoint critical value, perpetual confinement to the four labeled endpoints makes the \(T\)-orbit finite.
Otherwise the path uses a first transition out of those endpoints, from one of \(e_F+e_J\) possible labeled points.
The outgoing transition and all later points are determined, so at most that many further distinct infinite \(T\)-tails can arise.
Adding these two counts proves \(\delta(T)\le r(f)+r(g)\), allowing any overlap among the critical orbits.

### Epstein's multiplier-one count

I checked the [source unit](../literature/Eps99/Yin_U001.md#fixed-points) against equation (1), the parabolic weights and Theorem 1 on printed page 1 of [Epstein's 1999 preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9902158#page=1).
For a multiplier-one fixed point the order of the multiplier as a root of unity is one, so the integer \(\nu\) in that normal form is exactly \(\operatorname{ord}(T-\mathrm{id})-1\).
Both possible parabolic weights are at least \(\nu\).
The theorem bounds the sum of cycle weights by the number of distinct infinite critical orbit tails for every rational self-map of degree greater than one.
Its proof on printed pages 4–5 counts tails by the number of new images after all critical orbit relations are included, consistent with eventual meeting at possibly different iterate indices.
I used the theorem at statement scope without rechecking its analytic propositions.
The \(D_c\) disjoint fixed points of each counted pair can therefore be charged \(t-1\) each.
The reduced numerator and denominator of \(u\) each have degree at most \(\deg u\), giving the remaining degree bounds, including constant row ratios.
In the empty-pair case the left side is zero without application of the theorem.
The equal-degree unit-square example contributes at least \(2D_c\) since its positive measures \(m(c)\) and \(2m(c)\) distinguish the two orbits.
