# Independent review of the Galois obstruction at the first Salem trace level

<a id="strict-descent-galois-obstruction"></a>
## Separation of the secondary radial cost and passage to a composition factor

At 2026-11-17T03:11 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the complete C345v133 submission fixed at 2026-11-17T03:09 UTC.
The reviewer had no role in developing this version, reconstructed its estimates and block argument, and visually inspected the cited theorem in the retained published edition.

For \(k\) secondary exterior roots \(\beta\), the partners \(1/\bar\beta\) are distinct interior roots and none is a source row of another difference.
Row subtraction in the Vandermonde therefore preserves its determinant.
The difference in the \(j\)-th column is bounded by \(j|\beta-1/\bar\beta|\,|\beta|^{j-1}\), including the identically zero difference for \(j=0\).
The product of the normalized exterior row weights is at most \(e^{(d-1)L}\), with no double counting of an exterior modulus.
Since \(2\sinh(\log|\beta|)\le2eE\) under \(L\le1\), the nonzero integer discriminant gives the submitted inequality (3).
For \(\eta=1/|\beta|^2\), the two conjugate coordinates bound \(D\le d^2\) and \(h(\eta)\le2L/d\).
Its minimal polynomial has \(H(1)\) a nonzero integer, and \(1-\eta\le2E\).
Bounding each coefficient by \(\|H\|_{\mathbb T}\) gives \(\max_{[\eta,1]}|H'|\le D(D+1)\|H\|_{\mathbb T}/2\).
For the confluent determinant estimate C345v73, \(U=Dh(\eta)\le 2DL/d\le D\), so a permitted integer confluence order is comparable to \(\sqrt{D(1+U/\log(2D))}\) even at \(D=2\).
After taking logarithms in the derivative bound, the estimate becomes
\[
 \log(1/E)\ll\sqrt D\log(2D)+\sqrt{DU\log(2D)}
       \ll\sqrt D\log(2D)+D\sqrt{L\log(2D)/d}.
\]
This uses \(D\) at its own degree and absorbs the differentiated coefficient factor \(O(\log D)\).

The inverse-pair trace labels number \(d/2\) by reciprocity and separability.
Complex conjugation moves precisely the nonreal trace labels, each contributing a secondary exterior conjugate, so its support has size at most \(k\).
In a saturated chain of invariant partitions, the first one whose blocks are all fixed by conjugation has a preceding partition unless all traces were already real.
The induced action on the children of any one block is transitive; imprimitivity would give a partition stable under its stabilizer and hence a well-defined intermediate partition by transport to all blocks.
Choose a block \(B\) on which conjugation moves a child.
A moved child costs its entire size \(u\) in the support, and at least two children move.
Thus \(2u\le k\).
Every pair of complex-conjugate labels belongs to the same \(s\)-block, and so do its simultaneous Galois images.
Counting all unordered pairs in those blocks and four choices of lifted roots per pair gives \(D\le d(s-1)\).
For a real secondary exterior root one already has \(D\le d\) because \(\eta=\beta^{-2}\).

In a primitive solvable action a minimal nonidentity normal subgroup is elementary abelian and transitive, hence regular.
The point stabilizer acts faithfully and linearly on this group, so each nonidentity affine element moves at least half its points.
This yields \(s=ut\le2k\) in the nonreal case and a radial product of degree at most \(2dk\) in all cases.
For \(Y=\log(1/E)\ge2\log(2ed)\), the discriminant gives \(k\ll d\log(2d)/Y\).
Combining the last two bounds with the radial estimate and \(\ell=\log(2d)\) gives
\[
 Y\ll d\ell^{3/2}Y^{-1/2}
                 +d^{3/2}L^{1/2}\ell^{3/2}Y^{-1}.
\]
If the first term dominates, \(Y\ll d^{2/3}\ell\); otherwise \(Y\ll d^{3/4}L^{1/4}\ell^{3/4}\).
The smaller-\(Y\) range is already covered.

For the original shrinking Salem inputs, Dobrowolski gives \(a\gg H_0^{-3}\), \(H_0=\log(2m)\), so it also ensures \(a\gg\log(2m)/\sqrt m\) as required to apply C345v125 at level \(-1\).
The slope and signed displacement give \(T-2\cosh p=\exp(-(1+o(1))ma)=o(a^2)\), with \(p=\log\tau<a\).
Then \(p/a\to1\), and division by \(2\sinh v\) for \(v\in(p,a)\) changes the displacement logarithm by \(O(1+|\log a|)=o(ma)\).
Strict descent makes \(L<a\), and the nonreciprocal gap forces \(F\) reciprocal.
The unique real exterior trace of the complete shift lies in \((2,T)\), eventually containing no integer.
This excludes a quadratic distinguished factor, and a reciprocal non-Salem output must have a nonreal trace and positive secondary cost \(E<a-p\).
Hence \(Y\ge(1-o(1))ma\), \(k\ll dH_0/(ma)\), and \(d\le2m\).
From the two summands of the radial estimate, one obtains either \(D/d\gg ma^2/H_0^2\) or \(D/d\gg\sqrt{ma/H_0}\).
The former is stronger eventually since the ratio is \(\sqrt m(a/H_0)^{3/2}\gg\sqrt m/H_0^6\to\infty\).
Consequently \(s\gg\sqrt{ma/H_0}\) whereas \(k^2\ll H_0^2/a^2=o(s)\).

Praeger and Shalev define minimal degree as the least support of a nonidentity permutation on the [inspected printed page 255](../../literature/pdf/PS01--published.pdf#page=13).
Theorem 7.2 gives \(m(J)\ge(\sqrt t-1)/2\) for a quasiprimitive permutation group of degree \(t\) other than the natural \(A_t,S_t\).
The square root covers \(t\) alone and the inequality is weak.
The submitted application requires only this published statement; the original group-theoretic proof was outside the review.
The faithful primitive child action \(J\) is quasiprimitive since the orbits of any nontrivial normal subgroup form an invariant partition.
Conjugation moves at most \(k/u\) children in \(B\).
Outside the exceptions the source theorem would imply \(s\le u(2k/u+1)^2=O(k^2)\), contradicting the lower bound.
Therefore \(J=A_t\) or \(S_t\) in its natural action and \(t=s/u\ge2s/k\gg\sqrt m(a/H_0)^{3/2}\).
In particular \(t\ge5\) eventually.
The kernel \(K\) on all \(s\)-blocks is a normal subgroup of the full trace group, lies in the stabilizer of \(B\), and contains conjugation.
Its nontrivial image on the children of \(B\) is normal in \(J\).
That image contains \(A_t\), by simplicity and the trivial centralizer of \(A_t\) in \(S_t\), and its quotient by \(A_t\) has order at most two.
Thus \(A_t\) is a composition factor of a quotient of \(K\), hence of \(K\) and of the full trace group.
The use of a normal kernel is needed here because composition factors of a point or block stabilizer alone need not be composition factors of the ambient group.
The reviewer found both the general solvable-group estimate and the conditional first-level obstruction valid as stated.
