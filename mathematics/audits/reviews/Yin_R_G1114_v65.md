The equivalences pass for both normalized statistics, including at the selected sparse test degrees.

For degree \(d\) and its largest prime factor \(p_d\), the closed integer interval \([d,2d]\) contains exactly \(d/p_d+1\) multiples of \(p_d\), since both endpoints are multiples.
The stipulated condition \(d/p_d\le s(d)\) gives the claimed upper bound \(\lfloor s(d)\rfloor+1=o(d)\) whenever deletions occur.
At \(Y=d\), the lower primitive estimate in L462v70 loses at most \(d/2+o(d)\) orders.
The bounded mean of \(j/\varphi(j)\) permits removing only \(O(c_0d)\) more to ensure \(\varphi(j)\ge c_0d\), and the primes cost \(o(d)\).
This leaves a positive proportion of composites with \(\log D_j\gg d\,m(P)\).
Since the full resultant is the product of nonzero integer cyclotomic resultants at all divisors of \(j\), \(\log C_j\ge\log D_j\).
Division by \(j\le2d\) or by \(\varphi(j)\le2d\) gives the needed uniform lower bounds on the same composite orders under the positive Mahler gap.
The additional degree-dependent deletion cannot remove their whole set.

Conversely, fix any nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(D\), with monic minimal polynomial \(Q\).
Over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) a proper factor of \(z^p-\alpha\) of degree \(k<p\) would provide the product \(b\) of its roots with \(b^p=\alpha^k\).
Bézout for \(k,p\) then makes \(\alpha\) a \(p\)-th power in this fixed field.
Such roots are integral, bounded at every conjugate and of degree at most \(D\), hence range over finitely many possibilities by the elementary coefficient bound.
Nontorsion lets any one candidate serve at most one prime.
Thus \(Q(z^p)\) really is a minimal polynomial of degree \(Dp\) for all sufficiently large primes, and its measure is \(m(Q)\).
For \(p>D\) its degree's largest prime factor is \(p\), while eventual \(s(Dp)\ge D\) activates the deletion.
At every retained order, powering by \(p\) permutes both the full and primitive root sets, hence preserves both normalized sampling statistics of \(Q\).
For this fixed \(Q\), the [two-logarithm estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v2.md#circle) at circle roots gives the recorded full error \(O_Q(\log(2j))\) before division by \(j\).
Möbius inversion bounds the primitive error before normalization by \(O_Q(\tau(j)\log(2j))=o(\varphi(j))\).
Consequently the convergence of the sampled values to \(m(Q)\) holds throughout the retained orders as their minimum tends to infinity.
A single retained witness of either kind forces \(m(Q)\ge c\).
At a discarded \(j=kp\) the image orbit has order \(k\) with equal multiplicities, including for primitive nodes by surjectivity on the reduced residue classes.
Here \(D\le k\le2D\), which verifies the stated limitation of that converse.

In the sparse-degree construction, for each fixed stage \(n\) all sufficiently large primes meet the three size requirements because \(s(d)\to\infty\).
The test degrees and exceptional orders are chosen independently of the polynomial.
For each \(D\), infinitely many stages have \(D_n=D\), their primes tend to infinity, and eventual irreducibility on that subsequence suffices for the same converse.
Allowing prime orders in addition to composite witnesses does not affect either implication.
