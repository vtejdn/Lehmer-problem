## Outcome of the proof review

For the specified positive Salem seed the exclusion through width eight holds for every sufficiently large positive integer exponent, without row coprimality.
The variable-sign transform follows at every sufficiently large odd exponent and in particular at odd primes.
The lower rate \((45/16)\log\rho\) strictly exceeds \(\log2\).

### The common finite divisor and the archimedean upper estimate

The octic trace polynomial is irreducible modulo two and has three trace roots between \(-2\) and \(2\) and one above \(2\).
At an interior real trace embedding its quadratic lift has negative discriminant, verifying the irreducible Salem input from C412.
For each inverse-pair representative an embedding of the splitting field \(L\) sends it to \(\rho\) and the other three into the unit circle.
Taking absolute values at these four embeddings proves independence modulo torsion and distinctness of all eight powered roots for every positive exponent.
Formal reversal about \(8p+1\) gives both signed graphs and \(UU^\iota\ne VV^\iota\) makes both signed rows nonzero.
If a signed evaluation vanished, its degree-eight bound would force a nonzero multiple of \(Q\); then \(Q(X^p)(1+\varepsilon X)\) has a simple zero at the representative since \(x_i\ne\pm1\) and the power map there is unramified.
This proves nonvanishing for all evaluations in the ratio.

At a finite prime choose an index giving the largest contact \(r_{ij}\), independently of the sign.
C411 bounds both signed valuations below by \(2r_{ij}-2(\nu(p)+\nu(x_i-x_j))\), since at the value equation the two rows differ by a unit.
The minimum of those valuations has this same lower bound.
After summing, replacing the maximum by the total costs \(o_P(p)\) from the triple overlaps, while the normalized costs of \(p\) and the fixed differences are \(O_P(\log(2p))\).
The valuation sum over \(j\ne i\) is that of \(Q'(z_i)\), whose normalized log norm is \(\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|/8\).
The powered discriminant has logarithm \(14ph+O_P(\log(2p))\) since the six zero log moduli and the two opposite nonzero ones give \(D(P)=14h\).
This gives common finite contribution at least \(7ph/2-o_P(p)\), including primes dividing \(p\).

The two archimedean row bounds save one power of \(|z_i|\) at either end for each sign separately.
Taking the maximum pointwise gives exponent \(7ph\) at the exterior conjugate, \(-ph\) at the interior one, and no exponential contribution at the other six.
With signed row one-norm at most \(2H\), the resulting average is at most \(\log(2H)+(6/8)ph+O_P(\log(2p))\).
For the height of the quotient the finite contributions are subtracted because the evaluations are integral.
Taking the quotient of the two signed ratios at \(i=1,2\) thus gives \(h_{\rm W}(R_p)\le2\log H-(11/2)ph+o_P(p)\), with the error uniform in all rows.

### Recalculation of the certificate

I checked primality of \(1987\), substituted all eight residues into \(P\), and multiplied each stated pair to get one modulo \(1987\).
Hence at a chosen prime over \(1987\) the labeling is available, with eight distinct reductions.
For each of the four matrices I computed all nine signed maximal minors by exact integer determinants reduced modulo \(1987\), checked their vector was in the kernel, and normalized its last coordinate to one.
The independently recovered entries in order (last minor, value at \(Y_1\), value at \(Y_2\)) were

| \(Y_1\) | Plus | Minus | Cross-ratio |
| ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 2 | \(1768,653,1909\) | \(1884,330,1286\) | 1957 |
| 4 | \(318,590,438\) | \(1219,132,747\) | 904 |

The value and Euler-derivative rows are the correct derivatives of the signed monomial graphs; the \(+1\) on the reversed term comes from the factor \(X\).
The two nonzero, unequal cross-ratios disprove the polynomial identity obtained by cross-multiplication at two independent \(Y_1\) values with every other variable held fixed.
This argument works on the unreduced integral minors, so common factors or denominators in a later reduced representation cannot account for the inequality.
The reduced pair \(F,G\) must therefore have \(W_1\ge1\).

### Specialization over all positive integer exponents

I checked Xiao's Theorem 3.3 on printed p. 13 and Lemma 4.1 on pp. 23–24 of the retained arXiv v3, accepting those results at statement scope.
For a fixed nonzero polynomial in \(N,\mathbf Y\), collecting equal exponent vectors gives fixed polynomial coefficients in \(p\) and bases with no torsion ratios.
At each embedding, dividing by the power of a largest-modulus base leaves a nondegenerate recurrence with a base of modulus one.
Lemma 4.1 supplies the eventual lower log bound \(-o(p)\), and the fixed coefficients give an upper bound \(O(\log(2p))\) after this normalization.
In particular the nonzero minors and evaluation factors needed for the ratio stay nonzero for every sufficiently large integer \(p\).
There the rank is eight, and the two actual signed rows span the same two respective lines as the minors, so their two scalar normalizations cancel.

Reduce \(F/G\) over \(L[N,\mathbf Y]\) and clear coefficient denominators by joint scaling.
For \(u=(p,x_1^p,\ldots,x_4^p)\) the outside-archimedean almost-unit cost of \(u,u^{-1}\) is exactly \(\log p\), since the last four coordinates are units.
Its height is at least \(ph/8\) and its sum of coordinate heights is \(O_P(p)\).
For each fixed tolerance \(\delta>0\) sufficiently small, Theorem 3.3 gives an outside-archimedean gcd bound \(O_P(\sqrt\delta\,p)\) off a fixed proper closed subset.
A nonzero Laurent equation containing this subset can be multiplied by a monomial to use the eventual nonvanishing just proved; coefficients in a fixed finite algebraic extension also work.
Thus only finitely many positive indices for this \(\delta\) are exceptional.
Letting \(\delta\) decrease after taking the limit superior gives an \(o_P(p)\) finite logarithmic common divisor.
Integral coefficients and coordinates identify this with precisely the subtracted finite contribution, with a bounded contribution if either reduced polynomial is constant.

Exactly \([L:\mathbb Q]/8\) embeddings send any given representative to \(\rho\), and the same count sends it to \(\rho^{-1}\).
These eight classes partition all embeddings.
At each of them the larger archimedean log evaluation of \(F,G\) selects the appropriate maximum exponent or negative minimum exponent in their joint support, even when several monomials tie.
Consequently the absolute projective height is \(ph\sum_k W_k/8+o_P(p)\).
With \(W_1\ge1\) the upper estimate yields \(\log H\ge(45/16)ph-o_P(p)\).
I obtained \(25^8P(32/25)=-1788481599\) and checked \(32^{45}-2^{16}25^{45}>0\) by integer arithmetic.
The unique positive exterior zero thus gives the strict cutoff margin, independent of all qualifying rows; its onset is ineffective here.
