## Outcome of the proof review

The construction supplies the asserted two rows, including the leading constant \(3\) in its logarithmic upper bound.
The smaller-prime finite exclusion criterion holds with the stated quantifiers.

**Transportation and derivatives.**
Degree preservation makes the \(z_j\) distinct, so the \(j\)-th value of \(T\) is \(\alpha_j^cQ'(z_j)\).
Multiplication by \(z_j^{1-m-\eta}\) gives exactly \(\alpha_j^{-w}D(z_j)\), including \(w=0,\pm p\).
Galois invariance and integrality give integer coefficients, while \(0\le c\le p\) bounds each weighted factor product by \(2^{e-1}\prod_k\max(1,|z_k|)\).
This proves the uniform bound for \(V\) and uses no primality assumption.

For \(J=A,W\), the expansion of \((\mathcal EJ)/D\) is integral because each monomial before differentiation contains at least two factors \(D\).
At \(q(z)=0\), differentiation of \(q(\mathcal EJ)/D\) restores precisely the derivative removed from \(J\).
Also \(W(z)=\alpha^{-w}A(z)\), and the derivatives of \(qDL,qVL,wqDV\delta\) are respectively \(D(z)^2L(z),\alpha^{-w}D(z)^2L(z),w\alpha^{-w}A(z)\).
These evaluations give both signed jet equations with \(u=pA(z)\ne0\), since \(z\ne z^{-1}\) and \(Q'(z)\ne0\).
Inversion fixes \(A(z)\) and \(D(z)^2\), and \(S(z)\) has degree \(e/2\) because \(z\) generates the seed field and is quadratic over its trace field.
Thus at least one of \(\kappa,\kappa S(z)\) is outside \(\mathbb Q\).
Doubling this choice gives two irrational ratios with nonzero difference and determinant.
In the centered graph the Euler derivative at \(\alpha\) is \(p\ell_i+wu-p(\ell_i+wu/p)=0\), and its value is \(u-u=0\).
Separability and \(\alpha\ne0\) give divisibility by \(P^2\) in the Laurent ring.

**Support and cost.**
The three factors supported on \([-m,m]\) in the largest products and the single factor of radius one give support radius at most \(3m+1\).
After shifting by \(2e=4m\), both lists therefore start at exponent at least \(m-1\) and end at most at \(7m+1<8m\), also at the endpoint \(m=2\).
The added \(Q^2(1+Y^{2e})\) sets both extreme coefficients of \(a\) to one, and all further increments are interior and vanish twice at \(z\).
Since \(b(z)\ne0\), reciprocity excludes all roots of \(Q\) from the reversal of \(b\).
Each of at most \(B-1\) nonzero roots of that reversal thus forbids at most one trial coefficient \(t\), leaving a choice among the \(B\) proposed integers.
The constant coefficient of \(a\) already excludes zero as a common root.
Each product in the expanded formulas costs at most \((2ep)^C M^3\) in one-norm for absolute \(C\), because the number of terms is bounded absolutely and Euler differentiation on the base supports costs at most a factor \(m\).
The two corrections add at most \((B+2)M^2\), which leaves the stated \(3ph+3e\log2+O(\log(2ep))\) bound intact.

**One-prime criterion.**
For the positive sign and \(w=1\), these lists satisfy every condition of the [C406 test](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#statement) directly.
Voutier's Corollary 2 gives \(h>2/\log^3(3e)\) for every seed here, so the relative contribution of \(e+\log(2ep_e)\) tends uniformly to zero at \(p_e>16e^4\).
I checked the corollary and its normalization in the cited author manuscript and used it at statement scope. [Voutier, Theorem and Corollary 2](../literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height)

For a prime \(p>e+1\), the relative degree of \(\alpha\) over the powered field is coprime to \(p\).
Taking the norm of \(\alpha\) and applying Bézout produces a \(p\)-th root of \(z\) in that field.
If it differed from \(\alpha\), the degree-\(e\) seed field would contain a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity of degree \(p-1>e\).
Thus the construction applies at each such degree-only prime. [Norm argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#power)

The reflected determinant at \(w=1\) has integral quotient by \(Q^2\) and nonzero extreme coefficients, implying \(\log H(P)\ge ph-\log(4e+1)\).
A positive gap consequently excludes the fixed boxes for some \(k\) and all sufficiently large even degrees.
Conversely, failure of the gap supplies reciprocal-unit seeds with \(h\to0\) and unbounded even degrees by the nonreciprocal lower bound and bounded-degree coefficient finiteness.
Along such a sequence the constructed upper bound divided by \(p(e)\) tends to zero, since \(e/p(e)\to0\) and \(\log(2ep(e))/p(e)\to0\).
It puts a pair in the box \(2^{\lfloor p(e)/k\rfloor}\) eventually for every fixed \(k\).
The least prime above \(e\log(2e)\) meets the growth and preservation conditions.
This checks both implications without a substitution prime or an assumption that the Lehmer gap holds.
