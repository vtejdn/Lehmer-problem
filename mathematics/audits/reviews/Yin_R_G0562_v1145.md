# Review of simultaneous residue-order bounds and the Salem family with an uncontrolled factor

## Congruence depths, original-field weights and simultaneous prime prescriptions

At 2026-11-25T00:05 UTC an agent outside the development of v85 independently reviewed its [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v85.md#statement) and all four proof sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and requested scope without development dialogue or previous findings on v85.
Both simultaneous inequalities, the linear-in-measure consequences and the Salem realization with an aggregate lower bound hold under the stated hypotheses.

**All finite primes and depths.**
One representative of each distinct conjugate class gives an affinely spanning set of dimension \(b\), since the sum of the classes vanishes by the unit norm and their actual multiplicities are equal.
The product count in v77 therefore gives at least the stated \(D\ge2\) distinct classes represented by products of \(s\) original conjugates.
Choose this many representatives once for all primes.
The Vandermonde \(V\) on these distinct actual units is a nonzero algebraic integer.
At each infinite embedding, dividing the row for \(u_i\) by \(\max(1,|u_i|)^{D-1}\) bounds its norm by \(\sqrt D\).
Taking the normalized archimedean mean and dividing by \(\binom D2\) gives \(\log D/(D-1)+(2/D)\sum_i h(u_i)\le H_s\).
The product formula for this same \(V\) pays its entire finite-place cost.

For a fixed \(P\mid p\), each unordered pair of nodes contributes exactly \(\operatorname{ord}_P(u_i-u_k)\) collisions when one sums over \(P^j,\ j\ge1\).
At each depth at most \(B_{p,j}\) classes are occupied since the nodes belong to \(\Gamma\).
The identity \(\sum\binom{c_i}2=(\sum c_i^2-D)/2\) and Cauchy's inequality give the summand in (1), including the zero lower bound for large images.
In the normalization \(|p|_P=p^{-1}\), each unit of ideal valuation costs \(\log p/e_p\).
All \(P\mid p\) have the same image size because the Galois group preserves \(\Gamma\), and their local-degree weights sum to one.
Ramification, including wild ramification, changes the displayed denominator but not the collision identity.
Only primes dividing \(V\) can contribute positively, and only finitely many depths at any of them can contribute.
At depth one the cyclic residue group makes \(B_{p,1}\) the least common multiple of the orders of the actual reduced roots, which are all nonzero.
The distinct roots of the reduction in an algebraic closure of the residue field are precisely these images, since the original monic minimum splits in \(L\).
Repeated factors do not affect that least common multiple.

**A subset of the roots locally.**
For one specified conjugate \(a\) and \(K'=\mathbb Q(a)\), the ratio between the number of \(P\)'s above a fixed \(v\) of \(K'\) and their number above \(p\) is
\[
 \frac{[L:K']/[L_P:K'_v]}{[L:\mathbb Q]/[L_P:\mathbb Q_p]}
      =\frac{[K'_v:\mathbb Q_p]}{d}
      =\frac{e_v f_v}{d}.
\]
This uses normality of \(L/\mathbb Q\) and \(L/K'\) and needs no normality of \(K'/\mathbb Q\).
Transport to \(K\) preserves the order test with the corresponding generator.
Thus each of the \(s\) factors of a chosen node is good with probability \(\gamma_p\), and the union bound gives \(\mathbb E M_P\ge D\beta_p\) without an independence hypothesis.

At a good factor use the Teichmüller lift of its residue in its own completion \(K'_v\).
It has the same order as its residue and differs from \(a\) by valuation at least \(1/e_v\) for \(v_p(p)=1\).
The lift is compatible with passage to \(L_P\) by uniqueness, including when \(p\mid T_p\), since its own order is prime to \(p\).
Multiplying good factors preserves the valuation lower bound \(1/E_p\) by the ultrametric inequality.
Their lift products are roots of \(X^{T_p}-1\), so at most \(T_p\) such products occur.
Pairs with a common lift pay at least \(\log p/E_p\).
The mean number of these pairs is at least \((D^2\beta_p^2/T_p-D)_+/2\) by convexity and \(M_P\le D\), with the positive part taken using nonnegativity of the mean.
This proves (2) by charging each tested prime to its own part of the same finite-place budget.
The tests can therefore use different \(E_p,T_p\).

A multiplicity-one irreducible factor in the reduction is coprime to its complement.
It lifts to a local factor of the same degree whose separable irreducible reduction gives an unramified completion with that residue degree.
This remains valid when the complementary factors have repetitions or the global defining order is not maximal.
Thus the successful simple factors give the stated lower bound with \(E_p=1\).
For squarefree reduction these factors give every local component, and the residue orders are obtained exactly by divisibility into \(X^{T_p}-1\).

**Constants and varying scales.**
For \(s=11\) and sufficiently large \(n\), the effective v77 bound gives \(n\log D/(D-1)\le m\).
Then \(nH_{11}\le23m\) since \(n\le d\).
On \(B_{p,j}\le D/2\) the collision coefficient is at least \(1/(2B_{p,j})\).
On \(\gamma_p\ge21/22\) one has \(\beta_p\ge1/2\), and \(T_p\le D/8\) gives a coefficient at least \(1/(8T_p)\).
This gives the two constants \(46\) and \(184\).
For general fixed \(s\ge11\), \(D\ge(n/s)\binom b{s-1}\) follows directly from \(n\ge b+1\) and the expression for \(D\).
The inequalities \(n\le3^{b^2}\) and \(D\le n^s\) then give the required power of \(\log n\), eventually covering \(n(\log n)^A\) inside \(D/8\) when \((s-1)/2>A\).
At the measure side, \(n\log D/(D-1)\ll_s(\log n)^{1-(s-1)/2}=o((\log n)^{-3})\).
Voutier's lower bound applies to the same-measure compressed unit of degree \(n\), making this error at most \(m\) eventually.
The constants \(2(1+2s)\) and \(8(1+2s)\), as well as the vanishing on every shrinking sequence, follow at the stated scales.

**Salem inputs with one large-order residue factor.**
For \(X=d(\log d)^3\), the prime number theorem gives \(\asymp X/\log X\) primes in \([X,2X]\), enough for the prescribed \(\lceil(\log d)^{5/2}\rceil\).
For \(t=\lfloor r/22\rfloor\) the cyclic group of \(\mathbb F_{p^{2t}}^\times\) contains an element of order \(p^t+1\).
No proper subfield can contain it since all proper subfield degrees divide \(2t\) and are at most \(t\).
Frobenius to the power \(t\) acts by inversion, so the trace belongs to \(\mathbb F_{p^t}\) and has degree \(t\) by the quadratic relation.
The other \(r-t\) pairs can be chosen distinct from the \((p-3)/2\) split nonendpoint pairs, with distinct traces.
This gives the required squarefree reduction with \(d-2t\) nonzero linear factors and one factor of degree \(2t\).
The identical order argument at \(p=2\), applied with \(r\) in place of \(t\), makes the full reciprocal lift irreducible modulo two.

Coefficientwise CRT combines the trace congruences for the fixed prime list at each degree.
Rounding with bounded errors preserves \(r-1\) separate sign brackets in \((-2,2)\) and one in \((R/2,3R/2)\) for large \(R\), since the endpoint errors in the latter bracket are \(O(R^{r-1})\) against main values of order \(R^r\).
The irreducible reciprocal lift therefore has one positive exterior root with all nonreal roots on the circle, giving a Salem unit.
Transporting a hypothetical torsion-equivalent pair to its unique exterior root rules out torsion collisions, hence \(n=d\).
At the chosen primes the linear factors alone give \(\gamma_p\ge 1-t/r\ge21/22\) with \(E_p=1,T_p=p-1\).
The large-order factor has \(p^t+1>D\) eventually since \(D\le d^{11}\), so the full-image test contributes zero at those primes already at depth one and at later depths as well.
Nevertheless \(2X<D/8\) eventually and each selected prime contributes \(\asymp(\log d)^{-2}\) to the original-field sum after multiplication by \(d\).
Their number gives the asserted \(\gg\sqrt{\log d}\) lower bound for \(m\).
The prime prescriptions hold for the constructed family and impose no such local behavior on arbitrary small units.
