# Review of all-delay factor bounds and rectangle densities in thin seed progressions

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of the thin-family statements

At 2026-11-21T02:20 (UTC), an agent outside development of C345 v721 reviewed its whole fixed statement and proof for Sheng Yin and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had contributed to earlier radical, exceptional-seed count and fixed-delay results used here, including v716, and checked the particular inputs to this new argument.
The review used the fixed claim and its cited materials, without v721 proof-development dialogue, author explanations or any prior verdict on v721.

For both choices of \(Q\) the interval \(1\le b\le K=256Q\) parametrizes \(K\) distinct seeds, each between \(Q\) and \(256Q^2\).
Every prime \(v\ge5\) is coprime to \(Q\) and can divide only one of \(Qb-1,Qb,Qb+1\).
Thus exactly three distinct classes modulo \(v^2\) are forbidden.
For a fixed finite set of primes the survivor count divided by \(K\) approaches the product of the local fractions uniformly in \(Q\), since a complete period has that exact fraction and at most one incomplete period remains.
A prime in the tail must be at most \(\sqrt{256Q^2+1}<17Q\); its three classes remove at most \(3(K/v^2+1)\) values.
After division by \(K\), the sum of the additive errors tends to zero because \(\pi(17Q)=o(Q)\).
The elementary proof supplied in the claim counts integers coprime to a fixed primorial and then lets its prime bound grow, using the divergent harmonic sum to make the surviving fraction tend to zero.
The sum of \(v^{-2}\) controls the other tail uniformly.
This verifies the positive limiting fraction relative to the thin progression itself.

The exceptional count from v703 already takes the union over all delays and both signs before projecting onto the distinct seeds.
Using \(X=256Q^2\) there costs at most \(\exp(O(\sqrt{\log Q}))=o(K)\) seeds, even if all these exceptions were multiples of \(Q\).
If \(a+1=w^2\), the positive \(w\) satisfies \(w<17Q\).
There are at most two square roots of one modulo the three-power of \(Q\), and at most four modulo \(2^j\).
For \(j\ge3\) the latter follows by factoring \((w-1)(w+1)\), where one valuation is exactly one and the other at least \(j-1\); the two small exponents satisfy the same upper bound directly.
The resulting eight residue classes contain at most \(8\cdot17=136\) eligible positive integers in this interval.
Thus all three denominators have the same asymptotic \(K\prod_{v\ge5}(1-3/v^2)\) over primes \(v\).
The even-seed square criterion applies at every sign and delay to every seed left after the square deletion.

At both progressions \(a/\operatorname{rad}(a)\) contains \(Q/6\), and the square sieve excludes every prime other than two and three from \(H(a)\).
The exterior bound \(r<a+1\) then gives a vanishing upper bound on \(\log r/H(a)\) simultaneously over the full sifted set and all delays.
For \(Q=6^j\) both \(2^{v_2(a)}\) and \(3^{v_3(a)}\) exceed \((a+1)^{1/8}\) eventually, since \(\log(a+1)\le2j\log6+O(1)\) and \(8\log2>2\log6\).
The fixed-share consequence of v715 therefore bounds the full prime power \(p^{v_p(R)}\) for each of the two primes dividing \(R\), with an effective bound independent of \(n,a,j,\epsilon\).
The restriction \(R\mid H(a)\) is needed when multiplying those bounds to control \(R\).
The same multiplication gives the stated extension to a fixed finite support with an arbitrary fixed positive logarithmic share.

For \(3^e\mid R\), \(e=1,2\), the positive radical \(\gamma=r^{1/3^e}\) generates the Salem field.
The powered conjugates correspond bijectively to those of \(r\); the coefficient reduction by Frobenius puts \(Y=\gamma^{n+1}\) in the class \(\epsilon\bmod3\mathcal O_K\) because the reduced minimum divides \(X^{n+1}-\epsilon\).
At every valuation over three, normalized by \(v(3)=1\), cubing \(\epsilon+x\) with \(v(x)\ge1\) increases the valuation of the difference from \(\epsilon\) by exactly one.
The three binomial terms have valuations \(1+v(x),1+2v(x),3v(x)\) with a unique minimum, including at ramified primes.
The seed equation then forces \(Y-\epsilon\in3^{v_3(a)-e}\mathcal O_K\) when the exponent is nonnegative.
This element is nonzero at the exterior embedding.
The norm lower bound is \(3^{(v_3(a)-e)d}\), and its absolute norm is at most \(2^d\gamma^{n+1}\), since there is just one exterior conjugate.
With \(d\ge n-1,\ n\ge7,\ r<a+1\), this yields precisely the strict inequality (3).

At \(Q=6^j,\ e=2\), the two exponential rates are \(\log3\) and \(8\log6/27\); the former is larger.
At \(Q=2^j3^{6j},\ e=1\), the difference is \((6\log3-8\log2)/9>0\).
Fixed factors such as 256 have no effect on the resulting strict exclusions for all sufficiently large \(j\).
These two contradictions use only the valuation and size of the original progression.
On \(\mathcal A_Q^\circ\) the additional exclusion of squares gives respectively \(R\mid3\) and \(R=1\).
The positive-unit factor formula yields all assertions about the least measure and irreducibility at each \(k\), and the removed linear factors produce only roots of unity after substitution.

For the translated rectangles, I checked the sieve before restricting the seed variable to any of the three sets.
At fixed \(m=3^u,\ u\ge2\), choose \(\lambda\equiv1+m\bmod3m\) and a point \(z\) of order \(m\) in \(\mathbb F_\lambda^\times\).
For each allowed \(t=n\bmod m\), the coefficient \(z^t-\epsilon z\) is nonzero, so the equation \(G_a(z)=0\) fixes exactly one seed residue.
It differs from \(z\) because \(z^2\ne1\).
At that seed, \(G_a'(z)\) is affine in \(n\bmod\lambda\) with nonzero slope \(z^{t-1}(z-a)\).
Outside its unique zero the root is simple, remains in \(P\), and has cube preimages of order \(3m\) and Frobenius period three.
Hence this residue rectangle excludes \(3\mid R\).
Each \(\lambda\ge5\) is prime to \(Q\), so multiplication by \(Q\) only permutes the seed residues of \(b\).

Conditional on the fixed class \(t\), the excluded fraction at each \(\lambda\) is \((\lambda-1)/\lambda^2\).
The Chinese remainder theorem multiplies the surviving fractions on the unrestricted \(b,n\) residue space for each fixed finite list.
In intervals \(1\le b\le K,\ M<n\le M+N\), endpoint errors vanish for fixed combined modulus as \(K,N\to\infty\), uniformly in \(M\) and the residue of \(Q\).
The omitted delay classes have total fraction \(3^{1-u}\).
The prime reciprocal sum diverges in the stated reduced class for each fixed \(u\); first enlarging the prime list and then increasing \(u\) gives \(o(KN)\) potentially cubic pairs at a given sign.
Taking the union of two signs preserves this estimate.
This proof requires no independence conditional on \(a\in\mathcal S_Q\), on the all-delay index bound, or on removal of square successors.

On \(\mathcal A_Q^\circ\) the only possible cause of reducibility is the remaining cubic prime.
For each of the three actual seed sets the exceptional numerator is thus at most the unrestricted cubic count plus \(N|\mathcal S_Q\setminus\mathcal A_Q^\circ|\).
The latter is \(o(KN)\), and the denominator is asymptotic to a fixed positive constant times \(KN\) in every case.
This yields the claimed supremum over translates for growing length and growing \(j\), regardless of their relative rates.
For initial intervals a contrary sequence of lengths either has a subsequence tending to infinity, already excluded, or has a bounded subsequence.
The bounded case involves a fixed finite set of delays, each with a finite exceptional seed set for both signs by v694.
Since \(a\ge Q\to\infty\), eventually none of those seeds occurs.
This establishes uniformity over all initial lengths without extending it to arbitrary translates of bounded length.
