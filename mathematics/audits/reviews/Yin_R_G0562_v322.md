### Trace signatures and pair action

The peak indices strictly above this point run from \(\lfloor p/4\rfloor+1\) through \((p-1)/2\).
Each such peak is positive and both adjacent valleys are negative.
The rising half-wave is strictly increasing.
At any zero on the falling half-wave, \(\theta-\pi/2\ge\pi/(2p)\) gives \(\sin^2(p\theta)\ge-\cos\theta\ge1/p\), with \(\sin(p\theta)>0\).
Every zero on that half-wave therefore has negative derivative, giving a unique crossing.
A falling half-wave starting below the threshold can reach it only after advancing at least \(\pi/2\) in phase, where its phase cosine is already nonpositive.

Any one gives a real embedding where \(t^2-4<0\), establishing degree \(2p\) for \(x\).
The \(2\lfloor p/4\rfloor\) nonreal traces each yield one exterior root, paired by conjugation.

For \(p>23\) congruent to \(3\bmod 4\), \(c=(p-3)/4\) lies strictly between zero and \((p-1)/4\), and both real and neutral pairs occur.
These are the hypotheses of the [classification](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#classification) and [even sign subgroup](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#signs), reviewed at their [fixed version](Yin_R_G0562_v315.md#prime-support-rigidity-review).

### Local degree, different and discriminant

Each generates a prime ideal of residue degree one, and the first ideal contains \(4\) and therefore \(t+2\), so the two coincide.
