## Exterior traces, disjoint near pairs and the proportion of nonreal roots

The ledger's sequence theorem takes the strict full odd-grid chamber and \(1<M(P)\to1\).
The sign transition forces an odd number of roots of \(G\) in its gap, and the established degree bound for every zero there implies \(d\to\infty\).
For \(\limsup\log d/\log p\le a<1/2\), one can choose \(\epsilon>0\) so that eventually \(d\le p^{1/2-\epsilon}\).
The accepted shrinking-sequence measure bound is applicable in this range and gives \(M(G^*)\le2\) eventually.

The two estimates on the roots themselves also hold for positive integral leading coefficient \(A\) and bounded \(M(G^*)\), as required by the separate claim.
For the \(q\) exterior real traces, write \(\ell_y=\log\lambda_y>0\).
Their contributions to \(|G(2)G(-2)|\) are bounded by \(4\ell_y^2\lambda_y^2\), while every other trace contributes at most \(4\lambda_y^2\).
The product over all lift moduli includes the leading coefficient, giving \(A^2\prod\lambda_y^2=M(G^*)^2\).
The nonzero integral endpoint product and the arithmetic-geometric mean yield \(q\log(q/L)\le d\log2+L\) when \(q>0\), where \(L=\log M(G^*)>0\) in this case.
This implies \(q=O_C(d/\log d)\) at bounded measure \(C\), with \(q=0\) already allowed.
These evaluations of \(G(\pm2)\) are available because the factors of \(G\) each have an interior zero and cannot have either rational endpoint as a conjugate.

The lift \(G^*\) is squarefree since \(G\) is squarefree and each quadratic has distinct roots, with no overlap for different traces.
Each upper gap between successive grid angles contributes pairs of its roots, leaving one upper root unpaired in the transition gap.
Conjugation duplicates these interior pairings below.
In the two endpoint arcs each upper root pairs directly with its lower conjugate, at angular distance at most \(4\pi/p\) across \(1\) and \(2\pi/p\) across \(-1\).
Thus exactly two of the \(2I\) lifted interior roots remain unpaired, giving \(I-1\) disjoint short pairs, even if several roots lie in either endpoint arc.

With \(D=2d\), the integral nonzero discriminant gives \(|\det V|\ge A^{-(D-1)}\).
Subtracting one row in each disjoint unit-circle pair costs at most a factor \(D(4\pi/p)\) in its Hadamard row bound relative to \(\sqrt D\).
After extracting \(\sqrt D\) from each of the \(D\) row bounds, the ordinary rows contribute at most \((M(G^*)/A)^{D-1}\) through their exterior root moduli.
This cancels the leading coefficient and gives the asserted determinant inequality for arbitrary \(A\).
With \(M(G^*)\le2\), division by \(d\log(p/(8\pi d))\) gives \(\limsup I/d\le a/(1-a)\), including \(a=0\).
Using \(d=I+q+H\) and \(q/d\to0\) yields the stated \(\liminf H/d\).
Taking a smaller-exponent subsequence proves the final assertion for totally real factors.
