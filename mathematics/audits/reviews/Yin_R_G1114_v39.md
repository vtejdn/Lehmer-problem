## The exterior roots and the irreducible residual

I obtained \(N/G\) by substituting \(K=1\) in the normalization of L462v36 and solving for \(F\).
The identity \(G+zN=(1-z^2)^2\) and \(N(\pm1)=\pm1\) exclude any canceled root.
The signs of \(A\) at \(-1,0,1,q\), together with the signs at infinity, locate its three simple real roots as claimed.
On the circle \(|A|^2-|N|^2=-2(z-z^{-1})^2\), so Rouché applies strictly to every perturbation parameter \(0<s<1\).
The leading coefficient remains one throughout the deformation, and exterior zeros of the endpoint polynomial persist with multiplicity for nearby parameters.
It follows that the endpoint has at most two exterior zeros and that its only possible circle zeros are \(\pm1\).

At \(1\) the plus case has derivative \(4-4q-n<0\) and the minus case has value \(-2\).
The signs just beyond \(1\), at \(q\), and near positive infinity are respectively negative, negative and positive.
There must be an odd number of positive exterior roots counted with multiplicities, at least one beyond \(q\), and the upper bound of two forces it to be a single simple root.
Complex conjugation now makes every further exterior root negative and simple.
At \(-1\), substitution gives \(p-\eta\), where \(p=(-1)^n\), and the sign at negative infinity is \(-p\).
If \(\eta=p\), differentiation gives \(p(4q+4-n)\), so a simple zero below \(-1\) occurs exactly when \(n>4q+4\).
At equality the second derivative evaluates to \(-8\), excluding a further zero on the negative ray and giving multiplicity two at \(-1\).
These sign checks cover both parities and the boundary case.
The only removed factor with negative constant is \(z-1\), occurring once exactly for \(\eta=1\), so the residual has constant \(-q\).

Every nonconstant monic integer factor of the residual requires an exterior zero by its nonzero integer constant and absence of unit-circle zeros.
If the two exterior roots belonged to different factors, changing sign on the negative root would give a positive Pisot number \(t\) with Mahler measure \(t\).
At that root \(|A(-t)/N(-t)|=|1-2/(t^2+q(t-t^{-1}))|=t^{-n}\).
The denominator is increasing with \(t\ge1\) and strictly exceeds two at \(13/10\) even for \(q=2\).
Whenever \(1-2/(c^2+q(c-c^{-1}))>c^{-n}\) at \(c=13/10\), monotonicity excludes every \(t\ge c\).
The case \(1<t<13/10\) is impossible for a nonreciprocal Pisot number since \((13/10)^3-13/10-1<0\) and Smyth's lower bound applies to this nonzero algebraic integer.
For a reciprocal Pisot number, all conjugates other than \(t,t^{-1}\) are excluded by inversion, and the integral trace \(t+t^{-1}\ge3\) also rules out this interval.
I used Smyth's theorem at statement level.

I ran the complete embedded certificate in SymPy with rational arithmetic.

In these exceptional two-exterior cases the sign \(-p\) gives a factor \(z-1\) exactly when \(n\) is odd and never gives \(z+1\).

The simple exterior roots exclude repeated residual factors.
