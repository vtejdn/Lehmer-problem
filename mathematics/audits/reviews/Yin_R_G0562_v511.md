## Outcome of the proof review

The eventual exclusion through width four holds even at equality with the cutoff.
At width five the surviving quadratic has nonzero outer coefficient and obeys both bounds in (1).
Its existence for unbounded primes remains open.

For a qualifying pair, the coprimality and value equation recover \(\alpha=-U(\alpha^p)/V(\alpha^p)\).
Thus the powered polynomial \(Q\) has the original degree and \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\).
Reversing the graph preserves \(P^2\), and elimination gives a double zero of the reflected determinant at each powered conjugate since the power map is locally invertible there.
Hence \(\deg P\le b\) and \(M(P)\le2\) at the cutoff.
Inverse pairing gives even degree, and a nontorsion reciprocal quadratic has absolute trace at least three.
In the quartic case L242's two real trace roots have exactly one absolute value larger than two.
After making it positive, writing it as \(t\) gives \(2<t\le5/2\), trace sum \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), and integral product \(t(j-t)\).
The successive intervals \([5j/2-25/4,2j-4)\) leave only products \(-3,-1\) at \(j=1,2\) respectively.
This yields the two claimed positive exterior traces.

At width four reversal around \(4p+1\) gives signed components \(A=U\pm V^*\) of norm at most \(2H\).
Each is nonzero because an identically zero component forces \(D=0\).
In the notation of L246, the collision congruences for the value and Euler derivative give, for \(k=a_4-a_0\ne0\),
\[
 r_++r_-\le \nu(pk)+c_++c_-+8\min(t,t').
\]
Here the centered antisymmetric row is \(\delta(a_3-a_1+kS)\), and applying \(\delta\mathcal E-S\) extracts \(\delta^3k\).
The smaller collision depth is at most \(t\), and \(\min(r_\pm,t)\le t'\), which gives the displayed overlap loss even above two or \(p\).
The trace difference has those combined collision depths and has conjugates only up to sign, so its normalized log norm is \(p\log\rho+O_P(1)\).
The two fixed units are multiplicatively independent by their different absolute values and the nontorsion of the circle conjugate.
CZ05 applied to their \(2p\)-th powers bounds the normalized overlap by \(o_P(p)\), independent of the rows.
This proves \(\log|k|\ge p\log\rho-o_P(p)\) whenever \(k\ne0\).
If both signs had \(k=0\), the two derivative identities in L246 would impose proportionality to within \(O_P(1/p)\) of the two vectors \(((t_i-2)/d_i,(t_i+2)/d_i)\), with the same scalar \(S_2/\delta_2\).
Eliminating the scalar gives the fixed nonzero determinant \(4(t_1-t_2)/(d_1d_2)=O_P(1/p)\).
This avoids any lower bound on \(|\delta_2|\).
For the sign with \(k\ne0\), subtracting \(4p\) times the value equation from its Euler derivative at \(\rho\) bounds \(|a_0|\ll_P pH/\rho^p\), and the value equation then bounds \(|k|\) by the same order.
The two estimates give \(\log H\ge 2p\log\rho-o_P(p)\) at every sufficiently large integer exponent.

At width five the same exterior evaluation on the original and reversed pairs bounds \(u_5,v_5,v_0,u_0\) by \(O_P(pH/\rho^p)\).
If all four vanish, the original pair meets the width-four hypotheses.
Otherwise, at \(c=0\) an integral linear combination of the two pairs kills both top coefficients with multipliers \(a,b\) bounded by the same order, using the two entries of one nonzero matrix column.
The resulting pair is nonzero since the determinant of the two original pairs is \(Y^5D\).
If \(a=\pm b\), width-five reversal also forces both constant terms to vanish.
Division by \(Y\) gives a nonzero double-root graph of width three.
I checked its exclusion in L247 from the fractional-linear identity \(B'_1B'_2=((B_1-B_2)/(S_1-S_2))^2\).
With \(y=\rho^p\) and \(j_2=it\), the derivative product is bounded below by a positive seed constant times \((1+t^2)/(py)\), while the squared secant is \(O_P((1+t^2)/y^2)\).
Both estimates hold uniformly in the neutral node since \(|t|/(1+t^2)\le1/2\).
Consequently \(a^2-b^2\ne0\), and direct expansion gives the new reflected determinant \((a^2-b^2)D\).
The width-four bound applies to this pair even if the rows have a common divisor.
Its cost is \(O_P(pH^2/\rho^p)\), yielding \(2\log H\ge3p\log\rho-o_P(p)\).

Both exterior traces exceed \(89/40=8/5+5/8\), and \((8/5)^3>4\).
It follows that \(\rho^{3/2}>2\) for both seeds, so taking the maximum of their eventual thresholds gives strict cost exclusion where required.
For odd \(p\) the stated substitution on rows sends the graph to its value at \(-X\), preserves the coefficient cost and rational gcd, and sends \(D(Y)\) to \(D(-Y)\).
In particular the coefficient \(c\) changes sign and its vanishing is invariant.
I also checked the recorded J177 output against an independent enumeration of the 168 primes.
All 336 distinct seed/prime entries report no witness, with 334 directly exceeding the squared-length cutoff and the two remaining cases covered by the reviewed finite search.
The census and eventual thresholds are separate ranges.

Finally \(Y^5D\) is reciprocal in formal degree ten, and \(Q^2\) is monic reciprocal of degree eight with constant coefficient one.
Division gives an integral quotient equal to its formal degree-two reversal, with coefficients \((c,d,c)\).
The Laurent coefficient at exponent five is exactly \(u_5u_0-v_5v_0\), including when either row is padded.
The preceding exclusion makes the surviving quotient a polynomial of actual degree two.
On the circle the two autocorrelations are real numbers in \([0,H^2]\), so their difference has absolute value at most \(H^2\).
Multiplicativity of Mahler measure gives \(M(cY^2+dY+c)\le H^2/\rho^{2p}\).
For a quadratic with leading coefficient \(c\) and roots \(r_1,r_2\), its one-norm is at most \(|c|(1+|r_1|)(1+|r_2|)\le4M\), and \(|c|\le M\).
These are exactly the two coefficient inequalities, including the asserted specialization to \(H\le2^p\).
