### The binary equation and its transfer to growing exponents

For \(a=1,\ b=q+1\), reduction of \(b\) modulo \(8,30\) gives \(1,17\), respectively, because \(4\mid k\).
The torsion table therefore removes precisely \(\Phi_8\).
For sufficiently large slope the partition into positive exterior-pair counts summing to \(a=1\) leaves one simple reciprocal rational factor.
Its trace polynomial is irreducible since any proper trace factor would lift to a proper factor of the parent.

In characteristic two, with \(y=x+1\), direct expansion gives
\[
             \bar A_{1,q+1}(1+y)=y^4+y^q(1+y^2+y^3+y^4).
\]
Thus \(w=1/y\) gives the indicated affine linearized polynomial with derivative one.
Neither \(0\) nor \(1\) is a root in the \(w\) coordinate, and the factor at \(y=0\) has multiplicity four since \(q\ge16\).
For a zero \(u\) of the ordinary polynomial \(E\), the identity \(k=2^t\) gives \((S^t+S+1)u=1\).
Every zero of \(Z^t+Z+1\) is nonzero, differs from one, and is fixed by the \(t^2\)-power map, so adjoining the simple zero of \(Z+1\) still gives a divisor of \(Z^{t^2-1}-1\).
This puts \(u\) in \(\mathbb F_{2^{t^2-1}}\).
Since \(E(0)=1,\ E'=1\), the whole \(E\) divides \(Z^H-1\), and at \(1\) the derivatives of these two polynomials both equal one.
The quotient consequently has value one there.
Applying the quotient in the operator \(S\) to \(E(S)w=1\) gives \(w^{2^H}=w+1\).
The two values of \(w\) give inverse values of \(x\), so their trace belongs to \(\mathbb F_{2^H}\).
Every inverse pair has size two, and two different pairs have distinct traces by the quadratic equation with that trace and constant one.
This gives precisely \(q/2\) distinct trace roots, with residue degrees dividing \(H\), while \(\log H/\log q\to0\).

In the second family \(x^{2^H+1}=1\) on the selected set, which is closed under the \(a=q\) power by Frobenius.
The exponent for \(b\) therefore gives the new section equation on the entire selected set.
The derivative \(X^{b-1}\bar B(X^a)\) cannot vanish at a section root by the unit constant and the Bézout identity for \(g,B\).
Among the three possible characteristic-zero torsion orders, their binary reductions have orders \(1,3,5\).
The selected roots exclude order one, and odd \(H\) excludes order five because \(2^H\not\equiv-1\bmod5\).
At most two roots of order three are lost.
Projection gives at least \((a-2)/2\) distinct surviving trace values and hence at least \((a-2)/(2H)\) residue factors.
Both \(a\) and \(b/a\) grow, and no rational irreducibility conclusion for this second family is needed.
As a separate exact check, at \(r=1\) I obtained the binary trace degrees \(1,7\) for \((1,17)\), verified that its degree-16 core divides \(X^{129}-1\), and found 14 of those roots in the core for \((16,401)\).

### Simultaneous congruences and the witnessing prime

For positive \(b_0\equiv13\bmod120\), the seed and both new primitive directions have odd exponents satisfying neither odd-odd entry of the torsion table.
The quotient modulo \(P_0\) over \(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\) has \(L^{2n_0}\) elements and \(X\) is a unit there by its constant term.
The multiples in \(M\) preserve both exponents modulo \(t_Y\), so evaluation in this quotient annihilates the new parent.
In the second construction \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(1+b_0 M,b_0)=1\) and \(b/a=M+b_0/a\).
The Laurent algebra over the composite coefficient ring has the presentation \(R[Y',X]/(X^2-Y'X+1)\), with inverse \(X^{-1}=Y'-X\).
Its free basis \(1,X\) over \(R[Y']\) lets one compare coefficients in divisibility by \(H_0(Y')\), including when \(R\) has zero divisors.
Reduction gives the same monic trace divisor at every modulus dividing \(L\).
When a maximal prime power first appears between \(j\) and \(2j\), its term in the valuation of \(\binom{2j}j\) is one and all other terms are nonnegative.
There is at most one such new power per prime, giving the dyadic lcm estimate, \(\log t_Y=O_{b_0}(Y)\) and hence \(\log(a+b)=O_{b_0}(Y)\) for both choices.

The graph polynomial is irreducible over the rational Laurent ring since it is linear in its second variable with coprime coefficients, and its support differences span rank two.
The primitive directions thus satisfy the uniform nontorsion degree-growth hypotheses.
A fixed-degree rational trace factor would lift to a positive-degree parent divisor of twice its degree, contradicting that growth for large \(Y\).
For \(n_0=8,\ n\ge16\), both monic factors modulo each tested prime have degrees strictly below \(n-1\), even if they share a residue root.
The same conclusion holds eventually for any of the larger fixed \(n_0\).
The one-pair theorem gives rational irreducibility in the first family, so the single-long-cycle criterion supplies a good witnessing prime and each such prime exceeds \(Y\).
The second family establishes the modular obstruction independently of its rational factorization.

### The other reciprocal graph

Odd \(a\) is coprime to \(a+b=2^j\), and the translated monic polynomial reduces to \(X^{2^j}\) with constant term \(-2\), giving Eisenstein irreducibility.
Writing \(T=X^a\) identifies its roots with the projected equation because \(T^b=((1-2T)/(2-T))^a\), both coordinates are nonzero at a root and Bézout recovers \(X\).
The phase derivative of the circle map is \((1-1/4)/|T-1/2|^2\), with maximum three and total winding one.
For \(b/a>3\) the difference of phases is strictly increasing through \(b-a\) full turns, giving \(b-a\) simple circle roots.
The original degree \(a+b\) and reciprocity leave exactly \(a\) exterior roots.
On the boundary \(|T-2|=1/2\), the exponential factor \(|T|^{b/a}\ge(3/2)^{b/a}\) eventually makes the first term dominate, as \(|1-2T|\le6\).
Rouché's count there gives all \(a\) exterior projected roots, each at distance at most \(6(3/2)^{-b/a}\) from two.
Their original logarithmic moduli are \(a^{-1}\log|T|\), so their sum tends to \(\log2\) along the stated growing odd-\(a\) choices.
