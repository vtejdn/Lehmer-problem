## Common moduli at the five prime-order residues

I checked the families, degree and divisor case against the [Salem construction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#plateau).
For \(t=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), association in \(\mathbb Z[t]\) gives equality of the two lifted ideals containing \(S_p\), hence equality of the gcds at each integer and of the absolute norms.
For \(k=(p+1)/2\), the equality \(u=\zeta^{-k}(1+\zeta)=\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k}\) gives an element of the real order whose absolute norm is one by \(\Phi_p(-1)=1\).

I expanded the eight numerator identities at \(J\equiv1,-1,-2,2\bmod p\) and checked the trace normalization \(\zeta^{-(J+2)/2}\) in each case.
At \(J\equiv1\) the traces are \(u^{-1}(t^2-t-4)\) and \(u(t-3)\).
The integer \(3\) realizes the full norm \(|S_p(3)|=\phi^p-\phi^{-p}\), by evaluating the trace identity at \(z=\phi^2\).
At \(J\equiv-1\) the first trace is a unit since both \(1+\zeta^4\) and \(1+\zeta\) are units, and the second is associated to \(t-2\), with full norm \(S_p(2)=p\) attained at \(2\).
At \(J\equiv-2\) the first trace is a unit by the ratio \((1+\zeta^6)/(1+\zeta^2)\) with \(p>3\), and the second is associated to \(\sigma_2(t-3)=t^2-5\).
Composing the inverse automorphism with evaluation at \(3\) sends \(t\) to \(D_k(3)\bmod L_p\), so both defining polynomials vanish there modulo \(L_p\).
Every achievable gcd divides the resultant, which is already \(L_p\) by norm invariance.
At \(J\equiv2\) direct division gives precisely the two stated quartics with traces \(t^2-t-3,t^2-t-5\).
At \(J\equiv0\) the [unit computation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#periods) gives both primitive resultants one.
These reductions verify all five rows, the \(p=3\) qualification, and the exact logarithm and lower bound from \(L_p=\phi^p(1-\phi^{-2p})\).

For the lcm family, each prime through \(y\) divides \(J\), and every prime divisor \(p\) of \(J-1\) satisfies \(y<p\le d-3\).
For arbitrary allowed \(J\to\infty\), bounded largest prime factor of \((J-1)(J+2)\) along a subsequence would put \(((J+2)/3,(J-1)/3)\) in a fixed finite-rank subgroup of \((\mathbb Q^\times)^2\).
The [unit equation theorem](../literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound) applies to their difference one because neither coordinate vanishes.
It would give finitely many such pairs, contrary to the growth of \(J\).
The difference of \(J-1\) and \(J-2\) gives the second growing largest prime in exactly the same finite-rank setting.
The respective primes are eventually odd and bounded above by \(d,d-3\).
For \(c=3,4,5\) the discriminants \(13,17,21\) are nonsquares, the larger trace root \(t_c>2\) increases with \(c\), and the second is in \((-2,2)\).
Negativity of \(t_c^2-4\) under the second embedding gives quadratic degree over the trace field and thus a Salem quartic of measure \(\lambda_c\).
The [fixed-base common divisor theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) applies to each of these three fixed quartics, and their finite number gives the claimed simultaneous lower limit whichever residue occurs.
The question left open concerns varying reciprocal inputs and prime orders within the degree, as specified in the section heading.

## Sparse trace coefficients within a multiple of the first fractional index

I checked the ordered-lattice density lemma for real coefficients.
An integral exposing direction can be chosen primitive and injective on the finite support of \(S\).
The slice leading coefficient is a unit in the transverse Laurent ring over \(\mathbb C\), and its slice constant is one.
The continuous slice logarithmic measure has mean zero and lower bound zero, so each slice has no open-disk roots.
For almost every transverse phase the quotient is real on the circle and absolutely integrable there, eliminating all its nonzero finite poles by reciprocal reflection and circle integrability.
The [Laurent division](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#division) consequently has zero remainder over \(\mathbb C\).
For constant \(L=2f>0\), total mass and integrality of the constant of \(SL\) leave only \(1,2\), both excluded.

For nonconstant \(L\) the unique top coefficient \(S_a L_b\) in a lattice-injective real direction is an integer, while \(|S_a|\le1\) by vertex Jensen.
Reality gives opposite support and shows that the only multiples of \(b\) in the support other than zero can be \(\pm b\).
Averaging over the entire annihilator of \(\mathbb Zb\), including its components when \(b\) is imprimitive, gives \(2\ge L_0\ge2|L_b|\ge2\).
Equality gives \(L_b=\epsilon=\pm1\) and exhausts \(\mu\).
On each fiber \(X^b=-\epsilon\) the nonnegative continuous \(L\) and its angular derivative in the primitive coordinate vanish.
One-variable division in that coordinate gives the squared binomial divisor, since every coefficient of its remainder is zero on the transverse torus.
Width in the injective direction forces the quotient by \(2+\epsilon(X^b+X^{-b})\) to be a monomial, and comparison of the top exponent and coefficient gives the constant one.
The original total order selects one of \(\pm b\) for the sole positive harmonic of \(J\).
The resulting binomial recurrence recovers integral coefficients of \(S\) separately on each cyclic coset using its finite support, independently of the order type of the full lattice.

The [first fractional index theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) applies because the sparse lift has measure one and bounded circle norm.
Failure on \([0,A d_i]\) selects an actual coefficient uniformly separated from the integers and hence a nonintegral subsequential limit.
Freezing the entire bounded tuple preserves measure one by the [uniform logarithmic sublevel bound and Lawton specialization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#moving), including when a limiting coefficient is zero.
The [separating functional](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#intermediate) exposes the terms of order \(O(s)\) at each separated pair \(s,H\), and the distinguished term survives on every face because its index is at most \(A d_i\).
Jensen along this functional and at the constant vertex gives face measure one.
Specialization retains the constant one and yields measures converging to one on the stabilized face lattice.

The ordered base moments use integrality of \(B_i^\sharp\) at the evaluated characters, whose eventual signs handle the Taylor cutoff, including the constant.
At \(kt+O(s)\), all active Taylor indices are positive and lie in the common prefix.
Each coefficient replacement costs \(o(1)\) uniformly by the bounded absolute coefficient error and bounded moments.
The joint weighted transform has finite range even for a real frozen tuple, since each layer uses a fixed finite combination of uniformly bounded integers.
I checked the [coset and slope calculation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#rows) in arbitrary finite rank.
After division off \(S=0\), high common harmonics separate the finitely many different slopes, giving integer Laurent \(2Sf\) for each grouped graph density.
The ordered-lattice lemma excludes these densities, and last-harmonic positivity with \(M(S)=1\) excludes the full-torus rows since a Haar marginal would give nonintegral \(SJ=S\).
The zero set contributes nothing to the weighted rows.
Each remaining proper component of the fixed weighted marginal has a nonzero character equal to one there after killing its torsion translation.
The stabilized evaluations give degrees \(O(s)\) and nonzero binomials, and the [moving-index argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#multiplier) supplies one zero interval uniform in the intermediate index.

The [initial all-interval bound](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial) uses integral inverse coefficients below \(d_i\), obtained by formal division, and the signed measure and full numerator bounds of the actual inputs.
At each step \(C=PU_iB\) has bounded absolute sum through \(H/L\) by its zero interval after an \(O(s)\) shift and translated initial estimates.
For \(V=PU_iS_i\), substitution of \(VJ_i\) for \(C\) in the two-sided Gram identity costs \(o(1)\) per matrix entry even when \(P\) has real coefficients.
I checked the [finite-index Bessel estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v108.md#return) with \(k\) fixed before taking limits, and the absolute coefficient bound on transitions from the earlier scale by \(VB=(U_iS_i)(PB)\).
Outside the transitions the coefficient alphabet gives a uniform positive minimum for nonzero values, so the Bessel bound controls their count on each block.
Every interval of length \(H\) needs only boundedly many such blocks, and the multipliers retain bounded support and a finite real coefficient alphabet at each of the finitely many steps.
At \(h_i\) this covers the full support of \(PB\).
Its measure exceeds one because \(M(B)>1\) and every factor of \(P\) has measure at least its constant modulus one.
Its measure tends to one since each specialized face does, contradicting the [endpoint argument for each fixed coefficient tuple](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations).
Consequently the uniform distance tends to zero, and the distance of a noninteger rational of denominator \(b\) is at least \(1/b\), which proves divergence of the first fractional denominator.
