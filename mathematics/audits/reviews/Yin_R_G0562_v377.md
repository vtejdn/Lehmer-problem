# Critical points on a power disk and the resultant at three

<a id="power-disk-review"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T12:17 UTC an independent reviewer for Yin checked the complete [mixed-characteristic disk estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v372.md#decision-mixed-characteristic-disk-submitted) and its [application to double Salem zeros](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v373.md#decision-double-salem-prime-three-submitted).
The reviewer had no role in developing either proof and received no development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanation or earlier finding on either submission.
Both disk estimates hold, and the application gives the stated lower bounds for the resultant and extra Mahler measure, together with a witness of matching valuation order for every pair of classes.

**The critical polynomial.**
At a zero of the Laurent sum, coprimality makes both row values nonzero.
Their ratio determines \(\alpha^k\), which separates the points of its \(p\)-th power fiber even for negative \(k\).
Passing to a local inverse of \(x\mapsto x^p\) and then scaling by \(\tau\) preserves the characteristic-zero vanishing order.
If \(h=a+f_i b\) is the expression on that branch, substitution in \(K\) gives \(K=pX(h'b-hb')-\tau k hb\), since \(pXf_i'=\tau k f_i\).
Thus each of the \(m\) distinct nodes contributes at least \(e-1\) to the root count with multiplicities.

Before differentiation a scaled coefficient of index \(j\) has valuation at least \(js-g\).
For the constant part of \(X\) in the Wronskian, the two indices contributing to degree \(j\) sum to \(j+1\).
For its \(\tau Y\) part and the term \(-\tau k ab\), they sum to \(j\) and the extra \(\tau\) supplies the missing \(s\).
Consequently every coefficient of \(K\) at degree \(j\) has valuation at least \((j+1)s-2g\).
To bound the minimum \(\nu\) of these valuations, factor the reduced quotient as \(\psi(Y^{\ell^t})\) with \(\psi'\ne0\).
Degree gives \(\ell^t\le n\) after cancellation.
The excluded zeros and poles form a finite set, so a finite residue field extension supplies a regular point with \(B(\bar y_0)\ne0\) and \(\psi'(\bar y_0^{\ell^t})\ne0\).
The translated reduced quotient has nonzero coefficient \(\psi'(\bar y_0^{\ell^t})\) at order \(\ell^t\).
Multiplication by the inverse \(k\)-th power of the local unit root leaves that coefficient a unit before reduction, since the multiplier has constant reduction one.
The derivative of the twisted quotient then has coefficient of valuation exactly \(t\) at degree \(\ell^t-1\).
It equals an integral unit series times the translate of \(K\), forcing \(\nu\le t\).
After normalization, the degree of the nonzero reduction of \(K\) is at most \((2g+\nu)/s-1\).
Factoring into linear polynomials of Gauss norm one shows that this degree counts all integral roots with multiplicities, including distinct roots with the same reduction.
This proves the claimed inequality with the loss \(t\).

**A constant reduced ratio and separated centers.**
The \(p\)-th root branch centered at \(c\) with a prescribed original residue exists over the entire scaled disk.
Recursive division by the unit \(p\) gives integral coefficients in the formal root of \(1+T\), and substituting \(T=\tau Y/c\) gives a restricted series with constant reduction.
It takes the required value at every node of that residue, by distinctness of the reductions of the \(p\)-th roots of unity.
At an integral root \(y\), division by \(Y-y\) preserves restricted integral coefficients, since each quotient coefficient is a convergent tail sum of the original coefficients times powers of \(y\).
Successive divisions give the power \(e m_C\) at the common scaled residue after reduction.
For a proportional pair \(J(a_0,b_0)\), at most one of the distinct branch values \(\lambda_C\) can annihilate \(a_0+\lambda_C b_0\), also when one reduced row is zero.
The other factors divide \(J\) at distinct places, giving the subtraction of at most the largest group size.
For the resultant step, a primitive linear factor has positive weight at at most one of the chosen center residues.
Its determinant with a factor of the other row covers the product of their weights divided by the depth there.
After common content removal one row is primitive, so the sum of its disk valuations divided by their respective depths is at most its degree.
The scalar content of the other row therefore covers every scalar contribution, yielding the sum of the squared common minima divided by the depths.

**The Salem disks.**
Since \(q\) is the full three-part of \(d\), the roots of \(x^{ps}-1\) are distinct in the residue field.
Here \(s\) is even, and the factors \(x+1\) and \(x-1\) respectively add one to the multiplicity at \(-1\) and subtract one at \(1\).
Each remaining original residue contains \(q\) simple characteristic-zero Salem roots.
Their powers are distinct because \(\beta\) determines \(\alpha^d\), which determines \(\alpha\) through \((\alpha^d+2)\alpha=2\alpha^d+1\).
For each \(b\in\mu_s\) there are at least \(p-1\) usable groups, since the two exceptional residues have different \(p\)-th powers.
On each of these groups \((\beta^{qs}-1)/3=(1-\alpha)/(\alpha-2)\) is a unit.
At an input of positive depth \(\delta\) in \((1+T)^{qs}-1\), the coefficients below degree \(q\) have positive three-adic valuation and the degree-\(q\) coefficient is a unit.
For \(\delta<1/q\) the term of degree \(q\) alone has smallest valuation, and for \(\delta>1/q\) every term has valuation greater than one.
Thus the powered nodes lie at depth exactly \(1/q\).
Upon division by three only the degree-\(q\) term survives reduction at this scale.
The reduced fractional linear function of \(\bar\alpha\) in the unit identity has determinant \(-2\), nonzero in characteristic three.
It separates the chosen groups, and injectivity of Frobenius gives distinct scaled residues with \(q\) nodes apiece.

In a constant reduction all but at most one of these groups contribute \(2q\) each to the degree, giving \(g\ge2(p-2)\).
Otherwise the critical polynomial estimate gives \(2g\ge p-1+1/q-\log_3 n\).
If \(g<p/2\), the binomial coefficient bound \(n\le qg\) gives \(n<d\).
For \(g\ge p/2\) the desired lower bound for \(g\) holds directly.
The \(s\) disks have distinct center residues, so the resultant valuation covers their sum of \(qg^2\), with \(sq=d/p\).
Restoring common content can only increase this valuation.
The power-index comparison uses \(v_3(I_p)=(d-2)(p-1)/2\), obtained in the cited Salem calculation by removing the pairs involving the extra root \(1\) from the full Vandermonde product.
The earlier multiplicity-one witness with absolute resultant \(3^{d/p}\) gives the quoted upper bound on the two-group contribution after multiplication by four.

**Measure and the witnesses for every offset.**
The arithmetic transfer applies to this primitive ordinary \(F\) since its two stripped rows are coprime and the distinct powered conjugates preserve degree.
The Salem theorem supplies the nontorsion unit and \(m(S_d)\to\log2\).
Only the zero class can reach row degree \(16d+1\) under the stated cap, so \(N\le32d+1\).
Using \(v_3(H)\log3\le\log H\) and the bound on the exponent span gives the displayed finite inequality.
After dividing by \(p\), the squared factor divided by \(p^2\) tends to one, \(d/(32d+1)\) tends to \(1/32\), and both error terms tend to zero under \(\log d=o(p)\).
This yields \((\log3)/128\).

For the upper bound, the constant and linear terms in the expansion at \(A=B\) vanish and the quadratic coefficient is \(rp(p-r)B^{p-2}/2\).
It is nonzero on the Salem zeros because \(A=B\) there, \(x\) is a unit there, and \(V,W\) have no common zero.
The factor \(A-B=(x-1)S_d\) has a simple zero at each such point, giving exact multiplicity two.
The coprime constant and leading coefficients \(p-r,r\) of \(U_r\) give primitivity.
Monomial translation realizes any prescribed pair and preserves the stripped rows and the required degree cap.
At the \(h=d/p\) roots of \(V\), evaluating \(U_r\) gives the absolute resultant \((p-r)^h3^{hp}\).
At the roots of \(W\), the absolute product of the row values is \(r^h(1/2)(3/2)^{hp}\).
The leading coefficient \(2\) of \(W\) contributes \(2^{d+1}\), giving \(r^h3^{hp}\) for the absolute resultant.
Both values are nonzero, giving coprime rows.
The scalar \(-p\) in \(V_r\) contributes \(p^{d+1}\), and the exponents of \(W,V\) give \(3^{dp}r^{h(p-r)}(p-r)^{hr}\) for the remaining factors of the absolute resultant.
The two final integer bases are at most \(p\) and their exponents sum to \(d\), proving the claimed three-adic upper bound.
