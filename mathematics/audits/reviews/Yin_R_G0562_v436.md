### The signed dyadic section

For either \(\delta=\pm1\), \(\beta=\delta\alpha\) gives \(F_\delta(\beta^a,\beta^b)=0\) and \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Modulo two the signs disappear.
Euclid's algorithm reduces the gcd of the row polynomials to \(\gcd(U^{s-2}+1,C)=C\), using \(\bar g(0)=1\).
Thus removal of one \(C=U^2+U+1\) leaves coprime rows even when \(U^{s-2}+1\) has repeated factors.
The resulting primitive linear polynomial \(H\) in \(V\) is absolutely irreducible.
Its exponents include \((0,0),(0,1),(1,1)\), so no nonzero toric derivative direction modulo two acts on \(H\) by a scalar.
The tangency estimate from v389 applies to \(H\) itself and yields \(\sum_{h_x>0}(h_x-1)\le D^2\).
Away from the vertical component a residue of multiplicity \(h_x\) contains at most \(h_x\) conjugates of \(\beta\), so nonsimple residues account for at most \(2D^2\).

At a simple residue inertia fixes its unique conjugate and therefore its square root belonging to the same root field.
In the resulting unramified local extension \(F_\delta(\gamma^a,\gamma^b)\) reduces to zero by Frobenius and is divisible by two.
Since \(F_\delta(\gamma^{2a},\gamma^{2b})=0\), the correction \((F_\delta(U^2,V^2)-F_\delta(U,V)^2)/2\) reduces to zero there.
The pair of reductions lies on \(H\) and off \(C\), since those equations have coefficients in \(\mathbb F_2\) and its square is the original pair.
Differentiating the integral correction before reduction gives \(E_iJ=C^2(E_iH)^2\) on \(H\).
Divisibility \(H\mid J\) would give the same quotient there equal to both \(C^2 E_1H\) and \(C^2 E_2H\), as both derivatives are nonzero.
This contradicts the nonvanishing of \((E_1-E_2)H\) on \(H\).
Plane Bézout now bounds the number of pairs by \(2D^2\), and Bézout in \(a,b\) recovers the reduced square root from its pair of powers.
Squaring recovers the original residue, so the total off the vertical component is at most \(4D^2\).

### Lifting a vertical residue

Over a sufficiently large finite unramified \(E/\mathbb Q_2\), take the odd-order lift \(\xi\) of \(x\).
The monic Hensel factor of degree \(m_x\) consists of all roots reducing to \(x\), and every complementary factor has unit value at \(\xi\).
When \(v_2(T(\xi))=1\), translation by \(\xi\) makes that factor Eisenstein.
Its roots \(\beta'\) have \(v_2(\beta'-\xi)=1/m_x\) in a totally ramified extension of degree \(m_x\).
The odd-order lift \(\eta\) of the residue square root satisfies \(\eta^2=\xi\).
For a square root \(\gamma'\) in that field, if \(v_2(\gamma'-\eta)\ge1\), the product \((\gamma'-\eta)(\gamma'+\eta)\) has valuation at least two.
Otherwise the two valuations agree because the factors differ by \(2\eta\), and the common valuation would be \(1/(2m_x)\), outside the value group.
Every conjugate of the proposed square in its root field would remain a square in \(E(\beta')\), so this argument also covers initially ramified conjugates.

Writing \(\omega=\xi^a\), the coefficient pairs \((g,B)\) at \(\omega\) and at \(-\omega\) are respectively \((2\omega^2,2)\) and either \((-2,-2\omega^2)\) for even \(s\) or \((2\omega,-2\omega)\) for odd \(s\).
After division by two the exceptional congruence thus fixes \(x^b\) for each of the two choices of \(x^a\).
Primitivity gives at most two exceptional residues in all.
For \(4\nmid a\) each has \(m_x=2^{v_2(a)}+h_x\le 3+D^2\), incorporating tangencies on \(H\).

For \(4\mid a\), \(b\) is odd, and the exceptional congruence forces \(\xi^b=\omega^2\) by uniqueness of the odd-order lift.
The sign \(z=-\delta\xi\) then gives \(F((\delta z)^a,(\delta z)^b)=F(\omega,-\omega^2)=0\) in characteristic zero.
At the reduced point, differentiating \(CH\) in \(U\) gives \(H=\partial_U\bar F=1+\bar\omega^2\ne0\) for either parity of \(s\).
Thus \(m_x=2^{v_2(a)}\ge4\).
In \(zT'(z)\) the first derivative term is divisible by four, while the second is \(b(-\omega^2)B(\omega)=-2b\omega^2\).
After removing \(X-z\), the remaining local factor evaluated at \(z\) therefore has valuation one.
Translated by \(z\), it is Eisenstein of degree \(m_x-1\).
Each of its roots has distance from \(z\) of valuation \(1/(m_x-1)<1\), also its valuation of distance from \(\xi\) since \(z=\pm\xi\).
The same square-root argument applies with degree \(m_x-1\), and the removed \(z\) is itself torsion.
Counting all cases gives at most \(4D^2+2(3+D^2)\le8(D+1)^2\) conjugates of a nontorsion signed square.

### Dilation and allocation

The fixed polynomial has coefficient length six, degree \(D=s+1\) and unit endpoints after every positive specialization.
The good-prime result covers both signs at all primes except two in the exceptional seed degrees, and the preceding bound covers both signs there.
In the norm induction the norm of an \(m\)-th root in an irreducible binomial extension is \((-1)^{m+1}\alpha\), so these two signs suffice even for repeated factors of two in the dilation.
The degree of each individual nontorsion factor tends to infinity uniformly as the primitive direction grows by v253.
These facts imply the stated finite exception set and preserve individual log measures and parent multiplicities after any common dilation.

The two seed polynomials are coprime reversals with simple roots and no roots on the circle by the simultaneous derivative and unit-circle tests in the proof.
Since \(g\) has absolute root product one and a root above one, \(B\) has a zero outside the unit circle, giving an exterior pole of \(f=-g/B\).
With \(T=X^a\), the equation \(T^b=f(T)^a\) has no solution at a zero or pole of \(f\) or at zero.
For \(au+bv=1\), \(X=T^u f(T)^v\) gives both required powers, and the product over rotations of \(X\) preserves multiplicity since at most one rotation can be a root at a given \(X\).
On the circle the argument of \(f\) has degree \(t-(s-t)=2t-s\).
For sufficiently large \(b/a\) the equation for the argument is transverse and gives \(b-a(2t-s)\) distinct simple circle roots.
From degree \(sa+b\) and reciprocity, precisely \(at\) roots counted with multiplicity remain outside.
Rouché's inequality counts \(a\) in each fixed exterior disk, exhausting them.
There every solution has \(|T-\tau_j|\ll_s e^{-c_sr}\) from the simple zero of \(B\).
A multiple root would satisfy \(Tf'/f=r\), whose modulus near the solution is bounded below by a positive multiple of \(e^{c_sr}\), a contradiction for sufficiently large \(r\).
Reciprocity handles interior simplicity.
The uniform error in \(\log|T|\) on each cluster is \(O_s(e^{-c_sr})\), and division by \(a\) gives (2) after allocation to any factor.

For \(a\ge4b\), the leading block has coefficient length two and all its zeros on the circle, and \(a-3b\ge(sa+b)/(4s+1)\) gives the separated-block hypothesis.
For \(a<4b\), both endpoint gaps are at least \(\min(a,b)\), including after cancellation, and the term count and coefficient length are at most six.
On any bounded interval of \(b/a\) in this second range the finite endpoint bound gives a uniform positive lower bound for the individual measures.
Both estimates apply before primitive reduction and force \(b/a\to\infty\) along any shrinking sequence.
The primitive directions then grow and the all-dilation result transfers those factors to primitive parents with unchanged measure.
Since the finite list of exterior pole log moduli is bounded above and bounded away from zero, (2) gives exactly the condition \(\sum_j k_j(P)=o(a)\).
Kronecker gives each nontorsion factor at least one exterior root, forcing \(a\to\infty\), and a sole nontorsion factor would receive all \(at\).
This verifies the equivalence and leaves existence of such a sequence open.
