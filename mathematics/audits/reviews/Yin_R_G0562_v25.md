## The two uniform excess bounds

The sparse height bound applies to the normalized lift on every branch, since its constant is one and its roots are on the circle in both parities.
The first fractional coefficient therefore occurs at \(o(h)\), inside the prescribed prefix for every fixed \(\eta>0\).
Its index is at least \(g\), including when some low coefficients of the proposed profile vanish.
Hence \(g=o(h)\), every fixed multiple of \(g\) can be tested within the prefix, and the inverse representation bounds all coefficients of the reciprocal integer denominator \(B\).

Testing at \(rg\) gives integral coefficients of \(SJ_0\) for the limiting marginal.
Testing at \(kt+rg\), where \(g/t,t/h\to0\) and \(k>0\), gives an integer transform for the projection of \(2S(w)u^k\Omega\).
All shifted Taylor indices in the latter test are positive, and freezing the bounded-degree coefficients costs \(o(1)\).
The zero and nonzero layers together give finite Fourier range for \(2|S|^2\Omega\).
Its coset decomposition can be divided by the weight off the finite zero set of \(S\), and mass over that set contributes zero to each weighted row.
The integer transform of the row itself gives an integer Laurent density for its absolutely continuous part on the base.
Taking high common multiples of the graph periods separates different slopes by diverging frequency translations.
Fourier decay thus gives \(2Sf\) integral Laurent for the nonnegative projected density \(f\) of each group of coincident slopes.

I checked the density argument at circle zeros as well as away from them.
Integrability removes the possible circle poles of \(f\), and reality reflects an exterior pole to the root-free open disk.
For a nonconstant \(2f\) of outer frequency \(k\), the outer coefficient has modulus at least one since \(0<|\operatorname{lc}(S)|\le1\).
Positivity after averaging over the \(k\)-th roots forces its mean to equal two, exhausting the marginal mass.
The \(k\) zeros at the minimum of that average are each at least double for the original nonnegative Laurent polynomial, forcing \(J_0=1\pm w^k\).
Division of the integral series \(SJ_0\) by this integral unit contradicts nonintegrality of \(S\).
A positive constant would give \(2f=1\) or \(2\), forcing respectively \(S\) integral or a Haar marginal, which is likewise impossible.
For the full-torus component the last possible positive harmonic has integer Laurent density \(H_k\) with \(|H_k|\le |S|f_0\), by conditional positivity after the \(k\)-th power map.
Here \(M(S)=1\) and \(M(f_0)<1\) since equality requires the marginal to be Haar.
All positive weighted rows consequently live on the fixed finite torsion set of the weighted marginal.
One binomial \(U=1-z^{Tg}\) kills every such row, and a violating intermediate index would contradict exact integrality of \(UB\).

For \(V=PU S(z^g)\), the exact zero interval for \(UB\) and the all-location bound for \(PB\) on length-\(g\) intervals bound the absolute coefficient sum of \(PUB\) through \(h/L\).
Replacing \(S_i\) by \(S\) introduces a uniform \(o(1)\) per coefficient in the two-sided moment identity.
On a fixed set of \(b\) characters the error has norm at most \(b\,o(1)\), as does the correction for negative mass.
This order of limits suffices because each nonzero coefficient of \(VB\) has modulus at least a fixed \(\delta>0\) from its finite coefficient set.
On each block away from the support transitions, Bessel's inequality bounds the sum of squares against a single bounded partial multiplier.
Choosing \(b\) before taking the limit bounds the number of terms per block.
The finitely many transition intervals have length \(O(g)\), where the estimate for \(PB\) bounds the coefficient sum of \(VB=(US(z^g))(PB)\).
The full degree is \(O(h)\), so only boundedly many blocks are needed.
Reversal places the constant one at the leading endpoint, and all nonzero real coefficients can be frozen after extraction.
The phase-translation proof for that tuple forces equality of the specialized measures at the endpoint, contrary to \(M(VB)=M(B)>1\) tending to one.
This proves the uniform neighborhood assertion by taking the profile distance and excess simultaneously to zero in its negation.

For bounded \(D\) the lower profile lies in a fixed coefficient space and the normalized lift has at most \(2(K+1)\) terms.
The exact reflection and lower coefficient bound exclude empty support and constant limits on an escaping sequence.
The sublinear gcd and the root-free open disk supplied by lower-profile compactness permit the preceding prefix theorem at every nonintegral limit.
At an integral limit, the root product and unit constant force all roots onto the circle and leading coefficient \(\pm1\), giving the reciprocal identity required by the folding argument below.
Together the two cases exhaust the compact set of possible limits, proving the uniform bound in \(K\) and hence \(D\to\infty\) on every vanishing-excess sequence.
The exact derivative identity gives \(q=j/(\theta-q)+H_q'(0)-\theta^{-1}\le j/c_K+2\).
I find both assertions and their consequences valid at the stated scope.

## Folded coefficients and reciprocal real limits

For a real reciprocal limit \(S\) of degree \(n\), \(k=ng=o(h)\) gives \(k<p=2h+2-k\) eventually.
The reflection error is at most twice the full coefficient distance, including any terms above the limiting degree.
I checked reduction modulo \(z^p-\sigma\) at both ends of the period.
For \(l\le k\) reversal gives \(I_l=b_l+\sigma b_{k-l}\), and the sign \(a_{k-r}=\sigma a_r\) supplies the missing Taylor half in \(2\int v^l A(v^{-1})\,d\omega\).
At \(l=r\) both halves contribute one, while for \(k<l<p\) the reflected Taylor indices are strictly negative and vanish.
Convolution with the uniformly bounded Taylor coefficients makes the error uniform throughout this period.

The support of the signed inverse measure is in the zero set of the actual lift, so \(|A(v^{-1})|\,|1-\sigma v^p|=o(1)\) uniformly there.
The angular inequality \(l|\phi|\le p|\phi|\le(\pi/2)|1-\sigma v^p|\) bounds every moment error in the first period even at ties.
Tied weights can be shared while preserving conjugation symmetry.
The projected moments are real, have bounded Fourier algebra norm and have the same signed continuation \(l\mapsto l+p\) as the integer function on the doubled cyclic group.
The near-integer lemma therefore yields the bounded norm needed for Sanders's finite-group theorem.
Each coset restricts to an empty set or a single progression of step dividing \(2p\) in the first period.
Clearing its binomial leaves only the ends, so a bounded-term product \(Q\) of such binomials makes \(QI\) bounded-term, also for singleton progressions and the empty sum.

For bounded \(g\), the truncation through \(k\) already has bounded degree.
For unbounded \(g\), integrality up to \(g-1\), the shrinking negative mass and the \(2h\) positive charged lifts allow the local model theorem at that scale.
The length-\(g\) estimate for \(UB\) uses bounded partial sums of the actual numerator and the matched Gram on fixed-cardinality tests.
When the geometric model requires the binomial \(1-\epsilon z^u\), the truncation adds the terms \(-\epsilon b_r\) with \(k-u<r\le k\).
After splitting at the numerator exponents, each piece has a fixed bounded partial multiplier and diameter less than \(u\), so its nonzero Gram differences vanish in the model.
The same fixed-cardinality estimate now bounds the unweighted integer coefficients on that boundary interval.

Reciprocity gives the quotient \(z^kP_k(1/z)\) in the exact division identity.
Multiplication by \(U^{\rm rev}\) reverses the bounded-term polynomial \(UP_k\), so multiplication of the identity by \(QU^{\rm rev}\) gives a bounded-term nonzero integer multiple of \(B\).
Both multipliers have Mahler measure one, contradicting the fixed-support gap along the escaping sequence.
This excludes every real reciprocal limit.
Together with the known circle root and absence of interior roots it forces an exterior root and \(0<|\operatorname{lc}(S)|<1\), excluding integral limits.
In the padded coefficient space the constant-one condition fixes the leading coefficient on each reciprocal degree and sign stratum.
Each such stratum is closed, their union is finite, and the integer lattice is closed.
Bounded heights and subsequence extraction therefore give the asserted common neighborhood and positive excess.
I find all five sections of L462v107 valid, including the neighborhood conclusion independently of the stronger claim above.
