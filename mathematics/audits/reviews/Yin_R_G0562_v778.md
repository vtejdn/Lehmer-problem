# Review of exact and approximate equality for moving Blaschke coefficients

## Arithmetic equality, moving index and propagation to its multiples

At 2026-11-20T20:44 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently inspected the entire [submitted proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v641.md#statement) and the fixed dependency sections used below.
No development dialogue, separate author explanation or previous finding on this submission entered the review.
All three assertions and the examples hold with the stated distinctions between integral denominators and analytic products.

Write \(t=|b|=e^{-L}\).
Reflection pairs the nonreal roots with their conjugates, so the normalized outer polynomial and \(J\) have real coefficients.
The reflected zeros have total radial displacement \(2m\) by the unit product of the original roots.
On the boundary, \(|J|=1\) and its mean is \(b\), so \(J-b\) and \(z^g(1-bJ)\) both have squared norm \(1-b^2\).
In their cross term, the coefficient of order \(g>0\) in \(J-2b+b^2\bar J\) is the real number \(J_g\).
This gives the displayed residual norm (5) for every positive \(g\).
Cauchy--Schwarz for the Taylor series at \(\zeta_j\) multiplies the squared point evaluation by \(1-|\zeta_j|^2\), yielding (2) with the claimed sign and constant.

Zero residual gives the analytic identity \(J=(b+\epsilon z^g)/(1+\epsilon b z^g)\), since \(|b|<1\).
Its \(g\) distinct zeros satisfy \(z^g=-\epsilon b\), while the denominator does not vanish in the disk.
The original reflected product has \(d\) zeros counting multiplicity, so \(g=d\).
On each of those \(d\) rays the original root and its inverse conjugate supply one root at each of the two radii.
Using reality of \(P\), these give exactly the combined root multisets of \(P\) and \(P^\#\).
Writing \(q=-\epsilon b\), the monic product is therefore \((z^d-q)(z^d-q^{-1})=P(0)P P^\#\).
The two distinct radii ensure simplicity and coprimality.
Its middle coefficient gives the integral \(T=q+q^{-1}=\sigma(t+t^{-1})\), and its value at one gives \(|2-T|=P(1)^2\).
Now \(M(P)^2+M(P)^{-2}=|T|\ge3\), whose larger solution bound is \(M(P)^2\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
For \(P=z^2-z-1\), the reflected zeros are \(\pm1/\varphi\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2=M(P)\).
Their squares are \(t=\varphi^{-2}\).
Taking \(-\epsilon b=t\) gives a Blaschke product with the same zeros and constant as \(J\), fixing its unimodular scalar and verifying equality.

For the moving coefficient, each normalized factor with zero \(\zeta=e^{-\ell+i\theta}\) contributes \(2\sinh(n\ell)e^{-in\theta}/n\) to \([z^n](-\log(J/b))\).
Under \(gL\le1\) all displacements satisfy \(n\ell\le1\) through order \(g\).
Bounding \(\sinh x/x\) there gives \(|d_n|\le3L\), and bounding the cubic remainder gives \(|d_g-2L\widehat\mu(g)|=O(g^2 L^3)\).
At order \(g\) the terms of exponential degree \(r\) have \(\binom{g-1}{r-1}\) ordered positive compositions.
Their sum over \(r\ge2\) is bounded by \(3L(e^{3gL}-1)=O(gL^2)\), uniformly for \(gL\le1\).
Since \(g^2L^3\le gL^2\), dividing \(J_g\) by \(1-t^2\) leaves the asserted \(O(gL)\) error with leading term \(-s\widehat\mu(g)\).
Reality gives \(\int |z^g-\sigma|^2\,d\mu=2(1-\sigma\widehat\mu(g))=2\delta+O(gL)\).
This proves both directions when \(gL\to0\).

If \(\ell_j\ge cL\), the two factors \(1-e^{-2L}\) and \(1-e^{-2\ell_j}\) in (2) have bounded ratio for small \(L\), with a bound depending only on \(c\).
The reverse triangle inequality thus gives \(e^{-g\ell_j}\ge e^{-L}-O_c(\sqrt\delta)\).
The right side tends to one, so applying the negative logarithm and using \(L/\ell_j\le1/c\) gives \(gL=O_c(L+\sqrt\delta)\).
This puts the sequence within the uniform expansion and gives the same bound for the angular integral.
A subsequence with the largest radial fraction bounded below would have such an index at each step, proving the stated vanishing of the largest fraction at positive \(gL\).

The boundary density in the proof is nonnegative and its integral is \((1-sb)/(1-t)=1\).
Its positive-index Fourier coefficients are \(-sJ_n/[2(1-t)]\).
Integrating \(|1-w^k|^2\le k^2|1-w|^2\) with \(w=\sigma z^g\) gives, on setting \(A=s\sigma^k J_{kg}/(1-b^2)\),
\[
        1+\frac{1+t}{2}A
        \le k^2\left(1-\frac{1+t}{2}(1-\delta)\right).
\]
Solving for \(1+A\) yields precisely \(k^2\delta+(k^2-1)(1-t)/(1+t)\) as the upper bound.
Cauchy--Schwarz in the endpoint bound gives \(1+A\ge0\) at every index, and \((1-t)/(1+t)=O(L)\) proves uniform saturation under the stated condition on \(K\).
The degree-one product has \(J_g/(1-b^2)=-b^{g-1}\) for \(b=e^{-L}\), which differs by a positive limiting amount from \(-1\) if \(gL\) tends to a positive constant.
The product \((b+z^g)/(1+bz^g)\) has \(J_g=1-b^2\) and individual displacements \(L/g\), including for \(g=\lceil L^{-2}\rceil\).
The arithmetic bound already excludes these exactly saturated products from the integral class as \(L\to0\).
