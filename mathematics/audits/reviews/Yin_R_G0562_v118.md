## All orders through the smaller Bernoulli mode

At 2026-11-17T01:48 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the complete C345v118 submission fixed at 2026-11-17T01:47 UTC.
The reviewer had no role in its development and received the fixed claim without its development discussion or a prior verdict for this version.

In the C345v115 Taylor identity the coefficient of \(z^j\) in \((z-1)B(z)\) is \(P_{j-1}-P_j\).
Thus \(D_j=P_j-P_{j-1}\) has the required positive sign through the smaller mode \(K\), including the case of two equal consecutive maxima.
C345v112 supplies \(E=K+O(1)\) and \(K\sim ma/2\).
On the positive angular arc with \(2-u\ge 2\cos\theta\ge2-2u\), the derivative has modulus comparable to \(\sqrt u\), so the arc length is comparable to \(\sqrt u\asymp a\).
The degree \(2m\) tends to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
The input Dobrowolski bound makes this arc wider than the C345v108 threshold by a factor tending to infinity.
It contains \(\gg ma\) distinct neutral traces, each giving \(p_i\in[1/3,1/2]\).
Their contribution to the variance and the upper bound \(V\le E\) prove \(V\asymp K\to\infty\).

The product of the Bernoulli moduli on \([-\pi,\pi]\) is bounded by \(\exp(-2V\sin^2(t/2))\).
The first and second backward differences of the probabilities have Fourier multipliers \(1-e^{it}\) and \((1-e^{it})^2\), up to the harmless Fourier sign convention.
Their absolute integrals are respectively \(O(V^{-1})\) and \(O(V^{-3/2})\).
At least \(3/4\) of the probability lies on at most \(4\sqrt V+2\) integers by Chebyshev, giving \(P_K\gg K^{-1/2}\) and \(P_{K-1}=P_K+O(K^{-1})\gg K^{-1/2}\).
Newton's normalized coefficient inequality for \(B\), of degree \(N=m-1\), gives
\[
 \frac{P_{K-2}}{P_{K-1}}\le
 \frac{K-1}{K}\frac{N-K+1}{N-K+2}\frac{P_{K-1}}{P_K}
 \le 1-\frac1K.
\]
It follows that \(D_{K-1}\ge P_{K-1}/K\gg K^{-3/2}\), and the second-difference estimate bounds \(D_{K-2}/D_{K-1}\) absolutely.
Newton's inequality also applies to the real-rooted polynomial \((1-z)B\), including at three positive coefficients even though its later coefficients change sign.
Reversal after differentiation preserves real-rootedness, and the final quadratic discriminant supplies the normalized inequality without a global sign hypothesis.
Consequently \(D_{j-1}/D_j\) is nondecreasing through \(j=K-1\), giving the claimed bound \(C_1u\) for \(|h_{j-1}/h_j|\).
The polynomial of order \(K\) only uses \(h_0,\ldots,h_{K-1}\) in its subtracted section, so possible proximity of \(D_K\) to zero does not affect this last order.

For \(|w|\ge1/2\), the ratio of each preceding term's modulus to the following term's modulus is at most \(2C_1u\).
As \(u\to0\), the sum of preceding moduli is at most half of the last one, including the empty preceding sum when \(k=1\).
Since \(|h_0|\ge1\), the last modulus is at least \((2C_1u)^{1-k}\).
Evaluating \(Z^m S(Z+Z^{-1})\) at the member of the inverse pair of modulus \(e^{-|v|}\) then gives the claimed displacement bound \(|v|\ge b_k\).
This evaluation uses only the reciprocal trace lift of \(Q_k\), independently of whether the chosen factor itself is reciprocal.

The monic \(Q_k\) has degree \(m-k\), giving \(U_k\) monic of degree \(2(m-k)\) and constant one.
For any factor root \(\beta\), \(w=(\beta-1)^2/\beta\) is integral and is nonzero because \(Q_k(2)=h_k<0\).
Summation over the \(n\) embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) therefore gives a nonnegative logarithm of the absolute norm of \(w\), with every subfield value repeated to its actual multiplicity.
Writing \(L=\log M(F)\), the unit product gives \(\sum |v|=2L\).
The pointwise bound \(|w|\le 4 e^{|v|}\) follows by comparing \(|\beta|\) with one.
The \(n-s\) small moduli and \(s\) remaining embeddings therefore give \(n\log2\le s\log8+2L\), while \(sb_k\le2L\).
Multiplying the first inequality by \(b_k\) eliminates \(s\) and yields (1) without dividing by \(b_k\) when it is zero.
A cyclotomic factor has \(L=0\), so \(b_k>0\) excludes it.

C345v73 at degree \(2m\), input measure \(a\) and confluence order \(\lfloor\sqrt{2m}\rfloor\) gives \(H_m=O(\sqrt m\log(2m))\).
Since \(a\gg(\log(2m))^{-3}\) and \(u\sim a^2\), this term is \(o(ma\log(1/a))\).
For fixed \(\delta>0\) and \(\delta K\le k\le K\), it follows that \(b_k\gg_\delta a\log(1/a)\), whereas the uniform upper bound \(O(a\log(1/a))\) tends to zero.
The factor heights can thus be summed with their degrees and multiplicities, which total \(2(m-k)\sim 2m\).
If instead \(0<L\le a\), bounded-degree finiteness forces \(n\to\infty\) along any such selections.
Dobrowolski gives \(n\ge\exp(ca^{-1/3})\), and (1) gives \(b_k\ll a/n\).
The positive-part definition always yields \((k-1)\log(1/(Cu))\le H_m+\log2+mb_k\), including when \(b_k=0\).
Substitution proves (2), since \(\log(1/(Cu))\asymp\log(1/a)\).
After division by \(K\), the two degree terms have inverse powers of \(m\) times at most powers of \(\log(2m)\) and \(1/a=O((\log(2m))^3)\).
Those terms and the remaining exponential are \(o(a^A)\) for every fixed \(A>0\).
The reviewer found the statement and all four proof sections valid, including the weak inequality \(L\le a\) and the stated open possibility at small orders.
Dobrowolski's theorem was used at its retained statement scope.
