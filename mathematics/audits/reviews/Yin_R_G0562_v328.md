### A squarefree section of controlled size

After a common Laurent shift the row has occupied extreme exponents \(0,s\) with \(s\le2d\).
At least one polynomial is nonzero at zero, and any common nonzero complex zero would have a rational minimal polynomial dividing every occupied row.
Consequently \(B(t)>0\), and the normalized ordinary polynomials have no base point.
For \(s=0\) the largest absolute coefficient is an integer at least one, which also handles the only possible single-row case.

For \(s>0\) choose a degree-\(s\) member \(P_0\) of the span.
At each of its finitely many roots the vanishing linear combinations form a proper hyperplane, so a single \(Q\) can avoid all of them.
The coprime ratio \(P_0/Q\) is nonconstant.
Its derivative numerator is nonzero, and a multiple root of \(P_0+\lambda Q\) must be one of its zeros with \(Q\ne0\).
Each candidate determines one \(\lambda\), and at most one additional value can cancel the leading term.
This proves nonvanishing of the parameter polynomial formed from the discriminant and the leading coefficient.
Its total degree is at most \(2s-1\), including the linear case.
The grid with \(2s\) distinct coordinates on every axis detects nonvanishing by successive ordinary interpolation.
The selected integer section thus satisfies \(|P|\le H B\) with \(H=2s(M+1)\), has degree \(s\), and has nonzero integral discriminant.
Every nonzero fiber in \(Y\) has each coefficient bounded by \(2^M\) times its multiplicative measure, even at a degree drop.
Integrating gives \(m(P)\le\log H+I+M\log2\).
The leading coefficient inequality in Jensen's formula gives \(I\ge0\) on integration because the highest occupied row has nonnegative univariate measure in logarithmic scale.

### The unsmoothed circle integral

For two roots on opposite sides of the circle the normalized distance is exactly \(|1-w_i\overline{w_j}|\).
On the same side, the identity for \(1-z\bar w\) in the closed disk bounds the normalized distance from above by this quantity, with exterior roots first reflected.
This applies when a root is zero or on the boundary.
Shrinking by \(q\) decreases the modulus in this lower bound by at most a factor \(\sqrt q\).
The \(s(s-1)\) ordered off-diagonal pairs therefore cost at most \(-s(s-1)\log q/2\) in addition to the unregularized bound.
The sum of the negative logarithms of the normalized pair distances equals \(2(s-1)m(P)-\log|\operatorname{disc}P|\), using the leading coefficient to power \(2s-2\) in the discriminant.
The diagonal terms are bounded by \(s\log(1/(1-q))\).
This derivation of the ledger estimate needs only distinct roots and accounts for nonmonic and reducible integer sections.

With \(q=1-1/(2s)\), the off-diagonal regularization cost is \(O(s)\), and the nonzero integer discriminant contributes a nonpositive term.
The smoothed sum of logarithms has Fourier coefficients of absolute value \(\rho^\ell|\sum_i w_i^\ell|/(2\ell)\) in both frequency signs, where \(\rho=\sqrt q\).
Parseval gives the stated factor \(1/2\) and the additional denominator \(\ell\), compared with the energy.
For the unsmoothing, the boundary logarithm for each root has mean zero even at \(|w_i|=1\).
The maximum of \(|(1-z)/(1-\rho z)|\) on the unit disk is \(2/(1+\rho)\), as the squared boundary ratio decreases with \(\Re z\).
The integral of the positive part of the difference is therefore \(O(1-\rho)\), and its zero mean gives twice this bound for the absolute difference.
Summing over the \(s\) roots costs \(O(1)\).
Since \(m(P)\ge0\), the centered absolute logarithm bounds \(\log^-|P|\), and the bound for \(\log^- B\) requires only the further additive term \(\log H\).
Substitution of \(s\le2d\) gives the first inequality of (1), including its \(o(d)\) consequence at fixed \(M\).
The fiberwise bound for \(\int\log B\) gives the second inequality.
Multiplication by a base factor adds \(m(H(X))\) to each of the two Mahler means whenever the specialized polynomial is nonzero.
Laurent shifts leave these means unchanged.

### A resultant-one example

At an index \(t\mid N\) the multiplicities before removing the gcd are the two missing-prime counts \(i,j\).
After division the signs of \(i-j\) distinguish the two supports.
Changing one prime coordinate changes \(i-j\) by one, so indices from opposite supports must differ in at least two coordinates.
Reduction of \(\Phi_t\) modulo a prime \(\ell\) gives roots of exact order \(t\) with its \(\ell\)-part removed.
This follows from separability when \(\ell\nmid t\), and from \(\Phi_{\ell b}\equiv\Phi_b^{\ell-1}\pmod\ell\) when \(t=\ell b\) and \((\ell,b)=1\), as holds here.
At least one differing coordinate survives in every characteristic.
Monicity keeps the degrees unchanged on reduction, so no prime divides the resultant.

Writing \(u=\log|A|\) and \(v=\log|B_0|\), both circle means are zero, and linear Jensen gives \(I=\|u-v\|_1/2\).
In their difference the removed gcd cancels.
For \(h_a=\log|1-e^{iat}|\) and \(h_b\), the common positive frequencies are precisely the multiples of \(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\), and the cosine expansion gives inner product \(\pi^2\gcd(a,b)^2/(12ab)\).
With \(a=N/p\), the diagonal contribution to the squared norm is \(O(k)\), and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds all absolute off-diagonal terms by \(O(k\sum_{r\ge2}r^{-2})\).
This gives the asserted \(O(\sqrt k)\) bound on \(I\).

Only the factor with frequency one in the Möbius expansion of \(\log|\Phi_t|\) contributes to its first Fourier coefficient, with value \(-\mu(t)/2\).
Grouping by \(l=i-j>0\) sums the binomial weights to \(\binom{2k}{k+l}\), with sign \((-1)^l\).
The two alternating tails in the weighted sum give
\[
 k\left\{\binom{2k-2}{k}-\binom{2k-2}{k-1}\right\}
 =-\binom{2k-2}{k-1}.
\]
This includes \(k=1\) with the out-of-range coefficient zero.
Thus \(\int u^-=\|u\|_1/2\ge|\widehat u(1)|/2=\binom{2k-2}{k-1}/4\).
Taking the maximum with \(v\) decreases the negative-part integral by at most \(\|u-v\|_1\), proving (2).
The central binomial coefficient grows exponentially, excluding a uniform bound by \(C(1+I)\).
The example leaves open the case of bounded \(I\) and the convergence of the diagonal discrepancy described in the claim.
