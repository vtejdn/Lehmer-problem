# Independent review of the individual-factor asymptotic at long slopes

<a id="finding"></a>
## Uniformity in the factor and both degree-share implications

At 12:20 UTC on November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the [long-slope claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v548.md#statement) submitted at 12:17 UTC, without participating in its development.
The displayed bounds, reciprocity, and the equivalence for the logarithmic measure \(m=\log M(P)\) hold at the stated scopes.
In multiplicative notation the same equivalence reads \(M(P)\to1\) if and only if \(d=o(b)\).

For every factor the interior radii sum to \(-m\) in logarithms by the unit product formula, and the exterior radii sum to \(m\).
Applying the section equation inside the circle and the three-term bound outside therefore gives \(0\le\log R\le d\log3-(b-as)m\) before making any pairing assumption.
In the long-slope region this gives \(am/d\ll 1/r\).
The square-root measure bound applies there for large \(s\), since \(s^2\log(2s)\gg s(\log(2s))^8\), and gives \(a/d\ll(sr)^{-1/2}\).
Voutier's corollary and \(d\le2b\) give \(d\gg b/(\log(6b))^3\ge b^{0.99}\) eventually, with the constant in the first inequality absorbed by the power saving.

Every inner seed root has modulus greater than \(1/2\), so its \(O(s^{-4})\) localization error also changes its logarithmic radius by \(O(s^{-4})\).
The minimum pole radius hence gives \(|\log |x||\gg1/(as^2)\) for all exterior roots, and inversion in the parent gives the same for all interior roots irrespective of the factor.
Their combined number divided by \(d\) is \(O(as^2m/d)=O(s^2/r)=o(1)\).
A circle conjugate then has its inverse in the same factor by complex conjugation, forcing the entire irreducible factor to be reciprocal.
For the resulting inverse pairs, \(z=x^a\) at the inside member satisfies \(c/s\le |B(z)|\le3\) from uniform localization and the separated primitive cube roots.
The pair contribution is exactly \((r-s)\log|z|+2\log|B(z)|\), while the positive integral resultant divides the parent resultant of absolute value at most \(4^a\).
These give the asserted normalized norm identity for each factor.

For all retained nonzero frequencies, \(0<|i+js|\le 2s^{5/4}\) because \(H<s\).
The powered degree is at least \(d/|i+js|\gg s^{0.73}\) and the height is \(O(s^{-3/4})\).
With the indicated annular cutoff the discrepancy theorem applies to compact Lipschitz functions of bounded energy, and the fraction outside the cutoff plateau is at most twice this height divided by \(\log2\).
Every conjugate of the power occurs with the same multiplicity, also for negative powers.
After Fejér smoothing, Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz give an absolute coefficient sum \(O(H\log(2s))\).
Thus the larger Fourier error is \(O(s^{-0.115}(\log(2s))^{3/2})\), and the uniform smoothing error is \(O(s^{-1/8}\log(2s))\), each \(O(s^{-1/16})\).
Discarding off-circle embeddings costs \(O(s^2\log(2s)/r)\) at the capped logarithm.

The planar sublevel majorant of transition width \(s^{-1/4}\) has discrepancy \(O(s^{1/4}/d+s^{1/4}\sqrt{1/r+(\log d)/d})=O(s^{-1/4})\) by the same theorem and the degree bound.
The fixed-circle floor \(c/s\) makes the integrated sublevel error \(O(s^{-1/8}+s^{-1/4}\log(2s))\), while Haar sublevel mass \(O(v)\) suffices down to zero.
Jensen integration in the second variable gives the common constant \(\mu>0\) because \(|1+e^{it}|>1\) on a positive-length arc.
The correction from \((b-as)m/d\) to \(bm/d\) costs \(O(s/r)\).
At \(r\asymp s^3\) the remaining slope error \(O(s^2\log(2s)/r)\) is absorbed by \(O(s^{-1/16})\).
Whenever \(r/(s^2\log(2s))\to\infty\) and \(s\to\infty\), one has \((\mu/2)d/b\le m\le 2\mu d/b\) eventually, which proves both implications for every sequence of selected factors.
This review uses the cited localization, lower bounds and discrepancy theorem at their stated scopes and establishes no existence assertion for a sequence with vanishing degree share.
