## Tuple counts, primitive lists and large mixed blocks

At 2026-11-20 16:45 UTC a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the statement and all three arguments of C0345v589.
The initial inspection used the fixed claim, its mathematical inputs and the primary theorem without proof-development discussion, author explanations or an earlier finding on this target.
The estimates hold over the indicated degree ranges, with the exponential bound on primitive lists used only as a hypothesis.
The underlying terminal and finite-window witness theorems are used at their stated scope.

Fix the coefficients and one independent embedding.
In each minimal block with conjugate pivot indexed by \(j\), a term indexed by \(i\) on the original side yields the coordinate \(\alpha^{t_i}\beta^{-t_j}\).
There is such a term because neither side has a vanishing subsum, and independence uniquely recovers both integers, including when either is zero.
Every original-side exponent is recovered this way from its own block.
The coefficient of each coordinate is a fixed signed quotient of coefficients, also when exponents coincide.
Minimality excludes every nonempty zero subsum on the left of the normalized equation.

I checked Theorem 6.2, equation (6.24), on internal p. 26 (PDF p. 27) of the [retained Torino author manuscript](../../literature/pdf/AV09--Torino.pdf) at statement level.
It applies over the algebraic closure of the rationals with nonzero coefficients and a finite-rank subgroup of tuples.
With \(n=\ell-1\ge1\) and rank at most \(2n\), the logarithm of its count is at most \(4n^4(3n+1)\log(8n)\le16n^5\log(8n)\).
Summing over blocks uses \(n^5\le nk^4\) and \(\sum n\le2s\), which gives the stated constant 32.
Labeling each position by the least member of its block bounds the number of partitions by \((2s)^{2s}\).
The subgroup theorem and the injection therefore bound the union over all eligible partitions, even when a list admits several.
Of the at least \(d/B\) embeddings fixing the output, at most \(2d/d_*\le d/(2B)\) are dependent.
Summing the single-embedding count over at most \(d\) embeddings yields the factor \(2B\) and, with \(k=2s\), the absolute \(O(r^5\log(2r))\) exponent.

For a witness, \(Bm\to0\) gives \(\log(2B)\le L+O(1)\).
Since \(r\to\infty\), subtracting this term and the constant logarithm per exponent leaves a lower bound of order \(rL\), forcing \(L\ll r^4\log(2r)\).
Splitting at \(r=L\) gives the asserted absolute lower weight even for the restricted finite-window witness.
At strict records \(d_*=d\), and the established primitive count loses \(o(rL)\) when \(\log B=o(L)\) and \(D=o(rL)\).
The weight bound gives that last condition on the displayed range, including its power-law subrange since the extra positive power dominates the logarithm.
Conversely, the finite-window exponential hypothesis gives \(r\ll 1+D/L\), so eventually \(rL\ll D\) and \(L\ll D\).
Combining this with the lower weight and taking fourth powers yields \(L^5\ll D^4\log(2+D)\), as claimed.

For the last assertion the numerator of the incidence fraction is at most \(dE(r-1,k)\) and the denominator is at least \((d/(2B))(c_0/m)^{r-2}\), also after restricting to the finite witness.
The logarithms of the partition factor, the product of block counts and the prefactor are respectively \(O(r\log(2r))\), \(O(rk^4\log(8k))\) and \(O(L+r)\).
Each is \(o(rL)\) under the stated limits, making the fraction tend to zero.
The conclusion concerns pairs and requires a large block in every minimal-block partition for each pair outside the counted union.
