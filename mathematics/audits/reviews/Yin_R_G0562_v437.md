### Exhaustion and attainment

The degrees of \(\mathbb F_\ell(z)\) and \(\mathbb F_\ell(z^D)\) are respectively the orders of \(\ell\) modulo \(N\) and \(N/D\), since these are the least powers of Frobenius fixing the two elements.
If their quotient equals \(D\), then \(1,z,\ldots,z^{D-1}\) are independent over the smaller field.
Every nonempty class of the original six terms therefore needs at least two occurrences, even in characteristic two or when specialized exponents collide.
Two independent differences within classes have determinant of absolute value at most \(3\cdot1+3\cdot1=6\).
Taking their adjugate shows that \(D\) divides both the determinant times \(a\) and the determinant times \(b\), hence the determinant itself.
If all differences are horizontal, each three-element row lies in one class, forcing \(D\mid a\).
Whenever \(D\mid a\), the two row classes are distinct and their vanishing contradicts \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\).
For parallel nonhorizontal differences, every group must be a cross-row pair, all with the same horizontal displacement when oriented upwards.
Translation cannot take \(\{0,1,3\}\) to \(\{0,2,3\}\), since their equal minima would force zero displacement.
This bounds the remaining check by \(2\le D\le6\).

I independently enumerated \(1\le a<D,\ 0\le b<D\) with \(\gcd(a,b,D)=1\), grouping the six exponent residues with multiplicities.
In terms of raw pairs \((a,b)\), the surviving sets were \(\{(1,0),(1,1)\}\) for \(D=2\), all four pairs with entries \(1,2\) for \(D=3\), \(\{(1,1),(3,3)\}\) for \(D=4\), and \(\{(1,3),(2,1),(3,4),(4,2)\}\) for \(D=5\).
There were none for \(D=6\).
Every surviving \(a\) is a unit modulo \(D\).

For \(D=2,c=0\), the two sums give \(y(1-t^2)=1\) and \(t^2 y=t^2-1\).
They imply \(t^4-t^2+1=0\) and \(y=t^2\).
For \(D=2,c=1\), they give \(t^3 y=-1\) and \((t^2-1)(t-y)=0\).
For \(D=3,c=1\), they give \(t^2 y=t^3-1\) and \(t=y(1-t^3)\), yielding \(t^6-t^3+1=0\) and \(y=t^4\).
For \(D=3,c=2\), the equations give \(t^3=1\) and \(ty=-1\).
For \(D=4,c=1\), they require \(y=t\) and \(t^3y=-1\).
For \(D=5,c=3\), they require \(y=-t^3\) and \(t^2 y=-1\).
In the last three cases the remaining group equation follows on substitution.
These reductions use only the invertibility of \(t,y\) and remain valid in the small characteristics.

Because \(z^a,z^b\) generate \(\langle z\rangle\), these equations bound \(N\) by divisibility by \(12,8,18,6,8,10\), in row order.
For example \((V+1)(V^2-V+1)=V^3+1\) applies to \(V=t^2\) and \(V=t^3\) in the first and third rows.
I checked all divisors \(N\) of each respective bound with \(D\mid N\), and all units \(q\bmod N\), by testing \(\operatorname{ord}_N(q)=D\operatorname{ord}_{N/D}(q)\).
The allowed orders by row are \(\{4,12\},\{4,8\},\{9,18\},\varnothing,\varnothing,\varnothing\).
For each of the five stated witnesses I also reduced \(A\) modulo \(\Phi_N\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), obtaining zero, and recomputed the two multiplicative orders as \((2,1),(2,1),(2,1),(6,2),(6,2)\).
These finite integer and polynomial checks agree with the displayed low-degree factorizations and prove attainment for every primitive \(N\)-th root in the indicated characteristic.

### Valuations and trace degrees

For \(d=\operatorname{ord}_R(\ell)\), every exponent yielding one modulo \(N\) is \(dj\).
At a prime \(p^e\parallel N\) with \(e>v_p(R)\), write \(\ell^d=1+p^u c\) with \(p\nmid c\).
For odd \(p\) one has \(u\ge1\), and for \(p=2\) one has \(u\ge2\) because \(4\mid R\) in this case.
In the binomial expansion of the \(p\)-th power the first term above one has valuation \(u+1\) and all subsequent terms have larger valuation.
An exponent prime to \(p\) preserves the valuation, so \(v_p(\ell^{dj}-1)=u+v_p(j)\).
The least \(j\) is the product of \(p^{\max(0,e-u)}\) over the primes with excess exponent.
If any one contributes nontrivially, replacing \(N\) by \(N/p\) preserves \(R\) and \(d\) and divides that product by exactly \(p\).
The corresponding relative degree would be \(p\), which the classification excludes outside its five exceptional orders.
This proves (2), also for \(N=1\) by the stated convention.
The odd-prime-power conclusion follows by taking \(R=p\), and for a power of two the order modulo \(R=4\) divides two.

For a modular root \(z+z^{-1}\) of degree \(h\), adjoining \(z\) has relative degree one or two by its quadratic equation.
Every exceptional order has \(\varphi(N)\le6\), so none can occur at \(h>6\).
Hence \(h\le\operatorname{ord}_R(\ell)\le\varphi(R)\).
Each odd \(p\mid R\) contributes \(p-1\) to \(\varphi(R)\), and its possible factor \(4\) contributes \(2\), giving \(\varphi(R)\le\operatorname{rad}(N)\) including pure powers of two.
The existing modular trace test is used at this necessary-condition interface.

### The factor carrying the primitive ninth roots

For odd coprime \(a,b\), \(3\nmid a\), \(b\equiv4a\bmod9\), every primitive ninth root \(z\) in characteristic two gives \(t=z^a\) of order nine and \(y=t^4\).
The equations of the \(D=3,c=1\) row hold since \(\Phi_9(t)=t^6+t^3+1=0\).
I checked by polynomial expansion over \(\mathbb F_2\) that \(T(g'B-gB')-gB=(T+1)^6\).
Applying \(X\,d/dX\) to \(A(X)\) and evaluating at \(z\) gives this expression for \(zB(t)A'(z)\) after multiplication by \(B(t)\), using \(yB(t)=-g(t)\) and odd \(a,b\).
Since \(t\ne1\), the roots are simple.
The six roots form one orbit because \(2\) has order six modulo nine.
The monic integral factors of \(-A\) reduce without a degree drop, and this simple irreducible modular factor occurs in exactly one of them, with exponent one.
Of the cyclotomics permitted for odd \(a,b\), \(\Phi_8\) reduces to \((X+1)^4\) and \(\Phi_{30}=\Phi_{15}(-X)\) reduces to \(\Phi_{15}(X)\), whose roots have order fifteen.
This excludes cyclotomic origin for the ninth-order orbit.

The equality \(B=g^*\) makes \(A\) reciprocal of degree \(3a+b\).
Reversing its distinguished monic factor and normalizing the sign gives a monic factor containing the same modular orbit, since inversion preserves that orbit.
Uniqueness therefore gives reciprocity up to sign, with antisymmetry or odd degree excluded by the resulting root \(1\) or \(-1\) in a nontorsion irreducible polynomial.
The reciprocal factor of even degree thus has its integral trace representation.
For \(Y=z+z^{-1}\) the characteristic-two identity \(Y^3+Y=z^3+z^{-3}=1\) gives the irreducible cubic \(Y^3+Y+1\).
If its square divided the trace reduction, its pullback would give a repeated root of the original factor, already excluded.
Inversion acts as the third Frobenius power because \(2^3\equiv-1\bmod9\).
Finally \(b=(13+18j)a+18\) is odd, is congruent to \(4a\bmod9\), and satisfies \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,18)=1\) under the proposed choice.
Taking both \(a\) and \(j\) to infinity gives the claimed family.
The orbit identifies this factor but leaves its number of exterior pairs and the factorization of the rest of the nontorsion part open.
