## The common divisor and the two signed height rates

The gcd is \(\prod_i z_i^2(z_i-1)^4\) up to a nonzero scalar, and the limiting logarithmic one-norm divided by \(n\) is \((6d-4)\log\delta\) for each sign.

If \(\prod_j x_j^{e_j}\) is torsion, taking absolute values at an embedding sending \(x_i\) to \(\delta\) gives \(\delta^{e_i}=1\), hence \(e_i=0\).
This verifies the hypotheses of the minimum-support theorem for the two degree-\(d\) minima and gives the torsion-free group required for the full-sequence height formula.
At a generic weight each assigned pair of complementary columns contributes a determinant \(\pm c\), so its term survives the square substitution and \(N=0\).
Continuity of the maximum weight for the center minor, also after \(N=0\), gives both endpoint exponents \(2,6m-2\).

At an ordinary collision the equations of the first site eliminate both centered values at \(\xi=0\) exactly.
The two residual rows in first derivatives have determinant \(c\theta^2-b(1-c/b)^2N^2+O((|N|+|\theta|)^3)\).
In a nonexceptional direction they impose first derivatives at the two inverse points in addition to the two limiting values.
For a last collision the reciprocal kernel factor has simple zeros at both points, so projection away from the moving first derivatives sends the two second derivatives to the same nonzero limit.
The projected obstruction in either exceptional direction is \((1+q)(c+q^{-1})/2\), nonzero by \(b\ne c\), \(bc\ne1\) and \(q^2=b/c\).
This reasoning still applies to the reciprocal orientation and to the varying base at a primitive cube root, because differentiation fixes that base and the chosen polynomial.

At an inversion fixed point the coefficient of the first derivative after eliminating the value is \((1-c)N-2c\theta/(1+c)\).
On the limiting three-dimensional kernel the value and its first two derivatives are independent when \(H(\xi)\ne0\).
The third-coordinate equation at the exceptional direction has leading term \((1+c)N\theta\), while its error divided by \(N^2\) tends to zero.
This checks the single inversion collisions at \(z=\pm\mathrm i\).
At \(z=-1\) simultaneous exception would imply \(x_i+x_i^{-1}=2\).
At \(z=z_j^{\pm1}\) it would imply \(q+q^{-1}=\pm2\) with \(q^2=x_j^{\pm1}/x_i\ne1\).
In each simultaneous case a nonexceptional block therefore supplies independent limiting derivative conditions before the last-pair test.
Polynomial interpolation with these multiplicities gives the claimed kernel of degree at most two after division by \(H\).
The remaining external collisions \(z^2=z_j^{\pm1}\), \(z=z_j^{\pm2}\) and \(z=-z_j^{\pm1}\) each give just one ordinary collision up to inversion.
These cases exhaust all node equalities except \(z=1\) on the algebraically independent slice.

At \(z=1\) the analytic jet basis of the five-dimensional kernel makes the least possible determinant exponent \(0+1+2+3-2=4\).
I recalculated the corresponding leading coefficient \(32b^2-41ab+8a^2\) up to sign and checked its nonvanishing by the indicated conjugate traces for both signs.
For bounded \(\theta/N\), the unique even-column annihilator imposes the two stated odd-column equations, whose elimination gives \((5b-4a)r+(10a-8b)R=0\).
Substitution of the two sign-specific formulas for \(b\) gives the nonzero values in the proof.
For unbounded \(|\theta/N|\), the four limiting evaluations occur at the four distinct numbers \(1,-1,2,-2\).
Thus the fourth-order factor accounts both for the whole multiplicity at sufficiently small fixed \(N\ne0\) and for the only possible nearby common-zero locus at this collision.

After division the center minor has nonzero endpoint coefficients at \(N=0\) on the slice.
Compactness of the projective line now excludes common zeros for sufficiently small \(N\ne0\), and specialization preserves the leading coefficient of any putative factor of positive \(z_i\)-degree for all but finitely many \(N\).
The nonzero constant extremal coefficient also excludes content in \(K[N]\).
Both endpoint arguments apply for each \(i\), leaving width \(6m-8\).
I checked the placewise maximum and finite-place gcd estimates in the fixed-support formula at \((n,x_1^n,\ldots,x_s^n)\), and the two embedding frequencies \(1/d\) for each inverse pair.
They give \(s(6m-8)\log\delta/d\) for the primitive integral row and an additive error at most \(\log(m+1)\) when passing from its log maximum norm to its log one-norm.
Voutier's strict bound for \(\log M(\delta)=\log\delta\) applies at degree \(d\), giving the stated divergent lower bound for the fixed-seed limits.

In the earlier lower bound each of the \(8(s-1)\) distinct nonzero slice points retains two Vandermonde zeros at \(N=0\), since a vanishing gcd there would persist at nearby \(N\ne0\) by Rouché's theorem.
The coefficient tuple of the monic node polynomial has endpoints \(-3,3\), giving the earlier lower width \(16(s-1)+6\).
The external theorems of Xiao and Voutier were used at statement scope and their original proofs were outside this inspection.
The estimates allow the onset of the height asymptotic to depend on the seed.
