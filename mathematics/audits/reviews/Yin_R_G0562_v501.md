## Outcome of the proof review

The stated limit and both directions of the prime exclusion criterion hold.
The proof reaches all sufficiently large primes for each fixed seed, even when the positive integer cap \(b(p)\) tends to infinity arbitrarily slowly or nonmonotonically.

**Lower bound and choice of seeds.**
At a qualifying root, coprimality forces \(V(z)\ne0\) and the graph equation recovers \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\), so powering preserves the field.
Reflecting the graph gives a second multiple of \(P^2\), and eliminating the offset term gives the determinant after substitution.
Unramified powering at \(\alpha\ne0\) transfers multiplicity two to \(Q\).
The determinant has nonnegative-measure integral quotient after a monomial shift, and its modulus on the unit circle is at most \(H^2\).
Thus \(p\,m(P)\le\log H\), including when the graph has additional repeated factors, and the initial candidate \(2^p\) never lowers the normalized cost below \(\lambda\).

After discarding monomial factors, any polynomial of positive log measure below both \(\log 2\) and the nonreciprocal threshold has a positive-measure irreducible reciprocal unit factor of no greater measure.
The degree-ten example has log measure strictly between zero and \(\log(6/5)\), which is less than \(\log2\) and the logarithm of the plastic constant since \(x^3-x-1<0\) at \(6/5\).
Hence the infimum over these reciprocal seeds equals the unrestricted \(\lambda\), regardless of whether it is attained.
I used Smyth's bound at the scope of the identified theorem. [Smyth's statement](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) · [Degree-ten comparison](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#fixed-degree-benchmarks)

**Upper bound and prime quantifiers.**
The seed calculation in L231 allows \(r=1\) before choosing any substitution prime.
Its underlying contraction estimate applies to both signs at every sufficiently large odd prime for a fixed reciprocal seed.
For \(j=1,2\) the same valuation computation for \(P^j\) multiplies the exponent of each divisor by \(j\), yielding the lower rate \(j/2-o(1)\) for the extra measure in units of \(ph\) when the degree is \(o(p^2)\).
These are precisely the two rates needed before and after removing a common powered factor. [Seed-variable jets](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v231.md#jets) · [Fixed-input divisor limit](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#fixed-input)

Write \(b=b_*\) during the construction.
With \(2b+1\) positions, degree \(pb\) and coefficient ceiling \(T=\lfloor\exp((1+\epsilon)ph)\rfloor\), subtraction within the largest fiber gives at least \(\exp((2\epsilon+o(1))bph)\) distinct solutions.
Each nonzero solution costs at most \((2b+1)T\) in one-norm.
Removing a common \(Q(x^p)\) would cost \(ph\) and still leave one seed factor, requiring another \((1/2-o(1))ph\), which exceeds that budget.
In particular no nonzero solution occupies a single class.
For one rational row ratio its primitive coprime generator would retain \(P^2\) and cost at least \((1-o(1))ph\) in log measure.
The polynomial multiplier would then have degree at most \(b\) and log measure at most \((\epsilon+o(1))ph\).
Bounding each of its coefficients by \(2^b\) times its multiplicative measure leaves at most \(\exp((\epsilon+o(1))bph)\) such multipliers, since \(b=o(p)\) and the seed has fixed \(h>0\).
Thus two ratios occur.
If neither ratio gives unequal moduli, vanishing of both cross terms for multipliers \(1,Y\) forces the two ratios equal, so at least one of the four choices has nonzero determinant. [Separation argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v231.md#separation)

The resulting raw graph has one-norm at most \(2(2b+1)T\) and degree at most \(p(b+1)\), so the same common-\(Q\) obstruction still applies after taking sums.
Primitive gcd division preserves both seed factors and the unequal moduli, and the integral quotient rows have logarithmic norm at most \(\log H_{\rm raw}+(b+1)\log2\) by the elementary symmetric bounds.
The extra cost is \(o(p)\), and \(b+1\le b(p)\) eventually.
For any fixed seed of log measure \(h<\log2\), choose \(\epsilon>0\) with \((1+\epsilon)h<\log2\) so the cutoff admits these witnesses for every sufficiently large prime.
Letting \(\epsilon\) decrease first gives \(\limsup \log M_p/p\le h\) for this seed, after which the infimum over the seeds gives the claimed limit.
Neither step takes a uniform onset over varying seeds.
For \(\lambda>0\), a \(k\ge2\) with \((\log2)/k<\lambda\) excludes the small box at every prime.
For \(\lambda=0\), the normalized rate is eventually below \((\log2)/(2k)\) for each fixed \(k\), so a genuine candidate lies within the floored cutoff eventually.
These implications justify the equivalence using infinitely many exclusions.

**Finite factor test.**
Both auxiliary degrees are at most \(2b(p)\), and the valuation at zero of \(UV\) supplies a nonzero coefficient of \(W\) since \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0U-\operatorname{ord}_0V)\ne1\).
At an actual qualifying root the Euler derivative of the graph equals \(W(z)/V(z)\), and the double determinant factor puts \(Q\) in both \(H_0\) and \(H_0'\).
Conversely a retained irreducible \(Q\) and the modular equality give \(\alpha^p=z\) in \(\mathbb Q(z)\), hence equality of the fields and integrality of both \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) since both \(z,z^{-1}\) are integral.
The denominator stays nonzero under the inverse involution, and \(H_0(z)=0\) gives \(\alpha\,\iota(\alpha)=1\).
The resulting nontorsion reciprocal unit is a double root by the graph equation and \(W(z)=0\).
At this fixed point the rational-map derivative equals \(pz(U'V-UV')/(UV)\), which is one by the same identity.
The bounded coefficient lists form a finite search, and binary exponentiation preserves the claimed modular multiplication count.
The limit identifies the infimum without evaluating it or deciding its positivity.
