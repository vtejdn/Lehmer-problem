## Odd powers in the root field and irreducibility after dilation

The argument establishes both (1) and (2), including simultaneous irreducibility for every positive integer substitution exponent.

Suppose \(\alpha=\gamma^p\) for an odd prime \(p\) with \(\gamma\in K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The two monic equations for \(\gamma,\gamma^{-1}\) give integrality, and the inclusions of fields give \(K=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
In particular \(\gamma\) is a nontorsion unit of the same degree.
The cubic graph has nonzero coordinates and \(B\ne0\) at each root in characteristic \(p\), by the polynomial Bézout identity.
If \(p\mid a\), then \(p\nmid b\) and the derivative at every root is \(bX^{b-1}B(X^a)\ne0\).
Otherwise the change \(t=X^a\) is unramified at the roots and the degree-six derivative numerator in the [collision estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#inertia) bounds the sum of the multiplicities minus one by six.
Here \(t\) determines \(X^b\) on the graph, and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) makes the \(t\)-coordinates of distinct roots distinct.
The total multiplicity in nonsimple residue classes is therefore at most twelve, even if the characteristic-zero parent has repeated factors.

Fix one place over \(p\) of the global splitting field.
Every simple residue of the parent has a unique root above it, fixed by the local inertia group.
This puts its local root field and the corresponding conjugate of \(\gamma\) in the unramified subextension.
Only these simple-residue embeddings are needed.
For \(u=\gamma^a,\ v=\gamma^b\), the exact equality \(F(u^p,v^p)=0\) implies \(\overline{F(u,v)}=0\).
The unramified valuation group gives \(v_p(F(u,v))\ge1\), hence \(v_p(J_p(u,v))\ge p-1\), also when \(F(u,v)=0\).
Since \(\bar B(\bar u)\ne0\), substitution of \(-g/B\) for \(V\) in the integral \(J_p\), followed by multiplication by \(B^p\), gives (3) as an integral polynomial vanishing at \(\bar u\).
Its degree is at most \(6p\), and its coefficient of \(U\) is \(p/p=1\), from the term \((1+U-U^3)^p B(U^p)\).
Distinct simple residues of \(\alpha\) give distinct residues of \(\gamma\), since their \(p\)-th powers differ.
The value \(\bar u\) determines \(\bar v\), and a Bézout combination of \(a,b\) recovers \(\bar\gamma\) using both units.
Counting distinct zeros of the same nonzero residue polynomial therefore gives \(d-12\le6p\).

The monic parent and its factors have nonnegative integral-polynomial logarithmic measures.
Jensen and Parseval bound \(m(P)\) by the logarithm of its parent's coefficient norm.
The only actual overlaps of the six terms require \(b=a\) or \(b=3a\), and primitivity forces \(a=1\) there.
The norms in those cases are \(\sqrt2\) and \(\sqrt8\), respectively, and disjoint terms have norm \(\sqrt6\).
Using \(m(\gamma)=m(P)/p\), the inequalities in (2) use [Voutier's Theorem and Corollary 2](../literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) for \(d\,h(\gamma)\).
I checked those statements in the author's edition at that normalization and accept them at statement scope.
For \(s=\log d\), the logarithmic derivative of \((s/\log s)^3 e^{-s}\) in \(s\) is \(3(1-1/\log s)/s-1<0\) for \(s\ge\log1024\).
The value of \(12/d\) also decreases, so checking \(d=1024\) suffices in the contradiction.
Indeed \(3.6(6.94/1.93)^3<167.39\) and \(12+6(167.39)<1024\).
On \(16\le d<1024\) one has \(\log d>e\), making \(\log d/\log\log d\) increasing and giving \(p<168\).
For \(2\le d\le15\), \((\log\sqrt8)(\log45)^3/2<29\), which suffices for the separate range.

The cubic Bézout identity and reversal bound \(\max(|g(t)|,|B(t)|)/\max(1,|t|)^3\) between \(1/3\) and \(3\).
At \(t=\alpha^a\) the pair \(g(t),B(t)\) consists of units, so averaging the logarithms bounds \(|b-3a|h(\alpha)\le\log3\).
The monic equation for \(t\) in the [height calculation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#frobenius) gives \(a h(\alpha)\le\log3+|b-3a|h(\alpha)\le2\log3\) without a small-degree hypothesis.
Substituting \(h(\alpha)=p h(\gamma)>2p/(d(\log(3d))^3)\) yields both strict inequalities in (2).
Since \(d/p\le10\), the coarse majorants for \(a\) and \(|b-3a|\) are \(11(8.04)^3=5716.903104\) and half this value, respectively.
Thus either of the parameter cutoffs in (1) excludes every odd power case, as does \(d\ge1024\).

I used the [adopted dyadic theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v380.md#statement) for both signs in the same root field and every primitive direction.
For any field of characteristic zero, a proper monic factor of \(X^p-z\) has degree \(j\) coprime to \(p\).
Its root product \(c\) is in the field and satisfies \(c^p=z^j\), so integers \(u,v\) with \(ju+pv=1\) give \(z=(c^u z^v)^p\).
At the induction step the norm of a root of the already irreducible \(X^m-\alpha\) is \((-1)^{m+1}\alpha\).
The norm of a \(p\)-th power would be a \(p\)-th power, excluded for odd \(p\) by absorbing the sign and for \(p=2\) by the two dyadic exclusions.
Each prime step therefore multiplies the relative degree by \(p\), also when that prime has occurred earlier.
Starting at \(m=1\) yields degree \(qd\) over \(\mathbb Q\) since the \(q\)-th power generates \(K\).
The \(q\) lifts of each root contribute its original total measure and the power map has nonzero derivative there.
Distinct original factors have disjoint lifts and torsion roots have torsion lifts.
This checks the statement about multiplicities after dilation and the finite scope of the potential exceptions.
