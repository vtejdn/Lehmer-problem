# Independent review of integral derivatives of the marked half polynomial

<a id="finding"></a>
## Critical-point measures, reciprocity and the orders below half the degree

At 2026-11-25T02:49 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v130 independently reviewed for Yin its fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v130.md#statement) and all three proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed mathematics and its dependencies without development discussion or earlier findings on v130.
The lower bound at the indicated integral derivatives, the unconditional nonvanishing of some derivative root through order \(b/2\), and the uniform count of exceptional orders all follow.

The square pattern of v109 gives two real halves over the marked block, with \(r>1\) and \(b-1\) interior roots in the chosen half, and only neutral pairs in the remaining blocks.
The half polynomials partition the actual simple conjugates of \(r\).
As in v128, complex conjugation fixes the marked halves separately and exchanges the halves over each other block, giving \(2u-2\) coefficient embeddings with \(b\) distinct neutral points.

For a monic polynomial \(P\) of degree \(s\), in an orthonormal basis ending in the normalized constant vector the last diagonal cofactor of \(XI-\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\ldots,z_s)\) is the characteristic polynomial of its compression to the perpendicular space.
The resolvent computes it as \(P(X)\sum_i (X-z_i)^{-1}/s=P'(X)/s\), also for repeated roots by the polynomial identity.
The product of all exterior compressed eigenvalues occurs in the corresponding exterior power by triangularization.
Its modulus is at most the norm of that exterior power, bounded by the product of the largest original singular values of the same number, hence by \(M(P)\).
If there are no exterior eigenvalues the required lower normalization is one.
Iterating this monic inequality gives exactly the normalization \(b(b-1)\cdots(b-j+1)\) of the \(j\)-th derivative.
Multiplying over the coefficient embeddings bounds the measure of the rational norm polynomial by \(M(r)\), without assuming that the new roots are unrepeated.

Taking the inner product on a normalized eigenvector of the compression represents its eigenvalue as \(\sum_i |v_i|^2z_i\).
If all \(z_i\) are distinct on the unit circle, a convex combination of them has modulus one only when supported on a single point, which is impossible for a vector perpendicular to the constant vector.
Higher normalized derivatives then have only interior roots by convexity of the open disk.
At the marked real embedding the successive convex hulls lie in the hull of the disk and \(r\).
For any \(z\) in this hull with \(|z|>1\), maximizing the functional \(w\mapsto\Re(\bar z w)\) over the hull gives \(r\Re z\ge |z|^2>|z|\).
In particular every such root has strictly positive real part.

Under the contrary bound \(M(r)<\theta\), integrality of \(g_j\) makes its rational norm monic integral.
Removing its zero roots leaves a monic polynomial with nonzero constant and unchanged measure.
Every irreducible factor has measure at least one, so each has measure strictly below Smyth's multiplicative bound \(\theta\).
Every factor is reciprocal up to sign, and the interior and exterior counts among the nonzero roots coincide, with multiplicities.
The multiplicity of zero in \(g_j\) is the index of its first nonzero coefficient and does not change with the embedding, so there are \(q>0\) nonzero roots in each half.
The neutral halves alone give \((2u-2)q\) strict interior roots out of \(2uq\), which exceeds \(uq\) if \(u\ge3\).
For \(u=2\) equality forces all \(2q\) remaining roots to be strictly exterior, allowing no neutral roots.
In the marked half the sum of the nonzero roots therefore has strictly positive real part.

Writing \(S\) for the sum of the roots of \(f\), the second coefficient of \(g_j\) is \(-(b-j)S/b\).
Thus \(S\ne0\) and \((b-j)S/b\) is integral in the case that remained, while \(S\) itself is integral.
An integral linear combination of these two elements gives \(eS/b\) with \(e=\gcd(b,j)\).
For \(e=1\) the normalized first sum meets precisely the nonvanishing and integrality conditions of v128, which give \(r\ge1+b(B_2-1)\).
Since \(B_2>6/5,\ b\ge3\), this contradicts \(r^2=M(r)<\theta\).
A half polynomial with only its leading and constant terms would have roots of a single modulus.
Hence some intermediate exponent occurs, and its complementary exponent occurs as well by the conjugate reciprocal identity with unit constant.
One such exponent is at least \(b/2\); differentiation through every integer order up to \(b/2\) retains a nonleading term.
The derivative therefore has a nonzero root in all those orders.
In particular the first derivative test applies to every \(b\ge3\).

For \(u=2\), the four degree-\(b\) half polynomials have simple roots, nonzero integral discriminant product and total logarithmic measure less than \(\log\theta\).
With smoothing factor \(\rho=e^{-1/b}\), the energy bound in v628 gives the sum of their four nonnegative energies as \(O(b\log(2b))\).
The first Fourier term bounds each reflected first sum by \(\rho^{-1}\sqrt{O(b\log(2b))}\).
For exterior roots the cost of reflection in that sum is at most \(2m_\sigma e^{m_\sigma}\) by summing \(e^t-e^{-t}\le2te^t\), where \(m_\sigma\le\log\theta\).
All four actual first sums are thus bounded in modulus by an absolute constant times \(\sqrt{b\log(2b)}\).
For \(e\le b^{1/2-\delta}\) the conjugates of the nonzero algebraic integer \(eS/b\) have modulus \(O(b^{-\delta}\sqrt{\log(2b)})<1\) for sufficiently large \(b\) depending only on \(\delta\).
Their product cannot be a nonzero integer.
There are at most \(\sum_{e\mid b,\ e>b^{1/2-\delta}} b/e\le b^{1/2+\delta}\tau(b)\) orders up to \(b\) outside this estimate.
The elementary divisor bound \(\tau(b)=O_\gamma(b^\gamma)\) and any positive \(\delta,\gamma\) with \(\delta+\gamma<\epsilon\) yield the claimed \(O_\epsilon(b^{1/2+\epsilon})\) exceptions among the orders with automatic nonzero roots.
