## Pullback, normalized power averages and the limiting circle maximum

At 2026-11-24T20:42 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v25.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v25.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v25.
The dilation inequality, its coprime equality case, the circle-maximum limit and the two applications follow with the stated quantifiers.

Substitution preserves the coefficient one-norm, and a root of order \(c\) of the substituted cofactor maps to an original root of order \(c/\gcd(c,n)\) upon taking its \(n\)-th power.
Conversely, multiplying the \(n\) rotations of a substituted witness produces \(P(Y)^{an}\) times the monic cyclotomic product of the powered cofactor roots, up to sign, with \(Y=X^n\).
The one-norm of the product is bounded by the original witness norm to the \(n\)-th power.
These operations accommodate real coefficients of \(P\) and an infinite forbidden set, since the cofactor itself is a finite monic integer polynomial.
For the set of multiples of \(M\), membership in the forbidden preimage is exactly \(v_p(c)\ge v_p(M)+v_p(n)\) for every \(p\mid M\).
That preimage is contained in the original forbidden set and equals it when \(\gcd(n,M)=1\).
Repetition of a fixed witness, with the bounded remaining power of \(P\), gives the limiting infimum at increasing multiplicity.

The normalized operators \(A_k\) are positive, preserve Haar integrals, commute under composition and contract \(L^1\).
The unnormalized \(V_k=kA_k\) send the logarithm of each monic cyclotomic factor to the log modulus of the monic product of the powered roots, retaining multiplicities.
These are \(L^1\) identities, so the finitely many circle zeros pose no subtraction problem.
Every allowed primitive order has \(v_p(c)\le e_p-1\) at some \(p\mid M\).
After raising its entire orbit to \(p^{e_p-1}\), the remaining order is coprime to \(p\), and further \(p\)-th powers permute the orbit with its uniform multiplicities.
Thus the corresponding difference of \(V\)-operators annihilates that factor whenever \(r_p\ge e_p-1\).
Commutativity makes the full product annihilate the sum of their logarithms.
For a nonempty subset \(T\) of the primes dividing \(M\), the replacement \(p^{r_p}\mapsto p^{e_p-1}\) for \(p\in T\) gives index \(n\prod_{p\in T}p^{e_p-1-r_p}\), including the residual prime-to-\(M\) factor.
Dividing by \(n\) therefore gives signed normalized averages with absolute weights summing to (3), also if some indices coincide before combining them.
Since \(\log|C|\) has Haar mean zero, its positive and negative parts have equal integral.
Either sign of an averaged term contributes negative-part integral at most this common value times its weight.

For \(F=P(X^n)^a C\), the mean of \(a^{-1}\log|F|\) is \(h=m(P)\).
The positive part of \(g(z^n)-a^{-1}\log|F|\) has integral at most \(H-h+u-h\) by centering both functions at \(h\).
When \(u\le H\) this bounds \(a^{-1}\int(\log|C|)_-\) by \(2(H-h)\).
Applying \(A_n\) to the pointwise upper bound for \(a^{-1}\log|F|\) gives \(g-u\le-a^{-1}A_n\log|C|\) almost everywhere, proving (4).
The argument allows either \(h\) or \(H\) to be negative.

At a maximizing angle the angular derivative of \(P(e^{i\theta})\) has modulus at most \(d e^H\).
For \(x=H-u\ge0\), on the arc of radius \(\min(1,x)/(4d)\) in radians, the log modulus is at least \(H+\log(1-\min(1,x)/4)\ge H-x/2\).
Its normalized length is \(\min(1,x)/(4\pi d)\), so (4) gives exactly \(x\min(1,x)\le16\pi d(H-h)\delta(n,M)\).
Witnesses with \(u>H\) already exceed the required lower bound.
At fixed \(P,M\), all \(r_p\to\infty\) send \(\delta(n,M)\to0\), so the deficit tends uniformly to zero over witnesses at each such \(n\).
The empty cofactor and \(\|P^a\|_1\le\sqrt{ad+1}\|P\|_{\mathbb T}^a\) supply the matching upper rate.
For constant \(P\) the circle maximum equals the Haar logarithmic mean and the empty cofactor is already sharp.

For \(L\), the fourth-order lower bound applies, and the matching witness uses only the allowed orders \(9,10,15,21\), giving exactly \(\log3\) before dilation.
Every positive substitution is irreducible by the cited Lehmer power result and retains its measure, with (1) giving the same lower bound after substitution.
Under the zero-gap hypothesis choose reciprocal nontorsion units with positive measures tending to zero.
For each one separately choose a stage of a coherent \(M\)-root tower after which all successive relative degrees are \(M\).
Its minimum \(Q\) has no larger positive measure, and each later minimum has degree \(M^j\deg Q\), forcing it to equal \(Q(X^{M^j})\).
Each such \(Q\) has at least three occupied coefficients because an integral monic unit binomial has torsion roots.
Parseval gives \(\|Q\|_{\mathbb T}\ge\sqrt3\).
Applying (2) for this fixed \(Q\), one can take \(j\) large enough to lose at most \(1/i\) at the \(i\)-th input.
This yields the conditional sequence with the claimed rate, with neither a common tower onset nor an assertion that the unrestricted Lehmer gap vanishes.
