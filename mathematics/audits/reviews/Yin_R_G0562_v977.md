## Local divisibility and the field norm

Multiplying the coefficient polynomial by \(2^h-T\) gives the recurrence in the proof, including the minus sign on the shifted exponents.
At \(s=1\) the empty product is one and \(w(0)=1\).
Since both signed polynomials have constant term \(\pm1\), each positive or negative power of a dyadic evaluation has an integral polynomial presentation with unit constant terms.
If \(W_h(X)=1+aX^h+O(X^{h+1})\), raising to \(2^h\) and substituting \(2X\) both give coefficient \(2^ha\) at degree \(h\), and division cancels it.
Thus the polynomial numerator of \(W_s-1\) in this presentation is divisible by \(X^s\), and its denominator evaluated at \(q\) is a unit at each prime above \((q)\).

For \(q>R\) all the real evaluations are positive and the expansion at infinity converges absolutely at each of the finitely many arguments.
Its coefficient at \(q^{-k}\) acquires the positive factor \(\prod_{h=1}^{s-1}(2^h-2^{-k})\le B_s\).
Together with \(4\log R=2m\), the real series bound gives the claimed positive logarithm and its upper bound, also when \(s=1\).
In particular the norm comparison uses a nonzero element.

A negative valuation of \(W_s(q)\) persists with the same order in \(W_s(q)-1\), while at every other prime the difference is integral.
The reduced denominator therefore clears the difference, and the resulting ideal has valuation at least \(s v_{\mathfrak P}(q)\) at each prime dividing \(q\).
Its norm is at least \(q^{sn}\).
The reciprocal real values contribute \((e^{H_s}-1)(1-e^{-H_s})=4\sinh^2(H_s/2)\) in absolute value, and the other \(n-2\) embeddings contribute at most \(2^{n-2}\) in total because all their values have modulus one.
Division gives the stated lower bound with \(n=[K:\mathbb Q]=2u\), including the case of two real embeddings alone.
For fixed \(s\) and sufficiently small \(m>0\), \(R\le3/2\) gives \(H_s(2)\le6B_s m\), so \(\sinh^2(H_s(2)/2)\ll_s m^2\) uniformly in the degree and block size.
The numerator at two is exactly \(2^{(s-1)n}\).

The involution inverts every factor, forcing its valuations to vanish at fixed primes and giving opposite valuations at the two members of a split pair.
Those primes have equal absolute norms, so exactly the absolute value of the signed valuation sum enters the log denominator norm from each pair, with zero contribution when the valuations cancel completely.
For \(s=2\) the polynomial \(2-T\) yields the indicated quotient, and the argument still applies with identical prime support.
The whole claim follows under the reversed-block hypotheses, with no bound on the multiplicities at previously occurring primes.
