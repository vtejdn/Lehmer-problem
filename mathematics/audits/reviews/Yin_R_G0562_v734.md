## The simultaneous permutation construction

At 2026-11-20T17:21 UTC a separate reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the full fixed statement and argument of C0345v597 and found them valid.
The reviewer had worked on upper bounds for symmetric dilations, outside the development of this construction.
The review request identified the claimed obstruction and the fixed proof without supplying its construction, the author's derivation or an earlier review of it.

For a proper nonempty job subset, dominant terms at an exterior embedding and its reciprocal give the stated absolute asymptotics, regardless of their complex phases.
With two distinct exterior moduli, division eliminates the rational coefficient; the discrepancy in the two exponent predictions is an integer of absolute size tending to zero along any unbounded sequence of exceptions.
The exterior moduli therefore force the exponent to equal the nonpositive displacement of the largest selected index, while the two interior moduli force it to equal the nonnegative displacement of the smallest.
Both vanish and both endpoints are selected; the nonempty complementary ratio would then also be rational, nonzero and subject to the same incompatible endpoint requirement.
With a neutral conjugate, compactness gives unbounded even times taking the conjugate to one in the limit, including the periodic case.
The modulus of the rational coefficient at that embedding tends to the subset size divided by the full size, strictly between zero and one.
Its square as calculated at a reciprocal pair of off-circle embeddings tends to zero or one according to the span, excluding each subset eventually along these times.
There are finitely many subsets, so the same time works for all of them.

The simultaneous independence lemma applies to the distinct integer positions of one group because all powers preserve the degree.
The first nonzero coefficient in its local analytic determinant is a nonzero product of Vandermonde determinants, so a common dilation realizes full rank on every set of as many columns as the degree.
Multiplying all columns of a group by the same field element preserves their rational independence.
Since the group size exceeds the degree by two, prescribing its field sum leaves an affine plane over the rationals.
The group spacing preserves the order of all terms under each job assignment, and the sum of all resulting positive exponents is independent of this assignment.
Distinct assignments differ in at least one group and hence give different arrays; proportional arrays with the same positive coordinate sum must coincide.

The image of the linear part on a single group is the intersection of the two complementary column spans, giving the stated rank formula by dimension addition and full rank of their sum.
The two-parameter groups vary independently, so two different singleton directions add to dimension two because their quotient is a nonzero power of a nontorsion unit and cannot be rational.
If only one singleton direction occurs, its translate has base point either a nonempty proper job ratio or one, except for the excluded cut of size one.
The proper ratio belongs to no rational power line, and one is outside the singleton direction at a positive exponent.
A translated line can be contained in a forbidden line only if the two directions coincide and its base point lies on that line.
All zero-dimensional eligible cuts are covered by proper nonempty job ratios.
Subsets involving the marked term are tested by their outgoing complement, using the full zero sum and symmetry of the carrier condition under sign change.
The full outgoing set has nonzero sum, and each coordinate varies on its group plane, which also permits simultaneous exclusion of zeros at singleton cuts.

A single integer clears denominators in the finitely many affine parameter coefficients independently of the parameter box size.
The resulting integral cut values have height at most the logarithm of the box size plus a fixed constant, by bounding every conjugate separately.
For a nonzero carrier, the rational absolute norm controls the height of its rational scalar by the height of the value, leaving only logarithmically many possible integer exponents in that box.
Each proper affine preimage occupies at most one box point for each choice of all but one parameter, and the finitely many permutations and cuts are fixed before the box grows.
Their union and the singleton-zero planes therefore omit an integer point for all sufficiently large boxes, simultaneously giving the same integer coefficients and output for every permutation.
For fixed degree the ratio of the logarithm of the factorial to the weight tends to infinity, giving the claimed obstruction at unbounded weights.
The Salem and strict-record input conditions follow respectively from power-degree preservation for Salem numbers and the two-circle descent at strict records of unbounded degree.
The construction places no quantitative constraint on coefficient heights or spans that would put its witnesses in the shrinking-measure windows.
