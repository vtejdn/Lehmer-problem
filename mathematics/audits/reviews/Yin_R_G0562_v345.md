### Specialization, reciprocity and separability

The strict plastic threshold applies to the whole \(P\), whose nonzero constant term permits reversal in its actual degree \(n\).
In the prime lift each centered exponent occurs with its negative and \(d=(n-2c)/p\) is an integer.
The reversal of \(P\) shows that \(F(X,Y)\) and \(\sigma X^d F(X^{-1},Y^{-1})\) agree after substitution.
Their difference has fast exponents in the same interval of width \(h<p\).
Equality \(pj+be=pj'+be'\) forces \(e=e'\) by reduction modulo \(p\), then \(j=j'\), so every coefficient of this difference vanishes.
For \(|X|=1\) the identity gives \(q_X(Y)=\sigma X^dY^h\overline{q_X(1/\bar Y)}\).

In the integral Laurent ring widths in \(Y\) add in products because both extreme coefficients multiply without vanishing.
Every factor of \(F\) consequently has width at most \(h\).
The same exponent calculation preserves distinct terms and their coefficients within each such factor, even if its interval is translated.
A many-term factor remains a nonunit, as does a one-term factor with coefficient of absolute value greater than one.
Localization at \(x\) preserves irreducibility of \(P\) since \(P(0)\ne0\) and \(P\) has at least two terms.
This rules out a Laurent factorization into two nonunits, including one supplied by a nonunit common row divisor since the quotient would still occupy \(k\) rows.
The polynomial \(q_X\) has a nonzero constant coefficient in \(Y\), so its Laurent irreducibility and primitivity imply irreducibility in \(\mathbb Q(X)[Y]\) by Gauss's lemma.
Characteristic zero now gives a nonzero discriminant \(\mathcal D\).
After clearing powers of \(X\), its integral leading coefficient has absolute value at least one, so Jensen's formula gives \(m(\mathcal D)\ge0\).
The zeros of the two extreme rows and of \(\mathcal D\) on the circle form a finite set.

### Determinant and signed logarithmic discrepancies

On every remaining fiber the reciprocal conjugation pairs each exterior root \(Ru\) uniquely with \(R^{-1}u\).
Dividing row \(i\) of the ordinary Vandermonde matrix by \(\max(1,|z_i|)^{h-1}\) gives squared absolute determinant \(|\mathcal D(X)|e^{-2(h-1)m_X}\), since \(|A|^{2(h-1)}\) is the leading-coefficient factor of the absolute discriminant.
The \(j\)-th entry of an exterior-minus-interior row is \(u^j(r^{h-1-j}-r^j)\) for \(r=R^{-1}\).
For integers \(0\le a,b\le h-1\), \(|r^a-r^b|\le |a-b|(1-r)\le h(1-r^2)\).
Thus each of the \(s\) replaced rows contributes at most \(\sqrt h\,h(1-r^2)\) to the Hadamard product, and each unchanged row contributes at most \(\sqrt h\).
Squaring gives \(h^{h+2s}\) times the product of the squared radial deficits, with \(h+2s\le2h\).
This includes the empty product when all roots are on the circle.

As \(x\) ranges over \(x^p=X\), the \(p\) values of \(x^b\) run through \(Y^p=X^b\) exactly once.
If \(J_p(X)\) denotes the corresponding mean of \(\log|q_X(Y)|\), the product of the grid distances to each root and Jensen's formula give
\[
 p(J_p-m_X)=\sum_z\log\frac{|z^p-X^b|}{\max(1,|z|)^p}.
\]
The absolute value of each normalized factor is at most two.
For either member of a radial pair it is at least \(1-r^p\ge1-r^2\), so the pair contributes at least \(2\log(1-r^2)\).
Taking logarithms of the determinant bound gives the claimed lower bound for the sum over those pairs.
Each unit root contributes \(\log 2-\log(2/|u^p-X^b|)\), which accounts for the sign of \(L_p\).

The positive part of the torus logarithm is bounded, and \(m_X\ge\log|A(X)|\) has an integrable lower bound, so fiber integration gives \(\int m_X=m(F)\).
The conditional mean \(J_p\) is integrable with integral \(m(P)\), since it averages a univariate polynomial logarithm after multiplication by monomials of modulus one.
In particular, exact grid coincidence is confined to images of the finitely many unit roots of \(P\).
The unit-root restriction of a locally labeled simple root is measurable by continuity of its modulus.
Its symmetric loss sum is bounded by the nonnegative total deficit \(h\log 2-p(J_p-m_X)\) almost everywhere, proving \(0\le L_p<\infty\).
Integrating the pair estimate and dropping the nonnegative unit-root count times \(\log 2\) gives exactly the lower side of (1), while summing the upper bound \(\log 2\) gives its upper side.
For \(h=2\) the direct separation formula of the three-row comparison yields the indicated stronger estimate.

### The limiting loss

The projected-support theorem applies because the \(k\) nonzero rows have unit gcd.
In its zero case every cyclotomic factor specializes to a polynomial of measure zero or to a nonzero integer constant, since the product specializes to a nonzero polynomial.
Hence the specialized measure would be zero or at least \(\log 2\), contrary to \(0<m(P)<\log\theta\).
This proves \(m(F)\ge\eta_k\).
For fixed \(k\), the fast span gives \(h\log h/p\to0\) and \(2(h-1)/p\to0\).
Once \(1-2(h-1)/p>0\), inequality (1) yields
\[
 \frac{L_p}{p}\ \ge\ \left(1-\frac{2(h-1)}p\right)\eta_k
                         -\frac{2h\log h}{p}-m(P).
\]
This proves (2) without an upper bound on \(m(F)\), and when \(L_p=0\) it gives \(m(P)\ge\eta_k/2\) for all sufficiently large \(p\) in the stated class.
With the cutoff \(2e^{-\gamma p/h}\) every discarded unit root costs at most \(\gamma p/h\), including equality, and there are at most \(h\) roots per fiber.
The retained lower limit is therefore at least \(\eta_k-\gamma\).
The proof supplies a necessary concentration estimate for these lifts and leaves the total near-resonance loss for general fibers unresolved.
