## Moment bounds, endpoint constants and tails

The normalization at zero leaves an integral Taylor germ since \(P(0)=\pm1\).
A finite germ or a common root after a monomial shift would imply Laurent divisibility by \(P\), using monicity and irreducibility.
The normalized Blaschke product of the interior poles thus has modulus \(e^{-m}\) at zero, by the product of all root moduli and the definition of \(m\).
On the span of \(X^{s_1},\ldots,X^{s_k}\), the compressed inner multiplication is contractive and triangular with diagonal \(e^{-m}\).
Its smallest singular value is at least the modulus \(e^{-km}\) of its determinant.
For each output position all earlier nonzero entries of the input germ occur in this compression, so the prefix inequality (3) holds even when that germ has a pole in the disk.

For the coefficient test polynomial, the \(L^2\) identity and the \(L^\infty\) bound give the factor \(k^{1/2-1/p'}\) in its \(L^{p'}\) norm, with factor one when \(p=2\).
Pairing with \(F\) and dividing by the nonzero coefficient-vector norm leaves the factor \(k^{1/p'}e^{-km}\).
Its \(p\)-th power is \(k^{p-1}e^{-pkm}\).
The integer maximum exists by exponential decay.
At fixed \(p>1\), rounding the real maximizer down changes its argument by less than one, so both the power and exponential factors have ratios tending to one.

For the endpoint estimate, removing the finite inner factor of the open-disk zeros gives a zero-free analytic function in the open disk with continuous boundary modulus.
The squared moduli of the analytic square root and its product with that inner factor converge uniformly along radii to \(|F|\), even at boundary zeros.
By Parseval each coefficient vector has squared norm \(\|F\|_1\).
In each convolution at \(n>0\), one of the factors has index in \([\lceil n/2\rceil,n]\).
Each index in that range contributes for at most \(D(S)\) values of \(n\).
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the sum of squares of each half by \(D(S)\|F\|_1^2\), and the square of their sum costs the additional factor two.
This recovers \(4D(S)\) and the separate constant-coefficient contribution in (2), including \(k=1\).
For \(k=\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor\), division by \(k\) shows that \(\liminf D_k/k\ge 1/(4\mathrm e C^2)\) under the uniform norm bound.
Conversely (2) gives divergence when \((1+4D_k)/k\to0\).

For the shorter prefix the integral coefficients have squared sum at most \(kC^2 e^{2km}\).
The off-diagonal inner coefficients in a single row have squared sum at most \(1-e^{-2m}\), since their indices are distinct.
With \(k=\lfloor c/m\rfloor\ge1\), the resulting error is at most \(e^{1/64}\sqrt{2}/8<1/4\), and the diagonal term has magnitude at least \(e^{-1/64}>3/4\).
One representative per occupied half-open dyadic interval meets every closed interval \([\ell,2\ell]\) in at most two positions, including when \(\ell\) is a power of two.
The lower bound \(1/2\) for each coefficient and (4) therefore give at most \(32C^2\) representatives.

For the reciprocal application, inversion preserves the principal Laurent ideal of \(P\), and its quotient is a domain by monicity and irreducibility.
The reciprocal product numerator is therefore admissible whenever \(P\nmid B\).
Its quotient has circle modulus \(W\), and the monomial and sign in \(u\) preserve its normalized Taylor support.
On \(W\le t\) the contribution to the \(p\)-th moment is at most \(Ct^{p-1}\).
At \(t=\delta/m\) the scaled lower limit is at least \(((p-1)/(p\mathrm e))^{p-1}-C\delta^{p-1}\), strictly positive under the stated condition.
The exact pointwise criterion gives \(W\le4\) and meets the quadratic-gap hypothesis after shifting the numerator to a polynomial.
The short-square comparison uses precisely the strict width condition and the least nonzero coefficient.
All the stated conclusions follow, and the endpoint inequalities leave the unrestricted-width divergence question open.
