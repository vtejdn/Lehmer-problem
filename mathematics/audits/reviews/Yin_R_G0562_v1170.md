## The norm bounds, square classes and cycle divisibility

At 2026-11-25T01:37 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v110.md#statement) and its three proof sections at SHA-256 97AC1CD0D87709906F9169D441B220B30E07FA3503B93A3425F3EDFC7C0F93A2.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or earlier findings on v110.
The degree estimate, the two parity-dependent square conditions, the Frobenius cycle formula and its exclusion of the specified example hold.

At a common dilation the primitive power is again a nontorsion algebraic unit.
The parent is exactly the positive \(F_+\) of the short-exponent-one theorem, which puts the unique exterior root \(\alpha\) in \((1,p/q)\), requires \(v>L\) and makes it a reciprocal algebraic unit.
This applies even when the given primitive power was another conjugate.
Indeed its unit minimum must contain an exterior root, and there is only one in the parent.
The other trace conjugates lie strictly in \((-2,2)\).
The endpoints would force a torsion root, and the reciprocal interior member of the real pair gives the same distinguished trace.
The groupings by inversion give \([B:\mathbb Q]=n\) and \(|S(1)|=|N_{B/\mathbb Q}(w)|\).

Multiplying the pole denominator by its inverse conjugate gives \((p-q\alpha)(p-q/\alpha)=p^2+q^2-pq\,t=1-Mw=\delta\).
At the exterior embedding \(0<\delta<1\), and its norm over \(B\) is the signed pole norm one over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Thus \(\delta^{-1}=\prod_{i>1}(1+M|w_i|)\ge M^{n-1}|S(1)|/w>M^n|S(1)|\).
The equation \(Y=\alpha^v=(p\alpha-q)/(p-q\alpha)\) gives \(Y\delta=(p\alpha-q)(p-q/\alpha)>1\), since \(\alpha>1\) and \(p-q=1\).
This proves both strict bounds, including the use of \(|S(1)|\ge1\) for the logarithmic degree bound.
For fixed trace, the sum of logarithms of \(1-xw_i\) starts at zero, has derivative \(-\operatorname{Tr}(w)\) there, is strictly concave on the stated interval, and tends to \(-\infty\) at its far end.
It has exactly one positive zero if its initial derivative is positive, and none otherwise.
Since \(q(q+1)\) is strictly increasing, the coefficient-parameter uniqueness follows.

The unit \(A=1/(p-q\alpha)=(Y+1)/(\alpha+1)\) satisfies \(\iota(A)=A\alpha^{1-v}\), by replacing \(\alpha,Y\) with their inverses.
Thus \(A\iota(A)=\delta^{-1}\).
For odd \(v\), \(A\alpha^{(1-v)/2}\) is an actual fixed unit with positive distinguished value, and its square is \(\delta^{-1}\).
It exceeds one there and has modulus less than one at all other trace embeddings, since the corresponding \(\delta_i\) exceed one.
For even \(v\), squaring \(\alpha^{v/2}+\alpha^{-v/2}\) gives \((Y+1)^2/Y=(t+2)/\delta\).
The reciprocal lift gives \(S(-1)=N_B(t+2)\), and v877 supplies \(S(-1)=1\) precisely in this even case.
Hence the trace square root, already integral, is a unit of \(B\), proving equality of the two classes in its full unit group.
Together the tests rule out both parities only when the necessary conditions for each have failed.

For an odd prime \(\ell\nmid L\), the matrix of the fractional linear map \(X\mapsto(pX-q)/(p-qX)\) has determinant \(L\), and the Cayley map is invertible because two is a unit.
Direct division gives \((Y-1)/(Y+1)=L(X-1)/(X+1)\).
For \(v=\ell^a\), the power map on Cayley coordinates is the \(a\)-th power of residue Frobenius.
The homogeneous equality therefore has polynomial \(Z_0Z_1(Z_0^{v-1}-LZ_1^{v-1})\), up to nonzero scalar.
The axes are simple, and every remaining root is simple because \(\ell\nmid v-1\) and \(L\ne0\) in the residue field.
This is a projective computation, including the primes dividing \(q\) or \(p\) as long as \(\ell\nmid L\).
The integral primitive factorization includes \(X-1,X+1\) for odd \(v\), each distinct from the nontorsion unit minimum \(S\).
Their homogeneous factors reduce to the two separate axes, and separability of the entire homogeneous parent forces the reduced \(S\) to be disjoint from both and squarefree.
The leading coefficient and constant coefficient of \(S\) remain units.
Lifting its simple roots over an unramified local extension whose residue field contains them shows that the full splitting field of \(S\) is unramified at \(\ell\).
This justifies the action of arithmetic Frobenius on the actual roots and applies at each place above the test prime, with conjugate permutations for different choices of place.

Inversion sends \(Z\) to \(-Z\), so \(Z^2\) separates the non-axis inverse pairs in odd characteristic.
Frobenius to the \(a\)-th power sends every such label to \(L^2 Z^2\).
Since the labels are nonzero and finite, every cycle of this powered permutation has length exactly \(h=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(L^2)\).
This gives \(f/\gcd(f,a)=h\) on each original Frobenius cycle, and for any prime \(r\mid h\) it gives \(v_r(f)=v_r(a)+v_r(h)\).
The resulting divisor \(h_a\) divides each \(f\) and their sum \(n\), also when \(h=1\).
The strict numerical upper bound on \(n\) permits the stated weak exclusion inequality.

For \(q=3,\ v=223\), \(7^{37}=1\bmod223\), neither 7 nor 49 is one modulo 223, and 37 is prime, so both orders equal 37.
Direct integer comparison gives \(4^8<12\cdot3^8\), hence \(n<223/8<37\).
The weaker coefficient-prime norm test gives \(37\le2n\) under its degree condition \(2n>2\), which holds for a candidate here because a real quadratic reciprocal unit would have integer trace strictly between 2 and \(4/3+3/4<3\).
Independently the parent reduces modulo 7 to \(3(X+1)(X^{223}-1)\).
Outside the two endpoints the roots have order 223, and no power of 7 equals \(-1\bmod223\) because 7 has odd order there.
Thus the 111 inverse pairs in the residual parent have three cycles of length 37 at this second prime as well.
As an additional finite check I counted exact Frobenius cycles on all non-axis labels for \(3\le q\le20,\ 1\le a\le4,\ \ell\in\{3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23\}\) with \(\ell\nmid L\), giving 500 cases.
For \(N=(\ell^a-1)/2\), these labels satisfy \(W^N=L\); counting its solutions over each \(\mathbb F_{\ell^f}\) by the cyclic group order and subtracting proper-divisor contributions reproduced the claimed cycle equation throughout.
The exclusions concern the necessary conditions on the primitive factor and transfer to every common dilation by the initial powering argument.
