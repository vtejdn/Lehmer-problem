## The common three positions give a power of degree at most six

At 2026-11-24T18:34 an agent outside the development of the proof independently checked the full fixed claim for Yin.
The reviewer received the text and assignment without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on this result.

Reflecting the chosen template if needed and translating its first element to zero gives \(\{0,u,v\}\) with \(0<u<v\).
The two row translations supply separate monomial factors.
Removing the first from the graph leaves \(P(X^a,X^b)+X^t Q(X^a,X^b)\), with \(a=pu,\ b=pv,\ t\equiv1\bmod p\).
The second row uses either positive or negative template exponents.
Omitted vertices require no change to this representation.
At both inverse points the ordinary graph equation forces simultaneous row vanishing if either row vanishes, contradicting coprimality.
Consequently \(R=-P/Q\) is defined and nonzero there.
The double zero of \(R(X^a,X^b)-X^t\) gives \(\partial R/R=t\) at each point for \(\partial=aZ\partial_Z+bW\partial_W\).
Pullback by inversion reverses the sign of this derivation, so at \(y=(\alpha^a,\alpha^b)\) one has \(RR^\iota=1\) and \(\partial(RR^\iota)=0\).
Multiplying by \(QQ^\iota\) gives both vanishings for \(\Delta\).
In either template orientation the cleared monomial translations cancel in each row's product with its inverse.
Thus the nonzero original reflected determinant remains nonzero after specialization, including when the two horizontal exponents happen to create collisions between monomials.

For either orientation the possible nonconstant exponent differences form the same six primitive vectors.
Directly, two distinct nonopposite vectors in this list have primitive difference.
Opposite outer points cannot bound an edge of a two-dimensional centrally symmetric polygon, since their segment crosses the interior at the origin.
Every edge in dimension two thus has lattice length one.
If a nonunit irreducible factor in the Laurent ring occurred twice, either an edge of its polygon or its entire nontrivial segment would give a face of the product of length at least two.
This follows by adding the exposed faces in a common perpendicular direction.
It rules out all repeated factors over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).

For a common irreducible Laurent divisor \(J\) of \(\Delta,\partial\Delta\), squarefreeness and the product rule give \(J\mid\partial J\).
When \(\partial J\ne0\), its Newton polygon lies inside that of \(J\).
The quotient polygon must be \(\{0\}\), since a translate of a bounded polygon by any nonzero vector cannot lie inside that same polygon.
Thus \(\partial J\) is a scalar multiple of \(J\), including the zero-derivative case.
All terms of \(J\) have equal \((a,b)\)-weight, and its specialization is a single Laurent monomial times the sum of its coefficients.
If it vanished at \(y\), it would vanish on the entire specialized subgroup and force the original determinant to be zero.
Hence no common component passes through \(y\).
In dimension one the support is one opposite pair with a possible constant.
Writing its primitive direction as \(T\), the derivative is \(cw(T-T^{-1})\), where \(w\) is its weight.
A zero weight would give a constant specialization, and a nonzero weight would force \(\alpha^{2w}=1\).
Both contradict the hypotheses.
Dimension zero gives a nonzero constant and is also impossible.

In the remaining dimension-two case \(\partial\Delta\ne0\), since weight-zero exponents lie on a single line.
After multiplication by \(ZW\) and homogenization with a third coordinate \(S\), both cubics are supported on \(ZWS,Z^2W,W^2Z,ZS^2,WS^2,Z^2S,W^2S\).
Consequently the three distinct coordinate vertices are base points.
If the cubics share no component, their total intersection multiplicity is nine and the vertices consume at least three, leaving at most six torus points.
Otherwise their homogeneous gcd may be taken over \(\mathbb Q\), since the common geometric factors with multiplicities are invariant under Galois up to their scalar normalization.
None contains \(y\), either by the torus argument or because it lies at the projective boundary.
After division the coprime residual forms have degrees at most two, leaving at most four intersection points, with the same conclusion for a constant residual.
All \(\mathbb Q\)-conjugates of \(y\) remain isolated torus points, so the orbit count gives \([\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\le6\).

Evaluating the rational function \(R\) at \(y\) gives \(\alpha^t\in\mathbb Q(y)\).
Bézout for \(a,b,t\) and the reverse inclusions from their common divisor give \(\mathbb Q(y)=\mathbb Q(\alpha^{g_0})\).
With both rows fully occupied, \(a,b\) occur as differences in the first row and \(t\) as the difference of the two template origins, giving the exact support gcd in both orientations.
The relative norm of \(\alpha\) into its power field gives a nontorsion unit of degree at most six and total logarithmic measure at most \(m(\alpha)\), including the unchanged-field case where one may use \(\alpha\) itself.
The house theorem at statement scope therefore gives \(m(\alpha)\ge(\log2)/24\).

For the individual row-height bound, coprimality and the original value equation give \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), so the powered minimum has total log measure \(p\,m(\alpha)\).
The original nonzero reflected determinant has a double zero at \(\alpha^p\) by the tangency and the nonzero derivative of \(X^p\) at \(\alpha\).
Its integral Laurent polynomial therefore contains the square of that monic minimum, and its modulus on the circle is bounded by \(H^2\).
Taking logarithmic measures gives \(2p\,m(\alpha)\le2\log H\) for every integer \(p\ge2\), including \(p=2\) and composite exponents.
The conclusion covers subsets of a common translated or reflected triple and imposes no height bound on its coefficients.

For the general two-variable rows, inversion of the parameter has nonzero derivative at \(\alpha\), and the two order-\(r\) congruences give \(R(X^a,X^b)R(X^{-a},X^{-b})\equiv1\bmod (X-\alpha)^r\) locally.
All denominators here are units at the points where they are used.
Multiplication by \(QQ^\iota\) and the identity between the parameter Euler derivative and the specialized \(\partial\) yield \(\partial^j\Delta(y)=0\) for \(0\le j<r\).
The earlier weight argument excludes \(J\mid\partial J\) for each geometric irreducible factor \(J\) through \(y\), with arbitrary signed horizontal weights.
If both horizontal weights were zero, the specialized determinant would be constant, contradicting its nonzero specialization and its zero at \(y\).
Writing \(\Delta=J^s E\) with \(J\nmid E\), the only terms in the \(s\)-fold product rule surviving modulo \(J\) differentiate each of the \(s\) copies once.
The resulting \(s!(\partial J)^s E\) is nonzero modulo \(J\) in characteristic zero.
Hence, when every such \(s<r\), each factor through \(y\) defines a proper rational linear subspace of coefficient choices for \(L=\sum_{j<r}c_j\partial^j\Delta\).
Being a proper subspace follows even for a nonrational \(J\), since the rational coefficient vector selecting the \(s\)-th derivative lies outside it.
The finitely many subspaces cannot cover \(\mathbb Q^r\).
The resulting rational pair \(\Delta,L\) has \(y\) and all its conjugates as isolated common zeros.

The operator \(\partial\) preserves each Laurent monomial up to scalar, so \(N(L)\subseteq N(\Delta)=N\).
After removal of the geometric gcd, the residual Newton polygons are Minkowski summands of the originals up to translation.
Mixed area is translation-invariant, nonnegative, additive under Minkowski sum and monotone under inclusion.
Thus the torus intersection bound for the two residual coprime equations gives at most \(B(N,N)=\operatorname{area}(2N)-2\operatorname{area}(N)=2\operatorname{area}(N)\) isolated points.
This includes zero area, where there could be no isolated point in this alternative.
The value equation and Bézout give the asserted field equality for all signed \(a,b,t\) with positive gcd.
For a squarefree determinant of positive area \(A\), the first alternative is impossible and norm descent gives \(m(\alpha)\ge(\log2)/(8A)\).
The mixed-area intersection bound is used at the stated theorem scope.
