## The reciprocal gcd and the cofactor bound

The whole proof establishes \(p\,m(G)\le m(H)\le2m(F)\) when \(H\ne0\).
The coprime row hypothesis and \((p,r)=1\) enter at distinct steps.
The latter permutes the \(p\) phases in each fiber, so its exact product has modulus at least \(\big||U|^p-|V|^p\big|\).
Writing the larger squared modulus as the smaller one plus their difference and using \(p/2\ge1\) gives the lower bound \(|H|^{p/2}\).
Integration of the fiber sum counts each angle with total weight \(p\), and each nonzero Laurent polynomial has at most integrable logarithmic singularities on the circle.

For every nonzero \(z\), the Laurent gcd counts the minimum of the multiplicities of \(z\) and \(z^{-1}\) in \(F\).
The displayed elimination identity cancels the cross terms exactly.
Over a fiber where \(F\) vanishes both row values must be nonzero by coprimality, so their ratio prescribes \(x^r\) and selects just one point of the fiber.
At that point the derivative of \(x^p\) is nonzero.
Thus the order of \(H\) at the image bounds the entire gcd multiplicity at the preimage, with no competing root above that image.

For \(G\) of leading coefficient \(c\), the resultant of \(G(y)\) and \(X-y^p\) is \(c^p\prod_j(X-z_j^p)\), with integral coefficients.
After substituting \(X=x^p\) it is, up to sign, the product of \(G(\zeta x)\) over the \(p\)-th roots of unity.
Modulo a prime ideal above any rational prime, primitivity of \(G\) and invertibility of each \(\zeta\) keep every factor nonzero in the residue polynomial ring.
This proves primitivity of the resultant even at primes dividing \(c\) or \(p\), where some twists can coincide after reduction.
Its root formula gives measure \(p\,m(G)\) on the logarithmic scale, including the contribution \(p\log c\).
Gauss's lemma after clearing Laurent shifts gives an integral quotient of \(H\) by this resultant, so the quotient has nonnegative log measure.
The constant primitive gcd gives the same conclusion.

If \(H=0\), coprimality and reversal give \(V\mid\iota U\) and \(\iota U\mid V\).
The resulting Laurent unit has coefficient \(\pm1\) by the norm identity, and reversal interchanges the summands of \(F\) after a monomial shift.
Conversely the exponent reflection of a reciprocal \(F\) can fix both residue classes only if \(p\mid2r\), hence only at \(p=2\).
When it interchanges them, comparison gives \(V(X)=\pm X^a U(1/X)\).
At \(p=2\), the reciprocal \(F=2x^2+5x+2\) with \(r=1\) and coprime rows \(2(X+1),5\) gives \(H=4X+4/X-17\ne0\).
Here \(G=F\), \(G_{[2]}=4X^2-17X+4\), and the endpoint inequality is an equality.

Finally \(P\mid G\) contributes at least \(m(P)\) to \(m(G)\), since \(G/P\) is integral.
After removal of the scalar content, factoring \(F/P\) with multiplicities loses only factors of zero log measure when retaining \(R\).
This proves the pointwise bound for every admissible pair and its implication from strict violation to row symmetry, without an asymptotic restriction or a bound on coefficients or degree.
The [fixed-input limit](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement) and [two-row phase estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#two) give context at their stated hypotheses.
Neither comparison supplies a premise of the finite inequality.
