## The squarefree recursion, prime integral and auxiliary-height implication

At 2026-11-24T22:58 UTC an agent outside the development of v71 independently reviewed the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v71.md#statement) and all three proof sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and requested scope without its development dialogue or previous findings on v71.
The local multiplicity bound, the degree fractions and the asymptotic constant hold as stated.
The positive-real height conclusion requires the separate existence of input-avoiding auxiliaries at the specified degree and norm.

**Norms and recursion.**
For \(m=ps\) squarefree, \(p\nmid s\), evaluating \(\Phi_s(X^p)/\Phi_s(X)\) at each primitive \(s\)-th root gives \(p\eta^{p-1}\Phi_s'(\eta^p)/\Phi_s'(\eta)\).
Multiplication over these roots cancels the absolute values of the derivative ratios, also for \(s=1\).
The absolute resultant is therefore \(p^{\varphi(s)}\), giving the first part of (4) since \(\varphi(m)=(p-1)\varphi(s)\).
For the derivative of \(\Phi_m\), the factor \(X^m-1\) has a simple zero at a primitive root and the other Möbius factors are nonzero.
For each proper divisor \(d\mid m\), powering to \(d\) distributes the primitive roots equally over the primitive roots of order \(m/d\).
The value at one of the corresponding cyclotomic polynomial contributes only for prime \(m/d\), then with Möbius sign \(-1\).
This gives the second part of (4).
In \(F/\Phi_m^{b_m}\) the lower-order cyclotomic factors are distinct and the remaining factor has nonzero integral norm.
Applying the derivative bound over all primitive roots gives (5), including \(b_m=0\).

Multiplication of (5) by \(m/(a\log L)\) turns the lower-order contributions into \((p/(p-1))\theta_p u_{m/p}\) and the circle-maximum term into \(\kappa_L x_m\).
The binomial bound leaves precisely \(u_m+u_m(1+E_m-\log u_m)/\log L\) on the upper side.
The extra numerator has maximum \(e^{E_m}\) at \(u_m=e^{E_m}\), with the continuous zero convention.
This proves the error in (6).
At fixed \(k,V,\epsilon\), all participating primes are at least \(L^\epsilon\), all \(\theta_p\le1+o(1)\), and \(E_m=o(1)\) uniformly.
Every proper divisor has \(x_s\le V L^{-\epsilon}\), so the induction loses \(o(1)\) at each of its fixed number of steps when \(\kappa_L\) stays bounded.
Summing the \(j\) lower-divisor contributions multiplies \((j-1)!\) by \(j\), with \(u_1=1\).
Using nonnegativity only after the estimate gives the positive part in (3).

**Counting conductors.**
On a compact box with \(s\le x\le t\) and exponents in the simplex interior, fix \(P=p_1\cdots p_{k-1}\) and \(\theta'_k=1-\log P/\log L\).
The last prime interval has fixed endpoint ratio and lower endpoint at least a positive power of \(L\).
The ordinary prime number theorem gives its count uniformly as \((1+o(1))(t-s)L/(P\theta'_k)\), since the endpoint logarithms are \(\theta'_k\log L+O(\log\log L)\).
Excluding already selected last primes costs at most \(k-1\) per tuple against a uniformly diverging main term.
Partial summation gives \(\sum_{L^\alpha<p\le L^\beta}p^{-1}\to\log(\beta/\alpha)\) on \(0<\alpha<\beta\).
For coincident earlier primes the squared reciprocal sum tends to zero and the other reciprocal sums are bounded on the box.
These checks give (7) with the measure of ordered tuples divided by \(L\), including the point simplex for \(k=1\).
The actual final exponent differs from \(\theta'_k\) by \(o(1)\) uniformly there.

The contribution of each conductor is \(\varphi(m)b_m/D=L^{-1}(\varphi(m)/m)u_m\), with \(\varphi(m)/m\to1\) uniformly on compact interior ranges.
Dividing by \(k!\) removes all orderings, and compact symmetric cutoffs allow a lower limit without a uniform estimate at the simplex boundary.
For \(\kappa=0\) the \(k=1\) bound on a sufficiently long compact \(x\)-interval already exceeds the entire degree budget.
For \(\kappa>0\) the integral of \((A-\kappa x)_+\) is \(A^2/(2\kappa)\), with \(A=k!\prod_i\theta_i\).
After division by \(k!\prod_i\theta_i\), integrating the remaining product of the coordinates over the simplex gives \(\Gamma(2)^k/\Gamma(2k)=1/(2k-1)!\).
This proves (2).
In the positive-fraction assertion for \(k=2\) it is the product of the two growing primes that has scale \(L\log L\).
The individual exponents may be confined to compact subintervals of \((0,1)\) with the stated positive-part condition.

For each fixed finite set of \(k\)'s the degree fractions add to at most one, avoiding any interchange of an infinite sum with the initial lower limit.
Taking increasing finite sets after a subsequence realizing a finite liminf proves (1), and an infinite liminf requires no bound.
The first contributions to \(C_*\) are \(1/2,1/6,1/40,1/420\).
The term ratio is \((k+1)/(2k(2k+1))\).
Taking the first eleven terms and bounding the rest geometrically places \(C_*\) between \(0.69422240235198\) and \(0.69422240235200\).
The coefficient one-norm dominates the circle maximum, and specializing the earlier near-circle estimate to the root one gives the stated all-\(L\) comparison.

**Quantitative scope.**
If \(\Phi_q\nmid F\), (5) directly charges \(a\log q/(q-1)\) to \(\log H\).
For products of powers of \(X^j-1\) supported at \(j\le N\), a prime in \((N,2N]\) gives \(\log H/a\ge(\log N)/(2N)\).
This excludes the proposed \(O(1/L)\) bound for that family when \(N\asymp L\to\infty\).
The Fourier matrix at a primitive \(N\)-th root has orthogonal columns of squared length \(N\), giving exactly \(N^{N/2}\) for the consecutive-power Vandermonde.
The separate fixed-forbidden-order theorem allows an excess-degree-to-multiplicity ratio tending to infinity.

At a positive real conjugate \(v\), Taylor's remainder below one and reversal above one bound the normalized contribution by \(\min(1,eL|\log v|)^a\).
The other infinite places cost at most \(H\) after the degree factor is removed, and integrality gives factor one at the finite places.
Concavity of \(\log\min(1,s)\), the height budget \(2h/p\) on the selected places, and the product formula at \(F(z)\ne0\) give (8).
If the required auxiliaries exist with \(p^{-1}\le L\le A/p\), \(\log H/a\le C/L\), then \(h\le c p^2\) would imply \(\log(1/(2eAc))\le C+Ac\) for small \(c>0\).
For example \(c=e^{-(C+A+1)}/(2eA)\) contradicts this inequality for \(A\ge1,\ C\ge0\).
Squaring a nontorsion input doubles its height and preserves at least the original real fraction as positive values by counting over the same field.
No existence of the required auxiliaries, optimality of \(C_*\), or resolution of the unrestricted Mahler gap follows from the necessary multiplicities.
