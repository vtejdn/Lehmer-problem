## Which limits can follow the first different coefficient?

Near zero, convergence of integral Taylor coefficients makes equality with each specified finite geometric prefix eventual.
The damped denominator has one disk zero by the strict circle inequality, so including the endpoint permits at most one pole counted with multiplicity.
The numerator and denominator have equal modulus on the circle and hence common circle zeros with equal orders.
Taking the reciprocal of \(F_i\) on compact subsets shows that the possible pole escapes, since the limiting denominator after division by \(F_i\) is \(-2(1-z)^2\).
The normalized Blaschke factors of such poles tend to one on compact subsets, giving a Schur limit \(G\) on extraction.
The term divisible by \(z^{n+1}\) in the denominator identity cannot affect the first \(n+1\) coefficients of \(2(1-z)^2V_i\).
Their integrality passes to \(2(1-z)^2G\), whose square-summable integral coefficients have finite support.
Its radial bound at one forces divisibility by \((1-z)^2\).
The resulting integer polynomial \(2G\) has nonzero constant \(-\delta_i\) eventually, and the three-term norm bound \(\sqrt5>2\) excludes every support of size at least three.
For a binomial, one can align the two terms on the circle, forcing each coefficient to have modulus one.

Boyd's width formula gives \(4(2/4)=2\) at the stated function.
At either extreme discrepancy the next interval length tends to zero as the index grows, so for every sufficiently late such discrepancy all subsequent intervals remain shorter than one.
Thus there is at most one integral continuation for the prescribed signed prefix.
For \(p=z^n(z-2)+t\), damping the constant term counts at most one exterior root in the limit, and \(p(3/2)<0\) supplies one for large \(n\).
Every circle root is one, so the cofactor of the Pisot factor is cyclotomic.
The normalized quotient of this trinomial has the required discrepancy \(-2t\).
The unique pole consequently identifies the actual seed, and multiplying either signed delay polynomial by the cyclotomic cofactor gives a polynomial of at most six terms and the same measure.
This proves the positive gap for the two extreme discrepancies.

I checked the reversal before stripping \(z^n\), obtaining \(sU^*=(z-2)Q-zA\).
For a unit discrepancy the constant of \(\bar U\) is one, so the least exponent of \(z^D\bar U(1/z)\) is \(n+1\), giving degree \(d-2n\) and reciprocity modulo two.
The determinant of the two defining combinations is \(-2(1-z)^2\), which excludes common disk zeros and bounds the order at the only possible circle zero by two.
A positive matched measure excess supplies a disk zero of \(Q+z^2A\), at which \(1+z^{n+1}V=0\).
The maximum principle then forces the single disk pole, and \(b_{1/\theta}(x)>|x|\) excludes a negative location.
Its reciprocal is the simple exterior zero \(\beta\to1\) of \(U\).
A monic irreducible factor containing \(\beta\) would have all its other roots strictly inside the disk and give Pisot numbers approaching one.
Closedness of the Pisot set excludes this, leaving leading coefficient of absolute value two for that factor and only factors \(z-1\) for the cofactor.

## The side of two and the order of the two exterior roots

At \(r=1/\theta\) the numerator \(A(r)\) is nonzero.
Substitution in \(V\) gives \(2r-1=r^{n+1}V(r)\), whence \(n|2r-1|\to0\) on every profile subsequence and \(2^n(\theta-2)\to-R(1/2)/2\).
The possible limit magnitudes lie between \(1/4\) and \(3/4\), and their signs agree with the discrepancy.
Subsequence extraction therefore yields the asserted logarithmic scale and eventual sign on the original sequence.

Let \(\tau=1/\alpha\) be the sole disk zero of the matched delay polynomial.
The simple zero of \(Q\) preceding \(\tau\) gives \(Q(\tau)<0\), and substitution of \(F(\tau)=-\tau^{-2}\) gives \(sU^*(\tau)=Q(\tau)(1-\tau)^2/\tau<0\).
Since \(sU^*(0)=-2\), its sole simple disk zero \(b>0\) lies above \(\tau\).
Applying the Schur bound to \(b_bV\) at \(\tau\) gives \((\alpha-\beta)/(\alpha\beta-1)\le\alpha^{-n-1}\) with \(\beta=1/b<\alpha\).
Solving for \(\beta\) gives the claimed lower endpoint, strictly greater than one.
The primitive factor of \(\beta\) is nonreciprocal by disk coprimality and hence has no circle conjugate.
Smyth's bound excludes monicity as \(\beta\to1\).
Every cyclotomic multiple of this factor has leading coefficient of modulus two and measure \(2\beta\), so the gap at endpoint coefficient two forces the minimum term count to diverge.
For \(\delta=-1\), the opposite signs at zero and \(\tau\) supply a positive zero \(y<\tau\).
The cyclotomic cofactor has no interior root and each limiting profile is zero-free inside, so \(y\) is a conjugate of \(\beta\) tending to one.
Dividing \(b_bV\) by the Blaschke factor of \(y\) preserves its Schur bound and supplies the extra factor \((\tau-y)/(1-y\tau)\) in the inequality.

## Which limits occur for actual Pisot units?

For the three-term \(P\) of L462v119, the strict exterior inequality rules out exterior zeros, and equality on the circle forces \(z^k=ab\).
Consequently \(Q=cP^*\) is zero-free in the disk and \(P/Q\) has only removable circle singularities.
Writing \(Q=2+cbz^{n+1}+caz^{n+1+k}\) gives the required parity for both halves \(A,B\), and reversal at degree \(n+1+D\) gives \(B=cA^*\).
The damped denominator in (1) has precisely one disk zero for \(t<1\).
At full strength the real signs at \(1/3\) and \(2/3\) are opposite even for \(n=2\), since \((2/3)^3<1/3\).
This gives exactly one simple exterior root for \(p=cA\), and every circle root is a root of unity.
Removing the cyclotomic cofactor \(C\) leaves a Pisot unit and preserves the normalized quotient because the normalizing sign becomes \(f(0)=cC(0)\).

Substituting (1) in the centered quotient recovers \(P/Q\) exactly, with first discrepancy \(-a\).
Fixed \(k\) gives either sign at either endpoint of the binomial, while \(k\to\infty\) gives both unit constants.
Here the numerator and denominator in (1) converge locally to \(1\) and \(1-2z\), so convergence holds meromorphically including at the seed pole in the limit.
The pole identity used in the preceding finding requires only this compact convergence and gives the two stated signed scales.
For the matched delay, factoring out \((1-z)^2Q/2\) leaves \(1+z^{n+1}V\), which is zero-free inside.
Reciprocity then gives measure one, also after cyclotomic cancellation.
If the minimal polynomial had positive constant, its matched output would take opposite signs at zero and one, contradicting this zero count.
Finally the extreme trinomial quotients give \(V=\pm1\) exactly and their displayed matched delay factorizations have only circle roots.
The constructions realize the complete list in the Pisot class at matched measure one.
Existence of positive excess tending to zero at any of the profiles with unit constant term remains open.
