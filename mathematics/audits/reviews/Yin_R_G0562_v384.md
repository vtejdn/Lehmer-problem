## Independent review of the invariant factors and the class of the generator

At 2026-11-18T12:35 UTC an agent outside the proof development checked the [whole ledger argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v161.md#group) for Yin using the submitted mathematics and its cited inputs.
The reviewer received no development discussion, author explanations or previous review findings on this argument.
The decomposition and the assertion that the class of \(\alpha\) has order \(3^{p-1}\) hold for every positive \(d\) divisible by six and every prime \(p>3\) dividing \(d\).

The Salem family theorem gives simple roots of \(P\), all different from one, for every such \(d\).
Writing \(E=(x-1)P\), the identity \(E(\zeta\xi)=(\zeta-1)\xi(\xi^d+2)\) at a root \(\xi\) of \(E\) uses just \(\zeta^p=1\) and \(p\mid d\).
The factor \(\xi^d+2\) cannot vanish since \(E\) takes the value three at each root of \(x^d+2\).
Thus all \(d+1\) values \(\xi^p\) are distinct, and the powers \(1,X,\ldots,X^d\) are a basis for \(R\) by the monic integral characteristic polynomial of \(X=x^p\) in \(D\).
The index is the absolute ratio \(J\) of the Vandermonde determinants on \(\xi^p\) and \(\xi\).
The product of \(|E(\zeta\xi)|\) over \(\xi\) and \(\zeta\ne1\) equals \(p^{d+1}3^{d(p-1)}\) by the resultant with \(x^d+2\).
Factoring \(E\) instead gives \(p^{d+1}J^2\), with the diagonal supplying \(p^{d+1}\) and each off-diagonal pair contributing twice to the logarithm of \(J\).
Deleting the pairs involving one divides \(J\) by \(\prod_{\zeta\ne1}|P(\zeta)|=3^{p-1}\), since \(P(\zeta)=3\).
These calculations give both indices over the stated range without a restriction on the size of \(p\) relative to \(\log d\).

In \(D\), \((X^m+2)(x-2)=-3\) follows on expanding \(E\).
Multiplying the binomial expansion of \((Z+2)^p\) by \(B^t\) sends the terms of degree below \(t\) into \(R\), and their complement becomes \((-3)^t Y_{p-t}(Z)\).
Within each block \(X^j\), the monicity and degree \(r\) of \(Y_r(x-2)\) give a triangular change of integral basis with diagonal one.
The block bases together with the final element \(X^m\) account for all \(d+1\) powers.
After passage to \(D/R\), the surviving classes generate and each is killed by its assigned power of three, since \(X^j\) belongs to \(R\).
The proposed direct sum has order \(3^{m(1+\cdots+(p-1))}=3^{d(p-1)/2}\), the full index just computed, so the generating map has trivial kernel.

Evaluation at \(\alpha\) is surjective, and its kernel in \(D\) is \((P)=\mathbb ZP\) because \(P\) is minimal and \(xP=P\).
On additive quotients the kernel is \((R+\mathbb ZP)/R\), since \(R\) maps onto \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
The ratio of the indices gives this cyclic subgroup order \(3^{p-1}\).
Any element of that order in the direct sum must have a unit coordinate in a summand of that order, since otherwise \(3^{p-2}\) would kill it.
Projection onto that coordinate is an isomorphism on the cyclic subgroup, making all other coordinates together an additive complement.
The quotient therefore loses exactly one summand of order \(3^{p-1}\).

Finally \(Y_1(x-2)=x+2(p-1)\), so the \(m\) classes \(X^jx\) agree modulo \(R\) with the respective, distinct top-order basis generators.
Their \(3^{p-2}\)-multiples span an \(m\)-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_3\).
If \(3^{p-2}\alpha\in\mathbb Z[\beta]\), then multiplication by each \(\beta^j\) puts that entire vector space in the cyclic kernel, whose subgroup killed by three has dimension one.
Here \(6\mid m\), which excludes this possibility.
The class of \(\alpha\) therefore has the full exponent for every \(m\) in the statement, and \((\log s)/p=(1-1/p)\log 3\) tends to \(\log3\) as \(p\) grows.
The application to the resultant concerns its upper bound in terms of \(s\).
