### Unit input, normalization and reciprocal negative sets

For the v7 unit reduction, the extreme large-root counts would make alpha an integer greater than one, which cannot be an algebraic unit.
At the remaining counts the absolute norm of alpha is a product of positive integer powers of the absolute leading and constant coefficients of beta's primitive polynomial.
That norm is one, so both coefficient moduli are one and every allowed beta is a unit.
Inversion preserves its degree, field, Mahler measure and raw square norm.
The all-place N1 convention consequently reduces to the stated actual-embedding sum for both beta and gamma, with complex places counted twice and no finite-place term omitted for an unproved unit.
The positive and negative logarithmic masses are exactly m and -m, with both counts nonzero.

The rational sign in the exterior-root product is fixed by every Galois automorphism.
An automorphism taking alpha to alpha^(-1) therefore takes the positive-root subset of size p to a subset of logarithmic sum -m.
A subset has that minimum sum only if it contains every negative entry and no positive entry.
It may contain zero entries, so this first yields p>=q.
Applying the same argument to the inverse realization gives q>=p, without assuming reciprocal conjugacy for beta or invoking its chosen orientation again.
Thus p=q, and no zero entry fits into the original minimizing p-element subset.
This proves Delta_(alpha^(-1))=Delta_- exactly.
Transport under an automorphism taking alpha to a proves the disjointness of Delta_a and Delta_(a^(-1)) for every a.
The incidence identity nr=dp and nonzero p then give the full 1<=r<=t domain, including possible neutral roots of beta.

### Actual atom products, stabilizers and field equality

In v7, the exterior-root subset uniquely maximizes absolute product among subsets of its cardinality, which makes Delta_a well defined.
The equivariant membership fibers are the coarsest compatible Galois block partition.
If two atom products coincided, grouping atoms by their product would give a strictly coarser invariant partition still compatible with the exterior subset: product modulus greater than one occurs exactly in the exterior atoms.
This is impossible.
Hence the b products are distinct, and b=n/e is the actual endpoint degree.
The atom containing beta_tilde is the orbit under its set stabilizer, so its product is the ordinary relative norm to Q(gamma), of extension degree e.
The stabilizers of beta_tilde and of L_alpha respectively give gamma in Q(beta) and gamma in L_alpha.
The atomwise same-sign positive-part identity and Cauchy inequality give M(gamma)=alpha and M_2(gamma)<=M_2(beta).

For the chosen atom, preserving its membership signature S is equivalent to preserving that atom.
Distinct atom products make this also equivalent to fixing gamma.
The subgroup Gal(F/L_alpha) lies in all these stabilizers, so passing to G_alpha preserves the equality, and b is exactly |G_alpha.S|.
The polynomial product_(u in S)(T-u) has distinct roots; an automorphism fixes all its coefficients exactly when it preserves S.
Galois correspondence therefore makes the coefficient field exactly Q(gamma), while the pointwise-root stabilizer gives Q(gamma) subset Q(u:u in S).
This proves the asserted input-dependent rational-function representation over Q.
A denominator nonzero at the selected tuple stays nonzero at every Galois-conjugate tuple, so that representation introduces no undefined orbit value.
No monomial identity, fixed function complexity or globally basal property follows or is used.

### Exact radial counts and neutral-orbit interface

If a signature contains alpha, every root in its atom has strictly positive logarithmic modulus; if it contains alpha^(-1), every root has strictly negative logarithmic modulus.
If it contains neither, every root has logarithmic modulus zero.
Thus the atom product is off the circle exactly for the first two cases, with the asserted signs.
For each of the r selected original roots the uniform G_alpha probability of mapping to alpha is 1/d, and likewise for alpha^(-1).
These events are disjoint among selected roots, because they belong to distinct inverse pairs and automorphisms preserve inverse-pair distinction.
Every embedding of Q(gamma) has the same number of extensions to L_alpha.
Consequently the positive and negative counts are each rb/d and k_gamma/b=2r/d at the actual degree b.

Nontorsion follows from M(gamma)=alpha>1.

### Both orbit bounds, including full support

For each inverse pair i, a conjugate of ordinary complex conjugation fixes that pair pointwise and flips every other pair.
Its vector is 1+e_i, and the product of the conjugations for i and j flips exactly those two pairs.
Thus all even flips are actual elements of the inverse-pair kernel, independently of any full symmetric top group or split-extension assertion.
When r<t, every orientation on the selected support is realized by completing a prescribed flip with one unused pair if its parity is odd.
This gives 2^r signatures on each support.
The actual orbit of an r-element support has union all t pairs by transitivity, so it has at least t/r members.
Combining these counts gives b>=2^r t/r.
When r=t the even flips instead give 2^(t-1) distinct signatures, which handles the branch where no unused pair exists.
Both branches imply b>=2^(r-1), including t=2 and r=1 or r=t.
The universal upper count is exactly the number of all oriented r-transversals, 2^r binom(t,r), and its binomial sum is 3^t.
These counts concern the signature orbit whose size is b, rather than merely an ambient field order.

### Finite nonempty class and its exact minimum

Every endpoint polynomial has degree at most H_t and satisfies the irreducibility, monicity, constant-unit, splitting-field and exact-Mahler filters defining T_alpha.
A genuine Salem alpha realizes its own measure, so the endpoint construction also proves nonemptiness.
For each s<=t, choosing one element from each of s distinct inverse pairs gives an injection into all s-subsets of the 2t roots.
Hence 2^s binom(t,s)<=binom(2t,s)<=binom(2t,t), and the separate binomial-sum bound gives H_t<=3^t.
Every product of a subset of roots of a monic polynomial of measure alpha has modulus at most alpha.
The coefficient bound therefore follows by the triangle inequality, and bounded degree plus integral bounded coefficients makes T_alpha finite.

Let its minimum raw value be A, which is well defined and independent of the chosen root of each irreducible polynomial.
Every original beta has an endpoint in T_alpha of no greater raw value, so A<=M_2(beta) for every beta and A is at most the original infimum.
Every root from T_alpha is itself an allowed realization, giving the reverse inequality.
This proves S6/IF10a without an algorithm for comparing squared logarithmic sums.
Applying the endpoint construction to a minimizing realization cannot strictly lower the attained infimum, so a minimizer with the stated signature and field properties exists.
This remains a fixed-alpha statement and makes no basal or degree-at-most-d assertion.

### Raw norm and the exact degree costs

The unit product formula gives sum_j |log|gamma_j||=2m.
Cauchy on the k_gamma nonzero terms therefore yields M_2(gamma)>=2m sqrt(b/k_gamma)=m sqrt(2d/r).

The relative hypothesis M_2(beta)<=C m cancels positive m and gives S9/IF13 without any height theorem.
Since r<=d/2, it also gives C>=2 as a necessary condition; equality C=2 is not excluded or supplied.

For the original Salem alpha, its algebraic-integrality, nontorsion and actual-degree assumptions meet the accepted numerical input.

The use is at original degree d, not output degree b, and it needs no small-height assumption from C147's different application.

The v3 BA8 comparison separately treats d/r<exp(e) before applying its output-degree bound in the complementary branch; this weaker eventual comparison is compatible with the present hypotheses and is not needed for IF12--IF14.
