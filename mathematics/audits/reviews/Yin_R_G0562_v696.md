## Degree, multiplicity and the complementary factors

At 13:13 UTC on November 20, 2026, a separate agent reviewing for Sheng Yin independently checked version 559 without participating in its development.
The degree and multiplicity conclusions hold for all four sign choices, and the stated strict inequality gives the full rational factorization.

In characteristic three the two factors are \(X^k-s\) and \((X-r)^L\), with the first separable since \(3\nmid k\).
When \(r^k=s\), direct evaluation gives \(P(r)=6\sigma\).
When \(r^k=-s\), direct differentiation gives \(rP'(r)=\sigma(L-3k)\), of valuation one because \(9\mid L\) and \(3\nmid k\).
Removing the simple linear root in the latter case leaves value \(P'(r)\) at \(r\).
In each case the complementary Hensel factor is a unit at \(r\), so translation gives the degree-\(e\) factor constant valuation one and reduction \(X^e\).
Eisenstein irreducibility and the coprime complementary reduction give a single occurrence in the rational factorization as well.
A root of unity in this cluster has prime-to-three part equal to \(r\), by injectivity of reduction on prime-to-three torsion.
Its degree over \(\mathbb Q_3\) is consequently one or twice a power of three, whereas \(e=L\pm1\) is prime to three and at least eight.

Any other monic rational irreducible factor except the removed linear factor divides the local complement and hence reduces to a divisor of \(X^k-s\).
For its root \(\alpha\), ordinary division by the monic minimal polynomial gives \(\alpha^k-s\in3\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
A nonzero norm then has absolute value at least \(3^d\), while the product of \(2\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^k\) over the conjugates gives the opposing bound \(2^d M(S)^k\).
The four distinct terms, coefficient length six and unit endpoints satisfy version 250's hypotheses for the upper bound \(D\log M(S)\le 3d\log 6\).
Combining the two bounds gives the required strict threshold, and \((3/2)^{14}>6^3\) certifies the integer sufficient condition.

For torsion roots of the complement the same norm comparison forces \(\alpha^k=s\), and substitution gives \(3s\alpha^L+3\sigma=0\).
Since \(\gcd(k,L)=1\), the squared power identities give \(\alpha^2=1\), and odd \(L\) gives \(\alpha=-r\).
This root appears exactly when \((-r)^k=s\) and reduces away from the Hensel cluster, where squarefree reduction ensures simplicity.
For odd \(k\) the two linear roots occur together exactly when \(\sigma=-1\).
For even \(k\), the root \(-r\) occurs for \(s=1\) and \(r\) occurs for \(s=-1\), giving the single root \(-\sigma\) in either case.
The proposed odd exponents are prime to three for large \(a\), tend to infinity and satisfy \(k/L\to0\), so both parents with \(\sigma=1\) are then irreducible.
This review used the upper bound of version 250 and the standard local factorization and torsion results at their stated scopes.
