## Independent review of the matched power traces and all sixteen polynomial gcds

At 2026-11-21T00:08 (UTC), an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the entire submitted C345 v695 and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer did not develop v695 and inspected the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on v695.
The polynomial table was recomputed in exact rational arithmetic from Newton sums.

At delay seven and positive sign the two linear factors at \(1,-1\) both occur and exhaust the torsion cofactor for every integer \(a\ge3\).
Multiplying \(X^3 f_a(X+X^{-1})\) by \(X^2-1\) recovers \(X^8-aX^7+aX-1\).
For a positive field radical \(\beta=r^{1/q}\), its \(q\)-th power generates the same degree-six field.
The powers of the six conjugates therefore give distinct images, with one exterior image and four neutral images.
The inverse of a neutral conjugate is its complex conjugate, forcing reciprocity of the irreducible polynomial of \(\beta\).
The three inverse-pair traces are distinct, are permuted by Galois, and their transforms under \(D_q\) give exactly the roots of \(f_a\).

For \(q=2\), the six roots of \(f_a(y^2-2)\) are the three radical traces and their negatives with multiplicities, giving the asserted monic factorization.
At \(y=1\), the product \(g(1)g(-1)=-1\) forces half-sum \(B+D=0\) and half-difference \(1+C=\pm1\).
The constant coefficient of the composition is \(-4-3a\), while the coefficient of \(y^4\) is \(-6-a\).
The factored expressions give \(-D^2\) and \(2C-B^2\) at those same powers.
Eliminating \(B,D\) yields \(a=1+C\), outside the stated seed range.

For every \(q\ge2\) coprime to six, the cosine formula fixes \(s=1,-1\) and gives \(|D_q'(s)|=q\).
The divisibility by the monic integral \(g\) gives two unit values there.
Their half-difference and half-sum are exactly the \(c,e\) in the four pencils.
The monic resultant has the elementary symmetric coefficients of the three transformed values, so the matched equations are \(w=-2\), \(u=-v\), \(a=-v\).
To check that their gcd is nonzero, divide the pencil by \(B\) and differentiate at \(1/B=0\) at each of the simple roots \(s\).
The resulting \(B^{-1}\) term is \(-(cs+e)/(2s)\), nonzero for all four pencils.
The third root is \(-B+O(1)\), whence the transformed sum has magnitude comparable to \(B^q\).
If both matched equations were identities, the cubic equation at \(t_s=D_q(y_s)\) would give \(t_s^2-1=t_s/(t_s+v)=O_q(B^{-q})\).
Since \(t_s\to s\), this contradicts the nonzero \(B^{-1}\) displacement obtained from \(D_q'(s)\).
The four gcds thus yield finitely many integral \(B\), and factoring \(P(X^q)\) at \(a=-v(B)\ge3\) and isolating its unique positive root greater than one decides membership by comparing the degree of its irreducible factor to six.

For an independent computation, form the reciprocal sextic \(F(X)=X^3g(X+X^{-1})\) over \(\mathbb Q[B]\), and let \(s_j\) be its \(j\)-th root power sum.
Newton's identities for the six coefficients of \(F\) compute these polynomials through \(j=39\).
For the transformed cubic roots the first three power sums are \(s_q,\ s_{2q}+6,\ s_{3q}+3s_q\), respectively.
These follow from the binomial expansions in each of the three inverse pairs.
Writing them as \(h_1,h_2,h_3\) gives
\[
  v=-h_1,\qquad w=(h_1^2-h_2)/2,\qquad
  u=-(h_1^3-3h_1h_2+2h_3)/6.
\]
Exact gcds of \(w+2\) and \(u+v\) in \(\mathbb Q[B]\) were computed with SymPy 1.14.0 for all four pencils and all four specified odd primes.
In the columns \((1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)\) every monic gcd was one.
In the \((-1,0)\) column the gcds in order \(q=5,7,11,13\) were \(B(B^2-1), B^2, B(B^2-1), B(B^2-1)^2\), including the displayed multiplicities.
These computations agree with the submitted recurrence since \(D_q\bmod g\) evaluates to \(D_q\) at every root of \(g\).
For every surviving \(B\in\{-1,0,1\}\), the signs of \(g\) at \(-2,-1,1,2\) are \(-,+,-,+\).
The three intervening intervals therefore contain all of its roots, excluding the required trace greater than two.
The positive-unit substitution criterion applies to every \(m\) with prime divisors in the stated five-element set, including \(m=1\).
The decision procedure at other exponents coprime to six requires the indicated finite candidate tests.
