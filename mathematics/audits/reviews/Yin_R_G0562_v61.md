## Common-power classes, commensurable radii and the equal-radius unit

For any positive number \(e\) of exterior roots, transitivity makes the torsion-quotient classes equicardinal and they have constant modulus individually.
Thus their size \(f\) divides \(e\).
With \(N\) killing all within-class quotients, every representative \(\xi\in C\) satisfies \(v_C^N=\xi^{fN}\).
A torsion quotient between two class products would therefore give a torsion quotient between their representatives, so it identifies the same class.
Taking one product per class consequently gives \(2m/f\) distinct algebraic units in a single Galois orbit stable under inversion.
None of those units is torsion since the orbit contains an exterior product.
The class-product polynomial is monic, irreducible and reciprocal, and has \(e/f\) exterior roots.
Multiplying their moduli uses every original exterior modulus once, preserving \(M(P)\).
This proves the single-step descent whenever a torsion collision occurs anywhere in the root set, including among neutral roots.

For distinct commensurable radii let \(a>b>0\) be coprime as in the statement, so \(\alpha^a=\beta^b\) for \(\alpha=R^2,\ \beta=S^2\).
The signed log sums after a field automorphism satisfy \(aF=bG\), so the sum \(G\) of the weights at the image of the large pair is divisible by \(a\).
A single small weight \(\pm b\) together with \(0\) or \(\pm a\) cannot give zero modulo \(a\).
Thus an image of the large pair meeting the small pair is the entire small pair.
This implication also holds for \(a=2,b=1\), regardless of the relative signs of the two small weights.
If two translates share a label, transitivity sends that label to a small one, forcing both translates to be the small pair in that embedding.
The translates therefore give a partition into pairs that includes both \(A\) and \(B\).

An automorphism sending \(A\) to \(B\) cannot map the two outward choices to equal signs, since \(|F|=2a\) would force \(|G|=2a^2/b>2a\).
Consequently any conjugate of \(\alpha\) on \(B\) or a neutral block has modulus one.
On \(A\) the only exterior and interior values are exactly \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\).
The norm of the unit forces the latter value to occur.
Likewise any exterior value of a conjugate of \(\beta\) must have modulus \(S^2\) by the power identity, forcing the two outward large roots themselves and giving exactly \(\beta\).
The inward case gives \(\beta^{-1}\).
An automorphism therefore fixes \(\alpha\) precisely when it fixes \(\beta\), proving equality of their fields.

Fix \(h\) sending \(A\) to \(B\).
If an element stabilizing \(A\) reverses just one of its two outward choices, composing with \(h\) reverses exactly one sign in the opposite-sign image on \(B\).
The excluded equal-sign case results even if the stabilizer permutes the labels of \(A\).
Thus at most two distinct values of \(\alpha\) occur per block, giving degree at most \(2(m/2)=m\).
Bézout coefficients \(bu+av=1\) give the positive unit \(\gamma=\alpha^u\beta^v=\alpha^{1/b}>1\) and \(\gamma^a=\beta\).
It generates the common field.
An exterior conjugate must have \(b\)-th and \(a\)-th powers equal to \(\alpha,\beta\), respectively, so their Bézout combination gives \(\gamma\) uniquely.
Any interior conjugate is likewise \(\gamma^{-1}\), and the unit norm forces one to occur.
The reciprocal minimal polynomial is quadratic or has a unit-circle conjugate, giving exactly the alternative claimed.
In particular \(M(\gamma)=\gamma<\gamma^{a+b}=M(P)\) at degree at most \(m\), as needed for the degree-bounded minimum assertion.

For equal radii, \(\alpha=R^2\) has two product representations on disjoint pairs, and every conjugate \(c\) inherits both.
Its possible moduli are \(R^2,R,1,R^{-1},R^{-2}\).
If \(|c|=R^2\), its two products exhaust the four exterior roots, giving \(c^2=R^4\).
Every conjugate of \(\theta=\alpha^2\) is such a square or has smaller modulus, proving strict dominance among distinct conjugates.
If \(|c|=R\), write \(c=xu=yv\) on two distinct exterior roots \(x,y\) and two neutral roots \(u,v\).
Fix an ordering of \(\{x,y\}\).
The torsion-collision exclusion makes \(y/x\) a nontorsion algebraic unit, so Kronecker's theorem and the unit norm give one embedding \(g\) with \(|g(y/x)|>1\).
This one \(g\) applies to every solution for the fixed ordered pair.
Then \(|g(u)|>|g(v)|\), so \(g(u)\) is one of the four exterior roots or \(g(v)\) one of the four interior roots.
Specifying either value determines the other from the fixed conjugate quotient, then determines \(c\).
Hence there are at most \(8\binom42=48\) such values, allowing for double counting and for coincident squares in this upper bound.
Thus \(\theta\) has at most \(49\) exterior conjugates and \(M(\theta)\le R^4(R^2)^{48}=M(P)^{25}\).
