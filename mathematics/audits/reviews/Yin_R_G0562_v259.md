### Support, multiplicities and norms

Normalizing a unit exponent modulo \(3q\) to be one modulo three gives a unique representative up to inversion of the original root and identifies the real cyclotomic Galois group with \((\mathbb Z/q)^\times\).
The binary Frobenius has normalized exponent \(-2\), so the trace orbit size is \(f\), with two inverse roots for each trace.
The primes over two in \(L\) thus label the \(I\) primitive trace orbits.
The primitive traces retain distinct residue values for distinct inverse pairs, and none coincides with the order-three trace, since all the odd orders survive reduction.

Monicity and reciprocity give integral trace factors of \(W\) for the selected polynomial and its complement.
At the primitive trace, \(W(\tau)=-2\zeta^{-l}(1+\zeta^{2l})\).
The latter parenthesis is an algebraic integer of absolute norm one because squaring permutes the primitive roots of the odd order of \(\zeta^{2l}\ne1\).
This uses only \(0<l<q\).
The two trace factors have disjoint residual roots, and two is unramified in \(L\), so the selected factor picks up precisely valuation one at its labeled primes and zero at all other finite primes.
In a subfield \(K\) with complete splitting at two, the relative residue degree of each labeled prime is \(f\).
Taking the norm thereby gives exactly the powers in (1), independently of any order-three selection.
In the quadratic case, even \(I\) puts the decomposition group inside the squares, and the classes of the two primes above two are inverse.
The standard quadratic Gauss sum generates the real quadratic field of discriminant \(q\) for \(q\equiv1\bmod4\), and its product with \(\sqrt{-3}\) gives the real quadratic field of discriminant \(3q\) for \(q\equiv3\bmod4\).
This agrees with the two cosets and yields (2).

### The possible generator residues

Inertia at \(q\) maps onto the full real Galois group, so conjugation acts trivially on the unique residue field \(\mathbb F_q\) in \(L\) and in each \(K\).
The sampled trace reduces to \(-1\), giving the claimed residue of its norm, and omission of order three makes \(T(-1)\) an odd divisor of either \(2\) or \(-4\).
For a generator \(\lambda\) of the specified principal ideal, its signed rational norm is one of \(\pm2^{fM}\), where \(M=\sum n_{\mathfrak p}\).
Reducing this norm modulo \(q\) yields \(\bar\lambda^g\), so \(\bar\lambda^{2g}=1\).
When \(j=[L:K]\) is even, \(K\) lies in the real \(q\)-th cyclotomic field and the unit norms from that field realize precisely the \((j/2)\)-th powers in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\).
These form the order-\(2g\) subgroup and suffice to adjust the generator to any \(b^j\).
When \(j\) is odd, inertia at three has order two in \(K\) because the unique involution of the cyclic group survives.
The ideal of \(\lambda\) is prime to three, and each residue norm in its rational norm modulo three occurs twice, so its signed norm is \(1\bmod3\).
This forces the sign \((-1)^{fM}\), giving \(\bar\lambda^g=1\) at \(q\).
The norms from \(L\) of the displayed real cyclotomic units now cover the \(j\)-th powers, exactly the order-\(g\) subgroup.
The argument concerns adjustment of an unspecified generator of this ideal and gives no sufficiency test for a rational lift.

### The two class orders

For \(q=257\), I obtained \(f=16,\ I=16\), norm \(8\) for the displayed element and norm \(-1\) for the scaling unit.
The element of norm eight is nonzero modulo \(2\mathcal O_K\), so its ideal is supported at just one prime over two.
The bound on \(|y|\) in the norm-\(\pm2\) equation is less than \(0.52\), so neither prime is principal and each has class order three.

For \(q=5419\), the computed residues of \(3^{(q-1)/p}\) are \(5418,5291,64,4406\), and each residue minus one has gcd one with \(q\).
Together with \(3^{5418}\equiv1\), this forces order 5418 modulo every prime divisor of \(q\) and certifies primality.
I obtained \(f=21,\ I=258\), norm \(-32\) for \((127+\sqrt{16257})/2\), and norm one for the stated scaling unit.
Scaling a possible norm-\(\pm2\) generator bounds \(|y|<3.27\).
An even \(y\) would make its norm odd or divisible by four, and for \(|y|=1,3\) the four possible squares are \(16249,16265,146305,146321\), strictly between consecutive squares.
Thus the prime class has order five.
A label represents \(42\) roots and \(129\) such labels give degree \(5418\).
Their allocation to one sign violates (2), whereas the counts \(67,62\) satisfy it.
Squarefree Hensel lifting gives the local divisors, and the fifth-root filter gives exactly the reported fraction of subsets satisfying this test.
