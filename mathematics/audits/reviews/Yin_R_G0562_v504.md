### Exhausting the possible seeds

For a qualifying repeated factor \(P\), the coprime row values at \(z=\alpha^p\) recover \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\).
Thus the powered minimum \(Q\) has degree \(e=\deg P\) and logarithmic measure \(p\,m(P)\).
The square \(Q^2\) divides the nonzero reflected polynomial of degree at most \(2b\), giving \(e\le b\).
The circle bound for this polynomial gives \(p\,m(P)\le\log H\), hence \(M(P)\le2\) at the cutoff.
Nontorsion excludes fixed points of inversion on the conjugates, so \(e\) is even.
A reciprocal quadratic nontorsion unit has integral trace of absolute value at least three, making \(M(P)\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
This rules out every seed when \(b\le3\) and leaves only \(e=4\) at widths four and five.

The quadratic trace minimum \(T\) is monic integral and irreducible, since the field of a quartic reciprocal root has degree two over its inverse-fixed trace field.
For nonreal traces the two exterior roots of \(P\) are conjugates of modulus \(\rho\le\sqrt2\).
Their trace imaginary parts have absolute value at most \(\rho-\rho^{-1}\le1/\sqrt2\), whereas a negative quadratic discriminant is at most \(-3\), giving a lower bound \(\sqrt3/2\).
For real traces any exterior trace has absolute value in \((2,5/2]\).
Two exterior traces of the same sign would have squared difference at most \(1/4\), contradicting the positive integral discriminant.
With opposite signs their integral sum would be zero, and their common squared magnitude would be an integer at least five.
The expanding moduli would then coincide and the measure bound would make the squared magnitude at most \((\sqrt2+1/\sqrt2)^2=9/2\).
Irreducibility excludes the endpoints and Kronecker excludes both traces in \((-2,2)\).

Taking the single exterior trace \(t>2\) after sign change gives \(T=S^2-jS+c\), with \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\).
The function \(t(j-t)\) is decreasing for \(2<t\le5/2\) in all four cases.
The intervals for \(c\) are respectively \([-15/4,-2),[-5/4,0),[5/4,2),[15/4,4)\).
They leave just \((j,c)=(1,-3),(2,-1)\).
Both quadratics are irreducible, each has one root inside \((-2,2)\) and the other in \((2,5/2)\).
The inner real embedding makes \(t^2-4\) negative there, hence nonsquare in the real quadratic trace field, so the two lifts are irreducible quartics.
Each has only one exterior conjugate.
This singleton fiber after any positive power forces all conjugate fibers to be singletons by Galois transitivity.
For odd \(p\) the substitution \(x\mapsto-x\) sends a row pair to \((U(-Y),-V(-Y))\), preserving its cost, both multiplicity and gcd conditions, and unequal moduli.
This accounts for the omitted signs of the quartic seeds.

### The two rational lines at minimal width

For a reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) of degree \(e\) with degree-preserving power \(p\), the graph-reversal involution on the kernel follows by reversal about \(pe+1\), even for rows of smaller actual degree.
Each pair determinant is of degree at most \(2e\) and contains \(Q^2\) by elimination and unramified powering.
Thus each is a scalar multiple of \(Q^2\).
The determinant of a pair with its involution equals \(Y^e(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)\).
If nonzero, the two pairs are independent over \(\mathbb Q(Y)\), and Cramer's rule uses only ratios of the scalar multiples above.
They span the entire kernel over \(\mathbb Q\), with one line for each sign of the involution.
Conversely for the two generators of the signed lines their determinant is \(-(AC^*+CA^*)\).
Since \(Q(0)\ne0\), its constant coefficient detects whether the determinant is identically zero.
If that coefficient is nonzero, the determinant against the involution of a mixed vector is nonzero precisely when both mixing coefficients are nonzero.

Any such mixed pair has two nonzero entries because a single nonzero entry would need the divisor \(Q^2\) in degree at most \(e\).
A common factor \(Q\) in the two entries would make them constant multiples of \(Q\).
The single zero of \(Q(x^p)\) at \(\alpha\) would leave a rational polynomial of degree at most one required to vanish at \(\alpha\), which is impossible for a nonzero pair.
Every other common irreducible factor could be cancelled from both rows while preserving the double seed and unequal moduli.
The resulting lower-width determinant would still contain \(Q^2\), an impossibility by degree alone.
Thus the mixed pairs are coprime.

Primitivity of the two generators forces \(U+V^*=kA,\ U-V^*=lC\) with integers \(k,l\).
Their parity condition is necessary and sufficient for the halved rows to be integral.
Neither generator vanishes modulo two, so either both coefficients are even or both are odd, the latter allowed exactly when \(A\equiv C\bmod2\).
For fixed \(l\), the maximum of \(\|kA+lC\|_1,\|kA-lC\|_1\) is convex and even in \(k\), hence nondecreasing for \(k\ge0\), and the same argument holds with \(k,l\) exchanged.
This proves the stated minimum and handles an empty signed line, a line of higher dimension and the vanishing determinant cases through their indicated exclusions.
