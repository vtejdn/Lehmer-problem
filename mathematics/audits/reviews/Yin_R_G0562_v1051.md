# Independent review of six-term double contacts with a power-field drop or support reflection

## Relative degrees, original-root measure and graph height

At 2026-11-24T18:30 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [complete statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0903_v3.md#statement) and all four proof sections of C0903 v3.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings on v3.
Both conditional gaps and their field-degree and graph-height consequences follow.

Dividing the exponent differences by \(g\) transfers multiplicities from \(\alpha^{\pm1}\) to \(\beta^{\pm1}\) since the power map is unramified at nonzero points.
The deletion gcds are pairwise coprime because any common prime for two deletions would identify all six exponents modulo that prime, using any exponent present in both lists.
For \(3\le s\le5\) surviving terms, Lagrange interpolation describes the two moment kernels by polynomials \(Q_\pm\) of degrees at most \(s-3\).
Their product has degree at most \(2(s-3)<s\) and belongs to \(K[T]\) by interpolation at the \(s\) rational nodes.
Conjugates of the monic normalization of \(Q_+\) are monic divisors of this product of one fixed degree, giving at most \(\binom{2(s-3)}{s-3}\) choices even with repeated roots.
Ratios of its node values recover the powers of the normalized root with exponents the differences, and Bézout recovers the root itself.
This proof works over the infinite algebraic field \(H_{\rm ab}\) since the coefficient extensions in the divisor argument are finite and separable.

A power-degree drop identifies two conjugates with torsion quotient by the equality of the number of conjugates and the degree in characteristic zero.
A single Galois automorphism can send the first to a conjugate off the circle and preserves the order \(q\) of the quotient.
In the rotated polynomial the coefficient at \(b_j\) is a real cyclotomic scalar times \(c_j\), and it vanishes exactly for \(q\mid b_j-b_i\).
The two inverse evaluation points give arguments \(\zeta x,x\) and \(x^{-1},(\zeta x)^{-1}\), respectively, so both double zeros survive.
Primitivity of the normalized support makes the rotation nonzero.
The four-term exclusion for real coefficients at reciprocal points off the circle therefore leaves exactly five terms at each deletion.
This proves \(q\ge6\) and the pairwise distinctness modulo \(q\).
The factor \(\omega^{e_i}\) belongs to \(H_{\rm ab}\), so the five-term degree bound applies to \(x^{e_i}\) over that field.
Normality transfers it to \(\beta^{e_i}\), and two such fields generate \(H_{\rm ab}(\beta)\) by coprimality of their exponents, giving relative degree at most \(36\).

For \(j=g e_i\), all \(l\) relative conjugates in \(u=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)}\alpha\) have \(j\)-th power \(\alpha^j\).
It follows that \(u^j=\alpha^{jl}\), \(u\) is a nontorsion unit, and the absolute logarithmic height obeys \(h(u)=l h(\alpha)\).
Writing \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\) gives \(m(\alpha)=[K_0:\mathbb Q]h(u)\).
The inclusion \(H_{\rm ab}(u)\subset H_{\rm ab}(\alpha^j)\) gives \(1\le D=[H_{\rm ab}(u):H_{\rm ab}]\le6\), and \(D\le[K_0:\mathbb Q]\).
The finitely many coefficients of the minimal polynomial over \(H_{\rm ab}\), together with \(\sqrt5\), lie in a finite abelian field \(L/\mathbb Q\).
That polynomial stays irreducible over \(L\) since it is irreducible over the larger field, giving \([L(u):L]=D\).
The reviewer checked Theorem 1.3 directly in the author postprint at statement scope.
The extension \(L/\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) is abelian, the quadratic base has \(g(2)=1\) and discriminant \(5\), and the unconditional inequality bounds \(D h(u)\) below by \(5^{-c}(\log\log(5D))^3/(\log(2D))^4\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
Taking the positive minimum over the six values of \(D\) proves the asserted absolute gap for \(m(\alpha)\).
The threshold excludes all power-degree drops simultaneously, irrespective of \(g\), for each sufficiently late normalized root on a shrinking-measure sequence. [Amoroso and Delsinne, Theorem 1.3](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/2342a197-c1ea-4cca-9494-25db736adef5/RelativoII16.pdf)

For reflected support, the cancelling scalar is rational and the difference is nonzero under the nonproportionality hypothesis.
The reciprocal points are again double zeros, so the same real four-term exclusion proves that precisely one term disappears.
The resulting rational degree bound for every \(\beta^{e_i}\) is six, and substituting \(X^g\) into the cancelled five-term polynomial gives the original-root bound \((\log2)/24\).
Two of the power fields have compositum \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and intersection containing \(\mathbb Q(\beta^{e_i e_j})\), of degree at least two because rational algebraic units are torsion.
The upper product bound on the compositum degree divided by the intersection degree gives \(18\), without a Galois hypothesis on these fields.
For a Salem root, the unique exterior conjugate has singleton fiber under each positive power, hence all fibers are singletons and one deletion bounds the original degree by six.
For the ordinary graph, reflection interchanges the two residue classes because fixing both would require \(p\mid2\).
If the reflected polynomial were proportional to the original, involutivity would force scalar \(\pm1\) and hence \(U(Y)=\pm Y^{(L-1)/p}V(Y^{-1})\).
This gives zero reflected determinant, establishing the required nonproportionality.
Coprime integral rows and the reciprocal repeated factor give \(\log H\ge p\,m(\alpha)\) by reflected elimination, so \(H\ge2^{p/24}\).
The two exclusions leave the case of an all-power normalized field and support outside the nonproportional reflection hypothesis open.
