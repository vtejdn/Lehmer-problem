### Quadratic fields and the height product

The trace field \(K\) is totally real and contains neither Salem root, since each has a nonreal conjugate.
Both discriminants thus define nontrivial quadratic extensions of \(K\), and their quotient is a square exactly when those extensions coincide.
Equality puts \(\tau\) in \(K(\sigma)=\mathbb Q(\sigma)\), so the fixed-field generator theorem makes both numbers positive powers of the same \(\eta\), each of degree \(d\).
This gives \(h=1\) since Salem degrees are twice their trace degrees.
Conversely a nontrivial relation between positive numbers greater than one gives equality of positive powers, whose generated fields are preserved.
For \(h>1\), each of the other \(h-1\) embeddings fixing \(T_\tau\) takes \(T_\sigma\) into \((-2,2)\), confirming the negative sign of the discriminant quotient there.

In the independent case the distinct quadratic extensions have intersection \(K\), and their compositum is \(\mathbb Q(\sigma,\tau)\) of absolute degree \(4[K:\mathbb Q]=2d\).
The product of absolute logarithmic heights is \(hab/d^2\), since \(\tau\) has degree \(d/h\) and each Salem root is its unique conjugate outside the unit circle.
The rank-two product bound uses \(D=2d\), \(\log(3D)=\log(6d)\) and \(2\kappa(2)=2(3\cdot6^2-2)=212\).
This gives (1), including its equivalent upper bound for \(\deg\tau\).
For bounded \(a,b\) it excludes independence whenever \(h=o(d/(\log(6d))^{212})\).
The trace of a Salem number is irrational, so in the absence of a proper intermediate field one has \(h=1\).
The cited comparison for a rational function of the Salem root itself uses joint degree \(d\) at its own scope.

### Degree of a rational trace function

The nonconstant reduced fraction \(F/G\) gives a nonzero polynomial \(F-T_\tau G\) over \(\mathbb Q(T_\tau)\), whence \(h\le k\).
In the dependent case \(t=T_\eta\) has degree \(d/2\), since \(\eta\) generates the same Salem field as \(\sigma\).
If \(F(C_u)=C_vG(C_u)\) identically, composing a Bézout relation for \(F,G\) shows that \(G(C_u)\) is a unit.
Hence \(G\) is constant, degrees give \(v=ku\), and \(C_{ku}=C_k\circ C_u\) with injective substitution forces \(F/G=C_k\).
Otherwise the trace minimal polynomial divides their nonzero difference, whose degree is at most \(ku+v\), or \(\max(ku,v)\) for constant \(G\).
Multiplication by \(\log\eta\) gives both forms of (2).
Voutier's lower bound on the logarithmic Mahler measure applies to \(\log\eta\) at the full degree \(d\).
For \(u=1\) and bounded \(b\), it gives \(v=o(d)\), so the polynomial case forces \(k\ge d/2\) eventually.
Together the dependent and independent bounds give the stated rigidity for \(k=o(d/(\log(6d))^{212})\) under bounded \(a,b\).
The earlier angular bound permits unbounded \(b\) for an integral polynomial.

### Membership in the trace order and the quartic computation

Every commensurable Salem output is a positive power \(\eta^v\), whose trace lies in \(K\), and all elements of \(K=\mathbb Q(T_\sigma)\) have polynomial representatives with rational coefficients.
Integral polynomial representatives instead give the order \(B_u=\mathbb Z[C_u(t)]\).
Both power bases have \(d/2\) elements, since the underlying trace fields coincide by inclusion and equality of degrees.
The determinant of the integral change-of-basis columns has absolute value \(J=[A:B_u]\), so multiplication by \(J\) kills \(A/B_u\).
Membership therefore reduces exactly modulo \(JA\), where the recurrence for consecutive \(C_v(t)\) is invertible with inverse \((r,s)\mapsto(tr-s,r)\).
The admissibility test applies to \(\eta^v\) and the endpoint \(v=u\,e(\sigma)\) passes both tests, proving the bounded minimum in (3).
Voutier's Corollary 2 bounds the search for the primitive exponent \(u\), since \(\log\sigma=u\log\eta\).
A Salem \(j\)-th root of degree \(d\) contains \(\sigma\) in its field, and equality of degrees then puts that root in \(\mathbb Q(\sigma)\).
The generator theorem forces \(j\mid u\), with \(j=u\) attained.
This verifies the proposed recovery procedure.

In a trace field without a proper intermediate field the bounded window eventually excludes every independent output.
The two computed minima then decide both window-membership statements.
On a supplied sequence tending to one, bounded-degree finiteness gives \(d\to\infty\), so necessity follows by comparison to those minima, and sufficiency takes the minimizing powers themselves.

For \(t^2-t-3=0\), the other real embedding makes the quadratic discriminant negative and gives the stated quartic Salem polynomial.
The inequality \(|(2-T_\zeta)(2-t')|\ge1\) is the absolute value of a nonzero integral norm, and \(2-t'<4\) gives \(T_\zeta>9/4>3/\sqrt2\).
Every quartic Salem number therefore exceeds \(\sqrt2\), whereas a proper Salem root of \(\eta<2\) in its field would lie below \(\sqrt2\) and have the same degree.
This proves primitivity.
Modulo two the quartic has four distinct roots of order five, giving \(w_5=2\) and admissibility precisely when \(5\mid v\).
Direct reduction yields \(C_4(t)=3t+2\), index three and the diagonal subring in \(A/3A\) under evaluation at zero and one.
The two recurrence sequences have values \(2,0,1,0\) and \(2,1,2,1\) modulo three over each four indices starting at zero.
Their equality is equivalent to \(4\mid v\), so the least field and order exponents are five and twenty, with \(e(\eta^4)=5\).
The external height inequalities and the fixed-field generator theorem were used at their recorded statement scopes.
