## The common divisor in varying exponent directions

At 2026-11-20T08:56 UTC, an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete [fixed-seed theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#statement), including its rank-one assertion and torsion example.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and its scope and had no role in its development.
All three estimates hold with the stated quantifiers.

The Vandermonde quotient gives the index as the absolute product of integral divided differences because the powered conjugates are distinct.
Taking local ideal valuations in the splitting field and summing with weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\) recovers ordinary integer logarithms, since \(\sum_{\mathfrak l\mid\ell}e_{\mathfrak l}f_{\mathfrak l}=[L:\mathbb Q]\).
At each place the inequality for the minimum of two sums follows by applying \(\min(s,\sum_j t_j)\le\sum_j\min(s,t_j)\) twice.
If a quotient of conjugates \(r\ne1\) is torsion, removal of the unit multiplier leaves a divided difference in a finite set of nonzero algebraic integers.
Its ideal therefore contributes a bounded logarithmic norm even if \(p\) is composite.

For nontorsion \(r\), division by \(r-1\) can only reduce the valuation of \(r^p-1\).
This leaves the ideal gcd of \(u-1,w-1\) with \(u=r^p,\ w=x^py^{-q}\), where both differences are nonzero by the hypotheses.
I checked the normalization on printed p. 3, Proposition 2 on p. 7 and the conclusion on connected subgroups on p. 13 in the [Corvaja–Zannier preprint](../literature/CZ05/readme.md#arxiv-v2).
Taking \(S\) to consist of the archimedean places puts every \(u,w\) here in its unit group, and dropping the nonnegative archimedean contribution bounds the logarithmic ideal gcd.
For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), this gives an upper bound \(\epsilon\max(h(u),h(w))\) off finitely many pairs and finitely many equations \(u^A=w^B\).
An exceptional pair contributes a fixed finite gcd even if encountered repeatedly.
An equation with \(B=0\) would make \(u\) torsion, whereas \(r\) is nontorsion.
For \(B\ne0\), the equality \(B[x]-A[r]=(q/p)B[y]\) in the rational unit group modulo torsion determines at most one value of \(q/p\), since \([y]\ne0\).
All positive integer multiples of the primitive exponent pair at such a ratio have the same maximum reduced coordinate.
Consequently all those directions are eventually absent as \(N/\gcd(p,q)\to\infty\), while \(h(u)\le p h(r)\) and \(h(w)\le p h(x)+q h(y)\) give a uniform bound by a seed-dependent multiple of \(N\).
After division by \(N\), taking the limit superior and then decreasing \(\epsilon\) gives zero for each factor.
Exchanging the seeds uses the inverse unit \(w^{-1}\), whose difference from one has the same finite valuations.
There are finitely many pairs of factors, and \(\gcd(|C|,I_pJ_q)\le\gcd(|C|,I_p)\gcd(|C|,J_q)\).
This argument uses nontorsion of each seed and applies also when \(P=Q\).
At prime exponents, an internal collision requires its exponent to equal the order of one of finitely many torsion ratios of distinct conjugates.
A cross collision gives \(p h(x)=q h(y)\) with positive heights, so its fixed ratio can occur for at most one ordered pair of distinct primes.

For each pair of roots the quotient of the smaller power by the larger in absolute value satisfies \(|z|\le1\) and \(z\ne1\).
When \(|1-z|\ge1/2\) the logarithmic error is bounded, since also \(|1-z|\le2\).
In the other case the principal logarithm of \(z\) differs from the signed combination \(p\log x-q\log y\), taken with the sign of the quotient, by \(2\pi i k\) with \(|k|=O(N)\).
I checked the complex logarithmic form theorem in §5, printed pp. 5–6, of [Wüstholz's chapter](../literature/Wus02/readme.md#cambridge-2002).
All three arguments \(x,y,-1\) are fixed algebraic numbers different from zero and one, and exponentiation shows the form is nonzero, even for dependent arguments.
Its coefficient height is \(O(\log(2N))\), so the theorem and \(|\log z|\le2|1-z|\) give the claimed logarithmic error.
Summing over the \(ef\) labelled pairs proves the estimate at every eligible pair of exponents.
The logarithms of the conjugate moduli of a unit sum to zero, and their positive parts sum to the logarithm of its Mahler measure.
Applying convexity to each row and then to each column gives both stated lower bounds for \(T\).
The two external theorems were used at the level of their published statements and the normalizations needed here.

For graph contacts with distinct seed minima, rational coprimality makes \(V\) nonzero at every powered node, and \(-U/V\) recovers the seed there.
This gives the two full degrees and disjointness needed to identify \(C\) in the integral divisor from v496 under the stated graph contacts.
At a prime with \(v(C)=c,\ v(I_pJ_q)=s,\ v(p^e q^f\rho^3)=b\), the removed exponent is \(\min(4c,db+4ds)\le db+4d\min(c,s)\).
The remaining exponent \(\max(0,4c-v(K^d))\) is at most \(v(R)\) by v496.
Since \(P,Q\) and \(\rho\ne0\) are fixed, the logarithmic loss from \(p^e q^f\rho^3\) is \(O(\log(2N))\), and the index loss is \(o(N)\) independently of the rows.
These bounds give (3) and the row-resultant benchmark.
The gcd estimate bounds the smaller valuation at each prime and leaves open the excess of \(v(C)\) at primes dividing the indices.

In rank one the torsion order \(m\) and the exponents of a generator \(t\) are fixed.
For a torsion ratio of order \(k\), choosing \(ph\equiv1\bmod k\) exhibits a geometric sum inverse to the divided difference after removing its unit multiplier.
For \(r=\zeta_r t^c,\ x=\zeta_x t^a,\ y=\zeta_y t^b\) with \(bc\ne0\), a shared prime gives \(\bar t^{mcp}=\bar t^{m(ap-bq)}=1\).
The positive integer \(\gcd(cp,ap-bq)\) is at most \(|c|\gcd(p,bq)\le |bc|\) for distinct primes \(p,q\).
Thus every such prime divides some \(t^{mk}-1\ne0\) with \(1\le k\le|bc|\), a finite collection for all the root choices together.
At a fixed member of this finite set of prime ideals, either the order of \(\bar r\) exceeds one and must equal \(p\), or the divided difference reduces to \(p\) times a unit and the residue characteristic must equal \(p\).
Both alternatives disappear for sufficiently large \(p\), and the reversed argument handles the other index for sufficiently large \(q\).

For the quadratic torsion-boundary example, \(a+a^{-1}=3\) gives \((a-1)^2=a\).
It follows that \(|(a^p-1)(a^{-p}-1)|=S_p^2\).
For odd \(p\) both \(S_p\) and \(a^{-(p-1)/2}(a^p+1)/(a+1)\) are reciprocal Laurent polynomials in \(a\) with integer coefficients and hence are rational integers.
Their product equals \((a^p-a^{-p})/(a-a^{-1})=I_p\), while \(\log S_p=(p/2)\log a+O(1)\).
The \(Q=X-1\) node remains disjoint for every \(q\), so one may take distinct comparable primes and obtain a loss of order \(N\).
