**Normalized sparse averages.**

The expansion of \(f_J\) uses only frequencies \(|k|\le Jd\).
Its uniform tail is \(o(1)\) because \(J\rho^2/R^2\) grows as \((\log(2d))^{1/4}/(s+1)^2\), faster than \(\log(R^2/\rho^2)\) in the stated sparsity range.

**Exceptional orders.**
For a polynomial with at least two terms the extreme Fourier coefficient of \(|P|^2\) is nonzero.
The trigonometric polynomial defining the sublevel set is therefore nonconstant and has at most \(2d\) zeros counted with multiplicity.
The complement has a positive arc for \(h<1\) because \(\sup |P|\ge H\).

This proves the node bound by summing the arclength bound times \((Bd)^2\) and one possible extra point per component.

There are at most \(d\) such nodes, including exact zeros, and real coefficients pair them into distinct conjugates of the same order once \(Ad>2\).
This is the step yielding the factor \(1/2\).

On all other orders the integrated node count contributes \(O(d^2s^2\rho^a/a+dT)\), in addition to the two elementary terms at \(\sqrt\rho\) and above.
Because \(s\log(2s)=o(\log\log(3d))\), these terms together are \(o(d^2)\).
For \(\varphi(j)\ge\kappa d\), a fixed positive normalized loss uses at least a constant times \(\kappa d\) of that total per order.

Thus \(O_{A,B}(\kappa d)\) orders have smaller totient.
Taking the limit first with \(\kappa\) fixed proves the asserted exceptional count.

**Cyclotomic clearing.**

**Primes and unrestricted units.**
The dyadic Chebyshev estimates give \(L\asymp Y/\log Y\).
For the lower estimate the central binomial coefficient contributes \(2n\log2+O(\log n)\) in logarithm, whereas prime bases below \(2n/3\) above \(\sqrt{2n}\) contribute at most \((4/3)n\log2+O(\log^2 n)\).
Small prime bases together cost \(O(\sqrt n\log n)\), leaving a positive multiple of \(n\) from primes in \((n,2n]\).
With \(C_0\) sufficiently large this yields \(L\ge4d\) throughout \(Y\ge C_0d\log(2d)\).

For every exterior root inversion contributes exactly \((p-1)\log|\alpha|\) to the logarithm before evaluation at the inverted root.
A normalized root \(z=0\) contributes zero loss.
If \(|\Phi_p(z)|<e^{-t}\), radial and angular distance from some \(p\)-th root are \(O(e^{-t}/p)\).
For large fixed \(t_0\) that root cannot be \(1\), since the geometric sum would then differ from \(p\) by at most \(O(pe^{-t})\).
Deleting one prime of a nearest primitive node per root covers exact zeros.
Every centered angular arc that contains a remaining candidate node also contains the deleted nearest node, including in a tie.
Consequently the spacing estimate loses its additive one and bounds the remaining sublevel count by \(O(Ye^{-t})\).
Integrating its minimum with \(L\) gives \(O(L(1+\log(Y/L)))=O(L\log\log(3Y))\) per root.
Deletion over \(d\) roots and the summed-loss bound leave a positive fraction of primes as claimed, including for arbitrarily close roots and with multiplicities.

Dobrowolski's estimate is used for the total logarithmic measure of the unit at its original degree.
In bounded degree the needed positive lower bound follows from finiteness of monic integer polynomials of bounded degree and bounded measure and exclusion of torsion.
At \(Y=C d\log^3(2d)/(\log\log(3d))^2\), multiplication by the degree-dependent lower bound for \(m(P)\) leaves a constant times \(C d\log\log(3d)\), which dominates \(2C_1d\log\log(3Y)\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(C\).
This scale also meets the prime-count threshold.
For \(m(P)\ge\eta>0\) the scale \(C_\eta d\log(2d)\) suffices, and the full resultant gains the nonzero integer factor \(|P(1)|\).
