### Limit of counterexamples to the abstract statement

Negating the assertion permits using simultaneously \(\delta_i=c_i=1/i\) and \(N_{0,i}=i\).
The resulting measures have total variation at most \(1+2/i\), so their weak subsequential limit is a conjugation-invariant probability.
Every fixed coefficient of the transforms converges by testing against a character.
Since \(N_i\ge i\), each coefficient of \(B_\infty=(1-w/2)J_\infty\) is the limit of integers and stabilizes on this subsequence.
Its constant is one, so \(K=(2-w)/B_\infty\) has integer coefficients and its coefficient of \(w\) is \(-1-2[w]B_\infty\).
The probability representation gives \(\Re J_\infty>0\) in the open disk and hence \(\Re K>0\).

The positive Herglotz measure of \(K\), doubled, has integral Fourier coefficients at every index, including mass four at zero.
Thus the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes decomposition](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) supplies rationality, a squarefree cyclotomic denominator and a nonnegative Laurent density for its continuous part.
The [integral reciprocal argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#cyclotomic) applies to \(B_\infty=(2-w)/K\), which is rational and analytic in the disk.
The odd coefficient forces a zero at \(w=2\) and the radial Herglotz lower bound forces the other numerator zeros to be simple, giving \(K=(2-w)U_0/V\) in the stated normalization.
An identically zero density would make \(K(w)+K(1/w)=0\) a rational identity by continuation from a regular circle arc.
At \(w=2\) both sides of the sum are finite, and \(K(2)=0\) while \(K(1/2)>0\) by positivity and reality of the coefficients.
This independently supplies the nonvanishing hypothesis of the [nine-profile phase calculation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#phase) for abstract moment sequences.
Therefore \(B_\infty=V/U_0\) is one of the listed series.

### The first discrepancy and its moving harmonics

Failure against every listed series through \(\lfloor N_i/i\rfloor\) forces a first discrepancy \(n_i\) against this particular \(B_\infty\) by that index.
Coefficient stabilization gives \(n_i\to\infty\), and the upper bound gives \(n_i/N_i\to0\).
For every fixed \(k\ge1\) and \(l\in\mathbb Z\), both \(kn_i+l\) and its predecessor therefore tend to infinity within the integral range.
This accounts for the uniform fraction in the conclusion, beyond agreement at each separately fixed order.

To check the [two-circle comparison](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v52.md#harmonics), push \(2\omega_i^+\) forward by \(v\mapsto(v,v^{n_i})\) and extract a weak limit on the compact torus.
Removing \(2\omega_i^-\) changes every character integral by at most \(2/i\), including at moving indices.
The limiting first marginal is \(2\omega_\infty\), with a bounded rational continuous density \(\sigma=2\Re J_\infty\) at regular points and finitely many torsion atoms because \(J_\infty=2V/((2-w)U_0)\).
The powers \(v^{n_i}\) at all these atoms can be fixed simultaneously on a subsequence.
Agreement of \(B_i,B_\infty\) below \(n_i\) implies agreement of \(J_i,J_\infty\) there by division by their common multiplier \(1-w/2\).
Hence the first harmonic projection, minus the limiting atoms with their fixed phases, has vanishing moments at every negative offset.
Cesàro averaging these moments against the opposite phase at a specified point annihilates its atom.
At every marginal atom the conditional first moment is consequently the specified phase of modulus one, forcing a point mass on that fiber and determining all its higher harmonics.
This verifies the [atomic cancellation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#departure) without any prescribed location for the small negative parts.

After subtraction of those atoms and reflection in the first variable, the \(k\)-th projection has density \(\sigma c_k\) with \(|c_k|\le1\) and with \(c_k\) the moments of a single conditional probability.
The \(l\)-th coefficient of \(H_k=(1-w/2)\sigma c_k\) is the limit of the difference of the two \(B\) coefficients at \(kn_i+l\), using the corresponding \(J\) indices \(kn_i+l,kn_i+l-1\).
It is integral, and at \(k=1,l=0\) the convergent integer differences are nonzero, giving \(H_1\ne0\).
Boundedness of \(\sigma\) gives square summability, making each integral coefficient row a finite Laurent polynomial.
The conditional Toeplitz inequalities extend by continuity to all regular points with \(\sigma>0\).

For seven of the nine profiles the [bracket bounds](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#brackets) give \(M(\rho)<2\sqrt2\).
Then \(M(|1-w/2|\sigma)=M(\rho)/2<\sqrt2\), so the [separated-block argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes) bounds the list of nonzero \(H_k\) up to monomial shifts.
The two remaining profiles \(K=2-w,(2-w)/(1-w)\) satisfy the [exceptional envelope bounds](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#exceptions), giving the same finiteness and envelope measure strictly below two.
With these inputs the [large-prime specialization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) excludes \(H_1\ne0\) in every case.
This contradiction uses the integral prefixes and conditional moment conditions derived above, without any assumption of a Pisot origin for \(\omega_i\).

### Transfer to both coefficient progressions

Pushing a real symmetric signed measure forward by \(v\mapsto v^d\) preserves its mass and symmetry, sends its \(k\)-th character moment to the original \(kd\)-th moment, and cannot increase its negative mass.
The [signed measure for \(J_q\)](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) therefore gives the projected coefficients with mass one and negative mass at most \(1/(2(q-1))\), including when opposite-signed masses map to the same point.
For either sign, \((1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\) agrees with \(2\pm z^d\) in degrees strictly below \(2h+2-d\).
Multiplying by \(J_q/2\) in the trace identity gives the same cutoff for \(B^\sharp=(1\pm z^d/2)J_q\) as a coefficient identity.
In particular the coefficient at \(kd\) is the coefficient at \(k\) of \((1\pm w/2)\widetilde J\) for \(k\le\lfloor(2h+1-d)/d\rfloor\), including the constant term one.
The subtraction of one in this floor excludes the first possible reflected term even when \(d\) divides \(2h+2-d\).

Choosing \(q_0\ge2\) with \(1/(2(q_0-1))\le\delta\) gives the minus progression by the abstract result.
For the plus sign, rotating the projected measure by \(-1\) replaces \(B(w)\) by \(B(-w)\), preserves its integer prefix and negative-mass bound, and changes the multiplier to \(1-w/2\).
Undoing the rotation gives the coefficients of \(V(-w)/U_0(-w)\) in the original variable.
When \(q\ge q_0\) and \(d=o(h)\), the last forced index \(d\lfloor cN\rfloor\) satisfies \(d\lfloor cN\rfloor/h\to2c\), with \(c\) taken at most one.
The proof yields no explicit values for the thresholds and covers only the indicated progression.
