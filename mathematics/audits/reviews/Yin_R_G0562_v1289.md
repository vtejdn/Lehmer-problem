## The exact minimum, return modulo two and larger supports

For \(m=2^k\ge4\) and \(g=m^2\), the indices satisfy \(1<m+1<g\), and both are odd units modulo \(g\).
Their reflected exponents exceed \(g\), so the four exponents give exactly the two coefficient pairs counted in v5.
For every unit \(u\), the difference between representatives of \((m+1)u\) and \(u\) has the form \(m(u+jm)\).
The factor in parentheses is odd, so the absolute difference is at least \(m\).
Taking least absolute representatives bounds their sum of absolute values below by \(m\).
At \(u=m-1\), the representatives are \(m-1\) and \(-1\), attaining \(m=\sqrt g\).

The paired terms and the two endpoint coefficients give a monic reciprocal polynomial with constant term one, and the nonzero linear coefficient rules out compression.
The reduction of \(P(X+1)\) modulo two is \(X^{2g}\), since \(2g\) is a power of two.
Its constant coefficient \(P(1)=10\) has 2-adic valuation one, giving irreducibility by Eisenstein.
At \(-2\) the top two terms cancel, leaving \(1-4-2^{m+2}-2^{2g-m}<0\).
The positive limit at \(-\infty\) gives a real root below \(-2\), hence \(M(P)>2\) and \(P\) is noncyclotomic.
Over \(\mathbb F_2\) the whole polynomial is \(X^{2g}-1\), so the gcd at return time \(2g\) has degree \(2g\), with multiplicity.

Both conclusions hold for arbitrarily large \(g\).
The hypotheses of v5 apply, and its upper bound evaluates to \(C(1+\sqrt g)\) on this family.
This computation concerns that construction's position cost and supplies no lower bound on the volume of other realizations.
The measure bound also places these examples outside the proposed small-measure regime.

For general \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,g-1\}\), multiplication by a unit preserves distinctness and avoids zero.
At distance \(r>0\) the only possible residues are \(r\) and \(-r\), which coincide at the antipode when \(g\) is even.
Thus the \(\ell\)-th smallest distance is at least \(\lceil\ell/2\rceil\).
Summing its lower bound \(\ell/2\) gives \(s(s+1)/4\) for every unit and hence for the minimum.
The two endpoint terms, at most one central term and \(s\) reciprocal pairs give at most \(2s+3\) nonzero terms in the whole polynomial.
If \(s\) stayed bounded on a subsequence, the bounded-support theorem would give a positive minimum gap above one over its finitely many possible term counts, contradicting convergence of the measures to one from above.
Consequently the ratio of the minimum distance sum to \(s\) diverges on the stated hypothetical sequence.
The source input here is Dobrowolski's Theorem 1 as stated in U001, applied to the full polynomial.
