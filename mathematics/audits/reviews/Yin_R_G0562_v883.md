# Four-term concentration on a shrinking Salem orbit

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:00 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete [C345 v743](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing the result and received its fixed text without the development discussion or a prior verdict on it.

The degree tends to infinity by finiteness of bounded-degree integer polynomials of bounded Mahler measure.
The absolute sublevel condition at neutral roots implies the projective condition on the same rows, and their density in all embeddings is at least \(\eta/2\).
Taking the minimum of the thresholds for the three possible supports and \(1/2\) is therefore uniform.
The exact equation supplied by [v740](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#statement) uses a subset of the original support, and its coefficients, including their conjugates, form a finite set.
At the exterior root comparison with the largest term bounds the uppermost gap times \(m\).
Transporting the whole equation by an embedding taking the root to its reciprocal gives the corresponding lower gap, using the same finite coefficient bounds.
With two or three surviving exponents this bounds the entire surviving span times \(m\).
The exact relation would then vanish on a proportion bounded away from zero of all conjugates at span times height tending to zero, contradicting [v737](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#statement) after normalizing.
This last argument also precludes a subsequence with \(Wm/d\to0\) once four terms survive.
Consequently \(bm\to\infty\), since \((W-b)m\) stays bounded and \(d\to\infty\).

On each neutral root over the identity of the coefficient field, the two end binomials have equal moduli.
Their squared-modulus difference is a Laurent polynomial with at most five terms and subcritical span, so its dense exact zeros force the Laurent polynomial itself to vanish identically for all sufficiently large indices.
This normalization is permitted even for arbitrarily small coefficient norm since only exact zeros are used.
The end exponents must agree, since the coefficient on the largest positive exponent otherwise comes from precisely one product of nonzero coefficients.
The equal cross coefficients and squared sums give equal unordered pairs of moduli.
In the proportional case the exterior root cannot kill the factor \(1+\lambda X^b\), leaving a power in a fixed finite set.
All positive powers retain the Salem degree because a collision, transported to the exterior root, would give a different conjugate of the same exterior modulus.
Thus this case cannot persist.

In the other modulus ordering the cross-coefficient identity gives \((w,t)=\lambda(\bar v,\bar u)\).
The end-gap bound makes \(\alpha^a\) bounded, and the normalized equation puts it within \(O(e^{-bm})\) of the finite set of \(\xi\)'s.
Eventually the actual \(\xi\) must be real and at least one, and the inequality of the two moduli makes it strictly larger than one.
At \(\alpha^a=\xi\) the equation would imply \(1-\xi^2=0\), so solving for the unimodular multiplier proves it is real and fixes the sign in (1).
The resulting rational expression for \(B\) has nonzero denominator since \(b>a\), and inversion fixes it for both signs.
The Salem root generates a quadratic extension of its totally real trace field, with inversion the nontrivial automorphism, giving both the asserted field inclusion and total reality.
All embeddings fixing \(B\) preserve (1), including at least \(d/[\mathbb Q(B):\mathbb Q]-2\) neutral conjugates.

For fixed \(B\) the product formula on \(X-B\ne0\) gives precisely the projective height written for \(Y=\alpha^b\).
At finite places \(X=\alpha^a\) is a unit.
If \(|B|\ne1\), the maximum equals \(\max(1,|B|)\); if \(|B|=1\), the two elementary identities in the proof bound the maximum between \(|X^2-1|\) and one.
The sum of this last local error is bounded by \(L_1(X^2)\), which tends to zero by [v730](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) since \(X^2\) is nontorsion of vanishing height.
Every embedding of the fixed seed field gives the full relative Galois orbit of the corresponding conjugate of \(X\), of size \(d/[\mathbb Q(B):\mathbb Q]\).
The distinctness requirement of the [small-point theorem](../literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution) holds after taking further subsequences because the degrees diverge.
The canonical height for squaring is the usual height and the atomless Haar probability satisfies the degree-two pullback identity, giving the stated archimedean limit over each fixed embedding.
On the common compact disk the log of the maximum is continuous because the two linear factors have no common root for any conjugate of \(B\).
On the unit circle the moduli coincide for real \(\tau(B)\), and the circle mean gives \(\log^+|\tau(B)|\).
Equal embedding multiplicities and the full finite-place contribution give \(h(Y)-h(B)\to0\).
Finally \(am-\log B\to0\), with \(\log B\) uniformly bounded away from zero within the finite set, proves the ratio formula and the limiting statement for the whole span.
The theorem gives necessary structure for a shrinking sequence and its stated counting converse.
