## Extremal density, exterior zeros and periodic masks

A signed relation splits into its positive and negative binary supports, and a collision of distinct binary subsets gives a nonzero signed relation after their intersection cancels.
A relation on a translated suffix shifts back into the original mask by multiplication by a monomial, preserving divisibility.
The two interval restrictions therefore give subadditivity of the extremal cardinality, and Fekete's lemma gives its limiting density.

After reversal and rotation the constant coefficient has modulus one, so a zero at \(r\) requires \(1\le r/(1-r)\).
In particular \(1/2\le r<1\), placing it strictly inside the circle with center \(a=1/4\) and radius \(b=3/4\).
At its center the remaining terms have total modulus at most \(1/3\).
On the circle, \(1-|z|^2=2ab(1-\cos t)\), and the mean of \(\log(1-\cos t)\) is \(-\log 2\) by the half-angle formula.
The singular upper estimate at the tangency is integrable, and each boundary zero contributes zero in the polynomial form of Jensen's formula.

The mean bound divided in exponential form by the lower bound at the center gives \((2/(ab))/(2/3)=16\).
