## Translated Newton polygons, irreducibility and simultaneous degree restrictions

At 2026-11-25T02:24 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v123 independently checked for Yin the fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v123.md#statement) and all three proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed result and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or earlier findings on v123.
The local degrees and ramification indices, both rational irreducibility criteria, the assertions about dilations and the simultaneous two-prime exclusion follow in their stated ranges.

For \(\ell\mid b\) the reduced binomial at any unit root has exponent \(N=v+u\); for \(\ell\mid a\), division by \(X^u\) instead gives \(N=v-u\).
The Teichmüller lift \(\zeta\) of a residue root has \(\zeta^N=-\epsilon\), since \(\ell\) is odd and lifting preserves the signs.
In \(X=\zeta(1+T)\) the undisplaced binomial becomes a unit multiple of \((1+T)^N-1\).
The residual perturbations after division by \(\ell^r\) have constant values given, up to units, by \(1-\zeta^{-2u}\) and \(1-\zeta^{2u}\), respectively.
As \(\zeta^{2N}=1\), \(\gcd(u,N)=1\) and prime-to-\(\ell\) roots have distinct reductions, precisely the permitted sign centers have a vanishing perturbation.
There the perturbed binomial's derivative contributes valuation \(r\): its exponent in the perturbation is \(v-u\equiv-2u\) or \(v+u\equiv2u\bmod\ell\), and the derivative of the unperturbed term has valuation \(s>r\).
This gives simple actual sign roots and an initial coefficient of valuation \(r\) after division by \(T\).
At each other center the original constant already has that valuation.

For \(j\le\ell^s\), multiplication of \(\binom Nj\) by \(j\) leaves \(N\binom{N-1}{j-1}\), with the second factor an \(\ell\)-unit because the coefficients below degree \(\ell^s\) of \((1+T)^{N-1}\) modulo \(\ell\) alternate in sign.
The first positive binomial indices at successively smaller valuation levels are \(j=\ell^{s-r+1},\ldots,\ell^s\).
All intermediate indices have valuation at least the earlier level, and the entire perturbation is divisible by \(\ell^r\).
After subtracting the index \(e=0\) or \(1\) for the initial simple zero, the widths are \(L-e\) followed by \((\ell-1)L,\ldots,(\ell-1)\ell^{r-2}L\).
They increase strictly, each vertical drop is one, and after valuation zero is reached integrality excludes further negative slopes.
The unit power series used to remove \(X^u\) contributes zero minimum at every positive weight and leaves these edges unchanged.
As a separate exact check, for \(\ell=3,\ r=2,\ s=3,\ h=2\) the two coefficient orientations are realized by \((a,b,u,v)=(2,9,1,53)\) and \((9,2,1,55)\).
Directly expanding the four monomials at \(\zeta(1+T)\), using coefficient \(\sum_n c_n\zeta^n\binom nj\) in degree \(j\), gave in both examples noninitial vertices \((9,1),(27,0)\) for \(\epsilon=1\) at \(\zeta=\pm i\), of residue degree two.
For \(\epsilon=-1\) at each \(\zeta=\pm1\), removal of its simple root gave vertices \((8,1),(26,0)\).

Over the unramified field of the center there are exactly \(w\) roots counted with multiplicity at distance of valuation \(1/w\) for the edge of width \(w\).
Any one yields a field of ramification index divisible by \(w\); its conjugates over that unramified base lie among those same \(w\) roots.
The extension consequently has degree and ramification index exactly \(w\), with distinct roots.
The full root field over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) contains \(\zeta\) by the unique lift of its residue root of unity, and this unramified subfield has degree \(f\).
Each Frobenius orbit of centers thus gives exactly one factor of degree \(fw\) and residue degree \(f\) per edge.
A rational monic unit factor has only local unit roots, and the nontorsion hypothesis excludes the removed sign roots.
Its degree sums the selected local degrees, each divisible by \(L\) except for the exceptional first factors of degree \(L-1\).
There are at most two of the latter, proving both the corrected congruence and the lower bound.
No such exception occurs at odd primes with \(\epsilon=1\) and \(u,v\) odd.

For \(r=h=1\) the binomial degree \(N=\ell^s\) is odd, so the single unit center is \(-\epsilon\).
If \(\ell\mid b\), the translated quotient has degree \(N-1\), unit leading coefficient, every lower coefficient divisible by \(\ell\), and its constant has valuation one.
This independently gives Eisenstein irreducibility of the whole quotient over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\).
If \(\ell\mid a\), the Newton polygon at zero has two additional edges of width \(u\) and absolute vertical change one.
Each gives a single irreducible factor of degree \(u\) whose roots are all nonunits at \(\ell\).
Every rational proper factorization of the quotient therefore has a factor omitting the single unit-center factor, of degree \(d'\le2u\).
For a root \(\kappa\) of that factor the primitive minimal polynomial has leading or constant coefficient divisible by \(\ell\), since if both were units all its roots and their inverses would be integral at \(\ell\).
This gives \(d'h(\kappa)\ge\log\ell\).
The projective map induced by the linear forms \(a+bT,b+aT\) has determinant of absolute value \(|a^2-b^2|>0\), so the equation with \(T=\kappa^u\) has a nonvanishing denominator.
The local sup-norm inequality for the two forms gives \(vh(\kappa)\le uh(\kappa)+\log(|a|+|b|)\).
Together the bounds contradict the strict inequality in the second family.
In either family Gauss's lemma yields a primitive irreducible quotient with nonunit integral leading coefficient, hence none of its roots is an algebraic integer.
Raising a nontorsion unit to a positive integral power again gives a nontorsion unit, which proves the dilation exclusion.
A divisor of measure strictly between one and two would have unit leading and trailing nonzero coefficients and some positive-measure monic unit factor, which is impossible here.

For \(|a|=q, |b|=q+1\), the degree and exterior count used from v875 concern the rational factor of the reduced root, including when the original root came from a common dilation.
Its uniform equivalent \(J/D\sim u\log(q+1)/(v\log(1+1/q))\) and \(v/u\to\infty\) give the claimed equivalent with either \(N=v\pm u\).
Combining with \(D\ge L-1\) preserves the factor \(1-1/L\) uniformly in the local data.
The two exact coefficient valuations can be prescribed simultaneously on \(q\bmod \ell_1^2\ell_2^2\).
The initial congruences on \(v\) select one residue modulo \(6Q\), hence one integer in \([Q,7Q)\).
Among that integer and its first two translates by \(6Q\), each of the two excluded extra prime powers rules out at most one candidate, since \(6Q/\ell_i^{s_i}\) is invertible modulo \(\ell_i\ge5\).
The surviving \(v<19Q\) is odd and coprime to \(u=3\).
Both exceptional corrections vanish, hence the two coprime prime powers divide \(D\), giving \(N/D\le(v+3)/Q<19+3/Q\).
This contradicts the diverging equivalent, since \(1\le J\le3\), along any hypothetical sequence with \(q\to\infty\).
Taking the powers after \(q\) makes both cofactors large because \(v\ge Q\).
The argument does not require fixing either prime, the signs, the powers or the common dilation along this limit.
