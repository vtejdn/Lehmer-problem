## The middle coefficient, the prescribed real direction and the two gap implications

At 2026-11-25T03:09 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v134 independently reviewed for Yin the fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v134.md#statement) and all four proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed mathematics and the stated dependencies without development discussion or previous findings on v134.
The coefficient obstruction is necessary and sufficient at delay one, remains necessary at all positive delays in even degree, and the seed inequalities can be attained simultaneously as claimed.
The uniform-gap equivalence holds for the bounded-second-iterate reduction.

Under \(f^\tau=\epsilon f^*\) the equations for \(i\) and \(b-i\) are interchanged by \(\epsilon\tau\).
For distinct indices one coefficient may therefore be \(f_i\) and the paired coefficient zero, both integral; at \(i=b/2\) a solution is precisely a preimage under \(1+\epsilon\tau\).
The fixed leading coefficient of \(P\) handles both endpoints.
At delay \(k\) with monic seed of degree \(b-k\), the middle term is a coefficient plus \(\epsilon\) times its conjugate with the same index \(b/2-k\) whenever \(k\le b/2\).
For \(k>b/2\) both terms are absent, so this coefficient must be zero, still in the indicated image.
These observations impose no inequality or assumption about unramified primes.

In the homogeneous system each unmatched pair of distinct coefficient indices contributes one free \(K\)-coordinate, of dimension two over \(E\).
In even degree the middle coordinate has dimension one over \(E\), since \(\tau\) has its two one-dimensional eigenspaces in a quadratic extension in characteristic zero.
Together these give dimension \(b-1\) over \(E\).
The integral solutions contain the \(\mathcal O_E\)-span of an \(E\)-basis after clearing denominators and are discrete in the full coefficient Minkowski space, hence form a full lattice in the product of the local homogeneous spaces.
At the split place a first real tuple of \(b-1\) lower coefficients is arbitrary: the equations determine the second tuple by reversal and the sign, also for the middle coefficient.
At a nonsplit place the second embedding is the complex conjugate of the first, so one consistent target per conjugate pair suffices.

At the chosen real embedding the coefficients and multiset of interior roots are conjugation invariant.
The constructed outer polynomial \(O\) has constant \(r\), leading coefficient \(\epsilon/r\), and circle modulus \(|f|\) by reflecting all the interior linear factors.
The real reversed polynomial \(O^*\) has the same circle modulus.
Thus the specified \(U\) has constant \(\epsilon\), leading coefficient one, and \(|U/f|\le2/(r+r^{-1})<1\) uniformly on the circle.
The nonzero polynomial \(N=f-U\) has zero constant, degree at most \(b-1\), and exactly \(b-1\) interior roots by Rouché.
It has exact degree \(b-1\), and \(H=N/X\) has degree \(b-2\) with every root strictly interior.
Also \(\Re(zH/f)=\Re(1-U/f)>0\) there.
The factor \(1-r\) is negative, while \(\prod_{j\ge2}(1-a_j)\) is positive by pairing complex conjugates and using strict interiority for each real root.
Therefore \(f(1)<0\) and \(H(1)<0\).
The same pairing on \(H\) shows its leading coefficient is negative, also when \(b=2\) and \(H\) is constant.
If its constant vanishes, a sufficiently small real perturbation retains negative leading coefficient, strict interiority and strict positivity on the compact circle and gives nonzero constant.

The normalized derivative \(P_{\rm ref}=f'/b\) has monic degree \(b-1\).
At index \(i\) its contributions to the coefficient identity are \(if_i/b\) and \((b-i)f_i/b\), including the endpoints.
At each complex embedding the critical roots lie strictly inside the circle by the distinctness of the circle roots of \(f\).
This supplies a local target regardless of the denominators of the derivative.
Subtract an integral particular solution from the reference targets, with the prescribed \(\lambda H\) addition at the first real embedding and the forced adjustment at the second.
The resulting tuple lies in the full local homogeneous space.
For a totally positive unit \(v\) with the indicated logarithmic signs, dividing by \(v^n\), rounding within a bounded fundamental domain, and multiplying back keeps the corrections integral and homogeneous.
At every nonsplit place the errors tend to zero, since \(v_\sigma<1\); at the marked place they are \(O(v_*^n)=o(v_*^{2n})\).
The opposite real tuple satisfies the exact coefficient equations throughout, with no independent seed root condition required there.
Consequently the complex seed roots are all strictly inside eventually, and at the marked place \(P_n/v_*^{2n}\to H\) coefficientwise, with nonzero constant eventually.

On a centered circle of radius less than one containing the roots of \(H\), Rouché gives exactly \(b-2\) interior roots of \(P_n\), including zero such roots for \(b=2\).
The one remaining root is simple and real by conjugation and the degree count.
The sum of all roots is minus the \(X^{b-2}\)-coefficient, which tends to positive infinity because the corresponding coefficient of \(H\) is strictly negative.
The \(b-2\) bounded roots show that this remaining root itself tends to positive infinity.
Finally the exact identity \(|P_n|^2-|(P_n^\tau)^*|^2=2\Re(\overline f\,zP_n)-|f|^2\) on the circle, divided by \(v_*^{2n}\), tends uniformly to \(2|f|^2\Re(zH/f)>0\).
This establishes all the strict seed conditions together with unbounded exterior seed roots for the same \(f\).

A positive shrinking sequence of integer-polynomial measures with bounded second log measure yields, by v120, a shrinking subsequence of real Salem numbers or of compressed canonical square endpoints of v109.
For the latter, the marked half product is a real root of unity and hence a rational sign; the other blocks are neutral and the half-field embeddings have the required signatures.
Eventually the degree exceeds the fixed small-measure cutoff, and \(u=1\) is impossible by the oriented primitive-action conclusion of v274 since the endpoint field is embedded in \(\mathbb R\).
In odd half degree or when the even middle condition holds, the reconstruction gives the very same half polynomial, whose rational norm is the original minimum.
In the other even case the endpoint belongs to the specified residual class.
Every real Salem number is realized by a strict linear seed of delay one with norm the square of its minimum by v131.
Squaring its measure preserves convergence to one.

In the reverse direction, an obstructed square endpoint supplies its own monic minimum, with first measure \(r^2\) and second measure \(M(r^2)=M(r)^2=r^4\) by full degree of powers.
For a nondegenerate delay-one construction of any seed degree, its integral norm polynomial \(F\) has \(M(F)=R^2\) using the two real and all complex root multisets, with multiplicities.
The minimal polynomial of \(R\) divides this monic integer norm, so \(M(R)\le R^2\).
Even if some conjugates merge under squaring, \(M(R^2)\le M(R)^2\le R^4\).
A shrinking sequence from either class therefore supplies a bounded-second-iterate sequence of integer polynomials.
The argument makes no assertion about a shrinking sequence with unbounded second iterates.
