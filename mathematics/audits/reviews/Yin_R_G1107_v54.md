## The signed neutral comparison and the two explicit bounds

I find both upper bounds valid for every nonzero integer perturbation and strict cut in the stated domain.
Since \(n+1=m+d<r\), the lowest exponent of the lifted perturbation is \(n+1-\deg H<r\), with coefficient \(\operatorname{lc}(H)\).
The omitted tail of the neutral geometric factor starts at \(r\), so the largest matching index is exactly \(L=n-\deg H\).
Repeated cyclotomic factors preserve integrality of \(C_A\), since \(z^{-d}A(z)\) is an inversion-invariant Laurent polynomial with integer coefficients and \(z^j+z^{-j}\in\mathbb Z[z+z^{-1}]\).

For \(\delta_j=H(b_j)/B'_n(b_j)\), the coefficients of \(H/B_n\) at infinity give \(\sum_j b_j^\ell\delta_j=0\) through \(\ell=L-2\), followed by \(\operatorname{lc}(H)\) at \(L-1\).
Multiplying a test polynomial of degree at most \(L-3\) by \(t-a\) therefore gives the matching moments of \(X\) and \(G\).
For \(L\ge3\), multiplying \(t-a\) by the monic cosine trace of degree \(L-2\) instead gives the first changed moment.
Its absolute value is at most \((2n/p)\mathbb E|X-G|\), and its nonzero integral value yields the claimed strict lower bound \(\mathbb E|X-G|>1\).

The midpoint lies strictly between \(b_{c+1}\) and \(b_c\), so every \(X_j\) is positive.
Writing \(D_0=B_{n-1}\), the grid derivative gives \(D_0(b_j)/B'_n(b_j)=(4-b_j^2)/p\) and \(D_0(b_j)^2=2+b_j\).
The endpoint values \(B_n(2)=p\), \(B_n(-2)=(-1)^n\) give \(\prod_j(4-b_j^2)=p\) and \(\prod_j|D_0(b_j)|=1\).
Consequently \(\prod_j X_j=p|B_n(a)|\,|\operatorname{Res}(B_n,Q)|\ge1\), since the midpoint gives \(|B_n(a)|\ge1\) and strict residues make the integral resultant nonzero.
This argument includes the imprimitive angles of a composite conductor.

At a grid angle \(\theta_j\), \(r=p-2s\) gives \(B_m(b_j)=D_0(b_j)E_{s-1}(b_j)\), hence \(p\mu_j=(4-b_j^2)C_A(b_j)E_{s-1}(b_j)\).
The derivation survives multiplicities in \(A\) and includes \(s=1\).
The two separator factors multiply in modulus to \(|t-a|\), and the factors of exponents \(2,2s\) multiply in modulus to \(4|\sin\theta\,\sin(s\theta)|\).
The remaining modulus is \(|A(z)|=|C_A(t)|\).
Thus \(f=|G|\) on the grid, including where the neutral term vanishes, with no sign restriction on \(C_A E_{s-1}\).

For the polynomial \(U\), summing the geometric majorant for the discarded tail and dividing by \(|1-\lambda x|^{-1/2}\) bounds the relative error by \(\sqrt2 e^{-1/\eta}/\eta\le24\sqrt2\,\eta^3\le\tau\).
In particular \(U\) never vanishes on the circle.
The identity \(|1-\lambda x|^2=\eta^2+\lambda f_i^2\) proves \(w_i f_i\le1\), including \(f_i=0\).
The opposite estimate uses \(|1-\lambda x|\le f_i+\eta\), \((1-\eta)^{-1/2}\le1+\eta\), and \(((1+\tau)/(1-\tau))^2=1+4\tau/(1-\tau)^2\le1+5\tau\).
It follows that \(1/w_i\le(1+3\eta)(f_i+\eta)\le f_i+8\eta\).
The \(i\)-th Laurent factor has exponents in \([-a_i(N-1),a_i(N-1)]\) for factor exponent \(a_i\).
Inverting \(z\) exchanges conjugate phases of the same exponent, including the two at the separator.
The product is therefore real and even on the circle and is a real polynomial in \(t\) of degree at most \(D(N-1)\).
Telescoping gives \(0\le1/w-f\le8R2^R\eta\), since \((1+4\eta)^R\le e^{4R\eta}<2\).
The same estimate gives \(1/w\le2^{R+1}\).

For each exponent \(e\) and phase \(\sigma\), put \(g=\gcd(e,p)\).
The full-grid product is \(|1-(\lambda\sigma)^{p/g}|^g\le2^g\), even when the phase is nonreal or a power repeats on the grid.
Deleting \(z=1\) costs at most \(\log(1/\eta)\) per factor.
Conjugate pairing makes the *sum* of these logarithms equal at opposite angles, so the average over the \(n\) positive angles has denominator \(p-1=2n\) after deletion.
If \(L\ge D(N-1)+3\), then \(p-1\ge2L\) and \(D\ge R\), giving an upper bound \(\log(2/\eta)/(2(N-1))\le\eta\) for the grid contribution.
Indeed \(\log(2/\eta)\le1/\eta\) and \(2(N-1)\ge\eta^{-2}\).
The remaining logarithmic cost per factor is at most \(2\eta\), using \(-\log(1-\eta)\le\eta/(1-\eta)\) and \(\log((1+\tau)/(1-\tau))\le2\tau/(1-\tau)\).

With this degree budget the moments give \(\mathbb E Y=\mathbb E Gw\le1\), where \(Y=Xw>0\).
The product estimate for \(X\) then gives \(\mathbb E\psi(Y)\le-\mathbb E\log w\le(2R+1)\eta\).
Integrating \(\psi'(y)=1-1/y\) gives \((y-1)^2\le2\max(1,y)\psi(y)\), hence \(\mathbb E|Y-1|\le2\sqrt{(2R+1)\eta}\).
Using \(1/w\) and \(1/w-f\) as bounded above, the two contributions to \(\mathbb E|X-f|\) are at most \(2^{-2R-2}\) and \(2^{-6R-5}\), respectively.
Their sum is below \(1/2\).
Since \(G\le f\) and the constant moment gives \(\mathbb EX=\mathbb EG\), one has \(\mathbb E|G-f|\le\mathbb E|X-f|\).
This contradicts the first changed integer moment.
Finally \(N=2^{16(R+1)}\), and the full multiset of \(2d\) roots supplies \(k=2d\) factors of exponent one, so \(R=2d+4\) and \(D=4d+2\).
This gives the claimed parent-degree bound as well as the bound for a specified binomial representation.
The proof requires integrality and a nonzero leading coefficient for \(H\), and allows zeros of every neutral factor.
