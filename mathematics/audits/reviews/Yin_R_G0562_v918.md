# Descent of phases and inversion on translated sections

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of C345 v777

At 2026-11-21T06:08 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete [C345 v777](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v777.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no part in formulating or proving this version.
The reviewer previously worked on the untwisted graph case and on source inspections for its height and minimal-field inputs.

Let \(H=\mathbf Q^{\rm ab}\) and choose a finite Galois field \(E\) defining every geometric component.
Powering a point by \(-1\) in both coordinates preserves the torus, and multiplying by torsion preserves its coordinate field over \(EH\).
The primitive Bézout combination of the two coordinates after removal of their phases recovers \(\beta\).
Thus the field of the transformed point over \(EH\) is \(EH(\beta)\), including when one primitive exponent vanishes.
Distinct intersecting signed translates have intersection size at most \(d^2\) with the fixed projective degree bound used in the claim.
Their entire \(EH\)-orbit consists of such points and has that same bound.
The tower gives \([H(\beta):H]\le [E:\mathbf Q]d^2\) in that case.
The [section height bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives \(h(\beta)=O_F(1/n)\), so the [abelian relative inequality](../literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#ad07-u001) puts all long directions beyond that fixed bound.
The finite components all have rank-two support: the conjugate binomials of a one-dimensional torus coset would all have parallel exponent differences.
Equations of a stabilized component have the same support after translation, so they can differ only by a scalar.
Two independent exponent differences constrain both multipliers to a finite subgroup.
The identifications between a chosen signed pair of components form one coset of that subgroup when nonempty.
Taking the least common multiple of all torsion coordinate orders over both signs and all pairs gives the required uniform integer \(B\).

If \(M\) kills every torsion quotient of a conjugate of \(\beta\) by \(\beta\), the stabilizer of \(\beta^M\) is the stabilizer of its class modulo torsion.
Every power field contains its fixed field, and taking the \(M\)-th power itself realizes their intersection \(K_*\).
For any such automorphism the translating factors carrying \(P\) to its conjugate combine the transformed original phases and the corresponding torsion quotient to the powers \(p,q\).
Both factors are killed by \(B\).
It follows that \(x^B,y^B\) lie in \(K_*\).
Conversely the field of any representative of a nonzero rational multiple of the class contains \(K_*\), as one sees directly by taking stabilizers.
This proves the equalities for every indicated coordinate power and every Bézout representative, with exactly the stated restrictions on zero exponents.
The latter representative differs from \(\beta\) by torsion, so its powered height is \(Bh(\beta)\).
Dividing powered coordinates by \(\beta^{pB},\beta^{qB}\) descends the individual phase powers to \(\mathbf Q(\beta)\), while cancellation of \(\beta\) gives the mixed phase power in \(K_*\).
The order bounds follow, since the image of any root of unity under a real embedding must be a sign.

Over \(H\), all phases are fixed.
An automorphism fixing \(\beta^j\) has \(\sigma\beta/\beta=\lambda\) with \(\lambda^j=1\), and the two component multipliers are now just \(\lambda^p,\lambda^q\).
Hence \(\lambda^B=1\) since \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), and \(\sigma\) fixes \(\beta^{\gcd(j,B)}\).
The converse inclusion of stabilizers is immediate by powering.
The relative extension degree is at most this gcd by the polynomial of the indicated power.
For \(\alpha=(x^iy^l)^B\), every positive ordinary power generates \(K_*\), so the particular torsion exponent \(e(\alpha)\) in [Amoroso and David's Proposition 2.4](../literature/AD22/Yin_U001.md#power-field) satisfies the required equality over \(H\).
The normal-closure degree in that proposition is exactly \([N_*:H]\).
Its logarithmic height inequality gives first \(\log(16[N_*:H])\gg_F\log n\) and then the claimed factor \((\log\log n)^{1/3}\).
The normal closure of \(H(z)\) contains every \(H\)-conjugate of the subfield \(HK_*\).

When \(\sigma\beta=\beta^{-1}\), multiplication of \(\sigma P\) by \(P\) yields the two coordinatewise phase products in (3).
The signed comparison applies to this inverse curve, making both products \(B\)-torsion and establishing the action on the phase powers in the ordinary field.
Reciprocity of \(z\) implies reciprocity of \(\beta=z^g\) with the same automorphism.
In the absence of any signed inverse identification, high relative degree therefore rules out reciprocal roots.
The nonunit floor and [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) handle those roots.
All remaining primitive norms are bounded and [the uniform bounded-direction bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement) supplies a gap with no restriction on phases or common dilation.

In the example the constant and linear coefficients in \(Y\) have no common \(X\)-root, giving absolute irreducibility.
An inverse torsion translate of the curve would take opposite coefficients to one another up to one scalar, whose modulus is one by multiplication around the finite support permutation.
The moduli at \(1,XY\) differ, and the ordinary stabilizer is trivial by the two basis differences.
The intermediate-value argument produces the real nontorsion point for each positive \(n\).
For a fixed sufficiently large \(n\), replacing its chosen primitive generator \(t\) by \(\omega t\) leaves the relative degree over \(H\) unchanged.
The coordinate field remains \(\mathbf Q(t)\), whereas the prescribed power of \(\omega t\) need not lie in this real field.
This verifies the counterexample to using a uniform power of the unadjusted generator.
