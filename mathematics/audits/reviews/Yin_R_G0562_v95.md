## Local Fourier growth, phase and the weighted cancellation estimate

At 2026-11-17T00:22 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the whole C345v95 submission dated 2026-11-17T00:19.
The reviewer received the fixed version without development dialogue, author explanations or a previous verdict for this proof and had no role in developing this version.
The reviewer had previously contributed to the single-conductor Riesz test used as an input and to the monomial-summand case.
The independent finding here concerns the argument of C345v95 and its use of the stated dependencies.

For the weighted growth estimate, dilation by \(N=n/\operatorname{rad}(n)\) restricts the Fourier support to multiples of \(N\) without increasing any coefficient.
The expression \(-c_q(h)/(2|h|)\), with \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)\), has modulus at most \(1/2\) since the local factors of the Ramanujan sum give \(|c_q(h)|\le(q,h)\le|h|\).
The prescribed ranks contain at least \(r/5\) primes for large \(r=\omega(n)\), each at least \(r/4\), and at least \(r/2\) larger primes are outside these ranks.
For \(s=\lfloor(r/200)^{1/3}\rfloor\), if there are fewer than \(s\) successively separated groups, one group has at least \(r/(5s)\) primes.
One of its \(12s\) subintervals contains at least \(r/(60s^2)\ge s\).
Both choices give \(|h_J|\ge q/(2p_*)\) for odd subsets, by domination by the first reciprocal or by comparison with the nonzero odd sum of the signs, where \(p_*=\min J\).
The positive singleton coefficient is at least \(\varphi(q)/(2q)\), and the degree-\(j\) mixed coefficient gains \(j-1\) powers of \((r/4-1)^{-1}\).
Expanding the test with \(s^{-j/2}\binom{s}{j}\le s^{j/2}/j!\) gives the asserted \(O((\varphi(q)/q)s^{3/2}/r^2)\) total error for \(j\ge3\).

For a fixed nonzero frequency \(l\) of the weight, the translated coefficients vanish unless \(N\mid l\).
Otherwise \(e=l/N\ne0\) is bounded, and the prime-to-\(\prod_{p\in J}p\) part of \((q,h_J+e)\) divides \(e\) by squarefreeness.
The unused larger primes ensure \(|h_J+e|\ge q/(4p_*)\) for all large \(r\).
In the resulting bound \(2|e|p_*/\prod_{p\mid q,\ p\notin J}p\), one unused larger prime absorbs \(p_*\), leaving at least \(r/2-1\) factors of size at least two.
This beats the total expansion weight \(e^{\sqrt{s}}\).
The positive constant coefficient of the nonnegative nonzero weight retains the diverging unshifted correlation, since \(\varphi(q)/q\gg_\epsilon r^{-\epsilon}\).
Boundedness of the test and of \(\int WL_n\) then gives divergence of \(\int W L_n^+\) by \(2L_n^+=|L_n|+L_n\).

On the circle the phase difference \(\psi\) satisfies \(e^{2i\psi}=z^{d-2k}T(z)B(1/z)/(T(1/z)B(z))\) off a finite set, by the reciprocal identity for \(\Phi_n\).
Subtracting one gives the stated formula for \(|\sin\psi|\).
If \(R\ne0\), a monomial multiple of \(R\) is a nonzero integer polynomial, so its logarithmic measure is nonnegative.
Projection perpendicular to the other summand bounds \(|P|\) below by the larger summand modulus times \(|\sin\psi|\).
The latter logarithm has mean at least \(-\log2-m(T)-m(B)\), and the fixed factors' negative logarithmic parts are integrable.
This supplies divergence independently of the shift on this branch.
If \(R=0\), translation identifies the nonempty supports of \(T(z)B(1/z)\) and its reversal, both contained in \([-\max(\deg B,\deg T),\max(\deg B,\deg T)]\).
It follows that \(|d-2k|\le2\max(\deg T,\deg B)\), \(k\to\infty\) and \(D=\deg P\le d+O_{T,B}(1)\).

On the proposed bounded-measure subsequence, the order of vanishing at zero is eventually the fixed order of \(T\) and removing that power gives \(F(0)=c\ne0\) independent of \(n\).
Here \(\Phi_n(0)=1\) for \(n\ge2\).
For a fixed \(h>0\), the first \(h\) coefficients of \(F\) eventually receive no term from the translate, and only binomials of exponent at most \(h\) can change the corresponding cyclotomic Taylor coefficients.
Thus the coefficient of \(\log(F/c)\) is bounded at \(h\).
In the \(h\)-th Fourier coefficient of the boundary logarithm the exterior roots and unit roots give one half of their Taylor contributions.
For each interior root, replacing \(\alpha^{-h}\) by \(\bar\alpha^h\) changes the sum by at most \(2h b_\alpha e^{h b_\alpha}\), since the arguments agree and the radii are \(e^{h b_\alpha}\) and \(e^{-h b_\alpha}\).
The sum of the \(b_\alpha\) is \(m(P)-\log|c|\) by Jensen and is bounded.
Thus both signs of every fixed frequency and the constant coefficient are bounded, which controls the integral against the fixed trigonometric weight.

On the complement of the neighborhoods of the circle roots of \(B\), each positive curvature contribution from \(-\log|B|\) is at most a fixed multiple of \(\delta^{-2}\), including multiplicities.
The off-circle contributions have bounded second derivatives.
With \(\delta=C/\sqrt d\), the adverse total is at most half the circle-root curvature \(S\ge d/4\) after choosing \(C\) and then \(d\) sufficiently large.
The further estimate \(S\ge4/\ell^2\) uses the nearer endpoint of the original arc and survives the deletion of the neighborhoods.
The bound \(-u''\ge2/\ell^2\) gives variation at least the squared length divided by \(\ell^2\) on each monotone subinterval, even if it terminates at a neighborhood boundary.
There are at most two monotone branches on each of boundedly many remaining pieces of an original arc.
The summed band length is \(O_B(\sqrt t)\) in normalized measure.
Integrating the exponential band bound and expanding the nonnegative logarithmic series yields finite total cancellation loss, also after multiplication by the bounded nonnegative \(W\).
Common circle roots of \(B\) and \(T\Phi_n\) enter the removed neighborhoods and cause no missing intervals.

The product defining \(W\) is a fixed nonnegative nonzero trigonometric polynomial.
Its fourth-order zero at each excluded center gives \(\sup_E W=O_B(\delta^4)\) even where neighborhoods overlap.
The bound \(L_n^+\le d\log2\) follows from the \(d\) unit roots.
For each linear factor of \(P\) divided by \(\max(1,|\alpha|)\), its logarithm has mean zero and positive part at most \(\log2\).
The mean negative part is therefore also at most \(\log2\), and the remaining constant \(M(P)\ge1\) gives \(\int(\log|P|)^-\le D\log2\).
Since \(D=O(d)\), the two weighted errors on the omitted neighborhoods are \(O_{T,B}(d\delta^4)=o(1)\).
When \(B\) has no circle roots the weight is one and the omitted set is empty.
The lower bound on the complement and the weighted growth now contradict the bounded Fourier integrals on the remaining phase branch.

For bounded \(\omega(n)\) the Möbius product has at most \(2^{\omega(n)}\) numerator and denominator binomials combined, irrespective of the prime-power exponents.
After multiplication by the denominator, the two fixed factors contribute boundedly many terms and the shift only translates exponents.
The product is integral, remains nonzero by the input condition, and has the original measure because all denominator binomials have measure one.
The accepted Dobrowolski–Smyth source unit applies to this univariate total logarithmic measure without any irreducibility or height assumption.
Together with uniform divergence this proves the claimed gap.
The review found no defect in the stated range.
