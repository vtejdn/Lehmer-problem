## The joint embedding fraction, the determinant costs and the centralizer test

At 2026-11-24T22:41 UTC an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v66.md#statement) and both proof sections of C0901 v66.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical input without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v66.
Both finite bounds in (1), the fixed-rank estimate (2), the variable-rank estimate (3), and all the original-unit consequences hold with the stated constants and quantifiers.

In a finite containing field, each embedding of the compositum has the same number of extensions, so its fraction of embeddings taking every selected unit to a real value is preserved.
Distinct exponent tuples give distinct squared monomials by multiplicative independence, which also excludes any torsion coordinate.
The Vandermonde product is a nonzero algebraic integer, hence its mean log modulus over the embeddings is nonnegative.
Dividing the row at \(z\) by \(\max(1,|\sigma z|)^{D-1}\) bounds every matrix entry by one.
Thus each Euclidean row norm is at most \(\sqrt D\), giving the claimed \(D^{D/2}\) at every embedding without an independence assumption on the conjugate points beyond distinctness.

At one of the jointly real embeddings all squared monomials are positive, also when some of the individual images of the original units are negative.
For \(u\ge v>0\) the pair denominator is at least \(u\), so the normalized difference is at most \(1-v/u\) and at most \(\min(1,\log(u/v))\).
The difference of the logarithms of any two monomials has absolute value at most \(A_\sigma=2\sum_i(N_i-1)|\log|\sigma x_i||\).
Multiplying the pair estimates accounts for each monomial denominator exactly \(D-1\) times.
The average of \(A_\sigma\) on all embeddings is \(4H\) by the unit product formula, so its average on the joint fraction \(\delta>0\) is at most \(4H/\delta\).
There \(A_\sigma>0\), since a real conjugate of a nontorsion unit cannot have modulus one.
The nondecreasing concave function \(t\mapsto\log\min(1,t)\) consequently gives an average at most \(-\log^+(\delta/(4H))\) per pair on this same set.
For \(\delta=0\) the joint contribution vanishes as specified, while \(H>0\) by nontorsion.

The mean log cost of the row denominators is \((D-1)\sum_j h(z_j)\), since these monomials are units.
The triangle inequality and the exponent sum \(2\sum_j j_i=D(N_i-1)\) give \(\sum_j h(z_j)\le DH\).
After division by the number \(D(D-1)/2\) of pairs, the denominator contributes at most \(2H\) and the complementary fraction contributes at most \((1-\delta)\log D/(D-1)\).
These signs and constants give the first bound in (1).
If \(\delta\ge8H\), it implies \(\delta(\log2-1/4)\le\log D/(D-1)\).
The reciprocal of this positive coefficient is less than three, while \(\delta<8H\) directly gives the other case of the second bound.

For fixed \(s\) and \(N=\lceil(\log(1/h)/h)^{1/(s+1)}\rceil\), both \(sNh\) and \(\log(N^s)/(N^s-1)\) are \(O_s(h^{s/(s+1)}(\log(1/h))^{1/(s+1)})\) as \(h\to0\).
No uniform-in-\(s\) constant for this optimization is needed.
Separately, taking all \(N_i=2\) gives \(D=2^s\) and \(H=\sum_i h_i\), including \(s=1\).

For any conjugate \(a\) of the original unit \(w\), the positive unit \(a\,c(a)\) has logarithm \(2\log|a|\) and height at most \(2m/d\).
Rational independence of any selected such logarithms is exactly multiplicative independence of the corresponding positive units, by clearing denominators and exponentiating.
Hence \(r(w)\) is the maximal possible tuple size.
The joint real fraction of an independent tuple in the finite splitting group equals the fraction used above by embedding extension counts, giving (4) with coefficient \(16s\).
Every \(g\in C_G(c)\) fixes reality of each point, so \(1/[G:C_G(c)]\le p\).
For \(s=2\lceil\log_2d\rceil\) and growing degrees, \(s/\log d=O(1)\) and \(d/(2^s-1)=O(1/d)\).
Thus \(pd/\log d=O(m+1/d)\) with a common bound for all selected independent tuples of that size.
This proves the conditional lower bound, the uniform little-oh statement on shrinking sequences and the asserted divergence of \([G:C_G(c)]\log d/d\).
It also explains why the conclusion requires both enough independent coordinates and the indicated joint-real fraction.
