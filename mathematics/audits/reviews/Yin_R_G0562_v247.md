# Degree under two separated powers and a binary divisor with no rational lift

### Both heights in the two-variable specialization

A term with exponents \((a,j)\) specializes to exponent \(ak+jn\), where \(0\le a\le B\).
At equal \(j\) any distinct exponents differ by at least \(k\).
For increasing \(j\) the increase is at least \(n-Bk\ge k>0\), even when some of the coefficient polynomials vanish identically.
Thus the specialization is nonzero, with separated exponents also at the two ends after removing its initial monomial.
If it has only one term there is no nonzero algebraic root and the assertions about such factors are vacuous.

At any root with modulus greater than one, the leading coefficient has absolute value at least one, and all other powers lie at least \(k\) below it.
The triangle inequality gives \(|x|^k\le L\); the reversed polynomial gives \(|x|^{-k}\le L\) at any interior root.
All conjugates of a unit have total signed log modulus zero, so their absolute log moduli sum to \(2m\), including both members of each complex pair.
It follows that their squared log moduli sum to at most \(2m(\log L)/k\).
Part A of the [original-degree radial bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives at least \(1/(10d)\) for this sum at every nontorsion algebraic unit.
It requires neither reciprocity nor stability of the degree under powers.
This yields the lower bound \(k/(20d\log L)\) in both the vertical and nonvertical cases.

Vanishing of an integral coefficient polynomial at \(\alpha^k\) is invariant under every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
In the nonvertical case the largest nonvanishing index \(j\) is therefore the same for all embeddings.
It cannot be zero, since then the specialized equation would read \(A_0(\alpha^k)=0\).
At \(|z|>1\) the equation \(A_j(u)z^j=-\sum_{i<j}A_i(u)z^i\) gives \(|A_j(u)||z|\le\sum_{i<j}|A_i(u)|\) by division by \(|z|^{j-1}\).
For \(|z|\le1\), the corresponding inequality with \(\max(1,|z|)\) is immediate using all indices.
The sum is at most \(L\max(1,|u|)^B\).
Every \(A_j(x^k)\) is a nonzero algebraic integer, and their product over the original \(d\) embeddings is the nonzero integer norm, of absolute value at least one.
Taking the product of the inequalities thus gives \((n-Bk)m\le d\log L\), with exactly \(nm\) and \(Bkm\) from the two archimedean maxima.
Combining both inequalities yields the displayed degree constant \(1/(\sqrt{20}\log L)\).

If all coefficient polynomials vanish at the power, a nonzero one has positive degree at most \(B\), so \(\delta=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^k):\mathbb Q]\le B\).
With \(e=d/\delta\), the field norm \(\gamma\) satisfies \(\gamma^k=(\alpha^k)^e\), hence is a nontorsion unit of height \(e h(\alpha)\) in a field of degree \(\delta\).
It has \(m(\gamma)\le m(\alpha)\), exactly the [power-field norm identity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v240.md#height).
The same radial floor, followed by the square of the sum of absolute log moduli, gives \(1/10\le [\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q](2m(\gamma))^2\le4B m^2\).
The degree conclusion of the nonvertical branch is not needed for such roots.

### The five-term family and the entire range of offsets

For the five-term family the normalized monomial multiple \(X^kP_l\) is exactly the specialization of \(UV^2-2V^2+UV+U-2U^2\) at \(U=X^k,\ V=X^n\).
The coefficient length is seven, the \(U\)-degree is two, and the linear coefficient in \(V\) is \(U\), excluding the vertical case for every nonzero root.
When \(k\le n/4\), \(n\ge3k\) as required and \(n-2k\ge n/2\).
The general result gives \(d\ge\sqrt{nk}/(\sqrt{40}\log7)\).
When \(k>n/4\), the extreme gaps of \(P_l\) itself are both \(k\), even when the adjacent middle gaps \(n-k=l\) are small.
The radial argument gives \(m\ge k/(20d\log7)\) and \(m\le m(P_l)\le\log7\) by nonnegativity on integral factors, multiplicativity and Jensen's bound.
Thus \(d\ge k/(20(\log7)^2)>\sqrt{nk}/(40(\log7)^2)\).

The parent is monic with constant one, so its monic rational factors are integral unit polynomials.
The [cyclotomic exclusion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts) applies since \(n\) is odd and prime to three and \(l=n-k\) is even.
In detail, the squarefree binary reduction restricts torsion orders to \(3t\) or \(6t\), \(t\mid n\); the equation then requires the \(l\)-th power to have order four or six, incompatible with its odd order.
Every irreducible factor therefore satisfies the nontorsion hypotheses.
Any nonconstant rational divisor contains one such factor and has at least its degree, proving the assertion at its stated generality.

For the special \(n\), the [conductor count](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#conductors) gives \(O(A)\) levels of degree \(O(5^A)\) each and \((5^A)^2/7<n\le(5^A)^2\).
A rational monic factor with reduction \(B_A\) preserves degree under reduction, and so \(d\ll A\sqrt n\).
Together with \(d\gg\sqrt{nk}\) this implies \(k\ll A^2\) with an absolute constant.
For sufficiently large \(A\) the strict bound \(k<5^A/490\) follows regardless of that constant.
The [small-offset trace result](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v246.md#statement) applies on this whole range, with the same even level indices, odd \(k\), coprimality and \(l=n-k\).
When \(3\mid k\), \(2k<5^A/245<\min_t H_t\) and the mod-eight test gives five in place of the required one.
All remaining allowed offsets fail the mod-four test.
The interval proportional to \(\sqrt n\) is an example inside the cited range, not an additional hypothesis.
The unique monic reciprocal dyadic factor still exists for every offset by coprime Hensel factorization and normalized reversal.
The nonexistence conclusion concerns exactly this modular divisor and the specified rational parent.
