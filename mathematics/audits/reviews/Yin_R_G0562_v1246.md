## Multiplicity, the spectral edge and asymptotic sharpness

At 2026-11-25T13:24 UTC a reviewer outside development checked the complete fixed C0901 v168 for Yin, its directly used local statements, and the stated connection to the Parter–Wiener theorem.

Both multiplicity bounds, divergence of the minimum cost at the edge, the complement estimate for a Salem trace, and the connected asymptotic optimum follow within the specified finite simple graph setting.

The reciprocal polynomial is the product, with adjacency multiplicities, of \(z^2-\lambda z+1\).
Its contribution \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|\lambda|/2)\) on each of the two tails follows from the quadratic formula, including the negative tail.
Principal interlacing bounds both tail counts separately.
Their integral with density \(1/\sqrt{s^2-4}\) on \(s>2\) recovers the total cost and justifies its monotonicity for induced subgraphs.
The integrable singularity at the threshold does not charge eigenvalues equal to either endpoint.

For two eigenvectors, summing the antisymmetric edge flow on the component cut off by an edge gives zero on that edge.
Thus all pairs of adjacent nonzero coordinate functionals on the eigenspace are proportional.
If every coordinate functional were nonzero, connectivity would give rank at most one for the coordinate embedding, contrary to multiplicity at least two.
A path from a zero functional to a nonzero one supplies the stated neighbors \(i,j\).
At deletion of \(i\) all original eigenvectors give exactly the kernel of the neighbor functional on the new eigenspace.
Restriction to the component containing \(j\) gives a new eigenvector paired nontrivially, because a tree has only one edge from \(i\) into that component.
The increase is exactly one.
Theorem 1 of Johnson, Leal Duarte and Saiago includes this increase for general Hermitian matrices supported on a tree; the argument reviewed here independently suffices for adjacency matrices. [Published text, p. 353](https://www.aimath.org/WWN/matrixspectrum/JLDS03.pdf#page=2)

Fragmenting a forest \(s\) times adds \(s\) to the sum of the multiplicities, leaving single occurrences in \(r+s\) components.
Every charged component is itself a realizing tree and the others have nonnegative cost.
A connected multiple occurrence needs at least one deletion.
In a general graph the subspace of eigenvectors vanishing on the deleted coordinates has dimension at least \(m-f\) and restricts injectively to eigenvectors of the induced forest.
These facts cover zero surviving multiplicity and disconnected forests.

The Salez input admits each totally real algebraic integer as an individual eigenvalue and does not control the other eigenvalues.
At a given upper cost, v197 bounds the number of branch vertices independently of graph size, and v128 excludes individual tree eigenvalues just above two at that branch bound, allowing arbitrarily long subdivisions and unbounded vertex degrees.
Using a cost threshold greater by one if necessary handles the infimum without assuming that any tree achieves it.
For the decorated path the leaf equation \(ty_i=x_i\) yields the path parameter \(t-1/t\); the positive solution tends down to two along path parameters approaching \(3/2\) from above.
For a Salem trace the derivative of \(z+z^{-1}\) is nonzero at the exterior root.
The irreducible factor occurs with the claimed exact exponent, and subtracting its total log measure \(r\log\tau\) proves the complementary assertion.

For connected sharpness, deleting to a simple occurrence cannot increase cost, so simple realizations approximate the original infimum.
In the joined tree the antisymmetric copy subspace has exactly \(k-1\) copies of the original spectral block; the complementary block has coordinates on the old tree and the new hub with coupling \(\sqrt{k}\) at \(v\).
An eigenvector at \(t\) in that block would have hub value zero by pairing with the simple old eigenvector and would then have old coordinate zero at \(v\), hence would vanish throughout.
The block contributes \(O_T(\log(2k))\) in fixed dimension, using its stated norm bound and the quadratic contribution.
This gives a realization at each multiplicity \(k-1\), and the upper limiting cost per copy can approach \(c(t)\) for each fixed \(t\).
No rate uniform over varying \(t\), nor a gap for the trace source, enters this argument.
