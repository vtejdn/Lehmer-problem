## Exact depth and the contribution of a separated pair

The argument proves the full statement and its steep-section consequence.
At a section zero the derivative condition reduces to \(2A(u)v+u^2=0\), since \(l,v,x\) are units.
Eliminating \(v\) gives \(D(u)=0\), and Bézout's identity recovers exactly one \(x\) for each compatible pair.
I expanded the octic and recomputed its discriminant as \(2^{32}5^{12}23^2 1039^2\).
Its leading and constant coefficients are both \(-4\), so all eight roots reduce to distinct units away from the excluded primes.
Writing \(t=U-U^{-1}\) gives \(4(t^2-9)(t^2-16)=1\) and hence four distinct real nonzero values of \(t\).
Each gives two real roots of \(U^2-tU-1\), neither of absolute value one.
Thus none can equal the Teichmüller representative of its reduction.
Factoring out the simple zero in each of \(D\) and \(U^g-1\) shows that the remaining factor is a unit on the disk, giving the same finite positive depth for both evaluations.

In the unramified ring, raising \(1+p\mathcal O\) to the \(p\)-th power raises the guaranteed valuation of its difference from one by one.
Iteration freezes the horizontal coordinate modulo \(p^{e+1}\) at every integral \(s\in p\mathcal O\).
The derivative \(H'\) has a zero modulo \(p\) at zero, with unit linear coefficient because \(U'(0),U''(0)\) are multiples of \(p\) and \(2A(u)v^2\ne0\).
Successive Newton corrections there converge to the unique critical point, as the integral Taylor remainder after a correction of valuation \(n\) is divisible by \(p^{2n}\).
At that point the chain rule places \(F_V\) in \(p^e\mathcal O\), so replacing \(U\) by \(u_0\) puts \(V+u_0^2/(2A(u_0))\) in the same ideal.
Its square vanishes modulo \(p^{e+1}\), including the boundary \(e=1\).
Completing the square therefore gives the claimed sign and the exact valuation through \(c=e\).

Translation by the critical point gives an integral translated series because the center lies in \(p\mathcal O\).
The unit quadratic coefficient permits the indicated square-root change since \(1/2\in\mathbb Z_p\).
Both that change and the binomial section parameter have integral coefficients and integral inverses, hence converge on the maximal ideal of each finite extension and preserve valuations of differences in both directions.
Only the calculation of the critical value requires divisibility by \(p\) in the unramified ring.
The resulting quadratic has two simple roots with distance valuation \(c/2\), as 2 is a unit.
Every other root reduces elsewhere, giving derivative valuation \(c/2\) at either of these two roots by the monic product formula.
Odd \(c\) gives a ramified quadratic extension of the unramified base, and its involution exchanges both section roots because the inverse series are defined over the base.
Thus both section roots satisfy the same irreducible rational factor.
Each disk contributes \(c\log p\) to the derivative weight and at most \((c/2)\log p\) to the complementary resultant, where a separated pair occurs exactly once.
At most eight disks and \(c\le e\) give the constants 8 and 4 after summing \(e\log p\) over primes dividing \(k\).
