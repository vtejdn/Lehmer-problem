## The degree-twelve reduction and the interface with the small-degree search

At 2026-11-24T23:38 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v79.md#statement) and its reflection-group argument through the final application of the J0190 search.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v79.
The group argument is complete at the final bound \(d\le12\), and its contradiction with the stated J0190 output proves the plastic minimum at argument rank at most one and at rank exactly one.
This finding independently checks the group calculations, including the exceptional counts, and uses the finite polynomial enumeration at its stated interface.

For \(m<\log\theta\), v74 supplies the compressed nonzero class orbit of even cardinality \(d\le42\), with one negative conjugation direction and at most one neutral inverse pair.
Compression distinguishes actual roots from one another modulo torsion, so an orbit size for root classes is the actual degree.
The reflections are a single conjugacy class of \(G\), their primitive normals have squared norm two, and the positive integer \(K\) bounds every root pairing of \(x\).
I checked the cited height inequalities with \(F(u)=u\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/u})\), their factor \(j\) for the single torsion correction and the comparisons with \(\log\theta<0.282\).

In a product of \(t\) components, a nonzero projection to one connected component has a spanning Weyl orbit with sum zero, hence at least \(s+1\) vectors at component rank \(s\).
The component Weyl groups act independently and the support translates cover every component since \(G\) is transitive on the normal lines.
This yields all three estimates in (1), counting inactive components separately for \(p_x\).
For \(s\ge2\), \(q=1\) already gives \(m\ge3F(1/2)>\log\theta\).
At \(t\ge3\), the height bound \(dK<15\) excludes \(q=2\) by \(d\ge18\) and \(q\ge3\) by \(d\ge27\).
At \(t=q=2\) one has \(dK<28\), hence \(s\le4\).
For \(s=2\), the product of two nonzero \(A_2\) orbits has size divisible by nine, and the normal Weyl subgroup has equal-sized orbits on the entire \(G\)-orbit.
The even degree is at least eighteen, and for \(s=3,4\) the orbit already has size at least sixteen.
Thus \(K=1\), forcing two-level \(A_s\) coordinates or a \(D_4\) axis or half-sign vector.
Counting equal-value transpositions or perpendicular \(D_4\) lines gives \(p_x\ge1/3\) and \(m\ge8F(1/3)>\log\theta\).
For \(s=1\) all perpendicular reflections commute, so \(dK<8\) when \(t\ge4\), incompatible with each support size by (1).
For \(t=3\) one has \(dK<10\), and at \(t=2\) the group order is at most eight.

In \(A_{n-1}\), the product selected at coordinate \(i\) and nonzero level \(t\) has class sum \(s_tnt\,e_i^0/(n-1)\), since the other coordinates have equal sums by their permutations and the total coordinate sum is zero.
This guarantees nontorsion and fixation by the actual transpositions avoiding \(i\), giving at least \(P=(n-2)/n\) real embeddings.
The orbit with its negative shows that some positive value occurs on at most \(d/2\) roots, and on at most \(d/4\) if two distinct nonzero magnitudes occur.
The height bound \(h(z)\le s_t m/d\) now excludes every \(n\ge8\), and excludes two nonzero magnitudes at \(5\le n\le7\).
In the remaining cases the zero-sum coordinates are equally many \(t,-t\) and the rest zeros, and \(S_n\) already supplies their full signed orbit.
At \(n=5\) its possible sizes are 20 and 30, at \(n=6\) the sizes through 42 are 30 and 20, and at \(n=7\) only 42 remains.
For a single \(t,-t\) the fixed fraction is at least \(3/10\) and the degree at least twenty.
For three of each sign at \(n=6\), the fixed fraction is \(2/5\) in degree twenty, also giving the contradiction by the direct height bound.

For the remaining \(n=5\) orbit and the four-distinct-coordinate \(A_3\) orbit, permutations within the positive and within the nonpositive coordinates of the log-modulus functional preserve its one-norm when averaged.
Convexity and Weyl invariance decrease its mean absolute pairing with the class orbit under this averaging.
The resulting lower ratio can thus be checked just on a two-level functional with \(j\) entries \(n-j\) and \(n-j\) entries \(-j\), with complementary choices related by sign.
At \(n=5\), \(x=(1,1,0,-1,-1)\) in the rescaled coordinates gives mean absolute entry \(4/5\) and mean absolute sum of two distinct entries \(4/5\).
The functional has mean absolute coordinate \(2j(n-j)/n\) and its pairing with a permutation of \(x\) is \(n\) times the corresponding \(j\)-entry sum.
This gives ratios \(5/2,5/3\), independently verified by exact permutation averaging.
The twelve selected terms have class \(15e_i^0\), so \(h(z)\le9h(w)\) and \(m(w)\ge(30/18)F(3/5)>\log\theta\).

For \(A_3\) use \(d<28\) from v74.
A triple repeat joins two opposite four-point orbits, and \(p_x=1/2,d=8\) excludes it.
Two pairs give degree six.
With one pair and two singletons, the self-negative case forces the doubled value zero and gives degree twelve.
In the other case the two opposite orbits are distinct, hence \(d=24\).
At least one singleton is nonzero, its opposite is absent in that same permutation list and it differs from both the doubled value and its opposite, since the two singleton values sum to minus twice the doubled value.
That selected level occupies \(1/8\) of the full orbit at one coordinate, giving \(m\ge4F(1/2)\).

With four distinct values the orbit of size 24 must contain its negative and the values are \(t,u,-u,-t\), \(t>u>0\).
The normal-line lift for \(e_1-e_2\) has \(j=1\) since there is one perpendicular reflection, and exactly \(1/6\) of its embeddings are real by that twist and the necessary fixation of the oriented class.
The \(c\)-invariant log functional has entries \((A,A,B,C)\).
The real embeddings' absolute radial sum over lines is \(|B-C|\), at most one third of the total by the two copies of \(|A-B|+|A-C|\) from the other four nonneutral candidate lines.
For \(H=h(z)\) and \(R=1/6\), the normalized factors for \(1-z^2\) then have conditional real mean at most \(4H/(3R)\).
The other archimedean factors are at most two, the finite factors at most one, and the normalized product is \(e^{-2H}\).
Taking the \(1/R\)-th power and using \(e^{-2H/R}\ge1-2H/R\) gives \(H\ge1/268\).
Consequently \(24K\le536m<152\), \(K=2t\le6\), and \(t-u\) a positive integer gives \(u/t\le2/3\).
The six sums of two distinct entries are \(0,0,\pm(t+u),\pm(t-u)\).
The two-level ratios at \(j=1,2\) are \(4(t+u)/3,4t/3\), respectively.
The six roots at level \(u\) have class \(8u e_i^0\), giving \(h(z)\le6(u/t)h(w)\le4h(w)\) for the coordinate product.
Hence \(m\ge3F(1/2)>\log\theta\).

For \(D_b\), an axis has perpendicular fraction \((b-2)/b\), and equal absolute values in full support give fraction \(1/2\).
Both have degree at least eight and are excluded already by \(4F(1/2)\).
The even sign changes realize all supported signs with a spare zero and at least half the signs with no zero.
For full support with unequal magnitudes there are at least \(b\) placements of the magnitude pattern.
For \(b\ge6\), these bounds or the two endpoints of \(2^q\binom bq\) on \(2\le q\le b-1\) give degree above 42.
At \(b=5\) only two-coordinate support could still fit, but its zero coordinates imply integral entries and \(K\ge2\), incompatible with \(d\ge40\) and \(dK<71\).
For \(D_4\) the remaining alternatives have \(d\ge24\), hence \(K\le2\).
Three active entries or full support with differing magnitudes give \(K\ge2,d\ge32\), contrary to \(dK<58\).
Two active entries are both signed ones and their entire orbit is precisely the 24 roots, even allowing diagram automorphisms.
For the original compressed unit, reality then occurs exactly at the three perpendicular lines out of twelve since class fixation is actual fixation.
Its real share of total absolute logarithm is at most one third by v74's explicit normal evaluations, so the height floor \(3/152\) applies to this actual unit and excludes degree 24.

For the exceptional diagrams the stabilizer of a vector in a closed fundamental chamber is the parabolic subgroup generated by the chamber walls on which it lies.
Indeed all chambers incident to the point can be joined across incident walls by a generic path on a sufficiently small sphere, and the Weyl group acts simply transitively on chambers.
Since at least one label is positive, that stabilizer lies in the stabilizer of its corresponding fundamental weight, so the latter orbit size divides the Weyl orbit size, which in turn divides \(d\) by normality.
I independently formed the Cartan matrix of each displayed star diagram, inverted it over the rationals to initialize the fundamental weights in simple-root coordinates, and generated reflections there, truncating each orbit at 81.
The counts in node order were \(81,72,81,27,81,27\) for \(E_6\), \(81,81,81,81,81,81,56\) for \(E_7\), and eight copies of 81 for \(E_8\), where 81 means at least 81.
Thus only the two 27-element \(E_6\) orbits could meet the upper bound 42, and no positive even \(d\le42\) is divisible by 27.

The remaining group cases all have \(d\le12\) and at most one circle inverse pair.
J0190's cutoff \(53/40>\theta\) and its exclusion of all cyclotomic-free reciprocal polynomials in this degree range with so few circle pairs apply directly to a compressed irreducible nontorsion unit.
Together these inputs exclude \(m<\log\theta\).
The plastic unit has argument rank exactly one and attains the endpoint by v74.
A nonzero nonunit algebraic integer has absolute norm at least two and thus measure at least two, giving the final extension.
