## The conjugate distribution and its two scalar limits

At 2026-11-17T08:38 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [complete submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v191.md#decision-trinomial-radial-law-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof without its development discussion.
The argument proves the asserted joint weak limit and all its stated consequences.

For a unit-modulus zero \(z\), the equality \(|1+z|=1\) forces \(\Re z=-1/2\).
Both resulting points have order three, whereas \(1+z\) has order six.
Ljunggren's signed trinomial corollary applies with middle exponent one and \(n\ge3\), leaving the entire polynomial irreducible.
It is monic of constant term \(-1\), so the \(n\) distinct roots are the full orbit of a unit.
For \(|z|>\beta_n\), strict increase of \(x^n-x-1\) on \([1,\infty)\) contradicts \(|z|^n\le1+|z|\).
For \(|z|=\beta_n\), equality in the triangle inequality forces \(z>0\), hence \(z=\beta_n\).
Thus exactly one embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\beta_n)\) fixes \(\beta_n^j\) for any \(j\ge1\).
Separability gives relative degree one, so the degree of each power is \(n\).
After division by a monomial, a vanishing polynomial with two terms would yield a rational positive power, even with arbitrary nonzero integer coefficients.
A relation must therefore have at least three terms and length at least three, both attained by \(f_n\).

Every root satisfies \(n\log|z|=\log|1+z|\le\log(1+\beta_n)<\log3\).
The contradiction \(\epsilon\le1-|z|\le|1+z|\le(1-\epsilon)^n\) excludes the disk of radius \(1-\epsilon\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Together these two bounds give uniform convergence of all moduli to one.
The unit-circle norm bound on \(f_n\) gives \(h(\beta_n)=m(f_n)/n\le(\log3)/n\).
The proper algebraic subgroups of \(\mathbb G_m\) are finite groups of roots of unity, so \(\beta_n>1\) suffices for Bilu's strictness condition.
Projection \(z\mapsto z/|z|\) in his orbit theorem gives the desired angular equidistribution.
The alternative [discriminant estimate (3)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) bounds each fixed nonzero normalized Fourier coefficient by \(O_k(\sqrt{\log(2n)/n})+O_k(1/n)\) for these monic irreducible units of bounded measure.
The review used the existing inspections of Ljunggren's and Bilu's theorem statements and checked their applicability here.
The original proofs lie outside its scope.

On the complement of an open arc about \(\pi\), \(|1+e^{i\theta}|\) is bounded below.
Hence \(\log|1+z|-\log|1+e^{i\arg z}|\) tends uniformly to zero there and both logarithms stay in a fixed compact interval.
For a bounded continuous test \(F\), uniform continuity on this compact part permits replacement by the boundary value.
The composition with the limiting graph restricted to the complementary arc has only possible endpoint discontinuities, each of Haar mass zero.
The contribution to the difference on the omitted arc is bounded in the limit by twice \(\|F\|_\infty\) times its normalized length.
Letting that length tend to zero proves weak convergence in the presence of the logarithmic singularity.

The graph crosses \(u=0\) at \(\theta=\pm2\pi/3\), and its positive arc has normalized length \(2/3\).
For example, the finite proportion at \(n=3\) is \(1/3\) because the negative cubic discriminant gives a single real root \(\beta_3>1\) and the other two roots have modulus \(\beta_3^{-1/2}\).
The bounded continuous test \(\min(\log3,\max(0,u))\) agrees with the positive part on every orbit and almost everywhere on the limiting graph.
The factor \(1/n\) in the empirical average cancels the scale \(n\), giving the limit of the total logarithmic Mahler measure.
The integrand is positive near zero and Jensen integration in the last variable gives \(c_*=m(1+X+Y)\).
In the [exterior decomposition](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v190.md#statement) one can take \(P_n=S_n=f_n\), \(B=1\), \(j=1\) and \(T=\varnothing\), so its entire positive limit is the residual \(\delta=c_*\).

Stankov's Theorems 1 and 2 for \(X^n-aX-1\), at \(a=1\), already give both scalar limits in (1).
The exterior fraction there is \(\arccos(-1/2)/\pi\), and his limiting logarithmic integral is \((2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi/3}\log(2+2\cos t)\,dt=c_*\).
His Remark 2.2 also describes the angular dependence of \(|z|^{2n}\).
These passages supply a prior comparison for the present weak convergence proof. [Stankov, arXiv:1401.1688v2, pp. 3–4 and 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.1688v2)
The stated decimal \(1.38135644\) matches Boyd and Mossinghoff's reported multiplicative value.
The review used the value as a source comparison and the integral for the proof.
