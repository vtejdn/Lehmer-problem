**Concentration on a ray.**

In the ordered off-diagonal product the radial factors contribute \(2(d-1)m\), while the \(d\) diagonal logarithms in the smoothed kernel cost at most \(d\log d\).
The factor \(\log(2/(1+\rho))\) from smoothing contributes \(O(d)\) at \(\rho=1-1/d\), giving (4) with an absolute constant.
The original and reflected roots have the same phase.
The Fejér polynomial \(H_T\) is harmonic with nonnegative boundary values and hence nonnegative on the entire closed disk.
On the real interval in the proof, each retained power is at least \(e^{-1}\), so the lower bound is \(e^{-1}(1+2\sum_{k<T}(1-k/T))=e^{-1}T\).
The unit norm gives \(\sum_j|\log|z_j||=2m\), and at most \(2mT\) points are lost in the radial cutoff.
The phase rotation has modulus one, and Cauchy–Schwarz with (4) costs \(O(T\sqrt{d(m+\log(2d))})\), since \(\rho^{-k}\le4\) for \(k\le d\).
This analysis applies to any single prescribed ray, including points of unit modulus.
It imposes no restrictions on the arguments of the conjugates outside that ray.
For \(m<d/4\), the chosen rounded \(T\) has \(d/T+mT\ll\sqrt{d(1+m)}\), and \(m\ge d/4\) is immediate from \(r\le d\).

**House and degree.**
For a nontorsion unit with all exterior conjugates real, their number is bounded by the sum of the two ray counts.
Only these exterior conjugates contribute to the total logarithmic measure, each by at most the log house \(b\).
The same argument gives the stated factor \(q\) for a union of \(q\) rays.
When \(m\le\log(2d)\), division by the ray bound and Dobrowolski's lower bound on the *total* logarithmic measure of this individual nontorsion algebraic integer give the denominator \(\sqrt d(\log(2d))^{7/2}\) in (5).
For \(m>\log(2d)\) division gives \(b\gg\sqrt{m/d}\).
At bounded degree, bounded house gives finitely many possible monic minima, and house one is excluded by Kronecker's theorem.
The positive small-degree constant and the modified logarithms are therefore justified.
At \(m\le H\) the real conjugates number \(O_H(\sqrt{d\log(2d)})\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(M_2\ge m\sqrt{d/r_{\rm ext}}\) for the number \(r_{\rm ext}\) of exterior conjugates, yielding the displayed exponent \(13/4\) in the logarithmic denominator.
This application of the Dobrowolski bound uses its large-degree statement, as does the comparison to the earlier exponent \(1/8\).

**Coefficient agreement and the trace lift.**

Monicity and the unit constant coefficient ensure every irreducible factor is a unit minimum, so a noncyclotomic irreducible factor has an exterior root and qualifies for (5).
Taking that factor's own degree \(d\le D\), the log-house upper and lower bounds imply (1) after using eventual monotonicity and covering the bounded degrees and \(K<2\).

**Cyclotomic comparison grid.**
