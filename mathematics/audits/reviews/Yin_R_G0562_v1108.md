## Exact norm one, orthogonality and divisibility after root extraction

At 2026-11-24T22:08 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v51.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v51.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v51.
The exact norm projection (3), the contraction (4), and the positive integrality of every \(q_i\) hold for the stated primitive Salem inputs of degrees at least four, with no disjointness assumption on their splitting fields.

For every oriented root \(a\), the quadratic extension \(K_a/B_a\) has exactly one split real place, since \(\Delta_a=t_a^2-4\) is positive at the one trace conjugate above two and strictly negative at every other real embedding.
In the totally real compositum \(T=B_aB_b\), each radicand is nonsquare because a negative embedding extends to a real embedding of \(T\).
The set of elements of \(G\) taking one radicand to a positive value is the left coset specifying its unique marked embedding on the entire trace field.
If the two cosets differ, some embedding of \(T\) gives opposite signs, making the radicand ratio nonsquare in \(T\).
If the cosets agree, their stabilizers and fixed fields agree and they specify the same real place of \(B_a=B_b=T\).
Equal square classes would now give \(K_a=K_b\).
After an embedding above this marked place, both inputs yield positive generators greater than one of the very same relative norm-one group modulo signs, by taking inverses as needed.
That group has rank one modulo signs and therefore only one such primitive generator.
The input numbers must agree, and then the images of \(t_a,t_b\) are both that number plus its inverse.
Injectivity gives \(t_a=t_b\), which would make the two inverse pairs identical.
This contradiction covers the case of two different labels from a single input as well as distinct inputs.

Thus the quadratic extensions of \(T\) obtained by adjoining \(a\) and \(b\) are distinct, so \(b\) still has its quadratic minimum over \(TK_a\).
The other root is \(b^{-1}\), and the relative norm in \(TK_a(b)/TK_a\) is exactly \(bb^{-1}=1\).
First taking the norm from \(S\) to \(TK_a(b)\), then down to \(TK_a\) and \(K_a\), gives \(N_{S/K_a}b=1\), including its sign.
For a torsion product of the chosen oriented roots the norm to \(K_a\) therefore leaves \(a^{c_a[S:K_a]}\) torsion, forcing \(c_a=0\).
This applies to each coordinate and proves independence modulo torsion.

With Haar probability on the finite Galois group, the mean of \(X_b\) on \(g\operatorname{Gal}(S/K_a)\) is the log modulus of \(g(N_{S/K_a}b)\) divided by \([S:K_a]\).
It vanishes for \(b\) in any different inverse pair, while \(X_a\) is constant on that coset, proving the cross orthogonality.
For fixed \(a\in\mathcal A_i\) its positive and negative values occur with probability \(1/d_i\) each.
Within a single input at most one label has nonzero \(X_a(g)\) for each \(g\), since only one inverse pair is off the circle.
Hence \(|Y_i|\le1\), and \(\operatorname{sgn}(X_a)=X_a/\log\alpha_i\) allows all mixed terms in the expectation to be removed by orthogonality.
The diagonal terms give \(2\log(\alpha_i)\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_i}|c_a|/d_i\), proving (4) for signed exponents.
Both \(w\) and its inverse are integral by their monic power equations.
The unit product formula gives \(h(w)=\tfrac12\mathbb E|\log|g(w)||\), and the torsion twist contributes zero to log modulus at every embedding.
Thus (4) gives \(m(w)\ge q_i\log\alpha_i\) separately for each used index.

After reversing orientations of the negative coefficients, the stabilizer of \(w\) sends \(u\) to a torsion multiple of \(u\).
Each input's pairs are preserved as a set, since two distinct Salem numbers greater than one cannot be conjugates.
Independence makes the signed coefficient vector fixed, so each supported oriented root is sent to a supported oriented root with the same positive coefficient.
For its stabilizer orbit \(O\), restriction gives \(|O|=[E:\mathbb Q(w)]\) for \(E=\mathbb Q(w)K_a\).
Taking the norm of \(w^n=\xi u\) from \(S\) to \(K_a\) gives
\[
  \bigl(N_{E/K_a}w\bigr)^{n[S:E]}
      =\eta\,a^{c[S:K_a]},\qquad \eta=N_{S/K_a}\xi\in\mu(K_a).
\]
The relative norm of \(v=N_{E/K_a}w\) to \(B_a\) is consequently torsion because \(a\) has relative norm one.
The only roots of unity in the totally real \(B_a\) are \(1,-1\), and at a place becoming complex in \(K_a\) the norm of a nonzero element is positive.
Such a place exists already at Salem degree four, forcing \(N_{K_a/B_a}v=1\).
The rank-one norm kernel, the real embedding of \(K_a\), and primitivity of the transported orientation now give \(v=\pm a^j\).
Comparing nontorsion exponents yields \(j=c[E:K_a]/n>0\).
Since \(d_w|O|=d_i[E:K_a]\), summing these positive integers over supported orbits gives precisely \(q_i\).
This proves the lower bound by the largest \(\log\alpha_i\) actually occurring, also after arbitrary torsion twists and root extractions.
Taking a single smallest positive Salem input attains the finite-family minimum, using exponent \(-1\) if its chosen orientation was the inverse.
For an infinite nonempty family finite support gives the asserted infimum, with no attainment assertion required.
The result determines the measure only for the stated divisible group and leaves the unrestricted Lehmer and Salem questions open.
