## Finding on the complete argument

Both specialization estimates in (1) hold for the specified widths and support size, uniformly in the coefficients and phase.
The nonmonic component gap yields (2) with the displayed \(c_R\) under either of the stated arithmetic hypotheses.

Removing the common coefficient divisor in \(X\) leaves the root multiset unchanged off finitely many fibers and ensures a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree \(R\) on every fiber of the circle.
The squarefree part gives analytic paths between exceptional parameters.
At an exceptional parameter, disjoint neighborhoods of all its spherical roots count all \(R\) nearby roots with the correct multiplicities.
Each path has a unique limiting root there, since these neighborhoods can be shrunk arbitrarily, and the counts match across the parameter.
This also treats drops in degree through infinity.
Folding \(y\mapsto1/\bar y\) on the exterior extends continuously at infinity and agrees with the identity on the boundary.

For the vertical line of real part \(a\), the two polynomials in \(V\) have \(V\)-degree at most \(R\) and \(X\)-degree at most \(w_0\).
At one fixed unit \(X\) of full fiber degree the existence of a common complex root would give \(2a=y_i+\bar y_j\) for two of its finitely many roots.
Excluding these real levels leaves a nonzero resultant of \(X\)-degree at most \(2Rw_0\).
The horizontal line calculation has \(2ia=y_i-\bar y_j\) in its place.
The reciprocal-conjugate polynomial supplies the same counts for the exterior pieces, and the generic levels can avoid zero, the value assigned at infinity.
Each root of a nonzero resultant contributes at most \(2R\) branch-parameter pairs on the closed parameter interval, including both endpoints.
On any partition, integration over the levels strictly between successive endpoint values recovers the sum of absolute coordinate increments.
A level outside the finite exceptional and endpoint sets uses a distinct crossing for each straddling subinterval of a given path.
This bounds the sum of the lengths by \(O(R^2w)\), including when roots coincide with multiplicity or \(w_0=0\).

Fiberwise Jensen gives the stated \(b(t)\) almost everywhere with the original leading coefficient, thus retaining the vertical content in both measures.
Its integrable lower bound and the uniform upper bound for the positive log part justify the iterated integral.
On \(|Y|=1\) each interior root contributes \(|1-\bar vY|\) and each exterior root also contributes its original modulus to \(e^b\).
The periodic function \(\sum_i\bar v_i^j\) has variation \(O(jR^2w)\) by the Lipschitz bound \(j\) on the unit disk.
Its coefficient at frequency \(jn\) is \(O(R^2w/n)\) by integration by parts, with the boundary term cancelling even for permuted paths.
Summing against \(r^j/j\) proves (3).
The identity \(|1-rz|^2-r|1-z|^2=(1-r)(1-r|z|^2)\) accounts for the upper comparison cost \(-R\log(r)/2\).
With \(1-r=u/2\) this cost is \(O(Ru)\), absorbed by the first bound in (1).

The specialization map sends distinct support points to distinct frequencies because a nonzero \(Y\)-difference changes the exponent by at least \(n>w\) before the \(X\)-exponents are added.
Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz give \(\|P_\phi\|_\infty\ge B/\sqrt{k}>0\), with every phase preserving the coefficient moduli.
At almost every point where one folded factor has modulus \(D<v\), the product bound \(e^b\le B\) bounds the polynomial by \(2^{R-1}\sqrt{k}\,v\|P_\phi\|_\infty\).
The measurable-set inequality with imaginary frequencies has no exponential growth term.
Solving it for the normalized measure gives an absolute base constant times \((2^R\sqrt{k}\,v)^h\), \(h=1/(k-1)\).
For \(k\ge3\), splitting the layer integral at \(\epsilon\) bounds it by \(C(2^R\sqrt{k}\epsilon)^h(1/h+1/(1-h))\).
The proposed \(\epsilon\) lies in \((0,1/2)\), makes the sum of all \(R\) layer costs \(O(u)\), and gives precisely the logarithmic growth allowed by the lower comparison in (1).
For \(k=2\) the exponent difference is nonzero in each specialized binomial and the corresponding torus character is nonconstant before specialization.
Jensen therefore gives the same binomial mean on both sides.

For the arithmetic input, a nontorsion horizontal component supplies a nontorsion root on sufficiently fine torsion grids after excluding the proper projections of its torsion subvarieties and the zeros of its end coefficients.
The whole relative Galois orbit in the fiber has size \(D_{\rm ab}\le R\).
Jensen and the finite-place Gauss norm bound its total height by the mean over the rational grid orbit, and truncation transfers the lower bound to \(m_2(F)\).
In dimension one the relative height estimate has \(A_1=16\), exponent \(48\) and constant \(2\exp(64\cdot16^2)\), giving exactly the \(c_R\) in (2).
The finiteness and height inputs here are the recorded statements in [Bil97](../literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001) and [Del09](../literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001).

When every horizontal component is torsion, its rational orbit gives a factor \(\Phi_m(X^vY^b)\) with \(b\ne0\).
Additivity of \(X\)-width in the Laurent domain bounds the width of each such factor by \(w\), so terms in distinct \(Y\)-layers stay distinct at specialization.
In particular \(v+bn\ne0\), and \(\Phi_m(z^{v+bn})\) specializes up to a Laurent unit to a nonconstant product of cyclotomic polynomials.
The hypothesis \(R>0\) supplies at least one such nonunit factor, ruling out an irreducible noncyclotomic specialization in this case.
Any remaining base-only factor retains its own one-variable measure.
Finally \(k\le(R+1)(w+1)\) and \(w\ge1\) bound the error at fixed \(R\) by \(O_R(w^2\log(2n)/n)\), giving the claimed sufficient width.
