### The middle width and the conjugate estimates

Its norm has absolute value at least \(L^d\), while the product bound at the \(d\) embeddings is at most \((2(\|A\|_1+\|B\|_1))^d\exp((nw+1)h)\).
Dividing logarithms by \(dnw\) gives the upper limit \(h/d\) since \(w\to\infty\).

In v450 the short exact columns are chosen before the precision enters the proof.
Writing \(p=w/2-o(w)\) for its truncation index at the new width, the sum of the embedding bounds before subtraction of the safety margin is \(2inhp-d\log L\) at orbit \(i\).
The strict gap for \(i=1\) gives a positive multiple of \(nw\) for both orbits, with conjugate complex embeddings counted twice.
Consequently the same unit approximation absorbs the Hermite denominator, pole separation and binomial coefficient losses, including at poles of modulus one.
Rounding \(Lf\) now gives integral columns \(MN-LW\), with exactly the same support and coefficient bounds.
After either permitted translation, the translated \(MN\) is an ordinary exact column at width \(w\).

Thus the two orbit-specific rows give one perturbation missing both factors with upper slope \(3h\).
The fixed-column-width estimates allow successive cutoffs as their slope slack decreases.

### Genuineness, coprimality and the two norm bounds
