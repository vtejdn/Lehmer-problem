# Review of the circle norm and the endpoint discriminant bounds

## Finding on the finite inequality and its arithmetic consequences

At 2026-11-16T23:00 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin checked all four mathematical sections of [C345v73](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#statement), submitted at 22:59 UTC, and all its declared dependency interfaces.
The reviewer previously inspected the ordinary inverse-pair permutation action in C345v68 and took no part in developing the present argument.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and scope without development dialogue, an earlier verdict or a requested conclusion.
The finite bound, the root-discriminant consequence and the stated odd-degree signed-group test pass.

At \(z\) maximizing \(|P|\) on the circle, \(P(z)\ne0\) since \(P\) is monic.
The confluent Vandermonde with the \(d\) distinct roots and \(k\) copies of \(z\) has absolute determinant \(|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/2}|P(z)|^k\).
Replacing the coalescing ordinary rows by successive divided differences divides their determinant by the mutual Vandermonde factor and gives the normalized derivative rows in the limit, as in the [endpoint determinant](../../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#endpoint-degree).
The order-\(j\) row has entries \(\binom{\ell}{j}z^{\ell-j}\) for \(j\le\ell<N\), and all its other entries vanish.
Its norm is therefore at most \(\sqrt N\binom{N-1}{j}\), while the product over the ordinary root rows costs at most \(N^{d/2}M(P)^{N-1}\).
Separability gives a nonzero integer discriminant, proving the displayed finite estimate.

Since \(u\log(eN/u)\) increases on \((0,N]\), the sum of the logarithmic binomial costs is at most \(k^2\log(eN/k)\), including the zero cost at \(j=0\).
After division by \(kd\) and \(N\le2d\), this gives the three terms of the claimed normalized bound.
With \(k=\lfloor\tau d\rfloor\), \(L=o(d)\) gives a limiting upper bound \(\tau\log(2e/\tau)\), which tends to zero as \(\tau\downarrow0\).
The monic leading coefficient has modulus at most \(\|P\|_{\mathbb T}\), giving the lower bound zero for the normalized logarithm.
If instead \(\log|P(1)P(-1)|\ge\epsilon d\), that logarithm of the supremum divided by \(d\) is at least \(\epsilon/2\).
With the stated choice of \(\tau\), the third term is at most \(\epsilon/8\), including the floor because \(s\log(2e/s)\) increases for \(0<s\le1\).
The finite test bounds the first term by \(\epsilon/8\), leaving \(2L/k\ge\epsilon/4\) and hence \(L/d\ge\tau\epsilon/16\) once \(k\ge\tau d/2\).

The trace of a root identifies its inverse pair, so \([B:\mathbb Q]=m\) and adjoining one root has relative degree two.
The elements \(1,\lambda\) form an \(\mathcal O_B\)-basis of the order \(\mathcal O_B[\lambda]\) since \(\lambda^2=t\lambda-1\), and the discriminant of this basis is \(t^2-4\).
Locally the index contributes a square, proving the ideal divisibility.
The norm of \(t^2-4\) is \(S(2)S(-2)\), of absolute value \(|P(1)P(-1)|\).
The tower formula therefore gives
\[
 0\le\log\frac{\operatorname{rd}(K)}{\operatorname{rd}(B)}
 \le d^{-1}\log|P(1)P(-1)|
 \le 2d^{-1}\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}.
\]
This proves convergence of the ratio to one when the height tends to zero, and the opposite lower bound on the ratio supplies the stated finite endpoint hypothesis.

For the compressed connected two-exterior case with balanced signed graph, [the balanced-field criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v69.md#field) supplies the imaginary quadratic \(F\).
The [local endpoint estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v69.md#growth) gives \(W(B,F)\le\log|P(1)P(-1)|\), with weight \(2a_2\log2\) when 2 ramifies.
Thus under small height \(a_p\le W(B,F)/\log 2=o(m)\) uniformly over all ramified primes, also for varying \(F\).
Subtracting \(a_p\) from \(\sum_{v\mid p}e_v f_v=m\) gives the stated termwise comparison with \(2\sum_v(e_v-1)f_v\).
Over the maximal unramified local subextension the derivative of an Eisenstein uniformizer polynomial has valuation at least \(e_v-1\), giving the required lower bound for the local different and hence \(2v_p(\operatorname{disc}B)\ge m-a_p\).
For the positive-height implication from \(W(B,F)\ge\epsilon m\), the endpoint-density parameter is \(\epsilon/2\) since \(d=2m\).
The finite estimate handles large degrees, and bounded degree has a positive height gap for the noncyclotomic units by coefficient finiteness.

In the unbalanced case \(q=m-2\) neutral pairs give sign \((-1)^{m-2}\) for \(E\), which is negative for odd \(m\).
Thus the [signed-kernel alternatives](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#discriminant) leave only \(E/\operatorname{disc}(S)\) square for a proper signed action.
The order index squared takes the polynomial discriminant to the same rational square class as the field discriminant, so each prime with odd field discriminant valuation divides \(E\).
This yields the \(o(m)\) logarithmic bound on their product.

Finally the trace field has \(m-2\) real embeddings and one complex place.
In their real coordinates the covolume is \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}(B)|}/2\).
The norm of a nonzero algebraic integer and arithmetic-geometric mean exclude all nonzero lattice points from \(\sum |x_i|+2|z|<m\), so its half-scale translates are disjoint.
With \(u=2|z|\) the radial area element is \((\pi/2)u\,du\), while the \(m-2\) real absolute values give \(2^{m-2}\) orthants.
Integrating over the simplex gives volume \(2^{m-3}\pi m^m/m!\), and dividing by \(2^m\) and comparing with the covolume gives \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}(B)|}\ge(\pi/4)m^m/m!\).
Stirling's formula shows exponential growth, contradicting the proper unbalanced bound for squarefree absolute field discriminant.
A squarefree discriminant also contradicts the balanced valuation bound at a finite ramified prime of \(F\) once \(m\) is large.
This proves eventual full signed action under the specified hypotheses without asserting the existence of such a small-height sequence.
