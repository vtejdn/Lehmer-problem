# Independent review of the coefficient counts with a total excess bound

## Ordinary and polygon-preserving rational splits

On 2026-11-21 in Shanghai an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin inspected fixed C0345v606 and its cited counting, height and dilation estimates.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received the review scope without development discussion or a prior verdict on this version.
Both counting conclusions and the distinction in the four-term example hold as stated.
The ordinary conclusion requires the stated lower bound on the proportion of supports admitting a tree within budget.

At a vertex with largest length \(D\) and sum \(S\), adjoining \((2D-S)_+\le D\) gives total length at least \(2D\).
With a fixed left bracketing, consecutive intermediate values \(t,t+1\) produce intervals from \(|t-x|\) to \(t+x\) and from \(|t+1-x|\) to \(t+1+x\), whose union has no integer gap, including for \(x=0\).
For an old attainable interval \([l,u]\) the next minimum is \(\max(0,l-x,x-u)\), exactly the excess for the new children, and the maximum is \(u+x\).
Consequently each actual output satisfying the polygon inequalities admits a binary extension with the actual edge lengths intact.
Summing the binary output weight telescopes to the product of the incoming weights throughout the refined tree, which bounds the unweighted number of complete descriptions even if a description has several extensions.
The number of choices for at most \(j-2\) deficits is bounded by stars and bars after padding to \(j\), and their weight product is at most \((1+2K)^j\) by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
The other leaf weights, actual edge signs and plane trees each contribute at most an absolute exponential in \(j\).

The output length at each enlarged vertex is bounded by its incoming lengths, giving the subtree mass bound by induction.
Deleting the parent and a child of maximal mass gives an upper bound on the product remaining after deleting the two largest magnitudes.
Every retained edge has a sibling of at least equal mass, so the next retained ancestor has at least twice its mass, including on paths starting at an auxiliary leaf.
The per-unit charge for the logarithm above the scale \(b=1+K\) is bounded by the stated convergent series divided by \(b\), since each half-open dyadic interval occurs at most once.
The total mass is at most \(jb\), and the baseline logarithm is charged at most once per actual child edge.
This proves the claimed bound for the product of all local heights.

An original cut is a signed sum of original unit powers, hence its rational coefficient is an algebraic integer after division by its unit power.
The norm and height bound from C0345v595 applies before any embedding-radius condition is used and gives relative exponents in \([-2U,2U]\).
Fixing all signed coefficients, including the marked output, leaves only relative child positions to choose.
Opposite edge signs and the root translation reconstruct the support, with multiplicities only increasing the upper count.
The sum of the local degrees minus two is \(j-2\) when the subtraction is made at each vertex.
Taking logarithms of the reconstruction bound, the cost per unit of this sum is \(O_\lambda(\log(2+K))=o(L)\), and the assumed loss in support count is also \(o(L)\) per unit.
For the restricted rule all original lengths are one, both pieces of every allowed split retain polygonality, and complementation preserves nondegeneracy.
Splits shorten each list, so iteration gives a complete tree of restricted-terminal vertices with zero excess and the full original lower bound applies.
At a unit-circle conjugate the vector sum at either new vertex directly enforces its polygon inequalities.

A rational output is fixed by every embedding, so the fixed-weight bound in C0345v584 uses nondegeneracy alone even for restricted-terminal lists.
Power-field descent ensures the availability of an independent conjugate eventually and thus forces the maximizing weight to diverge in both counts.
On a nonzero marked ray there are at least two groups of exponents including the output, and cancellation within an entire group would give a proper zero subsum.
The collected Laurent polynomial therefore retains at most \(r\) nonzero terms and the same fixed coefficients on every dilation of the ray.
The determinant bound at degree-preserving powers, or the positive-root bound applied separately by parity at a negative real conjugate, costs \(O(L+\log(2r))\) in the logarithm.
This is negligible relative to \((r-2)L\), including after discarding the single possible all-zero list.

In the quadratic example the trace values at powers one, two and three are respectively \(3,7,18\).
The same-sign pairs sum to \(21\) and \(-21\).
The mixed pairs have norms \(58-21\cdot3=-5\) or \(58-21\cdot18=-320\), so they are nonzero and cannot be rational multiples of powers of this norm-one unit.
No triple vanishes because the complete sum is zero and its complementary term is nonzero.
The sole pair partition giving a rational carrier therefore fails the polygon condition since \(21>7+7\), while the original magnitudes \(7,7,3,3\) satisfy it.
The example establishes the difference of splitting rules at one input only.
