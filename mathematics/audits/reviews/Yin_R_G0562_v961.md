## Independent whole-proof finding

At 2026-11-21T09:51 UTC an independent agent reviewer for Sheng Yin recommends accepting the complete [09:46 UTC submission of v820](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v820.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed proof and questions about its local estimates and consequences, had no role in developing it, and checked the uses of the previously reviewed degree, resultant and modulus estimates at their stated scope.

At either pole the absolute norms of \((-c)^k\) and \(\beta^l\) are \(c^{2k}>1\) and one, so their difference is nonzero.
At any prime over a divisor of \(c\), this difference reduces to a unit, excluding two at both poles.
At a contributing prime its integral valuation equals that of \((-c)^k\beta^{-l}-1\), with both bases local units.
For an odd split prime the completion is \(\mathbb Q_p\), and if \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), a root of \(X^2+1\) lifts to this same field, giving a place of \(K(i)\) with absolute residue degree and ramification index one.
For \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) the original residue degree one satisfies Yu's alternative field hypothesis.
In the displayed bound on printed page 337 of Yu, the global degrees are at most four and the two height factors are each \(O(\log p)\) because the bases range over a fixed finite set.
Taking \(B=3+k+l\) thus bounds each split branch by \(O(pL)\).
The ramified odd primes are 13 for the first pole and 5 for the second, so all local and global parameters of the extension by \(i\) at those places are bounded independently of the exponents.
Restriction divides the resulting integral valuation by the relative ramification index, giving \(O(L)\) at the original prime.

At an inert prime the unique ideal is invariant under conjugation, so both \(\beta^l\) and \((\beta')^l\) agree with the unit \((-c)^k\) modulo \(p^t\).
Their quotient shows that \(\delta^l\equiv1\pmod{p^t}\), and the nontrivial residue Frobenius sends \(\delta\) to \(\delta^{-1}\).
For \(t>0\) the residue order \(r\) therefore divides \(l\) and \(p+1\).
If \(u=1+y\) with \(v(y)>0\), the first binomial term of \(u^m-1\) has strictly least valuation for \(p\nmid m\), and for \(m=p\) its valuation is \(v(y)+1\), strictly below the other terms because \(p\ge3\) and \(v(y)\) is integral.
Iteration gives the claimed lifting formula.
The real absolute value \(|\delta|=\beta^2>1\) shows the norm of \(\delta^r-1\) is nonzero, its logarithmic absolute norm is \(O(r)\), and its inert ideal contributes \(2v(\delta^r-1)\log p\) to that logarithm.
Consequently \(2t\log p\ll p+\log(2l)\), uniformly even when \(p\mid l\).

The two split branches cost \(O(L\sum_{p\le Y}p\log p)=O(Y^2L)\) by the Chebyshev bound multiplied by \(Y\).
Summing the inert estimate over all integers through \(Y\) costs \(O(Y^2+YL)\), and the finitely many ramified contributions cost \(O(L)\) including their logarithmic weights.
These estimates cover all real \(Y\ge2\) and both poles.
The positive integer divisibilities \(m_i\mid b_i\mid N\) transfer the bound for small prime powers to each individual modulus or resultant.
The resultant is the absolute norm of \(Q_i(\beta)\) by its product over the roots of the monic factor, so the two embedding estimates give the asserted uniform total log mass.
For a modulus the cited mass asymptotic additionally assumes \(k=o(d_i)\).
In either applicable case, \(Y^2L=o(d_i)\) preserves the leading term on removing the small primes.
If \(C\) is a common constant in (1), choose \(a>0\) so that \(Ca^2<\min_j\log(\beta_j)/8\).
Then at \(Y=a\sqrt{d_i/L}\) the surviving mass is eventually at least \((\log\beta_j)d_i/4\), a positive proportion of the total.
Indeed \(L\sim\log l\) and \(\log d_i\sim\log l\), so this real cutoff eventually exceeds two and has logarithm \((1/2+o(1))\log d_i\).
Positive mass gives a rational prime exceeding the cutoff, and for the qualifying moduli each such choice splits and is distinct across factor indices at the same pole by their simultaneous pairwise coprimality.
