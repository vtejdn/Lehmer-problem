## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T01:58 (UTC), an agent working for Sheng Yin independently checked the fixed C345 v716 proof and recommends accepting its exclusions, count and residual table.
The reviewer did not develop v716 and received the requested scope and fixed inputs without its proof-development discussion, an author explanation or a prior verdict.
The review covered the original proof, including the delay and valuation restrictions at both residual seeds.
Earlier related work by the reviewer includes v702 on radical primes above the delay and reviews of other mixed-prime restrictions.

**Even seeds.**
The root classification gives \(G_a=PU\), the stated two possible linear cofactor roots, \(a-1<r<a+1\) and \(d\ge n-1\).
Every field radical used here generates \(K\) since a positive power is \(r\), and the degree-preserving Salem signature gives a monic reciprocal minimal polynomial of even degree \(d\).
At the matching prime \(\ell\), Frobenius on the full multiset of roots yields \(S\equiv P\bmod\ell\).
The fixed monic cofactor divides \(X^{n+1}-\epsilon\), and \(\ell\mid a\) therefore gives \(\beta^{n+1}-\epsilon\in\ell\mathcal O_K\).
The dyadic part of v684 uses only \(2,6<n\) in the two trace comparisons, so it applies at every delay in v716.
For even \(a\), \(a^6-a^3\equiv0\bmod4\) gives absence of \(X+1\) from \(U\), and \(a^2-a\equiv0\bmod4\) gives \(4\mid a\).
Writing \(\beta^{n+1}=\epsilon+2u\) gives \(r^{n+1}\equiv1\bmod4\mathcal O_K\).
The original polynomial instead gives \(r^{n+1}\equiv\epsilon\bmod4\mathcal O_K\).
The difference two cannot belong to \(4\mathcal O_K\), since \(1/2\) is not integral.
Thus \(\epsilon=1\), and absence of the root \(-1\) forces even \(n\) and \(U=X-1\).
Direct evaluation yields \(P(-1)=(-2(a+1))/(-2)=a+1\).

For \(y=\beta+\beta^{-1}\), the embedding taking \(\beta\) to \(\beta^{-1}\) has image \(K\), fixes \(y\) and is nontrivial.
The quadratic equation for \(\beta\) consequently gives \([K:\mathbb Q(y)]=2\).
Since \(d\) is even and the reciprocal unit has norm one, \(P(-1)=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1+r)|=N_{\mathbb Q(y)/\mathbb Q}(y)^2\) follows from \(1+r=\beta y\).
Thus \(w=\sqrt{a+1}\) is an odd integer at least three.
Counting the odd integers from three through \(\sqrt{X+1}\) gives the asserted upper bound, including at \(X=3\).
If \(a=2^u\), then \(w-1,w+1\) are powers of two greater than one and differing by two, so \(w-1=2\) and \(a=8\).

For a fourth radical \(\gamma\), \(z=\gamma+\gamma^{-1}\) generates a field of degree \(d/2\) by the same argument.
It contains \(y=z^2-2\), thus it is the original \(F=\mathbb Q(y)\).
For the monic minimal polynomial \(T\) of \(z\), multiplying \(T(\sqrt2)\) and \(T(-\sqrt2)\) gives \(\prod_{z'}((z')^2-2)=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(y)\), including the sign.
Reduction of even and odd powers at \(\sqrt2\) gives \(T(\sqrt2)=A+B\sqrt2\) with integral \(A,B\).
If \(3\mid A^2-2B^2\), reducing modulo three forces both \(A\) and \(B\) divisible by three, so \(9\) divides the norm.
This excludes absolute norm three and hence \(4\mid R\) at seed eight.
The divisor description of the field exponents then gives \(v_2(R)=1\) whenever \(2\mid R\) at this seed.

**Odd prime powers.**
For each place over \(\ell\), normalize \(v(\ell)=1\), and let \(b=v(\beta^{n+1}-\epsilon)\).
The scalar congruence gives \(b\ge1\), and the real embedding gives nonvanishing of this difference.
For \(1\le j<\ell\) the binomial coefficient \(\binom{\ell}{j}\) has valuation one.
The first term has the unique least valuation \(1+b\), since \(\ell b>1+b\) at every odd \(\ell\), even at \(b=1\).
This proves the valuation formula also with ramification.
The equation \(r^{n+1}-\epsilon=\ell^u(r^n-\epsilon r)\) shows \(b\ge u-1\).
At every other finite place integrality is already available, giving the claimed ideal divisibility and the lower bound on the nonzero norm.
Exactly one conjugate of \(\beta\) is exterior, so the product of the individual bounds \(2\max(1,|\beta'|^{n+1})\) is \(2^d r^{(n+1)/\ell}\).
Combining with the norm lower bound and \((n+1)/d\le4/3\) gives (2).

For \(\ell\ge5,\ u\ge2\), splitting off \(\ell^u\) bounds the ratio by \((26/25)^{4/15}5^{1-11u/15}\), using the negative exponent \(1-11u/15\).
At \(\ell=3\) the stated ratio decreases for \(u\ge3\).
The endpoint comparisons are the integer inequalities \(2^{15}26^4<5^{15}\) and \(2^9 28^4<9^9\).
For exponent \(u=1\), the odd prime below the delay must divide \(a(a-1)(a+1)\) with valuation at least two, hence must divide \(a\) to order at least two here.
At a prime equal to the delay v686 supplies that same valuation explicitly when \(\ell\mid a\).
Above the delay \(\ell\ge11\), v698 gives \(\log\beta\ge3(0.427800)/4\) at its accepted numerical scope.
The decreasing upper bound \(\log(\ell+1)/\ell\le\log(12)/11\) is strictly smaller, for instance by \(\log(12)<3\).
At the single remaining odd seed \(9\), applying the below-delay trace valuation to \(3^{v_3(R)}\) gives \(v_3(R)\le1\), and divisibility by three gives equality.

**The delay restrictions.**
For \(a=8,\ n=2m,\ \epsilon=1\), division by \(X-1\) gives the two outer coefficients one and every intermediate coefficient \(-7\).
The trace fields of \(r\) and \(\beta\) agree because \(x=r+r^{-1}=y^2-2\) and both trace generators have degree \(m\).
The reciprocal expansion of \(P\) gives the stated monic degree-\(m\) polynomial \(f\) of \(x\), so \(f\) is its minimal polynomial.
The Chebyshev recurrence with \(t_0=2,t_1=X\) gives \(t_j(-2)=2(-1)^j\) and \(t'_j(-2)=(-1)^{j-1}j^2\).
Summing the alternating squares gives \(f(-2)=(-1)^m9\) and \(f'(-2)=(-1)^{m-1}m(9m-7)/2\).
For \(3\nmid m\), the derivative is a three-adic unit, and the root \(-2\) modulo nine lifts to an actual three-adic root \(t\equiv-2\bmod9\).
The embedding of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) given by this root also embeds \(y\), since the trace fields agree.
Taylor expansion modulo \(27\) gives \((t+2)/9\equiv2/[m(9m-7)]\equiv1/m\bmod3\).
This unit must be a square, being the square of the image of \(y/3\).
Thus \(m\equiv1\bmod3\) whenever \(3\nmid m\), excluding \(n\equiv4\bmod6\).
As an ancillary check, the reviewer constructed \(f\) by the recurrence at every \(4\le m\le38\), verified both evaluations exactly, and enumerated the lift modulo 27 whenever the derivative was a unit.

Still at seed eight, reduction modulo three gives \(\overline P=(X+1)(X^n-1)/(X-1)\).
If \(b=v_2(n)\ge3\), every primitive \(2^b\)-th root occurs with the odd multiplicity \(3^{v_3(n)}\): removal of the three-power makes the binomial separable, and neither linear factor affects this order.
All square preimages have exact order \(2^{b+1}\).
The Frobenius orbit size at order \(2^b\) is \(\operatorname{ord}_{2^b}(3)=2^{b-2}\), from \(v_2(3^2-1)=3\) and successive squaring of numbers one modulo eight.
The orbit at the source order doubles, so its contribution per target point is even, including when the source polynomial has repeated roots.
The exact multiset equality \(S^{[2]}=P\) rules out \(8\mid n\).

At \(a=9\) the reduction modulo two gives \(G_9=(X+1)(X^n+1)\), and \(U\) removes only roots reducing to one.
For \(b=v_3(n)\ge1\), each primitive \(3^b\)-th root in the target has multiplicity \(2^{v_2(n)}\), prime to three.
Cubing preimages of this order requires exact order \(3^{b+1}\).
Here \(\operatorname{ord}_{3^b}(2)=2\cdot3^{b-1}\) by \(v_3(2^2-1)=1\) and successive cubing, so the source orbit contributes a multiple of three per target point.
This contradicts \(S^{[3]}=P\) and excludes \(3\mid n\) for either sign.

If \(n+1=3j\), apply the characteristic-polynomial congruence modulo nine to the ninth and third powers of the companion matrix of \(S\).
This gives \(P^{[3]}\equiv P\bmod9\), and restoring \(U\), fixed by odd powering, gives the analogous coefficient congruence for \(G_9\).
The coefficients of the powered polynomial are integral universal polynomials in the original coefficients, so reduction modulo nine commutes with this operation.
Cubing the roots of \(X^{3j}-\epsilon\) gives every root of \(X^j-\epsilon\) three times.
The coefficient at \(X^{2j}\) in \((X^j-\epsilon)^3\) is \(-3\epsilon\), distinct modulo nine from zero in \(X^{3j}-\epsilon\).
This excludes \(n\equiv2\bmod3\) and leaves precisely the stated necessary class at seed nine.
The proof makes no existence assertion in either residual row.
