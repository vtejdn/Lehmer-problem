## Distinct integral remainders and their pairwise distances

At 2026-11-20T20:43 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently checked the complete [submitted proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#statement) and the fixed dependency sections used below.
The reviewer received the proof and its dependencies without development dialogue, additional explanations or earlier findings on this submission.
The finite inequality and both lower limits hold under the stated hypotheses.

Parseval and the pointwise bound by \(\max_{\mathbb T}|P|\) bound the sum of the squared integer coefficients on each energy sublevel.
That finite set yields an actual minimizer, and translating its first occupied term to zero preserves its energy.
At each stripping step \(P(0)=\pm1\) gives an integral nonzero quotient coefficient \(q_i\).
The top coefficient of \(T_i-q_iP\) is \(-q_i\) in degree \(d\), so division by its largest power of \(X\) preserves nonvanishing and reduces the degree below \(d\).
If two sign-normalized iterates agree, the telescoping relation gives \(P\mid(1\pm X^{u_j-u_i})T_i\) for \(j>i\).
The binomial has all its roots on the circle, so every factor of \(P\), with full multiplicity, must divide \(T_i\), contradicting its degree.
In particular the argument also separates the iterates of a reducible \(P\).

For the first \(N\) iterates set \(B=\max_i\|T_i/P\|_2\).
The constant-coefficient evaluation gives \(|q_i|\le B/\sqrt C\), and strict increase of \(u_i\) makes the norm of the first \(i\) deleted monomials exactly \((\sum_{j<i}q_j^2)^{1/2}\).
The triangle inequality in (4) therefore gives \(B\le\sqrt L+B\sqrt{(N-1)/C}\).
The restriction \(N-1<C\) makes division by \(1-\sqrt{(N-1)/C}\) legitimate.

The extremal polynomial \(S_*\) of constant one is orthogonal in the weighted norm to every short polynomial of constant zero.
For \(a_i=S_i(0)\ge1\) this gives \(\langle S_i/P,S_*/P\rangle=a_i C\), including for \(a_i>1\).
Consequently the vectors \((S_i-S_*)/P\) have squared norms at most \(B^2-C\), while every pairwise difference has squared norm at least \(L\) by nonvanishing and integrality.
The Hilbert space identity for the sum of squared pairwise distances gives \((N-1)L/(2N)\le B^2-C\), with both sides still ordered when \(N=1\).
Substitution of the bound on \(B\) gives (1), whose denominator is positive since its first term is at least one and its subtracted term is less than \(1/2\).
For \(N=\lfloor C^{1/3}\rfloor\) that denominator equals \(1/2+O(C^{-1/3})\) as \(C\to\infty\).
Since \(mC\to1/4\), this gives the claimed short bound with scaled lower limit \(1/2\).
Translation applies it to every integral Laurent numerator of width below \(d\).

For larger widths, division at the two ends gives a nonzero integral remainder \(R\) of degree less than \(d\), since the original numerator is indivisible.
The two selected support counts differ by at most one.
The projection bounds give \(E\ge e^{-2mn}\|R/P\|_2^2\) for their maximum \(n\) and \(E\ge2k e^{-2mk}\) when both counts reach \(k\).
With \(k=\lfloor1/(4m)\rfloor\), the range \(n\le k\) thus gives \(mE\ge e^{-2mk}mL\), and \(n>k\) implies both counts reach \(k\).
The two lower limits coincide at \(e^{-1/2}/2>1/4\), and the short-width bound is stronger.
This rules out the hypothesis of approach to \(1/4\) along any such sequence.
The argument establishes lower bounds conditional on the existence of shrinking denominators and gives no example attaining either constant.
