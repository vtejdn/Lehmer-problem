## A uniform lower bound through cubic slopes

At 2026-11-20T09:25 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently inspected the complete [statement of C345 v506](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#statement) and its four proof sections.
The reviewer had no role in developing this argument and received the fixed claim and review scope without development dialogue, author explanations or earlier findings about this version.
Both inequalities hold with absolute constants in the two stated ranges, and the positive lower limit for each fixed cubic-slope constant follows.

In the [localization argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#radial), inversion is legitimate even for a nonreciprocal factor because it preserves the parent equation.
For \(R=r/s\ge2\), the inequality \(1-|v|\le2e^{-w}\) forces \(|v|>1/2\) for \(w\) large, whence \(se^{-w}\ge w/4\).
The linear Taylor term on the proposed circle has modulus at least \(6e^{-Rw}\), while the constant term is at most \(3e^{-Rw}\).
The remainder divided by \(e^{-Rw}\) is at most \(288\exp(2sD)e^{-(R-1)w}\), with \(sD=12e^{-(R-1)w}\).
One absolute lower cutoff therefore suffices for Rouché's theorem and for \(D<1-|v|\).
The logarithm of the modulus varies by \(O(D)\) there, giving \(w/2\le sL_j\le3w/2\).
Since \((R-1)w\ge rL_j/3\), the disk estimate implies (2), while \(1-|v||v'|\ge1-|v|\gg L_j\) gives (3) after enlarging the same absolute cutoff.
This assigns each large-radius point without a separation hypothesis between different seed zeros.

For the [projected discriminant](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#discriminant), a common zero of the seed and its reversal would be a primitive cube root with \(z^s=-z^2\), impossible because the latter has cube \(-1\).
The section equation and Bézout therefore recover \(x\) from \(x^a\), so all projected conjugates remain distinct.
The endpoint coefficients make \(x\) an algebraic unit, and the unit product formula gives \(\log M(Q)=am=\sum_{|x|>1}L\).
[Dobrowolski's Lemma 1](../literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) applies to the monic associate of the six-term parent and its integral noncyclotomic factor, giving \(m\ge\log(1+0.31/6!)d/N\) by concavity.
In particular \(d/r\ll h\) uniformly for \(r\ge2s\).
The [reflected Vandermonde argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#collision) uses successive one-variable maximum modulus bounds on the product of reflected factors and then the ordinary boundary Vandermonde and Hadamard bound \(d^d\) for its square.
For points on the same strict side of the circle the disk ratio supplies a factor at most one, and for opposite sides the normalized distance is exactly the reflected factor.
Counting each occupied unordered pair once and squaring the ratio \(e^{-rL_j/4}\) yields \((r/4)\sum_j L_j k_j(k_j-1)\le d\log d+2(d-1)h\).
Adding \(\sum_jL_jk_j\le2h\) yields (4) with an absolute constant independent of the assignment cutoff.

In the [factor moment estimates](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#moments), the [Jensen disk count](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#poles) applies at radius \(e^{-t}\) to every interior seed zero because \(|1+\zeta_j|=e^{-sL_j}\).
Summing the tail by integration, including the endpoint term at \(t\), gives \(\sum_{sL_j\ge t}L_j\ll(1+t)e^{-t}\), and integrating the full count against \(3t^2/s^3\) gives \(\sum L_j^3\ll s^{-2}\).
For \(h\ge\log(2d)\), Cauchy–Schwarz applied to (4) bounds the measure above cutoff by \(O(h\sqrt{(1+A)e^{-A/2}})\).
A single absolute \(A\) leaves at least \(h/2\) below cutoff, giving \(h\ll k/s\).
A second application with \(\sum L_j^3\) bounds the sum of squared exterior radii by \(O(h/s)\), including the small radii by \(L^2\le (A/s)L\).
For a reciprocal factor, inversion doubles the exterior contribution in the [all-root square inequality, part A](../../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement), with scale factor \(a^{-2}\) and both members of a complex pair counted.
This gives \(1/10\ll dh/(a^2s)\ll rm^2/s\).
For a nonreciprocal factor [Smyth's theorem](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) applies to a nontorsion algebraic integer and gives \(m\ge\log\theta\), which suffices for \(r\ge2s\).
In each case \(k/a\gg sm\) gives the claimed count.

For the [small-measure range](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#small), a nontorsion unit has degree at least two.
[Voutier's Corollary 2](../literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) and \(am<\log(2d)\) give \(a\ll\log^4(3d)\).
Thus \(N\ll r\log^4(3N)\), so \(\log(3N)\le\log r+4\log\log(3N)+O(1)\) implies \(\log(3N)\ll\log(2r)\), absorbing the bounded range into the constant.
The [elementary radius and count bounds](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#count) use \(u=s\) throughout \(r\ge s\).
The radius estimate gives \(k/a\gg sm/\log(2s)\) and hence the stated logarithmic lower bounds.
Writing \(r_0=Cs\log^8(2s)\), the function \(\sqrt r/\log^3(2r)\) increases above \(r_0\) once \(\log(2r_0)>6\).
For fixed sufficiently large absolute \(C\), \(\log(2r_0)\ll\log(2s)\), and evaluation at \(r_0\) gives \(\sqrt{r/s}\gg\log(2s)\log^3(2r)\) for all \(r\ge r_0\).
This proves both estimates there, including the low-measure case.
Below \(r_0\) the earlier unconditional count gives \(k/a\gg s/\log^{11/2}(2s)\) by bounding \(\sqrt{s+r}\ll\sqrt{s}\log^4(2s)\).
Above \(r_0\) and through \(Bs^3\) the new bound gives \(k/a\ge c/\sqrt B\), so sequences crossing the threshold arbitrarily often satisfy the same positive lower-limit conclusion.
