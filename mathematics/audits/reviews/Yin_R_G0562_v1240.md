## Both finiteness statements

At 2026-11-25T10:48 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the complete submitted G0345 v894 for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its four dependencies without development dialogue, author explanations or previous opinions on this proof.
Both conclusions follow under the stated hypotheses.

For any realization, the ratio \(H(h)/Q(h)\) is positive, and the signed evaluations give the partial fractions \(H/Q=1-\sum_i r_i/(x-t_i)\) with \(r_i>0\).
Since \(h-t_i\le4+h\), evaluation at \(h\) bounds the sum of the \(r_i\) strictly by \(4+h\).
Multiplication by \(x-t_e\) removes the chosen pole and multiplies each other residue by \(t_i-t_e\), whose absolute value is at most four.
Thus the total absolute residue is less than \(16+4/6<17\), including deletions of either endpoint.
Shifting by two carries the remaining poles into \([-2,2]\), preserves their distinctness and gives monic integral numerator and denominator with degree difference one.
Division meets the integral linear quotient and proper remainder hypotheses of G0880 v1.
Its signed-residue conclusion gives a common positive gap for \(m(S)\).
The negative trace roots lift to the circle, and the positive trace root in \((0,h)\) gives a single exterior root strictly between \(1\) and \(p/q\).
Consequently \(0<m(S)<\log(p/q)\), excluding unbounded \(q\) for all these deletions.
An integral \(Q\) has the required integral quotient by \(x\).

On the exact extrema the interior factors come from the monic recurrence for \(U_k((x+2)/2)\), so the first conclusion applies to the entire grid.
Degree two is excluded by the sign simplex.
With \(D=2(n-1)\), multiplication by \(-\zeta^{-1}\) gives \(-(-1)^j H(u_j)>0\) in the first half of the cycle, and reflection preserves the sign in the second half.
The normalized Fourier transform of the integral coefficient function of \(R\) is \(D^{-1}R(\zeta^{-1})\), so its norm equals the average of the values of \(R\).
The average selects the two reciprocal coefficients of \(S\) at indices \(1,D+1\) with a minus sign.
The sum of the nodes is \(-2n\), hence \([x^{n-1}]H=2n-\sum_i r_i\) by expansion at infinity.
In the lift the leading trace power contributes \(-2n\) at reciprocal degree \(2n-1\), giving \(s_1=-\sum_i r_i\in\{-4,-3,-2,-1\}\).
The norm is therefore at most eight.

Sanders' theorem at this fixed norm allows repeated signed indicators and bounds their total number absolutely.
A coset of index \(d_i\mid D\) contributes precisely the claimed geometric polynomial with representative \(0\le v_i<d_i\).
The top two coefficients and constant coefficient of \(S+zR\) give the displayed quadratic quotient by \(z^D-1\), since \(\deg(zR)\le D\).
After clearing denominators, the quadratic contribution gives at most \(6\cdot2^l\) labeled monomials and the \(l\) cosets give at most \(l2^l\).
All coefficients have absolute value at most four before collection, including when cosets or exponents repeat.
The largest possible exponent is bounded by \(D+2+d=2n+d\) because \(v_i<d_i\).

The pole condition gives \(q^{2n}S(p/q)=(pq)^nH(h)=K\).
Homogenizing the two sides therefore produces total degrees \(2n+d\) and \(d\), and \(W(p/q)\ne0\) since every \(d_i\ge1\).
In a partition of the labeled terms into minimal vanishing subsums, the nonzero total of the low-degree side forces at least one mixed subsum.
Division by a low-degree term gives a unit equation in which every nonempty subsum on the left is nonzero by minimality.
For each \(q\) there are at most ten choices of scalar coefficient per position, and the number of positions is absolutely bounded.
The bare ratios belong coordinatewise to the group generated by \(p,q\), which is free of rank two because \(p,q>1\) are coprime.
The direct product in \(m\) positions thus has rank \(2m\), and the Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt estimate gives a uniform bound for each coefficient pattern.
For each fixed \(q\), each coordinate uniquely determines its two integer exponents.
The sum of those exponents is \(2n\) for a coordinate coming from the high-degree side, and the mixed subsum contains at least one such coordinate.
Counting all coordinates of all the boundedly many tuples therefore bounds the number of possible degrees uniformly in \(q\).
Together with the first conclusion this proves finiteness of the pairs on the exact grid.
