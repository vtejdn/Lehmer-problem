# Review of the Mahler gap at bounded degree in one variable

## Finding on the trace quadratic, the relative degree and the central specialization

At 2026-11-17T04:29 UTC, an agent outside proof development independently inspected the complete [v156 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#decision-monic-fiber-mahler-gap-submitted) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed submission and its necessary inputs, with no development discussion or separate explanation of v156.
The reviewer had independently inspected the central estimates in v154.

**Relative height and the trace quadratic.**
In dimension one the accepted Delsinne theorem has \(A_1=2\cdot 2^2\cdot2!=16\), \(\kappa_2(1)=3A_1=48\), and admissible denominator constant \(2\exp(64\cdot16^2)=2\exp(16384)\).
The exceptional proper torsion subvarieties at this dimension consist of roots of unity.
The inequality therefore applies to each nonzero nontorsion algebraic number with its absolute logarithmic Weil height and its degree over the maximal abelian extension of \(\mathbb Q\).
For a symmetric integral \(J\), every \(J(\zeta_p^u)\) is real and both roots above it have the same absolute logarithmic modulus \(g(J(\zeta_p^u))\).
They are units because the specialized polynomial and its reciprocal are monic with integral coefficients over the cyclotomic integers.
An arc on which \(|J|>2\) gives a conjugate off the circle for either chosen root \(\beta\), by extending an embedding of the base field.
The equal multiplicities of all such embedding extensions and the unit product formula give \(2h(\beta)\) for the mean of \(g\), including relative splitting at degree one.
Since \(D_{\rm ab}\le2\), (3) gives \(2h(\beta)>c_2\), and continuity on the circle yields the non-strict lower bound in the limit.
For zero measure, Parseval bounds \(a_0^2+2\sum_{j>0}a_j^2\) by four, with equality forcing a constant real \(J\).
For nonconstant \(J\) the strict inequality permits one pair of unit coefficients and possibly a constant of modulus one, which would give supremum three.
This leaves precisely the listed pair profiles and the constants of modulus at most two.

**Heights in each fiber and the limit over the grid.**
The positive width \(R\) and the nonvanishing of both end coefficients at the grid ensure exactly \(R\) nonzero roots counted with multiplicity.
The product grid for distinct prime orders is one rational orbit, and its probability measures converge on continuous tests to Haar measure as the smallest prime tends to infinity with \(h\) fixed.
A nonzero rational Laurent polynomial is nonzero on a relatively open set of the compact torus, so sufficiently fine orbits meet that open set.
If it vanished at any point of such an orbit, rational invariance would make it vanish everywhere on the same orbit.
This proves the required avoidance for every fixed proper rational exceptional set.

In a field \(K\) containing all relevant conjugates, averaging Jensen's formula over its \([K:\mathbb Q]\) embeddings counts each grid point equally and counts both conjugate complex embeddings separately.
At finite places the torsion coordinates are units, so Laurent specialization preserves integrality.
Multiplicativity of the Gauss norm implies that the sum of the local \(\log^+\) of the root moduli is at most \(-\log|A_R(x)|_v\).
With weights \([K_v:\mathbb Q_v]/[K:\mathbb Q]\), the product formula makes the sum of these upper bounds equal the archimedean average of \(\log|A_R(x)|\).
Consequently the grid mean dominates the sum of the full absolute heights of the \(R\) roots, with equality for a monic specialization.
Every conjugate of a chosen nontorsion root over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is a root over the same grid point, since every coefficient is fixed over that field.
Their number is \(D_{\rm ab}\le R\), by separability in characteristic zero, even when the polynomial has multiplicities.
All have the same absolute height, so Delsinne's inequality contributes \(D_{\rm ab}h(\beta)>[C_*(\log(3D_{\rm ab}))^{48}]^{-1}\ge c_R\).

Integrating the continuous truncation \(\max(\log|F|,-N)\) first in \(Y\) gives a continuous base test that dominates each fiber measure.
Passing to the product grid limit and then decreasing the truncation yields an upper bound \(m(F)\) on the untruncated limsup.
Jensen's lower bound by \(\log|A_R|\) on almost every fiber, induction on the number of variables and the global bound for \(\log^+|F|\) justify finiteness and the iterated integral.
For monic \(F\), continuity of root multisets gives continuous nonnegative fiber measure even at a zero root.
A positive integral gives an open set with an exterior root, so one of the fibers on each sufficiently fine orbit has a nontorsion root.
For \(h=0\) the same height estimate applies directly to the single fiber.

**Horizontal and vertical components.**
Bilu's cited Theorem 5.1 supplies finiteness of the maximal torsion translates on each fixed geometric component, including zero-dimensional translates.
On an irreducible horizontal hypersurface which itself is not a torsion translate, all of these have dimension at most \(h-1\).
Taking the closures of their projected sets preserves this dimension bound, and their finitely many rational conjugates still form a proper subset of the base.
Excluding those projections and the zeros of the geometric factor's end coefficients ensures a nonzero algebraic root on this component above each remaining grid point.
It is nontorsion since the base coordinates are torsion.
When \(h=0\) the selected nontorsion point already supplies the root.
These facts verify the nonmonic use of the fiber inequality.
For a torsion horizontal hypersurface the primitive defining character has a nonzero \(Y\)-exponent and can be oriented to make it positive.
The rational Galois orbit of its torsion value gives \(\Phi_m(X^vY^b)\), irreducible over the rational Laurent ring by a unimodular change of coordinates.
This primitive integral factor has measure zero, and collecting its multiplicities leaves an integral base factor up to a monomial, including the original integer content.

**The original central polynomial.**
Fixing \(L\le1\) makes the constants in the two v154 estimates absolute.
At \(s\le cD_d^{-2/3}\) the peak bound applies eventually since \(sD_d\le cD_d^{1/3}\to0\).
The terms \(s^{3/2}D_d\sqrt L\) and \(s^3D_d^2\) in the transfer error are bounded by \(c^{3/2}\sqrt L\) and \(c^3\), respectively, and the remaining terms vanish uniformly since \(s/\sqrt d\to0\).
A sufficiently small fixed \(c\) followed by sufficiently large \(d\) makes the total error less than \(c_2/2\).
The quadratic's two-variable measure is positive whenever this separable central \(P\) has a noncyclotomic factor, by the zero profiles and their binomial factorization.
Thus \(\lambda=c_2/2\) works, including \(L>1\), and the stated span exclusion follows for sequences of positive measures tending to zero.

The review supports all submitted implications with the constants and scopes stated.
The external inputs are Delsinne's Theorem 1.6 with its printed admissible constant and the finiteness statement of Bilu's Theorem 5.1 at the recorded source scopes.
The original proofs of those two theorems were outside this inspection.
