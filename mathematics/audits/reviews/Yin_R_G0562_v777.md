## Uniqueness, the two endpoint evaluations and limiting deficits

At 2026-11-20T20:27 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently examined the entire [submitted claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v640.md#statement).
The reviewer received the claim and its dependencies without development dialogue, author explanations or earlier conclusions about this claim.
The earlier reviews of the [short-width estimate](Yin_R_G0562_v758.md#finding) and the [two-ended division estimate](Yin_R_G0562_v775.md#finding) address inputs to the argument.
The finite bounds and all stated conditional conclusions hold.

For fixed \(P\) the orthogonal identity puts each candidate in the open ball of radius \(\sqrt C/2\) around the same complex minimizer in the norm \(\|\,\cdot\,/P\|_2\).
If the candidates were different, their difference would have a first occupied coefficient of modulus at least one, even though its constant term vanishes.
Translation to that first coefficient preserves the norm and gives width below \(d\), hence distance at least \(\sqrt C\), contradicting the strict triangle bound.
Reversing all of \(S\) preserves its boundary modulus and puts its leading coefficient at zero, so this nonzero integer has square less than \(5/4\).
The two endpoint units of \(S\) imply endpoint units in each integral factor by multiplication.
Reversing just one irreducible factor preserves the degree and energy, and multiplying the resulting polynomial by a sign restores constant coefficient one.
Uniqueness followed by cancellation gives reciprocity up to sign for that factor, with multiplicities unrestricted.
The constant candidate \(S=1\) also satisfies the asserted symmetry whenever it meets the energy cutoff.

Conjugate root pairing in the reflected polynomial gives real coefficients and \(O(0)=e^m\), so \(b=J(0)\) is real with \(|b|=e^{-2m}\).
The circle averages of \(J\) and \(\bar J\) both equal \(b\).
Using \(|J|=1\) shows that the two evaluation vectors have squared norm \(1-b^2\), and their cross term is the constant coefficient of \(z^g(\bar J-2b+b^2J)\).
Since \(g\ge1\), only \(z^g\bar J\) contributes, with value \(J_g\).
For \(f=S/O\), its inner product with \(1-bJ\) is \(1/O(0)\), since \(S/O^*\) has vanishing average.
For the second vector, \(z^g f\) has zero constant coefficient and \(z^g S/O^*\) has no exterior pole.
Its numerator and denominator both have degree \(d\), so the exterior constant term is the leading coefficient of \(S\) divided by \(O(0)\).
The signed sum of the two evaluations is therefore positive and at least \(2/O(0)\), including when \(w=0\) and \(g=d\).
Its evaluating vector has positive squared norm \(2(1-b^2+\epsilon J_g)\).
Cauchy--Schwarz and \(O(0)^2(1-b^2)=1/C\) give (2), with no singular denominator.

In a balanced division the short-width bound gives \(\rho^2=m\|R/P\|_2^2\ge |a|^2mC\).
The two-ended projection bound in the near-equality argument excludes positive limiting \(mn\).
Once \(mn\le(\log 2)/2\), its limiting quadratic would force \(\beta_0^2\le1-e^{-2x_0}<2x_0\le\beta_0^2\) for \(x_0>0\).
Thus \(\rho\to1/2\) and the first coefficient \(a\) eventually has modulus one, since \(mC\to1/4\).
Normalizing the remainder gives an integral polynomial with constant one and energy divided by \(C\) tending to one.
These inequalities hold for every balanced choice, including the empty quotient.
If some balanced choice failed (1) infinitely often, selecting those choices would contradict this convergence.
Consequently every balanced choice eventually lies in the same isolation ball and has the same normalized remainder.
Writing \(t=\epsilon J_g/(1-b^2)\), inequality (2) gives \(t\ge 2C/\|S/P\|_2^2-1\), and the vector norm bound gives \(t\le1\).
This proves (3) even for varying deficits.

For fixed \(k>0\), each reflected root with radial displacement \(\ell\) and angle \(\theta\) contributes \(2\sinh(k\ell)e^{-ik\theta}/k\) to the coefficient of \(-\log(FG)\), counting multiplicity.
The linear terms sum to \(4m\widehat\mu_P(k)\), while \(\ell\le m\) and \(\sum\ell=2m\) bound the error by \(O_k(m^3)\).
Through order \(k\), exponentiation contributes \(O_k(m^2)\) beyond the linear term, giving \(J_k/(1-b^2)=-b\widehat\mu_P(k)+O_k(m)\).
When \(g=k\) repeats along a convergent angular subsequence, (3) and \(|b|\to1\) force the limiting Fourier coefficient to have modulus one.
Haar measure has no such positive index, and for the uniform measure on \(z^N=\sigma\) the geometric sum vanishes unless \(N\mid k\).
The fixed-index estimates suffice for both conclusions and impose no uniform Taylor approximation at a growing deficit.
The existence of a sequence attaining \(m\|A/P\|_2^2\to1/4\) remains open.
