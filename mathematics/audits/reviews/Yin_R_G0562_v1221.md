## The in-field power test, both boundary components at two, and the common cutoff

The in-field power exclusion and simultaneous irreducibility at every common dilation hold, including the characteristic-two split reduction of the seven-term family.

If \(\beta=\rho^\ell\) with \(\rho\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\), the two embedded fields are equal, and the powers of the conjugates of \(\rho\) exhaust the distinct conjugates of \(\beta\) without repetitions.
Both elements are algebraic units, and the integral elementary symmetric functions of their conjugates agree modulo \(\ell\) by Frobenius.
The product over the nontrivial \(\ell\)-th roots of unity of \(Q(\zeta^jX)\) is rational by Galois invariance and has integral coefficients by algebraic integrality.
At \(1-\zeta\) it reduces to \(Q(X)^{\ell-1}\), and contraction to \(\mathbb Z\) gives the asserted congruence modulo \(\ell\).
The complete product equals \(S(X^\ell)\) up to its leading sign, since the roots are precisely all the lifts of the distinct roots of \(S\).
Evaluating at \(\beta\), the \(j=0\) factor and the combined remaining factor both lie in \(\ell\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Consequently \(S(\beta^\ell)\in\ell^2\mathbb Z[\beta]\), and evaluating the defect puts it in \(\ell\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Monicity identifies \(\mathbb Z[\beta]/\ell\mathbb Z[\beta]\) with \(\mathbb F_\ell[X]/(\bar S)\), including any nilpotents, so the full polynomial \(\bar S\) divides the reduced defect.

The product identity for the defect and monomial substitution hold in the Laurent rings before reduction.
Coprimality of \(a,b\) makes the specialized Laurent map surjective by Bézout on their exponents.
Since \(\bar S\) divides both specialized equations and its constant is nonzero, the indicated quotient surjects onto a vector space of dimension exactly \(\deg S\).
Clearing monomials does not alter the torus scheme, and multiplying a polynomial by a monomial multiplies its defect by the \(\ell\)-th power of that monomial.
After discarding boundary monomials the two polynomial degrees are at most \(4\) and \(4\ell\).
With no common torus component, planar Bézout bounds the length, including multiplicities, by \(16\ell\); shared coordinate-axis factors can be discarded before taking the projective closures.

At the simple boundary root the formal solution has tangent coefficient \(c=-B(\xi)/A'(\xi)\).
In the reduced defect its linear coefficient along the branch is \(c(\delta_\ell A)'(\xi)\), since the separate term \(A(\xi)^{\ell-1}B(\xi)\) vanishes.
Differentiating before reduction gives \((\delta_\ell A)'(\xi)=-\xi^{\ell-1}A'(\xi^\ell)\ne0\), since the coefficients are in the prime field and \(\xi\ne0\).
Thus the defect cannot vanish identically on the component carrying the branch, whether or not the constant term also vanishes there.

For the five-term polynomial the signed boundary chart has initial terms \(t-sz-w\).
In odd characteristic the discriminant factors \(X^2-X+1\) and \(X^2+3X+1\) have no common zero, since their difference is \(4X\) and neither vanishes at zero.
Their discriminants \(-3\) and \(5\) cannot both vanish, so the product has a simple zero and is nonsquare over the algebraically closed constant field.
At two, solving the quadratic in the rational function field would make \(1/C^2\) an Artin–Schreier value.
Adding \((1/C)^2+1/C\) makes \(1/C\) such a value as well, contradicting its simple poles at the roots of \(X^2+X+1\).
The unit leading coefficient in the Laurent coefficient ring rules out a vertical component.
The single boundary branch therefore excludes a common component at every prime.

For the seven-term polynomial both boundary charts have a simple root \(-s\) with their linear row nonzero there.
In odd characteristic the monic quartic discriminant could only have a monic quadratic square root of the form \(X^2-X+1\), by its cubic and linear coefficients.
Its quadratic coefficient would then be \(3\) in place of \(-5\), impossible modulo odd primes.
The coefficient pair \(1+X,1+X+X^2\) precludes vertical factors.
At two the two distinct primitive linear factors are \((1+X)Y+1\) and \(XY+X+1\).
At \(Y=0,X=1\) only the second vanishes; at \(w=1/Y=0,X=1\), only the reversed first factor \(1+X+w\) vanishes.
Thus the boundary checks cover both components, and changing the original signs preserves the conclusions in every characteristic.

The edges of the five-term diamond and seven-term hexagon are primitive binomial segments with unit coefficients.
The edge-polynomial degree theorem gives a common lower bound \(c\max(a,b)\) for the degrees of the nontorsion unit factors of the finitely many signed shapes.
The extreme exponents in the positive directions have unit coefficients in both families for sufficiently large primitive directions, so all such irreducible factors do give units.
The specialized length is at most seven.
Applying the bounded radical-denominator theorem with \(N=\ell\) and relative degree one restricts any in-field prime power extraction to a prime divisor of \(R_7\).

Once \(c\max(a,b)>16R_7\), the defect estimate excludes all these primes for both signs uniformly.
The extra possibility \(\beta\in-4K^4\) would make \(-\beta\) a square in \(K\).
Thus Capelli's binomial criterion and its composition theorem give irreducibility of \(S(Z^g)\) for every \(g\), including all factors of every dilated specialization by sending a root to its \(g\)-th power.
The product of the positive exterior moduli over the \(g\) lifts equals the original exterior contribution, so each resulting minimal polynomial has the asserted measure.

For the prime five-term subfamily the real root field has only the two rational roots of unity.
