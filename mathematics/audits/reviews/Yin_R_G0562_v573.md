## Independent whole-proof finding

At 2026-11-19T02:58 UTC I reviewed the [squarefree-numerator theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v441.md#statement) for Sheng Yin.
I had no part in developing this theorem and received the fixed claim and the requested scope without its development discussion, author explanations or prior conclusions about it.
I checked the derivative polynomial, the enumeration of functions and the uniform estimate for minimizing rows against the stated inputs.
All three conclusions hold, including for nonmonic powered minima and for either sign in the degree-restricted test.

### Orders and integral divisibility

Since \(f\) has rational coefficients, complex conjugation preserves its zero and pole divisors.
Inversion exchanges those two divisors, with multiplicity.
Hence reflection \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\) also exchanges them, precluding a zero or pole on the unit circle for the reduced function.
Among the primitive irreducible factors away from zero, inversion matches each numerator factor \(F\) with a distinct denominator factor equal up to sign to \(F^*(Y)=Y^{\deg F}F(1/Y)\).
The reversal has the same degree and coefficient content because \(F(0)\ne0\), and its Mahler measure equals that of \(F\).
A self-reciprocal factor cannot survive in either part of a reduced presentation.
Thus \(L\), the product of all such numerator and denominator factors once each, has degree \(2\deg R\) and log measure \(2m(R)\).

The expression \(K_n=(ns-1)L+n\sum_F k_F YF'(L/F)\) lies in \(\mathbb Z[Y]\), with \(s=\operatorname{ord}_0 f\).
The quotient \(L/F\) is the product of the other primitive integer factors, including when \(F\) is nonmonic.
Its leading-term contribution with \(YF'\) is \(\deg F\) times the leading coefficient of \(L\).
The combined multiplier is \(n(s+\sum_F k_F\deg F)-1\), which cannot vanish for integer \(n\ge2\).
This proves exact degree and nonvanishing also when \(L=1\).

At a counted root \(\alpha\), put \(\beta=\alpha^n\) and \(H(X)=f(X^n)-X\).
Both rows and \(L\) are nonzero at \(\beta\) by coprimality and the graph equation with \(\alpha\ne0\).
The identity \(XH'-H=nX^nf'(X^n)-f(X^n)\) shows directly that \(K_n(X^n)\) has order \(t_P-1\) there, because it differs from \(XH'-H\) by the local unit \(L(X^n)/f(X^n)\).
The power map is unramified at \(\alpha\), so \(K_n\) has this order at \(\beta\).
The equality \(\alpha=f(\beta)\) recovers the original field and commutes with all embeddings.
It gives the powered minimum \(Q\) degree \(\deg P\), and it prevents distinct original minima from giving a common powered minimum.
The absolute height power law gives \(m(Q)=n m(P)\) for the primitive positive-leading minima, also when there are finite-place height contributions.
The full product \(\prod_PQ^{t_P-1}\) is primitive by Gauss's lemma and divides \(K_n\) over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\) by the root orders.
Gauss's lemma therefore gives a quotient in \(\mathbb Z[Y]\), whose Mahler measure is at least one.
Taking degrees proves the first inequality.

On the unit circle the identity \(K_n/L=nYf'/f-1\) has no poles.
Its isolated zeros have integrable logarithms since the numerator is nonzero.
The integral of \(\log|K_n/L|\) is \(m(K_n)-m(L)\).
Jensen's inequality and the [degree bound for the integral of the absolute logarithmic derivative](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v418.md#circle) give
\[
 m(K_n)-m(L)\le\log\!\int_{\mathbb T}|nYf'/f-1|
                 \le\log(1+nd).
\]
The input counts the disk zero and pole contributions with multiplicities, including the contribution paired with infinity.
Combining this estimate with the integral divisor gives the asserted bound with \(2m(R)\).
For a constant or monomial satisfying the inversion identity the radical is one, \(K_n=ns-1\), and no nontorsion repeated factor occurs.

### Exhaustion of the finite minimum

Fix \(p\) and the given integer parameters.
Every candidate radical has degree at most \(b\) and each of its integer coefficients in degree \(j\) is bounded by \(\binom{\deg R}{j}C_p\), by the elementary symmetric functions of its roots.
The list of primitive positive-leading squarefree candidates with nonzero constant coefficient is finite, includes one and can be filtered by exact comparison of algebraic multiplicative Mahler measures.
For each actual function, the positive multiplicity of every numerator factor \(F\ne Y\) is at most \(b\), as is \(|\operatorname{ord}_0 f|\).
The rational function \(Y^s\prod_F(F/F^*)^{k_F}\) has the same divisor as \(f\), including at infinity, and its product with its inversion is one.
The remaining rational scalar must be \(1\) or \(-1\).
This proves coverage by the proposed enumeration.
Reductions, exact comparison of the surviving radical and the map degree test remove inconsistent candidate presentations.
The empty radical produces just the appropriate signed monomials.

The elimination test may factor the integral derivative polynomial and retain nonzero noncyclotomic powered minima.
For such a factor with residue class \(z\), the checks that \(u(z)v(z)\ne0\) and \(f(z)^p=z\) recover \(\alpha=f(z)\) by exact field arithmetic.
The order identity then equates the required multiplicity with the powered-factor test.
This supplies effective filtering independently of any presumed list of original roots.
The fallback contributes a normalized value at least \(\lambda\), since \(2\lfloor ap/2\rfloor\ge(a-1)p\) and \(\lambda\le\log 2\).
For an actual function the same lower bound follows from any qualifying factor and the radical inequality.
Every candidate admitted by the prior full-numerator minimum gives a candidate for the new minimum with \(M(R)\le M(u)\), since \(R\) divides the integral numerator of a primitive presentation.
The two normalized minima have the same denominator and additive term.
The convergence of the previous minimum and the preceding lower bound therefore prove the limit.

### Loss for degree-restricted rows

For every \(D\ge10\) the degree-ten example gives \(\lambda_D\le\log\rho<\log2\).
The relative upper estimate of the [degree-restricted theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#statement) tends uniformly to a factor of one over \(10\le D\le\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\), while the normalized fallback is at least \(\log2\).
Consequently an actual minimizing row exists strictly below the fallback throughout that range eventually.
At its designated powered node \(A\ne0\), and the sign-reversal graph equation gives a nonzero denominator as well.
Removing a common factor leaves the contact order intact.
The designated factor has degree at most \(D\) and positive log measure at least \(\lambda_D\).
The radical estimate at \(n=p\), with \(d\le b\), gives \(2m(R)\ge(a-1)p\lambda_D-\log(1+pb)\).
Factorization of the original integral row gives \(m(R)\le m(A)\le\log H_{p,D}\), including its content and any factors removed by reduction.
Subtracting the radical lower bound from the uniform upper bound for \(2\log H_{p,D}\) yields
\[
  2(\log H_{p,D}-m(R))
     \le(2+o(1))p\lambda_D+\log\frac{1+pb}{2pb+1}.
\]
The final logarithm is nonpositive, and dropping it preserves the uniform relative error.
This proves both sides of the loss estimate for any minimizing row in the specified range.
The result gives no unconditional positive lower bound for the unrestricted infimum.
