## Classification at every height \(q\ge2\)

On 2026-11-21T18:17 UTC a separate agent acting for Yin checked the classification in J185, including its finite search and its application to every common dilation.
The reviewer had no part in developing the classification and received no proof-generation dialogue.
Before inspecting it the reviewer consulted [the previous review](Yin_R_G0562_v1029.md) of the catalogue, the known sextic witness and the \(q\ge4\) degree bound.
The finding below checks the new classification and the use of these fixed inputs.

Writing \(Y=s z^g\), where \(s=-\operatorname{sgn}(b/a)\), gives normalized middle coefficient \(-(q+1)\) and sign \(\eta=\epsilon s^{v+1}\).
The circle map of degree \(v-1\) gives at least \(v-1\) distinct circle roots of the degree-\(v+1\) normalized parent.
Reciprocity and the unit norm then force every nontorsion unit factor to contain its sole exterior root and its reciprocal.
Complex conjugation forces the exterior root to be real, and \(r^v(qr+p)>pr+q\) for \(r>1\) rules out a negative exterior root.
For the positive exterior root \(\alpha\), the radial bound gives \(2<\alpha+\alpha^{-1}<2+2/q\le3\), excluding a reciprocal quadratic.
Thus \(\alpha\) is Salem of degree \([\mathbb Q(z^g):\mathbb Q]\).

I checked the catalogue exclusion needed here directly on the original coefficient rows.
The source states completeness simultaneously below \(13/10\) and through degree 44.
Their polynomial degrees and exact signs at \(13/10\) select 46 rows in this range.
For each I enclosed its unique exterior root between \(l\) and \(h\) by adding and subtracting \(10^{-10}\) from the printed decimal and checking opposite signs in the reciprocal polynomial.
All lower endpoints exceed \(7/6\), so the radial bound \(q<2\alpha/(\alpha-1)^2\) implies \(q\le83\).
For all 3,772 pairs of rows and \(2\le q\le83\), the denominator interval \([ql-p,qh-p]\) misses zero.
Bounding numerator and absolute denominator separately in \(\alpha^v=(p\alpha-q)/|q\alpha-p|\) and testing \(v\ge2\) up to the first power \(l^v\) above the upper quotient bound left no possible equality.
This uses the catalogue author's completeness statement at its stated scope.
For \(q\ge4\) the pole-norm result puts any exterior unit below \(p/q\le5/4\), so this excludes the entire range of degrees under review at these heights.

For \(q=2,3\), one may now assume \(\alpha\ge13/10\).
All conjugates other than \(\alpha\) have modulus at most one, so the nonzero integer norm of \(q\alpha-p\) gives \(|q\alpha-p|\ge(p+q)^{1-D}\).
The radial bound gives \(\alpha<3\), hence \((13/10)^v<(3p-q)(p+q)^{43}\).
The last possible indices are 271 and 327, respectively.
I reproduced the program's 540 and 652 normalized integer factorizations using python-flint 0.9.0.

As a second computation I removed all factors \(X\pm1\) from each parent and wrote its reciprocal remainder as \(X^n H(X+X^{-1})\) after normalizing the leading sign.
I formed \(H\) from the recurrence \(h_0=2,\ h_1=t,\ h_{j+1}=t h_j-h_{j-1}\) for \(X^j+X^{-j}\) and factored \(H\) over the integers.
Any qualifying Salem unit supplies a monic irreducible factor \(f\) of \(H\) of degree \(D/2\le22\), with one trace root greater than two and the others in \((-2,2)\), hence \(f(2)<0\).
The sole such factor was \(t^3-t^2-3t+1\), of multiplicity one, at \((q,v,\eta)=(2,13,+1)\).
Its quadratic lift is the known irreducible sextic \(S=X^6-X^5-X^3-X+1\).
Since \(v+1=14\), either sign of \(b/a\) gives \(\epsilon=+1\).
Every \(g\)-th root of \(s\) times any conjugate of this unit is a nontorsion algebraic unit, since both that root and its inverse satisfy monic equations over algebraic integers.
Its primitive degree is six, while the degree of the lift can be larger.
The classification leaves unbounded primitive degrees open.
