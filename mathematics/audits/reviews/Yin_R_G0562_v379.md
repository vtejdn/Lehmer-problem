# Review of the two-row resultant at a double Salem root

<a id="row-ideal-review"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T12:15 UTC an independent agent reviewer for Yin checked the full [submitted Salem estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v375.md#decision-salem-double-row-valuation-submitted) and the [general transfer inequality](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v157.md#transfer).
The reviewer had no role in developing either argument and received the fixed mathematical inputs without author discussions, exploration notes outside the reviewed material or previous verdicts on these arguments.
Both inequalities, the explicit witness valuation, and the stated uniform limits follow under the hypotheses given.

For the Salem polynomial, the rotation identity separates all \(d+1\) powered roots, including the extra root one.
The monic eliminant \(XB^p-A^p\) has exactly that degree, so deleting \(X-1\) leaves the entire powered minimal polynomial \(Q\).
At every root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), both \(A(\alpha^p)\) and \(\alpha^p-1\) are nonzero.
Consequently division by the simple factor \(E(x)=xB(x^p)-A(x^p)\) gives the value \(pA(\alpha^p)^{p-1}/(\alpha^p-1)\) for \(Q(x^p)/E(x)\) there.
This proves the first integral row congruence, and multiplication by \(-\alpha^r\) gives the second.
The exchanged rows \(XV,U\) give the congruences for \(p-r\), even when the original two rows require different powers of \(X\) to strip their monomials.

At depth \(1/q\), reduction of \(X^m+2\) modulo three gives \((X^{m/q}-1)^q\), which has disk valuation at least one about every \((m/q)\)-th root of unity.
Only the disk at one loses \(1/q\) through division by \(X-1\).
In each orientation \(j\), the congruences impose the common row bound \(p-j-v_3(j)-g_\lambda(X-1)\), since the modulus already has valuation at least \(p-1\).
A normalized linear factor of either row contributes positive valuation on at most one of the distinct residue disks.
There its weight is at most the depth, and a determinant of two such factors has valuation at least the smaller weight.
The product of the two weights divided by the depth can therefore be charged to that pair once.
The scalar of the row with positive content is covered by its valuation times the opposite row degree after division by common content.
These facts give the sum \(m k_r^2-2k_r+1/q\) for all witness degrees, and restoring common content can only increase the valuation.

In the proposed witness the homogeneous expression has a double zero along \(t=a\).
The leading and constant coefficients of its first row are \(r,p-r\), which are coprime.
At the \(m\) zeros of \(B\) the valuation of that row is \(p+v_3(p-r)\), and at the \(m\) zeros of \(A\) it is \(p+v_3(r)\), since these roots are units at three.
This verifies coprimality and, using multiplicities \(r,p-r\) in the second row and its unit leading coefficient, the exact upper witness valuation.
The first row supplies degree \(p(d+1)\) in \(x\), which exceeds \(pd+r\) from the second row.
After division by \(mp^2\), the errors from \(v_3(j)\le\log_3 p\) and the distinguished disk tend to zero uniformly for \(m\ge6\).
Thus the lower limit is at least \(1/4\) for arbitrary row differences, while \(k_r=p-1\) gives convergence to one for adjacent classes.
The single-root witness has valuation \(m\), bounding the two-group contribution for multiplicity two by \(4m\) at three.

For the general transfer, differentiating \(Q(x^p)T(x^p)=A(x^p)^p-(xB(x^p))^p\) at the unit \(\alpha\) gives a nonzero multiple of \(E'(\alpha)\) because \(Q'(\beta)T(\beta)\ne0\).
Substitution in the differentiated row identity gives \(rTV=pA^{p-r}L\) at \(\beta\), with the opposite sign for \(rTU=-pXB^{p-r}L\).
Both identities lift modulo the monic \(Q\) over \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
For each orientation the resulting row bound is truncated at \(v_D(Q)\) before subtracting \(v_D(T)+v_\ell(j)\).
Positivity of disk depth and unit centers permit the same monomial stripping and determinant argument as above.
The cofactor estimate \(q_D+t_D\ge p h_D\) gives the claimed linear growth when the stated bounds on \(h_D,t_D,j\) hold uniformly.
An integral Bézout identity forces \(h_D=0\), and the Salem presentation has \(T=1-X\).
For general varying inputs, the existence of a presentation and disks with sufficient total contribution remains open.
