### Equality before projection and the triangular faces

Equality leaves exactly one such cone inside each original cone.

The orthogonal complements give equal spans of differences.
The \(r\) independent opposite pairs of \(N(H)\) give an affine cross-polytope, and every support point of \(L\) is one of its own \(2r\) vertices.

### The two endpoints of a six-term polynomial

The six flat terms make every irreducible factor a unit polynomial with a monic normalization.
For a shrinking nontorsion factor the strict Smyth threshold gives reciprocity of that factor alone.
Write \(w=D-e_4,\ g=e_4-e_3\) at the upper endpoint, \(d=\deg P\), and \(t=\max\log|\alpha|>0\).
Comparing the leading term at a root of maximum modulus with the other five terms gives \(wt\le\log 5\), hence the stated \(\log 6\) bound.
Reciprocity bounds every absolute log radius by \(t\), and the sum of these absolute radii in logarithmic coordinates is \(2m\).
The all-root square bound gives \(1/10\le 2dmt\).
The sparse divisor estimate and concavity give \(m\ge(d/D)\log(1+0.31/6!)\), so \(t\gg1/(Dm^2)\) and \(w\ll Dm^2\).
All constants are absolute for six flat terms.

The top block is \(X^{e_4}\) times a binomial of width \(w\) whose roots have modulus one.
Its length is two, the other block has length four, and their gap is \(g\).
For \(g>8w+8\), use the capacity result with \(L=4,\delta=(g-2w)/D\in(0,1]\).
The required \(D\ge4/\delta\) holds since \(g-2w>4\).
The floor gives \(j\ge(g-5w)/2-1>g/8\), and then \(D'=j+3w\ge j\) and \(\lambda=(j+3w/2)/D'\ge1/2\).
The actual intermediate estimate \(d\ge k\lambda^2 D'/(384\log(2\cdot4^{10k}))\) gives \(d\gg g\), uniformly even for unbounded \(k\), because \(\log(2\cdot4^{10k})/k\le\log2+10\log4\).
Hence \(g\ll Dm\).
In the complementary range \(w\ge1\) gives \(g\le16w\), with the same conclusion for small \(m\).
Reversal of the whole parent sends its leading endpoint calculation to the other end because the reciprocal factor still divides the reversal, regardless of the symmetry of the parent.
Adding \(g+w\) gives the stated estimates for the third terms and the middle gap.
For the three centered pairs the exponents are the translates of \(\pm n_1,\pm n_2,\pm n_3\), so \(D=2n_1,\ w=n_1-n_2,\ w+g=n_1-n_3\).

At the maximal exterior root the two-term block has modulus at least \(e^{e_4t}(e^{wt}-1)\), while the other four terms have modulus at most \(4e^{e_3t}\).
Consequently \(wte^{gt}\le4\).
Writing \(u=gt\) gives \(e^u-1\le ue^u\le4g/w\), and the lower bound on \(t\) proves the additional logarithmic restriction at either endpoint.

### Exact flat split

Expanding the Chebyshev recurrence independently gives \(C_{14}-C_{10}+C_9=Y(Y^2-3)AB\) with the displayed coefficients.
The six translated exponents have gcd one because they include 4 and 5.
The successive Euclidean remainders modulo two are \(Y+1,1\), and evaluation gives \(A(-3)=-159,\ A(-2)=1\).
Expansion also gives the stated degree-ten lift of \(A\).
For \(B-1\) I verified the product of the real linear factors.
On \(Y=-x,\ 0\le x\le1\), the estimate follows from \(x(1-x)\le1/4\), with strict inequality in the product using the other factors.
On each of the remaining two intervals with negative product, the second derivative of the logarithm of its absolute value is a strictly negative sum of inverse squares.
A given level has at most two roots with multiplicity on each interval, since a critical root has multiplicity exactly two.
Hence the sextic has at most four real roots and has a nonreal root.
The quintic's root below \(-2\) and the sextic's nonreal root each give a nontorsion root of its reciprocal lift.

One can verify the exact rational factor count in this example directly.
The quintic modulo two has no linear factor and has remainder \(Y\) on division by \(Y^2+Y+1\), which rules out every proper factorization of degree five.
The sextic modulo three equals \(\Phi_7(Y)\), irreducible since \(3\) has order six modulo seven.
Thus \(A\) and \(B\) are irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Each also has a real root in \((-2,2)\), since \(A(-1)=1,\ A(0)=-3\), and \(B(1)=1,\ B(3/2)=-461/64\).
For such a trace root \(y\), the discriminant \(y^2-4\) is negative in a real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(y)\), so \(X^2-yX+1\) is irreducible over that field.
The towers therefore have degrees ten and twelve, which proves irreducibility of both lifts.
The other two trace factors lift to \(X^2+1\) and \(X^4-X^2+1\), exactly the fourth and twelfth cyclotomics.
This verifies the two distinct nontorsion factors with multiplicities one in that particular univariate polynomial.
