## Classification of the elements and the least rational-map degrees

At 2026-11-24T21:44 an agent outside proof development independently reviewed for Yin the complete [Salem-field claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed claim and scope without development dialogue or a prior verdict on this version.
The spectrum and both branches of the rational-map formula hold with the bounds and strict inequalities in the statement.

For a Salem generator \(\alpha\), its inverse is a conjugate generating the same field, and the corresponding involution \(\tau\) has fixed field \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) by the quadratic trace equation.
Every embedding of \(B\) is real because all traces of the conjugates are real.
At a nonreal embedding \(\sigma\) of \(K\), the image of \(\alpha\) has modulus one, so \(\bar\sigma=\sigma\tau\) on the generator and hence on \(K\).
For \(y\notin B\) this implies that \(\sigma(y)\) is nonreal, whereas the two real embeddings have distinct values \(y\) and \(\tau(y)\).
Thus the embedding of \(\mathbb Q(y)\) taking \(y\) to its specified real value has precisely one extension to \(K\).
Separability gives \([K:\mathbb Q(y)]=1\), proving the proper-subfield assertion without assuming normality.

For \(d=2n\) the unit ranks are \(2+(n-1)-1=n\) for \(K\) and \(n-1\) for \(B\).
The norm of a base-field unit is its square, so the norm image has full base-field rank and its kernel has rank one.
The only torsion in real \(K\) is \(\{\pm1\}\), both in the kernel.
If \(v^j\) belongs to the kernel for \(j\ge1\), then \(N_{K/B}(v)^j=1\), so \(N_{K/B}(v)\) is a real root of unity.
At any trace place that becomes complex this norm is a strictly positive squared modulus, forcing it to be \(+1\).
This proves saturation in the full unit group modulo signs.
Among the two orientations and their signs, exactly one primitive generator \(\epsilon\) has its specified real value greater than one.
It lies outside \(B\), has one exterior conjugate, and generates \(K\), with the same trace field since its inverse is its \(\tau\)-image.
Every nonzero power also lies outside \(B\) and generates \(K\), so \(m(\pm\epsilon^k)=|k|\log\epsilon\).
The original positive Salem generator is a positive integer power of \(\epsilon\).

For every nonzero element with \(m(y)<\log2\), both extreme coefficients of its primitive integral minimum have absolute value one by the leading and constant coefficient bounds.
This proves that \(y\) is a unit.
Schinzel's totally real estimate, also the real-fraction-one specialization of the bound proved by Garza, gives \(h(z)\ge c=\frac12\log\phi\) for totally real \(z\ne0,\pm1\).
This is a bound on absolute logarithmic height and must be multiplied by the degree to bound total measure.
The totally real estimate is stated on p. 385 and the general proof concludes on p. 388. [Garza's published paper](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/83776)
Since \(\phi^3=2+\sqrt5>4\), one has \(3c>\log2\).
For \(y\in B\), this leaves only degrees one or two in the specified range, and rational units have zero measure.
For quadratic units with larger absolute conjugate \(1<R<2\), norm \(+1\) gives \(2<|\operatorname{Tr} y|=R+1/R<5/2\).
Norm \(-1\) gives \(0<|\operatorname{Tr} y|=R-1/R<3/2\), so the absolute trace must be one.
The roots of \(X^2\pm X-1\) are exactly the four stated numbers, all of measure \(\log\phi\) and each generating \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
For \(y\notin B\), its full degree gives \(h(y\tau y)\le2h(y)<2\log2/d\le(\log2)/3<c\).
Its totally real norm is therefore torsion, and positivity at a complexified trace place again forces norm one.
This proves completeness in both directions.

For a reduced nonconstant \(R=A/D\) realizing a signed power with \(k\ne0\), clearing the denominator and any negative exponent gives a polynomial of degree at most \(\deg R+|k|\) vanishing at \(\epsilon\).
It is nonzero because signed monomials were excluded as rational functions, giving \(\deg R\ge d-|k|\).
For \(1\le k\le d/2\), quotient and remainder on division of the minimal polynomial \(P\) by \(X^k\) give \(P=X^kD+A\) with \(\deg D=d-k\), \(\deg A<k\) and \(D(\epsilon)\ne0\).
The function \(-A/D\) takes value \(\epsilon^k\), which forces a signed monomial representing that same function to be \(X^k\), contradicting \(P\ne0\).
Its degree is at most \(d-k\) and the lower bound forces equality even after reduction.
Signs and inversion preserve the degree and give the other powers.

If a finite value is quadratic, its cleared equation over that quadratic field gives \(\deg R\ge d/2\).
When \(\sqrt5\in B\), the monic polynomial of \(\epsilon\) over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) has degree \(d/2\).
Writing it in the basis \(1,\sqrt5\) gives rational polynomial coefficients \(A,D\) with \(\deg A=d/2\), \(\deg D<d/2\), and \(D\ne0\), since a rational polynomial of that degree cannot annihilate \(\epsilon\).
This produces \(\sqrt5=-A(\epsilon)/D(\epsilon)\), and the rational affine transformation \((1+X)/2\) realizes \(\phi\) at degree \(d/2\).
The quadratic value excludes every signed monomial, since each nonconstant power value generates \(K\).
Under \(2k_*\le d\), the best signed-power degree is \(d-k_*\ge d/2\), so the quadratic branch wins or ties exactly when its field and measure conditions permit it.
For fixed \(M\), the stated rational-base lower bound on \(d h(\epsilon)=\log\epsilon\) implies \(k_*=o(d)\), hence the size condition eventually holds whenever \(h_0\le M\).
A Salem sequence tending to one yields primitive generators tending to one by \(1<\epsilon\le\alpha\), with no fixed-field assumption.
