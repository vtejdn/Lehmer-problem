## Constant normalization, geometric approximants and progression growth

A monic integer polynomial with all roots on the circle has constant term of modulus one, so normalizing it divides by an integer sign and gives constant one.
Its boundary logarithm has zero mean and normalized angular measure is invariant under every positive integer dilation.
The dilated polynomial still has all roots on the circle, so C345v90 at modulus \(|a_i|\ge1\) gives a lower bound of half the undilated logarithmic norm minus its absolute loss constant, independently of the shift exponent.
The Poisson integral for each linear boundary-root logarithm then gives uniform upper bounds for both the normalized parent and its inverse on every compact subdisk.
Positivity and mass one of the kernel give a separate uniform radial mean bound for \(\log^+|B_i|\).
Normal-family compactness and the inverse bound yield a locally uniform, zero-free sublimit, still of constant one.
Each Taylor coefficient stabilizes to an integer by Cauchy's formula and discreteness.
The radial positive logarithmic means pass to the limit at each fixed radius with a bound independent of radius.
These are the holomorphic bounded-characteristic and integrality hypotheses of Cantor's Theorem 1, at precisely the statement scope of Can63-U001.
A preassigned coefficientwise limit has the same coefficients as any locally uniform sublimit and hence has the asserted rationality as well.

For a rational integral-coefficient series the finite Hankel rank over the rationals gives a finite-dimensional rational space of coefficient shifts.
The lattice of integral sequences there embeds by finitely many coordinates into an integer lattice, spans the space and is invariant under shifting.
Disk analyticity gives subexponential growth for the powers of the resulting integral matrix on a basis, so each nonzero eigenvalue and all its algebraic conjugates have modulus at most one.
Kronecker's theorem makes those eigenvalues roots of unity, bounding the finite poles of the resolvent, hence of the series, to roots of unity.
This applies also to the reciprocal series, whose integrality uses the constant coefficient one and whose analyticity uses the zero-free limit.
Thus all finite zeros and poles of the rational function are roots of unity, grouped into complete rational cyclotomic factors.
The constant value at zero fixes the scalar in the quotient of factors normalized individually at zero, including the sign of \(\Phi_1\).
Möbius factorization therefore gives exactly a finite product of normalized binomials with signed integer exponents.
For each fixed \(u\) its degree-\(u\) coefficient depends on lower exponents and on \(-b_u\), since higher factors start above that degree.
This proves uniqueness and stabilization of each exponent without any bound on the moving indices.

Conversely choose \(t\ge2\) and replace a negative factor by one geometric polynomial \((1-X^{ut})/(1-X^u)\) per copy.
All roots of each replacement are roots of unity and its constant coefficient and leading coefficient equal one.
Retaining the positive factors yields a nonconstant polynomial of constant one and leading coefficient \(s_t=\pm1\) whenever the signed support is nonempty.
Multiplying by \(s_t\) makes the parent monic with constant term \(s_t\), so normalization returns exactly the constructed approximant.
Each additional numerator \(1-X^{ut}\) tends locally uniformly to one, recovering the prescribed rational limit with its scalar unchanged.
For the empty support the monic parents \(X^t-1\) have normalized limit one.
The two logarithms from each geometric replacement and the logarithms of the positive factors have bounded total \(L^1\) norm because the one-binomial norm is invariant under integer dilation.
C345v90 therefore gives a common upper measure bound for these parents with arbitrary further dilation, arbitrary shift exponent and any scalar of modulus one.

For nested binomial supports, a hypothetical subsequence with bounded shifted measure has a rational sublimit of finite signed support.
In the infinite-union case every index in the union occurs eventually on that subsequence, so stabilization would give infinitely many nonzero limiting exponents.
In the finite-union case stabilization bounds their total absolute mass, contrary to its divergence.
The dyadic product example instead has one binomial index moving to infinity, even as its cyclotomic divisor sets increase.

The Euler product through \(y\) contains the harmonic sum through \(y\), and its multiplier \(\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-2})\) is at least the telescoping product \(\prod_{j\ge2}(1-j^{-2})=1/2\).
Hence \(\|L\|_1\ge\tfrac14\sum_{j\le y}1/j\), and the shifted logarithmic measure is at least \(\tfrac1{16}\log\log N-O(1)\) after the additional factor one half in C345v90.
This lower bound is uniform over the other nonnegative exponents, the original progression step and every allowed dilation and perturbation.
