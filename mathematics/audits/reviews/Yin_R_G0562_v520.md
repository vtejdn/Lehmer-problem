## The unique seed left by the row-value norm estimate

At 2026-11-18T22:39 UTC an agent outside the development of J178 version 2 independently reviewed its octic enumeration for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer contributed to the separate signed-row argument in L462 v252 and had previously inspected the sextic computation and the fixed-seed theorem.
This finding covers the octic census, its code and output, and the numerical comparisons with the two stated rates.
The proof of the signed-row height bound lies outside this review.

The fixed computation correctly gives 54 nontorsion sign classes of multiplicative measure at most two, with 23 classes having one exterior root and 31 having two.
Exactly one of the 54 has the C345 v411 lower rate at or below the cutoff.
Its positive Salem representative is \(X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1\), and the recorded sign-canonical representative is obtained by \(X\mapsto-X\).
The finite calculation yields no onset for the sufficiently-large-exponent exclusions.

### Exhaustive generation and the archived measure test

For the four inverse pairs choose moduli \(e^{t_j}\ge1\) with \(\sum_jt_j=\log M\).
As \(\cosh(x+y)+1\ge\cosh x+\cosh y\) for \(x,y\ge0\), the power sum obeys \(|s_k|\le6+M^k+M^{-k}\).
For \(M\le2\) and \(k\ge1\) this yields the necessary integer interval \(|s_k|\le6+2^k\), also for complex pairs and repeated roots.
In \(X^8+aX^7+bX^6+cX^5+dX^4+cX^3+bX^2+aX+1\), changing the variable sign simultaneously negates \(a,c\).
The restrictions on \(a,c\) choose one member of each orbit, including the singleton orbit when both vanish.
At each stage the implemented lower and upper integer coefficient bounds are the ceiling and floor obtained by solving Newton's identity for that coefficient.

For descending coefficients \(f_i\), expansion of \((-1)^d F(\sqrt X)F(-\sqrt X)\) gives \(g_j=\sum_i(-1)^i f_i f_{2j-i}\).
This produces an iterate with roots \(\alpha_i^2\).
I checked the implemented formula on \(X-1\), \((X-2)(X+3)\) and \((X-2)(X^2+1)\), including the double squared root \(-1\) in the third control.
Repeated Graeffe steps thus raise \(M\) to \(2^s\).
The coefficient lower bound follows by summing products of indexed roots, each of modulus at most \(M\).
The upper bound \(M\le\|F\|_2\) follows by Jensen's inequality and Parseval, and \(\|F\|_2\le 2^d M\) follows from the same coefficient bounds.
Consequently both positive-power integer comparisons enclose the measure with bounded multiplicative loss before taking the \(2^s\)-th root.
They eventually decide any strict comparison with \(B^{1/u}\).
Equality cannot occur in the calls made here because the real product of the exterior roots, up to sign, is an algebraic unit whereas \(B>1\) is a rational integer.
The trial factor list suffices by Gauss's lemma, the extreme unit coefficients and multiplicativity of the measure.
A reducible octic has at least one monic integral factor of degree at most four, whose individual coefficient bounds follow from its measure being at most two.

### A second exact measure test and a check of every threshold

I enumerated \(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4\) in the four bounded integer intervals and recovered the coefficients by Newton divisibility at each step, filtering the sign representatives.
After the further twelve power-sum checks there were exactly \(1\,504\) candidates.
For the measure cap I independently formed the polynomial of the products of every \(k\) distinct indexed roots for \(k=1,2,3,4\).
Writing \(s=s_n\), the power sums of the third and fourth such polynomials at index \(n\) are
\[
 (s^3-3s s_{2n}+2s_{3n})/6,\qquad
 (s^4-6s^2s_{2n}+3s_{2n}^2+8s s_{3n}-6s_{4n})/24.
\]
The first two are \(s_n\) and \((s_n^2-s_{2n})/2\).
Newton's identities reconstruct each monic product polynomial \(E_k\) from these sums.

I tested every coefficient of \(E_k(Z+2)\) and \((-1)^{\deg E_k}E_k(-Z-2)\) for nonnegativity using integer binomial expansions.
When \(M\le2\), every product root has modulus at most two, so the real linear factors and real factors from conjugate pairs of each shifted polynomial have nonnegative coefficients.
When \(M>2\), the product of all exterior roots is real with modulus \(M\), and uses between one and four roots by reciprocity.
One of the shifted polynomials then has a positive real root.
These implications give an exact test even with multiplicities and boundary roots.
Independent rational irreducibility, cyclotomicity and Sturm root counts for the quartic trace polynomial gave the following counts.

| Stage | Count |
| --- | ---: |
| First four powers and sign convention | 9,878 |
| First sixteen powers | 1,504 |

The trace polynomial is \(S^4+aS^3+(b-4)S^2+(c-3a)S+d-2b+2\), by expanding after \(S=X+X^{-1}\).
Irreducibility excludes repeated trace roots and endpoints.
Each interior real trace gives two circle roots and each remaining trace gives one exterior root, including each member of a nonreal conjugate trace pair.
The calculated exterior count of each class matches the corresponding output record.

In degree eight the multiplicity-two rate at width eight is \((2D-6h)/8\).
The opposite-sign log moduli and the pairs with zero give \(D\ge 2(8-g)h\), with equality when \(g=1\).
Put \(u=26-4g\).
For each archived exclusion I checked an integer certificate of \(M^u>256\) independent of the Graeffe loop.
If \(M^u\le256\), the product-root power sum \(s_{k,n}\) must satisfy \(|s_{k,n}|^u\le\binom8k^u 256^n\) for every \(k,n\).
Among \(1\le k\le4\), \(1\le n\le600\), this necessary inequality was violated for exactly 53 of the 54 sign classes.
The two latest first violations occur at \(n=366\) for \((1,1,0,-1)\) and at \(n=542\) for \((0,0,1,1)\).
The remaining tuple is \((0,0,1,-1)\), with one exterior root.

For its positive Salem representative the exterior modulus \(\rho\) satisfies \(32/25<\rho<9/7\).
Indeed its only positive exterior root is simple, the value at one is negative and the evaluations multiplied by \(25^8,7^8\) at those two rational points are \(-1\,788\,481\,599,552\,451\), respectively.
The integer comparisons \(9^{22}<2^8 7^{22}\) and \(32^{45}>2^{16}25^{45}\) give \(\rho^{22}<2^8\) and \(\rho^{45}>2^{16}\).
The first comparison, together with equality in the \(D\) bound for \(g=1\), proves that the actual C411 rate for this class falls strictly below the cutoff.
The second verifies the numerical threshold used with the separately derived signed-row rate.
I also executed the fixed program in memory and compared its complete returned object with the archived JSON.
Every stage count, coefficient tuple, exterior count, exponent and threshold flag agreed.
