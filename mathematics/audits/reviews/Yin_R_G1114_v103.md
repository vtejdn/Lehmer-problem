## The minimum circle mean and the limiting branch per degree

At 2026-11-16T20:07 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked all three sections of L462v130.
The reviewer had no role in developing this argument and received its fixed text and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict on L462v130.
The previously reviewed inverse and semicircle construction supplied the starting point for checking the change to even powers. [Semicircle criterion review](Yin_R_G0562_v32.md#semicircle-criterion).

For an integer polynomial \(W\) nonnegative on \([-2,2]\) with \(W(2)=0\) and \(W(T)>0\) at some \(T>2\), the arcsine mean is the constant coefficient \(B\) of \(U(z)=W(z+z^{-1})\).
The polynomial is nonzero and therefore has strictly positive integral on the interval.
Reciprocal symmetry and \(U(1)=0\) give \(B=-2\sum_{j>0}u_j\) for the integral Laurent coefficients \(u_j\).
At \(B=2\) the analytic polynomial \(1+\sum_{j>0}u_jz^j\) has positive real part in the disk by the Poisson formula and has a continuous representing density, as required in the cited classification.
One can also verify the needed polynomial case directly.
Let \(s\) be the highest positive frequency and average \(U\) over the \(s\) roots of \(z^s=-\operatorname{sgn}(u_s)\).
The average is \(2-2|u_s|\), so integrality and nonnegativity force \(|u_s|=1\) and a zero at every one of these roots.
The nonnegative differentiable function \(U(e^{it})\) has zero derivative there, so the degree-\(2s\) polynomial \(z^sU(z)\) is \(u_s(z^s+\operatorname{sgn}(u_s))^2\).
Vanishing at one forces \(U(z)=2-z^s-z^{-s}\), which is negative at the real \(z>1\) satisfying \(z+z^{-1}=T\).
Thus \(B\ge4\).
For \(W=(x^2-4)(D_k(x)-2)\), the Laurent factors are \(z^2+z^{-2}-2\) and \(z^k+z^{-k}-2\).
They are both nonpositive on the circle, zero at one and strictly positive for real \(z>1\).
For \(k\ge3\) only the product of constants contributes to the mean, giving \(B=4\).

For the varying weights, write \(d_i\) for the Salem degree and \(N_i=\deg W_i\).
The degrees \(d_i\) tend to infinity since bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound all coefficients of the monic minimal polynomials.
At any moving support frequency \(0<b_i\le N_i\), the number \(\sigma_i^{b_i}>1\) belongs to no proper algebraic subgroup of the one-dimensional torus, and its height is \(b_i\log(\sigma_i)/d_i=o(1)\).
Test the full powered orbit against the bounded continuous phase \(z/|z|\).
Each embedding of the power field has the same number of extensions to the Salem field, so the average over all \(d_i\) embeddings tends to the Haar phase mean zero.
Deleting the two real embeddings changes the sum divided by \(d_i\) by \(2/d_i\), independently of the sizes of the real powers.
This application uses the statement and orbit conventions of Bilu's Theorem 1.1. [Published theorem, p. 465](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~demarco/Math295/Bilu_equidistribution.pdf).

The same convergence holds at negative frequencies by conjugation and uniformly over the nonzero frequencies in each support by selecting a maximizing frequency on any failing subsequence.
Each Fourier coefficient of \(U_i/B_i\) has modulus at most one, since the density has integral one and is nonnegative.
The bounded support size therefore makes the aggregate nonconstant contribution \(o(1)\), even if \(B_i\) is unbounded.
The constant contributes \((d_i-2)/d_i\) before passing from phases to traces.
Pairing inverse phases gives \(\sum_\ell W_i(x_{i,\ell})/(d_iB_i)\to1/2\).
Exterior positivity and \(B_i\ge4\) give \(\liminf j_i/d_i\ge2\).

For the inverse example above the product has at most nine Laurent terms and \(N_i=k_i+2\).
At the zero exterior scale the construction has \(k_i\log\sigma_i=o(d_i)\) and exterior mass \(o(d_i)\), hence it attains \(j_i/d_i\to2\) along each hypothetical Salem sequence.
For any fixed \(\lambda\ge0\), let \(A_i\) be the target of that construction and choose the first even \(k_i=2l_i\) reaching it.
Then \(A_i\le X_i=\sigma_i^{k_i}\le\sigma_i^2 A_i\), so \(X_i/A_i\to1\), \(X_i/d_i\to\infty\), \(k_i\to\infty\) and \(\log X_i=o(d_i)\).
To check the inverse's least-power hypothesis exactly, apply it with \(L=X_i\), for which \(k_i\) is the least positive exponent reaching \(L\) and \(L\ge32(d_i/2)\) eventually.
The same inverse inequalities give \(q\sim4X_i\) and \(\theta-q=T_i-2+O(d_i/q)\).
The joint height tends to zero and each fixed character with exponents \((r,s)\ne(0,0)\) has \(r+sk_i\ne0\) eventually, so the same joint Haar limit and the same exterior mass \(\lambda\) give the residue limit \(2\mu_{\rm sc}+\lambda\delta_2\) and \(j_i/d_i\to2+\lambda\).

Finally \(D_{l+1}(x)-D_{l-1}(x)=(z-z^{-1})(z^l-z^{-l})\) for \(x=z+z^{-1}\), including \(l=1\) under \(D_0=2\).
Its square equals \((x^2-4)(D_{2l}(x)-2)\) identically.
The number \(T_i\) is an algebraic integer because \(\sigma_i\) is a unit, and the embeddings of its totally real degree-\(d_i/2\) trace field run once through \(T_i\) and all the interior traces.
Thus the actual integer branch \(j_i\) is the field trace of the square of \(D_{l_i+1}(T_i)-D_{l_i-1}(T_i)\), with the same weak limit.
The sharpness conclusion assumes a Salem sequence tending to one and concerns weights with the stated sparsity, positivity and height bounds.
