The image of the primitive section map in the two-torus is a coset, including for signed axis pairs, and cannot lie in any of the positive-area geometric components.
The cleared section is consequently a nonzero polynomial over the cyclotomic closure \(H\) of degree at most \(W_0\), even if coefficients at projected extremes cancel.
Thus a nontorsion primitive root \(\beta\) has \(1\le D=[H(\beta):H]\le W_0\).
The estimate for increasing primitive norm in C0345v769 is uniform over all twists and roots.

Summation with local degree weights cancels \(-\log|a|\) by the product formula, proving the required uniform upper bound for \(W_0 h(\beta)\).

For \(K=H(\beta)\), the extension obtained by adjoining any \(g\)-th root \(z\) is the splitting field of the separable binomial over \(K\).
Its group embeds in \(\mu_g\), and if its order is \(j\) then an integer power \(z^s\) is fixed precisely when \(j\mid s\).
For each \(e\mid g\), an \(e\)-th root of \(\beta\) lies in \(K\) exactly when \(z^{g/e}\in K\), since the two differ by a root of unity.
The admissible divisors are therefore exactly the divisors of \(g/j\), proving both maximality and the stronger divisibility property needed in the transfer.
The element \(\delta=z^j\) belongs to \(K\) and recovers \(\beta\) on raising it to \(e_g=g/j\); hence its relative degree is exactly \(D\).
It is nontorsion, so the audited rational-base relative height bound applies over the infinite abelian extension \(H\), with an absolute positive constant and the exponents of both logarithms as stated.
Combining that lower bound for \(h(\delta)=h(\beta)/e_g\) with the upper height bound gives the required estimate for \(e_g\).
In this comparison \(x(\log(2x))^4/(\log\log(5x))^3\) is eventually increasing, all its small positive integer values are finite, and the positive width of the fixed polygon is comparable to the primitive norm in all signed directions.
Rounding a uniform upper bound up gives a single positive integer \(B_F(n)\) for every twist, root and dilation at that norm.

For \(G=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B_F(n))\), the actual \(e_g\) divides \(g\) and \(G\), hence also \(h=\gcd(g,G)\).
This is valid even when \(g\) contains prime powers beyond those in \(G\).
The admissible divisors at \(h\) contain \(e_g\) and are contained in those at \(g\), giving \(e_h=e_g\) for the same primitive root.
For \(u=z^{g/h}\), subtracting no cyclotomic contribution is necessary: the ratio of the tower degrees over \(K\) gives \([H(z):H(u)]=g/h\).
Between \(\mathbb Q(u)\) and \(\mathbb Q(z)\) the degree is bounded below by this same number by base change and above by the binomial equation.
Multiplying the resulting degree equality by \(h(z)\) and using \(h(u)=(g/h)h(z)\) gives the equality of absolute logarithmic Mahler measures, including for nonintegral roots.
The composed polynomial has the resulting full rational degree and vanishes at \(z\), so it is irreducible up to rational scaling.
Every root at dilation \(h\) lifts by extracting a \((g/h)\)-th root and retains its original twists.
The same tower argument at \(h=e_g\) locates the measure at a dilation at most \(B_F(n)\) even on an exceptional fiber.
