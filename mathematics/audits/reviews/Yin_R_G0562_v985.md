## Independence for arbitrary finite selections

Induct on the number of simple alternating factors in a finite selection.
At the first failure of surjectivity, the kernel of projection onto the earlier factors is a normal subgroup of the last simple factor, so it must be trivial.
The new projection is then a surjective homomorphism from the preceding product to that simple factor.
Its commuting normal factor images force it to be an isomorphism on exactly one factor, even if several have the same degree.
The [single-section group](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v806.md#traces) excludes a pair inside one section.
Normality over the rational base preserves the graph on the two rationally invariant blocks.
Their equal orders give equal degrees, and above degree eight every alternating automorphism is induced by relabeling.
After relabeling, the ratio of the two permutations induced by any rational automorphism centralizes the alternating group and is trivial.
Thus matched label stabilizers coincide over the rational base, and the compositum of two quadratic lifts has degree \(D\le4d\) over the rationals.

For \(u\le v\) the two vertical exponents, the smaller trace has degree \(O(u)\), whereas the [larger section's cutoff](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v837.md#height) eventually exceeds \(\sqrt v\) times any fixed constant.
Hence \(v\le u^2\) eventually.
The [individual height estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) gives both heights \(O(u^{-1})\) while [AM16-U005](../literature/AM16/Yin_U005.md#am16-u005) gives a lower bound of order \(u^{-3/4}\) for their maximum under independence.
In rank one the generator of the image modulo torsion has a monomial lift in the joint field, of degree at most \(D\), so the same theorem gives height at least a constant times \(D^{-2}\).
Since both roots are nontorsion, inversion makes both exponents positive, with size \(O(D^2/u)\).
All embeddings of each root field extend to the joint field, and maximizing the generator's absolute value therefore realizes each individual maximum.

In the [pole allocation argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v790.md#allocation) the normalized count at each pole has positive limit before any use of bounded exterior count.
This supplies the required visit for arbitrary factors, while [exponential localization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v776.md#statement) separates the larger pole at the maximum.
Writing \(U_1=x^k,U_2=y^{k'}\), the exact identity \(bk'\log|U_1|=ak\log|U_2|\) and the estimates at that pole make the integer \(bk'-ak\) tend to zero.
Indeed every coefficient here is polynomially bounded in \(u\), while the localization error decays faster than any reciprocal power of \(u\).
Thus \(U_1/U_2\) is torsion, and its order is at most \(2D^2\) by the displayed totient bound.
The minimum distance \(2\sin(\pi/q)\ge4/q\) for a nonidentity root of order \(q\) exceeds the localization error eventually.
At the resulting common short coordinate the two reciprocal long coordinates tend to zero.
If distinct, subtracting their reciprocal quadratic equations and cancelling their difference gives a sum whose product with bounded \(A^*(U)\) equals \(-U^2\to-\beta_4^2\).
This proves equality of the long coordinates, and raising the two power identities to common exponents gives \(U^{lk'-l'k}=1\).
Since \(|U|>1\), primitivity identifies the directions.
These estimates exclude every sequence of exceptional pairs with minimum exponent tending to infinity, giving the same threshold for all finite selections.

The cyclotomic fiber in each finite compositum is a conjugacy-invariant set whose projection has equal-sized fibers and is a union of cosets of the full alternating product.
In each coset prescribed signs leave the uniform permutations on distinct blocks independent.

The short remainders sum to at most \(B/4\) within each section, forcing every admissible subset sum to contain a long cycle there.
Multiplying the block bounds and dividing the [Chebotarev density](../literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) by the positive density of the cyclotomic class gives the claimed product bound.
