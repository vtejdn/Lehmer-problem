### The conjugation quotient

The equality \(z+z^{-1}=u+u^{-1}\) identifies precisely the inverse pairs, which have two elements by nontorsion.
Their traces are algebraic integers forming a single Galois orbit of size \(p\), which also proves integrality and irreducibility of the monic trace polynomial.
The imaginary part of a trace vanishes exactly on the real axis or the unit circle, so conjugation on labels has \(c\) disjoint transpositions and at least one fixed point.
Its normal closure in the transitive prime-degree group \(H\) is transitive because its orbits form a block system.
A faithful transitive abelian group acts freely, so the nonidentity element with a fixed point forces a nontrivial derived subgroup of the normal closure.
That derived subgroup is again normal in \(H\) and transitive.
For the signed action, the pairwise commutators normally generate the derived subgroup of the normal closure \(T\) of conjugation, since their images make all the generators of \(T\) commute.
This proves that \(D=T'\) maps onto the transitive derived subgroup on labels.

Orbit-stabilizer gives \(p\mid |D|\), and the signed permutation group of degree \(p\) contains exactly one factor of \(p\) in its order.
Thus a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup \(S\) of \(D\) is also Sylow in \(G\).
Its generator projects to a \(p\)-cycle, and the product of its cycle signs equals one by the order condition.
Successive choices of inverse-pair representatives along the cycle therefore make the generator a pure translation.
A permutation normalizing translations acts affinely on \(\mathbb F_p\), and a sign vector preserving the pure translation subgroup is constant on its single orbit.
Both constant signs and all affine permutations do normalize it, yielding the stated normalizer.
For every normal \(K\triangleleft G\) containing \(S\), Sylow conjugacy inside \(K\) gives \(G=K N_G(S)\).
Consequently \(G/K\) is a quotient of a subgroup of \(C_2\times C_{p-1}\).
For \(K=D\) this gives \(D=G'\), the order divisor \(2(p-1)\) and the exponent divisor \(p-1\), using evenness of \(p-1\).
For any nonabelian quotient the image of \(S\) consequently has order \(p\) and lies in its derived subgroup.

Restriction to a normal subfield sends the conjugation commutators onto those in its Galois group, giving the asserted abelian criterion.
All conjugates of a totally real subfield of \(F\) lie in \(\mathbb R\), so their compositum is a totally real normal subfield fixed by \(D\).
Every intermediate field of the abelian extension \(F^D/\mathbb Q\) is Galois abelian of degree dividing \([F^D:\mathbb Q]\).

### Powers and real exterior roots

For a reciprocal unit \(w\in F\) with all exterior roots real, conjugation fixes real pairs pointwise and exchanges the two elements of each neutral pair.
It lies in an abelian normal sign group in the signed permutation action, so its conjugates commute and the splitting field of \(w\) is abelian.
Nontorsion gives an exterior conjugate by the bounded-coefficient argument on powers.
Conjugation fixes this real conjugate and, by centrality and transitivity, every conjugate of \(w\).

For an element \(y\) of the stated saturation one positive exponent clears both the original radical denominator and the torsion factor, giving \(w=y^N\in\mathcal O_F^\times\).
Both \(y\) and \(y^{-1}\) are integral over the algebraic integers, and the inverse conjugacy and nontorsion each persist under powering.
Extension of embeddings makes the map from conjugates of \(y\) to conjugates of \(w\) surjective, with each preimage of an exterior root exterior.
A neutral conjugate of \(y\) would have real \(N\)-th power of modulus one once \(w\) is totally real.
That power would be \(1\) or \(-1\), which forces torsion.
The remaining interior roots of \(y\) are inverses of real exterior roots, proving total reality for \(y\) itself.
In particular a Salem number of degree at least four is excluded by its neutral conjugates.

In degree \(2k\) each real inverse pair of exterior logarithmic modulus \(s_i\) contributes \(4\sinh^2 s_i\) to the absolute norm of \(y-y^{-1}\), also for negative conjugates.
The norm is a positive integer in absolute value, and \((\log\sinh s)''=-1/\sinh^2 s<0\).
Taking the \(2k\)-th root of Jensen's upper bound for the norm gives \(2\sinh(\log M(y)/k)\ge1\), hence \(M(y)\ge\varphi^k\).
For \(k=1\) the integer trace has absolute value at least three, giving the stronger \(\varphi^2\) floor needed in that case.
This derivation recovers the stated specialization of the [Schinzel height bound](../literature/Sch73/Yin_U001.md#linear-height) without relying on the original proof.

### Two exterior roots

With \(c\ge2\), a neutral conjugate of \(y\) remains neutral in \(w=y^N\).
At most two exterior roots survive, and the preceding criterion forces exactly one nonreal conjugate pair among them.
Its two inverse labels are exchanged and every other label is fixed.
If the splitting group \(J\) of \(w\) were abelian, conjugation would invert every conjugate of \(w\) because it inverts one neutral conjugate.
This would force all moduli to equal one.
Thus the image of \(S\) has order \(p\) in \(J'\) and retains that order in the unsigned action \(A\) because the kernel of \(J\to A\) is a 2-group.

Transitivity of \(A\) makes all components of the graph of conjugate transpositions have the same size \(s\).
Transpositions along paths generate the full symmetric group on a component, and each edge transposition acts trivially off that component.
The image of \(T\) is therefore the full direct product of the component groups, with derived group the product of their alternating groups.
Since this image of \(D\) contains the order-\(p\) subgroup, \(s\ge p\).
Each component then contributes a factor \(p\) to the product order, so \(v_p(|G|)=1\) forces a single component and \(A=S_s\).

Here \(s\ge p\ge5\).
The commutator with a transposition gives a 3-cycle from any permutation having a cycle of length at least three, or having a moved point and a fixed point.
A fixed-point-free involution has \(s\ge6\), and a transposition across two of its orbits first gives a double transposition with a fixed point.
Thus every nontrivial normal subgroup contains all 3-cycles, which precludes abelianity.
The original abelian sign kernel has trivial image in \(S_s\), giving \(s!\le |H|\le p!\).
It follows that \(H=S_p\) and that the map between the unsigned actions is an isomorphism.
Under an isomorphism the conjugacy class sizes must agree.
For \(k\ge2\) disjoint transpositions their class-size ratio to one transposition increases with the number of labels for \(p\ge 2k+1\).
At \(p=2k+1\) the ratio is \((2k-1)!/(2^{k-1}k!)\), which starts at \(3/2\) and increases by a factor \(k(2k+1)/(k+1)>1\).
Taking \(k=c\) gives the contradiction for every allowed odd prime, including \(p=5\).

Without neutral roots inversion pairs the exterior roots bijectively with the interior roots, giving degree two or four.
The all-real case has the preceding bound.
Otherwise the two exterior roots are \(z,\bar z\), and the two distinct traces \(t=z+z^{-1},\bar t\) form a quadratic conjugacy orbit.
The quadratic discriminant is \( -4(\operatorname{Im}t)^2\), a negative integer congruent to zero or one modulo four.
Hence \(|z|-|z|^{-1}\ge\sqrt3/2\), which gives \(M(y)=|z|^2\ge(\sqrt3+\sqrt{19})^2/16=(11+\sqrt{57})/8\).
This number exceeds two and is smaller than \(\varphi^2\).
