# Radicals of conjugate monomials at bounded Mahler measure

## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T17:18 UTC I independently reviewed for Yin the complete [C345 v276 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#decision-bounded-radicals-of-monomials-submitted).
I used the fixed proof and its named mathematical inputs, including the [previously reviewed v270](Yin_R_G0562_v270.md#finding) and the sign alternatives of v274.
I did not participate in developing v276 or receive proof-development discussion or prior conclusions on it.
The asserted restrictions hold uniformly in the exponents, torsion factor and radical order.

### Finite extensions and the compressed degree

Let \(L\) be the splitting field of \(x\), and suppose the ambient finite extension is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\) and solvable over \(L\).
If a subgroup \(U\) of a finite group \(G\) maps onto a nonabelian simple group \(S\) and \(B\trianglelefteq G\), the image of \(U\cap B\) is normal in \(S\).
If it is \(S\), then \(S\) is a section of \(B\).
Otherwise the map factors through \(U/(U\cap B)\le G/B\).
The splitting group of \(x\) has abelian sign kernel and a label image inside \(S_b^u\rtimes S_u\).
Repeated application of the section argument bounds the possible nonabelian simple sections by those of \(S_b\) or \(S_u\).
For \(j>b>u+2\) one has \(j!/2>b!\), so neither group can have an \(A_j\) section.
Solvability of the further kernel preserves the exclusion, as does restriction to the normal splitting field of \(y\). [Block action](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) · [Solvable comparison](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#closure)

The actual class product of \(y\) lies in its splitting field and has the same Mahler measure and compressed degree \(D\) in the sense of v264.
For \(D\) sufficiently large in terms of \(N\), v270 supplies an \(A_{b_y}\) section of this splitting group, and v264 gives \(b_y\gg_N D/\log^{212}(3D)\), also when \(y\) is nonreciprocal.
Hence \(b_y\le b\).
As \(\log^{212}(3D)\le\sqrt D\) eventually, \(D\ll_N b^2\), and inserting \(\log(3D)\ll_N\log(3b)\) back into the first inequality proves \(D\ll_N b\log^{212}(3b)\).
All smaller \(D\) can be absorbed in the constant. [Class products](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) · [Uniform block estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block)

For the actual equation write \(p=\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\), \(t=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\), and \(k=nt\).
Then \(y^k=p^t\).
Adjoin to \(L\) the \(k\)-th roots of unity and all the roots of \(Z^k-\sigma(p^t)\) for the finitely many rational conjugates of \(p^t\).
This is normal over \(\mathbb Q\) and contains every conjugate of \(y\).
Over \(L\) the cyclotomic extension is abelian, and over that extension an automorphism acts on any selected radical by multiplying it by an element of \(\mu_k\), giving an abelian radical layer.
Thus the comparison applies without a bound on \(k\), on \([L(y):L]\), or on the order of \(\zeta\).
In \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}/\mu_\infty\), the relation reads \(n[y]=[p]\).
Multiplication by \(n\) is injective since this quotient is torsion free, so \([p]\) and \([y]\) have identical stabilizers under the absolute Galois action.
The orbit size is exactly \(D\), the number of torsion-quotient classes of conjugates of \(y\), and \([\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\ge D\). [Orbit identification](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#inversions)

### Supported inversions, absolute heights and the first correlation

In the first case of v274 the even-sign subgroup of each block has support only on that block.
The roots represented by different labels are independent, since subtracting the image of a relation by a double flip meeting its support would produce a nonzero relation supported on at most two positions, excluded by v270.
Thus distinct signed images of the formal exponent vector give distinct conjugate vectors.
If exactly \(k<b\) coefficients are nonzero in a block, all \(\binom bk\) supports occur by the alternating action and all \(2^k\) sign choices occur on each support by compensating the parity at an unused position.
If \(k=b\), the parity restriction still gives \(2^{b-1}\) images.
The supports can be transported independently using the product of alternating groups on labels, even if the lifts introduce signs.
Further composition by the independently supported sign subgroups gives the listed count on each transported support, hence the product bound for fixed occupied blocks. [Sign alternative](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) · [Independent actions](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition)

A single occupied block contributes at least \(2b\), and for \(k\ge2\) at least \(2b(b-1)\) for large \(b\), including \(k=b-1,b\) where the exponential bound suffices.
The translates of a set of \(t\) occupied blocks cover all \(u\) blocks by transitivity, so at least \(u/t\) distinct support sets occur.
For one occupied block with two or more nonzero coordinates this gives \(D\ge2ub(b-1)=2v(b-1)\).
For \(t\ge2\), the quantity \((2b)^t/t\) is at least \(2b^2\), giving \(D\ge2ub^2\).
Both contradict the compressed-degree upper bound at fixed \(N\) as \(b\) tends to infinity. [Mixed coefficients](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#inversions)

For the quantitative \(n=1\) estimate put \(a_j\) at a coordinate of maximal absolute value.
Uniform signs in an even-sign block of size \(b\ge3\) have zero pair correlations at distinct coordinates, since any two prescribed signs have equally many completions.
Across independent blocks the correlation also vanishes, while \(\epsilon_j^2=1\).
For the fixed signed logs \(s_i'\) of any Galois image, the absolute value of the correlation of \(\sum_i a_i\epsilon_i s_i'\) with \(\epsilon_j\) is \(|a_j s_j'|\), bounded by the mean absolute value of that sum.
Uniform averaging on the finite Galois group permits insertion of independently uniform sign changes, since multiplication by any such sign element preserves uniform distribution.
For an algebraic unit \(z\), the product formula gives the mean of \(|\log|\sigma z||\) over embeddings as \(2h(z)\).
All \(x_i\) are conjugates of \(x\), and \(p\) is a unit.
This proves \(h(p)\ge\max_i|a_i|\,h(x)\).
For \(y=\zeta p\), height is unchanged and its degree is at least \(D\).
Multiplication by \(D\ge2v(b-1)\) gives \(m(y)\ge(b-1)\max_i|a_i|\,m(x)\) using \(d=2v\).
This last bound needs no prior bound on \(m(y)\). [First-correlation calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#inversions)

### Oriented blocks and the quotient of exponent vectors

In the second alternative v274 gives an unordered, equivariant pair of inverse orientations per block.
Choose either orientation independently in each block.
Every group element transports its chosen roots to the chosen roots or their simultaneous inverses in the image block.
The preimage of \((A_b)^u\) in the signed group stabilizes each block, so its action on these orientation pairs is abelian.
Its eventual derived subgroup still surjects onto the perfect product \((A_b)^u\) and preserves both orientations on every block.
Its action in the chosen coordinates therefore contains the independent pure alternating permutations.
This deduction uses the sign statement only, with no need for the additional field or real-signature conclusions of v274. [Canonical orientations](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement)

Let \(R\) be the rational relation space on representatives, and \(C\) the span of the \(u\) block-constant vectors in the formal space \(W=\mathbb Q^v\).
By v270 any nonzero vector in a block projection of \(R\) has no zero coordinate and all its coordinates have equal absolute value.
Evaluation at a coordinate is thus injective on the projection, which has dimension at most one.
The pure local \(A_b\) preserves this space, acts trivially on any such line by perfectness, and has only constant vectors fixed in its natural permutation representation.
It follows that \(R\subseteq C\).
The blockwise signs of a general group element also preserve \(C\), so the map \(W/R\to W/C\) is equivariant.
Thus the orbit in \(W/C\) gives a lower bound on the actual \(D\), even when a block product has infinite order. [Whole-block relations](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank) · [Quotient construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#orientations)

Inside one block a permutation and the original exponent vector have the same coordinate sum.
If they differ by a constant that constant must therefore be zero.
A nonconstant block has at least \(b\) images, by moving the positions occupied by a value with multiplicity between \(1\) and \(b-1\).
Except for the pattern with a value of multiplicity \(b-1\), either some value has multiplicity between \(2\) and \(b-2\), giving at least \(\binom b2\) images, or all values are distinct, giving \(b!/2\).
Pure sign-preserving block permutations vary these choices independently.
General elements permute the set of nonconstant blocks because they only introduce an overall sign on each block.
With \(t\ge2\) such blocks the count is \(D\ge(u/t)b^t\ge ub^2/2\).
With exactly one having neither constant nor one-exception pattern, it is \(D\ge u\binom b2\).
These quadratic lower bounds exceed \(O_N(b\log^{212}(3b))\) eventually.
The remaining coefficient patterns give the asserted block-product expression, and torsion block products vanish in the divisible group modulo torsion.
For each fixed \(M\), the block-size lower bound makes \(b\to\infty\) uniformly with \(d\).
Applying this for every fixed target bound \(N\) gives the claimed uniform divergence for disallowed patterns, regardless of the choice of \(n\) or of the presentation.
