### Normalization and degenerate parameters

Moving a positive isometry left across a transvection applies its inverse to the parameter.
That inverse is integral, preserves the positive pairing and fixes both cusps, so this operation preserves each parameter norm and the number of transvection factors.
It does not combine parameters or require a bound on how many positive factors occur.
Their ordered product is again an integral isometry of the same positive lattice.
Zero padding reaches exactly \(k\) slots without changing the word or either bound.

For each fixed positive Gram matrix, the column norm constraints give a finite set of integral isometries.
Thus the final \(R\) has a finite order \(N\), with no uniform bound on that order required.
Its entire characteristic polynomial is a neutral monic integer factor.
All transvections and their inverses are integral by the even-norm hypothesis, so the original \(P_A\) is nonzero and monic.

The extension to an empty positive summand is neutral because its only parameter is zero and the permitted positive isometry acts identically on \(U\).
For positive rank, \(1\le B<2\) also permits only zero parameters, since a positive even integral squared norm is at least two.
The proof includes those cases directly, as well as repeated, zero and linearly dependent vectors in every dimension.

### Integer finite columns and the complete determinant

For an e-slot, the three displayed entries of \(D_i\) give exactly
\[
SD_iFx=v_i(e,x)-e(v_i,x)-\tfrac12(v_i,v_i)e(e,x).
\]
The f-slot is identical with the cusp exchanged.
This verifies the formula for arbitrary input \(x\), including when the parameter column and its pairing row both vanish.
Each \(D_i\) is integral.

The ordered-product identity follows by multiplying the two updates and associating the cross term as \(SD(FS)EF\).
Since \(FS=S^{\mathsf T}G_0S\) is integral, induction preserves an integer matrix \(D\) for the complete word.
The argument needs neither independent columns nor a nonsingular Gram matrix on their span.
It remains valid when \(k>g\) or when all parameter columns are zero.
No estimate on the entries of \(D\) is used in the support count.

Factoring \(T=zI-\overline R\) from the original-dimensional matrix gives
\[
zI-A=T\bigl(I-T^{-1}\overline R SDF\bigr).
\]
The rectangular determinant identity then yields exactly
\[
P_A=\chi_R(z)(z-1)^2\det(I_m-DZ).
\]
The order \(DZ\), the factor \((z-1)^2\) and the dimension \(m=k+2\) are all correct.
No square inverse of \(S\) or \(F\) is involved.

The positive entries of \(Z\) are the actual pairings \((v_i,K_Rv_j)\).
Its hyperbolic block is off-diagonal because \(F\)'s e-row evaluates pairing with \(e\), hence the f-coordinate, and conversely.
Mixed entries vanish by the orthogonal direct sum.
The rational prefactor does not assert that the perturbed \(P_A\) itself retains two eigenvalues one; the later cross identity handles any cancellations at resolvent poles.

### Uniform Fourier norm and signed-coset conversion

The functions \(f_{ij}(a)\) are integer valued and periodic modulo the finite order of \(R\).
On the complexified positive space, \(R\) is unitary for the Hermitian extension of \(H\).
Its orthogonal spectral projections give Fourier coefficients \(\langle\Pi_\lambda v_i,\Pi_\lambda v_j\rangle_H\) for the characters \(a\mapsto\lambda^a\), with precisely the normalized convention in the accepted Sanders input.
There is no additional factor of \(N\).
Cauchy--Schwarz within and across the eigenspaces gives
\[
\|f_{ij}\|_A\le\|v_i\|_H\|v_j\|_H\le B,
\]
using the squared-norm bound on each vector.
Repeated eigenvalues are handled by whole eigenspace projections; absent eigenspaces and zero vectors contribute zero.
The trivial group \(N=1\) is included.

Sanders therefore supplies one finite integer \(L(B)\ge1\) valid for all \(k^2\) functions, independently of the group or lattice.
The theorem bounds the sum of absolute integer coefficients, so splitting them into signed repetitions gives precisely the needed length bound.
The zero function may use an empty sum.
Different functions and terms may use different subgroups.

The resolvent series starts at \(a=1\).
For a residue class modulo \(d\), its first positive representative is \(u\) when \(u>0\) and \(d\) when \(u=0\), giving the displayed numerator exponent between zero and \(d-1\).
In particular the full coset for \(d=1\) contributes \(1/(z-1)\), with no constant term.
All converted entries are rational functions equal to the original resolvent entries, not a pointwise approximation.

### Polynomial clearing, nonvanishing and every support factor

There are at most \(k^2L\) denominator occurrences from the positive entries and one extra factor \(z-1\) for the hyperbolic block.
Taking their product with multiplicity makes \(\Delta\) a nonzero monic cyclotomic product with at most \(2^q\) terms, \(q=1+k^2L\).
Every positive numerator can cancel its own actual denominator occurrence in that product, so every entry of \(\Delta Z\) is an integer polynomial.
The numerator exponents are nonnegative, and repeated denominators cause no division or integrality problem.
The bound \(L2^q\) applies to all entries, including \(\Delta/(z-1)\) and zeros.

Since \(\Delta\) is scalar,
\[
U=\Delta^m\det(I_m-DZ).
\]
Combining this with the original determinant yields the exact complete identity
\[
\chi_R(z)(z-1)^2U=\Delta^mP_A.
\]
Both sides are integer polynomials; equality over the rational-function field therefore retains all roots and multiplicities, including former poles.
The right side is nonzero, so \(U\ne0\).
Multiplicativity of complete Mahler measure removes only the neutral factors and proves \(m(U)=m(P_A)\).
Neither a divisibility assertion \(U/P_A\in\mathbb Z[z]\) nor a leading-coefficient omission is used.

An entry of \(D\Delta Z\) sums at most \(m\) constant-integer multiples of entries with support at most \(L2^q\).
Adding its possible diagonal \(\Delta\) term gives support at most \((1+mL)2^q\).
The determinant expansion has at most \(m!\) products of \(m\) such entries, proving exactly
\[
|\operatorname{supp}U|\le m!(1+mL)^m2^{mq}.
\]
Coincident exponents, zero entries and cancellations only decrease that count.
There is no dependence on coefficient magnitude, exponent size, rank, isometry order or the number of normalized positive factors.

This is exactly the nonzero integer-polynomial input of the accepted DS17 univariate consequence.
Its positive constant depends only on that support bound, hence only on \(k,B\).
The DS17 source's nonzero and monomial qualifications are respected; no derivative/height estimate is applied outside its scope.
The new theorem uses neither the older two-factor large-norm estimate nor a Coxeter representation.
