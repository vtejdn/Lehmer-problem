## Fixed-output representatives with a prescribed exterior mass

At 2026-11-16T20:03 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the full submitted statement and argument of C345v32, including the exact inverse and both limits in L462v129.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received the fixed inputs and scope without proof-development dialogue, author explanations or a previous verdict on this claim.
The fixed-output theorem was used at its previously reviewed scope for finite initial terms and for the converse. [Fixed-output review](Yin_R_G0562_v2.md#prescribed-constant-inverse-review).

For each sufficiently late term let \(X=\sigma^k\) be the first power at least as large as the chosen target \(A\).
Both choices in the claim have \(A/d\to\infty\), so eventually \(A\ge32m\) for \(m=d/2\), as required by the controlled inverse.
Division by the monic trace polynomial \(S\) gives an integral \(p\), and the residues of \(p/S\) are \(g(t)\) at the simple trace roots \(t\).
The leading coefficient in degree \(m-1\) is therefore \(\sum_t g(t)=q-1\).
The antisymmetry in degree \(2m-1\) of \((z-1)z^{m-1}p(z+z^{-1})\) preserves the reciprocal sum, and subtracting this polynomial from \(P\) gives constant \(q\) and degree \(2m\).
For an interior trace \(t\), put \(a_t=-g(t)=(t+2)(2-D_k(t))\).
An interior conjugate of the Salem number has infinite multiplicative order, so \(0<a_t<16\).
The residues required by the inverse simplex are \(b_t=(T-t)a_t>0\), and their endpoint sum is \(\sum_{t<T}(T-t)(2-D_k(t))<18m<q+T/2\), using \(q>3A\).
The strict simplex conditions give a Pisot minimal polynomial with exterior root \(\theta>q\).
In its exact excess identity the summands \(b_t/(\theta+\theta^{-1}-t)\) have denominator greater than \(q-2\), and the remaining correction to \(u=T-2\) is \(u/(\theta-1)\).
This gives \(\theta-q=u+O(d/q)\) with absolute implied constant throughout the range used. [Simplex criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex), [excess identity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order).

Substitution in the trace transform gives \(1-q+(x-2)p/S\).
Its constant term at infinity vanishes, and its residue at \(t\) is \(w_t=(t-2)g(t)>0\), including \(w_T=u g(T)>0\).
Writing \(F=f/f^*\) gives \(f/(zf+f^*)=F/(1+zF)\).
Consequently the coefficient of \(z\) in \((1-z^2)f/(zf+f^*)-(q-z)\) is \([z]F-q^2+1=j\).
Since \(1/x=z+O(z^3)\) near zero, this is exactly \(\sum_t w_t\).

Along a hypothetical Salem sequence the degrees tend to infinity because bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound every integral coefficient.
The norm inequality \(|S(2)|=u\prod_{t<T}(2-t)\ge1\) gives the needed upper bound on \(\log(1/u)\).
The one-dimensional orbit equidistributes since its height is \((\log\sigma)/d\) and \(\sigma>1\) has infinite order.
Each interior trace occurs twice, so truncation by \(\max(\log(2-t),-M)\), extended continuously at \(2\), bounds the upper limit of \(d^{-1}\log(1/u)\) by one half of the integral of the truncated function against the arcsine probability.
Letting \(M\to\infty\) gives zero by the integrable circle mean of \(\log|1-e^{it}|^2\).
As \(u\to0\), this proves \(\log(1/u)=o(d)\).
For \(\lambda>0\) take \(A=\lambda d/(4u)\), while for \(\lambda=0\) take \(A=d\min(\log(d+2),u^{-1/2})\).
In each case \(A\le X<\sigma A\), hence \(X/A\to1\), \(k\to\infty\), and \(k\log\sigma=\log X=o(d)\).
The bounded interior terms in \(q=1+\sum_t g(t)\) give \(q=g(T)+O(d)\sim4X\).
This yields \(qu/d\to\lambda\), including zero since \(u\min(\log(d+2),u^{-1/2})\le\sqrt u\), and the excess estimate gives \(\theta-q\to0\).

The height of \((\sigma,\sigma^k)\) is at most \((1+k)\log(\sigma)/d\).
Every proper algebraic subgroup of the two-dimensional torus lies in the kernel of a fixed nonzero character \((r,s)\), while \(\sigma^{r+sk}=1\) would require \(r+sk=0\).
This occurs only finitely often for each such character.
Bilu's Theorem 1.1 therefore applies to the joint orbit of size \(d\). [Published theorem, p. 465](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~demarco/Math295/Bilu_equidistribution.pdf).
One can evaluate bounded continuous tests on both phases by projecting \(z\) to \(z/|z|\) in each coordinate.
Deleting the two real embeddings from such a test costs \(O(1/d)\).
For a bounded continuous \(\varphi\) on the trace line, the two reciprocal lifts of each interior trace then give
\[
 \lim\frac1d\sum_{t<T}w_t\varphi(t)
 =\frac12\iint(4-x^2)(2-y)\varphi(x)\,d\rho(x)d\rho(y)
 =2\int\varphi\,d\mu_{\rm sc},
\]
where \(\rho\) is the arcsine probability and \(\int y\,d\rho(y)=0\).
The exterior contribution requires its actual weight, which satisfies \(u g(T)/d=uq/d+O(u)\to\lambda\) at the moving point \(T\to2\).
The test \(\varphi=1\) gives \(j/d\to2+\lambda\), so division by \(j\) at \(\lambda=0\) gives precisely the semicircle probability.

The fixed-output theorem supplies representatives at the finitely many terms outside the controlled range without changing any limit.
Conversely \(q/d\to\infty\) ensures \(q\ge2\) eventually, and the positive-constant Pisot return applies directly to \(\theta-q\to0\).
The full conditional criterion passes independent review, including both directions of the existence equivalence.
Bilu's theorem was checked at its statement and applicability scope.
The existence of a Salem sequence tending to one remains open.
