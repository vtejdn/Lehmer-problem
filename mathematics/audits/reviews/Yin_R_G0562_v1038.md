At 2026-11-23T08:52 an agent outside the development of the [degree-44 calculation](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/power_catalogue.md#coverage) reviewed its reduction and reproduced its exclusion for Yin.

For a hypothetical root put \(\beta=z^g\) and \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\).
This is a nontorsion unit since \(g\ge1\), and it is a root of the primitive parent.
The [height-five bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v886.md#statement) gives \(M(\beta)<13/10\) and \(u<2D\) for either choice of signs and every \(q\ge5\).
The cubic \(X^3-X-1\) increases on \([1,\infty)\) and has value \(-103/1000\) at \(13/10\), so [Smyth's theorem](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) makes the minimal polynomial reciprocal at every degree.
Odd degree would force the root \(-1\), contrary to irreducibility and the nontorsion hypothesis.
If the minimal polynomial were \(f(X^k)\) for \(k>1\), multiplying \(\beta\) by a primitive \(k\)-th root of unity would give another root of the same polynomial, contradicting the [torsion-translate theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v882.md#statement).
If \(D\le44\), its polynomial or its reflection must therefore occur among the 944 rows in the [reported complete degree range](../literature/Mos26/readme.md#complete-small-cores).
For \(q\ge7\), the hypothesis \(H=q+1\ge8\) of the [degree estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement) gives \(qu\le12D/\log2<18D\).
The program includes all \(u<2D\) at heights five and six, since both heights then satisfy \(qu<18D\).
For larger heights it includes every pair with \(qu<18D\).

Writing \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(b/a)\), substitution of \(T=-s\beta^u\) and \(Y=\epsilon s\beta^v\) converts the parent equation into \((p-qT)Y=pT-q\).
Hence \((p-qT)(Y+1)=T+1\), and integrality forces \(N(p-qT)\mid N(1+T)\) in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
The pole and endpoint are both nonzero, since \(T\) is a nontorsion unit.
Both signs of the short power cover every coefficient sign and the omitted generator reflection, which multiplies the short power by \((-1)^u\).
This necessity holds at every \(v>u\) without a cutoff on the long exponent.

The reviewer independently read all nonempty rows after the column heading in the [original table](../../literature/pdf/Mos26/AllSmallMeasures_200.txt), checking monicity and the stated reciprocal half length.
For each of the 944 polynomials \(A\) through degree 44 and each admissible \(u\), the reviewer computed \(h=\sigma (X^u\bmod A)\) by successive multiplication in \(\mathbb Z[X]/(A)\).
With \(\sigma=\pm1\), the two norms were obtained over the integers as
\[
 E=\left|\operatorname{Res}(A,1+h)\right|,\qquad K=\operatorname{Res}(A,q+1-qh).
\]
For a monic \(A\), these resultants are the products over its roots of the corresponding values, including any repetitions among the powered roots.
For the unit-value filter the reviewer used only \(q\mid D-\operatorname{Tr}(h)\), which follows from the linear coefficient of \(N(1+Q(1-h))\) if its value at \(q\) equals one.
Lagrange interpolation gives the trace as the coefficient of \(X^{D-1}\) in \(hA'\bmod A\).
For other divisors the bound \(q(q+1)\le E+1\) follows because reciprocity and even degree give \(N(1+Q(1-h))=1\) at \(Q=0,-1\).
Thus a pair omitted by both tests fails divisibility.
