## Outcome of the full review

The two primitive signed generators and the uncapped minimum have the same exact rate \(\sum_{j=1}^s(2s-2j+2)t_j\) at width \(e=2s\) for each seed in the stated class.
The margin above \((\log2)/3\) applies to the fixed-seed limits, and the diagonal exclusion holds with the specified choice of a slowly increasing budget.

**Fixed labels and cofactor content.**
Multiplicative independence modulo roots of unity makes all \(x_i^{\pm p}\) distinct, since any identification would give a nonzero integral relation modulo torsion.
In the formal matrices the variable nodes and the labels \(c_i=\varepsilon x_i\) play different roles.
For each choice of the other variable nodes away from each other, their inverses and the fixed points, the remaining-row kernel at \(N=0\) is \(H_0\langle1,Z,Z^2\rangle\).
Here \(H_0\) has simple roots at those other nodes and is nonzero at the two fixed points.
In the fixed-point computation of L253 the factors that have to be nonzero are \(c,\ 1+c,\ 1-c^2\).
At a moving-node collision the calculation uses \(c\ne d,\ cd\ne1\), with \(d=c_j\) or \(c_j^{-1}\), as well as the simple zero of \(H_0\).
These conditions hold for distinct inverse pairs under either sign.
The contradictions are valid with arbitrary complex absolute values of the labels.
When \(s=1\), one has \(H_0=1\) and the collision with another pair never arises.
The same fixed-point calculation and the nonzero extremal coefficients at zero and infinity still apply.
If a common divisor had positive degree in any variable, the other nodes could be chosen in the indicated open set while keeping the leading coefficient of this divisor nonzero as a polynomial in \(N\).
Specialization at a sufficiently small nonzero \(N\) would then contradict the absence of a common root in that variable.
This leaves only a possible factor depending on \(N\).

**Simultaneous attainment of the weight bound.**
Each minor selects \(2s\) distinct columns and consumes exactly two of them per node.
For \(u_i\ne0\), the larger of the two weights for column \(d\) is \(s u_i+|d-s||u_i|\).
The base terms sum to \(2s\sum_i u_i\).
When all \(|u_i|\) are positive and distinct, the rearrangement bound for the remaining terms is attained by taking both copies of each distance from \(s\) down to one, in that order against pairs of equal \(|u_i|\).
Thus for the center-deletion minor, equality forces the node ranked \(j\) to use the two columns at distance \(s-j+1\) and forces its exponent in each column.
There is precisely one block assignment before permutations inside these two-column blocks.
At a positive node the local determinant of the two maximizing columns is \(c_iN(e-j+1)-c_i(1+N(e-j+1))=-c_i\).
At a negative node it is \(c_i(1+N(j-1))-c_iN(j-1)=c_i\).
Hence the coefficient of the maximizing exponent vector in this single minor is \(\pm\prod_i c_i\), independently of \(N\).
This proves attainment at all nodes simultaneously and excludes a nonconstant common \(N\)-factor.
The maximum over a finite support is continuous, giving the same formula for the center minor at tied or zero weights before specialization.

**Specialization and genuine rows.**
At any embedding the distinct exponential bases associated to distinct monomials have nontorsion quotients, even when several of their moduli coincide.
Applying Xiao's local estimate after dividing by a base of largest modulus gives the support maximum at that embedding with error \(o_P(p)\).
The specialized minors are integral.
A nonzero minor and a fixed integral linear combination of the tuple can be chosen coprime by avoiding its finitely many irreducible divisors.
For the almost-unit theorem the sole outside-archimedean cost is \(\log p\), whereas one conjugate's height is \(ph/e\).
For fixed tolerance, eventual nonvanishing of every fixed nonzero polynomial on the specialization removes the exceptional closed set.
The resulting finite logarithmic loss is \(o_P(p)\).
The archimedean absolute weights sort to the same \(t_j\) at every embedding by permutation and inversion of the pairs.
The linear term \(e\sum_i u_i\) in the support formula has mean zero by the product formula for the units.
These facts yield the asserted projective heights without imposing the Salem condition on the moduli.

At \(N=0\) both signed line generators are multiples of the reciprocal monic polynomial of the formal nodes, which has constant coefficient one.
The two cofactors defining the signed scales are nonzero generically, and the edge expression there is twice their product.
Nonvanishing after specialization proves the required eventual existence.
Reversal transfers both jet conditions to each inverse root, and the remainder equations give the rational signed lines.
For an integral primitive generator, projective log height is the log of its largest absolute coefficient.
In L242 the parity multiplier is one or two, and the maximum of the one-norms of a sum and a difference is between the larger individual norm and twice that norm.
This verifies passage to the least cost of a genuine, coprime pair.

**Quantifiers at growing degree.**
The ordered weights have arithmetic mean \(s+1\), and pairing their differences with those of the decreasing \(t_j\) proves the lower bound \((s+1)h\).
Voutier's all-degree corollary yields a rate greater than \((e+2)/(\log(3e))^3\).
This diverges as the degree tends to infinity.
The derivative of \(x/(\log(3x))^3\) has the sign of \(\log(3x)-3\) for \(x>1/3\), giving minimum \(\exp(3)/81\).
The elementary comparisons in the ledger give \(\exp(3)/81>512/2187>7/30>(\log2)/3\).
This is a common margin for the limiting rates.
At a fixed degree bound the necessary constraint \(p\,m(P)\le\log H\) and the coefficient bound leave a finite set of seeds, so a common onset exists at that degree.
Choosing those onsets successively larger yields a budget \(B(p)\to\infty\) for which the claimed restricted exclusion is eventual.
The result asserts existence of such a budget and leaves its effectivity, prescribed growth, dependent representatives and larger row widths outside the conclusion.
