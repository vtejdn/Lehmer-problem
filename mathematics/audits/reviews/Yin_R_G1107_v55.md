## Agreement with the fixed profile and with a moving exceptional parent

A second reviewer outside development independently checked the complete equivalence section for Yin on 2026-11-16T08:53 UTC.

The second reviewer received only the fixed section and scope, and consulted its mathematical inputs without the proposed ordering, development discussions, author explanations or earlier conclusions on the equivalence.

For an exceptional integer perturbation in the strict chamber, C892 v1 gives \(L\le Cs\) for every matching length \(L\).
Its sharper exceptional-family conclusion is among the results adopted in [C892 v2](../../claims/Yin_C_G0892_v2.md#decision-general-parent-prefix-reviewed).
Thus \(L\to\infty\) forces \(s\to\infty\), and the identity \(F_s/(1-z)=(1-z^{2s})P_{*,0}\) gives fixed-profile agreement through \(\min(L,2s-1)\to\infty\) directly.

For the converse choice \(s=\lfloor(p-4)/4\rfloor\), the difference \(p-4s\) is five or seven, giving the required \(r=p-2s>2s+3\) and \(n=m+s\).
The neutral lift is \((1-z^r)(1-z^{2s})P_{*,0}\), and its residues \((4-b_j^2)b_j^2 E_{s-1}(b_j)^2/p\) are nonnegative at all nodes.
Hence the actual negative last residue forces the integral difference \(H=Q-Q_0\) to be nonzero.
The first term of \(z^{n+1}H(z+z^{-1})\) lies at index \(n+1-\deg H\) and has coefficient \(\operatorname{lc}(H)\).
Agreement through \(L'=\min(\ell,2s-1)\) therefore gives \(\deg H\le n-L'\le n-2\) eventually.
This represents the original \(Q\) and \(P\) on their original grid and preserves the strict endpoint and every residue sign.

The three initial coefficients of the common fixed profile are \(1,1,4\).
Thus \(Q/B=P(1-z)/(z(1-z^p))=z^{-1}+3z+O(z^2)\).
Comparison with \(t-A+(\sum_j\rho_j)t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})\) gives \(A=0\) and \(\sum_j\rho_j=2\), so the remainder \(Q-tB\) has degree \(n-1\) and leading coefficient two.
Both descriptions now satisfy the strict sign and endpoint hypotheses of C881 v10 with the same fixed reference \(U=(1+z^2)^2\) and \(V=(1-z)(1-z^2)\).
All roots of these two polynomials lie on the unit circle, \(U(0)=V(0)=1\), and their degrees sum to seven.
The matching index truncated to \(n+1\) still tends to infinity, giving \(1<M(P)\le(8q+10)^{2/q}\to1\).
This application follows the prefix transfer, so the conclusion holds even if \(\log s\) grows faster than the original moving matching index.
The equivalence and both measure implications hold at the stated scope, with existence of such an integer sequence open.
