# Review of the growing second-iterate threshold for a single Mahler value

<a id="finding"></a>
## The leading constituent, the growth constant and both preimage applications

At 2026-11-25T01:54 UTC an agent outside the development of v117 independently reviewed the [whole statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v117.md#statement), all three proof sections and their cited interfaces for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed candidate without development dialogue on v117.
The measure-product estimate remains valid with nonzero lower block constants, and the two alternatives, inverse growth bound, recurrence exclusion and primitive Salem preimage restriction hold at the specified thresholds.

**Extending the two orbit estimates.**
For \(m<\log 2\) both the leading and the nonzero constant coefficient of the primitive minimum of \(w\) have absolute value one, since each absolute value is bounded by \(M(w)\).
This places \(w\) in the unit setting of the chain.
For any nontorsion \(x\) in its divisible conjugate group, compression to \(\gamma\) preserves total measure and gives one distinct conjugate for each class orbit point.
The first active constituent \(W_i\) belongs to \(V_x\), and \(V_x\subseteq V_{z_i}\).
Kernels reverse the inclusion, so \(L_\gamma\subseteq L_{z_i}\), even if \(x\) lies outside both fields.
In the oriented case the embedded sum-zero subspace and the image of the constants are disjoint.
Projection to the former is equivariant, and the projected vector has orbit size at most \(D=\deg\gamma\).
The projection itself can be on an axis even though the full class does not match any actual root line of \(z_i\).

If \(D\le d_i^6\), the combinatorial proof in v103 concerns any nonzero projected vector and requires no absence of block constants from the full class.
Independent alternating actions and sign changes, or the centered value multiplicities in the oriented model, mark at most six occupied blocks and six labels per block.
These mark a nonempty invariant set, so an actual root above a marked label has orbit of size at most 72 under the stabilizer of \(\gamma\).
Its joint field with \(\gamma\) has degree at most \(72D\).
They are multiplicatively independent because the excluded match is for the full class, independent of the direction of its projection.
The two-coordinate AD99 estimate then gives \(m(x)m(z_i)\gg d_i/(\log(216D))^{212}\), implying (3) with an absolute constant.

If \(D>d_i^6\), work in the splitting group of \(z_i\) itself, which acts on \(\gamma\).
The fourth power of a lift of a supported three-cycle has order three and fixes all actual roots off its chosen three inverse pairs.
There are at most \(16\binom{d_i/2}{3}\) such signed permutations, bounding the entire conjugacy class.
If every conjugate preserved the rational line of \(\gamma\), it would fix its class since the only rational cube root of one is one.
Normality and the nonzero projection would then force the three-cycle to fix the whole irreducible \(W_i\), contrary to its explicit action.
Thus two independent conjugates of \(\gamma\) have joint degree \(O(d_i^3D)\).
Their heights both equal \(m(x)/D\).
AD99 gives \(m(x)^2\gg D/(d_i^3(\log(3D))^{212})\), since \(d_i^3<D^{1/2}\).
Monotonicity at large \(D\) and \(D>d_i^6\) yield \(m(x)\gg d_i^{3/2}/(\log(3d_i))^{106}\).
Multiplying by Voutier's bound \(m(z_i)>2/(\log(3d_i))^3\) suffices for (3).
In this case field inclusion removes the need to fix any other input field in order to choose the lift.
All chain degrees exceed the requisite thresholds uniformly when \(m\) is sufficiently small by bounded-degree finiteness.

**The two alternatives for \(A\).**
The positive measure value \(A\) is a signed product of the exterior conjugates, hence belongs to \(D(w)\).
On an actual full-coordinate match, the previous leading projections of the exterior product vanish, giving constant colors in each preceding block.
The associated positive integer half-product multiplicities give \(A=M(z_i)^E\) with zero loss at those steps.
At the first matched stage, subtract the full rational coordinate multiple before projecting to \(W_i\).
In the oriented case the difference between the selected entry and a second entry of the same block equals that multiple, so its absolute value is \(1\) or \(2\) since all exterior-product coefficients are \(0,\pm1\).
In the independent case the absolute value is one.
This use of the matched part of v109 depends only on the actual match.
Complex conjugation fixes the selected class and hence its actual compressed root.
Sign and inversion give \(r>1\) with \(M(z_i)=M(r)=r^j\).
Distinct conjugates of \(r\) stay distinct under powering, giving \(\mathbb Q(r^k)=\mathbb Q(r)=\mathbb Q(A)\) for \(k\ge1\).
The unique largest exterior-subset product makes \(A\) Perron and gives the same property for \(r\).
This proves \(M(A)=A^j\).
For \(j=1\), reciprocity and large degree make \(r\) and its powers Salem.
For \(j=2\), \(A\) is a square in its own field.
These facts justify the nonreciprocal exclusion and the primitive non-Salem exclusion.

**The constants at a growing ceiling.**
In the unmatched case put \(t=\log d_i\), \(a=(4m)^{-1/3}\).
Voutier's strict degree-times-height estimate together with \(m(z_i)\le m\) gives \(t>a\log t\).
At uniformly large stages this implies \(t>a\) and consequently \(t>a\log a\).
The logarithm of the lower bound (3) for \(m(A)\) is at least \(t-212\log(t+\log3)-\log m-O(1)\).
The \(t\)-dependent part is increasing eventually.
The leading term at \(a\log a\) is \(m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), and the subtracted logarithm is lower order.
Thus each fixed smaller positive \(\eta\) is valid with its own smallness threshold.

For the inverse estimate, \(m(A)\le B\) and \(m<1\) give \(d_i\ll B(\log(3d_i))^{212}\).
Taking logarithms gives \(\log d_i=O(\log B)\) first, and then \(\log d_i\le\log B+O(\log\log B)\).
The eventually decreasing function \(t\mapsto(\log t)/t\) in Voutier's formula gives the lower bound with coefficient \(1/4-o(1)\).
For inputs above a fixed positive smallness threshold the vanishing right side is automatically below \(\log A\).
In particular the uniformity is over the two specified exclusions and the input degree is unrestricted.

**Positive recurrences and single-number preimages.**
For \(R>0\) the sum \(\sum c_iR^{i-n}\) decreases strictly from infinity to zero, and at \(R=1\) it is at least two.
Hence the unique positive root \(R\) exceeds one and its inverse is not a root, excluding reciprocity of its minimum.
As that monic minimum divides the monic integral parent, its multiplicative measure is at most the parent's, which is bounded by the coefficient sum \(1+C\).
The logarithmic measure of \(R\) is thus at most \(\log(1+C)\), uniformly in the exponents, contradicting (1) for a sufficiently near-one Mahler value.
This uses the nonnegative coefficients and the fixed bound on their sum.

For \(A=\sigma\) primitive Salem, \(m(\sigma)=\log\sigma=m(w)\); the unmatched branch is impossible at sufficiently small positive \(\log\sigma\).
In the matched branch primitivity in \(\mathbb Q(\sigma)\) forces \(j=E=1\), so \(d_i=\deg\sigma\) by the full degree of powers.
If \(i=1\), the compressed preimage \(z_1\) is the prescribed signed conjugate or inverse.
If \(i>1\), then \(\deg\sigma=d_i\le d_2\le2u_1\).
Combining \(d_1=2u_1b_1\) with the sharp two-label estimate \(b_1\gg d_1/(m(w)^2(\log(3d_1))^{212})\) gives (4).
All preceding multiplicities equal one because they are positive integers with product \(E=1\).
This is a restriction on every preimage consisting of a single algebraic number and on its compressed degree; existence of further high-degree preimages does not follow from it.
