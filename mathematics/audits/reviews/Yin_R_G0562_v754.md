## Integral coefficient and quadratic estimates

At 2026-11-20T18:56 UTC, a separate agent acting for Sheng Yin reviewed the entire submitted [claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v617.md#statement).
The reviewer did not participate in its development and received the fixed proof and its scope without development dialogue, an author's explanation or earlier findings about this proof.
All five sections hold under their stated hypotheses.

For the analytic factorization, cancel every interior pole with its full multiplicity.
The remaining rational function has only finitely many possible boundary poles, whose positive logarithms under radial approach are dominated in \(L^1\) by a constant plus a sum of multiples of \(\log^+(1/|\zeta-\zeta_0|)\).
The analytic Poisson integrals for the dilates therefore converge locally uniformly, so the disk bound on the numerator passes to the limit.
The resulting denominator has value \(I(0)\exp(-\int\log^+(|H|/C)\,d\mu)=e^{-T_C(H)}\) at zero.
For the selected monomials the projected multiplication matrix is a contraction and triangular with this constant diagonal.
Its smallest singular value is at least its determinant modulus because every other singular value is at most one.
At the first \(k\) occupied positions the compressed product gives the actual coefficients of \(gH\), since any omitted nonzero coefficient lies beyond these positions.
Parseval for the bounded numerator gives the asserted squared norm.
For an infinite integral series the first \(k\) occupied positions contribute at least \(k\) to the squared norm, and the first crossing of integral energy \(J\) reaches at least \(J+1\) within \(J+1\) occupied positions.
Clearing a pole at zero preserves the boundary modulus and the nonzero pole sum and gives the stated version for integral shifted coefficients.

For the quadratic pencil, the roots over each regular circle point have product one and real sum \(-W/U\), giving the claimed fiber integral by Jensen.
Logarithmic singularities of the univariate coefficient and quotient are integrable.
If \(m(U)<\log 2\), both extremal coefficients have absolute value one, and Jensen at the lowest coefficient pays for every nonzero interior pole of \(W/U\), even after cancellation.
The monomial-normalized quotient has integral Taylor coefficients and is infinite by Laurent nondivisibility.
Equation (1) with eleven occupied positions then gives \(11\le 4\exp(22T_2)\), and the fiber integrand dominates the positive logarithm in \(T_2\).
In the hyperbolic application, the inverse of every root of \(P\) is again a root, so \(A(X)A(X^{-1})\) is nonzero at every such root, even when \(B\) shares roots with \(P\).
This verifies the required nondivisibility, and on the circle \(W/U=s-2\) for \(s=|A/B|^2/u\ge0\).
The fiber contribution is exactly \(D(s)\) and the base measure is \(m(P)+2m(B)\).
For a pointwise certificate the cited automatic coprimality applies, \(B=1\), and the fiber contribution vanishes, which gives the same lower bound on \(m(P)\).
The total-cost estimate controls the limiting whole-specialization bound and supplies no lower bound on a chosen factor of a specialization.

For the upper tail, the two polynomials \(V\) and \(V^2-2\) take the values \(2\cos t,2\cos(2t)\) when \(V=2\cos t\).
When \(|V|>2\) their moduli are \(2\cosh D(u),2\cosh(2D(u))\).
After a monomial shift the costs are thus bounded by \(\ell'\) and \(2\ell'\).
The crossing threshold at \(J=4\) is \(\log(5/4)/10\).
If \(20\ell'<\log(5/4)\), both energies are at most four.
For \(E=2\) the single symmetric pair forces \(u\) to vanish somewhere, and for \(E=3\) the constant \(\pm1\) and that pair give two coefficients of magnitude two in \(V^2-2\).
For \(E=4\) its constant is two and both outer coefficients are nonzero.
These exhaust the possibilities because integrality and nonconstancy give \(E\ge2\), and each contradicts positivity or the second energy bound.
Only strict positivity, integrality, symmetry and nonconstancy of \(u\) enter here.

For the rows, each of the \(\binom dj\) elementary-symmetric products is bounded in modulus by the product of \(\max(1,|\alpha|)\) over the whole fiber.
After integration and addition of the pole contribution this gives \(T_{C_j}\le m(F)\) for the monomial-normalized nonzero row quotients.
The unit lowest coefficient of \(U_d\) gives integrality at zero, so \(m(F)\) below the minimum of the finitely many crossing thresholds forces all these series to terminate with the stated energy bounds.
Monomial shifts preserve support size, and summing \(\binom dj^2\) gives the claimed bound \(\binom{2d}{d}\) on the support of \(F/U_d\).
For this bivariate factor I checked the definition of \(\mathcal H_k\) and Theorem 2 on page 2 of the authors' revision.
It covers integral Laurent polynomials in arbitrary finite dimension, so zero is isolated for this support bound.
The earlier source unit records the multivariate statement and had used its univariate consequence for a different application.
This review uses the primary theorem at statement and applicability depth, without auditing its original foundations. [Dobrowolski and Smyth, Theorem 2](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~chris/papers/Dobrowolski_Smyth_revision_submit0816.pdf)
The comparison with the earlier bounded-width theorem matches that theorem's horizontal-component hypothesis and its allowance for a base-only factor.
