## Pencil measures, radial counts and the scale in the prime window

The reviewer received the fixed proof without development dialogue or author explanations and had independently reviewed the reciprocal-family input.

The leading coefficients of the two degree-\(k\) homogenizations cannot both vanish, and finite coprimality excludes all other common projective zeros.
Consequently the absolute binary resultant \(B\) is positive and is at least one for integral rows.
If \(V\) has degree \(l<k=\deg U\), its degree-\(k\) homogenization contains \(k-l\) factors of the coordinate vanishing at infinity.
This produces exactly the multiplier \(|\operatorname{lc}U|^{k-l}\), including when \(V\) is constant.

For \(W_t=U-tV\), Jensen's circle formula applies even where a row vanishes or both moduli agree.
The positive logarithms are uniformly bounded on a compact set times the parameter circle, and the average signed logarithm is the continuous function \(f\).
The negative parts are thus integrable there against area or circle measure and parameter Haar measure, justifying both the distributional Laplacian and the later exchange of circle integrals.
The leading coefficient \(L_t\) vanishes for at most one phase, and \(\log|L_t|\) is integrable in that parameter.
In particular the averaged root measure has mass one, even when some fiber has a root at infinity.
Subtracting \(k\log^+|z|\) gives \(\Delta g=2\pi k(\nu-\sigma)\) in the plane.
In the reciprocal chart the maximum of the two reversed row moduli is positive near zero, so its logarithm is Lipschitz there.
There is no atom at infinity in the distributional identity on the sphere.
Smooth approximation of the Lipschitz \(g\) uniformly and in the Sobolev norm justifies pairing with the finite signed measure and gives \(\mathcal D(g)=k\int g\,d(\sigma-\nu)\) with the stated sign and factor.

The absolute binary resultant of \((W_t,V)\) stays equal to \(B\) under the row operation.
At each of its finite pencil roots \(f=\log|V|\), and on the full-degree fibers the root product gives \(\log B=k\log|L_t|+\sum\log|V(z)|\).
The factor is \(k\) even for a lower-degree \(V\), as required by the homogenization.
The ordinary root formula gives \(m(W_t)=\log|L_t|+\sum\log^+|z|\).
On averaging these equations, the latter nonnegative root sum has finite mean \(I-a\), and \(f\) is bounded below on the plane, so neither subtraction involves an indeterminate difference.
Thus \(k\int g\,d\nu=\log B-kI\), while \(\int g\,d\sigma=I\), proving the identity for arbitrary nonzero coprime complex rows with \(k>0\).

For a single root \(b\), its angular mean logarithmic potential is \(\max(s,\log|b|)\) at radius \(e^s\), with value \(s\) if \(b=0\).
Its slopes are bounded by one, so differentiation of the averaged potential at almost every \(s\) gives the cumulative pencil count.
In logarithmic polar coordinates the integral of the squared derivative of the angular mean is bounded by the full energy with exactly the stated normalization.
The two intervals of length \(\tau\) give the claimed lower bound, including atoms on either boundary circle.
For \(U=z^k,\ V=e^{-k\tau}\) the derivative equals \(k\) on \((-\tau,0)\) and vanishes elsewhere, giving equality.

Stripping each row separately preserves its circle modulus and removes all possible monomial common factors, so the binary resultant and the row integral apply to these rows.
Primitivity and multiplicativity give \(m(F)=2h+m(R)\), including extra copies of \(P\) retained in \(R\).
The cited lower row comparison uses the exponent span of the original witness, and the pointwise triangle bound adds at most \(\log2\).
All degree-preserving primes in the stated window exceed four for sufficiently large \(d\), preserving the inequality direction when the energy is bounded below by the radial estimate.
For every one of those primes and every pair of residues the cofactor comparison supplies a primitive witness with \(m(R)\le2ph\), since division by scalar content preserves \(R\), the rows and their degrees up to common scaling.
For every witness meeting this bound,
\[
  0\le\frac{I}{p}\le
  \frac{2h+2h/p+2\log(CD)/p^2}{1-4/p}.
\]
The cap gives \(\log(CD)=O(\log(3d))\) uniformly, and \(p\) is of order \((\log(3d))^4\).
Hence when \(h\to0\), both nonnegative terms of (1) are \(o(kp)\) uniformly over all such choices.
The annular conclusion follows by dividing (2) by \(kp\), even if \(\tau\) varies with the witness.
A uniform lower bound on \(\mathcal D(g)/(kp)\), or on \(\tau(n_-^2+n_+^2)/(kp)\), on all witnesses at one chosen admissible prime per input would contradict every shrinking sequence.

In the reciprocal family the positive \(x=(\log p)/p+4\log I_p/(pd)\) tends uniformly to zero.
The ceiling \(j=\lceil d\sqrt{x}\rceil\) therefore satisfies \(1\le j\le d\), \(dx/j\le\sqrt{x}\), and \(j/d\le\sqrt{x}+1/d\).

The denominator coefficient bound thus gives \(\log|\operatorname{lc}U|/p\le\log(6j)/p+dx/j=o(1)\).
With \(m=\deg V\le j\), the ordinary resultant estimate divided by \(p\) has terms bounded by \(\log(2j)/p,\ dx/j,\ jh/d\), and \(\log(3j/2)/(2p)\).
Since \(N/k\le2\), this proves \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|/(kp)\to0\) uniformly.
The additional homogeneous factor costs at most \(\log|\operatorname{lc}U|/p\) at the same normalization.
These witnesses have exact multiplicity two and lie below the comparison cap, so \(I\ge m(F)-\log2\ge ph/3+2h-\log2\).
Identity (1) now gives the claimed positive lower bound for \(\mathcal D(g)/(2kp)\), uniformly in the prescribed pairs and primes.
