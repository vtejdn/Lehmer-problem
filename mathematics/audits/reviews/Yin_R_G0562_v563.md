### Bounds and completeness

For a root \(\alpha\) of a proposed polynomial, put \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\) and \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):F]\).
The norm \(u=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/F}(\alpha)\) satisfies \(u^k=\alpha^{ke}\), hence \(h(u)=e h(\alpha)>0\) and \(1<M(u)\le M(\alpha)\le T\).
The [published degree theorem](../literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) gives \([F:\mathbb Q]\ge56\) since \(T\) is less than Lehmer's number.
A zero of any of the 34 auxiliaries at either signed trace would give \([F:\mathbb Q]\le2\deg Q\le16\).
Thus both signed resultants used in the ellipse inequality are nonzero.
I used the published theorem at statement scope.

Each weight row fixes the same function on the two boundary ellipses for all four degrees.
Convexity in the logarithmic radius, inversion symmetry and the bound \(k\log T\) on the sum of those logarithms give the upper bound \((D/2-1)U_{0,k}+U_{k,k}\) for either signed sum.
I reapplied the rational Bernstein and logarithm certificate to all 62 unit and outer caps used from the [degree-64 weights](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v7/readme.md#weights), using the stored rational witness points.
The regenerated certificates and every floor in the four boxes agreed.

Write \(h=D/2\).
In the integral expansion \(P(X)\equiv\prod_{j\le h}(1-X^j)^{e_j}\pmod{X^{h+1}}\), one has \(s_j=\sum_{i\mid j}i e_i\).
When a prime \(q\mid D\) and \(q\nmid j\), every divisor of \(j\) is also prime to \(q\).
If all sums at such proper divisors vanish, their Euler exponents vanish by induction, so \(j\mid s_j\).
For \(j>16\) in each box, \(B_j<j\) forces \(s_j=0\) in this case.
Consequently a prefix supported on multiples of \(q\) forces the first-half coefficients, and by reciprocity the full polynomial, to be supported on multiples of \(q\).
The converse follows from the same expansion.
In these degrees any nontrivial substitution has a prime exponent dividing \(D\) and yields a smaller polynomial of the same measure in degree at most 31.
This excludes the substitution by the published degree theorem.
Removing the case \(q=2\) leaves an odd-indexed nonzero sum through 15.
The involution \(X\mapsto-X\) changes its sign, giving exactly one representative with the first such sum positive, including when \(s_1=0\).
I checked the four prime lists and reran the independent congruence-tensor counts with inclusion and exclusion for every initial pair.
The largest product of the coordinate branching bounds \(\prod_j\lceil(2B_j+1)/j\rceil\) was \(2\,354\,388\,664\,320\), so the final unsigned counts lift uniquely.

### Reflection and arithmetic

For \(17\le j\le h\), every proper-divisor sum has already been fixed and each interval contains one or two representatives modulo \(j\).
Changing \(s_j\) by \(j\) subtracts the shift \(X^jP_0\) from the base coefficients through the center, since two such shifts have product of order at least 34.
Let \(V_j\) be that shift through the center followed by its reflected coefficients.
For \(k=h+1,\ldots,h+9\) the linear contribution of this change to \(s_k\) is \(k\) times the coefficient of \(X^k\) in \(V_j/P_0\).
In computing it the worker subtracts the coefficient of \(X^jP_0\) from its reflection at each \(t>h\) and uses \(1/P_0\) through degree eight.
Since \(V_j/P_0=X^j+O(X^{h+1})\), replacing a quadratic product by its leading monomial first introduces error in degree at least \(h+18\).
The cubic term starts at 51, and \(17+25=42>h+9\) places every relevant quadratic contribution in the lower list.
At degree \(2j\) the square contributes \(j\), and at \(i+j\) the two cross terms contribute \(i+j\).
I checked the list indices for each center, including \(V_h=X^h\).

Beyond the center the bound \(D-2+T^k+T^{-k}\) follows by bounding each inverse pair by \(2\cosh(k\log|\alpha|)\) and concentrating the available logarithmic radius in a single pair by convexity.
I independently constructed the rational trace and Graeffe caps and the coefficient majorants \(C_0=1,\ C_k=\lfloor\sum_{j=1}^k B_j C_{k-j}/k\rfloor\).
The first-half Newton numerators are at most \(\max_j B_j\sum_{i=0}^h C_i\), with maximum \(52\,751\,636\) over the four degrees.
At each subsequent comparison the earlier accepted caps bound the absolute Newton numerator.
My maxima for the first nine reflected numerators were \(9\,663\,652,\ 12\,895\,985,\ 16\,110\,905,\ 33\,721\,058\), respectively.
This justifies the centered 32-bit lift on each branch at its first failure as well as on surviving branches, even if the individual lists or the base recurrence wrap modulo \(2^{32}\).
The four maxima for the later signed sums were \(3\,608\,741\,989,\ 4\,388\,472\,657,\ 5\,006\,526\,607,\ 9\,256\,235\,975\), within signed 64-bit arithmetic.
Both endpoint sums include the middle coefficient once with its correct parity.
The \(\Phi_3,\Phi_4,\Phi_6\) Horner coordinates are bounded by the full coefficient one-norm because each reduced monomial has coordinates in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
That norm is at most \(2\sum_{i=0}^h C_i\le3\,767\,974\).
Squaring roots gives the even-power coefficients of \(P(X)P(-X)\) and raises the Mahler measure to its square.
At round \(g\) each centered residue magnitude modulo \(2^{64}\) is a lower bound on the absolute integer coefficient.
The comparison with \(\lfloor\binom Di T^{2^g}\rfloor\) can therefore only exclude a polynomial of measure greater than \(T\).
