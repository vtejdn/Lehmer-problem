# Review of the full power-index estimate for a row resultant

## The finite valuation inequality and its fixed-seed asymptotic hold

At 2026-11-24T19:02 an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the complete [row resultant claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v8.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed claim and scope, read its cited mathematical inputs and received no development discussion or previous verdict on this proof.
The local inequality, optional exponent-prime contribution, global estimate and counterexample hold with the stated normalizations.

At a powered node the two rows cannot both vanish, and the graph expresses the original conjugate as a rational function of the powered node.
This gives distinct powered nodes and justifies the determinant of the change of order bases as the product of the powered differences divided by the original differences.
At \(\ell\ne p\) a pair colliding before powering has the same valuation after powering, since the divided difference reduces to \(p\bar x^{p-1}\ne0\).
All contributions to the index from other pairs are their full powered valuations.
In the descending matching each missed positive edge is charged to one edge of at least its weight sharing a vertex.
The pair of edges in this charge identifies the missed edge uniquely, as precisely one edge was selected.
Deleting selected pairs that already coincided before powering therefore leaves total weight at least \(v_\ell(I)-E_\ell\).

For each retained pair the completed local ring admits the two inverse branches of the power map on its common residue disc, with distinct constant residues.
In the branch of \(x_i\), substitution of \(Y=y_i+T\) into \(P(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) gives divisibility of \(U+XV\) along that branch by \(T^2\).
The second branch gives divisibility by \((Y-y_j)^2\), since the power map is unramified and the branch takes value \(x_j\) there.
The two branch values differ by a unit in \(B_s\), so both \(U\) and \(V\) vanish in that ring.
The Taylor parameter \(T\) is square-zero in \(B_s\), and multiplication by \((Y-y_j)^2\) on the free rank-two Taylor module has determinant \((y_i-y_j)^4\).
This gives normalized length \(4r_{ij}\) also over a ramified splitting field.

On the degree-below-\(2b\) Hermite interpolation lattice, the two monic basis polynomials for representative \(s\) contribute a triangular diagonal block with determinant \(\prod_{t<s}(y_{i_s}-y_{i_t})^4\).
Taking the quotients \(B_s\) can only decrease the interpolation cokernel length.
Each representative cross-valuation is bounded by the earlier selected edge's weight since the later representative was free at that selection.
The cross terms charge distinct summands of \(E_\ell\).
The degree ceiling for the Sylvester target follows from the nonzero lowest coefficient of \(pY(U'V-UV')-UV\), including when a row is constant.
This polynomial vanishes at all \(e\) nodes, so \(\deg U+\deg V\ge e\ge2b\).
The cokernel of the Sylvester map surjects onto the image of its target in \(\bigoplus_s B_s\) because \(U,V\) vanish there.
Its normalized length is the valuation of the ordinary resultant.
The matching and interpolation losses contribute at most \(4E_\ell\) each.
For \(p\ge3M_p\), the separate exponent-prime bound applies with offset one and contributes on a prime omitted from this sum.

For triples containing a torsion ratio, the common valuation is bounded by that of one of finitely many nonzero differences between a root of unity and one.
For the other triples the vectors of archimedean logarithms of the conjugates are distinct points on one sphere, so the two differences from a fixed point are linearly independent.
The two unit ratios therefore satisfy the multiplicative independence hypothesis of Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2, for all positive powers.
Their heights grow at most linearly with \(p\), giving a total common-divisor loss \(o_P(p)\) for each triple.
The local-degree weights over \(\ell\) sum to one for the symmetric quantity \(E_\ell\) and valuations normalized by \(v(\ell)=1\). [Proposition 2, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311030)
For all sufficiently large \(p\), the original reductions are distinct at \(p\), and Frobenius preserves that property.
For a pair of equal complex moduli with nontorsion ratio, the nonzero logarithmic form has fixed algebraic arguments and integer coefficients \(O(p)\), giving the cited \(O_P(\log(2p))\) error.
For unequal moduli the ratio of the smaller modulus to the larger decays exponentially on powering.
The unit norm then gives \(\log|\operatorname{disc}Q_p|=pD(P)+O_P(\log(2p))\), and division by the fixed discriminant yields (1).
The external gcd and logarithmic-form results were used at theorem-statement scope. [Baker–Wüstholz bound in §5, pp. 5–6](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/07999/excerpt/9780521807999_excerpt.pdf)
Hadamard's inequality uses precisely the opposite row degree for each Euclidean norm, and the sum over opposite signs and zero log moduli alone gives \(D(P)\ge e\,m(P)\).
For the Salem signature the pair sum is \(2(e-1)\log\rho\).

For the explicit example, \(P(X-1)\) is Eisenstein since \(P(-1)=-1022\) has \(7\)-adic valuation one, and the trace \(-342\) excludes torsion.
The fractional linear graph recovers the original root and the differentiated identity kills the first derivative because \(7x^6\ne0\).
The constant and leading coefficients of \(U\) are nonzero while \(V(0)=0\), and the two degrees equal eight, proving the asserted nonzero reflected determinant.
At 11 the six reduced roots of \(P=\Phi_7\bmod 11\) are distinct, and the fractional linear identity gives valuation one to each of the fifteen powered differences.
The scaled monic polynomial \(11^{-6}Q(1+11Z)\) has squarefree reduction and derivative of unit leading coefficient.
At each derivative root the valuation of \(Q\) is therefore six.
The bracket multiplying \(Q\) in \(U\) contributes one further unit of valuation there and at \(Y=a\), while \(U(0)\) is an 11-adic unit.
This proves coprimality and yields resultant valuation \(49\).
As an independent arithmetic check, Newton sums of \(P\) through order 42 give \(Q\) from the sums of orders \(7,14,\ldots,42\).
Exact fraction-free Sylvester determinants gave \(v_{11}(\operatorname{disc}P)=0\), \(v_{11}(\operatorname{disc}Q)=30\) and \(v_{11}(\operatorname{Res}(U,V))=49\), confirming \(4v_{11}(I_{7'})=60\).
