# Square radicals of Salem seeds at translated delays

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T03:33 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin completed the independent review of [C345 v736](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v736.md#statement), including its two-parameter bound (5), and recommends acceptance of the complete claim.
The reviewer took no part in its development and inspected the fixed claim and its inputs without the author's development notes or a previous verdict on this version.

In the notation of the defining polynomial the even-seed test of v716 permits a field square root only at sign plus and even delay, where \(a+1=w^2\) and \(w\) is odd.
The odd-seed test of v718 first excludes even delays at both signs by the trace and norm conditions.
At odd delays it gives the two different signs and endpoint values stated in v736.
For \(a\equiv1\bmod4\), the square \(2(a+1)\) has the form \(4w^2\) with \(w\) odd, and \(w=1\) would give the excluded seed \(a=1\).
At \(a\equiv3\bmod4\) substitution of \(n=2k+1\) in the differentiated endpoint value gives exactly \(1+(a+1)k\).
The lower endpoint \(M\ge6\) guarantees positivity.
The sign restriction makes the bound for that progression applicable even though the exceptional-delay definition uses a union of the two signs.
The equivalence between absence of any positive field square root and irreducibility at every dyadic substitution follows for this positive Salem unit directly from the largest positive exponent and its divisors in v205.

The \(K\) odd delays give \(K\) consecutive integral indices in the progression with positive step \(A\).
Translation of the first index changes only the initial value, so the statement of Bombieri and Zannier inspected in BZ02 applies without an upper bound on the translate or on the step.
Increasing the constant covers \(K=1\) after replacing the logarithm by \(\log(2K)\); \(K=0\) has no permitted delay.
The resulting upper bound is \(o(N)\) uniformly as \(K\le N\).
For the other bound the interval of \(k\)'s lies in one of length \(N/2\), making the interval of positive square roots at most \(\sqrt{AN/2}\) long.
No rounding allowance is needed beyond the one permitted root per congruence class in addition to the interval length divided by \(A\).
For fixed \(A\), counting positive roots in those classes up to \(\sqrt{1+A\lfloor(6+N-1)/2\rfloor}\) gives leading factor \(\rho(A)/\sqrt{2A}\); excluding the finitely many lower endpoints changes only \(O_A(1)\).

The local counts of \(x^2=1\) give two classes at each odd prime power, two at modulus four, and four at every higher power of two, since the smaller of the valuations at \(x-1,x+1\) is one for odd \(x\).
Their Chinese remainder product divided by \(\sqrt A\) attains its dyadic maximum \(\sqrt2\) at modulus eight.
The odd prime-power factors have maximum product \(2/\sqrt3\) at modulus three, as every other factor is strictly below one.
Thus the maximal coefficient in front of \(\sqrt N\) is \(\sqrt2(2/\sqrt3)/\sqrt2=2/\sqrt3\), at \(A=24\).
Here \(\rho(24)=8\), giving \(8/\sqrt{48}\) in the fixed-seed asymptotic.
The claimed attainment concerns only the count passing the endpoint test.
The residual additive term is \(O_c(N^{1/4})\), since each large odd prime divisor absorbs its factor two in \(A^{1/(4c)}\) and the remaining small divisors contribute a constant.
This proves the stated translate-uniform estimate on \(A\le N^c\).

The order-doubling threshold agrees with direct valuation computation at its endpoints.
For \(\ell\equiv1\bmod4\), the least exponent giving one modulo \(2^s\) at \(s\ge h_\ell\) is \(2^{s-h_\ell}\).
For \(\ell\equiv3\bmod4\), writing \(u=v_2(\ell+1)\), it is \(2^{s-u}\) for \(s\ge u+1\), since every admissible exponent there is even.
Thus passage from \(s\) to \(s+1\) doubles the residue orbit length in all three uses.

For the even-seed row the two reductions in the indicated odd characteristics are \((X-1)(X^n+1)\) and \((X+1)(X^n-1)\).
In the former case the primitive root of order \(2^{1+v_2(n)}\) has \(n\)-th power minus one, and in the latter the order \(2^{v_2(n)}\) gives \(n\)-th power one.
For the odd-seed row in characteristic \(\ell\mid a\) the reduction \(X^{n+1}+1\) uses the first choice with \(n+1\) in place of \(n\).
In each case the primitive order is at least four when the selected threshold is reached, so neither linear binomial cofactor of the original Salem polynomial removes that root.
Stripping the \(\ell\)-part from the exponent in characteristic \(\ell\) leaves a separable binomial and proves the asserted odd multiplicity, even with arbitrarily high \(\ell\)-adic exponent.
The ratio of orbit sizes under dilation two is exactly two by the valuation calculation, hence is coprime to this multiplicity.
The fixed criterion in v717 therefore forces irreducibility after \(X\mapsto X^2\).
Reaching the minimum threshold suffices because its defining finite prime set contains a prime attaining it.
The sets use only odd primes in either row and give \(b\ge1\).
Translating to \(t=n+1\) in the second row preserves interval length, and the at most \(N/2+1\) even integers minus at least \(N/2^b-1\) multiples of \(2^b\) gives (4) with its displayed endpoint error.
The perfect-power exclusion is a valid extra restriction, while the comparison with v720 concerns its distinct two-parameter count.

For the extended result (5), each of \(w^2-1\) and \(2w^2-1\) in a translated seed interval of length \(H\) allows at most \(1+\sqrt H\) positive \(w\)'s by subtraction of square roots.
Charging every delay to every one of these seeds costs \(O(N\sqrt H)\) since \(H\ge1\).
In the other class \(a=4u-1,\ n=2k+1\) the required square is \(1+4uk\).
Both indices are strictly positive at the allowed lower endpoints.
For fixed \(u\) the progression has step \(4u\) and at most \(\lceil N/2\rceil\) terms, while for fixed \(k\) the step is \(4k\) and there are at most \(\lceil H/4\rceil\) terms.
Empty progressions contribute zero, and the uniform estimate from BZ02 covers lengths starting at one.
Thus the contributions in the two orders of summation are \(O(HN^{3/5}(\log(2N))^{C_2})\) and \(O(NH^{3/5}(\log(2H))^{C_2})\).
Taking their minimum and adding the quadratic-seed cost gives (5) with independent translations and no dependence on either initial index.
For \(N=1\) the \(O(\sqrt H)\) term is absorbed by \(O(H^{3/5}(\log(2H))^{C_2})\), so the claim covers even a single delay chosen after the seed interval is specified.
