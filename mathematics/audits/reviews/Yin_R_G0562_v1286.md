## The exact count and the full period on strengthened rows

The count equals the number of full admissible words on each specified row, and the construction realizes both order estimates on a single sequence for every prescribed \(\omega(d)\to\infty\).

For the count, cubing transfers a carry from \(m3^k\) to \(m3^{k+1}\), so each truncated coordinate adds modulo \(3^{s_m}\).
The v201 lifting criterion sets precisely these coordinates to zero.
Counting indices \(m3^k\le L\) gives \(\sum_m s_m=L+(\nu-1)(L-\lfloor L/3\rfloor)\).
Since \(N\) is odd, inverse pairs account for every root except \(1\), which an even-degree squarefree reciprocal divisor must omit.
The orbit subsets therefore enumerate distinct factors once each, and the coprime Hensel lift keeps their root orders.
Restricting to \(\mu_n\) imposes exactly that the order divide \(n\), so Möbius inversion isolates order \(N\).
For each \(n\mid N\) there are at most \(N\) orbit updates of \((d+1)|G|\) entries with at most \(N+1\) bits per entry.
The fixed subalgebra separates irreducible components because for any two of them at least one basis vector has unequal coordinates.
Splitting by those vectors, pairing reciprocal factors and extracting digits through \(3^{\nu-1}L\) consequently take polynomial time in \(d\) at fixed \(q,h\), using \(N=O_{q,h}(d)\).

Newton's identities force the first \(L\) moments to vanish, including indices divisible by three.
Frobenius and inversion propagate the zeros to \(\Omega\).
Since \(3\nmid N\), inverse Fourier transformation with frequencies supported on \(D\mathbb Z/N\) gives period \(N/D\) for the indicator.
The lifted roots of each resulting coset are a full fiber of the \(D\)-power map and contribute a polynomial \(X^D-\gamma\).
For coprime \(B,C\), support on the two axes gives a sum of two one-variable functions.
If both varied, the two nonzero changes from a fixed corner of their rectangle would coincide, and the opposite corner would take the third value in characteristic three.
For \(13^a\), \(v_{13}(3^3-1)=1\) gives order \(3\cdot13^{j-1}\) at \(13^j\), and adjoining inversion doubles that odd order.
The classes of \(1,2\) represent the two unit orbits at every level, yielding the stated coverage through valuations \(e-1\).

For the model rows, the exponent progressions of v198 preserve prescribed prime valuations while new prime divisors are adjoined.
Hence one can fix \(H>L\) throughout and enlarge \(R\) with \(g/R\) arbitrarily small.
The strengthened degree class is independent of \(R\), with \(d=2tS,\ A=tn,\ 15\mid n\), \(n\) odd, and \(|A-\theta d|\) bounded independently of the stage.
Thus \(N=A+2u\) is odd and prime to three.
At fixed \(R\), \(d/w\to R/\theta\), and for large enough \(R>H\) the \(R-H\) allowed labels exceed the budget while the primitive orbit fits.
Stopping at the first orbit that would exceed the budget gives \(K\le\Delta<K+wg<\varepsilon d\) on sufficiently large rows, taking \(0<\varepsilon<1\).
The complete fibers defining \(U\) carry order \(wR\) and avoid \(\mu_H\), since the \(w\)-power map permutes \(\mu_H\).
The integer \(\deg V\) also gives \(\Delta/w\ge\operatorname{dist}(d/w,\mathbb Z)\).
Its lower bound tends to a positive number because rationality of \(R/\theta\) would give multiplicative dependence of \(p\) and \(p/q\), contradicted by any prime dividing \(q\).
As \(w/d\to\theta/R\), this gives the required \(\delta_R>0\) at fixed \(R\).

In the filler, \(Q\) is invertible modulo \(H\), \(y<H\) is bounded, and the lower bound on \(\Delta\) eventually makes \(x\ge1\).
The coefficients of \((1-X)^{2Q}\) in degrees \(1,\ldots,L\) vanish modulo \(3^\nu\) by the choice of \(Q\), while \(H>L\) and eventually \(w>L\).
Thus the product has the strengthened prefix, and the v198 pole correction preserves the chosen word and the even-side word simultaneously.
The original dyadic bounds follow from that same even-side word, and the degree estimate uses the same fixed progression required by the real estimate.
The multiplicities on \(\mu_H\) are \(2x\) away from \(1\) and \(e=2x+2Qy\) at \(1\).
All remaining roots are simple, so capping multiplicities by \(3^k\) gives the displayed gcd formula for every \(k\ge0\).

In the truncated ring with \(Y^e=0\), reduction modulo three requires \(3^s\ge e\) for \((1+Y)^{3^s}=1\).
For these \(s\), the coefficient at \(i<e,\ i\ge1\) has valuation \(s-v_3(i)\), whose minimum is attained at \(i=3^{\lceil\log_3 e\rceil-1}\).
This gives exact order \(3^b\) with \(b=\nu-1+\lceil\log_3 e\rceil\), also when \(e\) is a power of three.
The two filler divisibilities give \(3^b\mid\operatorname{ord}_{G_0}X\mid H3^b\).
Coprime reductions lift to comaximality over \(\mathbb Z/3^\nu\), so the order of the whole word is the least common multiple with \(N\), exactly \(N3^b\).
At fixed \(H\), the inequalities \(\delta_R d/H\le\Delta/H\le 3^b\le3^\nu\Delta\) allow the degree to be chosen with \(\omega(d)\ge H/\delta_R\) after each choice of \(R\).
Letting \(\varepsilon\) decrease gives the two asserted growth bounds while the degree returning at \(N\) is \(d-\Delta+H\).
These conclusions concern selected words on actual rows.
The power-of-thirteen example is local, and the existence of full words of order exactly \(N\) on infinitely many strengthened rows remains open.
