# Review of the rational-map degree bound for algebraic numbers of bounded measure

<a id="finding"></a>
## The prime comparison gives the stated liminf constant one

At 2026-11-24T21:35 an agent outside the development of the proof independently reviewed the four fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without development discussion, author explanations or prior findings on this argument.

For \(d\ge2t+1=l\), distinct embeddings give \(l\) distinct conjugates \(x_i\), all with \(B(x_i)\ne0\) since the given value is finite and the rational pair is reduced.
If \((C,D)\) satisfies the interpolation equations over the normal closure, \(CB-DA\) of degree at most \(2t\) vanishes at those \(l\) points.
The resulting polynomial identity and coprimality imply \((C,D)=S(A,B)\) with polynomial \(S\).
Since one of \(A,B\) has degree \(t\), \(S\) is constant.
The interpolation matrix thus has rank \(l\), and its signed \(l\)-row minors give a nonzero vector proportional to the actual coefficients.

After deleting any column, the row for \(x_i,y_i\) has \(l\) entries bounded at an archimedean place by \(\max(1,|x_i|)^t\max(1,|y_i|)\).
Hadamard gives the factor \(l^{l/2}\) for each minor, while the finite-place bound has factor one.
Summing the local bounds with normalized place weights gives (2), since conjugates have the same absolute height and the total archimedean weight is one.
The scalar relating the minors to the rational primitive coefficient vector disappears by the product formula.
That vector has projective height \(\log H\), including when neither entire individual row is primitive.
The nonzero equation \(A(X)-yB(X)\) over \(\mathbb Q(y)\) gives \([\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q(y)]\le t\).
Thus \(h(x)\le M/d\) and \(h(y)\le tM/d\), using the respective actual degrees in the total measures.
These estimates include denominators at all finite places.

At \(v\mid p\) one can choose integral local coordinates for \(x\) with maximum absolute value one, irrespective of whether \(x\) is integral there.
The reductions of \(A_h,B_h\) have no common projective zero over the algebraic closure of the residue field because their resultant is a \(v\)-unit.
Both output coordinate pairs in the comparison therefore have maximum norm one, also when the original input reduces to infinity.
In the ring \(\mathcal O_v/p\mathcal O_v\), raising a sum to \(p\) is additive in characteristic \(p\), and the rational integer coefficients satisfy \(c^p=c\) in that ring.
Applied to each homogeneous polynomial this gives the two coordinatewise congruences modulo \(p\mathcal O_v\).
The calculation remains valid in a nonreduced quotient at a ramified place.
It does not use the generally false congruence \(z^p=z\) for arbitrary local coordinates.
In particular the determinant of the two pairs belongs to \(p\mathcal O_v\), giving the full absolute-value factor \(|p|_v\).

If the projective points were equal, the stated cross polynomial of degree at most \(2pt<d\) would vanish at \(x\) and hence vanish identically.
The rational-function identity would make the finite set of zeros and poles in \(\mathbb C^\times\) equal to its full preimage under powering.
The preimage has \(p\) distinct points per member, forcing this set to be empty.
The nonconstant map is then \(cX^k\) with \(k\ne0\), and \(c^{p-1}=1\) gives \(c=\pm1\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
This is excluded.
The same cross-polynomial test is valid when \(R(x^p)=\infty\), since the homogeneous pair never vanishes simultaneously.

For the resulting nonzero determinant, its local absolute value divided by the maximum norm of each coordinate pair is unchanged under local rescaling.
It is at most two at infinite places, at most one at every finite place and at most \(|p|_v\) above \(p\).
The weights of the places above \(p\) give \(\sum_{v\mid p} [\mathbb Q(x)_v:\mathbb Q_p]\log |p|_v/[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q]=-\log p\), with the convention extending the usual \(p\)-adic absolute value.
Applying the product formula using global coordinates therefore yields \(\log p\le\log2+h(y^p)+h(R(x^p))\).
The integral homogeneous forms have local evaluation bound one times the input maximum to the \(t\)-th power at finite places, and \((t+1)H\) times that power at infinite places.
This proves (4), including a projective value at infinity whose height is zero.

For \(t\le2\log d\), condition \(d\ge2t+1\) holds eventually and (2) gives \(\log H=O_M(\log d\,\log\log d)\).
The Sylvester matrix of the homogeneous resultant has \(2t\) rows of Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt{t+1}H\), also after coefficient padding for the lower-degree row.
There is no common point at infinity over characteristic zero because at least one row has degree exactly \(t\).
Thus the nonzero integer resultant has logarithm \(O_M((\log d)^2\log\log d)\).
At \(X=d/(4t)\), the prime number theorem for the von Mangoldt sum gives a contribution of order \(X\) on \((X/2,X]\), and the proper prime powers contribute only \(O(\sqrt X\log^2 d)=o(X)\).
This leaves a good odd prime in that interval, since the sum of the logarithms of distinct prime divisors of the resultant is bounded by its log absolute value.
Here \(2pt<d\), and \(p(h(y)+th(x))\le M/2\).
Equation (4) yields \(\log H\ge\log d-\log(8t)-\log(2(t+1))-M/2\).

Combining the two height bounds gives \(\log d\le(t+\tfrac12)\log(2t+1)+O_M(\log(2t)+t^2/d)\), forcing \(t\to\infty\) on this subsequence.
Since \(\log(2t+1)\le\log\log d+O(1)\) and the additive error is \(o(\log d)\), this gives \(t\ge(1-o(1))\log d/(\log\log d+O(1))\).
On the complementary indices \(t>2\log d\) the normalized quotient tends to infinity.
This verifies the liminf constant one for the full sequence, without assuming a fixed map, integral inputs, an equality of the two root fields or a lower bound on the positive measures.
