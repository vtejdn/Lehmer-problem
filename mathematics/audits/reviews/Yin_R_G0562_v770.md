## Separation, escaping classes and quadratic multipliers

The threshold \(2E_*\), the decomposition of every minimizer outside the exceptional orbits, and the three classifications of large quadratic integer multipliers follow under the stated hypotheses.

For a fixed Laurent representative \(Q\), every other representative has the form \(Q-PB\) with \(B\) integral Laurent.
Reality and exponential decay of the Laurent coefficients of \(Q/P\) allow nearest integers to be chosen with finite support, including either choice at a tie.
This verifies attainment in each class for general hyperbolic \(P\).
In the [finite minimum theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v622.md#finite-search), isolation applies to the energies of *all* admissible numerators, which is needed before rounding the two parts separately.
The classes in \(R\) differ from zero precisely when \(P\) does not divide the representative, so the [universal energy bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) applies to \(E_*\).

For a minimizer under the energy cutoff \(T\), Parseval bounds the sum of the absolute coefficients by \(H=\lfloor M^2T\rfloor\).
Across a gap \(g\), the mixed term is bounded by \(2V\|B\|_1\|C\|_1/g\le VH^2/(2g)\) because every exponent difference has absolute value at least \(g\).
Each nonempty part costs at least \(M^{-2}\), including a part divisible by \(P\).
Once the error is strictly smaller than this cost, deleting a divisible part would strictly improve the representative of the same class.
Thus both parts at such a gap are admissible.
For the lower sublevel, \(g>G\) makes the error strictly smaller than \(\varepsilon\) even at an integral cutoff, forcing \(e(A)>2E_*-\varepsilon\).
At most \(H\) occupied positions therefore fit in width \((H-1)G=W\), and for \(2E_*-\varepsilon<E_*\) the sublevel is empty.
After translation to \([0,W]\), the product of the embedding bounds is \(H^d\prod_\rho\max(1,|\rho|)^W=H^de^{mW}\).
A unit translate preserves absolute norm, while \(|N(kx)|=|k|^d|N(x)|\) tends to infinity.

In the upper window, the minimizers with no sufficiently large gap belong up to translation to one finite set of polynomials.
Excluding all orbits of their classes ensures the argument applies to every minimizing numerator of each remaining class.
At a large gap the sum of the two energies is strictly less than \(2E_*+2\sigma/3\).
As both are at least \(E_*\), isolation forces both to equal \(E_*\).
For the constructed classes \([(1+X^n)A]\), the norm grows as \(|N([A])|e^{nm}\) because every root is strictly inside or outside the circle and \(m>0\).
This forces escape, while the fixed coefficient sums make the mixed energy tend to zero.
The finite-sublevel lower bound and the trial representative then give the actual rounded limit.

In the reciprocal quadratic case, each exceptional orbit contains at most two integer values because \(|N(k)|=k^2\).
For two monomial minimizers, oddness and strict increase of \(D_j=\alpha^j-\alpha^{-j}\) force equal signs and opposite exponents since the supports are disjoint.
For two translates of \(1-X\), conjugation forces opposite signs and equality of the two values \(\alpha^u+\alpha^{-u-1}\) and \(\alpha^v+\alpha^{-v-1}\).
Their symmetry about exponent \(-1/2\) and strict increase away from that point force \(v=-u-1\), giving the stated consecutive trace difference up to sign.
For a mixed pair at \(a=4\), the difference \(D_{v+1}-D_v\) is unchanged under \(v\mapsto -v-1\).
For \(v\ge1\) the recurrence puts this value strictly between \(D_v\) and \(D_{v+1}\), and \(v=0,-1\) forces \(|u|=1\).
Hence no mixed pair yields an unbounded family.
Conversely the conjugate monomial pair and the oppositely signed binomial pair give the claimed integers with gaps tending to infinity.
The exact minimizer types in each row and the decay of their mixed term yield the upper cutoff for every sufficiently large listed value.
Together with the scalar lower bound this gives equality of the quadratic lower limit for each fixed \(a\).
