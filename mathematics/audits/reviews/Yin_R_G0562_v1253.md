# Independent review of effective finiteness at bounded reciprocal support

## Uniformity of the gap estimate and the finite search

At 2026-11-25T13:40 UTC a reviewer outside proof development checked the full [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#statement) and [argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#gaps) of C0901 v175 for Yin.
The assignment supplied the fixed text and the scope without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior finding on v175.
I consulted the earlier findings on the trace realization and the initial zero prefix and checked the interfaces used here.
All three bounds and the effective finiteness assertion follow for the stated integral realizations.

Write \(M=q(q+1)\), \(p=q+1\), and \(h=1/M\).
The [realization theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) gives irreducibility of \(H\), a trace root \(x\in(0,h)\), and \(D-1\) conjugates in \((-4,0)\).
At any negative trace conjugate the discriminant \(x(x+4)\) of the lift is negative, so it cannot be a square in the totally real field \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
The exterior root \(\lambda\) therefore has degree \(2D\), its minimal polynomial is \(F\), and its only exterior conjugate is itself.
The trace at \(p/q\) is \(h\), giving both \(1<\lambda<p/q\) and \(q^{2D}F(p/q)=M^DH(h)=K\).

Reciprocity permits either ordering of \(p,q\) in the homogeneous evaluation.
With \(u\) even and \(t\) odd, the first summand \(t^w\) makes \(\Gamma_w\) odd, and every term past a given occupied index \(w\) is divisible by \(u^j\), where \(j\) is the next occupied index.
No exact cancellation can give \(t^{2D-w}\Gamma_w=K\), because \(t\ge3\), \(w<2D\), and \(2q+1\) is congruent to \(1\) modulo \(q\) and to \(-1\) modulo \(p\).
These checks include the initial prefix and the gap ending at \(2D\).

In Yu's Theorem 1 take two bases in the quadratic field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\) and the prime above \(2\).
The polynomial \(X^2+X+1\) is separable and irreducible modulo \(2\), so \(e=1\), \(f=2\), and rational valuations agree with \(v_2\).
Both \(t\) and \(\Gamma_w/K\) have valuation zero, the multiplicative difference is nonzero and \(3D\) bounds the two exponents and \(3\).
The theorem applies to units of either real sign and imposes no multiplicative independence hypothesis.
The rational height of \(\Gamma_w/K\), after reduction, is at most \(\log\max(|\Gamma_w|,K)\), and the modified height takes the maximum with \(\log 2\).
Thus the source constant is fixed as \(q,D,w,j\) vary, and (2) includes the full dependence on the second height. [Yu, pp. 337–339, equations (1.2), (1.5), (1.8)–(1.10)](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa89/aa8944.pdf)

Every product of distinct roots in the elementary symmetric sum for \(c_i\) has modulus at most \(M(F)=\lambda\).
Hence \(|\Gamma_w|\le(w+1)(1+1/q)[2D(q+1)]^w\), which also suffices for \(w=0\).
Put \(X=\log(9D)\).
From \(0<\log M(\lambda)<1/q\), Voutier's Corollary 2 gives \(2q<(\log(6D))^3\), hence \(\log(2q+1)\le\log 2+3\log X\).
Inserting these bounds in (2) and using \(v_2(u)\ge1\) gives the asserted recurrence with an absolute effective multiplier.
Its \(s-1\) iterations give \(2D+1\le[C_0\log^2(3D)\log X]^{s-1}\).
Taking logarithms yields \(X/\log X\le As\) with an effective \(A\ge2\), since \(D\ge2\) and the two end coefficients are occupied.
For \(Y=2As\log(2As)\) one has \(\log Y\le2\log(2As)\), so \(Y/\log Y\ge As\).
The monotonicity of \(X/\log X\) for \(X>e\) proves the effective degree cutoff.
Voutier's main theorem at \(d=2D\ge4\) also gives \(q<4(\log d/\log\log d)^3\).
Indeed \(\log X=\log\log d+\log(1+\log(9/2)/\log d)\), so \(\log X/\log\log d\) is bounded by replacing \(d\) by \(4\) in the positive correction divided by \(\log\log d\).
This explicitly controls the small degrees and gives \(q\ll s^3\). [Voutier, Theorem and Corollary 2, pp. 3–4 of the author edition](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.3110)

For each allowed \(q,D\) the binomial coefficient bounds give finite integer ranges for every coefficient of \(F\).
The unit triangular change of coefficients in the [grid-independent test](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#digits) recovers the unique candidate \(H\) from a monic reciprocal \(F\).
The exact pole identity, squarefreeness and Sturm counts with endpoints excluded decide whether some separating grid realizes it.
This enumerates each pair \((q,H)\) once even when several grids realize it.
The two published theorems were checked at statement and application scope, with their original proofs accepted as external inputs.
This review establishes effectiveness in principle and gives no numerical census or unrestricted Mahler gap.
