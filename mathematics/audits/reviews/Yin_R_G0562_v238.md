## The two spans and the exceptional centers

At 2026-11-17T13:12 UTC an agent for Yin independently checked the [fixed v238 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#decision-reciprocal-four-both-primes-submitted).
The reviewer received the fixed text, scope and dependencies without proof-development discussion or earlier findings on this submission and did not develop its proof.
Both degree alternatives, the bounds for positive-measure divisors, and the consequences for reduced exponents hold.

At \(p\mid A\) one has \(H=X^{-k}P\), with \(T=D-2k,\ j=-k\), so \(T-j=D-k\) and \(\gcd(T,j)=1\).
At \(p\mid B\) one has \(H=P\), with \(T=D,\ j=k\).
In both cases \(\bar H\) has multiplicity \(N\) at each nonzero residual root of \(X^T+\sigma\), by the separability of its prime-to-\(p\) part.
Multiplication by the monomial between \(H\) and \(P\) preserves that multiplicity on the residue torus.
A unit factor has all its roots in the torus, as does the whole parent at \(p\mid B\) by its two endpoint units.
For odd \(p\) and positive sign, \(\xi^T=-1\) forces the exact two-part \(2^{v_2(T)+1}\) of the lift's order, so the orbit degree is divisible by \(F\).
Also \(F\le 2^{v_2(T)}\le h\) by Euler's theorem.
This includes \(F=1\) when \(T\) is odd.
At \(u\le w\), or \(p=2,\ u=w+1\), the orbit degree alone gives the claimed bound with an absolute constant.

In the remaining range \(J=p^{u-w+1}-1\) satisfies \(7\le J<N\) and \(J-2=pN/p^w-3\ge N/p^w\).
For every \(1\le i\le J\), \(v_p(i)\le u-w\) gives \(v_p\binom Ti\ge w\).
The coefficients coming from \(E\) already lie in \(p^w\) times the integer ring, including the negative-exponent binomial series.
For odd \(p\), the constant \(\sigma E(\xi^j-\xi^{-j})\) has valuation \(w\) unless \(\xi^{2j}=1\).
In the exceptional case \(\xi^{2T}=1\) and coprimality imply \(\xi^2=1\), so there are at most two such centers.
The linear coefficient is then \(\sigma(-CT+E\xi^j(2j-T))\), with valuation \(w\) since \(p\nmid 2j\) and \(u>w\).

For \(p=2,\ \sigma=-1\), the odd order of \(\xi\) divides \(T\), so \(\xi^j=\xi^{-j}\) requires \(\xi=1\).
There the constant vanishes, and \(CT+E(T-2j)\) has valuation \(w+1\) since \(u\ge w+2\) and \(j\) is odd.
In the quadratic coefficient the \(C\)-term has valuation \(u-1>w\).
As \(4\mid T\), reducing \(\binom{T-j}{2}-\binom j2\) modulo two gives \(\binom{-j}{2}-\binom j2=j\bmod2\), a unit for either sign of \(j\).
This gives the index two witness at a single center.
For \(p=2,\ \sigma=1\), the constant is \(2C+E(\xi^{-j}+\xi^j)\).
When \(w\ge2\) its valuation is one, so integrality and the first unit coefficient at \(N\) give one segment of slope \(-1/N\), including \(N=1\).
When \(w=1\), cancellation requires \(\bar\xi^j+\bar\xi^{-j}=1\), and hence the lift has order three, since powering by \(j\) preserves its order.
At either primitive cube root division of the linear coefficient by two gives that same nonzero trace times \(E/2\bmod2\), since \(u\ge3\) and \(j\) is odd.
Thus the sum of the required positive witness indices over all centers is at most two in each case.

Except in the valuation-one single-segment case already treated, the unit power series relating \(H\) and \(P\) has integral inverse and preserves the first index \(i_0\) of exact valuation \(w\).
Earlier coefficients have greater valuation, and the remaining coefficients through \(J\) have valuation at least \(w\).
With the original abscissas, the first unit lies at \(N\), and the polygon height at \(i_0\) is at most \(w\).
Any negative-slope segment extending to its right must end at some \(q>J\), since it ends at a coefficient point strictly below \(w\).
The drop from \(i_0\) to \(q\) is at least its absolute slope times \(q-i_0\), by convexity, and is at most \(w\).
Thus at most \(i_0\) roots escape the relative ramification bound \((J-2)/w\), including the exact roots at the lift.
Only segments before the first unit are used in this count, so the argument also applies when the leading coefficient of \(P\) is divisible by \(p\).
A rational factor with a nonexceptional root contains its conjugates over the unramified splitting field and their conjugates in every class of the Frobenius orbit.
This proves the claimed \(NF/(wp^w)\) bound, with at most two roots in total available to a factor composed solely of exceptional unit roots.

The width inequality for four distinct terms gives \(m\gg d/D\) uniformly in the coefficients.
For small positive measure the endpoint and nonreciprocal estimates give a reciprocal unit, and the radial floor gives both \(4dm^2\ge1/10\) and \(2dtm\ge1/10\).
Here \(t\) is the largest log modulus and inversion pairs the positive and negative logarithms.
The first inequality deals with degrees at most two.
The width bound combines directly with the second degree alternative when \(T=D\), or when \(T=D-2k\ge D/2\).
For \(k>D/4\), leading-term domination gives \(e^{kt}\le K\), so \(d\ge k/(20m\log K)\) and the width bound yields \(m^2\gg1/\log K\).
The displayed right sides are bounded, which justifies returning to all positive measures and adding integer content.
The common dilation uses the same coefficient length after dividing both exponents by \(g\), and v210 gives an individual loss of at most \(R_L\).
For fixed coefficients this implies the stated necessary divergence for each prime.
For odd fixed \(p\), successive squaring gives \(v_2(p^{2^j}-1)=v_2(p^2-1)+j-1\) for \(j\ge1\), and hence \(F\gg_p2^{v_2(T)}\) in the plus case, including bounded initial moduli.
