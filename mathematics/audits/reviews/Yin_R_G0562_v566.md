### The exact matrix and its determinant

A torsion quotient of two selected roots would give \(x^{an}=y^{a'n}\) for conjugates of \(\delta\) and some \(n>0\).
Their equal positive absolute heights force \(a=a'\), and preservation of the degree of \(\delta^{an}\) then forces \(x=y\).
Thus all powered nodes remain distinct even where several exterior moduli agree.
At such a radius \(R\), the phases \(z_c(n)=(R/c)^n\) are distinct algebraic numbers of complex modulus one.

After reversal and division by a common monomial, the local equation is \(U_1(X^{-n})-X V_1(X^{-n})=0\), up to a factor nonzero at \(X=c\).
Differentiation at \(c\) and multiplication by \(-c\) give \(nt(U_1'-cV_1')(t)+cV_1(t)=0\) at \(t=c^{-n}\).
Subtracting \(nk\) times the value equation replaces coefficient index \(i\) by \(i-k\) in the differentiated sum while preserving the term \(cV_1(t)\).
If the normalized log norms are bounded by \(C\), choose \(J\) with \((J+1)\min\log R>C+2\).
The differentiated tail is \(O(n(J+1)\exp(n(C-(J+1)\log R)))\), independently of the formal width.
After extraction, the two extreme maximizing indices \(k,k+d\) define a coefficient block whose largest contribution has magnitude \(\exp(nL(\log R)+o(n))\).
This is at least \(\exp(-o(n))\) because the constant coefficient vector is nonzero and integral.
The discarded finite terms have a strict slope deficit.
Dividing by the block scale and the initial phase therefore gives exponentially small errors in both exact matrix equations, even with the factor \(n\), and the retained coefficient vector has maximum norm one.

Temporarily replace the derivative multiplier \(n\) by \(X\).
At \(X=0\) every derivative row imposes \(cV(z)=0\).
For \(m\) double nodes and test degree \(m-1\), the \(m\) distinct phases force first \(V=0\), then \(U=0\).
For \(m\) double nodes, two simple nodes and test degree \(m\), both \(U,V\) are scalar multiples of the product of the \(m\) double-node linear factors.
At the two simple phases these factors are nonzero, and the remaining two equations have determinant proportional to the difference of the two original simple nodes.
This also treats \(m=0\).
Each square determinant at \(X=0\) is consequently nonzero for every positive phase exponent.

In the expansion \(\sum_\beta p_\beta(X)\beta^n\), take \(q\) divisible by the orders of all torsion ratios of distinct bases.
For \(n=a_0+qt\), \(1\le a_0\le q\), group by \(\theta=\beta^q\).
The sum of the grouped polynomials evaluated at \(X=0\) is the preceding nonzero determinant at phase exponent \(a_0\).
At least one grouped polynomial consequently survives \(X=a_0+qt\) as a polynomial in \(t\).
This argument includes the zero residue, where evaluating the phases at the positive exponent \(q\) keeps them distinct.
Two different surviving \(\theta\)'s have nontorsion ratio, since a torsion ratio after taking \(q\)-th powers already comes from a torsion ratio of the original bases.
I checked Xiao's Lemma 4.1 in the [retained preprint, pp. 23–24](../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=23).
Its polynomial coefficients, nondegeneracy hypothesis and absolute value with at least one base of modulus at least one apply to each grouped recurrence.
I use the lemma at statement scope.
For each positive \(\epsilon\) it gives \(-\log|D|\le\epsilon n\) eventually on each of the finitely many progressions, with eventual nonvanishing.
The cofactors grow polynomially because the dimension is fixed and the matrix entries are \(O(n)\).
The inverse matrix would take the exponentially small errors to a vector tending to zero if \(d\) were at most the test degree, contradicting the coefficient normalization.

### Lower bound, upper bound and count

The sum of positive breakpoints weighted by their index jumps equals \(L(0)-\gamma_0\), where \(\gamma_0\ge0\).
At each exterior radius the double nodes force a jump at least their number.
The combined lower bound is \(\sum_a m(P_a)=Th\), since all powers preserve degree.
This reasoning applies to every bounded-slope subsequence and therefore bounds the lower limit at unrestricted widths.

Lowering \(P_a\) to one copy still leaves the full double-node contribution \((T-a)h\).
A nonreal exterior conjugate \(x\) gives the two distinct additional simple nodes \(x^a,\bar x^a\), so their shared radius contributes an extra \(a\log|x|>0\).
If every exterior conjugate is real, equality of two exterior moduli would give a torsion quotient.
At an additional simple exterior node the [distinct-radius estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#simple) then supplies a strict excess, including when there are no remaining double factors.
Its norm step forces the exponentially small algebraic integer to vanish because its other conjugates have subexponential size.
The resulting polynomial dependence of the two rows at all embeddings would, at argument zero, make the exterior simple unit rational, which is impossible.
Each reduced list thus has an eventual positive excess uniform in the row width for this fixed input.

For the integral two-row count put \(l=2(w+1)\), \(J=2\sum_a\deg P_a\), and use exponent bound \(n(w+1)\).
The count at fixed \(w\) gives leading normalized log norm \(Th\,l/(l-J)\), with the derivative and counting overhead tending to zero.
It applies before a budget is imposed and permits an excess smaller than all reduced-list gains and than the desired final slack.
Removing one common powered minimum \(Q_{a,n}\) from both rows leaves the reduced list because \(Q_{a,n}(X^n)\) is simple at the original nodes and misses the other selected orbits.
Restoring it increases the log Mahler measure of a largest quotient row by \(nah\), with log one-norm to log measure loss at most \(w\log2\).
At this fixed width the gain therefore contradicts the chosen interpolation excess.
The graph value equation now ensures both row values are nonzero at each powered node.
The two reciprocal projections preserve the original factors, and multiplication by \(Y+1\) or \(Y-1\) realizes either sign at width \(w+1\).
At each selected node at least one projection has nonzero numerator, so a common integer linear combination with coefficient in \(\{0,\ldots,|I|\}\) preserves every nonzero value at bounded norm cost.
The reciprocal monomial-times-binomial multiplier then pads to any larger width at a factor of at most two.
This order of operations puts the coefficient-to-measure loss entirely at the fixed width.
Letting the fixed-width slack tend to zero gives the claimed upper limit even when the prescribed widths grow faster than exponentially.

If \(\lambda<B/[r(r+1)]\), primitive factorization gives a positive irreducible value \(h\) below that threshold.
The small-measure reduction gives a reciprocal unit, and the power-field norm replacement preserves its total measure and reciprocity while making all positive powers preserve degree.
For \(I=\{1,\ldots,r\}\) choose slack inside \(2Th<B\).
The padding cost and \(\log(2nb+1)=o(n)\) then leave a strictly passing row with the \(r\) distinct measures \(h,\ldots,rh\) at every sufficiently large \(n\).
This proves the implication for any infinite subsequence, including a subsequence of \(j!-1\), and yields \((\log2)/48\) for \(r=3\).
The actual exclusion on an infinite subsequence remains open.
