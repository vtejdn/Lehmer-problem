### Prime window and actual cofactor cost

For a fixed reciprocal nontorsion unit seed of degree \(e\) and measure \(h>0\), all sufficiently large prime \(n\) give the irreducible representative \(P_0(X^n)\) of measure \(h\).
Throughout its fourth-logarithmic window one eventually has \(e(e-1)+1<p<n\), so the seed powers have degree \(e\), the powers of full roots have degree \(ne\), and the powered minimum is \(Q=Q_0(X^n)\) of measure \(ph\).
This follows by distinctness of the seed conjugate powers and Bézout applied to \(n,p\).
The window thus works at every prime in it and permits any prescribed nonzero seed residue \(r\), specifically \(nr\equiv1\bmod p\) for the final adjacent rows.
For varying seeds the representative can be chosen afresh after fixing each seed and desired accuracy.

For \(P_0^j,\ j=1,2\), the local valuation difference in the contraction at either sign is exactly \(j\) times the difference for \(P_0\), including the maximum over primitive nodes.
The factor at the characteristic \(p\) is discarded in both copies of the contraction.
The two resultant divisibilities multiply because their product evaluates the cofactor \(R\) against \(x^{2p}-1\).
The root product bounds its logarithm by \((\deg R)\log2+2p\,m(R)\), retaining any nonmonic leading coefficient.
For each fixed \(P_0\), both contraction logarithms divided by \((p-1)^2\) tend to \(h/2\) at all large primes.
Hence \(m(R)/(ph)\ge j/2-o(1)\) whenever the original degree is \(o(p^2)\), uniformly for the specified degree budget.
This bound applies after the cyclotomic factors have been removed.
For a primitive polynomial, deleting just the two imposed copies and all cyclotomic factors subtracts exactly \(2h\), even when the residual cofactor contains more copies.

### Ordinary widths and two distinct short ratios

There are \(2b+1\) genuinely distinct exponent positions because \(0<r<p\), with maximum exponent \(pb\).
Counting the two integer jets in their archimedean boxes gives numerator \(2pbh+2e\log(6(2b+1))+e\log(1+pb)\) and denominator \(2b+1-2e\).
The additive coefficient norm term is \(\log(2(2b+1))\).
After division by \(ph\), the leading quotient is \(8e/(6e+1)<4/3\) at \(b=4e\), and tends to one for \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(p)\).
If both rows contained \(Q_0\), dividing by \(Q_0(t^p)\) would remove measure \(ph\) while retaining one imposed copy by separability.
The remaining seed polynomial has degree \(o(p^2)\), and its cofactor alone costs \((1/2-o(1))ph\).
The strict separation excludes such a common powered divisor in both width regimes.
It excludes a single active row as well, since double vanishing in that row forces \(Q_0^2\) in it.
Removal of the joint gcd and scalar content then retains both copies and cannot increase measure.
Substitution \(t=x^n\) preserves the measure and the content, and substituting in a Laurent Bézout identity preserves coprimality of the two rows, including the second-row monomial shift.
The degrees are at most \(4pd\) and \(npb\), respectively.

For \(T=\lfloor\exp((1+\eta)ph)\rfloor\) the largest jet fiber has logarithmic size at least \((2\eta+o(1))bph\).
Here the losses involving \(e\log T\), \(e\log(1+pb)\), and the integer rounding are \(o(bph)\) at each fixed seed and \(\eta>0\).
Subtracting one fiber element preserves the number of vectors and bounds their coefficient sums by \((2b+1)T\).
If all nonzero pairs had one ratio, joint coprimality and primitivity would express each as \(W(t^p)G_*\) with integral \(W\), \(\deg W\le b\).
The preceding common-divisor exclusion applies since \(1+\eta<3/2\), so \(G_*\) retains both copies and has measure at least \((1-o(1))ph\).
The coefficient estimate for \(W\) gives logarithmic count at most \((\eta+o(1))bph\), including the \(O(b^2)\) contribution, which is negligible since \(b=o(p)\).
This contradicts the fiber size after the one zero vector is discarded.
For two different ratios, the two hypothetical mixed norm identities give \(K+K^\iota=X^{-1}K+XK^\iota=0\), forcing \(K=0\).
Together with the second self-identity this would equate the ratios in \(\mathbb Q(X)\).
One of the four candidates has unequal row moduli, seed degree at most \(p(b+1)\) and coefficient sum at most \(2(2b+1)T\).
Common-factor removal preserves the norm inequality, and a slow diagonal choice of \(\eta\to0\) works for all \(r\) because the estimates did not depend on \(r\).

### Reflected rows and the lower bound after cleaning

In the reflection the two terms lie in classes zero and one modulo \(n\), so they cannot cancel and their common double factor follows from reciprocity.
Taking the product over the \(n\)-th roots on the circle gives the upper measure \(m(G)+(\log2)/n\) since the two seed expressions there have equal moduli.
At \(\beta=\gamma^p\), the first reflected row is a sum of nonzero elements of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(\gamma K\).
Indeed coprimality and the seed equation exclude a vanishing row at either \(\alpha^p\) or \(\alpha^{-p}\), and \(\beta^{-w}=\gamma\alpha^{-r}\).
The degree-\(n\) extension ensures the two subspaces have trivial intersection.
The row gcd therefore removes no imposed copy and is reciprocal up to a Laurent unit by reversal.
The degree stays at most \(np(b+1)+1\).

Writing the two summand row vectors as \(S,T\), rotation \(j\) yields \(S+\zeta^jT\).
The determinant of rotations \(i,j\) is \((\zeta^j-\zeta^i)\det(S,T)\) and is nonzero by the unequal seed row norms.
Gcd division only divides those rows by nonzero rotated factors.
For the computation one may retain classes \(0,1\bmod p\) after dividing out a common row monomial.
Stripping a further \(x\)-monomial to put a genuine endpoint at degree zero can translate both residue classes together, leaving adjacency, row integral, and measure invariant.
Consequently the normalized reciprocal conclusion does not require both endpoints or an unchanged residue origin.

Every rotated polynomial still contains \(P^2\).
Pairwise elimination and the unramified power map at its distinct nonzero roots give \(Q^2\) in each of the \(n(n-1)/2\) pair factors, with \(m(Q)=ph\).
The Fourier quotient is integral by reduction in the basis modulo \(Z^n-1\), regardless of either row constant.
Its pair factors prove nonvanishing independently of their values at zero.
Monic division by \(Q^{n(n-1)}\) leaves an integral nonzero polynomial of nonnegative measure.
The Fourier denominator cancels all \(n^{1/2}\) factors in the integrated Hadamard bound, so \(n(n-1)I(F)\ge n(n-1)ph\).
The coprime-row comparison then yields \(m(F)\ge(1-4/p)I(F)-2\log(C\deg F)/p\).
The degree logarithm is \(O(\log(3ne))\) for the specified width and window, making the error \(o(ph)\) for fixed \(h>0\).
Together with the upper bound, this proves the leading constant one for the actual cleaned reciprocal polynomial and its residual cofactor.
The prescribed sign applies before the gcd division, and independence of the reflected value and Euler derivative data is outside this construction.
