## Boundary periods, the six-term minimum and the partially filled fibers

The whole argument holds at the stated scope.
Factoring each \(x^h-1\) shows that the multiplicity \(e_n\) in \((x-1)C\) counts the cycles whose lengths are multiples of \(n\).
Inversion on multiples recovers all the \(m_h\), including \(m_1\), and \(e_1\ge1\) guarantees a nonempty permutation whenever the counts are nonnegative.
Passing from the permutation module to the capping kernel removes its invariant boundary sum.
For the modular assertion, the symplectic action makes \(P\) a monic unit polynomial, and the additional hypotheses in the reviewed text match the [signed binomial estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0904_v6.md#deficit).
Its [boundary inequality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0904_v6.md#boundary) counts each length \(h\) exactly \(m_h\) times.

In the prong coordinate, stretching the flat chart preserves each quadrant, so its lifted angular displacement has magnitude less than \(\pi/k\).
A nonidentity prong rotation can therefore be deformed to the rotation itself throughout a fixed-point-free boundary circle.
For the identity rotation the first-order displacement is a positive multiple of \(e^{i(1-k)\theta}\) on a circle and has winding \(1-k\).
The chart sign cancels in \(\dot z/z\), which also covers odd prong counts.
This verifies the extension beyond the orientable-foliation setting of [Lanneau and Thiffeault, Proposition 2.7](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_2011__61_1_105_0.pdf#page=11).
An orbit of period \(a\) and rotation order \(q\) contributes once positively at \(h=a\) and with weight \(-k/q\) at \(h=aq\) after inversion.
Thus \(b_h\) is an integer bounded above by the actual orbit count, also when \(q=1\) and both contributions occur at the same period.
Puncturing selected periodic points preserves the transverse foliations and their expansion factor, and the exact homology sequence multiplies the closed characteristic polynomial by \(C\).

For \(L\), the closed genus-five realization satisfies the homological criterion and its trace is \(-1\), so two of at least three fixed points give the six-term product \((x-1)L\).
Reciprocity, \(L(1)=-1\), and \(L(0)=1\) give two distinct positive roots on opposite sides of one.
Descartes' rule then gives at least \(t+2\) terms when there are \(t\) boundary cycles.
For \(t\le3\) I checked the displayed low coefficients by multiplication modulo \(x^4\), including all twenty triples of nonnegative \(m_1,m_2,m_3\) with sum at most three.
In each case the four low positions contain at least three nonzero coefficients, and signed reciprocity reflects them into disjoint high positions.
Direct multiplication verifies the stated identities for \((x-1)L\), \(\Phi_6L\) and \(\Phi_3L(-x)\).
The only nonzero contribution to \(m_3\) for \(C=\Phi_6\) is \(\mu(2)=-1\).

For the primitive class \((1,b)\) with \(3\mid b\), \(b\ge6\), Hironaka's equation (3) and Propositions 3.3 and 3.5 give the five-term specialization, boundary cycle lengths \(1,3\), and prong counts \(3b,b/3\).
The two successive extensions preserve the pseudo-Anosov structure even at \(b/3=2\), where the cap is regular.
The corresponding hyperbolic fillings have smaller volume than their fixed parent.
The final three-circle permutation has reduced characteristic polynomial \(x^2+x+1\), which identifies the intermediate whole polynomial using the closed calculation.
Below exponent \(b-1\), each complete block of three coefficients of \(1/\Phi_3\) contributes two occupied positions.
Dobrowolski's Theorem 1 applies to the five distinct terms of \(F_b\), and multiplying by \(\Phi_3\) preserves Mahler measure.
I used the published degree-ten record, Hironaka's fiber data, hyperbolization, strict volume decrease under hyperbolic filling and the sparse measure bound at statement scope.
The orbit certificate is sufficient, and the uniform volume conclusion concerns the specified filling family.
