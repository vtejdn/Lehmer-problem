## The discriminant bounds give the stated local balance and unique real limiting law

The reviewer developed the earlier v110 test supplying a square in the odd-index branch; the determinant and limiting-measure arguments of v124 were obtained separately.

The square root is an integral unit and generates the trace field because its square determines the generating trace.
Thus the \(n\) distinct real conjugates in the Vandermonde product are exactly the roots of its minimum.
At an odd coefficient prime exactly one difference from the two rational centers has positive valuation, and the divisibility of their product makes that valuation at least the full rational exponent \(e\).
At two, if either valuation is strictly below one the two are equal by the ultrametric inequality.
This gives individual lower bound \(e/2\) on the difference from one for \(e=1,2\).
For \(e\ge3\) the larger is strictly greater than one, the smaller equals one, and the larger is at least \(e-1\).
The two sign disks are disjoint and every pairwise difference has valuation at least one.
The local Galois group preserves each disk centered at a rational point; each place contributes its full local degree many embeddings, all in that one disk.
Extending the normalized valuation in a different way permutes the conjugates.

For an imbalance \(\Delta\) the number of same-sign pairs is \((n^2(1+\Delta^2)-2n)/4\).
At an odd prime, each such pair contributes at least \(2e\) to the discriminant valuation.
At a tested dyadic prime every pair contributes at least two, with an additional \(2(e-2)\) for each same-sign pair.
These give precisely the tested-prime bound in the text.
At an untested dyadic prime the common disk of depth \(e/2\) still contributes at least \(e\) per pair.
The total negative linear error is at most \((n/2)\log D\) since all tested weights are at most their coefficient exponents; the other finite prime valuations of the nonzero integral discriminant are nonnegative.

The even and odd columns from the normalized trace polynomials are monic of successive degrees, including the explicitly separate constant polynomial.
Dividing column \(k\) by \(D^{k/2}\) and squaring the determinant introduces \(D^{\binom n2}\).
The magnitudes of the scaled entries on neutral rows are bounded by six from the bounds two for the cosine polynomials and three for the scaled square root.
On the distinguished row an additional factor \(\alpha^{n/2}\) suffices for every column, and only this one row requires it.
Hadamard on row norms and squaring give the two additive terms \(n\log(36n)\) and \(n\log\alpha\).
Subtracting the finite-place baseline and dividing by \(n^2/2\) gives exactly (1).
The signed trace norm and signature give \(D<4n\) by v884 and also \(\alpha<1+1/q\).
Thus the right side tends to zero uniformly, and every positive tested weight times its prime logarithm is at least \(\log2\).
This yields simultaneous balance across the possibly varying prime sets.

If all signs at infinity coincide, an interval of length \(L\) contains all conjugates.
After rescaling the monic Chebyshev columns to this interval the bound for degree \(k\ge1\) is \(2(L/4)^k\).
The constant column obeys the same bound at \(k=0\).
Their squared determinant bound compared to the finite baseline yields the stated necessary inequality.
Since \(4\sqrt D/L\ge4\sqrt6/5>1.95\) and \(D<4n\), it already fails at \(n=10\), where \(9\log1.95>(3/2)\log40\).
For larger \(n\) the linear lower bound grows faster than \((3/2)\log(4n)\).
If \(\delta\) were a fourth power in this totally real field, both square roots would have constant real sign, so the exclusion applies directly.

Scaling every root by \(D^{-1/2}\) subtracts \(\binom n2\log D\) from the logarithm of the squared discriminant.
The finite lower bound then loses at most \((n/2)\log D=o(n^2)\), after discarding a nonnegative imbalance term.
All scaled roots lie in a common compact interval and at most one squared value lies outside the given shifted interval.
Any limit along \(1/D\to a\) is therefore supported on its real square-root preimage.
Truncating the logarithmic kernel below gives an upper bound for the off-diagonal sum and an \(O(H/n)\) normalized diagonal correction at fixed truncation.
This shows that the limiting log energy is at least zero, with finite positive and negative parts.
The equally signed lift of the arcsine law has constant potential zero on that preimage, since the sum of the logarithms at opposite square roots is the logarithm of the difference of their squares.
The identity includes the endpoints and the touching point at \(a=0\) by integrability of the circle logarithm.

Consequently the energy bound can be attained only when the Fourier transform of the difference vanishes, proving uniqueness and convergence.
