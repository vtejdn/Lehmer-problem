### Inertia at every finite prime

The endpoints of \(F\) are both \(-1\), so \(-F\) is monic and every characteristic-zero root is an integral unit.
If \(t\) is common to \(g,B\), then \(t\ne0\), \(t^{s-2}=-1\), and substitution in \(g\) gives \(t^2+t+1=0\).
Consequently \(t^3=1\), so cubing \(t^{s-2}=-1\) forces characteristic two.
In that characteristic \(t=1\) fails the quadratic equation, and the resulting order three would require \(s\equiv2\bmod3\).
This proves coprimality in characteristic zero and in every residue characteristic for the full stated range of \(s\).
At a residue root \(z\), the values \(z^a=t\) and \(z^b=-g(t)/B(t)\) are nonzero, and Bézout for \(a,b\) reconstructs \(z\) from the pair even in positive characteristic.

For \(\ell\mid a\) the derivative at \(z\) is \(b z^{b-1}B(t)\ne0\).
For \(\ell\nmid a\) I expanded the displayed derivative identity and checked the sign of the term \(-bgB\).
Its polynomial \(R\) has degree at most \(2s\) and constant \(b\).
When \(\ell\mid b\), the constant of the Wronskian \(g'B-gB'\) is \(-1\) as \(s\ge3\), so \(R=aT(g'B-gB')\ne0\).
At a root of multiplicity \(e\), the summands containing \(F,F'\) vanish to order at least \(e-1\), including when \(\ell\mid e\).
The substitution \(t=X^a\) preserves this order at \(z\) because \(az^{a-1}\ne0\).
Summing over distinct \(t\) proves the excess bound \(2s\).
Factoring the monic polynomial over a splitting field before reduction identifies the size of each residue cluster, counted with multiplicities, with the corresponding reduced multiplicity.
Inertia fixes residues, so it moves at most \(\sum_{e\ge2}e\le4s\) distinct characteristic-zero roots, with the sum taken over nonsingleton clusters.
This argument allows wild inertia and repeated characteristic-zero roots.

The subgroup generated by all finite inertia groups is normal and its fixed field is everywhere unramified at finite primes.
Minkowski's discriminant bound, in the form checked in C383, makes this field \(\mathbb Q\).
For \(E\ne\mathbb Q\) transitivity on its embeddings therefore gives some finite inertia element acting nontrivially there.
It moves at least two fibers of the restriction map from embeddings of \(K\), each of size \([K:E]\), so \(2[K:E]\le4s\).
When \(E/\mathbb Q\) is normal a nontrivial restriction moves every embedding of \(E\) and thus all \(d\) roots of the minimal polynomial of \(x\).
This verifies the strict cutoff \(d>4s\), including the possible choice \(E=K\).

### Reciprocity and primitivity of the trace action

The graph equation is primitive and linear in \(V\) over \(\mathbb C[U]\), with coprime coefficients, and its support includes \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\).
It is absolutely irreducible with support differences generating \(\mathbb Z^2\).
C253 therefore gives uniform growth of the degrees of every nontorsion factor as the primitive direction escapes.
The projected support gcd is \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), so C251 excludes distinct roots with torsion quotient in that range.

A nonconstant torus character integrates to zero along the section except possibly at one positive primitive direction.
Replacing the logarithm with \(\log\max(\delta,|g(U)+VB(U)|)\) gives continuous majorants of the original section integrands.
For each fixed \(\delta>0\) the section integrals of these majorants converge to their torus means by trigonometric approximation.
Decreasing the truncation and then using the one-variable Jensen formula yields the mean of \(\log\max(|g|,|B|)\).
It equals \(m(g)\) since \(B(T)=T^sg(T^{-1})\) and \(g\) has real coefficients, giving \(|B(T)|=|g(T)|\) on the unit circle.
The coefficient norm gives \(M(g)\le\sqrt3\), and at \(33/25\) the cubic \(T^3-T-1\) equals \(-313/15625\), while \((33/25)^4>3\).
Thus the eventual logarithmic upper bound has strict margin below \(2\log\theta\).
The parent is reciprocal and every irreducible factor has unit endpoints, so one nonreciprocal factor would force its distinct reversal, each costing at least \(\log\theta\) by Smyth's theorem.
The resulting reciprocity is uniform over the factors for each fixed seed.

The reciprocal roots pair off without fixed points, and absence of torsion quotients makes these pairs exactly the multiplicative labels.
Taking traces identifies pairs equivariantly with the \(d/2\) trace values.
The coefficient length bound gives \(m(x)\le\log6\), so C270 gives a minimal block of size \(h\to\infty\) supporting its own \(A_h\).
Take the block containing the trace of \(x\).
The corresponding \(2h\) roots form a block containing \(x\), whose setwise stabilizer contains the stabilizer of \(x\) with index \(2h\) by transitivity inside the block.
If the block were proper, its setwise stabilizer would fix a nontrivial subfield of \(K\) of index \(2h\), contrary to \([K:E]\le2s\) eventually.
Hence \(h=d/2\), and complex conjugation has sign \((-1)^c\) for \(c\) nonreal conjugate pairs of traces.
This gives \(S_{d/2}\) when \(c\) is odd.

### Quartic signs and residue classes

For \(a=3,\ b>18\), L210's monotone phase count gives three exterior roots with multiplicity, and its real-root classification gives one simple negative root and no positive one.
The remaining roots are consequently a single simple nonreal conjugate pair.
For odd \(b\) the negative exterior modulus satisfies \(\sigma^3<\beta_4<7/5<(9/8)^3\).
For even \(b\ge26\) I checked the strict rational inequality at \(w=9/8\), \(w^{26}V(w^3)>G(w^3)\), in L210's decreasing comparison.
The isolated-real-root factor in a split therefore gives \(\sigma<9/8\) throughout the claimed range.
Its reciprocity follows already from \(\sigma<\theta\), and degree two is excluded by \(2<\sigma+\sigma^{-1}<3\).
Thus this Salem implication holds for every indicated \(b\ge26\), independently of the asymptotic trace-group cutoff.

For large \(b\) with \(3\nmid b\), every nontorsion factor is reciprocal and receives an exterior root by Kronecker's theorem.
The three simple exterior roots imply that no such factor repeats and there are at most two, since a rational factor takes a nonreal root together with its conjugate.
For \(|z|>1\) nonreal, \(\operatorname{Im}(z+z^{-1})=(1-|z|^{-2})\operatorname{Im}z\ne0\).
The two nonreal exterior roots give distinct nonreal conjugate traces, and all other pairs give real traces.
The factor receiving them has a single transposition as complex conjugation and hence full symmetric trace group, also when the core splits.
In a split the two positive-degree monic integral trace factors retain their degrees after reduction at every prime.
In the one-factor case the trace has full group \(S_n\), and at primes away from its discriminant irreducibility of the reduction is equivalent to Frobenius being an \(n\)-cycle.

The sign field is generated by the Vandermonde square root of the discriminant.
Complex conjugation negates it, so the quadratic field \(E\) is imaginary.
Since the commutator subgroup is \(A_n\), the intersection of the trace splitting field with the cyclotomic field \(K_m\) is \(\mathbb Q\) or \(E\).
Over any specified cyclotomic automorphism, restriction identifies the fiber in the compositum with exactly the compatible subset of \(S_n\).
For the trivial intersection the subset is all of \(S_n\).
Otherwise it is one sign coset, even at the identity class for odd \(n\) and odd at the class of \(-1\) for even \(n\) because complex conjugation is nontrivial on \(E\).
There are \((n-1)!\) cycles of length \(n\), each of sign \((-1)^{n-1}\).
The compatible fiber therefore gives relative densities \(1/n\) and \(2/n\), respectively, by the fixed-field counting form of Chebotarev.
For \(m=1,2\) the two signs of the residue class agree modulo \(m\), \(K_m=\mathbb Q\), and the unrestricted density \(1/n\) applies.
Removing the finitely many bad primes leaves the densities and the existence conclusion unchanged.
The least certificate prime and the occurrence of a split remain open.
