## Equal logarithm spaces, the integer measure multiplier and the complex-conjugation test

At 2026-11-24T22:25 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v58.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v58.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v58.
The conclusions hold under irreducibility of the full signed representation over \(\mathbb R\).
Degree equality, uniqueness of the detected primitive value, the exact positive integer multiplier, the equality of divisible groups and the fixed-space obstruction hold at every degree.
The signed-power conclusion holds for odd \(n\ge5\) at arbitrary measure, and uniformly at sufficiently large \(n\) for each fixed measure bound \(M>0\).

Right translation satisfies \(R_sR_t=R_{st}\) and sends \(f_v\) to \(f_{s(v)}\).
Thus \(e_j\mapsto f_{w_j}\) intertwines the two actions, including the minus sign when \(s\) inverts the chosen orientation.
Restriction from any larger common finite Galois field onto the splitting group is surjective.
Kronecker's theorem makes the map nonzero because \(w\) is a nontorsion unit.
Its kernel is invariant, so real irreducibility makes the map injective and its image \(W\) irreducible of dimension \(n\).

When \(w\) detects \(\alpha\), the norm criterion gives a positive power of \(\alpha\) as a product of powers of conjugates of \(w\) up to torsion.
Taking all its conjugates and then log moduli gives inclusion of the actual Salem function space in \(W\).
That subspace is nonzero and invariant, so it is all of \(W\).
One oriented function per Salem inverse pair has nonempty support, and these supports are disjoint because at each automorphism exactly one pair maps to the real pair.
Their independence now gives \(\deg\alpha=2n\).
If two distinct positive primitive values were detected, enlarge the finite Galois field to include both.
The norm-one projections between distinct Salem inputs make their spans orthogonal there, whereas each would be the same nonzero \(W\).
The primitivity conventions in the norm and projection formulas agree here since any unit with a power of relative norm one has torsion relative norm, which is positive at a trace place becoming complex and thus equals one.

In the Salem basis the expansion coefficients are its projection coefficients, each rational by the conditional norm formula \(q_j=a_j/(2[\mathbb Q(w,\gamma_j):\mathbb Q(\gamma_j)])\) with a signed integer \(a_j\).
Clearing denominators by \(A>0\) gives a unit \(w^A/\prod_j\gamma_j^{Aq_j}\) with zero log modulus at every embedding, hence torsion.
This relation and its conjugates give one inclusion of divisible groups, and the detection relation gives the other.
Apply the orbit multiplier of v51 to this single primitive input, using radical exponent \(A\), exponents \(Aq_j\) and the equal absolute degrees \(2n\).
It gives precisely \(\sum_j|q_j|\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\).
Each oriented Salem function has mean absolute value \(\log(\alpha)/n\), so disjointness of supports gives equality in the absolute-value integral.
Multiplying by half the degree of \(w\) proves \(m(w)=\log(\alpha)\sum_j|q_j|\), in particular \(\log\alpha\le m(w)\) at every degree.

The stabilizer of \(w\) fixes \(f_w\), so the signed coefficient orbit has size at most \(2n\).
If \(k<n\) coordinates occur, all \(2^k\) sign choices can be realized over each support using even Salem flips, adjusting parity at a zero coordinate.
Transitivity gives at least \(n/k\) supports, whence \(2^k\le2k\) and \(k\le2\).
Full support has at least \(2^{n-1}>2n\) sign choices for \(n\ge5\).
For \(k=2\), four sign choices per support and the \(2n\) upper bound permit at most \(n/2\) supports, which cover all \(n\) labels.
They must be disjoint pairs, impossible at odd \(n\).
When \(n\) is even the block stabilizer contains a point stabilizer with index two, as an element taking that point to its partner preserves the unique block containing both.
The fixed field of the point stabilizer is the corresponding Salem trace field \(B\), quadratic over the block stabilizer's fixed field, so \(B\) has a nonidentity rational automorphism.
At fixed \(M\) and sufficiently large \(n\), v47 applied with \(H=M\) gives trivial rational automorphism group for this same base since its trace has exceptional logarithm \(\log\alpha\le M\).
This excludes the matching uniformly in \(w\) at that bound.

For \(k=1\) the surviving coefficient \(q\) is a nonzero integer by the multiplier.
The stabilizer of \(qf_\gamma\) fixes \(\gamma\) because inversion negates a nonzero function and a different label has disjoint support.
Consequently \(G_w\subseteq G_\gamma\), and equality of the indices implies equality of their fixed fields.
In this field the unit \(w/\gamma^q\) has all conjugates of modulus one and is torsion.
A real embedding of the Salem field forces this torsion to be \(1\) or \(-1\), even when the selected \(\gamma\) lies on the circle.
Conversely the log function of a signed nonzero power is a nonzero multiple of the corresponding Salem function, proving exclusivity of the alternatives.

A real reciprocal pair contributes one real exterior root and one fixed axis of \(c\), with either orientation and either sign for the exterior value.
A nonreal unit-circle reciprocal pair is inverted by \(c\) and contributes zero fixed dimensions.
Every other orbit of inversion and conjugation is a quartet with two conjugate exterior roots.
On its two reciprocal labels \(c\) acts by a signed swap whose signs have product one, contributing exactly one fixed dimension.
Roots equal to \(1\) or \(-1\) cannot occur because \(w\) is nontorsion.
This proves the count in every signature.
Under detection the two signed modules identify equivariantly through their common logarithm space, so their fixed dimensions for this same \(c\) agree, and the Salem count is one.

The kernel \(J\) of permutation on the labels acts diagonally by sign characters.
Character averaging over \(J\) extracts a single coordinate if the characters are pairwise distinct.
Every invariant subspace is then spanned by some coordinate axes, and label transitivity forces irreducibility.
For \(n\ge3\) an even flip of a specified label and a third label distinguishes its character from that of any other specified label.
Equality of characters defines an invariant partition because conjugation transports the diagonal actions.
With primitive trace action the only possibilities are all singleton classes or one full class.
When all traces are real, \(c\) is in \(J\), and its distinct values on real off-circle and nonreal circle pairs rule out the full class if both types occur.
The fixed-space count then forces complete orthogonality as soon as there are at least two real exterior roots in that mixed case.
