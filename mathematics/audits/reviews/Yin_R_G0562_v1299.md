## Finding

The logarithmic lower bound for the width holds for both signs, including blocks with repeated factors.
Removing \(X^t\) gives the same polynomial with parameters \(n+t,r-t\), so the bound for the reduced block implies the one for the original block.
Any nonconstant common divisor of the reduced \(Q,Q^*\) would be a proper divisor of \(P\), excluding both a reciprocal irreducible factor and a root on the unit circle.
The hypotheses of Smyth's bound hold for each irreducible factor separately.
The degree-ten competitor has its sole exterior root between \(1\) and \(19/16<\theta\), giving the stated ceiling for a degree record.

For an exterior pair the normalized absolute difference is \(|a_i-a_h|\), as it is for an interior pair.
For a mixed pair it is \(|1-\bar a_h a_i|\).
The removed logarithms sum to \(2\sum_i e_i(r-e_i)\log^+|\beta_i|\le2rm\), with \(i,h\) ordered.
Galois invariance and integrality give \(\log\mathcal D\ge0\) even for repeated factors.
In the smoothed kernel expansion the diagonal has total weight \(J\), while the increase from removing the smoothing on the other pairs is at most \(r^2\log(2/(1+e^{-1/r}))\le r\).
Dropping the nonpositive interior log distances therefore gives (1) with the asserted coefficient of \(J\log r\).

In a component of the disk union one can join any center to a point of its closure along at most \(r\) disks, each of hyperbolic diameter at most \(3\epsilon A/r^2\), where \(A\) is the largest center modulus.
The Euclidean bound by twice the pseudohyperbolic distance gives \(A\le |a_i|/(1-6\epsilon/r)\).
For \(a=|a_i|\) and \(\rho(a_i,z)\le\gamma_i\), the increase of \(\log|z|\) above \(\log a\) is at most \((\gamma_i/a)(1-a^2)\le16\epsilon x_i\).
On the boundary, a far center contributes at most three times its Green function at \(a_i\), by Harnack in the automorphism coordinate.
For a near center different from \(i\), the stated cutoff \(\log(2r^2/\epsilon)+x_i\) is at most twice \(\log(r/(16\epsilon))+x_i\) throughout \(0<\epsilon<1/1000\).
The factor at \(i\) contributes its own cutoff with multiplicity \(e_i\).

The disk factorization preserves zero multiplicities, and both Blaschke products are holomorphic on a neighborhood of the compact component closures.
At a good center the strict boundary inequality persists throughout the homotopy \(O-scz^nB\), \(0\le s\le1\).
Zeros therefore stay off the boundary and their number in that component is constant, including when boundary arcs touch.
This number is \(\sum_{a_h\in U}e_h\), and each such zero contributes at least the largest radial lower bound among the good centers in \(U\).
Hence the bad centers carry a fraction at least \(1-\log\lambda/((1-16\epsilon)m_0)>0\) of \(m\).
In their weighted sum the radial diagonal is bounded by \(rm\), and the other diagonal cost by \(J(2\log r+\log(1/\epsilon))\).
Since \(J\le r\max_i e_i\le rm/m_0\), division by \(m\) proves \(n\ll_\lambda r\log(2r)\), also for \(r=1\).
Adding \(r\) and using \(r\le d\) yields the stated lower bound on \(r\).

For the squarefree comparison set \(p_i=e^{-S_i}\).
Each pair of chosen radii sums to at most \(2\epsilon\rho(a_i,a_h)<\rho(a_i,a_h)\), so their closed disks are disjoint.
The other-factor product has modulus \(p_i\) at \(a_i\), and Schwarz–Pick bounds its boundary modulus below by \((p_i-\eta_i)/(1-p_i\eta_i)\ge(1-\epsilon)p_i\).
The empty product has modulus one throughout.
Multiplying by \(\eta_i\) gives the exponent \(2S_i+x_i\) and the stated additive constant.
The radial loss is at most \(2\epsilon x_i\), so the same positive bad-mass bound applies.
Substituting \(J=r\) in (1) gives the positive-part discriminant bound since \(r=O(rm)\).
When \(r=o(d)\), \(n/r\) tends to infinity, forcing the asserted normalized discriminant deficit to do the same.

For the shorter closures the signs at \(1\) and \(\alpha\) give the claimed intermediate real root in each surviving case, using \((Q^*)'(1)=rQ(1)-Q'(1)\) for the minus sign.
A negative value at \(\alpha\) forces a root above \(\alpha\) for every \(j<n\) of that sign.
The phase winds \(j+r-2k\) times since the interior and reflected exterior Blaschke factors have opposite orientations.
At a transverse level the positive crossing count minus the negative count equals this winding, including a crossing at \(1\) counted once.
All resulting circle roots are simple.
The remaining roots occur in reciprocal pairs, so exactly \(k-b_j\) are exterior with multiplicities.
Under the stated joint condition the sole exterior root lies in \((1,\alpha)\), making the lower-degree closure a strict competitor even if reducible.
The criterion leaves tangent crossings and the existence of a suitable exponent in the remaining range unresolved.
