## Independent review of the mixed-signature estimate

At 2026-11-20T19:37 UTC a separate agent for Sheng Yin checked the whole proof of the [mixed-signature gap](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v628.md#statement).
The reviewer took no part in developing this proof and received no development discussion, exploration notes, author explanations or previous findings about it.
After checking the fixed arguments, the reviewer compared the reused estimates with the earlier reviews of [the reflected inequality](Yin_R_G0562_v108.md#cyclotomic-parent-exclusion), [the discriminant product](Yin_R_G0562_v673.md#finding), [the relative norm](Yin_R_G0562_v753.md#finding) and [the first moment](Yin_R_G0562_v761.md#finding).
Both bounds in (2) and the uniform positive lower bound under (1) hold for either type of relative constant.

The field generated by the coefficients of \(q\) is contained in \(F\), and the degree of \(\alpha\) over that field is at most \(b=[K:F]\).
The tower law therefore gives equality of the fields, so some integral coefficient \(c\) is moved by \(\tau\).
Choosing the first such coefficient gives a nonzero \(w=c-\tau c\) with the same index under every embedding.
In the quadratic extension \(F/F^\tau\), the order on \(1,c\) has discriminant \(w^2\), divisible as an ideal by the field discriminant.
Its absolute norm over \(F^\tau\) equals \(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}w|\), because \(N_{F/F^\tau}w=-w^2\).
The tower product for \(q\) counts each rational conjugate once, giving \(\sum_\sigma m_\sigma=m\).

For two points in the disk, subtracting the squared distance from the squared reflected denominator gives \((1-|v|^2)(1-|w|^2)\ge0\).
For two exterior roots one first divides the original distance by their product of moduli, and for a mixed pair the resulting reflected denominator equals the scaled distance.
The smoothing step follows from \(|1-\rho^2 z|^2-\rho^2|1-z|^2=(1-\rho^2)(1-\rho^2|z|^2)\ge0\).
Summing logarithms over ordered unequal pairs gives the coefficient \(2(b-1)\) on the measure and \(-\log|\Delta_\sigma|\) on the discriminant.
The \(b\) diagonal terms contribute at most \(b\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\).
The logarithmic series converges absolutely since \(\rho<1\), so its sum is the stated nonnegative energy.
The \(\Delta_\sigma\) are the conjugates of one nonzero integral discriminant, giving \(\sum_\sigma\log|\Delta_\sigma|\ge0\), also for \(b=1\) with discriminant one.
This justifies the total energy \(W\) even when individual discriminants have modulus below one.

For a root of positive log modulus \(h\), reflection changes its \(j\)-th power by at most \(2jh e^{jh}\) in modulus.
Summing this cost uses \(h\le m_\sigma\) and \(\sum h=m_\sigma\) over exterior roots.
Retaining the \(j\)-th summand in the energy therefore gives (3).
The logarithmic derivative of the ratio of reversed monic polynomials gives \(S_j=-j\sigma(w)\) and all lower signed moments vanish.
For the first entry of (2), the exterior-power argument in v624 bounds the first nonzero signed moment by \(2b\) times the largest product of \(j\) distinct indexed root moduli, giving \(|S_j|\le 2b e^{\mu_\sigma}\).
For the second entry, applying (3) to both polynomials leaves an exponential \(e^{j\mu_\sigma}\) multiplying the bracket.
Right composition with \(\tau\) permutes the nonreal places, so Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the sum of the paired square roots by \(2\sqrt{sW}\).
Arithmetic–geometric mean now gives the factor \(B_j^s\).

At real places the two root multisets are conjugate reciprocals.
The reflected coefficient inequality gives \(4\sinh(\mu_\sigma/2)\cosh((m_\sigma-m_{\sigma\tau})/2)\), bounded above by \(4\sinh(\mu_\sigma/2)e^{\mu_\sigma}\).
Here \(\mu_\sigma>0\) follows from \(\sigma(w)\ne0\).
Concavity of \(\log\sinh\) and \(\sum_{\sigma\ {\rm real}}\mu_\sigma/2\le m\) give the common real factor in (2).
Over all places the exponent is at most \(2jm\) for the energy bound and \(2m\) for the first-moment bound.

For \(j>r\) the \(r\)-th root of the nonreal factor in the first bound is at most \(2^A(b/r)^t\), before its exponential.
For \(j\le r\), one has \(\rho^{-j}\le e^1\) because \(j\le b\), and \(1/(1-\rho^2)\le1+b/2\).
With \(m\le1\) the resulting \(B_j\) is at most an absolute multiple of \(\sqrt{b\log(2b)(1+1/t)}\).
Raising to \(t\) costs at most \(e^{1/2}\) from the last factor since \(\log(1+1/t)\le1/t\).
The exponential is at most \(e^{2m}\) in this branch.
Hypothesis (1) thus gives \(1\le O_{A,C}(r\sinh(m/r)e^{2m})\) in both cases.
Convexity gives \(r\sinh(m/r)\le\sinh(m)\) for every \(r\ge1\), which rules out a sequence with \(m\to0\) uniformly as \(t\) varies.

When \(s=r\), the degree condition bounds the first term of (1) by \(\sqrt{C_0}\,r\) and the second by \(C_0 r/\log 2\).
The comparison with the earlier estimate concerns the information in that inequality alone.
Indeed at \(j=1\) its \(r\)-th root for \(s=r\) has right side \(8b\sinh(m/r)e^{2m/r}\).
Taking the smallest numerical value \(\mathcal D=1\) allowed by the discriminant bound, the choice \(m=r/(8b)\) makes this expression at least one while \(m\to0\) for \(b\asymp r^2/\log(2r)\).
The comparison asserts no realization of these parameters by units of vanishing measure.
