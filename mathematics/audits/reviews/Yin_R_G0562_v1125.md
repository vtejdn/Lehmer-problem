# Independent review of the Lehmer gap at bounded rank of the conjugate arguments

<a id="finding"></a>
## Integral projection overlap, actual real conjugates and effective compression

At 2026-11-24T22:56 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v68.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v68.
The effective positive gap holds for all nontorsion algebraic units of bounded argument rank, with arbitrary degree, signature and log-modulus rank.
The strict bounds on \(b\) and \(k\), the compressed degree and the quantitative consequence for shrinking sequences all hold as stated.

For every conjugate \(a\), \((1-c)[a]\) is the class of \(a/\overline a=(a/|a|)^2\).
Squaring scales classes by two, so the dimension of their rational span agrees with the rank of the multiplicative arguments modulo torsion.
Passing from unit-circle phases to arguments identifies roots of unity with rational multiples of \(2\pi\).
Since \(c\) is an involution, this image is exactly its negative eigenspace on \(V\), including \(k=0\).

Average a rational positive definite inner product over the finite group \(G\).
For rational \(v\ne0\), at least one pairing \((v,[a])\) is nonzero since the conjugate classes span \(V\).
The sum of all pairings is zero since their product is the rational norm of a unit, equal to \(1\) or \(-1\).
Both strict signs occur, and reversing \(v\) if necessary leaves at most \(d/2\) strictly positive terms.
The product \(z\) of the actual selected conjugates has positive pairing of its class with the chosen \(v\), and is a nontorsion unit of height at most \((d/2)h(w)=m(w)/2\).
An element fixing \(v\) permutes the actual selected set because the inner product is invariant, even if several distinct elements of that set have the same multiplicative class.
It fixes the actual product \(z\).
Thus any \(g\) with \(g^{-1}cg\) fixing \(v\) gives \(\overline{g z}=g z\).
The fractions in \(G\) and among embeddings are related by uniform extension counts, giving at least that fraction of real conjugates of \(z\).
Garza's bound \(R\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/R})/2\) is increasing for \(R>0\).
At \(R\ge1/2\) it gives \(h(z)\ge\operatorname{arsinh}(1/4)/4\) and hence \(m(w)\ge c_0\).

Let \(W\) be a nonzero irreducible rational summand, and extend its averaged inner product to \(W_{\mathbb R}\).
Complex conjugation and each of its conjugates are orthogonal involutions.
Their negative projections \(P,P_g\) are consequently self-adjoint idempotents of rank \(j\).
Four times \(\operatorname{tr}(PP_g)\) is the sum with signs of the traces of \(1,c,g^{-1}cg,cg^{-1}cg\).
These traces are rational because \(W\) is rational and algebraic integers because finite-order matrices have root-of-unity eigenvalues.
Also \(\operatorname{tr}(PP_g)=\operatorname{tr}(PP_gP)=\|P_gP\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\), so positivity costs at least \(1/4\), and zero forces pointwise fixation of \(PW\) by \(g^{-1}cg\).

The averaging operator \(\mathbb E_g P_g\) belongs to the center of the rational group algebra acting on \(W\).
On each complex irreducible submodule it is scalar, with scalar equal to the multiplicity of the eigenvalue \(-1\) of \(c\) there divided by that submodule's dimension, because every conjugate has the same trace there.
This scalar is rational, even for a rational representation that splits after complexification or has repeated complex constituents.
The operator is diagonalizable on the complexification by complete reducibility.
Its rational eigenspaces are \(G\)-stable, hence irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) forces the single scalar \(j/r\) on the whole space.
Consequently the mean overlap trace is \(j^2/r\), and at most \(4j^2/r\) of the group elements have nonzero overlap.
If \(j>0\) and \(r\ge8j^2\), any nonzero rational vector in \(PW\), which exists because \(P\) is rational, passes the real-conjugate test on at least half of \(G\).
For \(p=1-4j^2/r>0\) the same calculation supplies \(m(w)\ge p\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/p})\).
For \(j=0\), every conjugate of \(c\) is the identity on \(W\) and any nonzero vector works.
Thus under \(m(w)<c_0\) each summand, with multiplicity, has \(j\ge1\) and \(r<8j^2\).
Summing dimensions gives \(k\ge1\) and \(b<8\sum j^2\le8k^2\).

Transitivity and Galois invariance give a common size \(e\) for all torsion-equivalence classes of roots.
The product on each class has class \(e[a]\), hence different classes give different products even modulo torsion, and the products form one full orbit.
Each class has constant modulus at the chosen embedding, so its exterior contribution to the total logarithmic measure is precisely that of its product.
The compressed unit has the same measure, the same rational representation spanned by the products, and the same argument rank.
This proof works also when the original unit is nonreciprocal.

The subgroup generated by the finitely many original classes is free abelian of rank \(b\) inside their rational span.
The finite group induced on it has integral invertible matrices, and reduction modulo three injects this finite group into \(\mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb F_3)\).
Indeed a nontrivial kernel would contain a matrix of prime order of the form \(I+3^aA\), \(a\ge1\), with \(A\) nonzero modulo three.
For a prime order different from three, the linear term in the binomial expansion survives modulo \(3^{a+1}\), and for order three it survives modulo \(3^{a+2}\).
The size of the orbit of the compressed classes is their actual field degree, so \(d_0\le3^{b^2}<3^{64k^4}\) below \(c_0\).

For each integral rank cutoff \(K\ge1\) the resulting finite coefficient box includes every compressed minimum of measure below \(c_0\), by the elementary symmetric coefficient bound and \(\exp(c_0)<2\).
Multiplicative measures strictly greater than one in that box can be recognized and ordered by real algebraic comparison, including comparison to \(\exp(c_0)=\sqrt{(1+\sqrt{17})/4}\).
Allowing extra polynomials or taking \(c_0\) if there are no eligible positive entries still gives an effective positive lower bound.
For real \(K\) one can take its integer part since \(k\) is integral, and the rank-zero case follows directly from the selected product.
Finally \(d_0\to\infty\) for shrinking positive measures by monic bounded-degree finiteness.
Taking logarithms of the strict compressed degree bound gives the claimed eventual lower bound on \(k\), and also \(k\to\infty\).
