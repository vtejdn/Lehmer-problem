## Classification under the limit hypotheses

Every limit has one of the nine displayed rational forms, with the stated density, atom masses and multiplicative Mahler measure.
The \(-1\) restriction eliminates rows one and five, and the argument leaves the realizability of the other seven open.

### From the finite trace identity to cyclotomic factors

The monic trace polynomial \(U_h(x/2)\) satisfies \(U_h(1)=h+1\), giving \(C_h(2)=h+2\) and leading coefficient \(j=2\).
At \(t_l=(l+1/2)\pi/(h+1)\), \(0\le l\le h\), the sign of \(2\sin((h+1)t_l)-\sin(ht_l)\) is \((-1)^l\).
This gives \(h\) simple roots of \(C_h\) in \((-2,2)\) and checks the [inverse construction's hypotheses](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse).
Its divisor condition forces \(h\to\infty\) as \(q\to\infty\).
The geometric sum gives \((1-z^2)z^hU_h(x/2)=1-z^{2h+2}\).
Subtraction of the term \(z(1-z^{2h})\) from twice this identity gives exactly \(2-z+z^{2h+1}(1-2z)\), the product \(K_qB^\#\) by the [trace formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace).
The constant of \(B^\#\) is one, so \([z]K_q=-1-2[z]B^\#\) is odd.

At fixed \(j=2\) the [limit argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) gives subsequential convergence on each strict disk and stabilization of each integer coefficient.
The [periodic decomposition](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) gives an integer numerator over \(1-z^d\) for some period \(d\), including when the generating function has a polynomial part.
Canceling common monic cyclotomic factors and normalizing the remaining denominator at zero gives integral \(U,V\), with \(V\) a signed squarefree cyclotomic product.
Because \(K(0)=2\), the first \(n\) coefficients of \((2-z)/K\) are determined by a fixed prefix of \(K\).
For every fixed \(n\) that prefix agrees eventually with \(K_q\), and the distant term of \(K_qB^\#\) is above degree \(n\).
Thus \(A=(2-z)/K\) has integral Taylor coefficients, including when \(h\) grows faster than \(q\).
Strict positivity of \(\Re K\) gives analyticity of \(A\) in the disk.

For a rational power series the shifted coefficient sequences span a finite-dimensional rational space, since their common recurrence holds after a finite prefix and the exceptional coordinates span a finite-dimensional space.
In this space a finite coordinate projection is injective since the coordinate functionals separate its points.
The integer span of all shifts therefore embeds into a finitely generated free abelian group and is itself finitely generated and free.
The shift endomorphism has an integral matrix \(S\) on that group, and a matrix coefficient of \((I-zS)^{-1}\) recovers the series of \(A\).
Hence every reciprocal pole of \(A\) is an algebraic integer.
Its conjugates are reciprocals of poles of the reduced rational function, so disk analyticity bounds all of their moduli by one.
The [nonzero form of Kronecker's theorem](../literature/MV226/Yin_U001.md#mv226-kronecker-algorithm) puts them on roots of unity.
In \(A=(2-z)V/U\), coprimality leaves room for exactly one cancellation away from torsion, namely a simple zero of \(U\) at \(2\).
This zero must occur because a polynomial with only torsion zeros and constant two is twice a signed integral cyclotomic product, which would make the linear coefficient of \(K\) even.
At a torsion zero of \(K\), multiplicity at least two would contradict the radial lower bound \(2(1-r)/(1+r)\) for \(\Re K(r\xi)\) from its positive Herglotz measure.
Thus \(U_0=U/(2-z)\) and \(V\) are coprime, squarefree and normalized to constant one.

### Filtering the sparse Laurent polynomial

Reciprocity gives \(K+K(1/z)=(U_0/V)Q\), where \(Q=(2-z)+\epsilon z^k(2z-1)\).
Away from the atoms this is the density \(\rho\) of \(2\mu\), since the boundary real part of an atomic Herglotz kernel vanishes there.
The periodic decomposition and [positive continuous mass](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#radial-limits) make \(\rho\) a nonzero nonnegative Laurent polynomial.
Its zeros on the circle have even order, as seen by using the local real parameter \(t\) in \(z=e^{it}\).
In particular a simple circle zero of \(Q\) outside torsion is impossible.

At a torsion zero, \(\eta=(2-\xi)/(2\xi-1)\) is torsion and \((\eta-\xi)/2=\xi\eta-1\) is an algebraic integer.
All conjugates have modulus at most one, so this integer is zero or torsion.
In the zero case \(\eta=\xi\) and \(\xi^2=1\).
Otherwise equality in \(|\eta-\xi|\le2\) forces \(\eta=-\xi\) and then \(\xi^2-\xi+1=0\).
Substitution at \(1,-1\) gives respectively \(\epsilon=-1\) and \(\epsilon(-1)^k=1\).
At a primitive sixth root the test is \(\xi^{k-1}=\epsilon\), giving the stated two congruences.

The circle factor \((2z-1)/(2-z)\) has degree one as a Blaschke product and phase derivative \(P(t)\in[1/3,3]\).
For \(k\ge0\) the phase of its product with \(z^k\) is strictly increasing with degree \(k+1\).
For \(k=-m\le-4\) the reciprocal factor times \(z^m\) has strictly increasing phase and degree \(m-1\).
At most four distinct torsion points can occur, giving \(-5\le k\le3\).
At \(k=-5,-4,2,3\) the maximum numbers of permitted roots for either sign are respectively \(2,1,1,2\), below the required \(4,3,3,4\).
For \(k=-3,\epsilon=1\) the polynomial \(z^3Q=z^2(-x^2+2x+2)\) has simple circle zeros over \(x=1-\sqrt3\), outside the three permitted traces.

Multiplying the remaining brackets by \(z^{\max(0,-k)}\) gives the following torsion factors of odd multiplicity, with the two signs listed in the order \(+,-\) and factors written up to sign.
For \(k=-3\) the surviving minus sign gives \((z-1)(z+1)\) from multiplicities three and one.
For \(k=-2\) the factors are \(z+1\) and \((z-1)\Phi_6\), since the extra factor \(z^2-3z+1\) in the plus case has positive real reciprocal roots off the circle.
For \(k=-1\) they are \(1\) and \((z-1)(z+1)\), with the first case coming from \(z^2-4z+1\).
For \(k=0\) they are \(z+1,z-1\), and for \(k=1\) they are \(\Phi_6,(z-1)(z+1)\).
Squarefreeness, nonnegativity and absence of density poles force each listed irreducible into exactly one of \(U_0,V\) and exclude all others.
Imposing \(v-u=k+1\) determines every assignment except the two allocations of \(z-1,z+1\) at \(k=-1,\epsilon=-1\).
For the discarded assignment \(K=(2-z)(1-z)/(1+z)=z-4+6/(1+z)\), so \(\rho=x-2\) is negative on every open arc away from \(1\).
Normalizing the constant terms of the surviving assignments gives the nine rows, with leading-coefficient signs agreeing with their respective \(\epsilon\).

### Densities, atoms and eventual prefixes

For the first two rows expanding the polynomial and its reciprocal gives \((x-2)^2(x+2)\) and \((3-x)(x+2)\).
In row three \(K=(1-z)^2+1/(1-z)\), giving density \((x-1)^2\) and mass \(1/2\) at \(1\).
Rows four through seven have \(K=2-z,\ z+2/(1-z),\ -1+3/(1+z),\ 1+1/(1-z)\) respectively.
Their densities are \(4-x,x+2,1,3\), and the atomic masses are respectively absent, \(1\) at \(1\), \(3/2\) at \(-1\), and \(1/2\) at \(1\).
In row eight \(\xi(2\xi-1)=\xi-2\) at each primitive sixth root, so the residue divided by \(-2\xi\) is \(1/2\) at both, and \(K+K(1/z)=2\).
In row nine \(K=(1/2)/(1-z)+(3/2)/(1+z)\), giving density two and masses \(1/4,3/4\) at \(1,-1\).
In every row half the mean of the stated density plus the sum of these atom masses is two.

The factors \(x\pm2\) and \(x-1\) have Mahler measure one after Laurent monomial normalization.
The remaining nonconstant factors \(3-x,4-x\) have measures \((3+\sqrt5)/2,2+\sqrt3\) by the exterior roots of their monic lifts.
Together with the constant densities these give all nine tabulated measures.
In rows one and five \(\rho\) vanishes at \(-1\), there is no atom there and \(M(\rho)=1\), so the [endpoint restriction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v40.md#statement) rules out both.
Finally, any sequence violating the assertion about a fixed prefix at unbounded \(q\) admits a convergent subsequence, on which integrality forces eventual equality with the corresponding listed prefix.
This yields a threshold for each fixed order without a bound uniform in that order.
