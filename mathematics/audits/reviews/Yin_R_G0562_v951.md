The reviewer received the fixed claim and had no role in developing its proof.

The eventual simple reciprocal factors have no root at either endpoint, so inversion defines a nontrivial automorphism of each root field fixing its trace and the quadratic equation gives relative degree two.
In characteristic two write \(u=U-U^{-1}\).
On the torus the two graph equations differ by \(V+1\), and both evaluate to one at \(V=1\).
Simultaneous inversion preserves \(u\) and interchanges the graphs after multiplication by the unit \(V\).
In a dyadic field completion the quadratic involution must induce a residue automorphism, which preserves each graph since its equation has prime-field coefficients.
This contradicts the interchange for both inert and ramified places and gives a split quadratic algebra at every dyadic trace place.

Each local norm of \(t^2-4\) above two is consequently a square, hence so is their product in \(\mathbb Q_2\).
Monicity and the product of the endpoint evaluations confine the odd part of the squareclass to 5 and 23, with 23 occurring to exponent one precisely on its unique carrier for odd \(l\).
The possible units \(\pm5^a23^e\), with \(a,e\in\{0,1\}\), are one modulo eight exactly when \(a=0\) and the sign is \((-1)^e\).
Each off-circle inverse pair contributes one exterior root, while each circle pair supplies one negative real image of \(t^2-4\) and conjugate nonreal embeddings contribute positive products.
Thus the sign is also \((-1)^{n-H}\), which gives the asserted degree dichotomy when \(H\) is even.
In the joint splitting field, permutations of pairs leave the product of their oriented differences unchanged apart from the sign of internal exchanges.
Its square is the rational norm, so the squareclass determines both triviality of the character and the quadratic fixed field of its kernel in the nontrivial case.

At a marked endpoint the normalized Laurent expression gives the coefficient \(c=8k^2+12l^2-28\eta^kkl\) and the trace derivative \(\eta c\).
Under (1), \(c\) reduces to twice a nonzero square modulo five.
Hensel factorization separates a linear factor at \(2\eta\) from a complementary factor which is a residue unit there, giving one root even over the algebraic closure and hence one degree-one trace completion across all factors.
Taylor expansion first gives \(v_5(t_0-2\eta)=2\), then \((t_0^2-4)/25\equiv-4/c\bmod5\), a nonsquare unit since \(-4\equiv1\bmod5\).
Its square root generates the unramified quadratic field over that completion.
The two marked residues, when present, are distinct, and the unmarked endpoint value is a 5-adic unit.
At 23 the norm valuation one forces a single place of residue degree one with valuation one on \(t^2-4\).
The local square-root polynomial is Eisenstein at that place and its basis discriminant has valuation one, giving tame quadratic ramification with relative discriminant exponent one.
The integral relative order with basis \(1,z\) excludes ramification at every other odd place away from the endpoint support.
For even \(l\) satisfying (1) the tower discriminant formula now gives equality of the absolute root discriminants.
