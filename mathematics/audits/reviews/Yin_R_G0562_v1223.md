## Polynomial realization, first-log limits and the boundedness criterion

At 2026-11-25T05:09 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v163 independently reviewed for Yin the fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v163.md#statement) and its three proofs.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or prior findings on v163.
The reviewer developed the earlier v152 family providing an example below the plastic number, and independently reviewed the v159 relative-height input.
The three alternatives for the exceptional set, both directions of the two-point test and the adjacent-degree result with a prescribed growth rate follow.

For a fixed degree bound, a bound on the Mahler measure bounds every coefficient by the usual elementary-symmetric-function estimate.
Thus a minimum positive measure through that degree exists whenever the ceiling includes a positive candidate.
The subplastic example from v152 ensures that a fixed finite cutoff suffices here.
At a strict decrease a factorization into smaller-degree factors would give a positive factor of no larger measure at an earlier degree, since each nonzero integer factor has measure at least one.
A factor \(X\) is likewise excluded, and content, leading coefficient and nonzero constant term all have modulus one below the plastic number.
Choosing the leading sign gives an irreducible monic unit minimum, reciprocal by Smyth.
Exhaustion of the degree bounds gives the unrestricted infimum, attained at the last decrease if the list is finite and approached by the decreasing record values if it is infinite.
Selecting another minimizing conjugacy class at a tie within a degree does not change any of the arguments.

If the \(d_i\) conjugates of \(w_i\) have classes modulo torsion of common size \(f>1\), their class products have degree \(d_i/f\) and the same total log measure.
This contradicts strict improvement at \(d_i\).
In particular an equality between the \(a\)-th powers of two conjugates for any positive integer \(a\) forces the conjugates to coincide.
The powered number therefore still has degree \(d_i\), and its monic minimum has measure \(A_i^a\) by taking the product of the powered conjugate moduli strictly above one.
The other minimum has degree \(d_{i+1}>d_i\).
Their product is squarefree over \(\mathbb Q\), belongs to \(\mathbb Z[X]\), has degree \(d_i+d_{i+1}\) and has first measure \(A_i^a A_{i+1}\).
Only the original \(w_i,w_{i+1}\) must be adjacent strict records.

Use v159 on those original sources with weights \(a,1\).
Their degrees tend to infinity on any infinite record list and their unpowered measures share a fixed ceiling.
The result gives \(m(C_{i,a})\ge a b_{i+1}m(A_i)\), uniformly in the positive integer \(a\).
As \(A_i>1\) is itself a conjugate contributing to its measure, \(m(A_i)\ge m_i\).
The canonical estimate \(b_{i+1}\gg d_{i+1}/(\log(3d_{i+1}))^{212}\) tends to infinity.
The first logs in each row have spacing \(m_i\) and endpoints \(m_i+m_{i+1}\) and \(\lceil(\log(3d_i))^3\rceil m_i+m_{i+1}\).
Voutier gives \(m_i>\frac14(\log\log d_i/\log d_i)^3\) at large \(d_i\).
Hence the upper endpoint tends to infinity even if \(m_i\to0\), while the lower endpoint tends to \(2\ell\) in either infinite-list case.

A finite list gives finitely many test inputs, so its exceptional set is empty, including when no adjacent pair survives the initial cutoff.
In the infinite-list case, membership of \(t\) in the exceptional set produces a sequence of row entries whose first logs approach \(t\) and whose second logs go to infinity, by taking successively shrinking neighborhoods and increasing thresholds.
Every fixed collection of finitely many rows has bounded second logs, forcing indices to tend to infinity along this sequence.
When \(\ell>0\), the bounded first logs imply bounded exponents because \(am_i\ge a\ell\).
A constant-exponent subsequence then forces \(t=(a+1)\ell\).
Conversely any fixed integer \(a\ge1\) is allowed in every sufficiently large row, where the second log is at least \(a b_{i+1}\ell\to\infty\).
This yields the exact lattice with integer multiples starting at two.

When \(\ell=0\), fix \(0<s\le t<\infty\).
For all sufficiently large rows the minimum first log is below \(s\) and the maximum exceeds \(t\).
For every \(x\in[s,t]\) the stated floor therefore selects an allowed positive exponent, including when \(x\) is already a progression point.
Its undershoot is less than \(m_i\), and \(a m_i>x-m_i-m_{i+1}\).
The lower bound \(b_{i+1}(s-m_i-m_{i+1})\) for the second log diverges independently of \(x\) since eventually the parenthesis is at least \(s/2\).
Hence every positive \(t\) is exceptional in this case.
At positive gap the exceptional set is empty or lies on the stated positive lattice, which cannot contain both prescribed logarithms when their ratio is irrational.
In particular \(\log 2/\log 3\) is irrational by unique prime factorization.
Nonmembership of a point is precisely the existence of a neighborhood with bounded log second measure there, equivalent by exponentiation to the multiplicative formulation.

For any chosen \(x_j\downarrow0\), apply the zero-gap row estimate at each fixed positive target.
A sufficiently late row puts its first log in \([x_j/2,x_j]\) and its second log above both \(j\) and the supremum of \(G\) on that compact interval, which is finite by local boundedness.
The rows may be chosen strictly increasing.
For \(x_j\le T\) the selected exponent is at most \(T/m_i\), and Voutier gives the displayed bound with constant four.
This conclusion allows powers of the earlier source.
If both factors are instead unpowered their distinct first logs decrease strictly to zero, so above any positive cutoff there are finitely many.
The indicated pointwise supremum plus one is therefore finite and locally bounded on the entire positive axis, and majorizes the corresponding second logs.
The test supplies a condition equivalent to a gap, while establishing boundedness at either specified first measure remains open.
