### The actual degree and the endpoint singularities

The constant \(1\) and the monic \(T_j\) for \(j\ge1\) form an integral triangular basis for the trace expansion, so deleting the two extremal monomials determines \(B\) and \(s<n\) uniquely.
If \(z^n=\epsilon\), then \(z^{2n}=1\) and the absolute value of \(P(z)\) equals that of \(U_\epsilon(z)\).
The two samples partition the full \(2n\)-th roots of unity.
The torsion-free hypothesis excludes every exact zero in these samples, including \(z=\pm1\), even if \(U_\epsilon\) itself has torsion roots.
For \(s\ge1\) each \(U_\epsilon\) has degree \(2s\) and nonzero constant, with the leading coefficient of \(B\) at both ends.
Reversing each product therefore contributes \(n\log|\mathrm{lc}(B)|\) in addition to the sum of \(\log|v^n-\epsilon|\) over its roots with multiplicities.

Each primitive minimal polynomial of such a root has degree at most \(2s\) and measure at most \(M(U_\epsilon)\), including when the endpoint polynomial is nonmonic or imprimitive.
For a nontorsion root, one can invert outside the circle, detach \(n\log^+|v|\), and use the C336v3 power-distance estimate at exponent \(n\) with phase of order at most two.
Its logarithmic-form argument applies both on the circle and when the inverted root's modulus is close to one.
For a torsion root the order is at most twice the square of its degree, and the nonzero chord distance from \(\epsilon\) is bounded below by a constant times the reciprocal of twice that order.
These give the uniform error in (1) after summing over the \(4s\) roots of the two polynomials.
Possible singularities in a logarithmic integral at endpoint roots require no uniform pointwise estimate at a zero.
The actual arithmetic samples omit those zeros by hypothesis, and nearby nontorsion samples are covered by the power-distance estimate.

If both endpoint measures are one, their leading coefficients have absolute value one and all their roots lie on the unit circle by integral factorization and Kronecker.
Every zero of \(B-2\) or \(B+2\) consequently belongs to \([-2,2]\), by lifting through \(z+z^{-1}\).
After changing the sign of \(B\) as needed, monicity and weak interlacing with the common derivative yield \((-1)^{s-i}(B(\xi_i)\pm2)\ge0\) for each \(i\).
This remains valid at repeated roots because equality in the linear-factor sign count is allowed.
The stronger inequalities \((-1)^{s-i}B(\xi_i)\ge2\) then give strict separation of consecutive nodes, including the endpoints.
The monic \(T_s\) has absolute value at most two on the whole interval.
Thus the coefficient of degree \(s\) in the interpolation formula for \(B-T_s\) is a sum of nonnegative terms, with each denominator having sign \((-1)^{s-i}\).
That coefficient is zero, so all the interpolation values vanish.
The argument also works for \(s=1\) using just the two endpoints.
Restoring the possible sign gives \(B=\epsilon T_s\), and direct multiplication gives the two torsion binomials claimed in the ledger.
In degree zero the two sampled constant values have absolute product \(|c^2-4|\), and \(|c|\le2\) gives only unit-circle roots.

For at most \(t\) terms of \(P\), shifting the middle part and adjusting its central coefficient gives each endpoint polynomial at most \(t+1\) terms.
At least one has positive logarithmic measure, so DS17 bounds their sum below in terms of \(t\).
Dividing (1) by \(d=2n\) leaves a positive lower bound under the stated error hypothesis.
Bounded support and bounded initial measure bound the coefficient sum uniformly by the cited sparse coefficient estimate.

### Factors of integral representatives modulo the sampling polynomial

This argument needs neither ordinary polynomial divisibility of \(U_\epsilon\) by \(F_\epsilon\) nor a degree bound on the chosen representative.
An integer constant contributes its content alone, including absolute content one for a unit.
The irreducible factor \(z\) has measure one and contributes absolute norm one since all sample nodes have modulus one.

### Five terms, both canonical choices, and a strict difference of rates

The quadratic lift is therefore irreducible over that field, giving degree 38, and the trace root in \((2,3)\) supplies an exterior conjugate.
I also took the integer determinants of multiplication by \(Q\bmod\Phi_s\) on the power bases of \(\mathbb Z[y]/(\Phi_s)\).
In order \(1,2,4,19,38,76\) they give \(-1,1,1,1,1,1\).
