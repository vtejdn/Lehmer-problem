## Independent review of the fixed-seed gap and the limiting radical bound

At 2026-11-20T23:27 (UTC), an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the whole submitted C345 v684 and found the all-delay and long-delay factor bounds, the distribution of \(H(a)\), and the consequences for \(D(a)\) sound.
The reviewer took no part in developing v684 and inspected the fixed proof and its cited inputs without development dialogue, author explanations or earlier findings on this proof.

For both signs and all \(n\ge7\), the integer-seed factorization gives \(a-1<r<a+1\), a single exterior root, and \(d=\deg P\ge n-1\).
Thus \(m(P)=\log r\) and the first positive radical \(r^{1/p}\) is exterior and distinct from \(r\), giving the nonzero value required in the sparse norm bound.
For each \(p^e\mid R\), the in-field positive radical \(r^{1/p^e}\) exists by the divisor characterization.
The norm bound with degree \(n+1\) and coefficient length \(2(a+1)\) gives \(p^e\le2(a+1)r^{(n+1)/(pd)}\).
The exponent is at most \(2/3\), including \(n=7,p=2,d=n-1\), so the largest power of each prime dividing \(R\) appears among the integers whose least common multiple defines \(B(a)\).
This step uses no large-delay inequality.

Under (1) the degree and measure estimate on \(r^{1/p}\) gives \(p<n\) for each prime dividing \(R\), independently of the seed residue.
For odd \(p\), the cited early and delayed trace congruences then imply \(p^{e+1}\mid(a-1)a(a+1)\).
Since two of these three integers can differ only by one or two, an odd prime dividing the product occurs in precisely one factor, where removing the radical costs one power of \(p\).
For \(p=2\), the companion congruence for \(P\) is available modulo \(2^{e+1}\) at both odd indices \(1\) and \(3\).
Every index used after doubling is at most six and therefore precedes the term of delay \(n\).
Subtracting the contributions of \(P\) from those of \(G\) gives \(2b\) at each comparison, where \(b\) counts the possible single factor \(X+1\).
If \(a\) is even, the second difference \(a^6-a^3\) vanishes modulo four, giving \(b=0\).
The oddness of \(a-1\) then implies \(2^{e+1}\mid a\).
For odd \(a\) the first difference gives \(2^{e+1}\mid a-1\) when \(b=0\), and \(2^{e+1}\mid(a-2)(a+1)\) when \(b=1\).
In the latter case \(a-2\) is odd.
This establishes \(R\mid H(a)\) without adding the valuations of the two even neighbors of an odd seed.

The exact minimum for irreducible factors of \(P(X^h)\) is \(\log r/\gcd(h,R)\), and the same input gives irreducibility when that gcd is one.
Every root of the substituted cofactor \(U(X^h)\) is torsion.
Consequently the two divisibility conclusions give the bounds for each noncyclotomic factor, including all substitutions and both signs over the stated delay ranges.

For each prime and each \(j\ge1\), the valuation of the least common multiple is at least \(j\) exactly when a neighbor is divisible by \(p^{j+1}\).
The three residue classes are distinct even modulo four.
Their densities give \(3p^{-j-1}\), with threshold events at distinct primes independent in the limit by the Chinese remainder theorem.
At seeds up to \(X\), the error from counting at most three extra representatives per prime up to \(\sqrt{X+1}\) is \(O(X^{-1/2})\) in proportion.
The residual bound \(3\sum_{p>Y}p^{-2}\) and the finite-prime high-exponent bound \(\sum_{p\le Y}3p^{-J-2}\) allow the finite residue calculation to approximate membership in any prescribed set of positive integers.
The limiting independent variables obey the same tail estimates, so their product is finite almost surely and yields the claimed law.
The probability of \(H=1\) is the indicated positive product, since the \(p=2\) factor is \(1/4\) and \(\sum_p p^{-2}<\infty\).

Summing the increments \(p^{sj}-p^{s(j-1)}\) against the valuation tail gives the local moment \(1+3(p^s-1)/(p^2(1-p^{s-1}))\) for \(0<s<1\).
Its excess over one is \(O_s(p^{s-2})\), so monotone convergence over finite sets of primes gives the finite moment \(C_s\).
For fixed \(a\) the long-delay family is nonempty, and taking its infimum preserves the common lower bound \(\log(a-1)/H(a)\).
Since \(\log(a-1)/\log a\to1\), the upper density of the exceptional seeds at any fixed \(t\) is bounded by \(C_s(t/(1-\eta))^s\) for every \(0<\eta<1\).
Letting \(\eta\) decrease to zero establishes the constant uniform in \(t\) in (3).
For each positive \(f=o(\log a)\) and fixed \(M\), eventual strict inequality \(Mf(a)<t\log a\) gives the required inclusion even when \(D(a)=Mf(a)\).
Taking \(t\) to zero gives density zero for that exceptional set.
