For \(w=\gamma^s\notin\mathbb R\) put \(\eta=|\Im w|\).
The imaginary parts of \(a+bw\) and \((a+bw)\bar w\) bound \(\eta|b|\) and \(\eta|a|\) by \(|a+bw|\) and \(|w|\,|a+bw|\), respectively.
Thus the ratio of the upper and lower comparison constants for \(|a+bw|/\max(|a|,|b|)\) is at most \(C=(1+|w|)^2/\eta\).
If the same index dominates the common envelope on an interval of radius \(K\) about \(p\log|\gamma|\), comparison at the right endpoint suppresses every larger index by \(e^{-|j-j_0|K}\), and comparison at the left endpoint does the same for each smaller index.
The sum of the actual nondominant magnitudes divided by the dominant magnitude is at most \(2C/(e^K-1)\).
This is strictly below one for \(K>K_\gamma\), which rules out a zero and proves the asserted localization on a closed interval.
The same reasoning excludes a sole occupied base index at such a zero.
The scalar geometric tails and their reversal appear on pp. 12–13 of the [Avendaño–Kogan–Nisse–Rojas manuscript](../literature/AKNR18/readme.md#edition).
The comparison by \(C\) proves their application here with a single envelope for several complex evaluations.

The slope on the terminal ray is \(j_{\max}\).
Integrating its difference from the envelope slope on the positive half-line counts each jump with its displacement from zero, giving exactly \(\log H-\log h_{j_{\max}}\).
Disjoint positive intervals thus contribute distinct jumps of size at least one.
The largest row coefficient \(H\) is the coefficient height of \(F\) because the two residue classes are distinct.
I checked Theorem 1 on p. 1 of [Dobrowolski and Smyth, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.04376).
After removal of an initial monomial there are still \(T\ge2\) terms, so the accepted sparse coefficient inequality gives the loss \((T-2)\log2\).
Removing integer content leaves \(P\) as a factor by primitivity, with \(m(F)=m(P)+m(R)\) for the resulting multiple.
These checks prove the finite estimate for every admissible disjoint subcollection.

The norm of a nonzero difference of squared radii is a nonzero integer, and its other conjugates are bounded by \(2e^{2L}=B\).
The degree bound \(d^4\) follows by adjoining at most four roots of \(P\), including when one of the two numbers compared is \(1\).
The derivative of \(\tfrac12\log z\) on \([1,e^{2L}]\) is at least \(B^{-1}\), giving the claimed spacing \(B^{-d^4}\).
The norm argument on \(\gamma-\bar\gamma\) gives \(2|\Im\gamma|\ge(2e^L)^{1-d^2}\), which suffices for \(K_*\).
The reciprocal nontorsion unit has an exterior root by Kronecker's theorem.
The stipulated pair at each exterior radius gives \(\sum_\rho\log\rho=m(P)/2\) and hence \(m(P)\ge2B^{-d^4}\).
Under (1) the centers and the origin are separated by at least \(p^{3/4}\), whereas \(K_*=O_L(1+d^2)\) and \(d=O_L((\log p)^{1/4})\).
The loss on the right of (3), relative to \(p\,m(P)\), is bounded by \(1/p+O_L((1+d^3+\sqrt p)/p^{3/4})\).
Here the two residues give \(T\le 2(b+3)\) even with a term at the degree cap.
This proves the lower estimate uniformly.

For the finite box bound, dividing the term \((pb+1)m(P)/(n-d)\) by \(p\,m(P)\) gives \(1/2+O((d+1)/b)\).
The other two terms after that division total \(O((1+d/(n-d))\log(6n)/p^{3/4})\).
Both errors are \(O_L(p^{-1/4})\) in this range.
Since \(p-1>d^2\) eventually, a quotient of distinct conjugates cannot have order \(p\).
All \(d\) powered conjugates are distinct, so a row factor carrying \(P\) after substitution would cost at least \(p\,m(P)\), more than the constructed polynomial.
This checks the exclusion of a missing row and preservation of \(P\) upon removing the common row gcd, retaining degree at most \(pb+1\) and support in both prescribed classes.
Bounded degree and measure bound the integer coefficients of \(R\), proving attainment in each class of witnesses.

In the example \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) has the specified real embedding and \(-\lambda\) is negative there.
For \(\xi^2=-\lambda\) the inclusion \(\lambda\in\mathbb Q(\xi)\) gives \([\mathbb Q(\xi):\mathbb Q(\lambda)]=2\).
The resulting minimal degree equals the degree of \(S(-x^2)\).
Since \(\deg S\) is even, the substitution preserves monicity and reciprocity, and the two lifts of the unique exterior root have modulus \(\sqrt\lambda\), giving total log measure \(\log\lambda\).
The rate estimate is relative to \(m(P)\), leaving the absolute gap question open.
