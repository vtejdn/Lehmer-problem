# Review of the least height of a rational circle parameter

<a id="finding"></a>
## The minimum is \(2G/\pi\) with the stated uniform error

At 2026-11-24T21:23 an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [parameter-height theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v36.md#statement), including its quantitative estimates and example.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and assignment without development dialogue or previous conclusions on this version.
The [norm-one construction](Yin_R_G0562_v1089.md#finding) and the [integer norm bound](Yin_R_G0562_v1083.md#finding) were used at their independently reviewed scopes.
The stated minimum, error uniform in the rational coordinate change, and total-measure consequences follow.

The formulas for \(u\) and \(\beta\) give \(\mathbb Q(u)\subseteq F\) and \(L=\mathbb Q(u,i)\).
Thus \([L:\mathbb Q(u)]\le2=[L:F]\) gives equality of \(\mathbb Q(u)\) and \(F\).
An invertible rational fractional linear image has the same field and cannot have a zero numerator or denominator when \(u\) is irrational.
The rational changes exhaust the birational coordinates because degrees of rational self-maps of the projective line multiply under composition.
The two original Gaussian coefficient pairs are conjugate at their two ends.
Their joint ideal is stable under conjugation, so dividing by a generator gives the indicated primitive forms with one shared unit \(\epsilon\) and nonzero determinant \(\Delta=\epsilon(A\bar B-B\bar A)\).

**Archimedean estimate.**
The minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb Q(i)\) has degree \(n=d/2\), is monic and separable, and has Gaussian integer coefficients by integrality and integral closure.
Its discriminant is a nonzero Gaussian integer, whose modulus is at least one at *each* embedding.
For two roots reflected into the disk, the stated distance bound follows from \(|1-a\bar b|^2-|a-b|^2=(1-|a|^2)(1-|b|^2)\) on the same side and direct cancellation on opposite sides.
After smoothing by \(\rho=1-1/n\), the logarithm summed over ordered distinct pairs incurs \(2(n-1)\sum_j\log^+|z_j|\) and at most \(n(n-1)\log(2/(1+\rho))\).
The discriminant term has the favorable sign at this individual embedding.
The \(n\) diagonal entries cost at most \(n\log n\).
Dividing by \(n^2\) and expanding the smoothed logarithm gives precisely the positive moment sum in (2).
The root moduli in one Gaussian fiber have sums bounded by the full-field values \(dh\) for the positive parts and \(2dh\) for their absolute logarithms.
No equal distribution of those radial sums between the two fibers is required.

The maximum of the two row moduli is bounded away from zero on the circle.
The difference of their squares has at most two zeros unless it vanishes identically, so integration by parts on the smooth arcs gives the claimed \(O_M(k^{-2})\) Fourier bound, including the finite derivative jumps.
At frequency \(k\), reflection changes the phase moment by at most \(k n^{-1}\sum_j|\log|z_j||\).
For the chosen \(K\), \(\rho^{-K}\le4\), \(1/K\le 1/n+\sqrt h\), and \(h\log(2K)=O(\sqrt h)\) for \(0<h\le1/2\).
Cauchy–Schwarz on (2) therefore gives the asserted phase error.
The corrected radial function is Lipschitz on the annulus and bounded on the sphere, so returning to the actual roots incurs \(O_M(h)\), including the restored \(\log^+\) contribution.
Each Gaussian embedding has weight \(1/2\) in the normalized archimedean height, and conjugating both forms preserves their Haar integral.

**Finite places.**
The projective height contribution is \(-d^{-1}\log N I\), and a linear combination gives \(\Delta\in I\).
At any place with positive \(v_{\mathfrak P}(I)\), a form with unit leading coefficient exists.
If both leading coefficients vanished modulo \(\pi\), primitivity would give a unit constant coefficient and the corresponding form would not vanish at integral \(\beta\).
Its root \(s\) has the same nonzero residue as \(\beta\), so \(s^k\equiv1\bmod\pi\) for the chosen multiple of the residue-group orders.
The resulting valuation of \(\beta^k-1\) is at least \(\min(v_{\mathfrak P}(I),v_{\mathfrak P}(\pi))\).
If \(v_{\mathfrak P}(I)>v_{\mathfrak P}(\pi)\), multiplication by \(v_\pi(\Delta)\) covers \(v_{\mathfrak P}(I)\) by the determinant bound.
In the complementary case the same conclusion holds because \(v_\pi(\Delta)\ge1\).
This covers every ramification index, including places above two, with no loss from the finite places not dividing the determinant.
Summing with ideal-norm weights and applying the degree-explicit norm bound yields \(\log N I/d\ll_M\log(N+1)/N+(N+2)kh/3\).
With \(N=\lceil\sqrt{\log(2/h)/h}\rceil\), both terms are \(O_M(\sqrt{h\log(2/h)})\).

**Haar minimum and varying matrices.**
Twice applying the circle mean identity for a linear polynomial gives (3), since the absolute values of the two inner coefficients are \(|A+zB|\) and \(|A+\bar zB|\).
The distinct row directions rule out simultaneous zeros, justifying integrability in the exchanged order.
The lower bound \(\log\max(|A|,|B|)\) disposes of every pair with either modulus at least two.
Below two the squared Gaussian lengths can only be one or two, and two lengths of squared norm two contradict primitivity at \(1+i\).
For two orthogonal units the circle integral is \(4\pi^{-1}\int_0^{\pi/4}\log(2\cos v)\,dv=2G/\pi\), by the odd-frequency sine values in the integrated cosine series.
It is strictly below \(\log2\), and the identity parameter has such rows.
In the mixed-length case pairing \(t\) with \(\pi-t\) gives a product of squared maxima \(5+12s+8s^2\), exceeding \((2+2s)^2\) for every \(0\le s=|\sin t|\le1\).
This proves the claimed minimum among fixed transformations.

At three distinct conjugates over \(\mathbb Q(i)\), equality of two candidate linear ratios forces their degree-at-most-two cross-product to vanish identically.
Since \(U,V\) are coprime and linearly independent, their projective coefficient vector is the unique kernel point of the three evaluation rows.
The local maximum of the signed minors is bounded by the product of row maxima with factor \(6\) only at archimedean places.
The four entries of each row have local maximum \(\max(1,|z_j|)\max(1,|v_j|)\), giving coefficient height at most \(\log6+3(h+h(M(u)))\).
For a primitive Gaussian coefficient vector this height equals \(\log\max(|A|,|B|)\), giving an effective bound on the finite set.
Hence if \(h\le1/2\) and \(h(M(u))\le1\), then \(\max(|A|,|B|)\le6e^{9/2}<541\).
These pairs and their Gaussian unit factors form one finite enumerable set.
The determinant, residue-group orders and row estimates are bounded on it.
In particular, inversion of the two-row matrix gives \(\max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|)\ge |\Delta|\max(1,|z|)/(2\max(|A|,|B|))\), so the annular and piecewise derivative estimates do not require an unquantified separation of roots of the two forms.
The proof provides an absolute asymptotic error order without specifying its optimal numerical coefficient.
The identity has height less than one eventually, so transformations outside this finite comparison cannot improve the minimum.
Since every parameter generates \(F\), multiplication by \([F:\mathbb Q]\) yields the claimed total measure.

The example's trace roots are \((1\pm\sqrt{13})/2\), with the smaller in \((-2,2)\) and the larger greater than two.
The negative discriminant at the smaller trace makes the quadratic lift irreducible over that trace field and the circle root nontorsion.
Every \(j\)-th root of the constructed \(\gamma\) has modulus one, is integral with integral inverse and lies in a field containing \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\).
Conjugation sends that generator to its inverse and fixes its trace field, so the family conditions apply.
The absolute heights \(h(\gamma)/j\) tend to zero, forcing degrees to diverge, while the power-degree inequality bounds all total measures above by \(m(\gamma)\).
The norm to the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) is a nontorsion unit with \(v^j=\gamma^b\).
The resulting identity \(m(\beta_j)=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q(v)]m(v)\) gives the asserted positive lower bound for this example.
