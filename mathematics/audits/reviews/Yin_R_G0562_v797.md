## The two signed differences give a strict numerical contradiction

At 2026-11-20T21:40 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently checked the [numerical increment](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v660.md#statement) from its fixed proof, its direct dependencies and the review questions.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the qualitative strict bound and received no development discussion, author explanation or prior verdict on the numerical claim.
The proof gives the increment \(10^{-8}\) for all sufficiently small positive \(m\), with an absolute cutoff whose value the argument leaves unspecified.

Let \(\eta=10^{-8}\) and \(L\) be any subsequential energy limit from a sequence violating the bound.
The difference between the long-prefix lower bound \(2\log(3/2)-4/9\) and \(K\) exceeds \(2/17+2(707/500)-53/18>0.0012\).
Thus localization applies throughout \(L\le K+\eta\).
The determinant estimate bounds both the quotient energy and the defect at every prefix, where the required inequality is \(5L/2<1\).
The complementary projection estimate consequently gives \(w\le W\) and \(w^2\le5L/4\) throughout this interval.
The short numerator estimate gives \(r\ge r_0=1/\sqrt2\).
Writing \(b=B-2x\), substitution gives \(r_0^2+B-2r_0 W=f(x)+b/3\), proving the stated slack identity with \(J=L-r^2-B+2rw\ge0\).
All terms are nonnegative because \(w<0.69<r_0\).
In particular \(r+r_0-2w>0.034>1/40\), while \(f''(x)=2\sqrt2 e^{-x}>1\) throughout the localized interval.
This gives all five bounds from \(L-f(x)\le\eta\) and \(f(x)-K\le\eta\).
The numerical enclosure for \(x\) is contained in \((0.34643,0.34672)\), and \(2(1-e^{-0.346})-\eta/\sqrt2>0.5849\).
With \(r<0.708\) this gives \(c=r/w<1.22\).
The limiting cross term \(2m\operatorname{Re}\langle H,DQ\rangle\to J-2rw\) gives exactly \(cJ\) for the limiting squared reconstruction error times \(m\).

For each end, \(s^2\|B_h\|_2^2\to2(x+b_\pm)<1\) justifies the actual-tail recurrence uniformly even at this positive slack.
Reflection interchanges the two cuts including the zero index.
Since \(|q|-1\le(q^2-1)/3\) for each occupied integral coefficient, the terminal upper bounds are \(p_\pm=M+\sqrt2 b_\pm/3\).
The two orthogonal output projections each lie inside their corresponding own-cut tails, hence \(w^2\le Z_-^2+Z_+^2\).
The triangle inequality gives \(\|(p_-,p_+)\|\le W\), and each difference \(p_\sigma^2-Z_\sigma^2\) is at least \(M(p_\sigma-Z_\sigma)\).
As \(W-w\le\eta/\sqrt2\), \(W<0.6\) and \(M>0.4\), this bounds each terminal deficit by \(3\eta\) at the scaled limit superior.
Advancing the recurrence back to \(k=\lfloor u/m\rfloor\), \(u=0.0004\), costs a factor tending to \(e^{x-u}<3/2\).
The resulting loss is below \(5\eta\) on each end, and the prefix absolute coefficient mass times \(m\) has limit superior at most \(u+b_\pm/3\le u+\eta\).

Under the probability density \(|I-I(0)|^2/s^2\), projection above the last prefix impulse annihilates \(I(0)B_k\).
Consequently the normalized prefix has limit inferior of its norm at least \(1-u\), since \(1-e^{-u}\ge u-u^2/2\) and \((5+\sqrt2)\eta<\sqrt2 u^2/2\).
Independent absolute-coefficient weighted sampling of the signed monomials gives mean squared distance at most \(4u+o(1)\).
The diagonal samples have distance zero and probability \(O(m)\), so conditioning on distinct samples divides the mean by \(1-O(m)\).
Thus each end supplies a positive lag and a sign with distance at most \(0.041\) eventually.
The full-end coefficient mass gives \(\limsup\sqrt m\|y_\pm\|_2\le\sqrt2(x+\eta)<1/2\), also with the additional factor \(0.041\) when the designated difference is inserted.
Reflection preserves each signed-binomial distance.

The uniform crossing-block estimate bounds the scaled projected difference by \((0.041+\sqrt{0.041})/2\) on each end.
The other difference has norm at most two, and the product acts on the reconstruction error with norm at most four.
For an exact comparison, \(\sqrt{0.041}<0.2025\) and \(\sqrt{1.22\eta}<0.000111\) by squaring.
Thus the final scaled norm has limit superior at most \(4(0.000111)+2.44(0.041+0.2025)<0.595\).
In contrast, \(\log 2>0.69\) and \(\sqrt2<1.415\) give \(K>0.36\).
Coprimality holds for both binomials even when \(P\) is reducible or has repeated roots.
Their product times \(R\) is therefore indivisible by \(P\), so the unrestricted floor gives limit inferior greater than \(0.6\) for the same norm.
Every possible subsequential limit from a violating sequence is excluded, including \(L=K+\eta\).
