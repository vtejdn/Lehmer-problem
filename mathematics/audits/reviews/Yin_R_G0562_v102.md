## Simple nontorsion factors, reflected roots and the two-thirds degree range

At 2026-11-17T00:46 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the complete C345v102 submission fixed at 2026-11-17T00:41 UTC.
The reviewer had no development role in C345v102 and received the fixed proof without its development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on it.
The reviewer had contributed previously to the C345v90 input and independently reviewed C345v101.
Their uses here were checked against the actual statements.
All sections pass at their stated scope.

For \(s=A(0)\), the centered trigonometric polynomial \(U=e^{-id\theta/2}A(e^{i\theta})\) satisfies \(\bar U=sU\).
Since \(d\) is even, \(U\) is periodic, and a fixed scalar makes it real for either sign.
With \(h\) distinct root angles, their multiplicities \(m_j\) contribute \(d-h\) zeros to the derivative, counted with multiplicity.
Rolle's theorem on the \(h\) intervening open gaps contributes \(h\) further distinct zeros, including the final gap or the one gap in the case of a single distinct angle.
Angular zero orders agree with polynomial zero orders under \(X=e^{i\theta}\) and multiplication by nonvanishing angular factors.
Explicit differentiation gives \(U'=(i/2)e^{-id\theta/2}(2XA'-dA)\) there, and the polynomial in parentheses has leading coefficient \(d\).
Consequently all of its \(d\) roots are on the unit circle.
The centered monomial contributes zero to \(2XG'-dG\), so every conjugate of any repeated irreducible factor of \(G\) lies on the circle.
Monicity and nonzero constant term give the cyclotomic conclusion by Kronecker.
This proves squarefreeness of \(Q_0\) even with a reducible parent or repeated torsion roots, and includes both signed reversal types.
The empty \(Q_0\) has \(f=v=0\) and requires no energy estimate.

For each remaining root the reflection \(\alpha/R^2\) preserves its argument, is inside the closed disk and is \(1/\bar\alpha\) for \(|\alpha|>1\).
The unit constant gives \(\sum t_i=2v\) and \(\sum\log R_i=v\), irrespective of whether \(Q_0\) is reciprocal.
The regularized logarithm series is absolutely convergent, and summing its real parts over ordered pairs gives the nonnegative Fourier energy.
For two roots on the same side, the reflected mutual distance is \(|\alpha_i-\alpha_j|/(R_iR_j)\), which is at most \(|1-w_i\bar w_j|\) by the disk identity.
For opposite sides the latter quantity equals that normalized original distance.
Regularization costs at most \(\eta/2\) on each ordered off-diagonal pair because \(|1-\rho z|^2\ge\rho|1-z|^2\).
Their original distance product is the absolute discriminant of the squarefree monic \(Q_0\), hence at least one even across distinct irreducible factors.
The factors \(R_iR_j\) contribute \(2(f-1)v\).
Each diagonal term is at most \(\log(1/(1-\rho))\), and \(\eta=d^{-3}\) gives \(\log(1/(1-\rho))\le\log(2d^3)\).
Since \(f\le d\), the resulting estimate is \(O(f(v+\Delta))\).
The inequalities apply to roots on the unit circle and to coinciding reflected directions, since the nonzero discriminant concerns the original roots.

The negative boundary mass \(\mu(\mathbb T)\) and \(\log|a|\) are \(O(Y)\) by C345v90 and the zero mean of \(L\).
At exterior roots, reversal makes \(1/\alpha_i\) a root of \(A+saX^{d/2}\), and conjugation, using its real coefficients, gives the same equation at \(1/\bar\alpha_i=w_i\).
Its scalar modulus is unchanged.
At interior roots \(w_i=\alpha_i\) directly.
Thus the Poisson inequality uses the angle of \(w_i\) used in the energy, also for an anti-reciprocal parent.
Roots with \(t_i=0\) need no boundary evaluation.

For \(r=e^{-t}\) and \(b=e^{-(t+\tau)}\) the ratio \(P_r/P_b\) increases with \(\cos x\), since the derivative has sign \((r-b)(1-rb)>0\).
At zero angle its value is \(\tanh((t+\tau)/2)/\tanh(t/2)\le2\) when \(t\ge\tau>0\).
The smoothed Fourier sum uses \(e^{-\tau l}\sum_i w_i^l\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives (4) with complementary squared weight \(l e^{-(2\tau-\eta)l}\), whose sum is \(O(\tau^{-2})\) for \(\eta\le\tau\le1\).
The small \(t_i\) contribute at most \(f\tau\).
The remaining kernel sum can be enlarged to all roots after smoothing because the kernels are nonnegative.
Summing against \(\mu\), using \(v\le m(G)\) in the energy, establishes (5) with an absolute constant.

The balancing parameter satisfies \(\tau_0^2=Y\sqrt{Y+\Delta}/(d\sqrt f)\) for \(f>0\).
Since \(Y\ge1,\ f\le d\), its square is at least \(d^{-3/2}\), and therefore \(\tau_0\ge\eta\).
When \(\tau_0\le1\), the two variable terms in (5) both equal \(f\tau_0=\sqrt{Y/d}\,f^{3/4}(Y+\Delta)^{1/4}\).
When \(\tau_0>1\), splitting at one bounds the small displacements by \(f\le f\tau_0\) and the others by \(O(Y f/d)\), since their original Poisson kernels have bounded supremum.
This verifies (1) at all parameter values, with \(m(G)-m(F)=m(Q)\) by multiplicativity.

For \(f=o(d^{2/3}\Delta^{-1/3})\), the second error divided by \(Y\) tends uniformly to zero, since \((Y+\Delta)^{1/4}/\sqrt Y=O(\Delta^{1/4})\).
For \(f\le Kd^{2/3}\Delta^{-1/3}\) it is instead \(O_K(Y^{-1/4})\) by \(1+Y/\Delta\le2Y\).
In either range \(f/d\to0\).
At full scale \(Y\to\infty\) therefore gives \(m(F)/m(G)\to1\), which excludes unbounded \(Y\) on a bounded-\(m(F)\) sequence.
In the smaller range this additionally gives \(v\to0\), sufficient to transfer an available positive gap from the parent.
The bounded-degree case follows from finiteness of integral \(F\) of bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure, independently of the possible size of the scalar in \(G\).
A fixed power saving \(Kd^{2/3-\epsilon}\) meets the stated little-oh window.

At full scale with the interval parameters fixed, bounded \(m(F)\) first bounds the whole measure.
C345v90 then bounds \(\|L\|_1\) and \(\log|a|\), so C345v98 bounds the total absolute binomial exponent.
The all-roots binomial bound of C345v101 gives \(|\log|\alpha||=O(\Delta/d)\).
If \(Q_0\ne1\), any irreducible factor has degree \(g>1\) since a linear unit factor is cyclotomic.
The recorded house theorem supplies a root with \(g\log|\alpha|\ge(\log2)/4\), whereas \(g\Delta/d\le f\Delta/d=O_K(d^{-1/3}\Delta^{2/3})\to0\).
This proves eventual exact equality in the interval class even when \(\deg Q\) itself is large due to torsion factors.
Its parent gap now transfers at full scale.
The general full-scale relative conclusion requires growing whole measure, and the general little-oh assertion only gives vanishing cofactor measure on bounded sequences.
