## Independent finding

The obstruction excludes exactly \(3^\nu-3\) classes by this coefficient test, and every one of these classes occurs on arbitrarily large rows of the construction.
For \(0<v_3(c)<\nu-1\), the near-complete divisors and the strict sublinear excess in the full order occur on the same rows.
The finite-field test is necessary and sufficient for an individual prime-to-\(r\) factor to lift with the zero prefix.
The sharpness example concerns the input length for this local test.
Existence of factors of the required degree and exact order on an unbounded sequence of zero-boundary rows remains open.

### The obstruction and the finite-field test

Since \(3\mid n\) and \(u\) is a power of two, \(3\nmid tn+2u\).
More generally, when \(r\nmid N\), coprime Hensel factorization gives a unique lift of each monic factor of \(X^N-1\bmod r\).
Frobenius on the unramified roots sends each lifted root \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^r\), so \(S_{rj}=S_j\) over \(\mathbb Z_r\).
For reciprocal \(W\) with constant one the reversed product \(B=\prod(1-\alpha X)\) reduces to \(W\) at the prescribed precision.
Through degree \(L\), the ratio \(B/(1+cX^L)\) has all nonconstant coefficients in \(r^\nu\mathbb Z_r\), and taking the logarithm preserves that divisibility since \(k\nu-v_r(k)\ge\nu\).
Writing \(e=v_r(L)>0\), the coefficient at \(L/r\) forces \(v_r(S_{L/r})\ge\nu+e-1\).
At \(L\), \(-S_L/L\equiv c\bmod r^\nu\), which gives \(r^{\nu-1}\mid c\).
In the stated family \(L=3(3^{\nu-1}+1)/2\) and \(e=1\).
Only the three multiples of \(3^{\nu-1}\bmod 3^\nu\) survive this necessary test.

For the zero prefix, exponentiation gives the converse to the logarithm test because \(k\nu-v_r(k!)\ge\nu\) for \(k\ge1\).
For each \(m\le L\) prime to \(r\), the strongest condition on \(S_m\) comes from the largest index \(m r^k\le L\).
Its required precision is \(h_m=\nu+\lfloor\log_r(L/m)\rfloor\).
Möbius inversion on divisors of \(m\) preserves these conditions in both directions because \(h_e\ge h_m\) for \(e\mid m\), and \(m\) is a unit.
The resulting exponents \(b_m\) satisfy \(\sum_{m\mid j,\,r\nmid m}m b_m=S_j\) also for \(r\mid j\), by Frobenius invariance.
Thus the Euler product in v201 has the same logarithm as \(B\).
In each fixed degree integer approximants to \(b_m\) give its binomial expansion by continuity of the binomial coefficients on \(\mathbb Z_r\).
After reduction its base-\(r\) digits give the exponents at \(m r^k\) by Frobenius, uniquely since the first unassigned coefficient successively determines each exponent.
Consequently the digits with \(0\le k<h_m\) are precisely those that must vanish, and \(m r^{h_m-1}\le r^{\nu-1}L\).
Reciprocity, degree and exact order on a row impose the further orbit, constant-term, size and least-common-multiple conditions stated in v201.
Prime-to-\(r\) reduction preserves the order of each selected root.

### Arithmetic realization of the boundary classes

The \(3\)-part of the original half-degree modulus has valuation \(\nu(L-1)\), since \(1-p\) generates the units congruent to one modulo \(3^\nu\) up to precision \(3^{\nu L}\).
The target \(1-2p\) belongs to this group.
At primes dividing \(q\), the stronger endpoint modulus still imposes only powers of those same primes on \(D\).
The remaining rotation congruences exclude primes dividing \(pq\) and therefore impose no further \(3\)-part.
Squaring the \(3\)-adic endpoint yields \(q^{2D_0}\equiv K_0\bmod p^L\).
For the full period \(P\), the principal-unit valuation gives \(v_3(q^{2P}-1)=\nu L\).
Thus each step \(D\mapsto D+P\) changes \((K_0-q^{2D})/p^L\bmod p\) by a fixed unit, and the prefactor \(q^{-(2D-L)}\) is constant modulo \(p\).
This covers all classes once as \(D\) traverses one period modulo \(pP\).

The primes of \(pP\) are at most \(p\), so rounding \(S\) in the required residue class for \(tS\) moves it by at most \(pP/2\) from \(n/(2\theta)\).
In v196 the mixed-block count has a strict asymptotic margin, while its two coprime filling sizes cover the remainder for every such \(S\).
The filling therefore supplies an even-side word of the new degree with exact order \(tn\), and the real-period error stays bounded.
For \(d>2L\) the low coefficients through \(L\) can be set freely in a reciprocal word and reflected at the high end.
For the application of v198, I checked the coefficient gluing directly.
With \(C=\operatorname{lcm}(q,2^h)\), the pole discrepancy after gluing is divisible by \(Cq^L\) from the even word and the free coefficient positions, and by \(p^{L+1}\) from the boundary coefficient.
These factors are coprime.
The free reciprocal pole increments have gcd \((pq)^L\) by the center and the first free pair in v172, so a \(Cp\)-multiple of the free lattice corrects the pole exactly.
The approximation in v172 applies to this integral representative since \(\ell\nmid Cp\), and preserves the assigned words and the required real neighborhood.
It permits a positive exact denominator exponent at \(\ell\), so the dependence obstruction of v179 applies.

### The large divisor and the excess order

I checked the portions of v198 needed to obtain \(R,w,U,G\) for these rows.
For \(15t\,11^j-4u>0\), restricting \(j\) by multiplicative orders fixes its valuations at the finitely many primes below any given bound, with valuation zero at \(11\).
Growth on the progression forces a larger prime divisor, and iteration preserves those already obtained.
For the resulting \(Q=\prod\rho_i^{e_i+1}\), taking \(a\equiv-1\bmod\operatorname{ord}_Q(2)\) is compatible with the original progression.
The two floor residues for the maximal block exponents have positive logarithmic density there, with moduli \(b_1=\operatorname{ord}_Q(11)\) and \(b_2=\operatorname{ord}_Q(7)\).
Indeed a nonconstant character has nonzero frequency \(\lambda=c_1/(b_1\log 11)+c_2/(b_2\log 7)\) by multiplicative independence.
Writing the combined progression as \(a=Hm-1\), the harmonic sum of that character is bounded by summation by parts at a nonintegral linear frequency.
At integral linear frequency, the remaining sum differs by a bounded amount from the integral of a constant times \(y^{-1-4\pi i\lambda}\), also bounded.
After harmonic normalization these bounds give equidistribution and hence the prescribed floor residues on arbitrarily large rows.
The congruence \(2N\equiv4u-15t\,11^{j_0}\bmod Q\) gives the exact valuations, so \(N=Rw\) with \(\gcd(R,w)=1\).
The refinement of \(D\) for \(c\) concerns only the subsequent choice of \(S\).

At \(p=3^\nu\) the subgroup generated by \(3,-1\bmod R\) has image of size at most four modulo the squares, while the full unit group has \(2^{k_0}\) square classes.
Thus the orbit sizes are at most \(g\le4R/2^{k_0}\).
The primitive orbit fits for large rows since \(d/w\to R/\theta\), and at most one orbit's size is lost upon stopping at the degree budget.
With the fixed reserve \(K\) of v198 this gives \(2L<K\le\Delta<K+wg<\eta d\).
Inverse-pairing gives even degree, and all selected factors lift since \(3\nmid R\).
The primitive orbit gives exact order \(R\) before substitution, and the free basis over the quotient in \(X^w\) gives exact order \(wR=N\) after substitution.
For the filler, \(\Delta/2=Hx+L_3y\) in the notation of v198 is soluble with \(0\le y<H\) since \(\gcd(H,L_3)=1\) and \(\Delta/2\ge H L_3\).
The displayed powers of \(X^H-1\) and \(X-1\) divide \((X^N-1)^\Delta\) in product since \(H\mid N\) and their exponents sum to at most \(\Delta\).
Their zero prefix follows from \(H>L\) and \(v_3\binom{2L_3}{j}\ge v_3(L_3)-v_3(j)\ge\nu\) for \(1\le j\le L\).

For \(3\mid c\) the modification of the two distinct filler coefficients preserves its reduction modulo three.
Since \(w>L\), multiplying by \(U\) gives exactly the required coefficient at \(L\).
In characteristic three, \(UG'\mid(X^N-1)^{\Delta+1}\mid X^{N3^a}-1\) for \(3^a\ge\Delta+1\).
Lifting this congruence in the full quotient costs at most another \(3^{\nu-1}\) in the exponent, by cubing \(1+3^i x\) at each precision \(i\ge1\).
The order is a multiple of \(N\) by \(U\) and hence equals \(N3^b\) with \(3^b\le3^\nu(\Delta+1)\).
The boundary obstruction excludes \(b=0\) throughout the stated range of \(c\).
First taking large degrees at fixed \(R\), then letting \(\eta\) tend to zero, proves the strict sublinear excess.
This checks the realization and order inputs used from the candidate v198, independently of its other conclusions about small divisors or prime cofactors.

### Sharpness and its scope

Write \(L=m r^e\) and \(H=r^{\nu+e-1}\).
Each root of \(E_{a_i}(X^m)\) has \(m\)-th power of exact order \(a_i\).
The factors are simple and pairwise disjoint, reciprocal with constant one, and all divide the same \(X^{m\prod_i a_i}-1\) in characteristic zero and at \(r\).
Since \(a_i>mH\), through degree \(mH\) the product agrees with \((1-X^m)^{-H}\).
Modulo \(r\) the latter equals \((1-X^{mH})^{-1}\), whose coefficient at \(mH\) is one.
In degree \(mj\) for \(1\le j\le r^e\), the binomial coefficient has valuation \(v_r(H)-v_r(j)\), because each factor \((H+i)/i\) for \(1\le i<j\) is congruent to one modulo \(r\).
This valuation first reaches \(\nu-1\) at \(j=r^e\), including \(e=0\) when the product over \(i\) is empty.
Hence the factor and \(1\) agree through residue degree \(r^{\nu-1}L-1\) and have different outcomes for the lifted zero prefix.
They share an ambient torsion polynomial, and their degrees and exact orders differ.
The example establishes the general cutoff for tests reading the residue prefix alone and the attainability of valuation \(\nu-1\) locally when \(r\mid L\).
It does not supply a degree-\(d\) exact-order-\(N\) factor on a prescribed trace row.
