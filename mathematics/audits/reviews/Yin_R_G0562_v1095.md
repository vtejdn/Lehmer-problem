## Irreducibility, strict dominance and the two limiting moments

At 2026-11-24T21:22 an agent outside the development of this proof independently checked the four fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and its scope without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on this proof.

The trace identity gives \(T_{3^j}=T_3^{\circ j}\) with \(T_3(U)=U^3-3U\).
Thus \(R_n\) is monic, is \(U^n\) modulo three, and has constant term \(-15\) because \(n\) is odd.
Eisenstein gives degree \(n\) for a root \(t\).
The product of \(t_i^2-4\) over its conjugates is \(R_n(2)R_n(-2)\).
Since \(T_n(2)=2\) and \(T_n(-2)=-2\), the respective evaluations are \(-19\) and \(-11\), giving norm \(209=11\cdot19\).
It is neither a rational square nor three times a rational square, as its valuation at eleven is odd.
Consequently \(t^2-4\) is nonsquare in \(H=\mathbb Q(t)\).
A root of \(X^2-tX+1\) recovers \(t\) by taking the sum with its inverse, so its degree over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(2n\) and its minimum is \(X^nR_n(X+X^{-1})=P_n(X)\).
This minimum is monic reciprocal with constant coefficient one.
Odd degree excludes \(\sqrt3\in H\).
If the quadratic \(K/H\) contained \(\sqrt3\), the equality \(H(\sqrt{t^2-4})=H(\sqrt3)\) would give \(t^2-4=3v^2\) for some \(v\in H\).
Its norm would lie in \(3(\mathbb Q^\times)^2\) since \(n\) is odd, contradicting 209.

For \(A=2\cosh x\), \(T_n'(A)=n\sinh(nx)/\sinh x\ge n^2\) for \(x>0\), with limit \(n^2\) at zero.
Here \(n\ge3\), so \(T_n(A)-15-3A\) is strictly increasing on \([2,\infty)\).
It is negative at \(2\) and positive at \(4\), since \(T_n(4)\ge T_3(4)=52>27\).
Its unique zero \(A_*\in(2,4)\) gives a positive root \(r_*>1\) with \(r_*+r_*^{-1}=A_*\).
For a general root \(z\), put \(r=|z|,\ A=r+r^{-1}\).
The two focal distances of \(W=z^n+z^{-n}\) have sum \(2(r^n+r^{-n})=2T_n(A)\), by \(|v+v^{-1}\pm2|=|v\pm1|^2/|v|\).
Since \(W\pm2=(15\pm2)+3z+3z^{-1}\), their half-sum is at most \(15+3A\).
Equality in either triangle inequality requires \(z,z^{-1}\) to have argument zero, because the constant summand is strictly positive.
Thus \(A\le A_*\), which gives \(r\le r_*\), and a root of modulus \(r_*\) must equal \(r_*\) itself.
An embedding fixing \(r_*^k\) fixes \(r_*\) for every \(k\ge1\), so separability gives \(\mathbb Q(r_*^k)=K\).
In particular no two conjugates can coalesce under any positive power.
Also \(|W|\ge15-3A>3\) and \(|W|\le T_n(A)\le15+3A<27\).
The identity \(T_n(A)=2\cosh(n|\log r|)\) proves both radial bounds, excluding all circle roots.

With \(K\) in its positive real embedding, \(K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\) lies in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
The norm argument excludes the nontrivial subfield, so the two inverters agree on the rational intersection.
The twelfth power of the twist generates \(K\), and division by the original generator then recovers \(\zeta_{12}\).
The compatible twist therefore generates the unramified quadratic norm-one field of rational degree \(4(2n)=8n\) and has total logarithmic measure \(4m(\alpha_n)\).
Each of the \(2n\) off-circle inputs has four lifts of unchanged modulus.
Each nonreal trace-embedding pair accounts for four lifted embeddings, giving \(r_2(F_n)=2n\).
In the definition of \(D_n\) each pair contributes \(4\sinh^2 b\) for the common absolute radial log \(|b|\).
Summing over all \(8n\) embeddings once instead gives \(D_n=4\sum_{P_n(z)=0}\sinh^2 b_z\).
The property that every positive power generates the input field concerns \(\alpha_n\), before twisting.

For fixed \(k\ge1\) and \(n>k+1\), the top \(k\) nonleading coefficients of \(P_n\) vanish, so the \(k\)-th root power sum is zero.
The difference between an angular \(k\)-th power and the corresponding root power is at most \(e^{k\operatorname{arcosh}(27/2)/n}-1\).
All fixed nonconstant angular moments thus tend to zero after normalization by \(2n\), including the negative ones by conjugation.
This implies weak convergence of the angular distributions to normalized arc length.
Uniformly for \(\theta=\arg z\), \(z+z^{-1}=2\cosh b_z\cos\theta+2i\sinh b_z\sin\theta=2\cos\theta+O(1/n)\).
For \(W\) approaching \(s=15+6\cos\theta\in[9,21]\), the quantity \((|W-2|+|W+2|)/4\) approaches \(s/2\) uniformly.
It equals \(\cosh(n|b_z|)\), so taking \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) on a compact interval away from one gives \(n|b_z|-g(\theta)\to0\) uniformly.
The original unit has \(m(\alpha_n)=\tfrac12\sum_z|b_z|\), the empirical mean of \(n|b_z|\), proving \(m(\beta_n)\to4\mu_1\).
The uniform bound \(|b_z|=O(1/n)\) gives \(n^2\sinh^2 b_z-(n b_z)^2\to0\) uniformly.
Consequently \(nD_n\) is eight times an empirical mean tending to \(\mu_2\).
This yields \(D_n\to0\) and \(r_2(F_n)D_n-m(\beta_n)^2\to16(\mu_2-\mu_1^2)>0\), with strictness from the nonconstant continuous \(g\).
