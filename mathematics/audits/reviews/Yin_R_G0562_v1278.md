## Findings

The arguments establish the six stated results with the indicated power-basis norms and total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
The finite-time recoveries concern an observable relative to \(m\) and leave the existence of a degree-independent positive lower bound open.

**Periodic data.** Writing \(D=A^j-I\), the fixed subgroup is \(D^{-1}\mathbb Z^d/\mathbb Z^d\), since irreducibility and nontorsion exclude every root of unity from the spectrum.
Its annihilator is \(D^\mathsf T\mathbb Z^d\), of index \(N_j\).
The cube with entries \(0,\ldots,\lfloor N_j^{1/d}\rfloor\) contains strictly more than \(N_j\) points, including when \(N_j\) is a perfect \(d\)-th power.
Every nonzero difference in the annihilator represents \((\alpha^j-1)b\) with \(b\) a nonzero algebraic integer.
Its absolute norm is at least \(N_j\), while each embedding is bounded by \(d\|k\|_\infty\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^{d-1}\).
The resulting two bounds squeeze \(j^{-1}\log r_j\) to \(m/d\) for fixed \(P\), using the periodic-point limit that includes nontorsion unit-circle eigenvalues. [Lind, §4, p. 55](../literature/Lin82/readme.md)
The exceptional inequality printed after Lind's limit has its sign reversed.
For a fixed algebraic unit-circle eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of infinite order, the logarithmic-form bound gives \(-\log|1-\lambda^j|\le C_\lambda\log(2j)\), so \(|1-\lambda^j|<e^{-\epsilon j}\) can hold only finitely often for each \(\epsilon>0\). [Unit-circle specialization](../literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle)

**Recovery in density.** The determinant is the full cyclic resultant in absolute value, so part 3 of the cited resultant estimate applies for \(Y=\lceil d(\log(2d))^4\rceil\).
The additional relative error is at most \((d\log d+(d-1)m)/(Ym)\).
Its first term is \(O((\log\log d)^{-3})\) by the lower bound for \(m\) in degree \(d\), and its second term is at most \(d/Y\).
A majority therefore lies in any prescribed relative neighborhood of \(m\) for all sufficiently large degrees, uniformly in the polynomial.
Bounded degree and bounded measure give finitely many monic polynomials by the bounds on their elementary symmetric coefficients.
For integer inputs of measure strictly between one and two, multiplicativity and the leading and nonzero constant coefficient bounds reduce any positive measure to at least that of one nontorsion monic unit factor after removing zero roots.
These observations give both directions of the claimed gap equivalence.

**Short intervals.** For \(1\le j<d\), \(X^j-1\) gives an admissible vector of norm one without reduction in the basis.
At \(Y=d\), part 1 of the resultant estimate applies to the upper tail uniformly.
On the cubic Salem sequence it gives \(r_j\le\rho^2+o(1)\) uniformly outside \(o(d)\) orders, and \(\rho^2<16/9<2\).
For the integer family, taking \(r\equiv1\bmod\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,H)\) makes \(\zeta^d=1\) for every \(j\)-th root with \(j\le H\).
The numerator \((\zeta-1)F_d(\zeta)\) then equals \(3(\zeta-1)\), and \(F_d(1)=3-d\) separately.
Taking the absolute product over all \(j\)-th roots gives \((d-3)3^{j-1}\), including \(j=1\).
Dirichlet's theorem permits arbitrarily large such primes for each fixed \(H\).

**Gaussian orbit energies.** Each nonconstant character maps to a distinct character with its accumulated scalar multiplier.
Unimodularity shifts the index interval \(1,\ldots,n\) to \(0,\ldots,n-1\) in the minimum.
Every nonzero lattice vector has squared norm at least one.
The orbit of the first basis vector attains equality for \(d\) terms, whereas every orbit consisting of signed basis vectors reaches the last coordinate within \(d\) steps.
Its next image contains the nonzero unit constant coefficient and an intermediate coefficient, since a monic binomial with unit constant coefficient has only roots of unity.
This gives the strict inequality at \(d+1\) and the stated first strict threshold whenever \(d\ge\lfloor1/\varepsilon\rfloor+1\).
For the small-noise limit at fixed \(P\), separability gives diagonalizability, nontorsion gives ergodicity, and the only irreducible block has entropy per dimension \(m/d\).
The Gaussian multiplier uses the source's parameter \(\alpha=1\). [Fannjiang and Wołowski, (17)–(20) and Theorem 3, pp. 346–348](../literature/FW03/readme.md)

**A deterministic time.** Conjugate embeddings occur in pairs with equal weights, so the weighted Gram matrix is real positive definite with determinant \(\Delta^2\prod_iw_i\), where \(\Delta=|\det V|\).
For each nonzero lattice vector the field norm and arithmetic-geometric mean bound its embedding energy below by \(d(\prod_iw_i)^{1/d}\).
For the upper bound the ball of radius \(\sqrt d\) contains the cube \([-1,1]^d\).
The associated ellipsoid volume and Minkowski's theorem, taking a limit from larger radii, give minimum embedding energy at most \(d\Delta^{2/d}(\prod_iw_i)^{1/d}\).
Passing back to the power basis divides the lower bound by \(\|V\|_2^2\) and multiplies the upper bound by \(\|V^{-1}\|_2^2\).
The cofactor estimate uses each selected root once, producing \(M^{d-1}\) before squaring.
The remaining determinant factor in the upper bound is \(\Delta^{2/d-2}\le1\) by integrality of the nonzero discriminant.
Bounding each geometric sum between \(\max(1,|\alpha_i|^{2(n-1)})\) and \(n\) times that number gives precisely the two logarithmic errors in the claim.
Upon division by \(2(T-1)m/d\), the \(d\log d\) contribution tends to zero by the same degree-dependent lower bound, the \(dm\) contribution is \(O((\log d)^{-4})\), and \(\log T=O(\log d)\) is smaller still.

**Substitution.** Each step permutes the residue classes and applies \(B\) on a wrap, so distinct starting classes remain orthogonal at every time.
For class \(a\), the exponent of \(B\) at time \(i\) is \(\lfloor(i+a)/k\rfloor\).
Counting its occurrences for \(0\le i<ks\) gives the two sums in the claim, each bounded below by \(\kappa_s(Q)\) for nonzero \(u\) since \(Bu\ne0\).
Class zero attains equality.
At time \(ks\) the annihilator is the direct sum of \(k\) copies of the \(s\)-step annihilator for \(Q\), giving both its shortest sup norm and its index.
In the example the even degree and constant coefficient \(-1\) give norm \(-1\) at each successful irreducibility step.
A square would have nonnegative rational norm, so the positive square root gives a quadratic extension and, over \(\mathbb Q\), the full doubled degree.
Each exterior root of \(Q\) contributes exactly its original modulus to the product over its \(k\)-th roots.
