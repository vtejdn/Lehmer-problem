## The Hermite direction, its normalized diagonal and the cofactor bound

On 2026-11-18T15:10 a separately assigned agent for Yin checked all five sections of [L462 v186](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v186.md#local-denominator) and their inputs at the scopes used.
The reviewer had no role in developing the argument and received its fixed text without development discussion or earlier findings about it.
The local formula, quartic example, normalization, derivative criterion and uniform subfamily bound all hold under their stated hypotheses.

The substitution map modulo \(Q^2,P^2\) has two free layers of rank \(d\).
Its determinants in absolute value are \(I\) and \(I|\operatorname N(p\alpha^{p-1}Q'(\beta)/P'(\alpha))|=p^d I^3\), since the discriminant quotient is \(I^2\) and \(\alpha\) is a unit.
Hence the rational preimage \(B\) of \(1-x^K\) is integral locally at \(\ell\nmid pI\).
Evaluating and differentiating the congruence gives \(B(\beta)=1-\alpha^K\) and \(p\beta B'(\beta)=-K\alpha^K\).
Subtracting from the prescribed jet gives \(T-B=QC\) with \(2p\beta Q'(\beta)C(\beta)=K(1+\alpha^K)\).
Monic division by \(Q\) preserves local coefficient integrality, and the power basis identifies integrality of the degree-\(<d\) representative with membership in \(O'_\ell\).
Here \(\ell\nmid I\) puts \(\alpha\) in that order and \(\ell\nmid 2p\) makes \(2p\beta\) a unit.
Thus \(\ell^j\) clears \(T\) if and only if it annihilates the indicated class, so the least clearing power equals its additive order, including order one when the class vanishes.
A prime outside the discriminant makes \(Q'(\beta)\) a unit by its norm.

For the quartic, one trace embedding sends \(t^2-4\) to a negative real number, so adjoining a root of \(x^2-tx+1\) doubles the quadratic trace degree.
There is one exterior conjugate, whose powered value is unique in modulus outside the circle for every positive exponent.
The equal embedding multiplicities then imply degree preservation.
At three, the reductions \(i,-i,-1\) have orders \(4,4,2\), and powering by any prime \(p\ge5\) preserves their distinctness.
For the one colliding residue pair the divided difference of powers is a unit by its reduction \(p(-1)^{p-1}\), and all other divided differences are units by distinctness.
The product formula for the index proves \(3\nmid I\).

The two Hensel lifts of the traces give coprime quadratic factors over \(\mathbb Z_3\).
At the lift \(t\equiv1\), factoring \(f(-2)=3\) gives \(v_3(t+2)=1\), while \(t-2\) is a unit.
The order \(\mathbb Z_3[\sqrt{t^2-4}]\) is maximal since the valuations of the two summands in its rational power basis lie in distinct classes modulo one.
In this component \(a^p-a^{-p}\) has valuation \(1/2\), and the other factors of \(Q'(a^p)\) are units.
In the complementary component the derivative is a unit.
For the indicated \(r=2\) or \(4\), one has \(r<p\), \(2\mid K\), and \(3\nmid K\) in both congruence classes, including \(p=5\).
Consequently \(K(1+a^K)\) is a unit in the ramified component and its class modulo \(Q'(a^p)\) has additive order three.
This gives exactly one factor of three in the coefficient denominator for every stated prime.

For the primitive integral row \(A=cQ^2+tT_0\), its leading coefficient is \(c\) and \((c,t)=1\), so primitivity of \(T_0\) forces content one.
Disjoint residue classes preserve that content on the diagonal.
When \(A(0)\ne0\) the least positive denominator after normalization is therefore \(|A(0)|\), and choosing \(t=\ell,\ c\equiv1\bmod\ell\) makes it prime to \(\ell\).
Only finitely many parameters can give a common row root because it must be a zero of \(T-T^*\), which is nonzero at each powered mark.
The rational parameters tending to zero are nonzero, so their reduced denominators \(c\) diverge and \(A(0)/c\to1\).
The normalized diagonal tends to \(Q(x^p)^2(1+x^r)\) in fixed monic degree, whose log measure is \(2ph\).
Multiplication by \(A(0)\) and removal of exactly two copies of \(P\) yield the term \(\log|A(0)|+(2p-2)h\) with the claimed fixed-input error.

Writing \(u=\sigma\alpha^J\), the value equation is \(A(\beta)=-u\,\iota A(\beta)\).
Subtracting \(J/2\) times this diagonal value from the Euler derivative gives \(p(Y-u\,\iota Y)\), where \(Y=\beta A'(\beta)-J A(\beta)/(2p)\).
The ratios in (3) are invariant exactly under these two equations, since \(\iota u=u^{-1}\) and \(u\ne\pm1\).
Degree preservation gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and values and derivatives there determine a unique rational class modulo \(Q^2\) by separability.
This verifies the scope of the freedom in (3) and \(b=1,z=0\) for the prescribed direction.

With \(z=0\), the integral \(W=2pXA'-JA\) vanishes at \(\beta\) and has nonzero constant term, so \(m(W)\ge m(Q)=ph\).
The root sum for \(A'/A\) and the uniform circle integral of \(|x-y|^{-1/2}\) give \(m(W)-m(A)\le E\), where \(E=\log(J+2p)+2\log(1+C_0n)\).
Reversal gives \(I(F)=m(A)\).
Applying the coprime-row comparison at span \(J\) and removing \(P^2\) from the primitive diagonal gives
\[
 \frac{m(R)}{ph}\ \ge\
 1-\frac6p-\left(1-\frac4p\right)\frac{E}{ph}
      -\frac{2\log(CJ)}{p^2h}.
\]
For \(p\in(y,2y]\) with \(y=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil\), and \(J\le p(16d+1)\), both \(E\) and \(\log(CJ)\) are \(O(\log(3d))\).
Voutier's theorem on p. 3 of the [author's edition](../literature/Vou96/readme.md#edition) gives \(h>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) in the total logarithmic measure convention.
I checked this source statement and accept it at that scope.
It gives \(ph/\log(3d)\to\infty\) uniformly, proving the \(1-o(1)\) lower bound and its consequence for a fixed saving \(\delta\).
The denominator identity concerns the specified jet, and the uniform lower bound applies to the centered-derivative subfamily.
