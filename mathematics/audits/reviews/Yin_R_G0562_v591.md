## Vanishing of the same normalized blocks decides all four contact orders

The stated compression, including the parity option and the effective uniform cutoff, holds under the given hypotheses.
The decisive point is to differentiate the normalized blocks after their lower derivatives have been shown to vanish.
This makes the derivative bound independent of the distances through which the blocks move.

For the cited gap principle I checked the unit calculation in the [earlier local argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) and the product formula in the proof of Lenstra's Proposition 2.3 on printed pages 270–271.
Lenstra bounds the height of the root at a surviving cut by estimating the two polynomial pieces at different places.
In the present integral specialization, suppose the separated sum \(L+X^tR\) vanishes at \(\alpha\), with \(\deg L\le u<t\).
Monicity makes \(R(\alpha)\) integral, so if it is nonzero the absolute value of the product of its conjugates is at least one.
For \(|z|>1\) the equation gives \(|R(z)|\le B|z|^{u-t}\), and for \(|z|\le1\) its own coefficients give \(|R(z)|\le B\).
Counting complex embeddings individually, the product of exterior moduli is \(M(P)\), hence the product bound contradicts \((t-u)\log M(P)>d\log B\).
This verifies the local extension to nonunits, since the high part has been divided by its initial monomial in the polynomial ring before evaluating its norm. [Lenstra, Proposition 2.3](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1999a/art.pdf#page=4)

There are at most \(2K\) reflected positions, so a block joined by differences below \(D\) has span at most \((2K-1)(D-1)\).
The exponent shift by one on \(XV\) costs at most one at a cut and adds at most one to each local degree.
Even for \(n=1\), coincidences within a block can only reduce its norm, and the gap \(nD-1\ge1\) keeps successive envelopes disjoint.
For each of the four choices of \(W\) the sum of local norms is therefore at most \(2H\).

For \(0\le k<a\), assume every block of a multiple of \(P_i^k\) has multiplicity at least \(k\) and take \(P_i^{k+1}\mid W\).
The initial case \(k=0\) imposes no constraint.
In \((E+nl_q)^k B_q(\alpha)\) every term of derivative order below \(k\) vanishes by the induction hypothesis.
Thus \(E^kW(\alpha)=0\) gives a zero of the sum of the shifted \(E^kB_q\).
For \(k>0\) this local differentiation multiplies a coefficient at relative degree \(e\) by \(e^k\), and for \(k=0\) the operator is the identity.
The total norm is bounded by \(2H(1+2nKD)^k\), within the strict threshold (1) at each cut and each \(P_i\).
Applying the cut to the whole sum at every boundary kills all prefix evaluations.
Differences of consecutive prefixes, including the empty and full sums, give \(\alpha^{nl_q}E^kB_q(\alpha)=0\) for every block.
This also covers a single block and identically zero pieces.
Since \(\alpha\ne0\), the leading ordinary derivative in \(E^k\) has nonzero multiplier \(\alpha^k\).
Separability and monic division over the integers give \(P_i^{k+1}\mid B_q\).
Conversely any power dividing every block divides their translated sum, proving both directions up to and including the cap.

Write the ordered reflected positions as \(s_1<\cdots<s_m\).
Then \(s_j+s_{m+1-j}=b\), so removing the two margins costs \(2s_1\) and the interior gaps occur in equal reflected pairs, apart from a possible central gap.
Capping gaps at \(D\) leaves an increasing map with complementary image positions summing to the new width.
Thus forward and reversed terms, with their original signs and the same possible shift by one, use precisely their old local polynomials.
The cap leaves every old cut at distance \(D\) or more, so the divisibility equivalence just proved applies again after translation.
The new width is the sum of at most \(2K-1\) positive differences bounded by \(D\), each at most its original value.
For a singleton the new width is zero and the original width is even.
In the general parity case only the central gap can change parity, and if capping changes its parity then the old gap is at least \(D+1\).
Replacing that single cap by \(D+1\) restores parity within both asserted bounds.
The strict order preserves every coefficient, its position in the list, the norm and the content.
Since \(a\ge1\), the four multiplicity comparisons detect both numerator zeros, using \(V(c)=c^{nb}A(c^{-n})\), and all prescribed signed divisibilities.
This gives the same genuine-contact conditions for reciprocal unit minima, with no relation required among roots.
Any upper constraint on a quantity nondecreasing in width for the fixed row data survives compression.

For fixed data, linear growth in \(D\) eventually dominates the logarithm in (1).
Under \(\log H\le Cn\), integrality gives \(K\le H\), whence \(\log(1+2nKD)\le\log(3D)+\log n+Cn\le n(\log(3D)+1+C)\).
Likewise \(\log(2H)\le n(C+\log2)\) and \(n(D-1)\le nD-1\), proving that (4) suffices also for \(a=1\).
For each \(P_i\), the hypothesis \(M(P_i)>1\) guarantees an exterior root, and root isolation gives a rational number strictly between one and the modulus of that root.
A positive rational lower bound for the logarithm of this number divided by \(\deg P_i\) bounds \(\log M(P_i)/\deg P_i\) from below.
Taking the minimum over the finite list gives the needed effective input.
Strict inequality can be certified at all sufficiently large trial widths, for instance by comparing the two sides with absolute errors bounded by one.
With \(K\) fixed the compressed support sets form a finite collection independent of \(n\).
