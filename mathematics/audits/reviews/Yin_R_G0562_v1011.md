## The simultaneous degree bound, individual measure bound and sparse field identity hold

At 2026-11-21T15:22 UTC a separate agent acting for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the fixed mathematical statement, the sparse corollary and all three general proof sections of C0345v863.
The reviewer had independently reviewed the plane fixed-exponent and radical-transfer inputs and took no part in developing the higher-dimensional claim.

In characteristic zero the normalized geometric Laurent factors form a single Galois orbit with identical support and no repetitions.
Minkowski addition thus gives full dimension for each component polytope and divides every width by exactly the number of components.
A constant nontrivial character on a component would put a hypersurface in an equal-dimensional component of a character fiber, forcing a binomial equation.
On a normal projective model, trivial divisor implies constant, and divisors of characters and their divisible classes occur in the finite free divisor group supported at the coordinate zeros and poles.
Intersecting the rational character plane with that group gives the stated finite saturation index even in dimension above one.
For scalar pullbacks finite étaleness gives only components dominating the original hypersurface, so the component count is the number of translation orbits on the simultaneous generic radicals.
The character group of the quotient by the radical Galois group injects into the saturation quotient: taking the power of a representative that maps to the identity forces all exponent residues zero by independence modulo constants.
This verifies the bound over the algebraic closure, including geometric components defined only after extending the rational field.

Primitivity permits a unimodular change for arbitrary signs and zero entries.
In the new coordinates the equation localized at the generic quotient point remains irreducible in the fiber variable and has positive width, giving a dominant projection of the stated degree.
Adjoining the base-coordinate radicals preserves the total degree by base change, and the power in the fiber variable multiplies it by the test dilation, since all generic roots are distinct and nonzero.
Deck translations give the same individual degrees as they act transitively on components and induce automorphisms of the base.
The scalar pullback surjects finitely onto the diagonal pullback, hence the component counts are bounded by the same lattice index.
Maximizing this bounded integer along scalar powers on the *whole* quotient torus gives irreducibility of each component after every such pullback: components over distinct original generic points cannot merge and a split increases the total.
These are exactly the multiplication maps in condition (PB) of Zannier's Theorem 2.1, including on bases of higher dimension.

The loss of fibers from normalization, intersections, zero extreme coefficients and ramification is contained in a proper closed subset of the base, by generic finiteness, generic separability and removal of the bad denominators.
Bilu's finite maximal torsion coset theorem bounds the torsion points of each such locus by a finite union of proper torsion cosets; it does not require the whole boundary to be such a union.
Isogenies map these cosets to proper torsion cosets, so excluding their images controls *all* lifts of the parameter.
Specialization over the cyclotomic closure of a field defining the components gives a lower degree bound over the smaller rational cyclotomic closure.
The fiber coordinate differs from the original root by a root of unity and the remaining coordinates already lie in that closure.
All extra cosets contained in the original hypersurface have dimension at most \(r-2\), hence their images in the quotient of dimension \(r-1\) are proper as well.
These exclusions leave the full primitive width and remove torsion roots at every dilation.

Uniformity in the dilation only needs a relative height floor for bounded degree at this fixed direction.
For a nonidentically zero primitive section, the local polynomial root bound gives a coefficient sum bound at archimedean places and coefficient maximum at most one at finite places; the actual leading coefficient cancels by the product formula.
Together with the relative height bound over the abelian closure and \([H(\beta):H]\le W_k\), this bounds the positive exponents of radicals already in \(H(\beta)\), uniformly over the torsion twists.
The cyclic radical subgroup calculation makes every admissible divisor divide the largest one, so the single least-common-multiple dilation simultaneously minimizes the normalized relative degree.
The exceptions in this construction are finite in number of cosets for each fixed direction, without requiring a uniform bound on that number or on the dilation used in the proof.

Every defining character of a torsion coset containing a point \(\xi\beta^k\) with nontorsion \(\beta\) must pair to zero with \(k\).
Such a coset contains the whole parameterized section if it contains this point, impossible on a nonidentically zero section when the coset lies inside the hypersurface.
Amoroso and Viada's Theorem 1.1 indeed gives a pointwise projective height floor for every algebraic point of the remaining locus of a proper rationally irreducible variety.
I checked its statement in the published edition, page 357, including its absolute height convention, degree parameter and the power of the logarithmic factor.
The exact height here is \(R_k h(\beta)\), because the positive and negative logarithms have equal total mass and the torsion phases have absolute value one at every place.
Both widths are positive on the real unit sphere and are homogeneous, so their ratio has a strictly positive minimum independent of the primitive direction.
For \(L=\mathbb Q(z)\), the identity \([HL:H]=[L:H\cap L]\) uses that the cyclotomic extension is Galois: in a finite normal overfield the subgroup defining a cyclotomic field is normal, and its product with the subgroup fixing \(L\) fixes the intersection.
One can take a sufficiently large finite cyclotomic subfield containing the intersection and the coefficients of the relative minimal polynomial.
Multiplying the exact degree-height identity by the resulting lower bounds yields the claimed \(d_Na_F/(cI)\), uniform in direction and twist.

For the sparse hyperplane each coordinate-zero divisor is a distinct reduced prime divisor in the affine chart of the projective hyperplane, with coordinate orders the corresponding unit vector, also for a line when \(r=2\).
Evaluating a potential divisible character at these divisors forces its rational exponent vector to be integral, yielding index one.
The width equals \(R_k\), so the relative degree is the full polynomial degree, and distinct exponents prevent cancellation and allow recovery of the ratio of every phased coefficient to the zeroth one regardless of which term was made monic.
The coefficient field is exactly the field generated by all phase ratios; their orders have least common multiple \(w\), giving \(A=\mathbb Q(\mu_w)\).
Over \(J=\mathbb Q(z)\cap H\) the minimal polynomial of \(z\) has the already attained degree over \(H\), hence equals the monic sparse polynomial and puts \(A\) in \(J\).
The defining equation then bounds \([\mathbb Q(z):A]\) above by \([\mathbb Q(z):J]\), forcing equality and \(A=J\).
The product over the coefficient embeddings is monic rational of the resulting absolute degree and vanishes at the given root, proving the norm identity even without integrality of the normalized coefficients.
The field identity uses the joint order of the ratios; a minimal conductor equal to that integer is unnecessary, since different orders can define the same cyclotomic field.
For the explicit constant the projective degree parameter is one, ambient dimension is \(r\), variety dimension is \(r-1\), and the width ratio is one, producing exactly \((935r^5\log(r^2))^{-r(r+1)^2}\).
