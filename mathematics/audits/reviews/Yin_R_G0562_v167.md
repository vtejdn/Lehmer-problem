## Whole-proof finding

The logarithmic lower bound holds for each positive-measure irreducible factor of every nonzero specialization.
The uniform bounds hold when the leading coefficient has no circle zero or when the number of exterior roots is bounded independently of \(n\).
The latter case includes all Salem factors of the fixed family.
The localization holds for every conjugate of maximal modulus and yields the stated exponential lower bound on the number of exterior roots along a shrinking sequence.
Both binary lifts satisfy the first uniformity condition.

### Degree and the two external bounds

The root formula bounds \(M(A)\) below by both endpoint coefficients in absolute value, including a constant prime factor.
After changing the overall sign, any positive-measure factor below \(\log 2\) in logarithmic measure is therefore monic with constant coefficient \(\pm1\).
An irreducible factor with zero constant coefficient is associated to \(X\), and the only monic linear units are \(X\pm1\).
Thus the small positive measures occur only among noncyclotomic irreducible units of degree \(a>1\).

For \(n>w\), the exponent intervals \([nj,nj+w]\) of distinct \(Y\)-degrees are disjoint.
Consequently \(F_n\) has exactly \(s\) nonzero terms and degree \(D\le (R+1)n\).
A single monomial has no nonconstant unit factor.
For \(s>1\), Dobrowolski's Lemma 1 on p. 203 requires that \(g\) be monic, have nonzero constant term and have no cyclotomic divisor.
The multiple \(f\) need only be integral with \(s>1\) terms, so \(g=A,\ f=F_n\) meets the hypotheses even if \(F_n(0)=0\), has content, or has other positive-measure factors.
With \(u=0.31a/(s!D)\le 0.31\), the inequalities \(\log(1+u)\ge u/(1+u)\) and \(D\le(R+1)n\) give (2) with a positive constant depending only on \(F\).
Each nonzero \(F_n\) for \(n\le w\) has finitely many irreducible factors up to associates, so a positive-measure sequence tending to zero eventually leaves these indices. [Dobrowolski, Lemma 1](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83526)

Dimitrov's bound gives \(\log H\ge h/a\) for \(h=(\log 2)/4\).
In the equivalent small-root statement, \(X^a A(1/X)\) has constant coefficient one and a root of modulus at most \(2^{-1/(4a)}\), whose inverse is a root of \(A\).
This applies for either sign of \(A(0)\).
The two external theorems were checked at statement and applicability scope. [Dimitrov, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12545) · [Annals statement](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/22975)

### The outer annulus and all maximal conjugates

In the case \(H\ge1+\eta\), the root formula gives a positive gap immediately.
In the remaining case one can choose a closed annulus \(1\le |z|\le 1+\eta\) excluding every noncircle zero of \(B_R\).
Its noncircle linear factors then have a uniform positive lower bound there.
The lower coefficients are uniformly bounded there, and division of \(F(\alpha,\alpha^n)=0\) by \(\alpha^{n(R-1)}\) gives \(|B_R(\alpha)|e^x\le C\) with \(x=n\log H>0\).
This division works also for \(R=1\).
The constant can be chosen positive if every lower coefficient vanishes.
In that case there is no root in the open outer annulus.

With \(t=0\), \(|B_R|\) has a positive minimum, hence \(x\) has a fixed upper bound, which can be increased to at least one.
Combining this with \(x\ge hn/a\) and (2) gives the uniform gap.
For \(t>0\), each circle root \(\xi\) satisfies \(|\alpha-\xi|\ge H-1\), with repeated roots contributing repeatedly to the product.
If \(x\ge1\), then \(H-1\ge x/n\ge1/n\) and \(e^x\le C' n^t\).
The range \(0<x<1\) already has a uniform bound.
Thus \(x\ll_F\log(2n)\), and \(a\ge hn/x\) gives the claimed lower bound on \(m(A)\).
All fixed lower bounds used for the excluded endpoint, modulus and small-\(n\) cases also give a bound of the form \(c_F/\log(2n)\) after choosing \(c_F\) small enough.

For \(0<m(A)\to0\), \(1<H\le e^{m(A)}\) and \(t>0\) by the preceding uniform gap.
Writing \(\delta\) for the distance from a maximal conjugate to the finite set of circle zeros, every circle factor is at least \(\delta\) in modulus.
Thus \(c\delta^t\le |B_R(\alpha)|\le Ce^{-x}\), with the same constants for all maximizing conjugates.
From (2), \(n/a\ge c_0(F)/m(A)\), so \(x\ge hc_0(F)/m(A)\).
Taking the \(t\)-th root gives (1), with the factor \(1/t\) in the exponential constant.
The root formula gives \(0<m(A)\le r(A)\log H\), while \(\log H\le H-1\le\delta\).
It follows that \(r(A)\ge C_F^{-1}m(A)e^{b_F/m(A)}\), whose right side tends to infinity as \(m(A)\downarrow0\).
If the positive measures at \(r(A)\le r_0\) had infimum zero, a sequence tending to zero in that class would contradict this lower bound.
A Salem minimal polynomial has exactly one exterior root, so \(r_0=1\) applies for every fixed \(F\).

### Both binary signs

In v166, \(f=X^q+X+1\), \(f^*=1+X^{q-1}+X^q\), and \(D^*=D\) with \((X-1)D=X^q-1\).
After \(Y=X^q\) the two factors in brackets become \(f\) and \(Xf^*\).
The signed term becomes \(\pm2X^{q+1}\), and the remaining summand is \(2X^2(X-1)^2D^2\).
These give exactly \(X(X-1)^2P_q^\pm\).
Before specialization the coefficient of \(Y^2\) equals \((X-1)^2(1+X)+2X^2=X^3+X^2-X+1\).
At any circle zero of this real polynomial \(B\), complex conjugation gives \(B^*=0\) as well.
Since \(B-B^*=2X(X-1)\), the only candidates are \(0,1\), where \(B\) takes the values \(1,2\).
Both lifts therefore have a uniform gap for their positive-measure factors, using the smaller of the two fixed constants for the two signs.
The [v166 finding](Yin_R_G0562_v166.md#whole-proof) establishes that the factors extracted from the minus sign are actual noncyclotomic irreducible units of unbounded degree with the stated binary period bound.
Their presence in \(P_q^-\) places each in the corresponding nonzero specialization, so their multiplicative measures are bounded away from one.
