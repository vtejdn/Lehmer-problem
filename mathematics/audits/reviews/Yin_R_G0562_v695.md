## The first dyadic digit and the power orders

At 13:13 UTC on November 20, 2026, a separate agent reviewing for Sheng Yin independently checked the fixed statement and both arguments of version 558.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received no prior explanation or finding about it.
The additive isomorphisms, conductors and indices hold simultaneously for all divisors of the prescribed power of three at the same completion of the marked factor.

For a root \(t\) of the marked residue, \(w=t^a\) has order three and \(z=t^j\) has order \(3a\) by coprimality.
Thus \(z\) generates the residue field and lies outside \(\mathbb F_4\), since \(a\ge3\).
Differentiating the section kills the term containing \(B(w)\) on reduction and leaves the unit \(at^{a-1}\) times \(1+z\) or \(w^2+wz\) according to the parity of \(s\).
Both values are nonzero.
Simple-root lifting in the unramified field of degree \(2a\) produces a root whose integral powers span the integer ring by reduction and Nakayama's lemma.
Its rational factor is the unique one carrying the marked residue in version 553.
The residue degree also forces the stated lower bound on the degree of any completion carrying this residue.

Writing \(Y=\xi^a\), the expansion modulo four at the lifted cube root \(\omega\) has constant term \(2(\omega^2+\xi^j)\) and linear term \((Y-\omega)(g'(\omega)+\xi^j B'(\omega))\).
Division by two recovers the displayed fractional linear function of \(z\).
Its determinants are \(1+w^2=w\) and \(w^2+w^3=w\), so its inverse recovers \(z\) from the digit over \(\mathbb F_4\).
For \(m=a/c\), the Teichmüller lift \(\eta\) of \(t^c\) is in the closed order generated by \(\xi^c\), since raising the principal unit \(\xi^c/\eta\) to successive powers of \(2^f\) makes it converge to one while fixing \(\eta\).
The resulting unramified coefficient ring has residue degree \(f=2a/c\), and its residue field contains \(w=(t^c)^m\).
The digit of \(\xi^a\) is the digit of \(\xi^c\) multiplied by the nonzero residue of \(m\eta^{m-1}\), hence the latter digit has degree \(c\) over this coefficient field on reduction.

The powers through degree \(c-1\) of \(u_c=(\xi^c-\eta)/2\) therefore give an integral basis over \(O_c\), by reduction and equal ranks.
The same powers scaled by \(2^i\) form a basis for the power order because \(2u_c\) has a monic equation of degree \(c\) over \(O_c\).
For \(c>1\) the final coordinate excludes \(2^{c-2}u_c^{c-1}\), while every coordinate admits \(2^{c-1}O_c\).
Since the ambient integer ring is an unramified discrete valuation ring, its largest ideal in the order is exactly \(2^{c-1}O\), with \(c=1\) giving the whole ring directly.
The additive quotient has \(f\) cyclic factors of order \(2^i\) for each \(1\le i<c\), with total order \(2^{fc(c-1)/2}=2^{a(c-1)}\).

At \(c=a\) the global quotient from version 556 is a finite two-group of exactly the local order, and evaluation after completion is surjective since \(\mathbb Z_2[\xi]=O\).
Under this isomorphism the intermediate global subgroup maps onto the local subgroup by polynomial evaluation in the corresponding \(c\)-th power.
Quotienting gives the asserted integral projection because completion preserves every finite two-group here.
This review uses the proved global quotient of version 556 and the marked-factor selection of version 553 at their stated scopes.
