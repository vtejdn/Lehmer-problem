## Finding

The proof establishes \((T_P,S_p)=(2,S_p)\) for either sign whenever \(p>12r+1\) and \(p\mid N\), including \(N=p\).
It determines every residue field, every integer-value gcd, and the two rates claimed for \(N=p^2\).

### The cofactor bound at every permitted delay

The positive root \(\theta\) of \(f\) is simple because \(\sum_{j=1}^r x^{-j}\) has strictly negative derivative at \(\theta\).
For a root \(z\) with \(1\le |z|\le\theta\), the identity \(z^r(2-z)=1\) and strict concavity of \(\log((2-x)x^r)\) exclude all interior moduli of this interval.
Equality at either end forces a positive real root, and \(1\) is not a root.
The triangle inequality on the defining sum gives \(|z|\le\theta\) for any exterior root.
A remaining nonconstant monic integral factor would have a nonzero constant of modulus less than one, so \(f\) is irreducible.
The interior coefficients of \(f,g\) coincide while their leading coefficients are opposite, ruling out proportionality and hence a common root.

At \(3/2\) both seed values are negative.
For the minus sign, with \(A=(3/2)^N\), the value is at most \((49-11A)/8<0\) as \(N\ge4\).
At \(2\) the plus value is \(2^N+3-2^{r+1}>0\) since \(N\ge r+1\), and the minus value is larger.
For \(0<s<1\), the strict inequality \(|s g|<|z^Nf|\) on the unit circle counts \(N+r-1\) interior roots by Rouché's theorem for either sign.
Continuity at \(s=1\) bounds the strictly exterior count by one, including multiplicities.
Reciprocity up to sign supplies the sole interior root \(\alpha^{-1}\).
The product of moduli for the monic integral factor containing \(\alpha\) is one, forcing it to contain that inverse.
It is thus reciprocal, while the rest is cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
The interval \(2<\alpha+\alpha^{-1}<5/2\) excludes a reciprocal quadratic factor, so \(P\) is Salem.

In the torsion calculation write \(V=g/f\).
The three conjugates \(\eta^2,-\eta^2,-\eta\) apply respectively to orders odd, twice odd, and divisible by four.
The conjugating exponents \(2,m/2+2,m/2+1\) are coprime to the corresponding orders \(m\), also in the cases \(m=1,2\).
For \(h=2,2,1\) the resulting rational identities at \(\eta\) equate \(V(\xi z^h)\) and a sign times \(V(z)^h\).
They give nonzero equations since only the second side has a pole at \(\theta\), and clearing denominators gives degrees at most \(4r,4r,2r\).
For \(H=z^Nf+\epsilon g\), direct differentiation gives \(zfH'-(Nf+zf')H=-\epsilon D\).
The leading derivative terms in \(D\) cancel while \(Nfg\) retains its term of degree \(2r\), so \(\deg\gcd(H,H')\le2r\).
Adding this allowance for repeated roots to the distinct-root bound gives \(\deg C\le12r<p-1\) and \(N-11r\le d\le N+r\) without a simplicity assumption on torsion roots.

### The evaluation in the cyclotomic order

As \(\zeta^N=1\), subtracting the two seed values gives \(2(\zeta^r-1)\), and adding them gives \(-2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\zeta^j\).
The indices \(r,r-1\) are both nonzero modulo \(p\).
For either index \(j\), \((1-\zeta^j)/(1-\zeta)\) is integral and has absolute norm one since numerator and denominator each have absolute norm \(p\).
It follows that \(H_+(\zeta)/2\) and \(H_-(\zeta)/(2(\zeta-1))\) are units.

Even norm for \(C(\zeta)\) would mean the resultant of \(C\) and \(\Phi_p\) vanishes modulo two.
Any common root there has exact order \(p\).
For \(m=2^a b\) with \(b\) odd, separability of \(z^b-1\) and the cyclotomic factorization show that the roots of \(\overline\Phi_b\) have exact order \(b\).
For \(a\ge1\) the reduction of \(\Phi_{2^a b}\) is \(\overline\Phi_b^{\,2^{a-1}}\), including \(b=1\).
Thus a contributing factor would have \(b=p\) and degree at least \(p-1\), exceeding the total cofactor degree.
For the plus sign the resulting odd norm divides \(2^{p-1}\) and hence \(C(\zeta)\) is a unit.
For the minus sign \(C\) contains \(z-1\), since \(H_-(1)=0\) and \(P\) is irreducible with \(\alpha>1\).
The absolute norm of \(C(\zeta)\) is therefore divisible by \(p\) and divides \(2^{p-1}p\), so the entire remaining factor \(C(\zeta)/(\zeta-1)\) has absolute norm one.
Cancelling these factors in the two evaluations proves \(P(\zeta)/2\) is a unit in each case.

Multiplication by \(\zeta^{-d/2}\) gives the real unit \(T_P(t)/2\).
Writing \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]=\mathbb Z[t]+\zeta\mathbb Z[t]\) shows its real elements belong to \(\mathbb Z[t]\) by comparing imaginary parts.
The same applies to the inverse.
Thus the two principal ideals agree in the trace order, and taking their inverse images under evaluation adds the kernel \((S_p)\), proving the ideal identity in \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
The absolute norm, taken now in the real field of degree \((p-1)/2\), is precisely \(B_p=2^{(p-1)/2}\).

### The residue degree and the rate

Reduction of the trace identity modulo two gives all the roots of \(\overline S_p\) by pairing the \(p-1\) distinct nontrivial \(p\)-th roots with their inverses.
The quadratic \(Y^2-tY+1\) identifies exactly these pairs, so their \((p-1)/2\) traces exhaust the degree with simple roots.
Frobenius to the power \(j\) fixes a trace precisely for \(2^j\equiv\pm1\bmod p\).
This gives \((p-1)/(2k_p)\) simple factors of degree \(k_p\) and the claimed product of fields by the Chinese remainder theorem.
The divisibility \(p\mid 2^{k_p}\pm1\) gives \(2^{k_p}\ge p-1\).
In particular there is no trace root in \(\mathbb F_2\), so evaluating the ideal identity gives \(\gcd(T_P(a),S_p(a))=1\) at every integer \(a\).

For each fixed \(1<x<2\), \(f(x)/x^r\) tends to a strictly negative number and \(g(x)/x^r\) stays bounded.
Since \(x^{p^2}\to\infty\), the sign at \(x\) is eventually negative uniformly over the two sign choices.
The interval \((x,2)\) then contains the unique exterior root, giving \(M(P)=\alpha\to2\).
The degree error is \(O(r)=O(p)\), so \(d/p^2\to1\) and \(p=o(d)\).
Consequently the normalized resultant is exactly \((\log 2)/2\) on the logarithmic scale, while the normalized common modulus is zero.
The argument admits \(p/r\to\infty\) and concerns the chosen prime divisor of the delay.
The [fixed-list selection theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v6.md#selection) concerns a prime chosen for a prescribed finite list, and the present family has measures tending to two.
