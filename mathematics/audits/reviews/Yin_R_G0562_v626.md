## Multiplicity at reciprocal contacts and growth over prime sets

At 2026-11-19T08:00 UTC, an agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the complete [fourth-power divisor and its applications](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#statement).
The reviewer had taken no part in the proof development and received the fixed proof and scope without development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings on this proof.
The local divisibility, its global product, both sequences of prime sets and the assertions about coefficient cost follow with the stated hypotheses.

In the basis \(1,t\) of \(O[t]/t^2\), multiplication by \((d+t)^2\) has diagonal entries \(d^2,d^2\) and lower left entry \(2d\).
Its cokernel therefore has length \(4v(d)\) in every residue characteristic.
Differentiating the original graph at \(c\) and multiplying by \(c/z\) gives \(p(U'+cV')(z)+(c/z)V(z)=0\).
This is exactly the derivative needed for the first linear equation on \(U,V\), and evaluation at \(c^{-1}\) gives the second with \(Y-w=t+d\).
Multiplying their determinant \(D_0+D_1t\) by \(D_0-D_1t\) leaves \(D_0^2\), which kills both row images.
For \(v(d)>v(q)+v(c^{-1}-c)\), the two terms of \(D_0\) have distinct valuations and the smaller is \(v(pq(c^{-1}-c))\).
The kernel of \(D_0^2\) has length at most \(4v(D_0)\) on a module with at most two cyclic summands.
In the complementary case \(v(d)-v(pq(c^{-1}-c))\le0\).
These arguments include coincident exponents, ramification and reduction of \(c\) to its inverse.
The degree-sum hypothesis puts \(1,Y\) in the Sylvester target, so its cokernel surjects onto the resulting quotient of the contact module.
The ordinary determinant therefore bounds the required length, including for a constant row, without any assumption on leading coefficients at the prime.

The ideal generated by \(\alpha^n-1\) and \(\alpha^m-1\) is the principal ideal at their exponent gcd by Euclidean subtraction.
At each finite prime the alternating sum of the subset minima equals the maximum of the valuations.
After cyclotomic expansion the coefficient of \(v(\Phi_k(\alpha))\) is one for each represented divisor \(k\), by scalar inclusion-exclusion on the set of multiples of \(k\).
This proves the asserted intersection even at prime ideals shared by several cyclotomic values.
Replacing \(pq\) by its common multiple \(L_S^2\) gives the global divisor.
For \(S=\{p\}\), taking norms gives \(e\log|R|\ge 4\log|N(\alpha^{2p}-1)|-8e\log p-4\log|N(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})|\).
The nonzero linear form at each unit-circle conjugate has coefficient size \(O(n)\), giving an \(O_P(\log(2n))\) error in the logarithmic norm of \(\alpha^n-1\).
Möbius inversion uses exactly \(2^{\omega(k)}\) nonzero terms, which yields the stated uniform error in the cyclotomic norm.

At the end of greedy selection at most \(O(r^3)\) primes were forbidden by collisions involving the new prime, while the entire dyadic interval contains \(\gg L/\log L\) primes.
The \(r(r+1)/2\) distinct sums each contribute \(\gg_P L/\log L\) to the product's logarithmic norm because \(\varphi(k)\ge k/(1+\log_2 k)\) dominates the \(O_P(\sqrt{k}\log(2k))\) error.
The remaining factors have nonnegative logarithmic norm and the denominator loss is \(O_P(r\log(2L))\).
The interpolation witnesses have width at most \(2er+2\), and their log cost is bounded by \(2h\Sigma+O_P(r\log(2L))\), with \(r\log(2L)=o(\Sigma)\).
For a qualifying pair with nonzero reflected determinant, the powered factors imply \(\max(\deg U,\deg V)\ge er\), enough for the Sylvester target.

Within a fixed shrinking-interval stage there are finitely many forbidden scales for the given seed and previously chosen sums.
The strictly separated sums stay distinct after taking the prime ratios sufficiently close to the scales.
For two resulting sums, Euclidean subtraction bounds their gcd by their difference, at most \(2r^{-4}L\).
The pairwise-overlap estimate thus loses \(O_P(k^2(1+r^{-4}L))\), with \(k=r(r+1)/2\), against a main term \(4h(r+1)\Sigma\).
Since \(\Sigma\ge rL\), increasing each stage's \(L\) absorbs the logarithmic and constant errors, while the \(O_P(L)\) overlap term already contributes \(O_P(r^{-2})\) relative error.
The lower bound applies to every admissible pair on the retained sets.
The sharp construction supplies one such pair at each stage, and the tangency bound gives the matching lower cost \(h\Sigma\).
For any fixed seed an exact search may test the finite height cap \(H^j\le(2M(\alpha)^j)^\Sigma\) and widths at most \(L\), choosing \(L\) arbitrarily large at each stage.
The fallback cap \(2^\Sigma\) of the common-minimum problem is needed only when claiming a witness within that additional cap.
For the Lehmer seed the unique exterior conjugate stays a singleton over its positive powers, so all powers preserve degree, and its measure is strictly below \(6/5\).
