# Review of the Smith factors of the groups of toral periodic points

<a id="periodic-smith-review"></a>
## Equal or inverse powers determine the leading invariant factors

On 2026-11-16T17:31 a separately assigned agent for Yin independently reviewed the [complete claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0391_v2.md#statement), its three argument sections and the hypotheses of their mathematical inputs.
The reviewer took no part in developing this proof and received the fixed claim location without its development dialogue or prior review conclusions.
The inspection found the divisibility statement, the all-exponent rates, the two limsups and the example valid.

Irreducibility and nontorsion imply that \(A^N-I\) has full rank at every \(N\ge1\).
Multiplication by this matrix identifies \((A^N-I)^{-1}\mathbb Z^D/\mathbb Z^D\) with its integral cokernel, giving the indicated Smith factors for the fixed-point group.
Each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^N)\) has exactly \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^N)]\) extensions, so this is the multiplicity of every powered value.
The set of conjugates is transitive under Galois and inversion commutes with its action.
Thus a single inverse in the powered orbit pairs all powered values, and none is self-inverse.
All classes consequently have size \(b_N\), which divides \(D\).
Equality or inversion for the \(N\)-th powers holds exactly when the order of the corresponding torsion ratio or product divides \(N\).
Their least common multiple gives a period from \(N=1\), including the case when only order one occurs.
The classes can only enlarge relative to \(N=1\), while every prime larger than the period gives exactly the partition at one.
For irreducible nontorsion \(Q\), an inverse pair among its conjugates makes the reversed polynomial equal to \(Q\) up to sign, and the negative sign would force the root \(1\).

Each difference \(x_i^N-1\) is nonzero and integral.
Taking inverse powers changes the difference by a unit, so its local exponent is constant on a class.
For independent conjugates, Corvaja–Zannier Proposition 2 at a fixed splitting field and fixed archimedean set \(S\) bounds the normalized sum of local minima by any positive multiple of their common height \(Nm(Q)/D\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
The powers remain independent, and the sequence meets each exceptional pair at most once.
For dependent conjugates the equality of their positive heights makes the two nonzero relation exponents equal in absolute value.
This gives \(x_j=\zeta x_i^{\pm1}\).
Outside a common class \(\zeta^N\ne1\), and the ideal of the two differences contains \(\zeta^N-1\), whose nonzero values belong to a fixed finite set.
Summation over finitely many pairs therefore gives the all-index \(o_A(N)\) bound even though the partition varies.
At each prime a member of one maximal class pairs with every index outside it, so those minima cover the entire sorted tail, including ties.

Since \(Q\) is separable, a single \(L\)-eigenbasis diagonalizes every \(A^N-I\).
After clearing denominators of that basis matrix and its inverse by \(h\), Cauchy–Binet in each direction gives a difference at most \(2r\,v_{\mathfrak P}(h)\) between the two local valuations of the ideals of minors of order \(r\).
The integral and diagonal valuations are respectively the sums of the \(r\) smallest Smith valuations and of the \(r\) smallest root valuations.
Subtracting the sums for \(r-1\) gives individual error at most \((4r-2)v_{\mathfrak P}(h)\), also for \(r=1\) with empty sum zero.
The first \(b_N\) sorted root valuations agree at every prime.
Hence the valuation of the integer \(s_1/s_{b_N}\) is at most \(8D\,v_{\mathfrak P}(h)\), which gives divisibility by \(h^{8D}\) at every \(N\).
For each nonzero rational integer \(d\), summing \(v_{\mathfrak P}(d)\) with weights \(\log N\mathfrak P/[L:\mathbb Q]\) gives \(\log|d|\), including at ramified primes by the identity \(\sum_{\mathfrak P\mid\ell}e_{\mathfrak P}f_{\mathfrak P}=[L:\mathbb Q]\).
The total error on summing the lower Smith factors is therefore bounded independently of \(N\).

The absolute determinant is the absolute norm of \(\alpha^N-1\), with logarithm \(Nm(Q)+O_A(\log(2N))\) by the signed resultant estimate.
Removing the sublinear tail leaves this leading rate distributed across \(b_N\) logarithms of bounded pairwise differences.
Since \(b_N\ge b_1\) and all sufficiently large primes have \(b_p=b_1\), the two stated prime-index limits realize the limsups.
A uniform positive gap gives a uniform lower bound on these limsups with at most the factor two lost to inversion.
Conversely, the integral companion matrix of every nontorsion algebraic unit realizes its polynomial, and its limsup is at most \(m(Q)\).
The characterizations of the earlier work on the gcd of entries and on higher minors are consistent with their stated scopes and supply no additional premise here. [Corvaja, Rudnick and Zannier, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0309215v3#S1) and [Magagna](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610277).

For the quartic example, the positive interior root has square \(u=(\sqrt5-1)/2\) of norm \(-1\), so \(u\) cannot be a square in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
This proves the degree is four and gives the four listed conjugates.
At odd exponents the roots of one modulus are real and those of the reciprocal modulus are purely imaginary, with no equalities inside either pair.
At an exponent congruent to two modulo four the two distinct values have product \(-1\).
At multiples of four they have product \(1\).
This gives class sizes one, two and four, respectively, for the same matrix.
