## The prime window and the two recovered measures

At each good prime, the rank and both individual codimension bounds hold for every indicated width and sign.
The coefficient estimate, recovery of a qualifying pair, vanishing-infimum equivalence and conditional uniform gap follow with the stated constants.

**Numerator rank.**
A good prime gives \(e_\xi=k_\xi=1\), so both strict local inequalities of the integral-kernel theorem hold.
The dimension \(r\) is the rank over \(\mathbb Q\), and reduction of the lattice gives every quotient \(C\) of degree less than \(r\).
In particular every lattice row evaluates to a multiple of \(p\) at each powered root over the unramified local field.
The first divided value can also be checked directly at the actual root \(x\).
Writing \(z=x^p\) and \(V=\varepsilon A_b^*\), the value and derivative equations imply \(A(z)=p z(A'(z)+xV'(z))\).
Modulo \(p\), the reductions of \(A,V\) are \(\bar G C,\varepsilon\bar G C^*_t\).
Dividing the exact value by \(p\) thus gives (5), with \(\bar z=\xi^p\) and \(\bar G'(\bar z)\ne0\).
The divided value descends to \(\Lambda/p\Lambda\), since adding \(p\) times a lattice row changes it by a value divisible by \(p\).
There are \(\lceil r/2\rceil\) independent coefficients in \(C=C_t^*\), including when \(r=1\).
At the \(d\) distinct powered residues of either one target all multipliers \(1+\varepsilon\xi\) are nonzero, and \(r-1<d\) gives injectivity on that reciprocal space.
In characteristic zero the kernel for that target is saturated in \(\Lambda\) and all conjugate evaluations vanish on it.
Its image modulo \(p\) therefore gives the asserted upper bound on its rank.

**Finite height.**
The two matrix entries at each inverse-pair representative are bounded by the stated \(2(1+pb)\) times its label factor and column exponential.
In a determinant the two copies of each site assign its absolute weight twice, giving \(U_b\) by rearrangement and the signed sum \(b\sum_x u_x\).
The latter averages to zero, the logarithms of all label factors sum to at most \(6h\), and finite-place terms are nonpositive.
The determinant expansion costs at most \(m!\), and conversion from max norm of the primitive wedge to Euclidean covolume costs \(\sqrt{\binom{b+1}{m}}\).
The largest individual weight is at most \(2h\), and the weights have sum \(3h\).
In particular at least \(h\) lies outside the first weight, yielding \(U_b\le(3b-2)h\).
In an orthonormal coordinate system on the real span, the lattice is full rank \(r\) and the Euclidean unit ball contains the cube of side \(2/\sqrt r\).
The upper inequality in Minkowski's second theorem then contributes \(r^{r/2}\) to the product bound.
The reviewer checked the theorem statement and the normalization of the successive minima in Henk's preprint. [Henk, Theorem 1.3](../literature/Hen02/Yin_U001.md#hen02-u001)
Of \(r-q+1\) independent rows at the corresponding minimum, at least one works at each target.
Choosing one for each target excludes at most two integer coefficients in their affine combination, so \(0,1,2\) suffice and the final factor is \(3\sqrt{b+1}\).

**Window.**
Hadamard's determinant bound on the Vandermonde matrix, squared, gives \(m^m M(G)^{2(m-1)}\).
Endpoint evaluations add at most \(2^{2m}M(G)^2\), and \(M(G)=\exp(3h)\).
Each prime greater than 3 that fails good reduction divides the nonzero integer \(\operatorname{disc}(G)G(1)G(-1)\).
In a binomial coefficient in row \(N-1\), each prime-power term of its factorial valuation is 0 or 1.
It therefore divides the least common multiple through \(N\).
Subtracting prime powers of exponent at least two costs at most \(\sqrt N\log N\), as the cost per underlying prime is at most \(\log N\).
At \(N=10^6\) the resulting lower bound on \(\theta(N)/N\) exceeds \(0.679\), and the error terms decrease thereafter.
Thus \(\theta(X)-\theta(L)>9L\log(2L)\), whereas all prime divisors to exclude cost less than \(4L\log(2L)\) for \(h\le1\).
The window gives at least one good prime, uniformly in the seed, while allowing other primes in the search.

**Recovery and limits.**
The derivative polynomial \(W=pY(U'V-UV')-UV\) is nonzero because its lowest product term has nonzero multiplier \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0 U-\operatorname{ord}_0 V)-1\).
At a genuine double contact, \(z=\alpha^p\) is a root of \(W\), \(V(z)\ne0\), and the rational recovery formula \(-U(z)/V(z)=\alpha\) preserves the field.
Conversely the recovery equation at a nonzero root of \(W\) with \(V(z)\ne0\) gives the graph root and its double contact by the derivative identity.
Nontorsion, reciprocity, unit status, degrees and the square partner are exact tests on the recovered minima.
Distinct original minima yield distinct powered minima, so their measures \(ph'\) and \(2ph'\) both occur in the derivative product bound, even at a prime of bad reduction.
All search sets are finite, and integer exponentiation after clearing rational denominators compares the logarithmic scores and their fallback exactly.
This proves \(\lambda\le T_d\) for either prescribed sign.

For \(\lambda=0\), the small-measure and all-power norm reductions retain each chosen positive irreducible measure and supply reciprocal seeds whose degrees tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
With the specified \(r\), one has \(q\ge r/2,\ dh/r\le\sqrt h,\ r\ge\sqrt d\), and \(p=O(d\log d)\) with \(p>d\).
They bound (1) divided by \(p\) by \(O(\sqrt h+\log(2d)/\sqrt d)\) uniformly over the good primes in that degree's window.
Thus the rows qualify under \(H\le2^p\), and \(r/d\) and the second score summand tend to zero.
In the converse both summands are nonnegative, so actual scores approaching zero force both the width ratio to approach 2 and each recovered seed's measure to approach zero.
The positive fallback cannot occur along that subsequence eventually.

At \(r=d\), the main slope is \((18-10/d)h\le18h\).
The seven logarithmic overheads in (6), in units of \(\log(2p)\), are bounded by \(4,12,1,1,1,2,2\), whose sum is 23.
The stated allowance of 24 therefore suffices.
At \(p=10^6\), \(24\log(2p)/p<0.000349<0.0005975<(\log 2)/(8\cdot145)\), and the left side decreases thereafter.
This leaves strict slack at \(h=(\log 2)/145\) in the passing inequality.
The construction works for both signs at each good prime, so one sign excluded at all window primes for each even degree would imply the claimed unrestricted lower bound.
The universal exclusion remains open.
