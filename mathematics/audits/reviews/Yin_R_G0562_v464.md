## Independent finding on rational divisors and finite precision

At 2026-11-18T18:37 UTC an agent outside the development of the pole test independently reviewed the [fixed v398 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v398.md#decision-fixed-prime-pole-test-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had previously computed the finite quartic census J175 and received the present proof and its mathematical dependencies without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior review of v398.
The equivalence with rational divisibility, its one-prime Hensel formulation, the uniform precision bound and both sign refinements hold in the stated range.

### Isolation of the two trace actions

The exact cyclotomic classification applies to \(a=3\) at every admissible \(b\).
In sufficiently large directions v397 applies with \(s=4\), and every nontorsion rational irreducible factor is reciprocal.
Its trace factor is irreducible, since a proper trace factorization would lift to a proper factorization of the reciprocal polynomial.
For \(b>18\) the three simple exterior roots consist of a negative real root and a nonreal conjugate pair.
Consequently the core contains either one or two distinct irreducible factors, since every nontorsion unit factor has an exterior root and a conjugate pair stays together.
The nonreal exterior roots give exactly one conjugate pair of nonreal traces, as \(\Im(z+z^{-1})=(|z|-|z|^{-1})\sin(\arg z)\).
Any second trace factor has only real roots.
The uniform lower bound for the degrees of nontorsion factors in primitive directions lets every individual trace degree exceed twelve.

The splitting field \(L_c\) of the first trace factor has group \(S_{n_c}\) by the alternating action and the odd trace permutation supplied by complex conjugation.
This permutation is a single transposition, since the remaining traces are real.
The field of totally real algebraic numbers is preserved by every rational automorphism of the algebraic closure, so its intersection with \(L_c\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
Complex conjugation fixes that intersection pointwise.
Its fixing subgroup therefore contains every conjugate of the transposition, hence all of \(S_{n_c}\).
Thus the intersection is \(\mathbb Q\).
In the split case all rational conjugates of every generating root of \(L_s\) remain real, so \(L_s\) is totally real.
The two finite Galois splitting fields have trivial intersection, making the compositum group their direct product, even if the two trace degrees are equal.
In particular the full alternating action on either factor fixes every trace in the other factor.

### The signs and the subset criterion

Expanding \(D(X)D(X^{-1})\) gives \(3+C_3-C_9-C_{12}=-W\).
The degree of \(W\) is twelve and it is monic, so the roots of a monic \(f\) contribute \(\prod_{f(t)=0}(-W(t))\) to \(U(f)\), with no additional sign from interchanging the arguments of the resultant.
For rational monic \(f\mid H_b\), its integral monic lift \(X^{\deg f} f(X+X^{-1})\) divides \(P_b\) and hence the parent.
The pole resultant with \(D\) first is \(U(f)\) by the fixed trace identity.
At a zero \(x\) of \(D\) the parent equals \(x^3(1+x^6)\), and the products of these two factors over the poles are \(-1\) and \(1\), giving the parent resultant \(-1\).
For the second product put \(t=x^3\) and evaluate \(t^4+t^3-1\) at \(i,-i\), getting \(-i,i\), with each quartic root having three lifts.
Multiplicativity and integrality give \(U(f)=\pm1\).
In particular each \(\lambda_i=-W(t_i)\) is itself an algebraic unit, as their full product is a unit.

For odd \(b\), every rational lift divides \(A_b/(X+1)\), and the two negative real roots lie strictly to the right of the negative real pole.
The possible real roots occur together in a reciprocal lift, and all remaining roots occur in conjugate pairs.
The lift is positive at both real poles, so its resultant is \(+1\).
For even \(b\) in a split, the negative pole lies between the two real roots of the signed Salem factor in the original variable \(X\).
That lift has negative value there and positive value at the positive pole, giving \(-1\).
The other lift has no real roots and is monic, hence positive at both real poles, giving \(+1\).
The nonreal poles always occur in conjugate pairs with positive products.

Equality of \(\lambda_i\) at two letters in a factor would propagate to every pair by double transitivity.
That would give \(W(Y)-c\) more than twelve distinct roots, impossible in degree twelve.
For any subset cutting one orbit properly, choose \(a\) inside and \(b\) outside that orbit's chosen part.
If two letters are outside, the cycle \((a\,b\,c)\) with \(c\) outside moves the chosen set by precisely one exchange.
If only \(b\) is outside, taking a second inside letter for \(c\) likewise exchanges exactly one inside letter with \(b\).
The supported three-cycle fixes the other orbit.
Thus if the global product were rational, Galois invariance and cancellation of nonzero terms would force equality of the two exchanged \(\lambda\)'s.
This excludes every partial orbit, independently of possible equal scalar values between different factors.
The passing subsets are exactly unions of whole orbits, and minimal nonempty ones give the individual rational factors.

### Squarefree reduction, lifting and the cutoff

In characteristic three the derivative is \(bX^{b-1}D(X)\).
The parent is coprime to \(X\) by its constant term and to \(D\) by the unit resultant, and \(3\nmid b\), so its reduction is squarefree.
The monic divisor \(P_b\) retains that property on reduction.
A repeated trace root would give a squared factor \((X^2-tX+1)^2\) in the reduced lift over the residue algebraic closure, contradicting squarefreeness.
The distinct residual irreducibles therefore have unique compatible monic Hensel lifts, and every monic divisor over \(\mathbb Z_3\) is a subset product.
After embedding a global splitting field in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_3\), those lifted factors group the images of actual global roots.
Their coefficients are symmetric polynomials of the grouped roots, and their scalar is the image of the corresponding global algebraic integer \(\lambda_J\).
Consequently a \(3\)-adic equality to either rational sign is a global equality.

The monic parent has coefficient length at most six and unit endpoints.
Comparing its leading term at an exterior root with the other terms bounds the root modulus by six, and reciprocity gives the inverse bound.
For every complex conjugate this gives \(|W(t)|\le 2(6^{12}+6^9+6^3)+3\le C\).
For a subset of \(j\) traces there are at most \(\binom nj\) orbit images, and each product has modulus at most \(C^j\).
For a sign \(\epsilon\) and \(\lambda_J\ne\epsilon\), the nonzero norm from \(\mathbb Q(\lambda_J)\) of the difference has absolute value at most \((1+C^j)^{\binom nj}\).
Embedding its norm product in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_3\), each conjugate difference is integral.
One factor of valuation at least \(k\) therefore forces the rational integer norm to be divisible by \(3^k\).
Taking \(j\le n\) and the largest binomial coefficient yields precisely the strict cutoff (2), including the empty and full subsets.
The resultant is polynomial in the input coefficients, so computing it on the lifts modulo \(3^k\) suffices.
At a smaller precision an absent proper candidate already excludes a rational split, since a genuine rational divisor passes at every precision.

A proper passing subset at the guaranteed precision is equivalent to a proper rational trace factor, which in this range is equivalent to splitting of the nontorsion reciprocal core.
For \(b\ge26\) in the stated range the exterior-count theorem shows that this splitting occurs exactly when some factor \(Q(X)\) of \(P_b\) has \(Q(-X)\) equal to the minimal polynomial of a Salem number strictly below \(9/8\).
The pole sign \(-1\) for even \(b\) belongs to the trace subset of \(Q(X)\) before this change of variable.
No actual split at an untested exponent follows from the criterion.
