### Laurent count, mandatory integer mass and fixed consequence

Substituting t=z+z inverse gives
\[

\]
Cancelling their geometric factor proves the stated Laurent identity for F_a exactly.
The intermediate exponents are consecutive multiples of three, with a negative and a positive block of a monomials each and no overlap.
Multiplication by z-1 produces two adjacent nonzero coefficients for each such exponent, with one zero separating successive pairs.
Thus there are exactly 4a coefficients of magnitude one, and only the 2a within-pair products contribute to the adjacent inner product, each with value -1.
The symmetry d_(-k)=d_k follows from the original polynomial in z+z inverse.
Constant Laurent coefficients consequently give the two integrals 4a and -4a, including the factor two in the t-weighted integral.

The algebraic identity \((t+1)^2g_a(t)^2=f_a(t)^2/(9a^4)\) holds as a polynomial identity, also at \(t=-1\).

### Neutral family and its actual residues

For r even at least four and s odd at least three, both binomials supply the two factors 1-z, and the even binomial also supplies 1+z.
The displayed quotient is therefore integral, monic, reciprocal, has constant coefficient one and degree p+1 for the odd integer p=r+s.
For each of the five profiles the quadratic factor is a cyclotomic product, including its repetitions at x=plus or minus two.
All remaining binomial factors after division are cyclotomic products as well, so the complete measure is exactly one.
The first tail term is at min(r,s), establishing the exact prefix for every L below that value.
Both lengths can grow without producing a positive-measure point.

A reciprocal integer polynomial of this even degree has an integral trace representation in the basis 1,T_1,...,T_(n+1).
Its coefficient at index one is 1-2x, whereas B_n has next-to-leading coefficient one, so division by B_n has quotient t-2x.
The v3 inverse identity E_(n-1)B_(n-1)=1 modulo B_n then gives the actual integral residue input h from the remainder C.
This checks the original affine object rather than merely an unrelated cyclotomic polynomial.

The pole formula was derived directly from the displayed quotient at zeta and the derivative of B_n.
The binomial product at a pole is 2-T_r(b_j), the quadratic square supplies zeta squared times (b_j-x) squared, and the denominator and trace factor cancel with the derivative's phase.
This gives exactly
\[
 r_j^{(0)}=\frac{(b_j-x)^2(2-T_r(b_j))}{p}\ge0.
\]
The stated phase relation is consistent with zeta=exp(2 pi i j/p) and the chosen half power exp(pi i j/p).
When gcd(r,p)=1, the sine factor is nonzero at every original index; a profile value absent from the grid also makes the other square nonzero.
If x=-1 and p is divisible by three, the actual -1 residue instead vanishes.
In all cases these completions fail the required strictly negative block, so their number cannot be a lower bound for the admissible positive count.

For the example r=2m at most n-1, the identity
\[
 2-T_{2m}(t)=(4-t^2)E_{m-1}(t)^2
\]
proves the stated h=(t-x) squared times E_(m-1) squared without reduction ambiguity, since its degree r is below n.
It is the same neutral family and retains the double trace factor; no separate supply is obtained.
