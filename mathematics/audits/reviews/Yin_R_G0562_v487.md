## Independent finding on the seed quotient and the individual cofactor constant

At 2026-11-18T20:26 UTC an agent outside the development of the [fixed v404 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v404.md#decision-single-orbit-determinant-submitted) independently checked its complete statement and three supporting sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the representative theorem v403 and received the new orbit determinant in its written form without its development discussion.
The determinant descends integrally to the seed, retains the power \(t\) from every pair of rotations, and gives the stated strict inequality for coprime rows.
The bound \(m(R)/(ph)\ge1-o(1)\) holds separately for each actual cofactor on the stated representatives and prime window.

### The integer determinant, its constant coefficient and its seed factor

Let \(B_{lk}(Y)\) be the coefficient of \(Z^l\) in the remainder modulo \(Z^n-1\) of
\(U(Z^pY)^{n-1-k}Z^{rk}V(Z^pY)^k\), with \(0\le l,k<n\).
The divisor \(Z^n-1\) is monic, so \(B\) is a matrix over \(\mathbb Z[Y]\).
Multiplying \(B\) on the left by the Fourier matrix gives exactly the numerator matrix in (2).
Thus the quotient of determinants is \(\det B\), with no residual denominator.
The homogeneous Vandermonde identity gives the displayed product over all \(N=n(n-1)/2\) pairs, with the same factor order in numerator and denominator.

At zero, column \(k\) has scalar \(U(0)^{n-1-k}V(0)^k\) times the Fourier column of frequency \(rk\bmod n\).
These frequencies are a permutation, since \((r,n)=1\).
Both endpoint exponents sum to \(N\), so \(D(0)=\pm(U(0)V(0))^N\ne0\).
Each individual numerator factor is also nonzero at zero because \(\zeta^{jr}\ne\zeta^{ir}\) for \(i\ne j\).
Replacing \(Y\) by \(\zeta^pY\) cycles the rows, with scalar \(\zeta^{-rk}\) on column \(k\).
The product of these scalar and permutation factors is independent of \(Y\) and must be one by evaluation at zero.
Since \(\zeta^p\) has order \(n\), only exponents divisible by \(n\) occur in the integral polynomial \(D(Y)\).
Reading off these coefficients gives \(D(Y)=D_0(Y^n)\) over \(\mathbb Z\).

For every pair \(i<j\), the identity
\[
 v_j(x^p)F(\zeta^i x)-v_i(x^p)F(\zeta^j x)
 =(u_i v_j-u_j v_i)(x^p)
\]
shows divisibility by \(P(x)^t\) because both rotations preserve \(P\).
All conjugates of a root of \(P\) are nonzero, so raising to power \(p\) is unramified at every one of them.
A zero of order at least \(t\) after substitution has order at least \(t\) at the image.
The hypothesis of degree preservation gives \(\deg P\) distinct images, so the same \(Q^t\) divides every one of the \(N\) pair factors over \(\mathbb C\).
Dividing their product by the nonzero Fourier Vandermonde constant consequently retains \(Q^{tN}\).

The monic irreducible \(P=P_0(X^n)\) contains the complete \(n\)-th root fiber above every seed root.
Powering maps such a fiber bijectively onto the complete fiber above the powered seed root since \((p,n)=1\).
Two coincident powered seed roots would therefore contradict the distinct images on the full orbit.
It follows that \(Q(Y)=Q_0(Y^n)\), \(m(Q_0)=p\,m(P_0)=ph\), and \(Q_0(0)\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Z\).
Integral monic division of \(D_0(T)\) by \(Q_0(T)^{tN}\) has zero remainder: a nonzero remainder would retain degree strictly smaller than \(\deg Q^{tN}\) after substitution \(T=Y^n\), yet would be divisible by \(Q^{tN}\).
The integral quotient \(E\) is thus nonzero and satisfies \(|E(0)|=|U(0)V(0)|^N\).
These general arguments need only the two displayed summands and permit \(p\mid r\); their residue classes modulo \(p\) are distinct whenever \(p\nmid r\), as in the window application.

As an exact finite check, take \(P_0(X)=X^2-X-1\), \(r=p\), \(V=1\), \(U(Y)=Q(Y)^t-Y\).
For \((n,p,t)=(2,1,2),(3,2,2),(4,3,1)\), direct polynomial checks give irreducible \(P_0(X^n)\) and the integer coefficient-matrix determinants give \(D/Q^{tN}=1,-1,-1\), respectively.
This also checks the Fourier sign for nonidentity permutations and an example with \(t>1\).

### Hadamard's inequality and its equality case

Each of the \(n\) entries in row \(j\) has modulus at most \(\max(|u_j|,|v_j|)^{n-1}\).
Hadamard thus contributes a factor \(\sqrt n\) per row, and orthogonality of the Fourier matrix gives denominator modulus exactly \(n^{n/2}\).
Taking logarithms and integrating, every rotated maximum contributes the same \(I(F)\) because \(|\zeta^{jr}|=1\) and Haar measure is rotation invariant.
The resulting upper bound for \(m(D)\) is \(n(n-1)I(F)=2N I(F)\).
Substitution preserves circle measure and additivity gives \(m(D)=tNph+m(E)\).
Jensen's formula gives \(m(E)\ge\log|E(0)|\), including when \(E\) is constant.
Dividing by \(N\) gives both inequalities in (1).

All logarithms used in the equality argument are integrable since the polynomials are nonzero and have only finitely many logarithmic singularities on the circle.
Equality of the integrals forces pointwise equality almost everywhere in both Hadamard and the bound on each row norm.
For \(n\ge2\), the first and last entries of a row already force \(|u_j|=|v_j|\) at those points.
Away from zeros set \(w_j=v_j/u_j\), of modulus one.
Orthogonality of rows zero and one gives the vanishing geometric sum with ratio \(w_1/w_0\) or its inverse, so \((w_1/w_0)^n=1\) almost everywhere.
This identity holds in \(\mathbb C(Y)\) because a polynomial numerator vanishing on infinitely many points is zero.
Factoring \(Z^n-1\) in this field makes \(w_1/w_0\) constant, and the nonzero endpoints identify its value as \(\zeta^r\).
Thus \(V/U\) is invariant under \(Y\mapsto\zeta^pY\).
If \(U,V\) are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), a Bézout identity implies they are coprime over \(\mathbb C\) as well.
The ratio identity and equality of degrees under rotation force each rotated polynomial to be a scalar multiple of the original, and its own nonzero constant term forces scalar one.
Consequently both polynomials in that ratio depend only on \(Y^n\).
At any root \(\gamma\) of \(P\), subtracting \(F(\gamma)=0\) from \(F(\zeta\gamma)=0\) then gives \((\zeta^r-1)\gamma^r V(\gamma^p)=0\).
Since \(\gamma\ne0\) and \(\zeta^r\ne1\), this forces a common zero of \(U,V\), a contradiction.
This proves strictness without needing a quantitative deficit in Hadamard.

### Transfer to the two actual cofactors

On the v403 representatives and all their window primes for sufficiently large degree, \(p<n\), so \((p,n)=1\), and powering preserves the full degree.
For either sign use \(r=1,\ U=A,\ V=\pm A^*\) and \(t=2\).
Their constant terms are \(A(0)\ne0\) and the signed leading coefficient of \(A\), also nonzero.
Real integral coefficients give \(|A(e^{it})|=|A^*(e^{it})|\), hence \(I(F)=m(A)\).
The actual degree \(4pd+1\) comes from the nonzero last coefficient of the second row, and the constant term of the first row is nonzero, so this is also the span.
The two coprime rows in \(\mathbb Q[Y]\) are coprime in the Laurent ring, have distinct classes \(0,1\bmod p\), and satisfy the hypotheses of the single-graph estimate of v364.
Its constant \(C\) is finite since its singularity has order \(1/2\).

The first inequality of (1) gives \(I(F)\ge ph+m(E)/(2N)\).
Using \(p>4\) in the circle transfer gives
\[
 m(F)\ge(p-4)h+(1-4/p)m(E)/(2N)-2\log(CD_F)/p .
\]
Primitive integer content, divisibility by \(P^2\) and the zero measure of every cyclotomic factor give \(m(R)=m(F)-2h\) with all remaining nontorsion multiplicities retained.
This gives exactly the \(p-6\) coefficient and both error terms in (3).
Since \(E\) is nonzero integral, \(m(E)\ge0\).
The representative height estimate \(\log(2D_F)=o(ph)\) holds uniformly over the entire window.
After division by \(ph\), the two potential losses are \(6/p\) and \(2\log(CD_F)/(p^2h)=o(1/p)\).
Both vanish uniformly, independently of the sign, coefficients and choice of the single eligible graph.
Applying this separately to each of the two v403 rows gives the asserted improvement for both actual \(R_i\), with their earlier upper estimates unchanged in scope.
The proof supplies no seed-independent positive lower bound for \(h\).
