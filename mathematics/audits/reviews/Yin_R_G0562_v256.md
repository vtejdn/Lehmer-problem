# A uniform gap from orders supported on finitely many primes

## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T14:50 UTC a reviewer outside proof development checked the whole [C345 v256 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v256.md#decision-finite-prime-period-gap-submitted) for Yin.

The weighted divisor estimate, optimized period bound and uniform lower bounds on factor measure and degree hold with the stated dependencies.

### Capturing divisor weight

Put \(x=1+\max(0,\log(B/d))\) and \(u=\log(B/Y)\) for \(0<Y\le B\).
In a shell \(u+h\le\log(B/s)<u+h+1\), the \(H\)-part of \(B/s\) has at most \(\tau(H)\) choices.
There are \(O((1+u+h)^{r-1})\) choices of the first \(r-1\) prime exponents, and the interval for the last exponent has length \(1/\log q_r\).
Since \(w_s\le Ks\), the total tail weight is at most a fixed multiple of
\[
    Y\sum_{h\ge0} e^{-h}(1+u+h)^{r-1}
       \ll Y(1+u)^{r-1}.
\]
The estimate holds at \(Y=B\) and when some prime exponents are zero.
Choose \(\eta\le1/K\) and \(Y=\eta d/x^{r-1}\), so \(Y\le B\) even when \(d>B\) in the numerical lemma.
Then \(1+u\le x+(r-1)\log x+\log(1/\eta)\).
The tail divided by \(d\) is \(O(\eta(1+\log(1/\eta))^{r-1})\), and a choice of \(\eta\) independent of \(B,d\) leaves at least half the weight in \((Y,Kd]\).

For this remaining weight all coordinate logarithms are \(O(x)\).
Grouping the first \(r-1\) into intervals of width \(W=x^{1-1/r}\) and fixing the \(H\)-part costs \(O((1+x/W)^{r-1})=O(W)\) groups.
Within one group the sum of all coordinate logarithms varies by at most \(\log(K/\eta)+(r-1)\log x\), so the last varies by \(O(W)\) too.
For \(r=1\) there are no coordinates to partition, \(W=1\), and the term \((r-1)\log x\) vanishes.
For \(r\ge2\), \(\log x\ll_r x^{1-1/r}\) on \(x\ge1\).
Replacing each complement exponent by its minimum within the group multiplies a divisor by at most \(\exp(O(W))\) across all coordinates and yields the group's least common multiple.
Some group therefore gives weight \(\gg d/W\) at a period \(J\ll d\exp(O(W))\).
This uses only the stated size and weight hypotheses and also permits the prime two in the numerical lemma.

### Frobenius orbits and the optimized bound

In the polynomial application \(B\) is odd, so \(X^B-1\) is separable over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Monicity then gives total weight exactly \(d\le B\).
For a represented order \(s>1\) every selected Frobenius orbit has full size \(f=\operatorname{ord}_s(2)\le d\), even if most orbits at that order are absent.
If \(q^e\mid s\) with \(e\ge1\) and \(f_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(2)\), then \(f_q\mid f\) and \(v_q(2^f-1)=c_q+v_q(f/f_q)\) for odd \(q\), where \(c_q=v_q(2^{f_q}-1)\).
As \(q\nmid f_q\), the factor \(q^{(e-c_q)_+}\) divides \(f\), including primes where \(c_q>1\).
Their product divides \(f\) by coprimality, giving \(s\le H(\prod q^{c_q})d\).
For \(s=1\) the same bound follows from \(d\ge1\).
The weight at \(s\) counts distinct roots of order \(s\), so \(w_s\le\varphi(s)\le s\).
These checks justify the divisor lemma for all selections of complete orbits at each order.

The \(r_J\) roots captured by \(\gcd(\bar S,X^J-1)\) include the entire selected group, hence \(r_J\gg d/W\).
In C345 v166 the period \(J\) and doubling index \(i=\lceil2d/r_J\rceil\) give numerator at least \(d\log2\) and denominator at most \(d\exp(O(W))\).
Absorbing fixed factors using \(W\ge1\) proves \(m(S)\ge E(S)\ge\exp(-CW)\).
Neither irreducibility of \(S\) nor a full cyclotomic level in its reduction is needed for this step.
When \(m(S)\le Kd/B\), the inequality \(x\le1+\log K+Cx^{1-1/r}\) bounds \(x\), since above \(x=(2C)^r\) the last term is at most \(x/2\).
This gives both \(d/B\gg1\) and \(m(S)\gg1\).
In particular the one-prime case already gives a constant period lower bound for all \(d\).
The conclusions continue to hold when \(B=d\) or when all varying exponents vanish.

For the antichain of C345 v243, \(B=3n\), \(5^{2A}/7<n\le5^{2A}\) and \(d\asymp A5^A\), so \(x=A\log5-\log A+O(1)\).
The upper bound \(E_A\le\exp(-c\sqrt A)\) there uses the same supremum over all periods and doublings, and therefore proves the stated order of sharpness on binary divisors.
Its use here makes no assumption that the antichain lifts to a rational factor of the five-term parent.

### Uniformity in the five-term offset

For \(0<l<n\) the five exponents are distinct, the integral coefficient length is seven, and reduction gives \(X^{2n}+X^n+1\), a divisor of \(X^{3n}-1\).
Every monic rational factor \(S\) is integral with unit constant term.
At any noncyclotomic irreducible factor, a nonempty one-term side of a cut cannot vanish, and a vanishing two-term side would force a positive power of a nontorsion unit to be a rational unit.
Thus all four cuts survive for every allowed \(l\), including \(1\) and \(n-1\).
With \(d=\deg S\) each gap \(G\) gives \(Gm(S)\le d\log7\), and summing the gaps of total width \(2n\) gives \(m(S)\le2(\log7)d/n\).
Taking \(B=3n\), \(H=3\) and \(K=6\log7\) in the preceding comparison proves (1), without any condition on \(\gcd(n,l)\).
An integral divisor of the monic parent has leading coefficient of modulus one, so positive measure requires at least one noncyclotomic irreducible factor.
Adding degrees bounds the number of such factors with multiplicities.
Bounding the largest prime divisor would place every prime factor of \(n\) in a fixed finite set, which rules out shrinking measures.
