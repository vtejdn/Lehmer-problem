For one cluster, the contour functions are divided differences of the exponential at the lifted nodes.
At distinct nodes the residue at the newest node has nonzero coefficient, so all original exponential rows are recovered by an invertible triangular change.
The contour has distance at least one from every parameter on the real interval, giving uniform bounds on the values and the first two time derivatives for fixed \(b,D\).
Computing the derivative integrals at time zero from the coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) gives zeros at orders below \(l-1\) and \(i^{l-1}\) at order \(l-1\).
This triangular derivative matrix proves independence even at every confluent parameter tuple.
Positivity of the weight in the open interval then makes the continuous weighted Gram positive definite.
Continuity on the compact parameter set and the finite number of allowed cluster sizes give a uniform positive lower eigenvalue.
Uniform Riemann-sum convergence preserves a positive lower bound once \(N\) is sufficiently large.

For an off-block entry, the sampled amplitude \(w f_l\overline{f_{l'}}\), extended by zero, has two bounded derivatives since the weight and its first two derivatives vanish at either endpoint.
Its second finite differences have sum of absolute values \(O(1/N)\), by \(O(N)\) samples of size \(O(1/N^2)\).
Two shifts of the finite exponential sum and the normalization \(1/N\) give \(O((N v)^{-2})\) because \(|e^{iv}-1|\gg v\) for angular distances at most \(\pi\).
Along each semicircle the \(j\)-th other center is at least \(jG/N\) away.
Summing over at most \(b\) rows from each such center bounds the off-block Hermitian operator norm by a constant times \(b/G^2\), independently of the number of centers.
Taking \(G\) large leaves half the positive diagonal-block lower bound.
Removing the weight, which lies between zero and one, preserves that lower Gram bound.
Each unweighted time column has squared norm \(O(u)\), so the row-space projection diagonal is \(O(u/N)\).
The proof never uses a lower bound on the spacing within a cluster.
The bibliographic reference to clustered Vandermonde matrices is contextual; the estimate used here follows from the displayed proof.

I checked the selection estimate by counting vertices through the nested graphs.
In a final component larger than \(b\), any vertex lies in a connected subset of \(b+1\) vertices reached in at most \(b\) edges, so its \(U/N\)-ball has more than \(b\) vertices once \(U\ge bt_J\).
Every other vertex changes its component in at most \(b-1\) of the \(J\) steps, since its component size increases strictly at each change.
A common index discards at most \(s(b-1)/J\) additional vertices.
Being unchanged is a property of a whole component.
Two retained components must be separated by more than \(t_{j+1}/N\), and their individual diameters are at most \(bt_j/N\).
This fits the cluster lemma at that index.
The graphs and the discarded vertex properties respect angle reversal, so all retained neutral roots occur with their conjugates.
All constants for this selection are fixed before taking the limit, including when \(U\) grows arbitrarily slowly.

For the real return covariance the retained complex rows are precisely the complexification of the corresponding real sine and cosine row space.

The squared column norms of \(A_0\) are bounded by the projection leverage \(C(b,J)s/N\), tending to zero since \(k\to\infty\).
The v713 entropy proof applies to this row isometry with its original full-past cost \(sh\) and a Gaussian error \(uE=o(s)\), uniformly in \(u\le s\).
Dividing (6) by \(\phi(\eta+\sqrt\delta)\to\infty\) bounds the compressed near-zero count by \(o(s)\); for \(u=0\) its count vanishes.
Interlacing allows at most \(s-u\) further small eigenvalues in the full covariance.
Following (5) by the limit \(J\to\infty\) at fixed \(b\) gives (1) and the stated subsequence consequence.

Under tightness one can fix successively larger \(b\) to make its limiting upper tail arbitrarily small, and then take \(J\) sufficiently large relative to that \(b\).
For each such pair, the sequence cutoff can ensure both \(U\ge bt_J\) and arbitrarily small \(C(b,J)s/N\).
This diagonal selection gives \(u/s\to1\).
Jensen in the logarithmic eigenvalue variable, now dividing by \(u\), has limiting cost at most \(1/2\) because \(sh/u\to1/2\).
It gives the compressed lower bound \(\log x_{1/2}\).
One may enlarge the deterministic regularizer to \(\varepsilon\to0\) with \((1-u/s)|\log\varepsilon|\to0\); for example when \(u<s\) take the maximum of \(\sqrt\delta\) and \(\exp(-1/\sqrt{1-u/s})\).

For \(U=N/H\), pairwise angles within \(1/H\) contribute at least a fixed constant times \(H\) to the nonnegative Fejér kernel.
