Rational Bézout gives a nonzero integer annihilator \(D_0\) of the additive group of \(R=\mathbb Z[Y]/(A,B)\).
For each \(p\mid D_0\) its quotient \(R/pR\) is a quotient of a finite-dimensional \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra because some polynomial remains nonzero, possibly a constant.
On each primary component the successive quotients of the filtration by powers of \(p\) are images of \(R/pR\), proving finiteness even for nonmonic blocks.
Base change under \(Y=X^k\) gives precisely \(k\) additive copies, since every polynomial decomposes uniquely by its exponent modulo \(k\).
The factor identity for a monic \(S\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) puts \(B(X^k)\) in \((S,A(X^k))\).
Consequently the multiplication cokernel on \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\) is a quotient of that finite group.
Its cardinality is the nonzero absolute resultant and divides \(|R|^k\).
For primitive \(q\mid A\), Gauss's lemma gives \(A/q\in\mathbb Z[Y]\), the pair \(q,B\) still satisfies both coprimality requirements, and \(B(X^k)\in(S,q(X^k))\).
Thus the same proof controls the individual resultant at every prime, without a leading-coefficient restriction.

For small positive \(m\) the universal endpoint and nonreciprocal bounds give a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit up to sign.
The hypothesis on \(N\) separates both the second highest term of \(A(X^k)\), when present, and all terms of \(B(X^k)\) from the leading exponent by at least \(k\).

The width is \(O_{A,B}(N)\) and there are at most \(\deg A+\deg B+2\) terms, so \(Nh\gg_{A,B}1\) and \(N/k\gg_{A,B}m^{-2}\).
