## Four disjoint matches at uniform precision

At 2026-11-25T14:59 UTC, an agent outside proof development independently checked the full [separation theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v182.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof without development dialogue, author explanations or prior findings on this version, and consulted the [finding on v181](Yin_R_G0562_v1259.md#finding) for context on the input bounds and real uniqueness.
The degree-four upper bound, the three listed residual divisors and both uniqueness criteria hold for arbitrary admissible heights and degrees at most \(X\), over every finite dyadic extension.

For a genuine realization \(T<p/q\), the graph coordinate is positive and the two-power criterion gives the coprime positive exponents \(u<v\).
For even \(q\), substitution gives \(Y=T V(x,T)\), and for odd \(q\) it gives \(Y=(T V(x,T))^{-1}\).
Raising to the power \(u\) gives the asserted relation for \(N=v-\epsilon u>0\).
Clearing the Laurent powers and unit denominators gives a polynomial over \(\mathbb Z_2\) vanishing at \(T\) in a chosen dyadic embedding.
The cleared identity has rational coefficients with odd denominators and vanishes in \(\mathbb Q(T)\), so the full rational minimum \(F\) divides it, with integral dyadic quotient by monic division.

Write \(\mathcal O\) for the integer ring of the chosen extension, \(\pi\) for a uniformizer, and \(\widetilde B\) for any monic lift of the common factor \(B\).
Then \(R=\mathcal O[z]/(\widetilde B)\) is finite free and \(R/2^bR\) receives the two quotient maps given by the divisibilities \(B\mid F_i\bmod 2^b\).
The constant term of each \(F_i\) is one, so \(\widetilde B\) has unit constant and \(z\) is invertible.
Geometric series invert \(1-x_i z^{\pm1}\) since \(x_i\in2\mathbb Z_2\).
Raising each relation to the other \(N_i\) now gives (2), including when the signed exponents are negative.

The logarithm series on \(1+2R\) converges in the complete free module, and the commuting power-series identity gives additivity there.
For \(t\in2^b R\) every term of \(\log(1+t)\) lies in \(2^bR\).
With \(a_1,a_2\) not simultaneously zero, the recursion and the divisibilities \(2\mid x_1+x_2\), \(4\mid x_1x_2\) propagate the lower bound \(v_2(a_j)\ge h+j-1\) from \(j=1,2\).
After division by \(2^h\) the \(j\)-th logarithm term for \(j\ge3\) is in \(2^{j-1-v_2(j)}R\), whose exponent is positive and tends to infinity.
Freeness justifies the division in the congruence, and \(b>h\) puts the resulting sum in \(\pi R\).
The two remaining scalar coefficients come from \(\mathbb Z_2\) with at least one odd, so their reductions form one of the three nonzero binary coefficient pairs.
In \(R/\pi R=(\mathcal O/\pi)[z]/(\overline B)\), the three resulting polynomials after multiplication by \(z^2\) factor as \(z(z+1)^2\), \((z+1)^4\) and \((z+1)^2(z^2+z+1)\).
The unit constant removes \(z\) in the first case, and monicity preserves the degree of \(B\).
This proves divisibility with multiplicities for every ramification index.

Bézout's identity gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(T)\), and the absolute values of the conjugates in \(T=\beta^u\) give \(\log M(\beta)=\log\beta\).
The positive unit \(\beta>1\) thus meets the hypotheses of Voutier's Corollary 2 at degree \(d\).
The review checked the external bound at statement and applicability scope against the [author edition, pp. 1, 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.3110#page=4).
Combining \(u\log\beta<1/q\) with \(YT<(2q+1)^d\) gives \(qu<(\log(3X))^3/2\) and \(v+u< X(\log(3X))^3\log(2H+1)/2\).
In particular \(q,u\le H\) and \(N\le W\) simultaneously for both solutions.
For \(x_i=A_i/D_i\) in the displayed parity conventions, \(D_i\) is odd and \(A_i,D_i\le H+1\).
Over \(D_1^jD_2^j\) the numerator of \(a_j\) is bounded by \(2HW(H+1)^{2j}\) in absolute value.
Each nonzero numerator for \(j=1,2\) therefore has valuation strictly below the stated \(b\).
Since \(H=O((\log(3X))^3)\) and \(W=O(XH\log(2H+1))\), this precision is \(O(\log(2X))\).

If both coefficients vanish, the identities \(m x_1=n x_2\ne0\) and \(m x_1^2=n x_2^2\) imply \(x_1=x_2\) and \(m=n\).
The side of one determines the parity and the rational number then determines \(q\).
After canceling the common sign, \(m=n\) gives \(u_1v_2=u_2v_1\).
For this common height, the increasing derivative of \(\log Y\) as a function of \(\log T\), together with its zero value at zero, makes \(\log Y/\log T\) strictly increasing on the admissible interval.
Thus the exceptional case identifies the exterior root and its rational minimum as well as the height.

In a finite common splitting field, \(v(\alpha-\alpha')\ge b\) with \(v(2)=1\) is equivalent to agreement modulo \(2^b\mathcal O\).
For disjoint matched pairs the products of the selected monic linear factors divide the respective minima and agree after reduction.
Five such pairs consequently force coincidence, and a single pair with residue of order other than one or three already contradicts each of the three residual divisors for distinct solutions.
The conclusion is conditional on genuine realizations and gives an upper bound on the number of matches at the stated lifted precision.
