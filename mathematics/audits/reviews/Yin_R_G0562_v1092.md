## Oriented conjugates, height product and Salem transfers

At 2026-11-24T21:20 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v35.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v35.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v35.
The conjugate list, degrees and measure in (1), the height bound and its consequences in (2), and the Salem identity and infimum comparison in (3) and the statement all follow.

Since \(L=F(i)\) has relative degree two, each embedding \(s\) has a unique extension sending \(i\) to \(i\).
Its value \(u_s\) and the value of the other extension are reciprocals.
Consequently \(g(u_s)=u_{gs}^{\epsilon(g)}\) with a sign independent of \(s\).
Complex conjugation gives \(u_{\bar s}=1/\overline{u_s}\), so every real label is neutral.
The one complex pair must be nonneutral by positive total measure, and gives \(u_a/u_b=|u_a|^2=U>1\) after orienting \(a\) outward.
The two lifts at every label account for \(\log U=m(\beta)\) and the single-pair formula \(D=U+U^{-1}-2\).

Conjugating complex conjugation gives the edge transpositions, each with negative sign on \(i\).
The edge transpositions generate the symmetric group on the vertices of each connected component in the permutation action, acting trivially on other components.
The edge orbit therefore contains every pair in each component, and each pair has a transposition lift conjugate to complex conjugation.
The normal subgroup \(N\) generated by those lifts has precisely these components as its orbits on labels.
An element of \(M\) has every conjugate real exactly when every conjugate of complex conjugation fixes it.
This gives \(F_0=F\cap M^N\), and the inclusion label with stabilizer \(H\) has orbit size \([NH:H]=[F:F_0]=\ell\).
Transitivity gives that size for all components.
Each has at least two labels, and \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\subset F_0\) gives at least two components and \(\ell\le d/2\).

An edge image provides one orientation of its ratio as a conjugate of \(U\).
A transposition lift from another component fixes both target labels but acts with negative sign, supplying the inverse orientation.
Conversely every image of \(U\) is an oriented ratio from an edge, proving all these ratios are nontorsion before invoking any relation calculation.
If an exponent vector gives a torsion monomial in the \(u_j\), comparison with its image under a within-component transposition, after taking the common sign into account, forces its two exchanged exponents to agree.
Conversely the product on each whole component is a unit and all its conjugates have modulus one, since the unique opposite pair of nonzero logarithms belongs to one component.
Kronecker's theorem makes each component product torsion.
The integral relation vectors modulo torsion are therefore exactly those constant on each component.
The difference of two oriented-edge exponent vectors sums to zero separately on each component, so it can be constant there only if it vanishes.
Distinct ordered pairs consequently give distinct ratios even modulo torsion, proving the degree \(d(\ell-1)\) of \(U\).

The two reciprocal roots over \(D\) are distinct and conjugate, giving the factor two in its degree.
The stabilizer of \(D\) is exactly the stabilizer of \(\{a,b\}\), by distinctness of those ratios.
It is contained in the block stabilizer \(NG_a\), whose fixed field is \(a(F)\cap M^N=a(F_0)\).
Thus \(\mathbb Q(D)\) contains \(a(\sqrt3)=\pm\sqrt3\), and equals \(a(F_0)\) when the block consists only of the two endpoints.
Only \(u_a/u_b\) and its inverse give the extreme moduli.
Each of the \(\ell-2\) neutral vertices in that block supplies two oriented ratios of modulus \(U^{1/2}\) and two of modulus \(U^{-1/2}\).
Every remaining ratio has modulus one, giving \(m(U)=(\ell-1)\log U\).
For \(\ell=2\) there are circle conjugates from the other block or blocks, the degree is \(d\ge4\), and \(U\) is Salem with trace field \(\mathbb Q(U+U^{-1})=\mathbb Q(D)\).

For \(\ell\ge3\) a third vertex in the component with exponent zero shows by the relation criterion that \(u_j,u_k\) are multiplicatively independent.
Their joint field degree counts conjugates of the ordered tuple and is at most \(2d(\ell-1)\), including the two possible common signs.
Each coordinate has absolute height \(m(\beta)/(2d)\) by the degree of \(L\).
Amoroso–David's Theorem 1.6 applies to this rank-two product of absolute heights with the joint degree over \(\mathbb Q\).
Its exponent is \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\), and taking the square root after substituting the joint-degree upper bound gives (2) with logarithm \(\log(6d(\ell-1))\) to the power \(106\).
The constant is absolute at this fixed rank.
For bounded \(\ell\ge3\) the bound grows at large \(d\), and bounded degrees admit a positive gap.
In the general branch multiplication by \(\ell-1\) gives growth of \(m(U)\) at least of order \(\sqrt{d(\ell-1)}/(\log(6d^2))^{106}\), which tends to infinity.
If \(m(\beta)\to0\), bounded-degree finiteness gives \(d\to\infty\) and the bounded-block gap gives \(\ell\to\infty\).
Squaring (2) and using \(k=d(\ell-1)/2\) then gives \(k(\log(6d^2))^{212}/d^2\to\infty\).

For the Salem input every positive power generates the original field by its unique exterior conjugate, so the twelfth-root construction can use \(\delta=\alpha\) itself at every measure.
Compatibility of inversion follows at a circle embedding as in the cited Salem specialization.
All off-circle conjugates of \(w\alpha\) have modulus \(\alpha\) or \(\alpha^{-1}\), so for both possible signatures \(D=r(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})^2=r(t_0^2-4)\).
Only \(t_0>2\) occurs outside \((-2,2)\) among the trace conjugates.
Hence \(-t_0\) is absent and fixing \(t_0^2\) forces \(t_0\) fixed, giving \(\mathbb Q(D)=\mathbb Q(t_0)\).
The product of \(t_i^2-4\) over its \(n\) embeddings equals \(R(2)R(-2)=(-1)^nP(1)P(-1)\), proving the asserted absolute norm and interval for the other \(D\)-conjugates.
When \(r=1\), \(U=\alpha^2\) is Salem and the absence of intermediate exterior moduli forces \(\ell=2\).
If the Salem trace field contains \(\sqrt3\), the real field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) meets \(\mathbb Q(w)\) in precisely \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
The degree multiplier is two and the embedding count gives \(r=1\), so \(m(\beta)=2\log\alpha\) with \(\ell=2\).
Conversely each single-pair input with \(\ell=2\) gives the Salem \(U\) of trace field containing \(\sqrt3\) and \(\log U=m(\beta)\).
The two infimum inequalities follow in the stated directions, also with the empty-family convention.
The quantitative bound leaves the unrestricted Lehmer problem open on the moving-block branch.
