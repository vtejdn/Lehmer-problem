### Repeated roots and the small characteristics

Differentiating \(QB_h=Z^{60h}-1\) and \(P_h=1+aB_h\) gives
\[
 QP_h'=B_h(a'Q-aQ')+60h\,aZ^{60h-1}.
\]
Direct expansion gives exactly \(W=Z(2Z^6+3Z^5-5Z^3+3Z+2)\).
For \(p\mid h\), the last derivative term vanishes.
At any root of \(\bar P_h\), the constant coefficient excludes zero, the geometric sum excludes every root of \(Q\), and \(aB_h=-1\) makes both factors nonzero.
Thus every repeated root must be a nonzero root of \(\bar W\).
The coefficient gcd of \(W\) is one, so its reduction is never the zero polynomial.
Removing its zero-root factor and normalizing gives a nonconstant monic \(w_p\) of degree at most six and unit constant coefficient.
The congruence \(P_h\equiv1\pmod{(p,G_p)}\) excludes every root of \(w_p\), even when \(w_p\) itself is not squarefree.
This proves squarefreeness of \(\bar P_h\) and hence nondivisibility of its monic polynomial discriminant by \(p\).

The three displayed reductions of \(W\) at two, three and five are exact.
At two and three, both \(60h\) and \(12h\) vanish for every \(h\), so the same exclusion of \(Q\)-roots applies without imposing \(p\mid h\).
The remaining candidates at two have order three, hence \(z^{60h}=1\) and \(B_h(z)=0\).
At three the sole remaining candidate is \(-1\), where the integral polynomial has value one.
At five, \(60h=0\) for every \(h\), while both \(Q\) and the nonzero part of \(W\) have only the root one.
Thus a repeated root is possible only at one, where \(P_h(1)=1-12h\) vanishes precisely when \(h\equiv3\pmod5\).
Differentiating reciprocity gives \(2P_h'(1)=60hP_h(1)\), and the corresponding integral identity \(P_h'(1)=30hP_h(1)\) proves that this root is repeated.
The criterion at five is therefore both necessary and sufficient.

### Exact signed discriminants

The identities \(a=Z^2f\), \(a-Q=f^*\), and \(V=W/Z\) give
\[
 Lff^*-V
   =Z^3\bigl(-(L+2)T^3-(L+3)T^2+(4L+6)T+5L+11\bigr),
 \qquad T=Z+Z^{-1}.
\]
The coefficients of the displayed cubic are correct.
At a root \(z\) of \(P_h\), neither \(a(z)\) nor \(Q(z)\) vanishes.
The first exclusion follows from \(P_h=1+aB_h\); the second follows from the nonzero endpoint and fifth-order values in C286.
Using \(z^L=f^*(z)/a(z)\) in the derivative identity gives exactly
\[
                  P_h'(z)=\frac{zH_L(z)}{a(z)Q(z)}.
\]
Taking products over the roots is legitimate because the established Salem minimal polynomial is separable.
Its even degree and constant coefficient one give \(\operatorname{Res}(P_h,Z)=1\).
Also \(P_h\) is one at every root of the monic cubic \(f\), so \(\operatorname{Res}(P_h,a)=1\), with no sign change on reversing the cubic resultant.
For \(Q\), evaluation at one contributes \(1-12h\).
The two primitive-fifth-root values are \(1+12h(3\pm\sqrt5)/2\), each with multiplicity two, giving the remaining factor \((144h^2+36h+1)^2\).
The resultant sign from exchanging degrees \(60h\) and five is positive, as is \((-1)^{60h(60h-1)/2}\) in the derivative formula for the discriminant.
This proves the first expression in (8), including its sign.

For the trace expression, pair each root \(z\) with \(z^{-1}\).
Their \(Z^3\) factors multiply to one, and their common trace is one root of the monic \(S_h\).
The resulting product is exactly \(\operatorname{Res}(S_h,C_L)^2\), without an omitted leading coefficient.
This square is nonzero, while the denominator has the sign of \(1-12h<0\).
Thus the signed discriminant is negative for every \(h\ge1\).
At \(p\mid h\), the reduction \(H_L=-V\) also agrees with the repeated-root calculation.

### Simultaneous conditions, degree bounds and local splitting

In every quotient ring used in the claim, monicity gives the stated cardinality and the unit constant coefficient makes \(Z\) invertible.
The order of \(Z^5\) therefore exists and is bounded by that cardinality.
For a prime test, \(pe_p\mid h\) makes the number \(12h/e_p\) of identical geometric blocks divisible by \(p\), so their sum is zero.
For an additional test, \(A_ie_i\mid h\) similarly makes the number of blocks divisible by \(A_i\).
These conditions remain sufficient in quotient rings with nilpotents and for powers of a testing polynomial.
Taking their least common multiple gives every asserted simultaneous congruence, and the product bound gives exactly (3), with the empty least common multiple equal to one.

For dyadic \(B\), the prime-power terms in \(\psi(2n)-\psi(n)\) contribute to the valuation of \(\binom{2n}{n}\), so \(\psi(2n)-\psi(n)\le2n\log2\).
Summing dyadically gives \(\psi(B)\le2B\log2\), and hence \(\log H_0\le7\sum_{p\le B}\log p\le14B\log2\).
The direction of (4) implies that the stated lower guaranteed prime cutoff is at most \(B\), as required.
The periods are divisible by every prime up to \(B\), so they tend to infinity.
The fixed additional tests contribute only the asserted constant to the logarithmic degree bound.
Thus the degrees are unbounded and the previously established exterior roots tend to the plastic number.
Equivalently, the multiplicative Mahler measures tend to that number and the logarithmic measures tend to its positive logarithm.

Finally, a squarefree reduction splits into distinct roots over some finite residue-field extension.
The corresponding finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) lifts each root by the simple-root form of Hensel's lemma, so the full polynomial splits there.
The local splitting field is consequently a subextension of an unramified extension and is itself unramified.
The rational splitting field is Galois, and each completion above \(p\) has this property.
This proves the absolute splitting-field assertion without relying on a maximal-order index or confusing it with relative quadratic ramification.
