## Cosets, bounded equations and prime divisors

At 2026-11-24T19:09 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0903_v10.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0903 v10, including the clarified support bounds and reciprocal near-degree application.
The reviewer received the argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v10.
The stated bound counts exceptional primes in the entire tail with uniformity in the unit, the positions and the rational coefficients under the given hypotheses.

The \(e\) torsion-quotient classes of conjugates can be distinguished by a common power, so for each \(s\le k\le e\) one can fix \(s\) representatives before the graph or the prime is chosen.
For a fixed offset list, every such graph gives a determinant zero at \(v=pj\), including a graph supported in only one row.
The rational coefficient vector annihilates the matrix at the chosen conjugates, and distinct column pairs are distinct occupied exponents for \(p\ge2\).
Partition the \(s!\) summands into minimal vanishing subsums at a zero.
Each normalized block has a nondegenerate solution over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), and the tuple of character ratios lies in the image of a homomorphism from \(\mathbb Z^s\), of rank at most \(s\).
The fixed coefficient ratios enter the equation coefficients and do not enlarge this rank.
The inspected statement of Amoroso and Viada's Theorem 6.2 bounds the number of solution tuples in each block by \(C_t\), with at most one solution in the one-variable case.
For prescribed tuples the simultaneous inverse image, when nonempty, is one translate of the common kernel \(L\), all of whose points annihilate the determinant.
The bounds \(t^t\) for partitions, \(C_t^t\) for choices of tuples, and \(k2^k\) for the lengths and label lists suffice independently of support positions.

If an actual support on one translate had \(j\in L\otimes\mathbb Q\), some positive integer \(b\) would give \(bj\in L\), and all \(D((p+nb)j)\) would vanish.
Order the nodes by distinct values of an integer functional on their group modulo torsion.
Sorted levels maximize the functional on the recurrence bases, and equal-level slots come in disjoint pairs with offsets \(0,1\).
Each tied pair contributes a factor equal up to sign to the difference of its two nodes, so the unique maximizing base has nonzero coefficient.
Its ratio to each lower-weight base has infinite order, and its \(b\)-th power therefore stays separate on the progression.
Independence of the remaining distinct exponential bases after grouping rules out identically vanishing values there.
Thus the affine rational span of each coset representing an actual support misses the origin.

For a character ratio, its \(s\) coordinate bases are units in a field of degree at most \(d^s\).
The product formula identifies the specified normalized sum of absolute logarithms over all embeddings into \(\mathbb C\) with absolute logarithmic height.
Every generator has height at most two and the kernel on units is torsion.
A nonzero difference of log vectors represents a nontorsion unit of degree \(D\le d^s\), whose height is at least \((\log2)/(4D^2)\) by the house bound.
In dimension \(l>0\), maximal absolute determinant bounds all generator coordinates by one in absolute value in the selected basis.
Each coset of the basis subgroup has a representative in the centered parallelepiped of norm at most \(l\), as every basis element has norm at most two.
Packing with the stated separation \(\eta\) gives finite index at most \((1+2l/\eta)^l\le I_k(d)\).
Multiplication by that index makes every coordinate integral, hence the resulting equations have integer coefficients bounded by \(I_k(d)\).
The case \(l=0\) imposes no condition on the rational span.
Stacking the equations for the ratios gives exactly \(L\otimes\mathbb Q\), since any integral solution maps to torsion in each ratio and a common multiple kills all these torsion values.

Since an actual coset has affine span missing zero, one row of this matrix takes a fixed nonzero integer value \(c\) throughout the coset.
Every represented prime divides \(c\).
Using the least represented prime \(p_0\ge X\) with positions in \([0,B]\) gives \(|c|\le sI_k(d)Bp_0\), while \(h\) distinct represented primes have product at least \(p_0^h\).
This gives the claimed logarithmic factor in (1) without requiring an upper bound on \(p_0\).
Summing over cosets yields the desired count even with overlaps.

For \(B\le Kd^a,\ X=d^\delta\), the logarithmic factor is bounded for fixed \(k,K,a,\delta\).
For the growing-\(k\) assertion, \(\log N_k\le\exp(O(k\log(2k)))=(\log d)^{o(1)}\), and the logarithmic factor contributes at most \(O_{K,a,\delta}(1+k^2)\).
The resulting \(d^{o(1)}\) exceptions have vanishing relative density uniformly in every \([X,2X]\) above \(d^\delta\), by the prime number theorem and monotonicity of \(X/\log X\) there for large \(d\).
On a shrinking reciprocal unit sequence the power-field norm bound gives \(e\to\infty\), allowing a slowly growing \(k\le e\).
The near-degree witnesses have width at most \(2d\) eventually.
Thus at almost every prime in \([d,2d]\) every such witness has increasing support.
This use of the count concerns the specified width and support bounds, with no coefficient cutoff or largest-prime bound.
