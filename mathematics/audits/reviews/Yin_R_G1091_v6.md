## Obligations and decisive checks

### Exact certificate domain, positivity and the excluded circle roots

Reciprocity and real coefficients make p real on the circle, and a positive-multiplier SOS makes it nonnegative.
At a circle zero alpha of P, the nonnegative summands would all vanish.
Each nonzero integer v_j has degree at most d, strictly less than the minimal-polynomial degree 2d of alpha.
It therefore cannot vanish there.
This proves strict positivity and absence of circle roots for both IR2 and IR7, without an unproved assertion about positivity of arbitrary original inputs.

A one-summand certificate would give a nonzero scalar multiple of P as the product of v_1 and its reversal padded to degree d.
Both factors have degree at most d<2d.
Irreducibility over Q makes that impossible, including when a factor has smaller actual degree.
Zero summands can be deleted without altering any hypothesis or cost.

The exclusion of irreducible Salem inputs follows from their unit-circle conjugates.
It limits this certificate class and supplies no contradiction to the original conjecture.
The sign epsilon is genuinely needed: for z^2-3z+1 the balanced value is 2 cos(theta)-3, which is negative.
The proof does not silently require epsilon=+1.

### The integral reflection and every original pole

With v=sum_k a_k z^k, the exponent in every entry of z^d v(z)v(z^(-1))^T is d+k-l>=0.
Thus A is an integer polynomial matrix.
The constant-one polynomial P has an integer formal inverse by coefficient recursion, proving integrality of the Taylor germ W.

The coefficient at lag d in IR2 gives a_d^T a_0=2 epsilon.
The constant coefficient of A is a_0 a_d^T, so
\[
 W_0=I-\varepsilon a_0a_d^T,\qquad W_0^2=I,\qquad \det W_0=-1.
\]
The full certificate gives v^star v=2 epsilon z^(-d)P.
Consequently A^2=2 epsilon P A, proving W^2=I as a rational identity.
The rank-one determinant formula gives det W=-1.
This makes both the germ and its constant value invertible without assuming orthogonality.

On the circle the same coefficient simplifies W to I-2vv*/(v*v).
Strict positivity makes the denominator nonzero, and the elementary rank-one projection identity makes this matrix Hermitian and unitary.

Characteristic-zero irreducibility gives simple roots of P, and P(0)=1 excludes zero.
At every root alpha, the displayed residue is a nonzero scalar times v(alpha)v(alpha^(-1))^T.
No nonzero component polynomial can vanish at alpha or alpha^(-1), because both have minimal polynomial P and each component has smaller degree.
Thus the residue is nonzero of rank one, proving a genuine simple pole at every original root.

For an analytic column h, the only negative coefficient of Wh is R_alpha h(alpha)/(z-alpha).
Therefore its local cancellation module is exactly the kernel of the onto map to the one-dimensional image of R_alpha.
The quotient dimension is one.
This checks the actual pole-module multiplicity directly, rather than counting copied scalar denominators or using the constant determinant to erase poles.

There are no other finite poles.
For a monic polynomial with constant term one, the sum of log absolute values of all roots is zero.
The total root formula then gives
\[
 N(W)=-\sum_{|\alpha|<1}\log|\alpha|
     =\sum_{|\alpha|>1}\log|\alpha|=m(P).
\]
All roots and their actual multiplicities are retained; no division by 2d or r occurs.
The genuine poles also establish nonconstancy.

### Both constant-involution alternatives

\[
 I+C_N^*C_N/4\le Q_0^{-*}Q_0^{-1}.

### Exact multiplier and common-content gate

For each nonzero v_j, padded reversal preserves its positive coefficient content h_j.
Prime by prime, multiplication of two primitive reductions gives a nonzero polynomial over the residue field.
Thus content(v_i v_j^{R,d})=h_i h_j.

The gcd of all h_i h_j is g^2.
Divisibility by g^2 is immediate, and the gcd of the included diagonal products h_i^2 is already g^2.
Therefore the gcd of all coefficients of all A_ij is exactly g^2.

The balanced polynomial p is primitive, since its extreme coefficients are epsilon.
The coefficients of cp have gcd c.
Writing v_j=g u_j in the certificate forces g^2 to divide c, so k=c/g^2 is a positive integer.

Because P and its inverse at zero are integral power series, an entry (2 epsilon/c)A_ij/P is an integer germ if and only if (2/c)A_ij is an integer polynomial.
Multiplication by P proves necessity as well as sufficiency.
Subtracting from I does not weaken this criterion.

Thus W_c is integral exactly when c divides twice every coefficient of every A_ij, equivalently c divides 2g^2.
Together with g^2|c, this is precisely k in {1,2}.
Removing the common content leaves an actual certificate kp=sum_j u_j u_j^star with unchanged degree bounds.
For k=1, duplicating the rows gives the required multiplier-two certificate.
This is the exact gate for this reflection formula, with no conclusion against unrelated matrix constructions using larger multipliers.
