# Coefficient embeddings, conjugate radii and height in a critical four-term relation

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:39 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the submitted [C345 v759](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v759.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the claim and received the fixed argument without its development discussion or a prior verdict.
The review covered the uniform estimates along varying finite-list data, all original embeddings, the local balance and the first-moment limit.

At an embedding of exterior modulus \(e^L\), the leading monomial in the exact four-term relation is separated from the exponent \(b\) by \(a\), and all coefficients and their conjugates have bounded lengths and positive lower bounds on their nonzero lengths.
The triangle inequality consequently bounds \(aL\).
For a reciprocal algebraic unit the \(d\) absolute logarithmic radii sum to \(2m\), with largest equal to \(L\).
The [raw square-logarithm inequality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(1/10\le d\sum s_i^2\le2dmL\), so \(a\ll dm\) and \(a h\ll m^2\).
If \(b h\to0\) were possible on a subsequence, dividing the nonzero four-term relation by its coefficient norm would give a subcritical exact test on all \(d/e\) embeddings fixing the coefficients.
The [subcritical zero bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#statement) excludes their positive limiting fraction, since the degrees \(e\) in the list are bounded.
This proves a uniform lower bound on \(bh\) by the same subsequence argument, and hence the two bounds on \(b/a\) and \(bL\).
At each largest root all possible \(X\)-values stay bounded by \(e^{aL}\), so the exact equation \(Y(X-\sigma\lambda)=\sigma\gamma(X-\sigma\mu)\) gives \(O(e^{-bL})\) for the indicated distance.
If \(|\sigma\lambda|=1\) this bounds \(e^{aL}-1\ge aL\), giving the strengthened estimate on \(a\).

A neutral embedding identifies complex conjugation with the root-field involution on the generator \(\alpha\), and therefore on all its rational expressions.
It must restrict to a compatible embedding of \(E\).
At such an embedding \(\tau\), the zero and pole of \(R^\tau\) are reciprocal conjugates, distinct and outside the circle itself, and \(|\tau\gamma|=|\tau\lambda|\).
This last equality follows from \(|\tau\nu|=1\).
The map takes the circle onto the circle and \(|\log|R^\tau(z)||\le C_\tau|\log|z||\) on an annulus, by bounded radial derivative there.
For any image of \(X\) in the annulus, the exact radial equality \(\log|Y|=(b/a)\log|X|\) forces neutrality once \(b/a>C_\tau\).
With a common smaller annulus over the finite list, the number of original embeddings outside it is \(O(dah)\) from the sum of absolute logarithmic radii of \(X\).
The number of missing neutral embeddings over the compatible places is therefore \(O(dm^2)\), giving (1) with the correct sign and uniform constant.
Finiteness of \(j/e\) gives both density consequences.
When \(j=e\), every embedding of the involution-fixed subfield extends to an embedding of \(E\) and is real by compatibility.
If the involution is nontrivial, a real embedding of \(E\) would force it to fix all elements, so \(E\) is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of that totally real fixed field.

Conversely, if the fractional linear map preserves the unit circle, reflection across it interchanges its unique pole and zero, and neither can belong to that circle.
On the circle the ratio \(|z-1/\overline{\tau\lambda}|/|z-\tau\lambda|\) equals \(1/|\tau\lambda|\).
Preservation thus gives both \(\tau(\iota\lambda)=\overline{\tau\lambda}\) and \(|\tau\nu|=1\), implying compatibility on \(\lambda,\iota\lambda,\nu\) and hence on all of \(E\).
At an incompatible place the two generalized circles have at most two intersections, a null set for the pushed-forward angular probability because the map is nonconstant and invertible.
For each fixed coefficient embedding the conjugates of \(X\) in its extension block constitute its complete relative orbit with each point repeated \([K:E(X)]\) times.
Dividing by the block size cancels this multiplicity exactly, also when the power lowers absolute degree.
The [fixed-field equidistribution theorem](../literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution) applies since these nontorsion \(X\)'s have height tending to zero and a sequence of pairwise distinct points can be extracted from each subsequence by Northcott.
Continuity on the sphere gives the pushforward law.
The exterior boundary has zero limiting probability at an incompatible place, while the established compatible-block count bounds each compatible exterior fraction by \(O(e m^2)\).

At a finite place \(w\) the absolute values of \(A(X),B(X)\) coincide at all extending places since \(Y=A(X)/B(X)\) is a unit.
If their coefficient maxima differ, normalize the larger by a maximal coefficient and reduce it.
The resulting nonzero polynomial, with its monomial factor removed, divides \(Z^N-1\) for some fixed \(N\) over the finite residue field, allowing characteristic multiplicities.
Lifting the divisibility and using \(|X|=1\) at every extension gives \(|X^N-1|\le\max(|\pi|,G_{\rm smaller}/G_{\rm larger})<1\).
The normalized weights of all extensions sum to the fixed positive weight of \(w\).
Thus the [finite-place cyclic norm](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) would have a fixed positive lower bound, contradicting \(h(X^N)\to0\).
The same reasoning applies whichever maximum was larger.
Only finitely many finite places can violate (3) at fixed data, giving the required simultaneous conclusion for the surviving coefficient choices.

The [fixed-map height formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#height) uses the maximum of both coefficient lists at finite places, which by (3) equals \(\max(1,|\lambda|_w)\).
At infinity its integrand decomposes almost everywhere into \(\log|z-\tau\lambda|+\log^+|R^\tau(z)|\).
Each summand is integrable also at poles on the circle, and the mean of the first is \(\log^+|\tau\lambda|\).
Their normalized totals give \(h(\lambda)+A_R\), positive by the preceding lower bound on \(bh\), without separately requiring positive height of the pole.
The first moment of \(\log^+|Y|\) over the original embeddings equals \(bh\) because \(Y\) is a unit.
Clipping this observable at any \(U>0\) gives a continuous function on the sphere.
The limits of the clipped means come from the block laws, and their limit as \(U\to\infty\) is \(A_R\) by monotone convergence.
Subtracting establishes the stated tail contribution \(h(\lambda)\).
Finally the [complex graph radical bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#statement) applies with its specified equality of power fields, the finite collection of maps and diverging slopes.
