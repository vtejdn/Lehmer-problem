# Review of exceptional measures in the divisible group of one unit

<a id="finding"></a>
## The finite torsion-difference description and the attained minimum

At 2026-11-24T20:18 an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the complete [fixed-unit claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v20.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed argument and scope without development dialogue or earlier conclusions on the claim.
The finite list of measures, its characterization of nonemptiness, and the preservation of its minimum at arbitrarily large degrees under every indicated finite torsion test all hold.

Each element of \(K(\mu_\infty)\) belongs to some \(K(\mu_N)\), a finite Galois extension of \(K\) with abelian group by restriction to \(\mathbb Q(\mu_N)\).
The intermediate extension generated by this element is therefore abelian.
At \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), Amoroso–Zannier's Theorem 1.2 supplies \(h(x)>3^{-d^2-2d-6}\) for nontorsion \(x\) in each such extension.
For \(x^t=\alpha\), \(h(x)=h(\alpha)/t\), so the eligible \(t\) form a bounded nonempty set of positive integers.
Any further prime root of the selected \(\delta\) in the same field would contradict maximality.
Choosing another \(t\)-th root \(\delta'=\omega\delta\) gives \(T_{\delta'}=\omega T_\delta\), preserving all the ratios. [Author postprint, Theorem 1.2](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/5b05af7c-a919-4761-8619-e3353b682320/AbelianoLMS2.pdf)

Reducing the exponents in a defining relation for \(\beta\) gives \(\beta^s=\xi\delta^r\) with \((r,s)=1\), since a root of a root of unity is again torsion.
Over \(L\) the splitting field of \(X^s-w\) is \(L(\beta)\).
Its group injects in \(\mu_s\) and fixes \(\beta^D\) if its order is \(D\).
If \(k=s/D>1\), then \(w\) and \(\xi\) are both \(k\)-th powers in \(L\), hence so is \(\delta^r\).
An integer identity \(Ar+Bk=1\) would make \(\delta=(\delta^r)^A(\delta^B)^k\) a \(k\)-th power, impossible by maximality.
Thus the relative degree over \(L\) equals \(s\).
Over \(\mathbb Q(w)\subset L\) the degree is at least this large by base change and at most \(s\) by the binomial.
The tower law and \(h(w)=s h(\beta)\) give exact equality of total measures.

For each embedding \(\sigma\) of \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\delta)\), the extensions to \(K_0(\eta)\) run through a full orbit of size \(D_\eta\) over \(\sigma K_0\).
The image of \(\eta\) always has its original order, even if the initial extension of \(\sigma\) varies with that order.
When \(|\sigma\delta|\ne1\), weak equidistribution and Jensen yield the limiting mean \(\log^+|\sigma\delta|\).
On the circle, nontorsion excludes a zero distance, and the archimedean step of Baker–Ih–Rumely uses quantitative angular equidistribution and Baker's estimate to control the logarithmic singularity.
The local average there uses the ordinary complex modulus and denominator \(D_\eta\).
This step applies to arbitrary roots of unity of increasing order, independently of the finite-place restrictions in their global integrality theorem. [Publisher's article, pp. 220–225, especially (6)–(9)](https://msp.org/ant/2008/2-2/ant-v2-n2-p06-p.pdf)
Summing over all embeddings of \(K_0\), counting a complex pair twice, gives \(D_\eta^{-1}\log|N_{K_0(\eta)/\mathbb Q}(\delta-\eta)|\to m(\delta)>0\).
An algebraic unit has norm of absolute value one, so only finitely many of these torsion parameters qualify.
Both external height and orbit results were checked at the stated input scope.
The finiteness conclusion concerns the fixed input and supplies no numerical conductor cutoff.

Taking all \(s\) conjugates over \(L\) gives \(\prod_{\omega\in\mu_s}(1-\omega\beta)=1-w\).
Each factor is an algebraic integer and is a unit whenever their product is a unit, while conjugacy preserves unit status in the other direction.
For \(r>0\), the factors of \(1-\xi\delta^r\) differ by an overall torsion unit from the product of \(\delta-\eta\) over \(\eta^r=\xi^{-1}\).
For \(r=-u<0\), multiplication by \(\delta^u\) gives the product over \(\eta^u=\xi\).
Thus \(|r|\) distinct roots of unity must all lie in the fixed finite set, bounding \(|r|\) and permitting only finitely many \(\xi\) for each \(r\).
Conversely the corresponding \(w\) belongs to the divisible group since \(w^t\) is a torsion multiple of \(\alpha^r\).
It realizes each successful measure.
For an eligible \(\eta\), the unit \(v=\delta/\eta\) is exceptional.
In a successful factorization \(v^{|r|}=w^{\pm1}\), so \([\mathbb Q(v):\mathbb Q(v^{|r|})]\le |r|\) gives \(m(v)\le m(w)\) in both sign cases.
Every eligible \(v\) itself belongs to the group, proving the asserted minimum when the set is nonempty.

For a minimizer \(v\), each root \(b^N=v\) has degree \(N\) over \(\mathbb Q(v)\) by the preceding full-orbit calculation with numerator exponent one.
Thus \([\mathbb Q(b):\mathbb Q]=N[\mathbb Q(v):\mathbb Q]\), \(m(b)=m(v)\), and \(b\) remains in the same divisible group.
The product of all \(b-\zeta\) for \(\zeta^N=1\) equals the unit \(v-1\), so every prescribed difference is a unit when \(N\) is a multiple of the specified orders.
Such \(N\) are unbounded.
Inclusion of the test at \(1\) guarantees the reverse bound for the minimum, as well as emptiness of the restricted set if \(T_\delta\) is empty.
