## Sparse initial segments, consecutive exceptional intervals and the Mahler gap

At 2026-11-17T01:13 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the fixed C345v107 submission of 2026-11-17T01:08 UTC.
The reviewer did not participate in developing C345v107 and received the fixed claim without its development dialogue or previous findings for this version.
The reviewer previously contributed to the C345v90 integral estimate and checked its use at the stated interface.
All four claim sections pass, with the seven declared inputs applied at the scopes below.

Write \(M=\|L\|_1\) for the boundary norm before any one extraction.
For a sparse prefix below \(D\ge2\), the flat-top kernel with \(E=D/2\) retains every frequency through \(E\) and annihilates frequencies of absolute value at least \(D\), including \(D\) itself.
The high-index logarithms have no constant term and have their first nonzero Fourier frequency at their index.
Thus \(P=V_E L_{<D}\) has \(\|P\|_4\le 3C\sqrt w B\), while \(\|P\|_1\le3M\) follows from the whole input.
The inverse-matrix rows for occupied \(n\le E\) use only divisors \(t\le E\), where the Fourier coefficients of \(P\) and \(L\) agree.
The absolute row-sum estimate for the Gram matrix of these rows bounds \(B_0\) by a constant times \(\sqrt w\|P\|_2\).
Interpolation gives \(B_0\ll_w M^{1/3}B^{2/3}\), including the case of no occupied row.

For \(E<n<D\), the proper divisors \(j\) satisfy \(j\le E\), and the weights \(j/n\) are distinct reciprocals \(1/r\) with \(r\ge2\).
Their squared sum is bounded by \(\zeta(2)-1\).
The divisor identity at \(n\) and the bound \(|\widehat L(n)|\le M\) therefore control each last-band coefficient by \(2M+\sqrt{\zeta(2)-1}\,B_0\).
At most \(w\) such indices occur in the single dyadic interval \([E,D)\), with its endpoint \(E\) already included in \(B_0\).
This gives \(B\le C_w(M+M^{1/3}B^{2/3})\).
If \(B>2C_w M\), division yields \(B\le(2C_w)^3 M\).
The empty prefix at \(D=1\), the endpoint case \(D=2\), and \(M=0\) all obey the bound, the last also by triangular recovery of the coefficients.
An integer prefix has absolute mass at most \(B^2\), and its boundary norm is \(O_w(B)\).

For a run \([D,E)\) of \(\ell\le h\) dyadic intervals, \(K=2E/D=2^{\ell+1}\) is independent of their location.
The remaining boundary logarithm has no positive frequency below \(D\).
The cosine coefficient of the dilation sum \(F\) at \(0<t\le2E\) is \(2\sum_{j\mid t,\,j\le K}(\mu(j)/j)\widehat L(t/j)\).
All omitted terms have \(t/j<D\), so inversion gives \(-b_t\) even if there are occupied indices immediately following the run.
The constant coefficient is zero, and integer dilation preserves the circle integral, giving \(\|F\|_1\le H_K M\) where \(H_K=\sum_{j\le K}1/j\).
Consequently \(|b_t|\le2H_K M\) throughout the guard interval \([E,2E)\).
After filtering, the guard contribution costs at most \(2wH_K M\) in \(L^1\), because it has at most \(w\) cosine terms with multipliers between zero and one.
Frequency \(E\) belongs to the guard and has multiplier one.
Frequency \(2E\) has multiplier zero, regardless of the occupancy of the following interval.
Removing the guard and doubling the remainder gives coefficients \(\pm b_n\) at both \(n\) and \(-n\) for \(D\le n<E\), with a common overall sign.
The prime-grid bound applies to this integer-coefficient trigonometric polynomial and bounds twice the absolute exponent mass using only \(h,w,M\).

Removing a sparse prefix or a dense run increases the norm of the remainder by at most the bound on the norm of the part removed.
For the dense part the latter is at most its exponent mass times the fixed first absolute mean of a single binomial logarithm.
Each maximal run of exceptional intervals is followed by an interval of occupancy at most \(w\), possibly empty.
There are at most \(h\) runs, each of length at most \(h\), so iterating these norm and mass bounds retains dependence only on the initial \(M,h,w\).
On the final sparse tail the global lower norm bound controls the square norm of the remaining integer vector and hence its absolute mass.
An empty tail contributes zero, and \(h=0\) requires only the global bound.

The polynomial hypothesis gives unit leading coefficient in modulus, all roots on the circle and mean-zero boundary logarithm.
With \(|a|\ge1\), the circle-root integral bounds the shifted measure below by \(M/2-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\) uniformly in the phase and in \(k\), giving the claimed divergence.
For \(0<m(A+aX^k)\le1\) this also bounds \(S\).
Clearing the denominator binomials gives a nonzero integer polynomial with at most \(2^{S+1}\) terms and the same measure, since each binomial has measure one and a nonzero root of \(A\) cannot annihilate the shifted polynomial.
The bounded-term gap used in C345v106 thus applies to all nonzero integer scalars, with the range \(m>1\) already separated from zero.
Cyclotomic products admit signed integral binomial presentations, so a subsequence with boundedly many intervals of occupancy above a fixed \(w\) cannot have positive measures tending to zero.
A constant unit product also cannot occur infinitely often in such a sequence, since an integer monomial shift of a unit constant has zero logarithmic measure or logarithmic measure at least \(\log 2\) whenever it is nonzero.
For every rank \(r\ge1\), the \(r\)-th largest occupancy exceeds \(w\) exactly when at least \(r\) intervals do, which proves the stated equivalence.
