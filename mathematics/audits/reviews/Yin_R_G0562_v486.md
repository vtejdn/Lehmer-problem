## Independent finding on the representative class and its unrestricted equivalence

At 2026-11-18T20:13 UTC an agent outside the development of the [fixed v403 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#decision-fourth-logarithmic-representatives-submitted) independently checked the full statement and its three supporting sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the two-row small-multiple theorem and received the new argument in written form without its development discussion.
The irreducible representative and tower constructions, uniform assertions for every prime of the window, exact coefficient identity, cyclotomic supremum bounds, both simultaneous cofactor estimates and the unrestricted equivalence hold.
The converse sequence statement has the required large-degree hypothesis \(d_j\to\infty\).

### Producing representatives and preserving their full degree

For a fixed reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), every \(v\in K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) with \(v^n=\alpha\) is an algebraic integer unit of degree at most \(e\).
Every conjugate has modulus bounded by the greater of one and the largest conjugate modulus of \(\alpha\).
The elementary symmetric bounds give only finitely many possible monic integral minimal polynomials for \(v\), hence finitely many \(v\).
An individual \(v\) gives at most one positive exponent \(n\), since otherwise it would be torsion.
For a prime \(n\) outside this finite set, a proper monic factor of \(X^n-\alpha\) of degree \(t\) would give the product \(b\in K\) of its roots with \(b^n=\alpha^t\).
Writing \(tu+nv=1\) would make \((b^u\alpha^v)^n=\alpha\), a contradiction.
The full rational degree of an \(n\)-th root is therefore \(ne\), since its field contains \(\alpha\).
All \(n\)-th roots of a given conjugate together contribute that conjugate's original positive logarithmic modulus, yielding \(m(P_0(X^n))=h\).
Monicity, reciprocity and constant coefficient one are preserved.
For each fixed \(e\), the bound \(e\le\log(3ne)\) holds at all sufficiently large \(n\), so there are arbitrarily large members of the stated prime-exponent class for every seed.

If the \(p\)-th powers of distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\) coincide, their quotient is a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity.
Its \(p-1\) rational conjugates all occur among the quotients of distinct ordered conjugate pairs, a set of size at most \(e(e-1)\).
Consequently \(p>e(e-1)+1\) forces \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The field of \(\gamma^p\), where \(\gamma^n=\alpha\), then contains \(\alpha\) because it contains \((\gamma^p)^n=\alpha^p\).
When \((n,p)=1\) it also contains \(\gamma\) by Bézout applied to \(\gamma^p,\gamma^n\).
This proves full degree and identifies the monic powered minimum as \(P_{0,p}(X^n)\) by equality of degrees.
The degree-preserved powering of the seed gives \(m(P_{0,p})=p h\).
Uniformly for \(e\le\log(3d)\), the window lower endpoint eventually exceeds \(e(e-1)+1\), whereas its upper endpoint is at most \(2((\log(3d))^4+1)<d/\log(3d)\le n\).
No primes of the window are lost, and the dyadic prime bound ensures it is nonempty at large \(d\).

For the separate tower, fix an odd \(\ell\) with \(\alpha\notin K^\ell\).
At an irreducible stage \(X^{\ell^k}-\alpha\), the norm of its root to \(K\) is \(\alpha\), including the sign since \(\ell^k\) is odd.
If the root acquired an \(\ell\)-th root in its own field, the corresponding norm would give an \(\ell\)-th root of \(\alpha\) in \(K\).
The prime-degree binomial argument thus supplies degree \(\ell\) at the next step.
This proves the tower claim for all \(k\), with preserved measure and the same powered identity at \(p>e(e-1)+1,\ p\ne\ell\).
For the fixed seed and fixed \(\ell\), this includes the entire window eventually.

### Angular variation, residue decomposition and all cyclotomic orders

For a single root, reflection into the closed disk gives a centered logarithm of squared \(L^2\) norm at most \(\pi^2/12\), including roots on the circle.
The triangle inequality and Haar invariance under \(z\mapsto z^n\) give \(J(Q)=J(P_{0,p})\le e\pi/(2\sqrt{12})\), since the centered positive part integrates to half the \(L^1\) norm.
Inserting zero coefficients between the coefficients of \(P_{0,p}\) leaves the one-norm unchanged.
Factoring the seed minimum gives \(\|P_{0,p}\|_1\le\prod_\rho(1+|\rho|)\le2^e\exp(ph)\).

For the dilation identity for \(C_D\), first put \(D_0=\lfloor D/n\rfloor\).
If the quotients by \(G\) of a finite eligible family have gcd one, a rational polynomial Bézout identity between them remains an identity upon substituting \(X^n\).
The substituted family therefore has gcd exactly \(G(X^n)\), has raw degrees at most \(nD_0\le D\) and preserves coefficient norms.
In the reverse direction the residue components in \(H_i=\sum_{r<n}X^rH_{ir}(X^n)\) partition the original coefficient lists.
Writing \(H_i=G(X^n)U_i\) and decomposing \(U_i\) by the same residues gives \(G\mid H_{ir}\).
A proper common factor of the nonzero \(H_{ir}/G\) would lift to a nonconstant common factor of every \(U_i\), contradicting the exact gcd.
At least one component is nonzero, and each such component has raw degree at most \(D_0\) and norm at most the original maximum.
This proves equality, including for \(n=1\) and degree caps that are not multiples of \(n\).
In the unrestricted case each competitor still consists of finitely many polynomials of finite degree, so the same two operations preserve the unrestricted minimum as well.

For a monic \(H\) with no root of unity, take a convergent subsequence of the simultaneous powers of all its circle roots and thin it so consecutive index gaps \(t_k\) tend to infinity.
The coordinatewise ratios of consecutive powers tend to one.
With \(M_k=t_k+1\), every circle root \(z\) satisfies \(z^{M_k}\to z\ne1\), so its contribution to \(M_k^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(H,X^{M_k}-1)|\) tends to zero.
A root inside the circle contributes zero in the limit, and a root outside contributes its positive logarithmic modulus.
Factorization of \(X^{M_k}-1\) yields the claimed convex combination of \(A_j(H)\), since \(\sum_{j\mid M_k}\varphi(j)=M_k\).
All resultants are nonzero, also for \(j=1\).
This proves the lower bound for the supremum without a uniform convergence assumption on any individual order.
For each \(j\), any family with gcd \(H\) contains a member \(H U\) coprime to \(\Phi_j\).
Its quotient \(U\) is integral by monic division, and its nonzero resultant with \(\Phi_j\) has absolute value at least one.
Evaluation at the \(\varphi(j)\) primitive roots bounds \(A_j(H)\) above by the logarithm of this member's coefficient norm, independently of degree.
Taking the supremum and then minimizing gives \(\sup_j A_j(H)\le\log C_\infty(H)\).
At \(H=Q\), monotonicity of the budgets and eligibility of the singleton \(\{Q\}\) complete (2).

### Uniform size, two actual remainders and quantifiers

Voutier's bound applies to the nontorsion algebraic number \(\gamma\) of degree \(d\) and to \(h=m(P)\), the total logarithmic measure of its minimal polynomial.
It gives \(p h/\log(3d)\gg(\log\log d)^3\) uniformly throughout the window.
As \(e\le\log(3d)\) and \(\log(2dp)=O(\log(3d))\), both are \(o(ph)\), uniformly across the entire representative class.
The \(L^1\) estimate and additive \(e\log2\) budget error then give the claimed relative asymptotics even for varying seeds of small measure.

For either fixed sign at each such \(p\), the singleton \(\{Q\}\) meets the small-multiple condition with budget \(B=\log\|Q\|_1\le ph+e\log2\) and raw degree \(d\le2d\).
L462 v227 produces two primitive diagonals of that same sign with \(P^2\) dividing each and with their two rows coprime in each diagonal.
It bounds both actual noncyclotomic parts \(R_i\) of the respective \(F_i/P^2\), with multiplicities, by \(O(ph+\log(2dp)+B)=O(ph)\).
It also gives the individual trace coefficient logarithms, squarefree first-trace measures, normalized individual remainder and evaluation norms, and the normalized joint determinant norm, all \(O(ph+\log(2dp))=O(ph)\) on those same two diagonals.
Their nonzero evaluations, nonrational ratios of the two reduced traces on each row, nonzero derivative remainders and nonzero determinant persist.
Alternatively L462 v222 supplies the cofactor upper bound from \(J(Q)\ll e\).
Every eligible diagonal has exactly the two occupied classes \(0,1\), degree \(4pd+1\le p(16d+1)\), and coprime Laurent rows.
The minimum lower bound of C345 v363 thus gives \(m(R_i)/p\ge h/3\) for each actual diagonal, and for every other eligible one, without assuming it minimizes the cofactor.

If positive integral-polynomial measures tend to zero, eventually no nontrivial integer content or nonunit leading coefficient can occur, and a nonunit constant term after deleting powers of \(X\) would likewise cost at least \(\log2\).
Factoring and choosing a factor of positive measure produces monic nontorsion unit inputs with measures tending to zero.
Smyth's positive lower bound for nonreciprocal inputs makes these seeds reciprocal eventually.
Each seed admits a representative of arbitrarily large degree with its original measure, so one may choose \(d_j\to\infty\) while retaining \(h_j\to0\).
Inequality (2) gives the asserted normalized vanishing of \(J\), the coefficient budget and the supremum, uniformly at every window prime.
At every one of those primes, L462 v227 gives a pair for each fixed sign with all the stated costs \(o(p)\).
Conversely, if \(d_j\to\infty\) and there is a single window prime and one admissible pair per representative with \(\max_i m(R_i)/p\to0\), (3) forces \(h_j\to0\).

For the final equivalence, a positive unrestricted gap \(\eta\) supplies \(c=\eta/3\) at every window prime for every eligible positive-sign pair on all sufficiently large representatives.
If there is no gap, choose the representatives above from inputs with \(h_j\to0\).
For any proposed \(c>0\), all sufficiently late representatives then have, at **every** window prime, an eligible positive-sign pair with maximum \(m(R_i)/p\ll h_j<c\).
Thus no choice of one prime on a sufficiently late representative can enforce the proposed bound for every pair.
Admissible pairs exist at each prime, so the universal quantifier over pairs introduces no vacuous case.
The equivalence leaves the existence of an unrestricted gap open and only transfers the measure of each seed, with no comparison asserted at its original degree or original primes.
