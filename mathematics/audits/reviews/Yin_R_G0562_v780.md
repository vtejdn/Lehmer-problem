# Review of the two-ended energy bound for unrestricted integral numerators

## The short remainder, the two defect norms and the limiting minimum

At 2026-11-20T20:44 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently checked the full argument for [the unrestricted energy bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement) and the short-remainder estimate it uses.
The reviewer received the fixed materials without proof-development dialogue, author explanations or earlier findings about this claim.
The lower limit \(K=\tfrac52+\log 2-2\sqrt2\) and all three necessary conditions for approaching it follow.

For the short-remainder input let \(D\) be the least squared energy of a nonzero integral polynomial of degree below \(d\), and write \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\).
The lower bound by the ordinary squared coefficient norm divided by \(\max_{\mathbb T}|P|^2\) gives attainment of \(D\).
Removing an initial monomial preserves the energy.
At each subsequent stripping step the subtracted multiple of \(P\) has nonzero integral multiplier \(q_i\) and creates a polynomial of degree exactly \(d\) with zero constant.
Removing its initial power of \(X\) restores a nonzero constant and degree below \(d\).
If two sign-normalized remainders coincided, the earlier remainder multiplied by \(X^h\pm1\), with \(h>0\), would be divisible by \(P\).
Coprimality over \(\mathbb C\) forces the earlier remainder to vanish by the degree bound, including when \(P\) has repeated factors.
Thus \(N\) successive normalized remainders are pairwise at squared distance at least \(D\) in the weighted norm.

The reflected-polynomial evaluation gives squared norm \(C\) for the minimizing polynomial of constant one and orthogonality to every polynomial of constant zero in the same weighted space.
In particular \(Cq_i^2\le\|T_i/P\|_2^2\).
The stripped monomials have distinct exponents, so their squared norm through any of the first \(N-1\) steps is at most \((N-1)B^2/C\), where \(B=\max_{i<N}\|T_i/P\|_2\).
The triangle inequality then gives \(B^2\le D(1-\sqrt{(N-1)/C})^{-2}\) when \(N-1<C\).
The squared distance of each sign-normalized remainder to the constant-one minimizer is at most \(B^2-C\), since its constant is a positive integer.
Summing squared pair distances gives \(\binom N2 D\le N^2(B^2-C)\).
Taking \(N=\lfloor C^{1/3}\rfloor\) makes the denominator in the resulting lower bound for \(D/C\) equal to \(1/2+O(C^{-1/3})\).
Since \(mC\to1/4\), every nonzero short remainder in the claim satisfies \(\liminf m\|R/P\|_2^2\ge1/2\).

In balanced division, the unit constant and leading coefficients of \(P\) give an integral quotient, and indivisibility gives a nonzero short remainder.
The difference of the two support counts crosses zero in steps of at most two, so a cut with counts differing by at most one exists.
For \(Y=R/O\), analyticity and the strictly negative exterior expansion of \(R/O^*\) give \(Y\perp H^2_-\oplus JH^2\).
Reading the negative inputs from the left and the nonnegative inputs from the right respectively uses the analytic multiplier \(U\) and the coanalytic multiplier \(\bar V\).
At either end a prefix of \(i\) occupied inputs therefore gives a triangular contraction, with determinant of modulus \(t^i\), where \(t=e^{-m}\).
Every singular value is at most one, so the selected output norm is at least \(t^i\) times its input norm.
The two selected squared norms together fit inside \(\|\Pi Z\|_2^2\le E\) because the two projection spaces are orthogonal.

For \(a=\log(3/2)\) and \(\ell=\lfloor a/m\rfloor\), an index with \(n>\ell\) has at least \(\ell\) inputs on both sides.
The finite backward recursion for the weights stops at \(e_\ell=0\), and \(t^{\ell-1}>1/(1+t)\) for small \(m\).
Thus \(s e_i\le tc_i\) for the nonincreasing positive weights \(c_i=(1+t)t^{i-1}-1\), with \(s=\sqrt{1-t^2}\).
The telescoped row inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give squared energy at least \(\sum_{i\le\ell}c_i^2\) at each end for arbitrary integral input signs.
This argument uses only the observed finite contractions.
After multiplication by \(m\), the two sums tend to \(2a-4(1-e^{-a})^2\).
Its excess over \(K\) is \(\log(9/8)+2\sqrt2-53/18>2/17+707/250-53/18>0\).
Consequently any subsequence with \(mE\to L\le K\) eventually has \(mn\le a\).

Let \(z_i\) be the nonnegative square root of the cumulative input energy minus selected output energy through row \(i\).
The prefix determinant bound and the bound on the full quotient give \(z_i^2\le(t^{-2n}-1)E\), uniformly over both ends.
Since \(s^2/m\to2\) and \(K<2/5\), we obtain \(\limsup\max_i s^2z_i^2\le5K/2<1\).
Thus \(sz_{i-1}\le t|q_i|\) eventually, using \(t\to1\) and \(|q_i|\ge1\).
After rotating the diagonal to \(t\), maximizing the last-row contraction inequality over its free final input bounds the contribution of earlier inputs by \(s z_{i-1}\).
Squaring \(|y_i|\ge t|q_i|-s z_{i-1}\) now gives \(z_i^2\le(tz_{i-1}+s|q_i|)^2\).
Iteration uses \(|q_i|-1\le(|q_i|^2-1)/3\), valid for every positive integer modulus.
It gives the stated sum of a geometric term and one third of \(s\) times the excess squared coefficient mass at each end, also for zero rows.

The energy outside the two projections is bounded above by the sum of the final squared defects, because the selected outputs account for part of each projection.
Let \(x,B,r,w\) have the limiting meanings in the claim.
The determinant bound controls \(B\), the triangle inequality controls \(r\), and \(w\) is bounded by the scaled quotient norm.
Balance gives \(mn_\pm\to x\) and \(B\ge2x\).
After multiplication by \(\sqrt m\), the Euclidean norm of the pair of geometric terms tends to \(2(1-e^{-x})\).
The norm of the two nonnegative excess terms is bounded by their sum, with limit \((\sqrt2/3)(B-2x)\).
Independently, \(\|(1-\Pi)Z\|_2^2\le(t^{-2n}-1)E\) gives \(w^2\le5K/4<1/2\), the strict inequality needed in the final step.

Orthogonality gives \(L\ge r^2+B-2rw\).
Its right side strictly increases in \(r\ge1/\sqrt2\), and substituting this endpoint and the upper bound for \(w\) leaves
\[
 L\ge f(x)+(B-2x)/3,\qquad f(x)=\tfrac12+2x-2\sqrt2(1-e^{-x}).
\]
The bound \(K<2/5\) used above follows from \(\log2<7/10\) and \(\sqrt2>7/5\).
The unique zero of \(f'(x)=2-2\sqrt2 e^{-x}\) is \((\log2)/2\), inside the cutoff interval, and \(f''(x)>0\).
The minimum is \(K\).
Equality in the limit forces \(x=(\log2)/2,\ B=2x,\ r=1/\sqrt2\) by strict convexity, nonnegativity of the excess and strict increase in \(r\).
Boundedness gives these same limits for every balanced choice by subsequence extraction.
A cut with empty quotient satisfies the short-remainder bound directly.
Existence of a sequence approaching \(K\) remains open.
