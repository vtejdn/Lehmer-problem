### The clipped comparison and the independent tail

For each fixed fast angle, \(|F_N|^2\) is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(2d\) in the slow angle.
The squares of the real coordinates defining \(A\) have the same degree bound.
The changes of maximizer, crossings of the clipping level, and critical points of the active squared quotient are cut out by a fixed number of trigonometric equations of degree at most \(4d\).
Identical equations permit fixed tie breaking or describe constant pieces, and \(A>0\) on the circle.
This gives the claimed \(O(d\log(C/u))\) variation of the clipped logarithm without a coefficient-dependent scale.
For fixed \(u\), its Lipschitz constant in the fast angles is uniform since the relative coefficients and the finite exponent set are bounded and clipping bounds the logarithmic slope by \(1/u\).

Convolution in the fast angles with a product Fejér kernel therefore gives uniform error at most any prescribed \(\xi>0\), at an order depending on \(u,\xi\) and the fixed exponents.
It preserves the fast-angle mean.
The variation of each resulting slow Fourier coefficient is bounded by integrating the slow variation of the slices, because the Fejér multiplier has modulus at most one.
For this one finite frequency set \(S\), rational independence gives \(\min_{h\in S\setminus\{0\}}|h\cdot q_N|\gg_S N\) if a nonzero mode is present.
Periodic integration by parts against the derivative measure bounds each nonzero diagonal integral by \(O_S(d\log(C/u)/N)\).
Taking \(N\to\infty\) before reducing \(\xi\) proves clipped convergence without a uniform lower bound on all integer relations.

An integral direction outside the finitely many hyperplanes perpendicular to the \(a_i-a_j\) separates the \(k\) fast exponents.
Translation along this direction preserves Haar measure after averaging the starting point.
On such a line the relative coefficient vector is bounded, and at least one coefficient has modulus at least \(1/2\) because the real coordinates used in \(A\) are doubled.
The Vandermonde matrix for the fixed distinct line frequencies bounds one derivative of order \(0\) through \(k-1\) away from zero at every point.
On a sufficiently fine fixed subdivision a real or imaginary part of the selected derivative keeps its sign and a uniform lower bound, using a bound for the next derivative.
Order zero excludes small values directly, and the real divided-difference inequality at order \(h>0\) gives a sublevel bound \(O(w^{1/h})\le O(w^{1/(k-1)})\) for \(w\le1\).
Integrating in \(dw/w\) up to \(u\) proves the independent clipping estimate.

### The slow zero measure and intervals of length \(2\pi/N\)

The realification and random phase sums in v332 use only the \(k\) integral Laurent rows.
A common nonzero complex root would force a common factor over \(\mathbb Q\), and the realified coordinates recover the original rows on the complex cylinder.
Consequently the averaged zero measure and the Gaussian integral cross-resultant of v332 are available for the same realified coordinates.
To apply its energy bound, shift the fast exponents into a box of coordinate widths \(w_j\).
For each nonzero coefficient at fixed slow angle, univariate coefficient extraction gives a factor at most \(2^{w_j}\) at step \(j\).
Integrating successively gives the bound \(2^{\sum w_j}\exp(m_Y(F_N(e^{it},Y)))\), since the zeros of a nonzero intermediate Laurent coefficient form a Haar-null subset of the remaining torus.
Zero coefficients satisfy the bound directly.
Together with the fixed realification factors this gives \(\int\log A\le I_N+O(1)\).
The swept measure has mass at most \(2d\) and squared-moment energy \(O(d(1+I_N))\).
Thus the cover of v332 gives \(\sum r_i=O(1)\), \(M_i=dr_i\ge\delta\), \(\sum M_i^2=O(d(1+I_N))\), and \(O(M_i)\) zero mass on the disk of radius \(256r_i\).

The Green normalization is real on the diameter, keeps all real ratios and bounds the normalized coordinates on that disk.
Its real anchor and one orthogonal rotation give all denominators a common anchor of log modulus at least \(-CM_i\).
Since \(Nr_i=(N/d)M_i\to\infty\) uniformly, Cauchy's estimates still bound the numerator polynomials for the derivatives normalized by \(N^{-h}\) through order \(k-1\).
Choosing separated threshold levels at the anchor and applying Jensen costs \(O(M_i)\) crossings.
Between crossings a first violating derivative makes the preceding bounded quantity vary monotonically with slope of magnitude at least a constant times \(N\).
Each such interval has length \(O(1/N)\), so the number of flagged grid intervals inside the counting arc is \(O(M_i)\).
These arguments are independent of the periods of the exponential sum.

On an unflagged interval the chart ratios yield, after inverse rotation and combination into complex pairs, a coefficient vector of norm bounded below by \(1/2\) and bounded above by a constant.
Its positive derivatives in the fast variable through order \(k-1\) are bounded by a fixed multiple of the chosen tolerance.
Freezing this vector over an interval of length \(2\pi\) therefore changes the derivative of every order used by at most a constant times the tolerance.
The frozen Vandermonde determinant has absolute value \(\prod_{i<j}|\omega_{i,N}-\omega_{j,N}|\), uniformly bounded below, and its entries are uniformly bounded.
The initial phases multiply its coefficient coordinates by scalars of modulus one.
The frozen derivatives through order \(k\) are bounded, so the real derivative and sublevel estimates apply on each subdivision for small fixed tolerance.

In the disk of radius \(16l\), the second Green normalization thus gives an anchor with normalized diagonal log modulus at least \(-CM_i\).

If the rows are constant, the finite-derivative estimate applies on every grid interval directly.

With this bound fixed, each smaller fixed \(u\) gives a discrepancy of limiting upper size \(O(u^{1/(k-1)})\).
The final limit \(u\downarrow0\) proves the claimed convergence.

### Arithmetic and compactness

Dividing an integral separating direction by the gcd of its coordinates preserves separation and permits completion to a unimodular change of fast coordinates.
The chosen coordinate has positive fixed width \(R\), and each occupied power has as coefficient a single row times a Laurent monomial in the remaining fast variables.
A common base divisor of these coefficients has width zero in each remaining fast variable by width additivity and is therefore, up to a Laurent monomial, a divisor of all original rows.
Integer content is included in the gcd assumption.
The nonmonic partial-degree theorem consequently gives \(I_N\ge c_R>0\) in the presence of a nontorsion horizontal component.
In the complementary case its torsion factorization has a unit base factor.
Returning to the original fast coordinates keeps the fast character vector \(v\) of each factor nonzero.
Coordinate width additivity gives \(|b|\varphi(e)\le2d\) and \(|v_j|\varphi(e)\le w_j\) for \(\Phi_e(X^bY^v)\).
The finitely many possible \(v\ne0\) have \(|v\cdot q_N|\gg N\) uniformly, so \(|b|=o(N)\) excludes zero specialization exponents.
Every resulting cyclotomic composition has logarithmic measure zero, also for a negative exponent.
Hence a positive diagonal measure eventually requires \(I_N\ge c_R\), contradicting \(\mu_N\to0\) by the comparison.

The nonzero constant and leading coefficients ensure that a finite Hausdorff limit of the normalized supports contains \(0\) and \(1\).
The subgroup generated by its points has a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(\beta\) with rationally independent entries and fixed distinct integer coordinates \(a_i\) for the points.
Rounding \(N\beta\) changes each \(N a_i\cdot\beta\) by \(O(1)\).
Hausdorff convergence ensures both a uniform \(o(N)\) width after recentering and eventual occupation of every limiting cluster.
Removing any common integral Laurent gcd leaves nonzero rows of joint width at most \(2d\) by width additivity.
Their specialized exponent sets remain pairwise disjoint because the centers have separation \(\gg N\).
At least two rows remain, so their diagonal is a nonunit, and irreducibility of the original polynomial survives localization at \(X\) because its constant coefficient is nonzero.
The common gcd must thus be a Laurent unit.

Failure for any fixed interval count would allow both the relative width and the positive measure to be less than or equal to \(1/j\) along a sequence.
Bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure would leave only finitely many integer polynomials by the coefficient estimate, so a subsequence has strictly increasing degrees.
Choosing one normalized occupied exponent in each nonempty interval yields a subsequence of convergent choices, with fewer distinct limits allowed when intervals coalesce.
Every support point is within \(1/j\) of a choice, and every choice is itself in the support.
This yields the forbidden finite Hausdorff limit, including the case of a single proposed interval, which cannot cover both endpoints at shrinking width.
Conversely a finite limit with \(k\) points gives a cover by \(k\) intervals of arbitrarily small relative length along that subsequence.
The compactness argument gives uniform existence for each fixed \(k\) despite the intermediate estimates depending on the limiting positions.
The review used the partial-degree height theorem and the torsion-component classification at their recorded interfaces.
