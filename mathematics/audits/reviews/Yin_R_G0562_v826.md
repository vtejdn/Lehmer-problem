## Independent review of the height and factor estimates

At 2026-11-20T23:49 (UTC), a reviewer independent of the development of C345 v689 checked its whole proof for Sheng Yin and recommends acceptance.
The review used the fixed Claim and its mathematical inputs.
No development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or previous findings on v689 were supplied.
The reviewer had previously checked the separate large-prime localization result used in the last step.

The two real roots and the linear cofactor bounds give \(a-1<r<a+1\) and \(n-1\le d\le n+1\) for both signs.
For any positive integer \(q\) in the statement, \(r=\beta^q\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb Q(r)\) give equality of the two fields.
The possible conjugate values of \(\beta\) thus map bijectively under \(q\)-th powering onto the conjugates of \(r\).
The positive unit \(\beta\) has one exterior image, one contracting image and neutral images.
The conjugate of a neutral image equals its inverse, so irreducibility gives reciprocal symmetry.
This verifies the Salem signature for composite \(q\) and for \(q=1\) as well as for primes.
Every neutral \(w\) has degree \(d\) and absolute logarithmic height \(t/d\).

From \(G(z)=0\) at \(z=w^q\) and \(|z|=1\) one obtains \(a|z^{n-1}-\epsilon|\le2\).
Factoring \(z^{2(n-1)}-1\) then gives the upper bound \(4/a\) for either sign.
The power differs from one because a conjugate of \(\beta>1\) cannot be a root of unity.
Adjoining \(-1\) leaves \(\mathbb Q(w)\) of degree \(d\).
For the two-logarithm estimate at \(M=2q(n-1)\), a principal logarithm of \(w\) has modulus at most \(\pi\) and the relative height is \(t\).
The two modified heights are therefore at most \(\max(t,\pi)/d\) and \(\pi/d\).
With the branch correction using a nearest integer, the coefficient height is at most \(\max(\log(2M),1/d)\).
The cited effective constant is \(O(d^4\log(4d))\).
After the two modified heights the degree dependence is \(O(d^2\log(4d))\), and the additive \(\log2\) from the distance estimate gives precisely the claimed left side \(\log(a/8)\).

Since \(\log r<\log(a+1)\le\log(a/8)+\log(32/3)\), the estimate also controls \(qt\) after increasing the absolute constant, including small seeds.
Division by \(t>0\) and Voutier's Corollary 2 give
\[
             \frac{q}{\log(4qn)}\ll d^2\log^4(3d).
\]
The derivative of \(q/\log(4qn)\) has the sign of \(\log(4qn)-1>0\).
At \(q_0=A d^2\log^5(3d)\), the denominator is at most \((C+\log A)\log(3d)\) for \(A\ge1\), using \(n\le d+1\).
Taking an effectively large \(A\) proves the uniform exponent bound, and substituting it back gives \(\log(4qn)=O(\log(3d))\).
This provides both the asserted seed estimate and \(q\ll d^2\log^2(3d)\max(1,1/t)\).

The exact minimum among factors of \(P(X^h)\) is \(\log r/\gcd(h,R)\) for every \(h\ge1\).
The first index bound thus gives the stated lower estimate for every factor after replacing \(d\) with the comparable parameter \(n\).
For the largest radical index \(R\), the estimate on \(\log a\) forces \(\log(r^{1/R})>1\) above the stated threshold with a sufficiently large effective absolute constant.
The second index estimate then removes the remaining three logarithmic powers.
The result diverges with \(a\) at fixed \(n\) uniformly in \(h\) and the sign.

If \(R\nmid H(a)\), the prime obstruction gives a prime \(p>n\) with \(r^{1/p}\in\mathbb Q(r)\).
Localization gives \(\log(r^{1/p})<\log(5p)\).
The uniform exponent bound at \(q=p\) implies \(\log(5p)=O(\log(3d))\), so the seed bound specializes to \(\log a\ll n^2\log^3(3n)\).
All constants in these uses of elementary inequalities can be chosen effectively.
The external logarithmic-form and height theorems were used at their recorded statement scopes.
