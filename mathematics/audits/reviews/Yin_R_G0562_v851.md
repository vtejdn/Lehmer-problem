# Review of height bounds for mixed radical indices from reciprocal root orders

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of the global and local multipliers

At 2026-11-21T01:34 (UTC), an agent independent of the development of C345 v711 checked the complete fixed proof for Sheng Yin and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had contributed to the earlier prime-power result v698 and checked its particular uses here.
The field-generation argument was checked in the clarified formulation for divisors of \(q\).

For \(b\mid q\), \(\gamma^b\) is an algebraic unit generating \(K\), since its \((q/b)\)-th power is \(r\).
The \(d\) embeddings therefore map its conjugates bijectively by powering to those of \(r\).
Its unique exterior conjugate is positive, and a neutral conjugate and its complex conjugate force its irreducible minimal polynomial to be reciprocal.
This applies to \(\gamma,\gamma^t,\gamma^h\) and later to \(\gamma^{h_*}\).
The signed seed polynomial has its stated cofactor, independently of \(a\).
For odd primes below the delay, the trace valuation at the positive \(\ell^e\)-th field radical places \(a\) in \(0,\pm1\bmod\ell\).
The diagonal result places it in zero at a prime equal to the delay.
The three representatives cover both small characteristics, and their availability is the stated extra hypothesis for primes above the delay.
The \(\ell^e\)-th power fixes the reduced coefficient polynomial in characteristic \(\ell\), including root multiplicities, so the target of the \(t\)-th power is \(\overline P_c\).

Every reduced root is a unit and its Frobenius orbit and inverse orbit have equal multiplicities.
Disjoint orbits of length \(j\) occupy at least \(2j\) places in degree \(d\).
For an orbit stable under inversion and of nontrivial inverse action, that action commutes with a cycle transitive on its points, so it is rotation by half its even length.
These cases give orders dividing \(\ell^j-1\) and \(\ell^j+1\), respectively, for \(j\le d/2\).
The inverse-fixed roots have order one or two, including just order one in characteristic two.
Thus \(D\) is prime to \(\ell\) and works for inseparable reductions as well as for squarefree ones.

With \(h=\gcd(t,D)\), the \(h\)-th powers lie in \(\mu_{D/h}\), and \(u=t/h\) is coprime to \(D/h\).
This coprimality holds prime by prime because each valuation of \(h\) is the minimum of the two valuations.
It permits \(u\) and \(h\) themselves to share primes.
The powered polynomials have the full root multisets because all the field elements defining them have degree \(d\).
For \(\ell\nmid s\), reducing \(\Phi_{s\ell^v}\) gives \(\overline\Phi_s^{\,\varphi(\ell^v)}\), including \(s=1\), by the cyclotomic quotient identity and Frobenius.
It follows that each primitive order in the reduced cyclotomic target has a single common multiplicity.
Consequently the automorphism of \(\mu_{D/h}\) given by the \(u\)-th power fixes this multiset, and applying its inverse yields \(S_{\gamma^h}\equiv P_c\bmod\ell\) as monic polynomials.
Equality of sets alone would lose the information needed in this step.

For each odd \(p\ne\ell\), \(p\mid D\) exactly when a positive power at most \(d/2\) is \(1\) or \(-1\bmod p\).
The first such exponent is the order \(k\) when \(k\) is odd and \(k/2\) when \(k\) is even.
For \(p=2\ne\ell\), \(\ell-1\) already suffices.
This proves the two order ranges, the valuation cap and the claimed \(h=1\) case.

The local test also works for composite orders and overlapping prime powers of \(m,t\).
For \(s=m/\gcd(m,t)\), reduction modulo \(s\) takes \(\langle\ell,-1\rangle\bmod m\) onto the corresponding subgroup modulo \(s\).
Each group acts freely on a chosen primitive root of its order.
The \(t\)-power map on a joint orbit therefore has \(L_m/L_s\) points over each image.
A source orbit of multiplicity \(b\ge1\) contributes \(bL_m/L_s\) copies per target point, bounded above by its total multiplicity \(\mu_s\).
Hence every actual source order is retained, including when the image is the identity.
All actual orders divide \(D_*\mid D\).
Rerunning the automorphism argument with \(h_*=\gcd(t,D_*)\) proves the refinement.
The proof requires only a necessary filter on the source orders.

For the height estimates put \(\xi=\gamma^h\), or \(\xi=\gamma^{h_*}\) for the refined bound.
The coefficient congruence makes \(P_c(\xi)\) divisible by \(\ell\) in \(\mathcal O_K\).
At two and three, a binomial multiple gives a nonzero scalar residue for \(\xi^J\).
The three cases give \(J=n+1\) at \(c=0\), \(J=n\) when \(X-c\) is removed, and \(J=n\ell\) when the remaining linear factor divides \(X^n+\epsilon c\).
In the last case \(P_c\) divides the squared binomial, and its reduction divides the \(\ell\)-th power even in characteristic two.
Also \(\deg U\le1\) there, so \(d/J\ge1/\ell\).
In the other cases \(d/J\ge3/4\).
The absolute height of the scalar power is \(J\log\xi/d\) by homogeneity, irrespective of the degree of that power.
The accepted scalar constants give \(\log\xi\ge0.427800/2\) and \(\log\xi\ge\log(\sqrt{10}/2)/3\), respectively.
The dyadic numerical input retains its published-evaluation scope.

At \(\ell\ge5\) the nonzero value \(G_c(\xi)\) is divisible in the full ring of integers, and the coefficient sum \(C=2(1+|c|)\le4\) is strictly below \(\ell\).
The neutral conjugates of \(\xi\) allow the Salem height transfer with degree \(n+1\).
The independent norm bound \(\ell^d\le C^d\xi^{n+1}\) follows from the pointwise coefficient bound and the single exterior embedding.
Taking the larger lower bound gives the last line of (1).
Since \(d/(n+1)\ge3/4,\ c_0>1/3\) and \(c_3=\tfrac12\log(5/2)<1/2\), all three cases give at least \(\delta\).
Finally \(q\log\gamma=\log r\) proves the complementary exponent estimate, and \(h\ge\delta/\log\gamma\) proves the uniform divergence along a shrinking sequence.
