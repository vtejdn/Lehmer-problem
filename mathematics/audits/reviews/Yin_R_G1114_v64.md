## The six-term gap and division by an integral formal unit

This contradicts the unit-circle roots of the trace lift.
Expanding \(t=\pm1\) gives the four coefficient pairs at \(j=2\), and the branch derivative bound yields \(q\le2/c+2\).

In the [integral-unit argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v71.md#unit-prefix), the formal quotient \(X\) is integral at every index and equals \((1+\lambda z^d)J\) through the common prefix.
The near tests at \(rt'\) and \(d+rt'\) in the [interior-ratio argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v15.md#interior) use just this identity at finitely many indices and the same actual signed measure.
The bounded local support and coefficient estimates therefore apply to \(X\) up to \(Ld\) for each fixed \(L\).
At the farther scale, \(kt'+ld\) and its predecessor shifted by \(d\) lie strictly between zero and the common cutoff for fixed \(k\ge1\) and integer \(l\).
The [far-row integrality argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows) and the rational, half-ratio and irrational extinction cases in the interior-ratio result apply even though \(X\) may have infinite degree.
The remaining atoms give period six in the projected index, and failure of the uniform middle range would give violating indices with \(d/t'\) and \(t'/h\) both tending to zero.

The exact comparison uses the original \(B^\sharp\), so it requires no bound on the formal unit on the circle.
The [two cutoffs](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) apply at degree \(O(h)\) with a vanishing prefix of length comparable to \(h\).

## The divisor condition, the half-ratio cases and support on moving intervals

The conditional divisor, quotient and signed-sum restrictions, their half-ratio refinements and the two support conclusions hold in their stated ranges.
The signed-sum restriction uses the same small-first-index hypothesis as the divisor restriction.
In the [mixed-moment test](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#mixed), for each fixed \(l\) the reflected terms are beyond \(d+lu\) since \(d=o(h)\) and \(e\le h\).
When \(c/u\) stays bounded but is nonintegral, each positive residual index \(lu-c\) is in the matched prefix and is not a multiple of \(u\), so contributes zero in either raw model.
For \(c=ku\) the Taylor constant is one on both seams, giving exactly the two contributions represented by \([w^l](AG)\).
Unbounded \(c/u\) and subsequences on which \(u\) fails to divide \(c\) are thus covered even if \(u\) stays bounded.

In the [polynomial marginal](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#two-models), integrality outside the support of \(Ag\) and Fourier decay give a Laurent polynomial \(F\).
The circle bound gives a double zero at \(-\epsilon\), so \(P/g\) is an integer Laurent polynomial taking \(A(-\epsilon)\) there.
Division of \(w^k-(-\epsilon)^k\) by \(g\) gives exactly \(k\) consecutive coefficients of modulus one, forcing distance \(\Delta\) on this entire block in \(F/g^2\).
For the geometric marginal, even indices tending to minus infinity first make the atomic coefficient an integer, then even indices tending to plus infinity make \(A(\epsilon)\) an integer.
The remaining density has the same consecutive block of distances and supremum at most one.
Parseval gives \(k\Delta^2\le1\).
At half ratios, four coefficients of modulus one half would force constant circle modulus, contradicted by the correlation of the extreme terms.
With three coefficients, equal extreme signs yield modulus \(3/2\) at one of \(1,-1\), and opposite extreme signs yield \(\sqrt5/2\) at \(i\).
The only surviving quotients are one and two.
For fixed \(c\) these also give the listed coefficientwise limits, since a surviving fixed first index eventually lies in every matched prefix.

For the [truncated support](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#truncated), \(1-\epsilon z^u\) divides \(1-z^{2c}\) with \(2k\) quotient terms, and the [raw Gram estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v68.md#low) includes the truncation edges.
For the [moving-interval statement](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals), the exact Taylor identity supplies a fixed bounded multiplier between successive offsets, even beyond the last offset.
Testing a fixed number of nonzero integral coefficients controls the error from the negative atom independently of the block's location and length.
In the geometric case the weighted Gram test applies in blocks on which both indices cross no offset.
Within a seam strip the two original lists split at the offsets into intervals of diameter less than \(u\), where all nonconstant Gram moments are zero.
Overlapping strips cost only the same bounded number of splits at endpoints.
The boundedly many translates arising from the quotient preserve the local support bound, and covering the full degree \(N+2c\) gives \(O(1+h/d)\).
This total can still grow with \(h/d\), leaving the general six-term case open.
