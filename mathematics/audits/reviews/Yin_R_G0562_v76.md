# Review of rational function degrees at compressed complex Salem inputs

## Finding on integral values, torsion and primitive generators

At 2026-11-16T23:14 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin checked all five mathematical sections of [C345v76](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v76.md#statement), fixed at 23:10 UTC, and their declared dependency interfaces.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the relative-unit and inverse-pair block results cited here, took no part in developing this proof, and received the fixed inputs without development dialogue or a requested verdict.
The uniform degree estimates and their torsion, zero and primitive specializations pass.

The [norm-one criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v75.md#norm) and [cyclicity of relative units](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v75.md#cyclic) apply with \(g=m-2\ge m/3\).
Thus for a nonzero integral output of height at most \(1/768\) the chosen generator gives \(\lambda=\zeta\rho^u\) and \(\alpha=\eta\rho^v\), with \(u>0\) by orientation at the exterior embedding.
For distinct embeddings of \(K\), a torsion quotient of their \(\rho\)-images would persist after raising to \(u\) and multiplying by the quotient of the two images of \(\zeta\).
It would therefore contradict the assumption on \(\lambda\).
The \(d\) resulting \(\rho\)-images are distinct modulo torsion and have the \(u\)-th roots of the corresponding \(\lambda\)-moduli.
They generate the same normal closure, and right composition of embeddings by \(\iota\) implements inversion on both sets of images.
Ordinary conjugation acting on the left identifies the same two exterior embeddings.
This verifies the identification of inverse labels, exterior-edge orbit and component sizes, as well as \([\mathbb Q(\rho):\mathbb Q]=d\).
For \(v\ne0\) the identical quotient test for the \(\eta\rho^v\)-images gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=K\).
Hence the height identities give \(uL_\rho=L_\lambda\) and \(|v|L_\rho=L_\alpha\), the latter also holding at \(v=0\) since a root of unity has measure one.

The field \(F=\mathbb Q(\mu(K))\) contains the two torsion factors.
After substituting \(\zeta X^u\), clearing numerator and denominator in \(R(\zeta X^u)-\eta X^v\), and multiplying by \(X^{-v}\) if \(v<0\), the resulting polynomial has degree at most \(ku+|v|\).
A rational function with all zeros and poles supported at zero and infinity would be \(cX^j\), with \(c\in\mathbb Q^\times\) for \(R\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Thus the allowed \(R\) has a zero or pole at a finite nonzero point whose preimages under \(\zeta X^u\) remain zeros or poles, so the cleared polynomial is nonzero also when \(v=0\).
Its vanishing at \(\rho\) implies \(ku+|v|\ge D=[K:F]\).
Multiplication by \(L_\rho=d h(\rho)\) yields the asserted lower bound on the weighted degrees.
In [Delsinne's Theorem 1.6](../literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001), rank one and nontorsion give a strict height lower bound with exponent \(3\cdot16=48\) and a fixed positive constant.
Base extension to \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) gives \(D_{\rm ab}\le D\), so the factor \(D\) cancels the relative degree loss and \(\log(3D_{\rm ab})\le\log(3d)\).

For \(h(\alpha)>1/768\), \(A(X)-\alpha B_0(X)\) is a nonzero polynomial over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) since \(R\) is nonconstant.
Consequently \(k\ge[K:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]=d h(\alpha)/L_\alpha>d/(768H)\).
In the complementary range \(kL_\lambda+L_\alpha\le2kH\) since \(k\ge1\).
Both estimates are independent of the rational coefficients and of the choice of \(R\) for each input.
For a zero value the minimal polynomial divides \(A\), and for every rational value \(A-\alpha B_0\) itself gives \(k\ge d\).

The action of \(\iota\) on roots of unity is inversion by the neutral embedding, hence \(F^+\subset B\).
Every embedding of \(F^+\) extends to \(B\), and restrictions are constant on commutator orbits since \(F^+/\mathbb Q\) is abelian.
For \(b\ge3\), the [independent block actions](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#action) place \(A_b^t=(S_b^t)'\) in that commutator group, transitively on each whole component.
It follows that \([F^+:\mathbb Q]\le t\) and \([F:\mathbb Q]\le2t\), including the rational case.
For a torsion output \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subset F\) this gives \(k\ge d/(2t)\) directly.
For any small integral output the same lower bound on \(D\), combined with the [block height estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) for \(\rho\), gives the exponent 106 and denominator \(\sqrt t\) stated in the claim.
Applied to \(\lambda\) with \(L_\lambda\le H\), it gives \(t\ll H^2(\log(3d))^{212}\), so \(b<3\) occurs only in a bounded degree range depending on \(H\).
These degrees are covered by reducing the constants using \(k\ge1\), and substituting the bound on \(t\) gives the stated exponent 212.
When \(t=1\) one has \(b=m\ge3\), hence \(D\ge d/2\).
The [absolute bound of Voutier](../literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) applies to the nontorsion degree-\(d\) generator and gives the cubic log-log ratio for \(L_\rho=d h(\rho)\).
The high-height linear estimate also suffices in this case.

If \(\lambda\) is primitive modulo torsion in the relative unit group, setting \(\rho=\lambda\) eliminates \(\zeta\), so clearing requires just \(\mathbb Q(\eta)\) and costs \(k+|v|\), with \(|v|=L_\alpha/L_\lambda\).
A degree-constrained record among complex Salem measures is primitive because the compressed generator constructed above has degree \(d\) and strictly smaller measure if \(u>1\).
In one unbalanced block, the [root-field theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#fields) gives rational torsion and excludes proper nontrivial subfields other than the degree-\(d/2\) trace field.
A nonrational \(\alpha\) of measure at most \(e^H\) then satisfies \(h(\alpha)\le2H/d\), meeting the small-height threshold uniformly for sufficiently large \(d\).
Thus \(k\ge d-H/L_\lambda\), and Voutier supplies the stated \(O_H((\log d/\log\log d)^3)\) loss, absorbing bounded degrees depending on \(H\).
Finally \(X+P(X)\) has both a nonzero constant and a degree-\(d\) term and evaluates to \(\lambda\), so it is an allowed interpolation example.
The two external height estimates were checked at their accepted statement and application scopes.
