## The numerator bound, upper-tail alternative and degree-twelve certificate

On 2026-11-21 in Shanghai an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the fixed C0345v605 proof and the cited specialization and torsion theorem interfaces.
The reviewer had no role in developing this proof and received no exploration notes, development dialogue, author explanation or prior verdict on this version.
Both bounds and the strict improvement in the example hold under their stated hypotheses.

On the circle the fiber has reciprocal roots of sum \(2-B/u\), so Jensen contributes \(\log u+D(B/u)\), including the double-root endpoints.
Integration gives the claimed limit since \(\int\log u=m(P)\) and the two-variable scalar specialization has just one limit index.
A nonzero root of \(P\) and its inverse both avoid the zero set of \(A\) by reciprocity and coprimality.
Consequently \(B\) is nonzero at every root of \(P\), ruling out a common vertical fiber and ruling out any nonvertical binomial component by surjectivity of its first-coordinate power map.
This also excludes the Laurent identity \(B=4u\), so the pointwise certificate supplies a strict arc away from the finitely many zeros of \(A\).
For \(A=1\), the monic polynomial has \(m(P)\ge0\), hence \(u\) attains at least one and the strict arc exists.

The torsion theorem then gives a fixed finite bound for the number of torsion pairs.
On a fixed strict subarc successive grid points of angular spacing \(\pi/N\) have opposite strict signs, producing a number of distinct zeros tending to infinity.
Each torsion zero would yield a distinct torsion pair by its first coordinate.
For large \(N\), the degrees \(\pm(N+h)\) have extreme coefficients equal to the same sign and all other terms are interior.
After clearing the monomial and normalizing the sign, every monic irreducible factor has unit constant coefficient and nonnegative logarithmic measure.
The factor containing a nontorsion circle zero has positive measure by Kronecker and measure at most the total measure of the specialization, so passage to the limit bounds the infimum.

For the cancelled polynomial the leading fiber coefficient is one and the root sum is \(2-u\), which yields exactly \(\int D(u)\).
The strict inequality \(m(P)<\log4\) and positivity force an arc with \(0<u<4\).
In a torsion-coset parametrization the constant-coordinate cases are excluded by nonconstancy, and when both exponents are nonzero a unit-circle parameter giving \(Y=1\) contradicts \(u(X)>0\).
The grid argument therefore applies again, with extreme coefficients one at degrees \(\pm N\) for \(N>h\).
On a sequence with \(m(P)\to0\), the upper-tail inequality applies eventually, while the optimized loss is bounded below by \(\gamma-m(P)\).

Expanding the proposed sum of squares on the circle gives the printed reciprocal polynomial and \(P(1)=1\), hence \(A=1-X\) is coprime to it.
The first summand is positive away from one and the square equals one at \(X=1\).
At angle \(\pi/9\) the square vanishes and \(4\sin^2(\pi/18)<1/4\), giving strictly positive loss for \(A=1\) on a neighborhood.
The choice \(A=1-X\) has \(B\le u\) throughout and thus zero loss.
Exact repeated powering and Euclidean gcd computations modulo 19 reproduced both displayed remainders, both unit gcds, and \(s_{12}=X\).
The latter congruence forces distinct irreducible factors of degrees dividing 12, and the gcds exclude all proper divisors, each of which divides 4 or 6.
The degree thus forces irreducibility modulo 19 and monicity gives irreducibility over the rationals.
