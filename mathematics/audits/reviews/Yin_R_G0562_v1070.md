# Review of integral fractional linear functions of the Chebyshev unit

## The denominator norm bounds give the stated exclusions

At 2026-11-24T19:51 UTC an agent outside the development of this argument independently reviewed all four sections of the fixed claim for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without its development discussion.
The argument covers the stated parameter range and the exclusion for all integral fractional linear images on the powers-of-three branch.

For \(a\ne\pm b\) the determinant of the fractional linear transformation is \(a^2-b^2\ne0\).
Its inverse is rational, so the real number \(Z\) generates the full degree-\(2n\) field.
The irrationality of \(T\) prevents a zero or pole for the allowed coefficients, and \(T\mapsto1/T\) exchanges \(Z\) and \(1/Z\).
In particular integrality of \(Z\) makes both \(Z\) and its reciprocal integral and \(Z\ne\pm1\).
The cases \(a=\pm b\) and \(ab=0\) agree with their separate formulas.

The product \((a-bT)(aT-b)=T(k^2-Aw)\) and \((T-1)^2/T=w\) give \(Z+Z^{-1}-2=S^2w/D\).
This element belongs to \(\mathcal O_B\), so \(N(D)\) divides \(S^{2n}N(w)\) over the integers up to sign.
Substitution into \(A^nH(k^2/A)\) gives the stated first expression for \(N(D)\).
For the second expression the coefficient of \(V\) is \(4(Mk^2-A)=L^2k^2-S^2\).
At odd \(\ell\mid S\), coprimality makes \(k\) and \(A\) units, \(A\equiv-k^2/4\), and \(C(-4)=-1\) gives \(V\equiv k^{2n-2}/2^{2n-1}\).
If \(\ell\nmid L\) the second term in the expression for \(N(D)\) is then a unit.
If \(\ell\mid L\) and \(1-2^{2n-1}\) is a nonsquare, both coefficients of \(S^2\) and \(L^2 k^2\) are units after the indicated rescaling.
When their valuations coincide, cancellation would make \((k(L/\ell^v)/(S/\ell^u))^2\equiv1-2^{2n-1}\pmod\ell\), which is impossible.
The common primes with zero residue are correctly left in \(E\), where only the original norm divisibility is used.
At two, even \(S\) gives \(v_{\mathfrak p}(k^2)\ge2n>v_{\mathfrak p}(Aw)=1\) at the unique place of residue degree one.
For odd \(S\) the general norm divisibility already bounds its dyadic part.
These checks give \(|N(D)|\le |N(w)|S^2 E^{2n-2}\) prime by prime, including primes in \(N(w)\) outside \(S\).

For \(A>0\), the \(n-3\) negative real traces give denominator factors at least \(A|w_i|\) in modulus.
The lower bound \(|\operatorname{Im}\xi|^2\ge2M-8\) and \(\operatorname{Re}\xi=O(n)\), with \(n=O(\log M)\), give a factor at least \(A|\xi|/2\) at each of the two nonreal traces.
Dividing out these \(n-1\) factors gives the asserted bound at the positive trace.
Dividing \(S^2w\) by this last factor and using \(|Z+Z^{-1}-2|\le4\max(|Z|,|Z|^{-1})\) gives the measure bound with constant \(\log16\).
For \(E=1\) the resulting error bound for \(Aw/k^2\) is \(20w\), since \((1+4A/k^2)/A^{n-1}\le5\).
Thus \(A/(Mk^2)=1+O(1/M)\) uniformly, and raising to the power \(n-1\) costs a bounded factor.
The more precise denominator estimate gives \(|A/k^2-1/w|\ll M^{2-n}|k|^{-2(n-1)}\).
Together with \(1/w-M=O(M^{2-n})\) it implies \(|A-Mk^2|\ll k^2M^{2-n}\).
For \(|k|>1\), \(\gcd(A,k)=1\) makes the left side a nonzero integer, yielding \(|k|\gg M^{(n-2)/2}\) with an absolute implied constant.
Substitution gives \(A\gg M^{n-1}\) and the claimed \((n-1)^2\log M-O(n)\) bound.

For \(|k|=1\), \(D=1-Aw\) is relatively prime to \(w\) as an ideal factor, so integrality forces \(D\mid S^2\) in \(\mathcal O_B\).
All rational primes in its norm divide \(S\), which is odd in this case, and the local bounds at \(E=1\) give \(|N(D)|\le S^2\).
For \(A=v(v+1)\ge2\), \(A\ne M\), one has \(2|M-A| A^{n-1}C(1/A)\ge6A^3>2(4A+1)=2S^2\).
This contradicts the exact norm identity even if cancellation occurs with its \(S^2\) term.
The exceptional pair therefore consists of \(q,q+1\) in either order of magnitudes, giving the known integral unit \(Y\) or its inverse.

For \(A<0\), \(|k/S|>1\) keeps \(s-t\) uniformly away from zero when \(t=(T-1)/(T+1)=O(M^{-1/2})\).
It follows that \(Z=(s+t)/(s-t)\) is positive and \(|Z-1|=O(M^{-1/2})\).
One of the real conjugates \(Z,Z^{-1}\) lies inside the disk and has the same endpoint distance bound.
The monic degree-\(2n\) minimum has nonzero integer value at \(1\), so the derivative lower estimate used at that endpoint applies directly.
The upper estimate for the same minimum only needs its integrality, separability, degree and measure.
The inequality \(2n\le(\log M)/\log2+3\) and the constant \(25/110+\log(25\mathrm e)/(24\log2)<0.49\) give a contradiction with the lower leading constant \(1/2\) whenever \(m(Z)\le(\log M)/110\) and \(q\) is sufficiently large.

For a hypothetical shrinking sequence of the specified nonconstant integral \(Z\), bounded-degree finiteness gives \(n\to\infty\), because the real generator has degree \(2n\) and is nontorsion.
Reciprocity bounds each conjugate modulus and its reciprocal by \(\exp(m(Z))\).
At the embedding with \(|\zeta|=r\to\infty\), exchanging \(a,b\) permits \(R=|a/b|\ge1\), and the modulus identity gives the lower bound \((R^2-1)(r^2-1)/(R+r)^2\) for \(|\sigma Z|^2-1\).
For \(R\ge r\) this grows without bound, and for \(1\le R<r\) it is at least \((R^2-1)(1-r^{-2})/4\).
Hence \(R\to1\), so \(|S|/\sqrt A=\sqrt R+1/\sqrt R\to2\) since \(A>0\) eventually.
The measure inequality now forces the positive integer \(|S|/E\) to be at most two eventually.

Finally any nonconstant rational fractional linear image generates \(K\).
There are \(n-3\) neutral inverse pairs, giving the uniform fraction \((n-3)/n\ge1/4\) in the small-height norm-one criterion.
An integral sequence of positive total measures tending to zero has absolute heights tending to zero, so the relative norm is one eventually.
The identity \(f(X)f(1/X)=1\) then follows by clearing denominators at a root of degree \(2n>2\).
Coefficient comparison gives \((c+d)^2=(a+b)^2\) and \((c-d)^2=(a-b)^2\) for \(f=(aX+b)/(cX+d)\).
The equal signs yield a constant map, while the other cases give a signed map of the analyzed form after scaling the two integral coefficients to be coprime.
Multiplication by \(-1\) preserves degree, integrality and total measure.
When \(L\) is a power of three, the only odd prime available for \(E\) is \(3\), where \(1-2^{2n-1}\equiv2\) is a nonsquare, so all remaining possibilities have the asserted bounds.
Parameters with \(q\) bounded have bounded degree and cannot supply a shrinking sequence either.
For general \(L\) the bounds leave the exceptional prime support allowed by the statement.
