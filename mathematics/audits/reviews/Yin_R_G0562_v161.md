# Review of specialization in a progression of occupied residue classes

## Finding on the uniform comparison, the arithmetic lift and the reciprocal examples

At 2026-11-17T04:45 UTC, an agent outside proof development independently inspected the complete [v161 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v161.md#decision-residue-progression-measure-submitted) for Yin.
The mathematical inputs were the fixed submission and its necessary fixed dependencies.
The reviewer had previously independently inspected v156 and received no development discussion or separate author explanation for v161.

**Fiber average.**
After normalization in \(Y\), equality of specialized exponents implies \(H\mid j-j'\), hence \(j=j'\) since \(0\le j,j'\le R<H\), followed by \(i=i'\).
This works for either sign of \(N\).
Consequently the specialization is nonzero for every phase and preserves all coefficient moduli.
For \(k=2\) the character given by the exponent difference has Haar-uniform value both on the two-torus and on the specialized circle.
The binomial integral is therefore exactly the logarithm of the larger coefficient modulus in both cases.
On each nonexceptional fiber the v159 identity follows by reflecting the exterior roots one at a time and putting the extracted moduli into the Jensen mean.
The mean \(b(X)\) is integrable even with common base content, since it lies between \(\log|A_R(X)|\) and the constant \(\log B\).
Away from the finitely many exceptional base points, conditioning on \(z^H=X\) gives the \(H\) equally likely solutions of \(Y^H=e^{iH\phi}X^N\), by coprimality.
Multiplying each root factor over this list gives \(1-r^H\bar v_i^H e^{iH\phi}X^N\).
At \(r=1\) the logarithm of the original nonzero Laurent polynomial is integrable and the conditional sum agrees almost everywhere.
Each term of the resulting finite sum is bounded above by \(\log2\), giving the upper error \(R\log2/H\).
At \(r<1\) the modulus is between \(1-r^H\) and \(1+r^H\), so the absolute error has the regularized bound in the submission.

**Uniform reverse estimate.**
Parseval for the noncolliding specialized terms gives \(\|P_\phi\|_\infty\ge B/\sqrt k\) with \(B\) the coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm.
For an interior root the distance to \(Y\) equals \(D\), and for an exterior root the distance equals \(D/|v_i|\le D/(1-D)\).
If \(D<1/(4R)\), then along the segment to the actual root the modulus of the second variable is at most \(1+2D\).
The vertical derivative is bounded by \(RB(1+2D)^{R-1}\le e^{1/2}RB\) everywhere on that segment.
Thus for every root selection \(D<v<1/(4R)\) entails \(|P_\phi|/\|P_\phi\|_\infty\le C R\sqrt k\,v\), independently of the leading coefficient and of the other \(R-1\) roots.
The measurable-set Turán–Nazarov bound applies on a full period to the \(k\) distinct frequencies, each with real part zero in the exponential representation.
Its absolute base constant survives extraction of the \((k-1)\)-st root as an absolute multiplier.
Enlarging the absolute constants at \(v\ge1/(4R)\) extends the resulting sublevel estimate to all \(v>0\).
It also rules out a positive-measure zero set for any selected factor.

For \(h=1/(k-1)\), the layer formula for \(\log(1+\epsilon/D)\) and the substitution \(v=\epsilon x\) leave the factor \((C_0R\sqrt k\,\epsilon)^h\) times a constant times \(\int_0^\infty x^{h-1}/(1+x)\,dx\).
The latter integral is at most \(1/h+1/(1-h)=O(k)\) because \(k\ge3\).
This controls each regularization excess and proves individual logarithmic integrability without assuming simple roots.
At \(\epsilon=H^{-(k-1)}/(C_0R\sqrt k)\) the factor with exponent \(h\) is exactly \(H^{-1}\).
The sum over the \(R\) roots is therefore \(O(Rk/H)\).
Since \(1-r^H\ge\epsilon\), the regularized fiber error is at most
\[
 \frac RH\left((k-1)\log H+\log C_0+\log R+\tfrac12\log k\right).
\]
With \(H>R\ge1\) and \(\log k\le k\), this is \(O(Rk\log(2H)/H)\) with one absolute constant, also when \(H,R,k\) all vary.
No estimate in these two steps involves the base exponents, their range, \(N\), the phase, or distances between distinct roots.

**Integral lift and examples.**
Subtracting the residue offset makes the lift of each exponent to \((i,j)\) unique for \(0\le j\le R\).
Its actual width \(R'\) satisfies \(1\le R'\le R\) by the two occupied classes, and the v156 bound \(c_{R'}\) is at least \(c_R\).
In the torsion alternative, the factorization in v156 contains a factor \(\Phi_m(X^aY^b)\) with \(0<b\le b\varphi(m)\le R'\).
The inequality follows from additivity of extreme exponents in products over a domain.
If \(aH+bN=0\), then \(H\mid b\), impossible in this range.
Its specialization is therefore a nonunit with cyclotomic irreducible factors even when \(aH+bN<0\), up to a Laurent unit.
This excludes the torsion alternative for the stipulated irreducible noncyclotomic \(P\).
Applying the reverse estimate to the lift gives (2), with the claimed fixed-\(R\) consequence after absorption.

In the example the two residue rows are disjoint, have \(M+1\) nonzero entries each, and interchange under reversal of degree \(MH+1\).
Both \(A_M\) and \(A_M^*\) have constant coefficient one and degree \(M\), and their linear coefficients differ for \(M\ge3\).
Hence their quotient cannot be a scalar monomial, including after cancellation of a common base factor.
The degree-one graph of its negative is a horizontal nontorsion component, since a torsion graph of degree one over the base has monomial defining function.
The equality \(|A_M|=|A_M^*|\) on the base circle gives \(m_2(F)=m(A_M)\) by the linear-fiber Jensen identity almost everywhere.
At \(M=\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor\) the error \(O(M\log(2H)/H)\) tends to zero, giving the stated uniform gap with no irreducibility hypothesis on this example.

All submitted bounds and examples hold at their stated scopes.
The external Turán–Nazarov theorem was used at its recorded statement scope, and the fixed v156 result supplies the arithmetic gap.
