## The primitive pencil bound and the range of its converse

On 2026-11-20 a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked [the submitted claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v534.md#statement) independently of its development.
I find the bound \(m(A)\gg kgwh\) proved uniformly for the stated dilations, the vanishing-cost equivalence proved for double contact, and the normalized ceiling proved for the specified primitive-norm credit.

Fix a candidate \(j\) before taking the limit in \(L\).
Its seed list, its powers of three, and its pairs of bounded integer exponents are finite.
For noninverse distinct conjugates an equality \(\alpha^{ga}=\beta^{gb'}\) gives \(|a|=|b'|\) by their common positive height.
Except at \((0,0)\) this would identify two distinct conjugates up to torsion, using reciprocity for opposite signs.
The differences are therefore nonzero algebraic integers, also for negative exponents since the roots are units.
Each rational prime dividing one of their absolute norms eventually satisfies \(\ell^e<2L\).
The primitive norm estimate at all sufficiently large orders and positivity of the finitely many measures give the other two tests for this fixed candidate.
This proves divergence of the largest passing candidate without assuming monotonicity in \(L\).
The degree and measure bounds from double tangency place every actual seed on the tested list.

At a fixed prime of the splitting field, reduction of the full factorization of \(P\) lifts every distinct residual root to a conjugate, even if \(P\bmod\ell\) has repeated factors.
An order-\(gn\) root of degree at most \(e\) forces \(\ell^e>gn\ge2L\), so a noninverse distinct pair cannot satisfy any of the tested short relations after reduction.
Its relation lattice has covolume \(n\) since either powered root generates the cyclic group of order \(n\).
A shortest vector has length \(j^2<\lambda\ll\sqrt n\) by exclusion and pigeonholing, and extends to a basis because it is primitive in the lattice.
Projecting the square of first exponents to the normal to this vector gives \(O(1+w\lambda/n)\) occupied lines of spacing \(n/\lambda\), each containing \(O(1+w/\lambda)\) lattice points.
Here \(w\lambda/n\ll w/\lambda\), giving the claimed intersection bound.
Inverse pairs have disjoint images since the represented first exponents are positive and strictly less than \(n/2\).
After summation over at most \(j\) other roots the lost fraction is \(O(j/w+1/j+jw/n)=o(1)\), uniformly because \(j\le K\), \(w/n\ll K^{-3}\), and \(j\to\infty\).
The retained coprime count thus leaves an absolute positive multiple of \(w\) exclusive images for each root.

For fixed \(\ell\), the valuation of a prime-to-order input norm is the sum over the primitive roots of unity lifting the residual roots, with \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
An orbit of degree \(f\) contributes \(f\) equal summands, each counted once when all residual roots are listed.
Other primitive roots have valuation zero, and orders divisible by \(\ell\) contribute zero to the prime-to-order norm at this prime.
Different retained \(n\) have different input orders for the single chosen dilation at this \(L\), giving at most \(e\) roots altogether with positive valuation.
At an exclusive output the integral period inclusion for \(F=P^k\) gives valuation at least \((kt_x-v_\ell(c))_+\) for the primitive row.
Distinct outputs at this order have distinct lifts in the unramified splitting field, and \(\deg A<\varphi(n)\) ensures a nonzero resultant.
Summing first over exclusive outputs and then over primes costs at most \(e\log c\) inside the parenthesis of the transfer inequality.
The coefficient minor bound and \(T\gg Ks\) put this expense at a fraction \(O(e/(kKh))=O(1/j)\) of the input mass.
The circle error after division by \(T\) is \(o(w/K)\), absorbed since \(Kh\ge ej\log 2\) and \(kg\ge2\).
The weighted slot bound counts at most \(m_{nt}\) images of an individual input root at output order \(t\), so perfect separation gives the normalized ceiling \(kh+ker\log 2/\Sigma\).
Here \(er/\Sigma\le K/(gL)\), proving the stated limit at fixed multiplicity.

For the double-contact converse, torsion-class products of reciprocal seeds remain reciprocal units, preserve logarithmic Mahler measure, and have nontorsion quotients between distinct conjugates.
For any such fixed seed the powered nodes within a slot remain distinct and the measures \(gp h\) distinguish the slots.
Integral Hermite interpolation therefore applies with the product of the actual exponents as denominator multiplier.
Its width \(2er+2\) fits \(rj(L)\) eventually, and its normalized log cost is at most \(2h+o(1)\), uniformly for the allowed schedules by \(\Sigma\ge grL\) and \(\log(2gL)/(gL)\to0\).
Taking fixed seeds of arbitrarily small positive measure proves this direction, while tangency gives \(h\Sigma\le\log H\) in the other direction and the fallback cost has positive normalized logarithm.
The construction establishes double contact and the lower bound itself applies at all the stated higher multiplicities.
