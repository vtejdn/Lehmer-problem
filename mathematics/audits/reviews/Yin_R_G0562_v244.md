## Exact rational values and a binary obstruction

At 2026-11-17T13:34 UTC an agent for Yin independently reviewed the [fixed v244 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#decision-conductor-trace-lift-submitted).
The reviewer previously checked v243's binary factorization and rational lifting obstruction, took no part in developing v244 and received its fixed text and scope without development discussion or earlier findings on v244.
The four integral evaluations, first binary correction, interval of exclusions and numerical example hold.

At a primitive \(\zeta\) of order \(3t\), the trace of \(\zeta^n\) equals \(-1\).
Replacing \(\zeta\) by \(-\zeta\) negates that trace since \(n\) is odd, and preserves the trace \(c\) of \(\zeta^l\) since \(l\) is even.
This gives the normalized values \(-2c\) and \(2(1-c)\).
When \(t>1\), the order of \(w=\zeta^l\) is \(t\) or \(3t\) since \((l,t)=1\).
The odd-order roots \(w,w^2,w^3\) are then all nontrivial.
For each of these \(u\), the integral \(1+u=(1-u^2)/(1-u)\) has absolute norm one by conjugacy, so both \(c=w^{-1}(1+w^2)\) and \(1-c=-w^{-1}(1+w^3)/(1+w)\) are units.
Thus at primes above two both parent evaluations have normalized valuation one, and elsewhere both have valuation zero.

The complementary monic factor to a rational divisor \(S\) is integral and its reduction is coprime to \(\bar S\), because the entire parent reduction is squarefree.
At each prime over two, \(\zeta\) and \(-\zeta\) have the same residue, so \(S\) takes the full parent valuation at both points or at neither.
The residue degrees of primes at which \(\bar S\) vanishes total \(r_t\), by the unramified factorization of the odd cyclotomic polynomial at two.
This proves both absolute resultants and the unit quotient at \(t>1\).
For \(t=1\), \(c=2\) if \(3\mid l\) and \(c=-1\) otherwise, giving the claimed valuations \((2,1)\) and \((1,2)\) in the indicated order.
Multiplying across conductors at \(r_1=0\) gives \(2^{\deg S}\) at either sign, since all reduced roots occur simply.
On substituting a root \(x\) of \(S\), the products of the two cyclotomic families are respectively \(2x^n(x^l+x^{-l})\) and \(2x^n(x^l+x^{-l}-1)\).
Each trace expression is integral, and the two norm products force both to be units since \(x\) is a unit.

For the prescribed full conductors, unique Hensel lifting and reciprocal normalization give \(S=S^*\), with constant one because \(P_l(1)=-1\).
Its degree is \(2D\) with \(D=\sum_t\varphi(t)\) even, since every \(t>1\) is odd and prime to three.
Pairing reciprocal powers gives an integral monic \(T\), and the leading Laurent term shows that substitution by \(X+X^{-1}\) is injective even in characteristic two.
Thus \(T\equiv T_B\bmod2\).
Each conductor \(3t\) and \(6t\) has at least two prime divisors, giving \(T_B(\pm2)=1\) using even \(D\).
The identity \(H(2)-H(-2)\equiv4H'(0)\bmod8\) first gives even derivative for \(T_B\) and then the congruence \(T(2)\equiv T(-2)\bmod8\).
There are no negative real roots of the parent because its normalized value there is \(-2\cosh(nv)-4\cosh(lv)+1<0\).
Consequently \(T(-2)=S(-1)>0\) divides five, while \(T(2)=S(1)\) divides \(-1\).
Congruence modulo eight leaves \(1,1\).
The conductor-three and conductor-six evaluations are units because \(r_1=0\).
Their trace values are integers, so the congruences \(T(-1)\equiv T(2)\) and \(T(1)\equiv T(-2)\bmod3\) determine their signs as positive.
The four distinct roots of \(T-1\) give the claimed integral divisibility.
For \(v>0\), the ratio \(\sinh(nv)/\sinh(lv)\) strictly increases since \(u\coth u\) strictly increases.
The normalized parent starts negative and tends to infinity, and its derivative has at most one sign change, from negative to positive.
There is exactly one simple positive root above one, which a monic divisor positive at one and at positive infinity cannot contain.

At each selected trace \(\tau=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), ordinary differentiation gives \(C'_n(\tau)=n(\zeta^n-\zeta^{-n})/(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})\), reducing to \(1/\bar\tau\).
The selected trace is neither zero nor one in residue, since its root order exceeds three and is odd.
Simple-root lifting for \(C_n+1-2C_l\) gives the correction \(2\bar\tau C_l(\bar\tau)\) modulo four.
The residue traces biject with inverse pairs, since their fibers solve \(z^2-\bar\tau z+1=0\).
The normalized product at \(-1\) adds the corrections \(\bar\tau C_l(\bar\tau)/(1+\bar\tau)\) over the pairs.
Writing \(g(z)=(z^2+1)/(z^2+z+1)\) converts this into the sum of \(z^lg(z)\) over all selected primitive roots.
For order \(3t\), the sum of the odd powers \(w,w^3,\ldots,w^{t-2}\) of \(w=z^3\) equals \((1+w)^{-1}\) in characteristic two by the full geometric sum.
This and \((1+z^3)g(z)=1+z+z^2+z^3\) give (2).
Multiplication by \(1+z^6\) leaves precisely the exponents \(0,1,2,4,5,6\) in the difference formula.
The computation needs no coprimality of \(n,l\) at intermediate offsets.

For a prime power \(p^e\Vert 3t\), its Ramanujan sum is odd exactly at \(v_p(l+j)=e-1\), by subtracting the two sums over all \(p^e\)-th and \(p^{e-1}\)-th roots.
At \(k_a=3h_a+2,\ h_a=5^{a-1}7^{b_a-1}\), the term with \(j=2\) has the required valuations at five and seven precisely for the designated interior conductor and is prime to three.
The pure endpoint exponents are too large for this evaluation.
At every other shift in (3), \(j-2\) is nonzero modulo both five and seven, so no conductor with a squared prime divisor contributes.
For large \(A\) every selected conductor has such a divisor since \(t_a>5^A/7\).
Hence exactly one member of each pair has residue three.
Both members are odd and coprime to \(35\), and the two collections have distinct residues \(2,-4\bmod35\).
Distinct eligible \(a\) give distinct \(h_a\), yielding the claimed count of disjoint pairs.

For the middle even index, put \(H=\min(5^{a-1},7^{b_a-1})\gg5^{A/2}\).
For any selected \(t_{a'}\), its quotient by its radical shares \(7^{b_a-1}\) with \(h_a\) if \(a'\le a\), and \(5^{a-1}\) if \(a'\ge a\).
A nonzero step from \(k\) to \(k-6\) therefore requires \(k-j-3h_a\) to be a multiple of an integer at least \(H\) for one of the six listed shifts.
For \(|k-3h_a|<H/2\), \(H>12\), the only possible multiple is zero.
On the progression \(k\equiv3h_a+2\bmod6\) only \(j=2\) qualifies, giving the unique step at \(k_a\).
Each side has length \(\gg H\) in the offset coordinate, so the side of residue three supplies \(\gg H\) admissible offsets after removing multiples of five or seven.
Indeed every 35 consecutive terms of this progression contain exactly 24 coprime to \(35\), irrespective of the side chosen.
All offsets are comparable to \(\sqrt n\), and \(H\gg n^{1/4}\).

For \(n=7\,503\,125\), direct coprime counts at \(t=2401,1225,625\) give \(1372,560,333\) traces greater than \(-1\).
The magnitudes are \(7,1,5\) by \(\Phi_{3t}(\omega)=\Phi_t(1)/\Phi_t(\omega)\) and \(|\Phi_t(\omega)|=1\) for a primitive cube root.
Thus the signed baseline is \(-35\equiv1\bmod4\).
Counting the parity of the integral Ramanujan sums reproduces all entries in the table.
As a separate check, for each \(f=\bar\Phi_{3t}\) of degree \(b\), the sum of \(h(z)=z^{n-k}g(z)\) over its roots is the coefficient of \(X^{b-1}\) in \(h f'\bmod f\), by Lagrange interpolation.
Reduction of the rational function in these three squarefree algebras gives the respective pairs of bits \((0,0),(0,1),(1,1)\) in the offset order \(107,101\).
The resulting trace residues are three and one, respectively.
