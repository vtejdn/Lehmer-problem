## Factor finiteness, level-set estimate and the uniform gap

At 2026-11-17T00:14 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the entire C345v92 submission dated 2026-11-17T00:10.
The reviewer had no development role and received the fixed proof and dependencies without author dialogue or a prior verdict on this version.

For a nonzero \(T\) of degree \(h\le H\), deleting the radius intervals of length \(2\delta\) around all \(h\) root moduli leaves a point in \([1/4,3/4]\) since \(2H\delta<1/2\).
For \(|\alpha|\le2\), separation gives \(|\rho z-\alpha|\ge\delta\) at \(|z|=1\), and for \(|\alpha|>2\) the triangle inequality gives at least \(|\alpha|/2\).
Multiplying produces the claimed factor \((\delta/2)^H M(T)\), also for a nonzero constant.
For each Möbius exponent \(0,1,-1\) the corresponding factor at \(\rho z\) has modulus at least \((1-(3/4)^j)/(1+(3/4)^j)\).
The product of these positive lower bounds over all \(j\ge1\) converges to a positive value since \(\sum (3/4)^j\) converges.
Thus the bounds are simultaneous in \(n\) and in the chosen radius.
The perturbation on the interior circle has modulus at most \(B\) also for \(k=0\).
If the resulting lower bound \(c_HM(T)-B\) is positive, the circle mean of \(\log|P|\) at radius one dominates the mean at \(\rho\) by Jensen's formula.
If it is nonpositive the asserted bound on \(M(T)\) is immediate.
For bounded \(M(P)\) the integer coefficients of \(T\) are then bounded by expansion in its roots and \(\deg T\le H\), proving finiteness of the possible factors.

Once \(T\ne0\) is fixed, its off-circle roots contribute a smooth part with bounded second derivative \(C_T\) to the boundary logarithm.
The circle roots, counted with multiplicity, contribute \(S\ge\varphi(n)/4\) from the cyclotomic roots and \(S\ge4/\ell^2\) from the nearer endpoint of a consecutive gap of angular length \(\ell\).
For one root angle the same estimate uses the two ends of its lifted \(2\pi\) interval.
At \(\varphi(n)\ge8C_T\) one has \(-u''\ge S-C_T\ge S/2\ge2/\ell^2\).
Both endpoint limits are \(-\infty\), so strict concavity gives a unique maximum.
On either monotone side the variation between two points at distance \(s\) is at least \(s^2/\ell^2\), by integrating the derivative bound from the endpoint nearer the maximum.
Restricting to a band of height \(2t\) on that side gives length at most \(\ell\sqrt{2t}\), hence normalized measure at most \(2\sqrt{2t}\) after summing both sides and all arcs.

For \(Y=|u-\log|a||\), integrating \(j e^{-jt}\) against the last distribution bound gives \(\int e^{-jY}\,d\nu\le\sqrt{2\pi/j}\).
Nonnegative series integration gives \(\int-\log(1-e^{-Y})\le\sqrt{2\pi}\,\zeta(3/2)\).
The matching level itself has measure zero.
The reverse triangle inequality therefore bounds \(m(P)\) below by the mean of \(\max(u,\log|a|)\) minus this constant for every offset \(k\).
Since \(\log|a|\ge0\), this maximum is at least \(L_n^+-h^-\), where the negative part of \(\log|T|\) is integrable even at its circle zeros.
The mean-zero cyclotomic logarithm gives \(\int L_n^+=\|L_n\|_1/2\).

For \(\omega(n)\to\infty\), the boundary norm in C345v91 diverges for prime powers of arbitrary multiplicity, and \(\varphi(n)\ge2^{\omega(n)-1}\).
A bounded-measure subsequence would contain a fixed nonzero \(T\) by the interior estimate, contradicting the resulting diverging lower bound.
For bounded \(\omega(n)\), the Möbius expression has at most \(2^{\omega(n)}\) nonzero-exponent binomials, even for a nonsquarefree conductor.
Multiplying by the denominator binomials gives a nonzero integer polynomial of the same measure, with term count bounded since \(T\) has at most \(H+1\) terms.
The fixed-support gap therefore excludes a positive sequence tending to one in the bounded-prime-count range.
Together the two alternatives give the uniform positive gap, with the branch \(T=0\) handled by \(M(aX^k)=|a|\).
Under the small primitive mean hypothesis, C345v89 gives a monic factor of degree \(d-\varphi(n)\le H\) and central residue \(0,\pm1\).
For nonzero residue the new theorem applies with \(B=1\).
For zero residue the measure is that of the bounded-degree factor, whose finitely many possible coefficients at measure at most two give the remaining gap.
