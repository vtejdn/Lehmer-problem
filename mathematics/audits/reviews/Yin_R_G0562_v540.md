## Outcome of the order calculation

The resultant bounds, the 47 excluded orders and the two endpoint restrictions hold for both choices of sign.
They give necessary conditions at 101 and leave the existence of a row with two distinct positive measures open.

The strict integer cutoff admits one-norm 58 and excludes one-norm 59, giving the exact coefficient bound at this prime.
The graph has both original factors at least twice, so reduction of the graph and its derivative gives the common divisor \(S\) for the two reduced rows.
For \(T\mid A\), write \(A=TC\) and \(T_t^*=\delta T\) with \(\delta=\pm1\).
Then \(G_A(X)=T(X^{101})[C(X^{101})+\varepsilon\delta X C_{b-t}^*(X^{101})]\).
At either qualifying root the value of \(A\) at its powered node is nonzero, so each remaining contact has the same multiplicity and qualifies for the smaller graph.
The injective recovery of original roots from powered nodes in C419 bounds their degree sum by \(2(b-t)\), independently of the one-norm of the quotient.
The premise \(t>57-D/2\) contradicts this bound, and \(t>57\) prevents the original division by degree.
Thus the resultant is a nonzero integer.
Multiplication on \(\mathbb F_{101}[Y]/(\bar T)\) has kernel dimension \(\deg\gcd(\bar A,\bar T)\) even if \(\bar T\) has repeated factors.
Each unit of nullity accounts for at least one power of 101 in the determinant by Smith normal form.
The ordinary root product for a monic \(T\) gives the upper bound by \(58^tM(T)^{57}\) regardless of the leading coefficient of \(A\).
Consequently the strict measure inequality excludes full squarefree containment, and the stated floor follows for cyclotomic tests.

A root of order \(n\) in either original factor carries its entire orbit under Frobenius and inversion within that same reduced factor.
For \(n\ge3\), \(101\nmid n\), the group generated by 101 in the quotient of \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times\) by \(\{1,-1\}\) has size \(k=\min\{j>0:101^j\equiv\pm1\pmod n\}\).
The full orbit therefore has size \(2k\).
If this equals \(\varphi(n)\), all the simple primitive roots lie in \(S\), contradicting the determinant bound since \(101>58\).
For the independent enumeration, I initialized \(u_n=n\) through \(6728\) and, at each prime \(q\), replaced \(u_n\) by \(u_n-u_n/q\) on its multiples.
This gives precisely 108 integers \(3\le n\le6728\) prime to 101 with \(u_n=\varphi(n)\le58\), the largest being 210.
Computing successive modular powers through the first signed return and retaining \(2k=u_n\) reproduced every displayed entry in order, with 47 entries of sum 2610.
The range is exhaustive because \(\varphi(q^e)^2/q^e=q^{e-2}(q-1)^2\) and the only local factor below one is \(1/2\) at \(2^1\).
Completeness here concerns the single-orbit computation in the stated totient range.

At either endpoint \(u=\pm1\), the divisibility \(101\mid A(u)\) and \(|A(u)|\le58\) force an integer linear factor.
If both endpoints occur, the distinct linear factors divide \(A\) jointly and their product can be removed in one step, leaving width 55 and degree capacity 110.
For \(D=112\) this rules out the pair but a single removal leaves capacity 112.
For \(D=114\) even one removal leaves capacity 112 and gives a contradiction.
Neither argument asserts realizability of any remaining order or degree pair.
