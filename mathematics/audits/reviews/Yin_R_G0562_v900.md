# Prime exponent sums and the degree of every noncyclotomic factor

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:54 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed [C345 v760](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v760.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received the submitted text without its development discussion or a prior verdict.
The author clarified during review that the lower bound against shrinking Salem factors is uniform in direction and dilation for each fixed integer pole \(B\).
The selected primes themselves are independent of \(B\) over all the rational poles with the prescribed numerator divisibility.

I read the introduction and directly inspected the rendered printed page 128 of the [retained Gupta and Murty article](../../literature/pdf/GM84.pdf#page=2).
The introduction fixes any three distinct primes and page 128 defines the subgroup generated by their residues for primes larger than the three.
Lemma 3 states that this subgroup is the entire multiplicative group for at least \(\delta x/\log^2x\) primes up to \(x\).
The quadratic nonresidue and the Lemma 2 bound remove the two types of possible prime divisors of its index within the proof.
This is already the input needed for the factor degrees, before the article's choice among thirteen products.

Each selected \(\ell>5\) is odd, so \(u,v\) have opposite parity, are distinct, and differ from the two endpoint exponents.
For the plus sign \(F(1)=0\), and for the minus sign \(F(-1)=0\) since the two middle terms cancel by opposite parity.
The four coefficients of \(F\) have gcd one and the monic linear divisor gives a primitive integer quotient with leading coefficient \(D\) and constant coefficient \(D\).
At each \(p_i\), the denominator \(D\) is invertible.
After scaling, the quotient reduces to \(\Phi_\ell\) or \(\Phi_{2\ell}\), with the two reductions identical in characteristic two.
All the relevant roots are simple since \(p_i\ne\ell\).
For odd \(p_i\) the orders modulo \(2\ell\) and \(\ell\) agree by the Chinese remainder theorem, and for \(p_i=2\) the order modulo \(\ell\) applies directly.
Thus each Frobenius orbit has length \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(p_i)\).
Every primitive rational factor retains its degree at each \(p_i\) since its leading coefficient divides \(D\).
Its degree must be a multiple of the least common multiple of the three orders, which equals \(\ell-1\) by cyclicity and Lemma 3.
The full quotient has that degree and is irreducible with multiplicity one for either sign.

The logarithmic measure of \(F\) equals that of the quotient since the removed linear polynomial has measure zero.
The exact support size four gives \(A\le 4\exp m(F)\) by the [sparse coefficient bound](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound), and Jensen's upper bound uses length \(2(A+D)\).
If \(D>1\), every root of the quotient has primitive minimal polynomial with nonunit leading coefficient.
A root in any dilation is likewise nonintegral since its power is nonintegral, giving the lower bound \(\log2\) separately for every irreducible factor.
If \(D=1\) and a positive exterior root exists, all roots of the quotient are units of one common positive height, whereas its positive \(q\)-th radical has strictly smaller positive height.
The evaluation of \(F\) at that radical is nonzero since the only additional root is \(\epsilon\).
For an in-field \(q^a\)-th root the [prime-power norm inequality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#norm-test) gives \(q^a\le L\exp(\ell m(P)/(q(\ell-1)))\le L^2\).
The [exact positive-root formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) applies to this irreducible monic unit polynomial, giving both the least measure and the stated sufficient irreducibility criterion.
If a dilation has a Salem root, its power is a positive exterior root of the original quotient, so the requisite sign hypothesis holds in that application.

The polynomial divided by \(D\) is the rational-pole section in the [winding calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#winding), which counts exactly \(u\) exterior roots and a simple positive one above the displayed threshold for both signs.
When \(u=1\), integrality, irreducibility, reciprocity, and the degree \(\ell-1>2\) ensure the positive exterior root is Salem.
For \(u\ge2\) all \(u\) exterior roots belong to the same irreducible quotient.
A positive power of a Salem root still has exactly one exterior conjugate, and its neutral conjugates stay neutral, excluding all Salem roots of all dilations in this range.
For \(t=z^u\) with \(z\) exterior the modulus equation is independent of the sign.
On \(1\le |t|\le (1+B)/2=a\) there is neither a zero nor a pole, and the radial derivative of the logarithmic modulus is bounded, giving its upper bound \(C_B\log|t|\).
Thus \(s>C_B\) places every exterior \(t\) outside this annulus and \(|t-B|\le(B+1)|t|^{1-s}\le(B+1)a^{1-s}\).
The logarithm of each exterior modulus is \((\log B+O_B(a^{-s}))/u\).
There are exactly \(u\) such roots counted with multiplicity, so the leading coefficient contributes \(\log D\) and the error stays \(O_B(a^{-s})\).
Choosing, for instance, \(u=\lfloor\sqrt{\ell}\rfloor\) verifies the growing direction and growing slope at the fixed integer pole \(30\).
