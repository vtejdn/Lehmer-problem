## Outcome of the full review

The residual descent, the two exceptional-shape bounds and the octic consequences hold with the stated row-uniform errors for a fixed seed.
For the octic, every sequence passing the width-nine cutoff has a primitive quadratic residual of logarithmic measure at least \((2h-\tfrac12\log2)p-o_P(p)\).
The resulting quantitative conditions allow a positive interval of possible residual heights.

**Exceptional shapes.**
Monicity of \(Q\) and formal reciprocity at degree \(2w\) give an integral residual \(cY^2+dY+c\) and account also for the loss of the outer coefficients.
If its value at an endpoint vanishes, then \((U+V_w^*)(U-V_w^*)\) vanishes there.
One nonzero signed pair can therefore be divided in both coordinates by the endpoint linear factor.
The width drops by one, the substituted factor is coprime to \(P\), and synthetic division costs a bounded factor.
The estimate at width \(e\) applies to this pair even without a coprimality or determinant condition, since some integral signed component is a nonzero multiple of its primitive generator.

When \(c=0\), the two top coefficient vectors of the original and swapped-reversed pairs have determinant zero.
At a conjugate \(x\) of largest modulus, the Euler derivative minus \(pw\) times the graph value leaves \(xv_w\) after normalizing by \(x^{pw}\), with lower-term sum \(O_P(pH/\rho^p)\).
The value equation bounds \(u_w\) at the same scale, and reversal bounds the other two top entries.
If all four vanish, the width has already dropped.
Otherwise a nonzero column supplies integer multipliers of size \(O_P(pH/\rho^p)\) killing both top coordinates.
The combination stays nonzero by its two source pairs' nonzero determinant.
The width-\(e\) lower bound on its cost is compatible only with the stated lower rate \((F(P)+\log\rho)/2\) for \(H\).

**Descent and primitive content.**
For an irreducible residual \(q\) different up to sign from \(Q\), a zero of either \(B\) or \(C\) at \(t\) would give divisibility by \(q\) in both coordinates of that signed pair.
The resulting width-\((e-1)\) signed row is zero for all sufficiently large \(p\).
In fact its square matrix at formal \(N=0\) imposes vanishing at \(e\) distinct generic nodes on a polynomial of degree below \(e\).
Independence and the fixed-place estimate preserve the nonzero determinant eventually, with onset depending only on \(P\).
Thus both evaluations are nonzero, and the determinant identity changes the sign of \(B/C\) under \(t\mapsto t^{-1}\).
The same sign change in \(J/K\) makes \(JB/(KC)\) rational in the quadratic field.
Evaluating integral polynomials of degree at most \(w+1\) gives a projective log height at most \(\log(4H)+(w+1)h_{\rm W}(t)\), including the finite absolute values when \(q\) is nonmonic.
The primitive rational relation makes the plus-signed combination \(mJ S_++nK S_-\) divisible by \(q\) in both coordinates.
It is nonzero by independence, and \(\gcd(q(X^p),P)=1\) preserves the original double factor.
Gauss's lemma gives integral quotients.
The fixed-degree coefficient bound for each nonzero quotient and additivity of measure subtract \(m(q)=2h_{\rm W}(t)\), leaving exactly \((w-1)h_{\rm W}(t)\).

If \(t=u/v\) is rational, the evaluation matrix has rank one.
Rank zero would give \(U(t)=V(t)=0\) by resolving both signed pairs, contradicting ordinary coprimality.
After multiplying its entries by \(v^w\), a nonzero row supplies an integral relation of log size at most \(\log(2H)+w h_{\rm W}(t)\), even when one multiplier is zero.
Dividing the resulting two coordinates by \(vY-u\) saves \(h_{\rm W}(t)\) and also drops the width to \(e\).
Repeated roots are already the endpoints, and if \(q=\pm Q\) then \(e=2\), when \(F(P)=2h\) and the individual bound \(\log H\ge ph\) suffices.
These cases use the primitive residual, so extra scalar content cannot substitute for the height required of its roots.

**Octic comparison and exact check.**
On the unit circle \(|D|=\big||U|^2-|V|^2\big|\le H^2\), giving the separate upper budget \(\log|k|+m(q)\le2\log H-2ph\).
Substitution in the descent bound for \(F(P)=eh\) gives \(2eh/(e+2)\) as a lower rate, equal to \(8h/5\) for the octic.
In the signed representations, the cross determinant is a nonzero scalar times \((a_+a_-J^2-b_+b_-K^2)Q^2\).
With either product zero the residual has an endpoint root, which the strict octic bound excludes at the cutoff.
After dividing the two nonzero products by their gcd, the outer and middle coefficients are \(A'-B''\) and \(2(A'+B'')\).
Their gcd divides \(4\) because \(A',B''\) are coprime.
The fixed degree and the invertible linear transformation on these two coefficients therefore identify primitive measure and the projective height of the product ratio up to \(O(1)\).
The elementary comparisons follow from \(25^8 P(32/25)=-1\,788\,481\,599\), \(7^8 P(9/7)=552\,451\) and uniqueness of the exterior root.

For the recorded \(p=17\) witness I separately computed \(Q\) by the resultant of \(P(X)\) and \(Y-X^{17}\), then performed exact polynomial division.
The residual was \(6(1465Y^2+1714Y+1465)\).
Modulo the primitive quadratic the ratio \(JB/(KC)\) was \(-18791/316683\).
The first coordinate \(316683JB+18791KC\) divided by that quadratic with zero remainder and integral quotient of degree eight.
