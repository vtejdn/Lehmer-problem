## Orbit sizes bound both the number of products and the quotient exponent

At 2026-11-24T23:49 UTC an agent outside the development of [C0901 v82](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v82.md#statement) independently reviewed the whole fixed text for Yin, including the integral generation assertion.
The reviewer received the finished claim and requested checks without its development discussion.
The signed products generate the rational space, and their integral class lattice has the stated exponent bound with at most \(\deg w\) generators.
The fixed-dimension, field and shrinking-sequence conclusions also hold at their stated scopes.

Let \(A\) be the elementary abelian image of \(C\) on \(V\), and for an actual conjugate \(b\) let \(H_b\) be its stabilizer in \(C\).
The actual orbit has size \(|O|=[C:H_b]\), regardless of whether the elements inducing the identity on classes permute the actual roots by torsion twists.
The signs for a character \(\chi\) of \(A\), inflated to \(C\), are constant on each set of representatives \(tH_b\) exactly when \(\chi\) is trivial on \(H_b\).
There are \(|A/\operatorname{im}H_b|\le[C:H_b]\) such characters, since all characters of an elementary abelian \(2\)-group are rational signs.
Left translation by \(s\in C\) permutes the orbit factors and changes every exponent by the same multiplier \(\chi(s)\).
Thus the sign or inversion law holds for the actual unit \(z_{O,\chi}\), without taking a power to remove torsion.
It uses \(|O|\) factors of height \(h(w)\), with unit exponents in absolute value.

Sum over \(C\) in the character idempotent and group terms by cosets of \(H_b\).
For an eligible character this gives \(|O|e_\chi[b]=[z_{O,\chi}]\); otherwise the character sum over \(H_b\) is zero.
The rational character idempotents of the image sum to the identity on \(V\).
For \(b'=s b\) one therefore has the integral identity
\[
            |O|[b']=\sum_{\chi\ {\rm eligible}}\chi(s)[z_{O,\chi}].
\]
Omitting the zero classes of torsion products leaves the same identity.
For the original class lattice \(\Lambda=\sum_b\mathbb Z[b]\) and the product lattice \(\Lambda'\), it gives
\(\operatorname{lcm}_{O}|O|\,\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda'\subseteq\Lambda\).
This makes the quotient finite, with exponent dividing that least common multiple; the bound is on the exponent rather than its order.
Summing the character counts over the disjoint actual orbits gives at most \(\sum_O|O|=\deg w\) products, each with height at most \(m(w)\).
Their rational span consequently yields the original divisible group upon adjoining roots and torsion.

The independent rational construction \(z(v)\) also works when an eigenspace has dimension greater than one.
The signed sum of pairings is strictly positive, and a rational orthogonal complement to any proper proposed span supplies a further vector whose own product pairs positively with it.
The trivial character gives a totally real unit since the normal group \(C\) fixes it.
For every nontrivial occurring character at least one conjugate of \(c\) acts by inversion, giving reciprocity and a conjugate of modulus one.
Every conjugate of \(c\) fixes the trace, making it totally real; any conjugate of the unit outside the circle's boundary is then real, and Kronecker plus inversion supplies an exterior real conjugate.
The same reasoning uses only the exact sign action and nontorsion for the integral generators.
Conversely, all conjugations preserve every totally real generator, and each sends a conjugate of a totally-real-trace generator to itself or its inverse by its trace quadratic.
Their actions hence commute on the entire generated rational class space.
The splitting group of the torsion-class compression acts faithfully on that same space, giving the asserted field formulation.

For the first bound the averaged fixed-space projector on \(W\) is \(aI\).
Its restrictions to the \(C\)-invariant rational subspace \(U\) have mean trace \(as\).
Each has trace between zero and \(s\), so the fraction with positive trace is at least \(a\).
Their actions on \(U\) lie in an image of order \(q\) preserving a full rank-\(s\) lattice, and reduction modulo three is faithful on this finite integral group.
Pigeonholing gives one image of probability at least \(a/q\) fixing a common nonzero rational vector.
The actual subset-product witness has height at most \(m(w)/2\) and at least that fraction of real embeddings.
The real-conjugate bound gives exactly the printed inequality.
If all the conjugation actions on \(W\) commute, restrict instead to one common rational eigenline: the proportion fixing it is exactly \(a\).
Both the Garza estimate and the strict determinant estimate apply there, giving the factor \(50\) after multiplying the latter by two.
This also gives uniform convergence of \(a\) to zero as \(m(w)\to0\), and the bounded-\(s\) assertion follows with \(q\le3^{s^2}\).

For each nontorsion element \(x\) of the divisible conjugate group its nonzero rational span is contained in \(V\).
The fixed-space identity computes its radial rank, so dividing by its span dimension gives a dimension-weighted average of the constituent fixed fractions.
Uniform convergence passes to all these elements simultaneously.
On strict shrinking records the constituent dimension bound from v80 and the complementary argument-rank identity give \((2/\log2-o(1))\log(\deg w)\) under the stated commutation hypothesis.
