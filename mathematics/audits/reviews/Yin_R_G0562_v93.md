# Independent review of the critical values of the odd squarefree cyclotomic trace polynomial

## Torsion derivatives, algebraic-integer fibers and the endpoint signs

At 2026-11-17T00:15 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the entire C345v93 submission dated 2026-11-17T00:10, including its corrected restriction of the two central-sign consequences to conductors with at least two prime factors.
The reviewer took no part in developing the proof and checked the fixed arguments and source interfaces independently.

In the logarithmic derivative \(D\log \Phi_q\), each term contributes \(\mu(q/j)j(h(z^j)+1/2)\).
The constant sum is \(\varphi(q)/2=d\) and is exactly removed by the centering factor \(z^{-d}\).
At order \(m=q\) the cyclotomic zero is already simple.
For other orders \(m>2\), put \(s=(q,m)\) and \(t=q/s\).
Since \(q\) is squarefree, every prime of \(t\) is prime to \(m\), and \(\mu(q/(eb))=\mu(s/e)\mu(t/b)\) for \(e\mid s,\ b\mid t\).
For \(e<m\), no \(eb\) is a multiple of \(m\), and the product of the commuting operators \(p\sigma_p-1\) expands with precisely the coefficients \(\mu(t/b)b\).
When \(s=m\) the omitted terms have \(e=m\), with \(t>1\).
In the local parameter \(z=\zeta e^u\), \(mb\,h(e^{mbu})=u^{-1}+O(u)\) by expansion of the exponential, with zero constant term.
The sum over \(b\) of the pole coefficients vanishes and the contribution to the regular value is zero.
This proves the operator formula also when \(m\) is a proper divisor of \(q\).
The empty operator product is the identity, and each individual operator is invertible on the cyclotomic field because \(p\sigma_p v=v\) implies \(p^{\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_p)}v=v\).

For \(F=1-x\cot x\) the Taylor series converges for \(|x|<\pi\), and \(xF'=x^2-F+F^2\) gives \(c_1=1/3\) and strictly positive subsequent coefficients.
At \(s=1\), \(h(\zeta)\ne0\) since \(\zeta\ne\pm1\).
For \(1<s<m\) all angles \(e\pi/m\) in the divisor sum are below \(\pi\).
The common constant \(1/x\) cancels and the coefficient of \(-c_kx^{2k-1}\) is \(\sum_{e\mid s}\mu(s/e)e^{2k}=\prod_{p\mid s}(p^{2k}-1)>0\).
The real cotangent sum is strictly negative and its multiplier \(-i/2\) is nonzero.

For \(s=m\), \(m\) is odd and squarefree, and substitution of \(\ell=m/e>1\) permits expansion strictly inside the convergence interval.
The constant sum is \(-1\), and the remaining term of order \(2k\) has factor \(1-\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2k})\), at most \(\sum_{p\mid m}p^{-2k}\).
Positivity and monotonicity of \(F(x)/x^2\) bound the resulting sum by \((9-\pi\sqrt3)\sum_{p\mid m}p^{-2}\).
The sum over odd \(j\ge3\) of \(1/(j^2-1)\) telescopes to \(1/4\) by writing \(j=2b+1\).
Thus the entire positive contribution is less than one, since \(9-\pi\sqrt3<4\).
This gives the required strict sign even in the shared-order case.
The rational-coefficient derivative is consequently nonzero at every primitive conjugate and every order greater than two.

The trace polynomial has \(d\) distinct real roots in \((-2,2)\).
Rolle's theorem supplies all \(d-1\) critical points between them, including the empty critical set when \(d=1\).
Their conjugates are still roots of the rational derivative.
If one critical point \(c\) were integral, both roots of \(z^2-cz+1\) would be nonzero algebraic integers, and all images under embeddings would lie on the circle because every conjugate of \(c\) lies strictly between \(-2\) and two.
Kronecker's theorem then forces a root of unity of order greater than two and \(A'_q(z)=(1-z^{-2})\Psi'_q(c)=0\), a contradiction.
If \(a=\Psi_q(c)\) were an algebraic integer, \(c\) would be integral over \(\mathbb Z[a]\) by the monic equation, hence integral over \(\mathbb Z\).
Thus all trace-polynomial fibers over algebraic integers are simple.
For \(d\ge2\), scaling the degree-\((d-1)\) derivative of leading coefficient \(d\) gives a monic integer equation for \(dc\).
Then \(d^d\Psi_q(c)\) is integral, so any rational critical value has reduced denominator dividing \(d^d\) and greater than one.

The trace substitution is unramified off \(z=\pm1\) and has exact ramification index two at both, as follows from the two factorizations of \(z+z^{-1}\mp2\).
The lifted polynomial has constant term one, so there is no additional root at zero.
For composite odd squarefree \(q\), \(d\) is even, \(\Phi_q(1)=1\) and \(\Phi_q(-1)=1\), yielding the retained endpoint values \(\Psi_q(\pm2)=1\).
The plus perturbation is squarefree, and removing both endpoint squares from the minus perturbation leaves a squarefree factor coprime to the endpoints.
For an odd prime \(q\), direct summation gives \(\Psi_q(2)=q\) and \(\Psi_q(-2)=(-1)^d\).
In particular at \(q=3\) the plus perturbation is \((z+1)^2\) and the minus perturbation is \(z^2+1\), consistent with the general simple-fiber rule and the corrected composite restriction.
