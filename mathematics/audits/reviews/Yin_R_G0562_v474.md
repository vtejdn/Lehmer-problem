### Frobenius on the odd orbit and the even orbits

The two types of primitive direction and the removal of all cyclotomic factors agree with C345 v400.
Simplicity and degree growth yield distinct rational trace factors of degrees \(n_i\ge9\).
The trace factor carrying the unique negative exterior root is the unique one of odd degree, and its root becomes Salem after sign change exactly when \(n_0<r\).
For every good prime, the monic polynomial \(H_0\) reduces with degree \(n_0\).
As this is odd, some irreducible factor of its reduction has odd degree, giving a well-defined \(\ell(p)\le n_0\).
At \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}H\), all roots of \(H\) reduce distinctly and their residue Frobenius orbits have lengths equal to the irreducible factor degrees.
Indeed the separable residue roots lift inside the maximal unramified local extension, and reduction preserves their permutation under Frobenius.

Put \(I=L\cap K_m\).
The full independent product \(D=\prod_i A_{n_i}\) lies in \(G\) and is perfect, while \(G/D\) embeds through the orbit signs in a product of groups of order two.
This proves \([G,G]=D\) and implies \(D\subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(L/I)\), since \(I/\mathbb Q\) is abelian.
The Galois group of the compositum consists of the pairs of automorphisms agreeing on \(I\).
Consequently the restriction of the fiber over \(\tau_u\) to \(L\) gives exactly one coset of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/I)\).
Each \(D\)-coset in it is the full product of the individual prescribed sign classes, even when global constraints couple signs on different orbits.

The identity progression permits an \(n_0\)-cycle on the odd orbit and the two cycles \((2,n_i-2)\) on each even orbit.
Each of these permutations is even, and the sole odd cycle in the entire tuple has length \(n_0\).
The tuple extends with the identity on \(K_m\).
Chebotarev supplies infinitely many primes with these conjugacy-invariant properties after omitting the finitely many primes dividing \(m\operatorname{disc}H\).
This proves the maximum in (1), including \(m=1\) and the case of a single rational orbit.

### The obstruction in a reduced residue class

The determinant character on orbit zero acts on its Vandermonde by the permutation sign.
The fixed field of the kernel of this character is \(E_0\), interpreted as \(\mathbb Q\) for the trivial character.
Restriction of the character to \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/I)\) is trivial precisely when \(E_0\subseteq I\), equivalently \(E_0\subseteq K_m\).
In this case the entire fiber has the sign that \(\tau_u\) prescribes on \(E_0\).
Otherwise this character maps \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/I)\) onto \(\{\pm1\}\), and either sign has half the fiber.

Within an even-sized orbit, the even-sign choice \((2,n_i-2)\) and the odd-sign choice \((n_i)\) both avoid odd cycle lengths.
Thus, regardless of the other prescribed signs, an even sign available on orbit zero attains \(n_0\).
For forced odd sign the odd orbit cannot be one \(n_0\)-cycle, since an odd-length cycle is even as a permutation.
It must still contain an odd cycle, whose length is therefore at most \(n_0-2\).
The type \((n_0-2,2)\) has the required odd permutation sign and achieves this precise bound.
Choosing the other orbit permutations as above realizes the tuple in the fiber, proving (2) by Chebotarev.

The single real exterior root together with conjugation of the other exterior roots makes \(s_0\) odd.
For either type of direction the uniform comparison \(2n_0/(rs_0)\to1\) permits, for example, a relative error at most \(1/6\) for sufficiently large \(r\).
When \(s_0=1\) this gives \(n_0\le7r/12<r\).
When \(s_0\ge3\) it gives \(n_0-2\ge5r/4-2\ge r\) for \(r\ge8\).
Thus in the Salem case no good prime passes the exclusion threshold, while in the other case even the smaller maximum in (2) passes it.
The slope used here is independent of the modulus and residue class.
The existence and density conclusions for primes are taken for each fixed input and progression.

### Conductor and conjugation at infinity

The discriminant of a product of coprime monic integral factors is the product of their discriminants times the squared pairwise resultants.
Consequently \(\operatorname{disc}H_0\) divides the nonzero integer \(\operatorname{disc}H\).
Write \(\operatorname{disc}H_0=dq^2\) with signed squarefree \(d\).
For \(d\ne1\) the integer cyclotomic conductor of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\) is \(|d|\) for \(d\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(4|d|\) otherwise.
It divides \(8|\operatorname{disc}H|\), allowing negative and even radicands as well.
For \(d=1\) containment in \(K_m\) is immediate.
The conductor here is the finite integer specifying a cyclotomic field, including for an imaginary quadratic field. [Milne, *Class Field Theory*, example on quadratic cyclotomic conductors](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=168)

For \(m=8|\operatorname{disc}H|\) the automorphism \(\tau_{-1}\) restricts to complex conjugation on \(E_0\).
Each inverse pair off the circle has a unique exterior root, and a nonreal exterior root gives a nonreal trace since \(\operatorname{Im}(z+z^{-1})=(1-|z|^{-2})\operatorname{Im}z\).
Two such roots have distinct traces unless they are inverse partners, which cannot both be exterior.
Circle traces are real, and the only real exterior root in this factor is the marked negative one.
Thus complex conjugation on the trace block has exactly \((s_0-1)/2\) transpositions.
Its sign determines whether the \(-1\) class loses two in (2), while the \(1\) class always attains \(n_0\).
The difference of maxima is consequently \(0\) for \(s_0\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(2\) for \(s_0\equiv3\bmod4\), as asserted.

### Relative prime densities

For even \(d\), the unsigned and signed cycle enumerators of permutations with even cycle lengths are \((1-z^2)^{-1/2}\) and \((1-z^2)^{1/2}\) when counts are divided by \(d!\).
The binomial series gives degree-\(d\) coefficients \(c_d=2^{-d}\binom{d}{d/2}\) and \(-c_d/(d-1)\).
Conditioning on an even or an odd permutation first selects half the sum or half the difference of the coefficients, then divides by \(1/2\) for the sign-class size.
The smaller conditional proportion is \(A(d)=c_d(d-2)/(d-1)>0\).
The odd orbit has \((n_0-1)!\) full cycles and \(n_0!/(2(n_0-2))\) permutations of type \((n_0-2,2)\), since the two cycle lengths are distinct.
Dividing by \(n_0!/2\) gives \(2/n_0\) and \(1/(n_0-2)\) in the respective sign classes.
As an independent arithmetic check, summing the permutation masses \(1/\prod_j j^{m_j}m_j!\) over integer partitions through degree 23 reproduced both even-orbit conditional proportions and both odd-orbit extrema throughout this finite range.

Within each compatible \(D\)-coset the orbit choices are independent, so their probabilities multiply.
For maximum \(n_0\) the usable part of the fiber has proportion \(w=1\) or \(1/2\), and yields the lower bound \(w(2/n_0)\prod_{i>0}A(n_i)\).
For an odd sign forced on orbit zero the whole fiber is usable, yielding \(\prod_{i>0}A(n_i)/(n_0-2)\).
This includes the empty product when there is no even orbit.
All counted cycle events, together with the fixed cyclotomic restriction, are unions of conjugacy classes in the compositum group.
Dividing their Chebotarev densities by the positive density \(1/\varphi(m)\) of the prescribed progression gives exactly the conditional normalization used in the two lower bounds.
