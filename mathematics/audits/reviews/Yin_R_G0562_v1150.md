**Dimensions and applicability of the order test.**
The cubic \(X^3-X-1\) takes the negative value \(-103/1000\) at \(13/10\), so both cutoffs are below the plastic number.
Smyth's result gives reciprocity, and a nontorsion irreducible reciprocal minimum has even degree \(2n\).
The \(u\) interior real traces give \(u\) neutral inverse pairs, leaving \(l=n-u\) individual exterior roots, including both members of each nonreal exterior pair.
The two conjugation eigenspaces have dimensions \(r,k\), hence \(b=r+k\).
The projected-frame bound \(nr\le lb\) counts the actual embeddings and thus allows repeated multiplicative classes.
Substituting \(b=r+k\) gives \(ur\le lk\); substituting \(r=b-k\) gives \(ub\le nk\).
Since \(r\ge1\), the condition \(u>2l\) excludes \(k\le2\) directly.

In the first degree range one can replace a hypothetical counterexample with the product over one class of torsion-equivalent conjugates.
This preserves measure and the rational conjugate module with its \(c\)-action, does not increase degree, and produces distinct actual roots modulo torsion.
Reciprocity persists since inversion sends a class product to the product of its inverse class.
The compressed degree is even and at most 44, and a nontrivial common exponent divisor would supply a pair of roots with root-of-unity quotient.
The compressed representative therefore occurs up to variable sign in the complete catalogue.
Its splitting group acts faithfully on the class module: fixing each class fixes each individual conjugate.
This applies the faithful-representation requirement to the representative reached by compression.
It supplies the exclusion for all original inputs, even when some rows as originally given have not been proved torsion-compressed.
Negating the variable preserves the signature, the factor degrees and squarefreeness in every characteristic.

The distinct cyclotomic factors in the minimal polynomial of a finite-order rational matrix have total degree at most the ambient dimension, and its order is the least common multiple of their orders.
Enumerating these possibilities gives \(1,2,3,4,6\) through dimension three and \(1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12\) through dimension four.
For instance an order from a degree-four cyclotomic factor uses the entire four dimensions, and combining degree-two factors permits the additional lcm \(12\).
These lists also handle a smaller actual rank than its computed upper bound.

**Independent check of all finite certificates.**

This gave \(0<u<n\) on every row.
It gave 810 direct exclusions and left 132 rows with \(\lfloor2n/u\rfloor=3\) and two with \(\lfloor2n/u\rfloor=4\).

For a remaining full polynomial \(P\) at \(p=2,3,11\), the reviewer formed the Frobenius map \(\mathcal T\) on the fixed algebra \(\mathbb F_p[X]/P\).
In the power basis its columns are the remainders of \(X^{pi}\), obtained by multiplication by \(X\) using the full \(P\) throughout.
Modular Gaussian elimination tests squarefreeness by invertibility of \(\mathcal T\): a product of finite fields has bijective Frobenius, whereas a nonzero nilpotent is killed by an iterate.
At squarefree reduction, \(\mathcal T^m(X)=X\) holds precisely when every irreducible factor degree divides \(m\).
Therefore nonidentity on \(X\) at all \(m\in\{4,6\}\), or at all \(m\in\{8,10,12\}\), excludes the respective allowed orders directly.
In addition the reviewer computed \(h_j=\deg\gcd(P,\mathcal T^j(X)-X)\) with the original modulus, through \(j=\deg P\).
The identity \(h_j=\sum_{e\mid j}e\,n_e\) recovers the number \(n_e\) of each factor degree without the native routine's successive factor removals.

**Second cutoff, equalities and degree consequence.**
If the reciprocal minimum is \(Q_0(\pm X^j)\), then the root \(w\) and the corresponding root \(\pm w^j\) of \(Q_0\) have the same rational conjugate span with the induced conjugation action.
Every conjugate of the latter is reached by extending an embedding, and the factor \(j\) does not alter the rational span.
The root product over the \(j\)-th roots of each root of \(Q_0\) preserves the whole multiplicative measure, and irreducibility descends to the core.
Thus exponent primitivity is sufficient in this second argument, which uses no order test on uncompressed rows.

Using diagonal-convolution root squaring and integer upper and lower coefficient comparisons, the reviewer again obtained the fourteen rows below \(3049/2500\), with 930 above through degree 44.
The five higher-degree lists had \(107,80,76,66,66\) rows respectively above the cutoff.
The published result for even degrees \(46,48,50,52,54\) is used only for its minimizing polynomials there, whose coefficients were matched in the [earlier catalogue computation](Yin_R_G0562_v1113.md#computation).
Odd degree 55 is excluded by reciprocity.
The secondary strict cutoff \(601/500\) selects exactly lines \(154,168,195,196\), all among the fourteen.

The five rows with one real exterior root have degree at least ten, so \(k\ge u/l=n-1\ge4\).
Eight rows with one nonreal exterior pair have degree at least eighteen and hence \(k\ge\lceil(n-2)/2\rceil\ge4\).
Line 197 has class dimension nine and radial rank two by its signed-group calculation, giving argument rank seven.
The three rational endpoints are nonintegral and cannot be the multiplicative measure of a unit: the exterior roots are closed under conjugation and their product up to sign is its measure, an algebraic unit.
A nonunit nonzero algebraic integer has measure at least two by its integer norm.
Finally Lehmer's degree-ten Salem root is below \(59/50\), as \(L(1)=-1\) and \(L(59/50)=9247445562263351/97656250000000000>0\) for its known minimum with a unique exterior root.
Thus a rank-at-most-two unit below Lehmer's number lies within both the second rational cutoff and the stated finite-reduction interface of v88.
It must have degree at least 56; the degree-55 argument leaves degree 56 open.
