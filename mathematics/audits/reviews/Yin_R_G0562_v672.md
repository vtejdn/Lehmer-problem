On the subfamily of size-\(Q\) blocks the compositum theorem gives the full direct product over the auxiliary field, even when several factors have the same block size.
This rules out diagonal linkage between isomorphic alternating factors there.
For the smaller fields, filter their compositum group by the kernels of successive coordinate projections.
Each successive quotient lies in one smaller wreath product.
Its block action has order at most \(u_i!\), and its kernel has a further coordinate filtration with quotients of order at most \(q_i!\).
Both bounds are at most \((Q-1)!<|A_Q|\).
The intersection field is Galois, and its group, a quotient of the smaller compositum group, inherits that exclusion.
The normal direct product in the larger-block group therefore maps trivially to the intersection group, since a nontrivial normal image would contribute \(A_Q\) as a composition factor.
Galois restriction over the intersection permits each supported three-cycle to act trivially on the smaller fields.

Squaring a lift kills each permutation of a fixed inverse pair and carries the selected triple around a three-cycle in the opposite direction.

The difference of the transformed and original exponent vectors gives a relation there, and its support omits a letter from the block since \(Q>3\).
Single-factor independence forces that difference to vanish, giving equal absolute coefficients around the triple for all possible signs.
Each nonzero block in an occurring maximal factor therefore contributes at least \(Q\) distinct pairs, while omitting one letter per block precludes a relation altogether and gives the asserted additive rank bound.

Its height is bounded by the sum of the absolute character exponents times the individual heights, each tending uniformly to zero by the critical estimates.
The small-height phase argument therefore gives a vanishing character mean, preserved under equal embedding multiplicities in the compositum.
