# Alternating sections and local finiteness of logarithmic Mahler measures

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T15:44 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently inspected the complete [C345 v264 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#decision-alternating-sections-mahler-spectrum-submitted) for Yin.
The review used the fixed argument and its mathematical sources without access to its derivation or author explanations.
For every fixed \(s\ge5\) and \(M>0\), exclusion of an \(A_s\) section gives finitely many compressed numbers and hence finitely many positive Mahler measures up to \(M\).
The proof also establishes its quantitative constraint at every minimal block and its specialization to the stated towers.

### From conjugates to local permutation groups

Multiplying an entire torsion-quotient class gives a conjugate of the product in the selected class, and division of any two such products gives a root of unity only when the original representatives already have torsion quotient.
All classes have size \(f\), so the product has exactly \(d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]/f\) conjugates.
Its absolute height is \(fh(\alpha)\) because it is a root of unity times \(\alpha^f\), including when finite places contribute.
Its normal closure lies in that of \(\alpha\).
After compression there is at most one root per point modulo torsion, and pairing a point with its negative gives at most two per label.
Transitivity makes this number \(w\) constant.
Distinct labels give independent roots because equality between two nonzero powers forces their exponents to have equal absolute value by positivity and equality of the two heights.

Take any block \(B\) of size \(b\ge2\).
For two distinct labels in it the ordered pair of root images has at most \(dwb\) possibilities, as the first image fixes the translated block.
The absolute joint degree is also at most \(d^2\).
The rank-two Amoroso–David inequality has logarithmic exponent \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\).
Substitution gives \(b\gg d/(m(\gamma)^2\log^{212}(3d))\), hence \(\log b\gg\log d\) for large \(d\) at fixed \(M\).
The stabilizer is transitive on \(B\), and its permutation image on the actual roots maps onto its image \(H\) on labels.
The former embeds in the integral automorphism group of the rank-\(r\) lattice generated by those roots modulo torsion because a torsion-quotient mate of any root equals that root.
An element fixing the labels but exchanging two roots in a label still acts nontrivially on the lattice.
For a putative element \(I+3^aT\) of prime order \(\ell\) in the level-three congruence kernel, choose \(a\) such that \(T\not\equiv0\bmod3\).
Its \(\ell\)-th power differs from \(I\) modulo \(3^{a+1}\) for \(\ell\ne3\) and modulo \(3^{a+2}\) for \(\ell=3\).
Thus \(b\le |H|\le3^{r^2}\), giving \(r\gg\sqrt{\log d}\).

Choose \(k\le r\) independent elements of the generating set of actual roots.
The image of their block has \(u=d/(wb)\) choices, their ordered label images have at most \(|H|\) choices there, and the images of the individual roots have at most \(2^k\) possibilities above the chosen labels.
This bounds the absolute joint degree by \(2^k u|H|\) and also bounds the joint degree \(J\) over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
The argument applies to the entire set as a block and makes no independence assumption on the actions in its translates.

### Growing dimension and alternating sections

Delsinne's product theorem applies at height \(h=m(\gamma)/d\) to the independent chosen roots with relative joint degree \(J\).
Writing \(A_k=2(k+1)^2(k+1)!\), one has \(\log A_k=O(k\log(2k))\).
The formulas \(3kA_k^k\) and \(k\log(2k^2)+64k^2k!A_k^{2k}\) therefore bound \(\kappa_2(k)\) and \(\log c_2(k)\) by \(\exp(Ck^2\log(2k))\) with an absolute constant.
Put \(L=\log\log(16d)\) and \(k=\lfloor c_1\sqrt{L/\log(2+L)}\rfloor\).
For small absolute \(c_1\), \(Ck^2\log(2k)\le(\log\log d)/3\) eventually, while \(k\to\infty\) and \(k\le r\).
If \(|H|\le d^{k/2}\), then \(\log J\le k\log2+(1+k/2)\log d\le(3k/4)\log d\), so \(\log\log(3J)=O(1+L)\).
Delsinne's denominator now requires \((k/4)\log d\le k\log M+O((\log d)^{1/3}(1+L))\), impossible at fixed \(M\).
This establishes the order bound uniformly over all the asserted blocks.

A block of the setwise stabilizer inside \(B\) is a block for the full group, because any element moving \(B\) takes it to a disjoint translate.
Hence at a minimal nontrivial block \(H\) acts primitively.
Babai's Definition 4.1 uses quotients of subgroups to define alternating thickness \(j\), and Theorem 4.2 gives \(\log |H|=O(j\log b)\), with the improved exponent attributed to Pyber.
The [Bab18 source unit](../literature/Bab18/Yin_U001.md#thickness) records the faithful permutation image and one absolute exponent multiplier for all degrees and all thicknesses.
I checked the two passages on p. 6 of the author manuscript and on pp. 3307–3308 of the author-hosted typeset copy, together with the restrictions on composition factors in the abstract of the 1982 publication.
Together with \(b\le d\), the order bound yields \(j\gg V(d)\). [Babai manuscript, §4](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10179670) · [Author-hosted typeset copy](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/icm18-babai.pdf) · [Babai–Cameron–Pálfy](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(82)90323-4)

The image \(H\) is a quotient of a subgroup of a quotient of \(G_\alpha\), and taking subgroup preimages lifts its sections to \(G_\alpha\).
For large \(d\), \(A_j\) is perfect and simple.
If \(U\le G_\alpha\) maps onto it, then \(U^{(\infty)}\le G_\alpha^{(\infty)}\) still maps onto it and the solvable normal group \(U^{(\infty)}\cap R(G_\alpha)\) maps trivially.
Thus it survives as a section of the indicated quotient.
Excluding \(A_s\) bounds \(j\) by \(s-1\), since \(A_j\) contains \(A_s\) for \(j\ge s\).
A one-label action already has \(d\le2\).
Otherwise the lower bound for \(j\) bounds \(d\), and the coefficient estimate including the leading coefficient gives finitely many primitive minimal polynomials of \(\gamma\).
The same estimate forces \(d\to\infty\) along any sequence of positive measures tending to zero.
Both occurrences in the submission now explicitly impose positivity, as required to exclude the constant sequence \(\alpha=1\).
Taking \(M=1\) yields a positive uniform gap even if that interval contributes no measures.

### Tower specialization

For a quotient \(U\twoheadrightarrow A_s\) of a subgroup of an extension with kernel \(N\), the image of \(U\cap N\) is either all of \(A_s\) or trivial.
This proves preservation of exclusion under extensions, in particular finite products, in addition to subgroups and quotients.
The choice \(s=\max(5,n+1)\) gives \(|A_s|>n!\).
Splitting the conjugates of an adjunction of degree at most \(n\) above the preceding normal closure has relative group embedding in a product of copies of \(S_n\).
For an arbitrary radical adjunction one first adjoins its roots of unity and then roots of every conjugate radicand.
The first relative group is abelian, and the second injects in a product of groups of roots of unity.
The resulting field is normal over \(\mathbb Q\), so induction and finite composita give the asserted conclusion also for the splitting fields of elements of the towers.
For radical-solvable elements alone \(s=5\) suffices.
Eisenstein at two verifies the degree in the example \(2^{1/n}\), whose conjugates have the same modulus and total logarithmic measure \(\log2\).
The finiteness statement bounds the compressed numbers and the measure values, while that example attains one value in unbounded original degrees.
The external results have been checked at theorem and application scope.
