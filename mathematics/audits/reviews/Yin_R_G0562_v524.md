# Signed kernel area and coefficient bounds at octic width nine

Sheng Yin, 2026-11-18T22:49.
An agent outside the development of L255 independently reviewed the whole fixed ledger.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the octic cross-ratio in L252 and had received an earlier development outline for the width-eight claim C413.
C413 is used here at its stated interfaces; this review concerns the new width-nine plane and row argument.

## Outcome of the proof review

Both signed lattice areas have logarithm \(9ph+o(p)\) for the fixed octic.
The uncapped genuine coefficient cost at width nine has lower limiting rate at least \(21h/8\) and upper limiting rate at most \(9h/2\).
Every genuine pair of cost at most \(2^p\) at a given sufficiently large integer \(p\) selects the same rational line as every other such pair in each sign separately.
The existence of a pair at that cutoff remains open.

### The rank, the two resultants and the lattice area

The two rows at each representative are its signed graph value and Euler derivative.
At \(N=0\) they require the coefficient polynomial to vanish at \(Y_i,Y_i^{-1}\).
Thus in ten coefficient dimensions they leave exactly two parameters over the variable field.
The inverse representatives give equivalent equations after specialization by formal reversal about \(9p+1\).
Every nonzero minor used to witness rank specializes to a nondegenerate recurrence with polynomial coefficients, so rank eight holds for every sufficiently large positive integer \(p\).
The specialized kernel over the splitting field is the scalar extension of the rational kernel because the equivalent remainder equations are rational.

I independently formed each of the four deleted-column determinants as a polynomial by Laplace expansion on its first two rows.
For retained columns \(i<j\) the two-row determinant has contributions
\[
 5(j-i)T^{i+j}
 +5(i-j)x_1^2T^{18-i-j}
 +\epsilon x_1(46-5j-5i)T^{i+9-j}
 +\epsilon x_1(5j+5i-46)T^{9-i+j}.
\]
For each pair I multiplied by the signed complementary six-row determinant, preserving the retained column order, and added modulo \(1987\).
Computing the polynomial resultants and gcds of these coefficient lists gave the following values.

| Sign | Degree pair | Constant terms | Leading terms | Resultant | Gcd degree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| \(+\) | \(18,18\) | \(1812,903\) | \(175,1084\) | 573 | 0 |
| \(-\) | \(18,18\) | \(1218,494\) | \(769,1493\) | 521 | 0 |

Only two rows involve \(T\), so the a priori degree bound eighteen also holds before specializing the other variables.
The resultant is a polynomial in the coefficients of that size, and its nonzero image proves coprimality in \(Y_1\) over the generic coefficient field.
Thus the common factor removed from all the minors has \(Y_1\)-degree zero.
The nonzero leading and constant terms prove actual width eighteen after reduction.
Conjugation permutes the inverse pairs transitively, and the reversal identity handles any simultaneous inversions in that permutation.
The plane is preserved, so its primitive Plücker tuple changes only by a common Laurent unit after the corresponding variable substitution.
All four variable widths are therefore eighteen for each sign.

The fixed-place recurrence estimate handles ties of dominant monomials, and the finite common valuations of the primitive tuple are \(o(p)\) by the coprime-combination and almost-unit argument in C413.
For the Salem embeddings only one representative has nonzero log modulus, with either sign occurring at proportion \(1/8\) for each index.
This gives projective height \(ph(4\cdot18)/8+o(p)=9ph+o(p)\).
An integral basis of the saturated rank-two lattice has primitive wedge, so the logarithm of its largest coordinate is this projective height and the Euclidean length of the wedge is the area \(\Delta\).
Their ratio is bounded because there are only 45 coordinates.
Completing a shortest vector to a basis and reducing the parallel projection of the second vector to at most \(\lambda_1/2\) gives perpendicular length \(\Delta/\lambda_1\ge\sqrt3\,\lambda_1/2\).
The reduced second vector consequently has length at most \(2\Delta/(\sqrt3\,\lambda_1)\); independent vectors have parallelogram area at least \(\Delta\).
This proves both comparisons for the successive minima and (1).

### Short signed rows and the cutoff

Multiplication of a width-eight signed pair by \(1+Y\) retains its sign at width nine, while multiplication by \(Y-1\) exchanges the signs.
The two width-eight pairs have nonzero determinant for all sufficiently large \(p\), so the stated two candidates per sign form bases of the two planes.
The four first coordinates are primitive by multiplicativity of content, and multiplication by either binomial and division on its image distort norm by bounded factors in these fixed dimensions.
Each of these directions therefore has shortest integral vector of logarithmic size \(8ph+o(p)\).
The shortest integral vector of either full plane has size at most \(\exp((9/2)ph+o(p))\) and hence cannot belong to those directions.
In the notation of L255, all four expansion scalars are nonzero.
The mixed determinant is \((ac(1+Y)^2-bd(Y-1)^2)\det(R_+,R_-)\), a nonzero polynomial since the two squares are linearly independent.
The sum of the short pairs has double divisibility, and its swapped reversal is their difference.
Their determinant differs from the mixed determinant by the nonzero factor \(-2\), so \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\).
Both rows of this sum are nonzero, since a one-row graph with the double factor would require \(Q^2\) in its nonzero row, of degree at most nine.

If a nonzero rational signed vector vanishes at one powered conjugate then \(A=Q(c_0+c_1Y)\).
Dividing its graph by \(Q(X^p)\) leaves a value constraint at each representative, since the removed zeros are simple.
The two rows of constraints have independent varying slopes, as the function \((Y+\epsilon x_i)/(1+\epsilon x_iY)\) is nonconstant for \(x_i^2\ne1\).
The resulting nonzero determinant specializes eventually nonzero; hence \(c_0=c_1=0\) would follow.
For the constructed sum a common \(Q\) in \(U,V\) would consequently be impossible, because it divides their formal reversals at width nine as well and thus both signed projections.
An integral primitive representative of the actual row gcd divides both rows in \(\mathbb Z[Y]\).
Its substitution is nonzero at every original conjugate, so dividing preserves double divisibility, and the reflected determinant loses just its nonzero pair of common factors.
The quotient one-norm is at most \(2^9\) times the original one-norm by the fixed-degree Mahler coefficient bound and \(M(\gcd)\ge1\).
This proves existence of genuine rows with the claimed upper rate at all sufficiently large integers.

The fixed-seed norm bound at \(e=8,w=9\) gives \((28-7)h/8\) for each genuine pair and also for each integral signed vector by its just-proved nonvanishing.
Thus (1) bounds the second limiting minimum from below by \(9h/2\) and from above by \((9-21/8)h\) in the stated liminf and limsup senses.
Two independent signed projections at the cutoff would both have length at most \(2^{p+1}\), contradicting the lower estimate for the second minimum.
I recomputed \(10^8P(13/10)=39127721\), \(2^8 10^{21}-13^{21}=8935470926549607295587\) and \(32^9-4\cdot25^9=19925583026332\).
Together with \(32/25<\rho<13/10\) these certify \(21h/8<\log2<9h/2<8h\).
