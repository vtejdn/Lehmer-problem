## Independent review of the weighted remainder argument

At 2026-11-20T21:09 UTC a separate agent for Yin reviewed the statement and both arguments of C G0345 v649.
The reviewer had no part in developing the proof and received its fixed text, the requested scope and the cited inputs without development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings on v649.
The conclusions (1) through (4), the limiting sign assertion and the application to the balanced remainder follow under the stated hypotheses.

Since \(P(0)=\pm1\), each nonzero constant in the stripping recursion gives a nonzero integer \(q_i\).
The numerator after subtraction has degree \(d\) and positive vanishing order, so stripping can continue indefinitely.
The identity for \(X^{u_i}T_i/P\) identifies each \(q_i\) with the Taylor coefficient at \(u_i\) by expansion at zero, without using convergence on the circle.
If \(F\) is the quotient minimizing the squared norm at constant numerator coefficient one, then \(\|F\|_2^2=C\) and the zero-constant subspace is orthogonal to \(F\).
Thus \(e=F/\sqrt C\) pairs with the weighted sum of remainders to give \(P(0)\sqrt C\sum_{i<N}q_i^2\) in the first-slot linear convention.
For each fixed \(i\), the shifted prefix has squared norm \(\sum_{j<i}q_j^2\) by orthogonality.
Cauchy–Schwarz on the \(N-1\) nonempty prefixes and the identity \(2\sum_{j<i<N}q_i^2q_j^2=b^4-\sum_{i<N}q_i^4\) give the claimed error.
At \(N=1\) the error is zero.

For \(2\le r\le\infty\), interpolation between \(b\) in \(L^2\) and \(\sqrt N b\) in \(L^\infty\) gives the factor \(N^{1/2-1/r}\).
In the product inequality this is \(N^{1/p}\), with \(r=\infty\) at \(p=2\) and \(r=2\) at \(p=\infty\).
For \(N=\lfloor C/2\rfloor\), the norm lower bound from (1) and \(b\ge\sqrt N\) is asymptotic to \(2^{-3/2+1/p}C^{1-1/p}\).
At the supremum endpoint \(mC\to1/4\) gives \(1/(8\sqrt2)\).
The reciprocal comparison also holds for repeated factors because the argument in v630 uses \(|P|=e^m|p^*|^2\) and orthogonality for the interior factor \(p\), which allow its roots to be counted with multiplicity.

The normalized polynomial \(D_N\) has squared norm \(1/N\) and supremum at most one, so the measure estimate follows from Chebyshev's inequality.
Splitting the weighted energy at \(|D_N|=\tau\) gives the factor \(1-\tau^2\) multiplying the energy on the selected set.
When \(N=\lfloor C/2\rfloor\), the first numerator term in (4) is at least \(C/4\), while the choice \(\tau^2=1/(8D)\) subtracts at most \(C/8\).
At scaled energy \(1/2\), one has \(\|H\|_2^2/C\to2\).
If \(N\to\infty\) and \(N=o(C)\), (4) therefore captures at least a fraction \(1/2-o(1)\) on a set of measure at most \(N^{-1/2}\).
The finite stripping estimate uses only the starting norm and \(Cq_i^2\le\|T_i/P\|_2^2\), giving \(\max_{i<N}q_i^2\le2+o(1)\).
Nonzero integrality forces every prefix coefficient to have modulus one simultaneously, so \(D_N=N^{-1}\sum_{i<N}q_i X^{-u_i}\).
The equality implication of v643 supplies the required starting energy for the balanced remainder after a monomial translation, which preserves its absolute value on the circle.
The review establishes concentration for that remainder and leaves the distribution of the removed polynomial open.
