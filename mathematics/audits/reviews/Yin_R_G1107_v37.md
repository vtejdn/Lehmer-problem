### Norm, degree and reciprocal structure

The identity \(a^\#=-a+2z\) is exact for real b.
Consequently the original expression equals \(z^m a+a^\#\), including all low-degree overlaps.
Conjugate reversal at degree \(m+2\) interchanges its two summands.
Thus reversing g alone gives its coefficient conjugate, and reversing their product gives the same P.

The leading and constant coefficients of g are both one for every \(m\ge1\).
The product has degree \(2m+4\), constant coefficient one and, for integer b, integer coefficients through \(P=A^2+B^2\).
There is no cancellation of a leading term or zero root in any permitted case.
Coefficient conjugation pairs the root multisets of g and its conjugate without changing moduli.
Multiplicativity of the full root product therefore gives \(M(P)=M(g)^2\), including common roots, repeated factors and the real b extension.

### Uniform large-cycle comparison and source applicability

For \(z=s e^{i\theta}\) with \(1\le s\le6/5\), the relevant real part is
\[
1+(s-s^{-1})\cos\theta\ge1-11/30=19/30.
\]

Hence a has no zero on the entire closed annulus, including both circles.
Its two root moduli have product one.
With neither root on the unit circle, exactly one lies inside it and the other lies strictly beyond radius \(6/5\), with multiplicities accounted for.

On the outer circle, \(a^\#/z=2-a/z\).
For \(Y=x+iy=a/z\), the displayed squared ratio is
\[
|2-Y|^2/|Y|^2=1+4(1-x)/(x^2+y^2).
