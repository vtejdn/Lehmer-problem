## Necessary rational limits for the two-term Chebyshev numerator

Both classifications hold as necessary conditions on subsequential limits.

### Rational reduction and the four open arcs

The geometric sum \((1-z^2)z^hU_h((z+z^{-1})/2)=1-z^{2h+2}\) gives the product \(2-z^d+z^{2h+2-d}(1-2z^d)\) with the lifted denominator.
For fixed \(d\) the divisor condition \(q-1\mid h+d+1\) forces \(h\to\infty\).
Each [mass-two limit](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) has positive real part, and [eventually periodic coefficients](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) give a reduced \(K=N/V\) with integral numerator and squarefree cyclotomic denominator normalized at zero.
The quotient \((2-z^d)/K\) is analytic in the disk and has integral Taylor coefficients by coefficientwise convergence of the integral \(B^\sharp\).
The [integer shift argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#cyclotomic) therefore makes all its poles torsion.
The only possible nontorsion zeros of \(N\) are simple roots of \(2-z^d\).
If there were none, \(N(0)=2\) would make \(K\) twice an integral series, impossible because multiplying by the integral limit of \(B^\sharp\) gives coefficient \(-1\) at degree \(d\).
Eisenstein's criterion at 2 then gives the whole factor \(2-z^d\) exactly once.
The other zeros have multiplicity one by the radial lower bound \(\Re K(r\xi)\ge2(1-r)/(1+r)\), so \(U=N/(2-z^d)\) is a squarefree signed cyclotomic product with constant one.

Reciprocity gives \(K(z)+K(1/z)=(U/V)P(z^g)\).
This density is nonnegative and has [nonzero continuous mass](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v5.md#radial-limits).
The map \(z\mapsto z^g\) preserves local multiplicity at circle points and sends torsion points to torsion points.
Thus an odd-order circle zero of \(P\) must be torsion, since multiplication by \(U/V\) cannot change the parity of the order over a nontorsion point.
For \(\alpha=w^a\) and \(\beta=(2-\alpha)/(2\alpha-1)=-\epsilon w^b\), both torsion, \((\beta-\alpha)/2=\alpha\beta-1\) is an algebraic integer with all conjugates of modulus at most one.
It is zero or torsion by Kronecker's theorem.
The former case gives \(\beta=\alpha\) and \(\alpha=\pm1\), and the latter gives \(\beta=-\alpha\) and \(\alpha^2-\alpha+1=0\).
It follows that \(w^a,w^b\) are sixth roots, and Bézout's identity gives \(w^6=1\) even for negative \(b\).

Across the \(a\) sectors the boundary condition for \(af(t)/(2\pi)\) is the union of residue classes \(a\mathbb Z-rb+\eta\), which exhausts \(\mathbb Z+\eta\) by coprimality.
At \(t=0,\pi/3,\pi,5\pi/3,2\pi\), the continuous argument of the degree-one Blaschke factor takes \(0,2\pi/3,\pi,4\pi/3,2\pi\).
Putting \(s=a+b\) gives the five scaled phase values of (1).
If consecutive values strictly straddle a level, real analyticity and the sign change give an odd-order crossing in an open arc, where \(w^a\) avoids all four permitted values.
The complex exponential has nonzero derivative at the crossed phase, so the zero order of \(P\) is the same odd number.
Endpoints touching a level are correctly allowed in this test.
The four step lengths are bounded by one, whence \(|s|\le4,\ |a+s|\le6,\ |2s-a|\le6\).
Twice \(a+s\le6\) plus \(a-2s\le6\) gives \(a\le6\), with \(a=6\) forcing \(s=0\) and violating coprimality.

### Exact enumeration and torsion factors

I tested \(1\le a\le5,\ -4\le s\le4\) with \(\gcd(a,s)=1\).
For each consecutive pair \(x,y\) of rational phase values the least level strictly greater than \(\min(x,y)\) is \(\lfloor\min(x,y)-\eta\rfloor+1+\eta\).
Requiring this to be at least \(\max(x,y)\) for all four pairs gives the following exact output before the open-arc test for \(a=5\).

| \(a\) | \(b=s-a\) for \(\epsilon=+1\) | \(b=s-a\) for \(\epsilon=-1\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | \(-2,-1,0,1\) | \(-3,-2,-1,0,1\) |
| 2 | \(-1\) | \(-1\) |
| 3 | \(-4\) | \(-2,-1\) |

| 5 | none | \(-4\) |

For \((5,-4,-1)\), direct expansion at \(w=e^{2it}\) gives \(P(w)=-2i e^{it}(2\sin t+\sin 9t)\).
The real factor is positive at \(\pi/9\) and vanishes at \(\pi/6\) with derivative \(\sqrt3>0\).
The intervening sign change gives an odd-order zero with \(10\pi/9<\arg(w^5)<5\pi/3\), excluding this last triple.

For each remaining \(a\ge2\), I tested \(w=e^{2\pi i r/6}\), \(r=0,\ldots,5\).
At \(w^a=1,-1\), \(w^b\) must equal \(-\epsilon,\epsilon\) respectively, and at a primitive sixth root it must equal \(\epsilon w^a\).
The last column below uses \(v-u=g(a+b)\).

The phase derivative is \(b+aP_{1/2}(\arg w^a)\), with the Poisson kernel equal to \(3\) or \(1/3\) at \(1\) or \(-1\).
All listed roots are simple except \(w=-1\) for \((3,-1,-)\), where the quadratic minimum of the kernel gives a zero of order two of the phase derivative and hence order three of the bracket.
Squarefreeness of \(U,V\) and even multiplicities in the nonnegative density require \(UV\) to contain precisely the odd-order circle zeros upstairs, each once.
Their number is \(g|a+b|=|v-u|\) in all seven rows.
Thus \(u+v=|v-u|\) and the sign of \(a+b\) fixes which factor is constant, justifying the normalized quotients even when \(1\pm z^g\) is reducible.
For \(a=1\) the [offset-one calculation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#phase) applies to the bracket in \(w\), with rational factor allocation performed after substituting \(w=z^g\).

### Unbounded offset

For either sign in \(C_h\), the three nonconstant exponents in the finite product are \(d,2h+2-d,2h+2\).
All tend to infinity under \(h\ge d\to\infty\), so \(1/W=2/K\) has integral Taylor coefficients as the limit of the lifted denominators.
The same pole argument and radial zero bound give \(W=U/V\) with both factors normalized at zero, cyclotomic, squarefree and coprime.
The doubled density for the Herglotz measure of \(W\) is \((U/V)(1+\epsilon z^{v-u})\), and the corresponding density for \(K=2W\) is twice that expression.
It is nonnegative and nonzero in either normalization.
For \(v=u\) the bracket is a nonzero constant, and simple zeros or poles of \(U/V\) would violate the even orders or regularity of the density.
For \(v\ne u\) the bracket has exactly \(|v-u|\) simple circle roots, which consequently exhaust the roots of \(UV\).
Thus one factor is constant and the other is \(1\pm z^{|v-u|}\) by its roots and normalization.
This proves the five families, including \(W=1\), for any relative growth of \(h\) and \(d\).
The argument supplies necessary forms and leaves their realization as limits undecided.
