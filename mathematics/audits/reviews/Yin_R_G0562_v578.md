## Independent whole-proof finding

At 2026-11-19T03:25 UTC I reviewed the [signed-column theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v446.md#statement) for Sheng Yin.
I had no part in developing the submitted argument and received its fixed text and scope without development dialogue, explanations or earlier findings on this theorem.
I find that every set of at most \(m=\deg G\) columns is independent throughout the stated eventual width range, and that the procedure for a fixed width certifies infinitely many dyadic exponents.
I checked the external almost-unit result at statement and application scope.

### The maximal base and its coefficient

In the multiplicative group generated by the roots, the images of distinct roots modulo torsion are distinct and nonzero.
An inverse pair gives opposite vectors, so a linear functional outside finitely many hyperplanes assigns exactly one member positive weight and orders all positive representatives strictly.
Evaluation and the Euler derivative at such a member both vanish on multiples of \(G^2\), as \(G\) is squarefree and its roots are nonzero.
Consequently an invertible square minor of these equations over the splitting field certifies rational independence of the corresponding remainder columns.

For any permutation of the column indices the weight of a term is a sum of its chosen exponents times their assigned positive node weights.
Replacing a lower exponent by the higher one increases the weight strictly, unless the two exponents coincide at the middle column.
Once every column has its larger exponent, interchanging an inverted pair of unequal levels at distinct nodes increases weight by the product of the two positive differences.
Thus the sorted levels allotted to each node are fixed in every maximizer, including the single last slot when the number of columns is odd.
Each maximizing assignment gives precisely the product of the node roots to these total exponents.
A smaller weight excludes even a torsion multiple of that product.

I checked the coefficient by summing over orders of columns at each double node first.
For a pair of levels \(l<l'\) the two-row determinant, divided by the base and by \(n(l'-l)\), is the product of the value multipliers times \(1+(e'-e)/(n(l'-l))\).
The shifts are bounded over the finite set of roots, including the middle-column shift \(\varepsilon\alpha/(1+\varepsilon\alpha)\) since no root equals \(\pm1\).
For a repeated level at one node the two columns are reflected partners, and their shifts differ by one, giving a determinant of absolute value \(|\alpha|>0\) after stripping the base.
The middle column occurs just once.

A level split across two nodes uses two consecutive slots and consists of one direct and one reflected column.
Exchanging the partners changes the permutation sign.
At leading order the two assignments contribute the alternating factor \(\pm\varepsilon(\beta-\alpha)\) for these distinct nodes.
Split pairs have disjoint columns, and at a node shared by two such pairs its distinct-level leading determinant is a product of one multiplier from each pair.
The signed sum therefore factors even in that configuration.
After the common normalization the leading products range over a finite set of nonzero numbers determined by the roots and sign.
The number of assignments is bounded in terms of \(m\), and every omitted correction is uniformly \(O_G(1/n)\), since a positive integer difference of levels is at least one.
This proves survival of the maximal base for every sufficiently large \(n\), including one-column minors and widths with a middle column.

### Uniform height estimates and the external input

There are at most \(2^s s!\) ungrouped terms for \(s\) columns.
Their coefficients contain at most \(s\) fixed root factors and derivative multipliers of size at most \(nb+1\).
The height of each grouped coefficient is thus bounded by \(H=C_G(1+\log n+\log(2+b))\), by the sum and product inequalities and height zero of roots of unity.
Any two bases of different surviving groups have a nontorsion unit ratio \(\gamma\) in the fixed splitting field \(K\).
Northcott finiteness and the height-zero criterion give a uniform lower bound \(h(\gamma)\ge\eta_K>0\).

In a minimal vanishing subsum normalized to \(y_1+\cdots+y_p=1\), write each \(y_i=q_i\gamma_i^n\) with \(h(q_i)\le2H\).
Then \(h(y_i)\ge n\eta_K-2H\) for every \(i\).
At a finite place \(\gamma_i\) is a unit, hence the total of the positive local logarithms of \(y_i\) and \(y_i^{-1}\) outside the archimedean set is at most \(2h(q_i)\le4H\).
On the exponential width range the chosen constants give \(4H\le4pH\le\delta_0 n\eta_K/2\le\delta_0 h(y_i)\).
This verifies the almost-unit condition separately for every coordinate even as the bases, coefficients and supports vary.

I compared Definition 1.2, Remark 2.9 and Lemma 2.11 of the retained arXiv v3 with this normalization.
Xiao's lemma uses the Cartesian power of the scalar almost-unit set and requires \(0<\delta_0<1/[p(p+1)]\).
The minimal subsum gives the required nondegeneracy.
For every one of the finitely many possible \(p\ge2\) the lemma gives finitely many such tuples, whereas their individual heights grow at least linearly in \(n\).
For \(p=1\) the equation itself forces height zero.
These conclusions also apply to \(\log(2+b)=o(n)\), eventually inside the exponential width range.
I used the finiteness statement as an external theorem, with Evertse's proof outside the review scope. [Source statement](../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=11)

### Sharp support for a unit and its square

In the remainder algebra \(X\) is a unit and reciprocal substitution respects the ideal \((G^2)\).
Multiplying reciprocal substitution by \(X^{nb+1}\) gives an involution, and applying it to a signed graph multiplies that graph by \(\varepsilon\).
After scalar extension each inverse pair supplies two two-dimensional local factors interchanged by mutually inverse linear maps.
Hence either sign eigenspace has dimension two per inverse pair, and rational dimension \(m\) in total.
For \(b<m\) the entire collection is independent.
For \(b\ge m\) every \(m+1\) selected columns therefore have a one-dimensional rational relation space, and every nonzero relation has full support because deleting any column gives an independent set.
Clearing denominators preserves that support.

For the indicated unit \(\delta\), all positive powers having the same field implies that distinct embeddings give distinct images under every such power.
A torsion quotient of distinct conjugates of \(\delta\) or of \(\delta^2\) would violate this property.
Across the two orbits the heights are respectively \(h(\delta)>0\) and \(2h(\delta)\), ruling out torsion quotients there as well.
The two reciprocal minima thus have combined degree \(2\deg P\).
A passing row for this pair has at least \(2\deg P+1\) nonzero positions.
The integral relations from the dimension argument certify divisibility alone.

### The fixed-width algorithm

For fixed input, eventual independence implies termination of the rational rank search over \(n=2^a\) with \(a\ge2+\lfloor\log_2 D\rfloor\), where \(D=\deg G^2\).
Reduction modulo the monic integer polynomial gives integral remainder columns, so one nonzero integer minor for each nonempty tested subset suffices.
Every odd prime dividing \(2t^2+1\) is coprime to \(t\) and admits the square root \(2t\) of \(-2\) modulo that prime.
The supplementary quadratic reciprocity laws put it in residue class \(1\) or \(3\) modulo eight.
Since \(t\) is odd the product is \(3\) modulo eight, so at least one prime factor is \(3\) modulo eight and avoids every selected minor by the choice of \(t\).

The nonzero constant coefficient of \(G^2\) is one, so \(X\) is invertible over the finite remainder ring.
After reduction to the residue fields the kernel on units is a \(p\)-group, since a sufficiently large \(p\)-power kills the nilpotent part of any element \(1+z\) in the kernel.
Thus the \(2\)-part of the order \(L\) of \(X\) is bounded by the largest \(2\)-part of \(p^f-1\) for the residue degrees \(f\le D\).
For \(p\equiv3\bmod8\) the valuations are \(1\) at odd \(f\) and \(2+v_2(f)\) at even \(f\), giving the advertised bound.
At each stated \(k\), both \(2^a\) and \(2^k\) are zero modulo the \(2\)-part of \(L\), and they agree modulo the odd part by the choice of \(M\).
This also covers an odd part equal to one.
Each graph column depends modulo \(p\) only on the residue of its exponent parameter modulo \(L\), so the certificate persists at every such \(k\).
This proves an effective progression for a specified width and sign, with no bound furnished for the last exceptional unrestricted exponent.

## Independent finding on consecutive dyadic completion

At 2026-11-19T03:27 UTC I checked the [integral completion and perturbation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v444.md#completion) for Sheng Yin.
I had no role in developing this construction and received the fixed material and the scope without its development dialogue or a prior review of it.
This finding covers the dyadic rank input, the unique local completion, and the perturbation that keeps the endpoints and enforces the stated nonvanishing properties.

Write \(n=2^k\).
The two distinct minima have degree \(d\), with \(\bar Q=\bar P\) by squaring the conjugates and reducing modulo two.
Squarefreeness and the unit constant coefficient let each common residue root lift uniquely to a root \(x\) of \(P\) and a distinct root \(y\) of \(Q\) in one unramified splitting field.
Put \(z=x^n,\ r=y/x,\ a=v_2(r-1)\ge1,\ \sigma=r^n,\ h=(\sigma-1)/n\).
Successive squaring gives \(v_2(h)\ge1\).
For a double contact of \(U(X^n)+XV(X^n)\) at \(x\), set \(v_0=V(z)/n,\ v_1=zV'(z)\).
The value and derivative equations give \(U(z)/n=-xv_0\) and \(zU'(z)=-xv_0-xv_1\), with all four entries integral.
At \(y\), expand the scaled values and Euler derivatives at \(z(1+nh)\) modulo \(n\).
Substituting the two identities at \(x\) gives the coefficient matrix on \((v_0,v_1)\)
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}y-x-xh&h(y-x)\\y-x\sigma&\sigma(y-x)+yh\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is \(\sigma(y-x)^2-xyh^2=\sigma xy(T_0-T_k/4^k)\), where \(T_j=r^{2^j}+r^{-2^j}-2\).
The recurrence \(T_{j+1}=4T_j+T_j^2\) gives \(v_2(T_j)\ge 2a+2j\).
In the telescoping difference \(T_k/4^k-T_0\) the term \(T_0^2/4\) has valuation \(4a-2\) and all later terms have strictly larger valuation.
For \(k\) beyond the maximum of \(4a-2\) over the residue disks, the adjugate annihilation modulo \(2^k\) therefore implies \(v_0,v_1\equiv0\bmod 2\).
It follows that \(U(z),U'(z)\) vanish modulo two.
As \(x\) runs over these lifts, Frobenius permutes the residue roots by \(x\mapsto x^n\bmod2\).
Taking \(U=A\), this proves \(\bar P^2\mid\bar A\) uniformly in the width.

The integral kernel is saturated because the remainder group is free abelian.
Its reduction inside coefficient space has dimension equal to its rank.
The signed graphs lie in a rational eigenspace of dimension \(2d\), by the exchange of the two-dimensional local factors at each inverse pair.
At \(b\ge2d-1\) this forces rank at least \(b+1-2d\).
The multiples of \(\bar P^2\) in the same width have exactly that dimension, proving equality and identifying the reduction.
A multiple vanishing off any deleted interval of \(2d\) consecutive positions is \(Y^iB\) with \(\deg B<2d\).
Since \(\bar P(0)=1\), divisibility forces \(B=0\).
Projection of an integral kernel basis onto the outside positions consequently has odd determinant, hence is invertible modulo every \(2^q\).
This produces exactly one reduction of an integral kernel vector over each outside residue vector, including the zero-dimensional case with no outside positions.

For an interior interval and outside integers with both endpoints one, take centered representatives of the completed coordinates.
They cost at most \(2d\,2^{q-1}=d\,2^q\) in one-norm.
There is an interior index \(i\) in the interval with \(2i\ne b\).
Varying its coefficient by \(2^qt\) preserves the residue vector and every outside integer.
A common zero of the row and its reversal is nonzero and is a root of \(Y^{b-i}A_0-Y^iA_{0,b}^*\).
Its two summands have different orders \(b-i\) and \(i\) at zero because the endpoints are one.
This nonzero polynomial has degree at most \(2b\), and each possible common root determines at most one \(t\) since the varied row slope there is nonzero.
For each of \(c=\delta,\delta^2\), the row and its reversal at \(c^n\) have nonzero slopes, and the graph value at \(c\) has slope \(2^q c^{ni}(1+\varepsilon c^{1+n(b-2i)})\).
The exponent is congruent to one modulo \(n\), and nontorsion of \(c\) ensures that this slope is nonzero as well.
These six value conditions exclude at most six further integers.
Thus an integer from \(0\) through \(2b+6\) satisfies all the conditions at total interval cost at most \((d+2b+6)2^q\).
The resulting row represents the required integral-kernel residue class.
The real coefficient construction and its error estimates lie outside this finding.
