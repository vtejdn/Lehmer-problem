# Review of the reduction to a quadratic norm-one family unramified at finite primes

## The family detects the unrestricted gap and preserves the stated circle condition

At 2026-11-24T21:08 an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed the four fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or prior findings on this proof.

For the chosen \(M\), two conjugates with equal \(sMj\)-th powers have torsion ratio and already have equal \(M\)-th powers.
The converse holds by divisibility, so \(\alpha^{sMj}\) and \(\alpha^M\) generate the same field by equality of degrees and inclusion.
The relative norm \(\delta\) is a unit and \(\delta^M=(\alpha^M)^s\).
Consequently every positive power of \(\delta\) generates \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^M)\), and \(h(\delta)=s h(\alpha)\).
Multiplication by \([K:\mathbb Q]=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]/s\) gives the equality of total logarithmic measures.
Inversion on \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) preserves the power field and sends the relative norm to its inverse.
The resulting \(\tau\) on \(K\) is an involution because it inverts the nontorsion generator \(\delta\), and the quadratic trace equation gives the stated fixed field.

For \(\beta=w\delta\), its twelfth power generates \(K\), so its own field contains first \(\delta\) and then \(w\).
The reverse inclusion is immediate and \(L=KE\).
The extension \(KE/K\) is Galois, and the fixed field in \(E\) of its restriction image is \(E\cap K=D\).
The Galois correspondence in \(E/\mathbb Q\) identifies that image with \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/D)\).
It follows that the torsion twist, which preserves absolute logarithmic height at every place, multiplies total measure by precisely \([E:D]\in\{1,2,4\}\).

Any automorphism inverting \(\beta\) must act on \(\delta^{12}\) by inversion, so restricts to \(\tau\) on \(K\).
It then sends \(w=\beta/\delta\) to \(w^{-1}\), requiring agreement of the restrictions on \(D\).
Conversely an extension of \(\tau\) to the algebraic closure preserves \(K\) and the normal field \(E\), hence gives an automorphism \(\sigma\) of their compositum.
In the compatible case \(c_E(\sigma|_E)^{-1}\) fixes \(D\), so it lifts to an automorphism fixing \(K\) by the restriction isomorphism above.
Composing that lift on the left with \(\sigma\) gives the required actions on \(K,E\), whose squares fix both generating fields.
The resulting involution inverts \(\beta\) and is nontrivial on \(E\).
Conversely, if \(\beta^{-1}\) is a conjugate, the corresponding embedding is an automorphism of \(L\) since both elements generate \(L\).
This verifies the claimed equivalence with reciprocity.
The possible restrictions to the three quadratic subfields are detected by their signs on \(i,\sqrt{-3},\sqrt3\).
Disagreement on an imaginary quadratic subfield means \(\tau\) fixes its generator, while disagreement on \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\) means \(\tau\) negates \(\sqrt3\).
These tests detect all disagreements even when \(D=E\), because the quadratic subfields generate \(E\).
Incompatibility forces \(D\ne\mathbb Q\), hence \([E:D]\le2\).
The twist is then a nonreciprocal nontorsion unit, so Smyth's bound yields \(c\le m(\beta)\le2m(\alpha)\).
Thus the transfer for each reciprocal input with \(m(\alpha)<c/2\) has exactly the stated strict threshold and multiplier.

In the compatible case the trace \(t=\beta+\beta^{-1}\) is fixed by the involution and \(\beta\) solves \(X^2-tX+1=0\).
The field \(\mathbb Q(t)\) therefore has index two and is the entire fixed field \(F\).
The restriction to \(E\) fixes \(w+w^{-1}=\sqrt3\) and negates \(w-w^{-1}=i\), giving \(L=F(i)=F(w)\) and relative norm one.
Conversely for any member satisfying (1), \(w\notin F\) and the nontrivial relative automorphism sends \(w\) to the other root \(w^{-1}\) of \(X^2-\sqrt3 X+1\).
For integral \(v\), the quotient \(y=(v-c_L(v))/i\) is integral because \(i\) is a unit, and is fixed by \(c_L\).
Then \(x=v-yw\) is integral and \(x-c_L(x)=v-c_L(v)-y(w-w^{-1})=0\).
Thus both coefficients belong to \(\mathcal O_F\), and linear independence of \(1,w\) establishes uniqueness.
This proves the full relative integral basis, whose discriminant equals \((w-w^{-1})^2=-1\).
For every finite prime the resulting quadratic residue algebra is separable, including residue characteristic two where the unit discriminant stays nonzero.
This allows splitting as well as inertness and proves the precise finite unramifiedness claimed.
Multiplying the generator by its relative conjugate gives (2), and \(\beta-\beta^{-1}=iy\) gives \(t^2+y^2=4\).

At an embedding with \(|\alpha|=1\), \(\bar\alpha=\alpha^{-1}\), so the unit is reciprocal.
The norm identity gives \(|\delta|=1\), and ordinary complex conjugation on this embedded \(K\) acts as its inverter \(\tau\).
It agrees with \(c_E\) on the intersection, and the twist still has modulus one at this place.
This establishes the circle-unit construction at all measures and, by inclusion and the multiplier bound, both inequalities in (5).
For an abstract change of embedding, extending it to the algebraic closure carries \(D\) to the intersection for the new copy of \(K\), since \(E\) is normal.
Its restriction to \(E\) commutes with \(c_E\) because \(E/\mathbb Q\) is abelian, so compatibility obtained at a circle embedding also holds for the positive real Salem embedding.

At a real embedding of \(F\), the two lifts to \(F(i)\) are conjugate and their values on \(\beta\) multiply to one.
At a nonreal embedding of \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), the image of \(t\) must be nonreal, and neither lift of \(\beta\) can have modulus one.
Hence each conjugate pair of nonreal \(F\)-embeddings contributes four off-circle images of \(\beta\).
Counting instead extensions of \(K\)-embeddings yields \([E:D]\,b(\delta)\), since every image of \(w\) has modulus one and \(\beta\) generates \(L\).
For a Salem number the uniqueness of its largest-modulus conjugate shows that its positive powers generate its field, by comparing embeddings fixing any of those powers.
One may take \(\delta=\alpha\), and there are exactly two off-circle conjugates.
In the positive real embedding the intersection with \(E\) lies in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\), so \([E:D]\) equals two or four, giving exactly one or two pairs of nonreal trace-field embeddings.
The same count applies when choosing the circle embedding for construction, by the correspondence of the intersections.
No embedding of \(L\) has real image since \(i\in L\), so the primitive generator \(\beta\) has no real conjugate.
For Salem numbers of degree at least four tending to one, their positive total logarithmic measures tend to zero, and this remains true after the bounded multiplier.

Finally each family member supplies a positive-measure integral minimum, giving \(\lambda\le\lambda_*\).
In the opposite direction the incompatible reciprocal inputs cost at least \(c/2\), and each compatible input costs at least \(\lambda_*/4\) by the constructed member.
Primitive nonunit extreme coefficients or scalar content cost at least \(\log2\) after dropping initial zero roots, while a nonreciprocal nontorsion unit costs at least \(c\).
These bounds and additivity under factorization give (3) for the full infimum over nonzero integer polynomials with positive measure.
In particular its vanishing is equivalent to vanishing of the family infimum.
The general comparison does not require a circle place or a totally real trace field, and the argument leaves the sign of the unrestricted gap open.
