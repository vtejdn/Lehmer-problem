# Review of the transfer to reciprocal units with few exterior conjugates

<a id="finding"></a>
## The relative norm, the two measure bounds and the complex Salem case

At 2026-11-24T22:34 UTC an agent outside the development of v59 independently reviewed its [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#statement) and all three proof sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed text and review scope without proof-development dialogue or earlier findings on v59.
The transfer bounds \(2k\,m(w)\) and \(k\,m(w)\), the complex Salem sequence equivalence and the degree-55 exclusion hold under the stated hypotheses.

**Detection and the witness field.**
For a witness \(a_0\) a positive multiple of \(f_{a_0}\) is an integer combination of the log functions of conjugates of \(w\), since the torsion multiplier has zero log function.
The squared norm of \(f_{a_0}\) is positive by Kronecker's theorem for units.
Pairing the identity with \(f_{a_0}\) therefore gives a nonzero term, and right translation on the common finite Galois group gives \(\langle f_w,f_a\rangle\ne0\) for a conjugate \(a\) of \(a_0\).
That conjugate is again a witness since \(D(w)\) is Galois-stable.
The inversion of \(a\) defines the nontrivial automorphism \(\tau\) of \(K=\mathbb Q(a)\) over \(B=\mathbb Q(a+a^{-1})\), using reciprocity and \(a\ne a^{-1}\).
For \(E=K(w)\) and \(e=[E:K]\), each embedding of \(K\) has \(e\) extensions to \(E\), all with the same number of lifts to the Galois field.
Their logs of \(w\) sum to the log of the absolute value of the corresponding image of \(v=N_{E/K}w\).
Thus the conditional mean is \(f_v/e\).
Precomposition with \(\tau\) permutes the embeddings of \(K\) and negates \(f_a\).
This gives \(\langle f_{v/\tau v},f_a\rangle=2e\langle f_w,f_a\rangle\ne0\).
Both \(v\) and \(\tau v\) are products of actual conjugates of \(w\), using an extension of \(\tau\) for the second product.
Their quotient \(u\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit in the ordinary group and lies in the chosen \(K\).

If \(a\) has \(l\) exterior conjugates among \(2n\) conjugates, there are \(n-l\ge1\) circle pairs.
At each corresponding real trace embedding the two extensions are exchanged by complex conjugation, which acts there by \(\tau\).
The equation \(\tau u=u^{-1}\) puts both images on the circle.
Each remaining pair has at most one exterior image of \(u\), including when its trace embedding is nonreal.
Restriction to \(\mathbb Q(u)\) can repeat values but cannot increase the number of distinct exterior images.
The inverse of \(u\) is a conjugate and the circle images persist, so \(u\in\mathcal B_k\).

**Cost with multiplicities.**
For \(A=\{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C:|\sigma u|>1\}\), the sum of the positive logs over the embeddings of \(K\) is \([K:\mathbb Q(u)]m(u)\).
To bound the numerator contribution from \(v\), expand its log as a sum over the embeddings of \(E\) lying over \(A\) and retain their positive parts.
For any fixed embedding of \(\mathbb Q(w)\) and any fixed \(\sigma\in A\), their prescribed values determine at most one embedding of their compositum \(E\).
Thus every conjugate of \(w\) is charged at most \(|A|\) times.
The denominator contribution from \(\tau v\) uses \(A\tau\) and \(w^{-1}\), with the same cardinality and \(m(w^{-1})=m(w)\) by the unit norm.
Adding gives \([K:\mathbb Q(u)]m(u)\le2|A|m(w)\le2l\,m(w)\).

For the improvement, \(h(v)\le e h(w)\) by the product bound for absolute height, and \(e\le[\mathbb Q(w):\mathbb Q]\) because a minimal polynomial over \(K\) divides the rational minimum.
Hence \(h(v)\le m(w)\).
Since \(n\ge l+1\) and \(l\le k\), the neutral fraction \((n-l)/n\) is at least \(\delta=1/(k+1)\).
Substituting this \(\delta\) into the small-height norm theorem gives exactly the inclusive threshold \((\delta/4)4^{-1/\delta}=\epsilon_k\).
Its hypotheses concern the reciprocal field of \(a\) and allow any nonzero \(v\in K\), even when \(B\) is not totally real.
It gives \(v\tau v=1\), so \(u=v^2\) and \(v\) itself is a nontorsion output with the same neutral-place guarantee.
For the exterior set of \(v\), the first extension count alone gives \([K:\mathbb Q(v)]m(v)\le l\,m(w)\).
This proves the refinement with \(v\) in the ordinary conjugate group.

**Complex Salem numbers and the degree cutoff.**
A complex Salem number as defined in v59 has a nonreal circle conjugate shared by its minimum and the reciprocal polynomial.
Irreducibility makes these polynomials proportional, and comparison at the two ends gives unit constant coefficient.
The antireciprocal sign would force a root at one.
Such a witness therefore has two exterior conjugates and exactly one nonreal pair of trace embeddings off the neutral locus.
The two nonneutral inverse pairs above this complex trace place are interchanged by complex conjugation.
For the small-height norm \(v\), nontorsion forces an exterior value there, and the only exterior embedding values are \(z,\bar z\).
If they are distinct, an exterior conjugate of \(v\) is already a complex Salem number.
If they agree, there is just one distinct exterior conjugate of \(v\), which becomes \(\beta>1\) after a possible sign change.
There is also its positive inverse and at least one circle conjugate, so \(\beta\) is a real Salem number with \(m(\beta)=m(v)\).
The polynomial \(X^2+\beta\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\subset\mathbb R\) in this embedding.
The two exterior roots of the lift are \(\pm i\sqrt\beta\), and the circle conjugates lift to circle roots.
Since adjoining this square root doubles the degree, its minimal polynomial has total measure \(m(\beta)\).
The sign and the square root are allowed in \(D(w)\), as required for this branch.
For \(m(w)\to0\) the small-height hypothesis with \(k=2\) holds eventually and yields complex Salem outputs with \(0<m\le2m(w)\).
In the converse direction a complex Salem number is itself a witness for its divisible conjugate group.

A group avoiding all complex Salem numbers also avoids every real Salem number, by the same quadratic lift and radical saturation.
The algebraic detection criterion therefore puts \(w\) in the Salem-orthogonal class.
For \(\deg w\le55\) and \(M(w)<601/500\), the classified possibilities in that class have irreducible minima \(P(\pm X^j)\) up to overall sign.
Therefore \(\pm w^j\) is a root of \(P\) and all of its conjugates belong to \(D(w)\).
The irreducible degree-eighteen \(P\) has fourteen circle roots and a nonreal conjugate pair of exterior roots, so that group contains a complex Salem number after all.
This proves the stated weak lower bound at the cutoff and uses the previously reviewed finite classification at its exact degree range.
Finally a lower bound \(b_k>0\) on \(\mathcal B_k\) yields \(\min(\epsilon_k,b_k/k)\) by splitting the two measure ranges.
Consequently for each fixed \(k\) with such a bound, every sequence of nontorsion units with measures tending to zero eventually avoids \(\mathcal B_k\) in its divisible conjugate groups.
The index beyond which avoidance holds may depend on \(k\).
