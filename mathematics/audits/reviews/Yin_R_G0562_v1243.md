## The orders and the complementary factor

The hypotheses give a grid by separating consecutive negative roots, and the pole value rules out any complementary negative-root factor by the bound and linear-factor test in v891.
The negative trace places make the quadratic lift irreducible over the trace field.
At the positive place \(1<T<p/q\), so the graph gives \(Y>T>1\).
The pole norm gives the integrality required in v883, where the two units generate the cyclic group with primitive exponents \(u,v\).

For the index, the norm of \(E\) proves that \(Y\) already lies in the localized short-power order away from \(L\).
At \(\ell\mid L\) the change from \(T\) to \(E\) preserves the power basis over \(\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}\) up to an invertible change of coordinates.
The coefficient of \(E^{2n-1}\) in \(E'=L/E\) is \(-1/L\), and the trace of \(E'\) supplies the integral coefficient in the quadratic reduction of \((E')^2\).
Thus one extra generator suffices locally and has exact annihilator \(L\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}\).
The class of \(Y\) generates at every such prime, which identifies the global quotient and excludes \(u=1\).

In the real order the norm identity for \(1-Mw\) places its norm divided by that element in \(\mathbb Z[w]\).
Its reduction modulo \(q\) is one, so the trace difference \(w_Y-w\) divided by \(q\) lies in the order.
This gives the trace of \(TY\).
The recurrence in the claim then gives all mixed traces, starting on the two rows from \(\tau(1),\tau(T),\tau(Y),\tau(TY)\).
In particular Bézout recovers \(\tau(\beta)\), and \(D_u\) gives the reverse inclusion.
For \(\ell\mid u\) the Frobenius image contains the algebra generator \(w\) and all scalars, making Frobenius an automorphism of the finite reduction.
The presentation by the minimal polynomial \(H\) now excludes repeated factors modulo \(\ell\).

On \(A/EA\) the two additive generators \(1,Y\) over \(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\) give a surjection between groups of the same order \(L^2\).
Taking the inverse of the Smith form of \(E\) and multiplying by \(L\) therefore gives a free quotient of rank \(2n-2\) for \(E'\), including when \(L\) has repeated prime factors.
Multiplication by \(E'\) injects \(A/EA\) into \(A/LA\) by cancellation in the field, and commutes with \(T\).
The resulting block triangular matrix gives the characteristic polynomial factorization even when the two pole ideals meet at a maximal ideal.
On the quotient \(Y=1\), the algebra is generated by \(T\), and \(T^v=1\).
The spanning power basis has size equal to the free rank at each prime power, so monic division gives the asserted divisibility of the characteristic polynomial over the full residue ring.

For even \(v\) the anti-reciprocal cofactor has both \(1\) and \(-1\) as roots, and these differ by a unit modulo \(L\).
After cancellation of \(X^2-1\), the reciprocal cofactor descends to the trace variable by pairing opposite Laurent powers, giving \(J\mid V_v\) and \(2n\le v\).
At \(W=-4\), the geometric sum gives \(V_v(-4)=(-1)^{(v-2)/2}v/2\).
The exact endpoint norm applies because coprimality forces \(u\) odd, and the root positions fix the sign of \(H(-4)\).
In the Taylor expansion about \(-4\), terms of order at least two retain a factor \(L^2\) after multiplication by \(M^n/L^2\).
The linear term gives \(M^{n-1}H'(-4)\equiv1-4^{-n}\pmod L\), so \(\gcd(L,4^n-1)\) divides \(J(-4)\) and hence \(v/2\).
This gives the even case at three, and the odd case follows from v895 together with the discriminant conclusion.
For \(L=81\), cubing \(4^3\equiv64\pmod{81}\) gives \(4^9\equiv28\pmod{81}\), then \(4^{27}\equiv1\pmod{81}\), as required in the example.
