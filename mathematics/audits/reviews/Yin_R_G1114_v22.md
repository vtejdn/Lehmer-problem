# Independent review of the two degree-index Salem resultants

The reviewer took no part in developing this material and received no development dialogue, author explanations or earlier finding about it.

The computations below check the new delays directly.

## Finding

For all sufficiently large multiples \(N\) of \(L\), the first family has full-turn and half-turn absolute resultants \(N-7\) and \(9\) at its own degree \(d=N+2\).
A shift to \(d-3\) recovers an exponential half-turn along an infinite subsequence.
The second family simultaneously excludes factors of small residue degree at a growing set of primes and has both degree-index resultants below \(2^D\).

**Irreducibility and degree.**
The prime-power factors of \(\varphi(m)\) bound both the prime divisors of \(m\) and their exponents when \(\varphi(m)\le12\), so \(L\) is finite and divisible by \(12\).
Expanding the numerator gives \(z^{N+3}-z^{N+1}-z^N+z^3+z^2-1\).
Division by \(z-1\) gives the stated coefficient list, which is symmetric of degree \(N+2\) with maximum absolute coefficient one.
For a primitive \(m\)-th root satisfying the numerator equation, the three exponents \(2,m/2+2,m/2+1\) are coprime to \(m\) in the respective cases stated in the ledger.
Writing the corresponding conjugate as \(\xi\zeta^h\), evenness of the delay implies \(\xi^N=1\).
The denominator \(f\) is nonzero on the circle, so the conjugate equations imply \(G(\xi\zeta^h)=G(\zeta)^h\).
After clearing denominators the degree is at most \(6h\).
The resulting polynomial is nonzero: when \(h=2\) the two constant terms are \(-1\) and \(1\), and for \(h=1,\xi=-1\) the linear coefficients are \(-1\) and \(1\).
Hence \(\varphi(m)\le12\), forcing \(\zeta^N=1\).
The quadratic factor of \(f-g\) would make the algebraic integer \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) equal to \(-3/2\).
The remaining root \(1\) is simple because the derivative is \(7-N\).

The other two roots of \(f\) are complex conjugates of modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}<1\).
On the circle, comparison with \(z^Nf\) for a multiplier \(0<t<1\) of \(g\) gives exactly \(N+2\) interior roots and one exterior root, counted with multiplicity.
At \(t=1\), two roots strictly outside would persist for nearby \(t<1\).
The signs at a fixed \(1<s<\theta\) and at \(\theta\) force an exterior root in \((s,\theta)\) for every sufficiently large \(N\), proving the asserted one-sided convergence.
Reciprocity puts its inverse inside and all remaining roots on the circle.
Every irreducible factor is monic with constant term of absolute value one, so the factor containing the exterior root also contains the unique interior root.
All remaining factors would be cyclotomic by [Kronecker's theorem](../literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization), already excluded by the torsion calculation.
The resulting Salem polynomial is separable, and a collision of positive powers of distinct conjugates would persist under transport of the first to its unique exterior root.
Their unequal moduli after transport rule out the collision, so the positive powers retain degree \(d\).

**The two exact resultants and the shifted return.**
Expanding \((z-1)\Phi_3^2\) and \(-(z+1)\Phi_{12}\) gives \(f-z^2g\) and \(f+z^2g\), respectively.
At the nontrivial \(d\)-th roots, the \(\Phi_3\) factor has absolute product one: its complete product is \(|1-\omega^d|\,|1-\bar\omega^d|=3\), and its value at 1 is 3.
The derivative at the omitted node supplies the entire factor \(N-7\).
At \(z^d=-1\), both \(z+1\) and \(z-1\) have absolute product two, since \(d\) is even.
The four primitive twelfth roots have \(d\)-th powers primitive of order six, each contributing \(\sqrt3\), giving the half-turn product \(9\).
Multiplying the exact products gives \(9(N-7)\) for \(z^{2d}-1\), and a monic integer divisor and its monic complementary factor each have nonzero integer resultant.

For \(n=N-1\), evaluation at \(z^n=-1\) changes the numerator to \(-(zf+g)\).
The denominator contributes two, and the monic quartic \(zf+g\) has trace equation \(t^2-t-4=0\).
The larger trace exceeds \(5/2=2+1/2\), so the exterior root \(\lambda\) exceeds two.
The smaller trace is strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), giving a conjugate pair \(\gamma,\bar\gamma\) distinct from \(-1\).
By compactness choose a convergent subsequence of \(\gamma^{Lj}\) with gaps between its indices tending to infinity.
Taking ratios of successive terms gives unbounded multiples \(N\) of \(L\) with \(\gamma^N\to1\).
Then both unit-circle factors in the interchanged product are bounded away from zero because \(\gamma^{N-1}\to\gamma^{-1}\ne-1\).
The exterior factor contributes \(n\log\lambda+O(1)\), and the inverse factor and constant denominator have bounded logarithms.
Applying \(|1+\alpha^n|\le2\max(1,|\alpha|)^n\) to each root of \(P_N\) gives the stated lower bound since \(d/n\to1\).

**The finite-field sieve.**
For the second delay \(D+2\), the identity \(z^2f-g=z^5-1\) gives the displayed integral quotient and its symmetric coefficients of height one.
The torsion-degree argument applies because \(D+2\) is even.
Every possible torsion root of the numerator has \(\zeta^D=1\) and thus \(\zeta^5=1\).
For each of the five possibilities the quotient value \(1+(D/5)(1-\zeta^3-\zeta^2)\) vanishes for at most one \(D\).
The same exterior-root count therefore gives irreducible Salem polynomials with measures tending to \(\theta\) from below.
Over \(\mathbb F_p\) a nonzero element of degree \(j\le K\) has order dividing \(p^j-1\), hence dividing \(D\).
The multiplicity of its zero in \(z^D-1\) is \(p^{v_p(D)}\ge p^3>5\), whereas in \(H=z^5-1\) its multiplicity is at most five, including in characteristic five.
Thus cancellation in \(z^2 f(z)(z^D-1)/H\) still leaves a zero there, so \(S_D\) equals one at that element and also at zero.
This proves the claimed exclusion of small residue degrees.

On \(z^D=1\) the only exceptional evaluations are the five affine functions of \(D\) above, giving the \(O(D^5)\) bound.
On \(z^D=-1\) the five roots of \(H\) each contribute two to the denominator product.
The numerator factors up to sign as \((z+1)(z^4-z^3-z^2-z+1)\).
Its quartic trace equation \(t^2-t-3=0\) gives one positive trace in \((2,5/2)\) and one trace in \((-2,2)\), so the sole exterior root satisfies \(1<\lambda_*<2\).
All remaining factors in the product are bounded, giving \(O(\lambda_*^D)\).
These two upper bounds are below \(2^D\) for sufficiently large allowed \(D\) at each \(T\).
The sieve proves the degree exclusion stated for this family.
The exact mixed valuations proved for the earlier family, and tests allowing the index to vary over a degree-sized interval, remain outside this conclusion.
