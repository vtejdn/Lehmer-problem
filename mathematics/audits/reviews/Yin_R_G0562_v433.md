# Review of Schinzel's conjecture and sparse-factor measures

## Cyclotomic shortness, a single conjectural obstruction and bounded coefficient length

On 2026-11-18T16:18 a separately assigned agent for Yin reviewed all three sections of [L462 v193](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v193.md#shortness) and the dependencies used in their proofs.
The reviewer took no part in developing the argument and received its fixed text and scope without development discussion or previous findings about it.
The conditional bounds, the unconditional implication from a shrinking sequence, and the alternative for unbounded coefficients all hold with the indicated quantifiers.

I checked Conjecture 2.1 across printed pp. 364–365 of the [published Amoroso–Sombra article](../literature/AS18/readme.md#journal).
For each rational irreducible noncyclotomic Laurent input there are two fixed finite sets, one of nonzero normal vectors and one of nonsingular integral matrices.
Outside the listed hyperplanes, an integral representation \(\mathbf a=M\mathbf b\) must exist for which the specialized factors of the pullback, with their cyclotomic parts removed and their multiplicities retained, give the irreducible factorization of the cyclotomic-free specialization.
Thus a factor \(G\) capturing a specified \(P\) in that factorization has primitive specialization equal to \(P\) times a product of cyclotomics, up to sign and a monomial.
Collecting terms cannot increase the coefficient length of an integral representative, and division by its positive content cannot increase it either.
The conjecture is used as a hypothesis at each required input.
The function-field result of the same paper has a different coefficient field and removes constant parts.

On a listed hyperplane an integral basis of the saturated kernel gives all integral directions there, in rank one less.
If the restriction is zero no specialization under consideration lies on that branch.
Each nonzero restriction has finitely many rational factors, and the off-hyperplane bound involves only the finitely many pullback factors at that node.
This proves finiteness of the length bound by descending rank, including the unit and rank-zero cases.
For an exceptional line in rank two the two primitive directions give fixed parents, whose factors under dilation have a gap by [C345 v210](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement).
The resulting single-input assertion in rank two concerns the gap.

For the converse obstruction, coefficient length of the fixed parent bounds the length of every nonzero primitive-direction specialization.
The radical inequality applies to \(\beta=\alpha^k\) in that specialization and gives \(0<m(\beta)\le R_Lm(\alpha)\) independently of \(k\).
A rational subspace of minimal dimension carrying infinitely many retained indices yields escape from every proper rational hyperplane after taking a basis of its integral lattice.
Saturation makes the subtorus connected and preserves primitivity of the new direction vectors.
Dimension one cannot support a shrinking sequence because it has just two primitive vectors and each nonzero parent has finitely many factors.
Passing to a single irreducible rational factor preserves escape.

Smyth's gap and the endpoint bounds give reciprocal \(P_j\) eventually, so the chosen Laurent factor \(H\) and its inverse-variable polynomial vanish on the same points.
If they were nonassociate, a common geometric divisor would descend, by taking all its distinct Galois conjugates, to a common rational divisor.
Their intersection therefore has codimension at least two in that case.
The [bounded-relation input](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#source) applies on each of the finitely many geometric components with all torsion twists equal to one.
The nontorsion base turns the bounded character identity into an exact linear relation on the direction.
The finite set of possible relations contradicts escape, proving reciprocity of \(H\).

Saturating the support-difference lattice permits an integral coordinate change after which \(H\) is a monomial times \(H_0(\mathbf U)\) of full support rank.
Irreducibility and reciprocity survive the removal of the unused variables.
To keep a nonzero restriction of the original parent, it suffices to choose the graph \(\mathbf Z=\mathbf U^A\) so that its current monomials stay distinct.
For each support difference \((d,e)\) with \(e\ne0\), the excluded equation \(d+A^{\mathsf T}e=0\) cuts out a proper affine subspace, and differences with \(e=0\) persist automatically.
An integer matrix outside this finite union gives a connected graph and a nonzero restricted parent divisible by \(H_0\).
The projected directions escape all fixed proper hyperplanes, so eventually even their univariate specializations of that parent are nonzero.
Dividing each projected vector by its gcd preserves escape, and the radical inequality applied to the fixed \(H_0\) again preserves shrinking positive measures.
The centrally symmetric full-dimensional Newton polytope has at least two vertices for each dimension, since opposite vertex pairs span its direction space.
The normal fan of a Minkowski sum refines that of either summand, so the restricted parent has at least this many vertices and at most the original number of terms.
These steps establish \(2r\le\#\operatorname{supp}F\) for the stated subtorus.

For a fixed finite matrix list, the number \(s\) of terms needed for each pullback factor is bounded independently of \(j\).
Every compatible pullback specializes to a nonzero parent and some \(G\) captures \(P_j\).
At a nonzero root, the first \(h\) Euler evaluations of an \(h\)-term polynomial give an invertible Vandermonde matrix on its distinct integer exponents, so its root multiplicity is at most \(h-1\).
I checked Dobrowolski's Theorem 1 on [printed p. 202](../literature/Dob06/readme.md#edition).
It bounds the positive logarithmic measure of the whole integral specialization \(g\) below by a constant \(\delta_s>0\).
Consequently removing every copy \(P_j^e\) leaves log measure at least \(\delta_s-(s-1)m(P_j)>0\) uniformly for this matrix list.
The quotient is primitive integral by Gauss's lemma, so its positive measure requires a distinct nontorsion factor.
This contradicts the exact conjectural factorization for any compatible matrix capturing \(P_j\), and failure to cover the direction by any matrix also contradicts the conjecture once all listed hyperplanes are avoided.
The plane conclusion uses the stated fixed-component degree comparison with primitive directions.

Bounded coefficient length gives finitely many ordered coefficient tuples for the affine-linear templates.
This yields a uniform conditional shortness bound and, conversely, a single template on a shrinking subsequence of bounded length.
The four- and five-term assertion uses the [finite family theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#statement) with coefficient height bounded by that length.
Under bounded term count alone, failure of any bounded-height subsequence means \(H(f_j)\to\infty\).
Then the [sparse coefficient bound](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) forces \(m(f_j/P_j)\to\infty\) by additivity, since \(m(P_j)\to0\).
It also gives the stated length bound under a full-parent measure bound.
Schinzel's rational conjecture and the existence of a shrinking sequence in these families remain open.
