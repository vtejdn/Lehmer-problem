## The degree asymptotic

At 2026-11-17T07:25 UTC an agent outside proof development independently reviewed the complete [v176 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v176.md#decision-salem-trinomial-resultant-balance-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its scope without development dialogue, author explanations, exploration notes or a previous verdict on v176.
The argument proves the stated absolute-error asymptotic for both signs and for every allowed pair of exponents, including \(b=0\).

For \(b>0\), a common root of \(Q,Q^*\) satisfies \(\beta^a=1+\beta^b\) and \(\beta^{-a}=1+\beta^{-b}\), so \(\beta^{2b}+\beta^b+1=0\).
For a multiple root of \(Q\), the derivative gives \(|\beta|^{a-b}=b/a<1\), whereas substitution in \(Q\) gives \(|\beta|^b=a/(a-b)>1\).
For \(b=0\), the roots of \(Q\) and \(Q^*\) have respective radii \(2^{1/a}\) and \(2^{-1/a}\).
Thus \(C\) is a product of distinct cyclotomic polynomials, with the empty product allowed.
Removing \(C\) preserves the Salem divisor and leaves a coprime pair \(q,q^*\) with \(m(q)=m(Q)\).
The reduced multiple \(F=X^Nq\pm q^*\) satisfies \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,F)|=|\operatorname{Res}(q,q^*)|\) by evaluation at the roots of monic \(q\).
Since \(F/S\) is integral, multiplicativity gives the integer upper and lower bounds for \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,S)|\).

Writing \(r=\deg q\), the pair product for \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,q^*)|\) has \(r^2\) root differences, each at most six, and leading factor \(|q(0)|^r\le2^a\).
Its nonzero integer value bounds each difference below by \(2^{-a}6^{-a^2}\).
In particular \(|\beta-1/\bar\beta|=(1+1/|\beta|)\big||\beta|-1\big|\) and \(|\beta|\ge1/3\) give the claimed radial separation.
Every factor \(|e^{i\theta}-\beta|\) is then between \(\tfrac14\,2^{-a}6^{-a^2}\) and \(4\).
Their logarithms sum to a function \(u\) of supremum norm \(O(a^3)\), and the extrema of \(|q(e^{i\theta})|^2\) divide a period into at most \(2a\) monotone pieces unless it is constant.
The resulting variation is \(O(a^4)\).

I checked equation (4) of v108 at the Salem signature, where \(m(S)=t\le1\).
Multiplication by \(2\pi/h\) normalizes the triangular kernel to mass one and gives uniform pointwise error \(O(d^{-3/8}\sqrt{\log(2d)}+d^{-3/4}\log(2d))\) for its convolution with the empirical measure at \(h=d^{-1/8}\).
Integrating over arcs expanded and contracted by \(h\) bounds the original empirical arc mass on both sides, with an additional \(O(h)\) from their changed lengths.
This gives the stated discrepancy uniformly even for arcs shorter than \(2h\) or with complements shorter than \(2h\).
Integration by parts against \(u\) costs \(O(a^4d^{7/8})\) in the unnormalized sum.
Both real conjugates have phase zero, so their deletion subtracts \(2u(0)\), of size \(O(a^3)\).
The angle integral equals \(m(q)\) by Jensen's formula.

At \(x=e^{-t}\), one has \(1\le1+x^b-x^a\le2\), \(|C(x)|\le2^a\), and \(|q(x)|\le4^a\).
The reduced equation at \(e^t\) therefore makes the contribution of the real pair to \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,q)|\) equal to \(-(N-r)t+2\log|q(x)|=-(N-r)t+O(a)\).
The whole log resultant lies between zero and \(O(a^2)\).
Combining these bounds with the neutral sum produces the claimed main term with \(r\) in place of \(a\).

The degree and width bounds apply to this \(S,Q\) by the reviewed length-six reduction.
I checked the primary statement of [Dobrowolski's Theorem 1, p. 391](../../literature/pdf/Dob79--published.pdf), using \(\epsilon=1/2\), \(M(e^t)=e^t\), and the fact that \(e^t>1\) is a nonzero algebraic integer of degree \(d\) and infinite multiplicative order.
The original proof is outside this review.
The degree tends to infinity as \(t\) tends to zero, so \(e^t-1\le 2t\) and the theorem give \(1/t\ll(\log(2d))^3\).
Consequently \(a\ll(\log(2d))^4\), replacing \(r\) by \(a\) costs at most \(at\), and the largest normalized error \(O((\log(2d))^{16}d^{-1/8})\) is \(O(d^{-1/16})\).
Finally \(Q(1)<0\) and \(Q\) is monic, so it has a positive real root greater than one whose irreducible factor is covered by the length-five bound.
The coefficient length is three, giving \(m(Q)\le\log 3\), and the binomial at \(b=0\) has measure \(\log 2\) by its root moduli.
The result gives a necessary condition and leaves existence of a sequence tending to one open.
