**Matrix estimates.**
For each \(u>t\), the \((k,l)\) entry of \(ab^*+ba^*\) is \(2\cosh((k-l)(u-t))e^{i(k-l)\theta}\).
Consequently \(E+2H-K\) has precisely the reflected moments, including diagonal \(d\).
On the orthogonal complement of the \(r\) vectors \(a+b\), the quadratic form of \(K\) is nonpositive, so its positive projection \(\Pi\) has rank at most \(r\).
Since \(M\ge0\), compression gives \({\rm tr}(\Pi K)\le {\rm tr}(\Pi E)+2{\rm tr}(\Pi H)\), where \({\rm tr}(\Pi H)\le rq\).
Subtracting the full trace \(2rq\) bounds \({\rm tr}(K_-)\) by \({\rm tr}(\Pi E)\), to which either norm bound applies even for indefinite \(E\).
The positive upper bound \(E_++2H+K_-\) on \(M\) permits eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue comparison.
After division by \(d\sqrt q\), the trace term \(rq\) costs at most \(r/\sqrt d\), and the compressed \(E\) costs \(O_C(\sqrt r)\) in the square-summed case or \(O_{\delta,C'}(r/\sqrt d)\) in the absolute-summed case.
Squaring, using \(r\le d\), gives both estimates in (7), including \(r=0\).
For \(t>0\), the row-sum bound uses the geometric series \(\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-2tj}=(e^{2t}-1)^{-1}\).
Taking the smaller compression estimate before squaring gives (8).

**Circle kernels.**
Expanding the Fejér kernel gives exactly \(W\) with the factor \(1/q\).
Pairs in each common interval contribute at least \(cq\,m_i^2\).
For cyclic interval separation \(h\ge2\) the angular distance is at least a constant times \((h-1)/q\), whereas the same and adjacent intervals use the peak bound \(O(d)\).
Summing the bounds \(O_\delta(d/(1+h^2))\) against the shifted mass products costs \(O_\delta(d\sum_i m_i^2)=O_\delta(W)\).
This reasoning permits atomic measures.
In the Poisson convolution each pair has total smoothing width at least \(2/d\) before and after changing \(t\) to zero, with difference at most \(2t\).
The kernel ratio at the same angle is fractional linear in the cosine, and its endpoint maximum is the ratio of the larger to the smaller hyperbolic tangent of the half-width.
By monotonicity of \(\tanh(v)/v\), this is at most the ratio of the widths, which is bounded by \(1+dt\).
The positive pair sum thus incurs one such factor, and \(e^{-2j/d}\ge e^{-2}\) for \(j\le d\) gives (10).
