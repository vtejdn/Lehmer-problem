# Review of the large-prime sieve for primitive cyclic resultants

<a id="large-prime-return-review"></a>
## The sieve exponents, degree count and single-order equivalences

At 2026-11-16T16:12 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin inspected the [large-prime return theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v1.md#statement), its three proof sections and all five listed dependency interfaces.
The reviewer had used the elementary reciprocal-prime sum in a separate smooth-order criterion and had checked the prime substitutions for an earlier resultant criterion.
The reviewer did not develop the sieve for prime divisors of the resultant or the large-prime-part converse under review, and received their fixed text without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict.
The theorem passes for the full primitive-polynomial class and the single-order assertions pass for both choices of resultant.

The strict restriction \(3\delta<e^{-1/(2A)}\) and the inequality \(1/2<e^{-1/(2A)}\) allow \(\max(1/2,3\delta)<\beta<e^{-1/(2A)}\), then \(e^{1/(2A)}\beta<\alpha<1\) and \(\delta<\gamma<(\beta-\delta)/2\).
In particular at \(A=1,\delta=1/5\) one may take \(\beta=601/1000,\ \alpha=999/1000,\ \gamma=801/4000\).
These give \(2\gamma+\delta=1201/2000<\beta\) and \((\alpha/\beta)^2>11/4>e\).
As \(A\) increases the allowed exponent approaches \(1/3\) from below.

For each candidate prime \(r\) the number of multiples in the window is \(Ad/r+O(1)\).
Uniqueness follows from \(d^{2\beta}>2Ad\), and \(r\le d^\alpha<Ad\) makes every one of the multiples composite.
The [Chebyshev estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units) bounds the first-power floor errors in \(\log(\lfloor x\rfloor!)\) by \(O(x)\).
Higher prime powers contribute \(O(x)\) by convergence of \(\sum_p\log p/(p(p-1))\).
Partial summation of \(\sum_{p\le x}\log(p)/p=\log x+O(1)\) therefore gives \(\sum_{d^\beta<r\le d^\alpha}1/r=\log(\alpha/\beta)+O(1/\log d)\).
Changing endpoint inclusion does not affect the main term.
For a small prime \(\ell\), any candidate \(r\) with \(\operatorname{ord}_r(\ell)\le d^\gamma\) divides one of the positive integers \(\ell^k-1\) in the stated product.
The log of that product is at most \(\sum_{\ell\le d^\delta}\log\ell\sum_{k\le d^\gamma} k=O(d^{2\gamma+\delta})\).
Each distinct bad \(r\) costs at least \(\beta\log d\) in this bound and divides \(O_A(d^{1-\beta})\) window orders.
The strict exponent inequality therefore bounds their total loss by \(o(d)\).

For a remaining order, \(\ell\le d^\delta\) differs from the distinguished \(r\), so \(r\) divides the \(\ell\)-free part \(u\) of \(j\).
The [cyclotomic reduction identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#cyclotomic-clearing) gives multiplicative order \(u\) at every root of \(\Phi_j\bmod\ell\), including \(k=0\) in \(j=u\ell^k\).
Because \(\Phi_j\) is monic, the resultant mod \(\ell\) tests the product of the evaluations of \(P\bmod\ell\) at these finite roots even when the degree of \(P\) drops.
Primitivity excludes zero reduction.
Each irreducible factor contributing such a root has degree \(f\) with \(r\mid\ell^f-1\), hence \(f>d^\gamma\).
There are at most \(d^{1-\gamma}\) distinct irreducible factors of this size, irrespective of multiplicity.
Each has just one root order \(u\) by Frobenius conjugacy.
The possible \(u\ell^k\) meet a closed dyadic interval at most twice, with two endpoint hits possible at \(\ell=2\).
Thus even summing over all primes \(\ell\le d^\delta\) loses \(O(d^{1+\delta-\gamma})=o(d)\) orders, with no restriction needed on valuations or repeated factors.

The [two-sided primitive approximation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#linear) at \(Y=Ad\) removes at most \(d/2+o(d)\) orders independently of the coefficients, factorization and multiplicities.
Its hypotheses include \(P\) nonmonic with possible zero roots and exclude every root of unity, as required here.
The sieve leaves more than \(d/2\) orders by a fixed positive multiple of \(d\).
The [totient mean](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#exceptional-orders) consumes less than this margin on choosing \(\eta>0\) small enough, so \(\log D_j\gg_{A,\delta}d\,m(P)\) on the claimed positive proportion with no small-prime divisor.

For unit minimal polynomials the forward implication at \(A=1,\delta=1/5\) uses \(D_j\mid C_j\), hence also divisibility between the corresponding large-prime parts.
Conversely use the [irreducible substitutions](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) at \(d=Dp\) for each fixed monic minimal polynomial \(Q\) of a nontorsion unit.
For a multiple \(kp\) in the window the primitive orbit map has constant multiplicity \(\varphi(kp)/\varphi(k)\), and the full orbit map has multiplicity \(p\).
The large-prime part is one in either case eventually because \(D\le k\le2D\) and every \(V_k(Q)\) is a fixed nonzero integer.
For any remaining single witness, the whole resultant equals \(V_j(Q)\) by orbit permutation.
Its log dominates the log of its large-prime part and both possible normalizing denominators are at most \(2d\).
The ordinary fixed-\(Q\) limits at \(j\to\infty\) give \(m(Q)\ge c/2\).
This works even if the order depends on the input, and the forward implication already provides composite witnesses.
The result leaves the uniform positive lower bound open.
