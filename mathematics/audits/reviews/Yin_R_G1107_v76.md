## Rational observables on primitive orbits

I checked the dynamics argument and its source statements directly, and another reviewer separately checked the normal-family inference and its projective scope.

Coprime homogeneous coordinate forms have no common projective zero, and this property is preserved by composition.
The determinant thus detects equality of the two projective values, including at poles and for constant \(f\).
It is a nonzero polynomial since varying \(Y\) varies \(\psi(Y)\) at fixed \(X\).
For the reciprocal-sum observable, positive arguments give values at least two, with infinity at the endpoints of the positive projective arc, whereas every finite negative argument gives a finite value at most minus two.

Writing indices as primitive exponents of their exact order, the negative block is precisely the open arc at distance less than \(w=(\kappa+1/2)/p\) from \(1/2\).
Orders below \(p^{1/4}\) contain \(O(p^{1/2})\) nodes altogether and include the possible profile poles eventually.
For each retained odd order \(d\), \((d-1)/2\) is primitive and at distance \(1/(2d)<w\) for large \(p\).
Möbius inversion for the number of primitive exponents in the arc has error \(O(2^{\omega(d)})\).
After division by \(\varphi(d)\), the error is \(O(4^{\omega(d)}/d)=O(d^{-1/2})\), using the elementary bound on the contribution from large primes.
Folding exponents modulo sign preserves the proportion since the arc is symmetric and every primitive exponent differs from its negative modulo the odd order \(d>1\).
The negative proportion is therefore positive and tends uniformly to zero over the retained orders.

Both index multiplications are permutations on every retained orbit and commute with its Galois action.
A single determinant zero consequently forces the entire orbit into the graph, and \(ba^{-1}\bmod d\) realizes \(f\) on its observable values.
These projective values are periodic and occur with equal multiplicity on their complete Galois orbit.
If its size is \(m\), the presence of a negative residue supplies at least \(1/m\) of the mass outside the compact set \(K\).
Sign separation makes that mass precisely the negative proportion, so persistent violations give distinct periodic points outside \(K\) of unbounded degree.

The Baker–Rumely input applies to these full rational Galois orbits at the archimedean place, since the periodic points have canonical height zero.
I checked the height identity, the atomless balanced complex measure and the complete-orbit hypothesis against printed pp. 628–631 at statement and applicability depth. [Baker and Rumely, Theorems 2.1(B) and 2.3](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.2196.pdf).
The limiting measure gives the open complement of \(K\) mass zero.
For an open set meeting \(f^{-1}(S)\), its image is open and meets the support \(S\), so the pullback gives that set positive mass by the local multiplicities.
An open set disjoint from \(f^{-1}(S)\) has zero pullback mass.
Balance gives \(f^{-1}(S)=S\), and atomlessness supplies three distinct points of \(S\) omitted by every iterate on its complement.
Montel's theorem applies there, and the whole image of the negative real interval belongs to one component by projective continuity, including at a real pole.

In a real chart about a periodic point fixed by a return, spherical normal convergence along a subsequence yields bounded Taylor coefficients on a smaller disk since the return powers all fix the center.
This applies also in the reciprocal chart at infinity.
A real multiplier of modulus greater than one would give unbounded first derivatives.
For multiplier \(+1\), or \(-1\) after squaring the return, the first nonlinear Taylor coefficient grows linearly with the number of iterates and is nonzero because a positive iterate has degree greater than one.
Every such point in the component is therefore attracting.
Two distinct real periodic points there admit a common return, whose powers converge to one of them on a neighborhood of that point.
A normal subsequential limit must agree with this constant throughout the connected component, while taking the second fixed value at the second point.
This excludes the unbounded sequence from the negative arc image.

A constant map has one periodic value.
An infinite-order projective transformation has only periods one and two, since a cycle of length at least three would make an iterate fix three points.
All such points are fixed by its nonidentity square, hence there are at most two.
In the finite-order case, iterating the permutation identity \(N\) times yields \(W_{a^N\ell}=W_{b^N\ell}\).
The common factor of the multipliers is a unit modulo \(d\) and can be removed by reindexing.
For opposite parities of the resulting \(A,B\), the primitive exponent \((d-1)/2\) puts the even multiple near zero and the odd multiple within \(O(1/d)<w\) of \(1/2\).
For odd \(A>B\), the interval for \(r\) has length proportional to \(dw\).
The ratio of the primitive-count main term modulo \(2d\) to the rounding error bound is bounded below by a constant times \(dw/4^{\omega(2d)}\), tending uniformly to infinity since \(d\ge p^{1/4}\) and \(w=p^{-o(1)}\).
Such an odd \(r\) gives a primitive \((d-r)/2\), and its multiples have distances \(Ar/(2d)>w>Br/(2d)\) with both distances tending to zero.
Thus the two sampled residues have opposite signs in both parity cases, contrary to equality of their sign-separating observables.

The nonzero determinant values multiplied by the appropriate powers of \(s_{[aj]}\) and \(s_{[bj]}\) are algebraic integers, and their product over complete retained orders is rational.
Each index permutation gives the same mean logarithmic clearing cost, tending to zero after deletion of \(o(n)\) nodes by the denominator estimates of v1.
The positive part of the logarithm of a fixed polynomial is uniformly integrable under these pairs by its polynomial growth in each permuted marginal.
Weak convergence with lower truncation therefore transfers the logarithmic lower bound to the joint law and yields zero mass on each tested graph.
The marginals remain nonatomic, so poles or algebraic values of a coordinate have probability zero.
Countability of rational functions gives the field exclusions in both orientations, which apply to distinct fixed dyadic times since the conductors are odd.

For \(L=q^r\), the trace congruence follows by taking the \(q^r\)-th power of the sum of a root of unity and its inverse in characteristic \(q\).
Every integral polynomial in that trace has the same compatibility.
With \(d_F=\deg F\), the cleared numerators are \(Z_{[Lj]}s_j^{d_F}\) and \(s_{[Lj]}\sum_i f_iZ_j^is_j^{d_F-i}\), where \(f_i\) are the coefficients of \(F\).
The reduction of the latter sum is \(Z_j^Ls_j^{d_F-L}\), so the two numerators agree modulo \(q\) even at primes dividing the denominators.
Taking a \(q\)-th power improves divisibility of their difference from \(q^e\) to \(q^{e+1}\) for \(e\ge1\) by the binomial coefficients and \(qe\ge e+1\).
The two possible real signs of a powered equality are excluded by the already proved graph result applied to \(F\) and \(-F\).
Dividing each nonzero numerator by \(q^{k+1}\) leaves algebraic integers whose product is a nonzero rational integer.
The mean logarithmic cost of \(D_j^{q^k}\) tends to zero for fixed \(k\).
This proves the stated \((k+1)\log q\) bound.
I accept the rational graph exclusions and prime-power inequalities under the fixed multiplier, sign separation, and conductor assumptions.
