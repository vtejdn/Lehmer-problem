The argument establishes (1)–(4), the fixed-degree corollary and the conditional gap criterion at the stated scopes.

For the Schur identity, \(zA^*=f-qf^*\), the product of the \(d-1\) roots of \(A\) is \(N/L\), and multiplication at those roots therefore contributes \((-N)^{d-1}L/N\).
The leading-coefficient factor \(L^{d-1}\) then gives precisely \(-N^{d-2}\operatorname{Res}(A,f)\).
Even degree permits exchanging \(f,A\), and monicity permits replacing \(A\) by \(f^*\) on the roots of \(f\).
The strictly stable resultant is positive, making the integral resultant of \(f,f^*\) negative and nonzero.
Modulo \(N\), the formal Sylvester columns of \((zC,-qC)\) belong to \(C\mathbb Q[z]_{\le d-1}\) in their specified integral lift.
At least \(2d-2-d=d-2\) columns from the difference are required in a nonzero determinant term, also for composite \(N\).
The stable upper bound divided by this power gives (1).
At \(q=d^2\), if \(\theta-q\to0\) along a shrinking Salem sequence, the branch formula forces \(j/q\to0\), whereas (5) and (1) would force \(c\le o(1)\).
Here the degree tends to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness, and both inequalities must concern the very same inverses.

In the trace coordinates, evaluation at \(T,x_i\) gives one factor \(Z\), one factor \(\operatorname{disc}H\) and \(\prod_i b_i=\mathfrak a\prod_i c_i\) in the absolute trace resultant.
Since \(D_Q=Z^2\operatorname{disc}H\), its denominator in (7) is \(Z\) to the first power.

Multiplication over the roots of \(f\) contributes \(f(-1)\) from \(z+1\), giving the claimed signed square.

For the bounded-residue inverse, evaluating \(H(T)/(T-x_i)\) as a product of \(h-1\) factors gives (8) uniformly even when trace roots approach each other.
The monic Chebyshev basis change has determinant one and its evaluation rows have Euclidean norms at most \(2\sqrt h\), proving (9), including \(h=1\).
The squared trace resultant contains \((\operatorname{disc}H)^2\) and \((\prod_i b_i)^2\).
Together with both endpoint bounds these give \((288B_h)^{2h}\) in (10), and \(q\ge B_h,\ q\ge2\) bound the remaining factor by \(50q^3\).
As \(B_h=O(d\sqrt{\log d})\), \(q=d^2\) eventually suffices and the prefactor after taking the root tends to one.
For fixed \(P\), bounded positive residues give \(O_P(q^3)\) with \(d\ge6\) sufficient for the asserted ratio to vanish.
Indeed at fixed \(P\) the full lattice has finite covering radius, so a fixed positive box can also be used if only a \(P\)-dependent constant is needed.

In the plastic family the endpoints give \((T+2)\mathfrak a=1\) and \(|P_n(1)|=d/5-1\).
At \(\sigma_n\), differentiation of the numerator gives the leading term \(\sigma_n^{d+2}g'(\sigma_n)\) with an additive error \(O(d)\), since \(\sigma_n^{d+2}g(\sigma_n)=g^*(\sigma_n)\).
The denominator is bounded away from zero, \(g'(\rho)>0\), and \(d|\log \sigma_n-\log\rho|\to0\), which verifies \(\log Z=m\log\rho+O(1)\).
The radius bound on the full lattice applies to every center of its fixed-\(q\) translate.
The error at \(t_d\mathbf1\) is \(o(t_d)\) uniformly in the coordinates and the upper bound \(t_d\le d/4\) leaves a strict margin in the simplex.
This gives integral inverses of the particular \(P_n\) at each sufficiently large \(d=60n\).
