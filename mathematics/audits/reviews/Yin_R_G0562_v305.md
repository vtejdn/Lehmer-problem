The proof establishes \(\min b(y)=e-D\) over all nontorsion radical outputs under full flips, and under even flips for \(m\ge3\) and \(1\le e<m\).
The stated reciprocal products attain the same minimum with the claimed degree and measure.
The following checks cover the two exceptional small-support counts, the full-support parity boundary and the concrete example.

### Distinct conjugates and cancellation

Dividing a torsion relation by its image under a single flip isolates a torsion power of the corresponding root.
With even flips the three double flips on a triple isolate a fourth power instead, which suffices for every coordinate when \(m\ge3\).
Consequently each signed exponent vector determines a distinct monomial.
Clearing denominators in a putative rational logarithmic relation and taking the product of the monomial with its complex conjugate gives coefficient \(2a_i\) at a real exterior label and \(a_i+a_j\) at each member of a conjugate exterior pair.
Independence forces each such coefficient to vanish, and the converse follows by multiplying back.
A weighted cancellation therefore pairs off all active labels in conjugate pairs of equal coefficient magnitude.
Raising a radical to a common power kills its torsion factor, and any embedding of the resulting monomial field extends to the radical field.
Different exterior images of the power have disjoint preimages, all exterior, so this reduction proves the required lower bound even for nonreciprocal outputs.

### Support, incidence and attainment

On a support of size \(s\) meeting the exterior labels, toggling one active sign leaves at most half the \(2^s\) sums zero, and reversal splits the rest equally.
For \(s\ge4\), \(2^{s-2}\ge s\).
For \(s=3\), two positives suffice when at most two labels are exterior, while three exterior labels have odd cardinality and admit no zero sum, giving four positives.
When the active labels constitute \(k\) cancellable pairs, precisely \(k\) separate sign constraints give \(2^{s-k}\) zero sums, verifying (11).
Transitivity gives a common positive incidence number, so summing the active-label count over distinct supports gives at least \(e\).
A singleton has exactly one positive choice on each exterior label, including the full-flip case \(m=1\).

On an edge there are zero, two, two or one positive choices in the four cases of the table.
The weaker bound of half the exterior incidence gives at least \(e\) on a regular edge orbit of valency at least two.
At valency one the orbit is an invariant perfect matching, and a single-exterior edge costs one extra while an edge joining conjugate exterior labels saves at most one.
Unit coefficients attain all these edge bounds simultaneously.
Transitivity puts every matching edge in the product orbit, sign flips give its four orientations, and the exponent-vector injection counts \(2m\) distinct products, including at \(m=2\) with full flips.
Inversion pairs the roots, giving a reciprocal unit polynomial, and the positive logarithms on an edge sum to \(2\max(\ell_i,\ell_j)\).
This gives (4), including its upper bound, and the uncompressed root covers \(D=0\).

### Even flips and the exterior thresholds

An unused coordinate supplies either parity on every proper support.
On full support a neutral coordinate instead gives exactly \(2^{m-1-e}\) choices per exterior sign assignment within either fixed parity class.
The bound \(2^{m-3}\ge m-1\) starts at equality for \(m=5\) and grows thereafter.
It also suffices for \(m=4,e\le2\), and \(m=4,e=3\) has four positives since three active labels cannot cancel.
For \(m=3,e=1\) each parity orbit has two positive choices.
For \(m=3,e=2\) with real exterior roots, conjugation flips only the neutral pair and transitivity produces the three individual flips.
With nonreal exterior roots, unequal active magnitudes give two positives already within a parity orbit.
At equal magnitudes \((a,b,u)\mapsto(b,a,-u)\) preserves the coordinatewise magnitudes and reverses the sign of \(abu\), so the orbit contains both parity classes and hence two distinct positive monomials.
For odd \(m\) the parity argument counts positive conjugates directly, even when simultaneous inversion of all labels is unavailable.
The even-flip assertion excludes \(e=m\).

For any matching, writing \(h=N_{\rm equal}\) and \(t=N_{\rm single}\) gives \(2h+t\le e\) and \(t\equiv e\pmod2\).
These inequalities give both lower bounds (16) and (17), including the option \(D=0\).
Equality with exterior count one at \(e=2\) forces \(h=1,t=0\), and a count of at most two at \(e=4\) forces \(h=2,t=0\).
In each case the paired logarithms give exactly \(M(P)\) by (4).
For one exterior conjugate the Salem-or-quadratic classification applies to the real exterior conjugate after choosing its sign.
An arbitrary representative on a neutral matching edge may itself lie on the circle.
An odd label set has no perfect matching, and an invariant matching on more than two labels contradicts primitivity.
The half-count bound alone gives the claimed obstruction as \(e\) grows.

### Degree-eight benchmark and source scope

The norm \(-1\) of \(-2+\sqrt5\) forces a quadratic extension of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), and the trace root generates this full quartic field.
The two real traces lie in \((-2,2)\), while the nonreal conjugate traces each give one exterior root.
The four distinct residues \(-2,4,-3,5\) give values \(116,116,319,319\) of \(T\), respectively, all multiples of \(29\).
Their distinctness exhausts the roots of the monic quartic modulo \(29\), so the simple-root form of Hensel's lemma applies and its splitting field embeds in \(\mathbb Q_{29}\).
Only one lifted trace is \(-2\) modulo \(29\), while none is \(2\).
The identity \(\prod_i(t_i+2)=116\) yields valuation one in that single coordinate of the discriminants \(t_i^2-4\).
Transport of the valuation gives the other three individual witnesses, so the square classes are independent.
The sign characters of the square-root basis then give the claimed multiquadratic degree by induction, hence four independent flips and transitivity on all eight lifted roots.
The degree-eight product orbit proves minimality of the displayed reciprocal lift.
Directly multiplying the two product traces gives \(t_1^2+t_2^2-4=-2p\), and eliminating \(p=3\pm\sqrt5\) gives the quartic and the degree-eight expansion (19)–(20).
The signs of \(Y^2-p(Y+2)\) at \(-2,0,2\) put three traces in \((-2,2)\) and one above \(2\), so the unique positive exterior root is Salem.
Finally \(|\operatorname{Im}(x+x^{-1})|\le\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\) at \(|x|=\lambda>1\) gives \(M(P)=\lambda^2>(1+\sqrt2)^2\).
