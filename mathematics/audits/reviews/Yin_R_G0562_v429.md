## Uniform slope bounds and the primitive allocation criterion

On 2026-11-18T15:57 UTC a separately assigned reviewer for Yin checked the complete [bounded-slope estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v191.md#bounded) and [construction criterion](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v191.md#criterion).
The reviewer took no part in developing L191 and received the fixed text and scope without its proof-development discussion or earlier findings.
The reviewer had contributed to the earlier conditional specialization result used here as an input.
The two conclusions of L191 follow, with a single constant independent of both exponents and their common divisor.

When \(a\ge4b\), take the leading block \(1-X^b\) at exponent \(3a\).
Its coefficient one-norm is two and all its zeros have modulus one.
The other block has degree \(2a+b\) and one-norm at most four.
The parent degree is \(3a+b\), the block separation is \(a-b\), and the quantity after subtracting twice the leading width is \(a-3b\).
The inequality with \(\delta=1/13\) is equivalent to \(13(a-3b)-(3a+b)=10(a-4b)\ge0\), with equality at \(a=4b\).
Thus the positive constant \(c_0=c'(4,1/13)\) from the separated-block theorem applies to every positive-measure irreducible factor here.

For all \(a,b>0\), the parent has constant and leading coefficients \(-1\).
Its four possible inner exponents \(a,3a,b,2a+b\) lie between \(\min(a,b)\) and \(3a+b-\min(a,b)\).
Coincident terms can change the coefficient or remove an occupied exponent, and cannot reduce either endpoint distance below this bound.
All monic irreducible factors are units, and \(s=6,\ L=6,\ G=\min(a,b)\) work in the sparse endpoint inequality whether or not terms coincide.
This yields the stated \(C=\log(1+0.31/720)/(20\log6)\).
On \(1/4<r\le1\), \(r/(3+r)\) is strictly increasing and has infimum \(1/13\) as \(r\downarrow1/4\).
On \(r\ge1\), \((1+r)/(3+r)\) increases from its minimum \(1/2\).
Equivalently the normalized endpoint factor \((1+r)\min(1,r)/(3+r)\) has infimum \(5/52\) on \(r>1/4\), obtained as a limit from above.
Thus the stated \(c=\min(c_0,\sqrt{C/13})\) suffices in all three ranges.
For each finite \(B>0\), \(c/\sqrt{1+B}\) works throughout \(0<b/a\le B\).

For a sequence of positive factor measures tending to zero, (1) forces the ratios to tend to infinity along the entire sequence.
Divide both exponents of each parent by their greatest common divisor to obtain a primitive direction \((a,b)\) with the same ratio.
The [common-dilation classification](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v388.md#statement) states precisely that each nontorsion factor is the substitution of an individual primitive factor and has that factor's measure.
The inference for these escaping sequences can also be checked using only the [large-direction substitution theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#statement), since \(b-3a=a(r-3)\to\infty\) and its threshold \(2859\) holds eventually for every dilation exponent.
This review checks that transfer at its point of use; the finite computations behind the stronger all-direction classification were outside the review.

In the resulting primitive directions the [exterior partition](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition) gives \(m(P)/(k/a)=\log\theta+O(e^{-c_1r})\) for some \(c_1>0\), with constants uniform over the factors and with \(\log\theta>0\).
Consequently measure tending to zero is equivalent to \(k/a\to0\) in this range.
Conversely the primitive factors are themselves members of the original family.
Since \(k\ge1\), any such allocation forces \(a\to\infty\); eventually \(k<a\), and \(\sum_P k_P=a\) forces a second nontorsion rational factor at every sufficiently late direction.
The [one-graph Schinzel implication](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v185.md#schinzel) asserts a single nontorsion irreducible factor for all sufficiently large primitive directions under its conjectural hypothesis.
It excludes precisely this eventual splitting.
An infimum of zero among positive measures would produce a sequence with \(0<m(P_j)<1/j\), so this exclusion gives a strictly positive infimum for the whole family under that hypothesis.
The conditional conclusion supplies neither a numerical constant for the unrestricted gap nor an unconditional decision on the existence of shrinking factors.
