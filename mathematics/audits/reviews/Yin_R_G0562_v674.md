# Independent review of the phase spectrum near the long powers

<a id="finding"></a>
## Uniform suppression and the first long-power moment

On November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the [phase spectrum](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v540.md#statement).
I received the candidate and requested review scope without participating in its development or receiving a preceding finding or substantive development discussion on this proof.
The fixed-coefficient limits, the uniform estimate, the explicit first moment and the qualified rational-harmonic conclusions follow under the stated critical-factor assumptions.

At a circle root, putting \(u=x^a\) and \(v=x^{as}\) gives the coefficient of \(x^b\) as \(1-(1+u^{-1})v\), with the opposite of the remaining terms equal to \(1+u-v\).
Solving for \(x^b\) gives \(R=(A-v)/(1-\overline A v)\), which has modulus one wherever defined, since \(|1-\overline A v|=|v-A|\) on the circle.
The excluded points satisfy \(v=A\) and \(2+2\Re u=1\), hence \(u=e^{\pm 2\pi i/3}\), impossible for a nonzero power of a nontorsion root.
Assigning the value one at those points makes every integer power of the quotient a bounded, almost everywhere continuous test.
The cited Haar limit applies with mass \(1/d\) per circle root, and the absolute contribution of the omitted phases is bounded by the off-circle proportion for every frequency.

For fixed \(k,\epsilon\), an \(L^1\) trigonometric approximation to \(R^k\) with error below \(\epsilon/2\) depends only on the torus test and the tolerance.
The empirical integral of the absolute approximation error converges to its Haar integral, giving a bound independent of the multiplying character \(x^\ell\).
The exceptional offsets are exactly the negatives of the frequencies in the approximant after substituting the two short powers.
For each surviving term the shifted integer \(t\) is nonzero, with \(|t|/b\le E_s/b+O((a+as)/b)\to0\).
A sequence of offending choices would therefore give nontorsion units \(x^t\) of height tending to zero, whose mean phase vanishes by the cited orbit estimate.
The equal number of extensions of each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(x^t)\) identifies that mean with \(T_P(t)\), including for negative \(t\).
The finite sum preserves uniform convergence and the chosen finite set works for every critical sequence and envelope.
In particular, eventual escape from each finite set of offsets gives zero response by letting the tolerance decrease.
Conversely, failure of a finite exceptional set for a fixed tolerance would allow a diagonal sequence of violations with \(s\to\infty\), \(|\ell|/b\to0\), outside successively larger integer boxes of pairs.
The equality of offsets for two fixed distinct pairs would require \(i-i'=s(j'-j)\), which eventually fails.

For \(|A|<1\) the denominator has no zero on the closed disk, and for \(|A|>1\) the numerator has none there.
The disk mean value formula applied to \(R^k\) or \(R^{-k}\), followed in the second case by boundary conjugation, yields the stated conditional moments.
For \(k=1\) the first arc contributes \(\pi^{-1}[t+\sin t]_{2\pi/3}^{\pi}=1/3-\sqrt3/(2\pi)\).
On the other arc the real part is \(1/2\) throughout a fraction \(2/3\) of the circle, and reflection cancels the imaginary parts.
This gives the positive constant \(2/3-\sqrt3/(2\pi)\).

For fixed \(p,q,h\) with \(qn=pb+\ell\), the relevant offset at the \(hq\)-th harmonic is \(h\ell\), so resonance or escape must be tested at that offset for each \(h\).
A moving offset can alternate between resonances, in which case the estimate itself asserts no common limiting moment.
For \(\ell=ia+jas\) with fixed \(i,j\), all integer moments of the powered law converge to \(c_{hp}(hi,hj)\), including the unit zeroth moment.
Trigonometric density determines the resulting law on the circle, and taking the \(q\)-th power leaves the choice of a lift unspecified.
The proof assumes the cited critical-scale, torus and small-height results at their recorded scopes and supplies no existence assertion for the factors.
