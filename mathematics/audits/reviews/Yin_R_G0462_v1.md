### Logarithmic domain, finite zero set and the infinite-norm branch

The inherited pointwise inequality B>=2G^2 holds on the circle for every rational polynomial pair, and G is nonzero.
Thus B is a nonzero real Laurent polynomial and is nonnegative there.
Its circle zeros form a finite set, since multiplication by a monomial gives a nonzero ordinary polynomial.
Away from that set log B is real and locally continuous.
Its positive part is bounded because B is continuous on the compact circle, and its negative part is bounded by the integrable logarithmic singularities of2G^2.
Therefore log B belongs to L1 and its Haar mean I is finite, with zero-set values immaterial to its Fourier coefficients.
The mean of B is finite and strictly positive, and E_G>0.
Every right side in K3--K4 is consequently finite for an individual input and supplied eta.
When W_* is infinite, both inequalities are valid in the extended-real sense without exponentiating infinity or assuming a finite continuous representative.

### Exact absolute Fourier representation and Fejer application

In the finite-W_* case, adding the finite constant coefficient I gives an absolutely summable full Fourier series.
The series converges uniformly to a continuous function whose Fourier coefficients agree with those of log B, by uniform termwise integration.
There is no factor-of-two ambiguity: W_* sums over ALL nonzero signed frequencies exactly as the statement defines it.
The absolute deviation of this continuous sum from I is therefore at most W_*.

I reread C177's actual proof rather than substituting a general Fejer theorem.
For the difference between log B and the continuous sum, every Fourier coefficient is zero, so every finite Fejer convolution is identically zero.
This difference is globally integrable and locally continuous at each circle point where B>0.
The positive Fejer kernel has normalized Haar mass1 and the retained bound1/[m sin(delta/2)^2] outside a fixed arc of radius delta.

The complementary contribution is bounded by the kernel tail times the finite L1 norm of the difference, together with its finite point value, and tends to zero.
The zero convolution thus forces the difference itself to vanish at every point where B>0.
The remaining finite set has measure zero, giving the asserted almost-everywhere equality.
All conditions of this exact argument are present, and no assertion of general L1 Fejer convergence is needed.

This proves |log B-I|<=W_* almost everywhere for the actual symbol.
