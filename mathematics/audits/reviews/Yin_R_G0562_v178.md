Conversely, each cyclotomic divisor of \(Q\) divides \(Q^*\), and \(Q(1)=-1\) excludes the only monic cyclotomic polynomial whose reversal changes its sign.
Thus \(C^*=C\) and \(q,q^*\) are coprime.
The two factors of monic \(Q=UV\) have unit constant terms, so the signed reversal of one factor gives a monic degree-\(a\) polynomial with the same Laurent autocorrelation.
The coefficients at zero and at \(a\) force exactly three unit coefficients and constant term \(-1\).
The two signed interior differences determine either \(Q\) or \(-Q^*\), including \(2b=a\), when both differences cancel.
Cancellation then makes one factor reciprocal up to sign.
In a splitting \(Q=(CA)B\) of the residual factor \(q=AB\), this puts \(A\) or \(B\) in the gcd of \(q,q^*\), so both cannot be nonconstant.

In each case \(Q(1)<0\), \(Q\) grows without bound, and \(Q'(x)\ge a-b\) for \(x\ge1\), giving a unique root \(\rho>1\) in \(q\).

The constant value of \(Q^*/Q\) at zero separates the binomial, which equals its own reduced seed.
For positive \(b\), the first nonzero term of \(1+Q^*/Q\) identifies \(r=\min(b,a-b)\), and coefficient two forces \(b=a-b=r\).
With \(z=X^r\in(0,1)\), the derivatives of the two displayed rational functions with respect to the other monomial have respective positive numerators \(1+z+z^2\) and \(1-z+z^2\).
For opposite positions of \(r\), cross multiplication gives \(y(1+w+z^2)=w(1+z^2)+z^2\).
When \(u<2r\) the lowest term forces \(y=w\), whence \(w^2=z^2\), and when \(u=2r\) its coefficient would have to be both one and two.
For \(u>2r\), the lowest exponent gives \(y=z^2\), leaving exactly \(w=z^4\).
