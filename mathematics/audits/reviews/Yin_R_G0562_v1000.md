## Both bounds hold throughout the stated charts

All zeros of an ordinary preparation satisfy \(v_p(\lambda-r_0)\ge1\) by the substitution \(r=r_0+pT\).
For a cubic slope image the same inequality follows from \(s(b)-r_0=(b-\beta)^2e(b-\beta)\) on the half-radius valuation ball.
Outside the slope disk every term therefore equals \(v_p(r-r_0)<1\), and there are at most three terms.
Inside, the finite integral rational slope has unit denominator in its primitive pair.
The ordinary inverse slope map gives an unramified point with both normalized coordinates in \(1+pO\).
The residue subgroup has order prime to \(p\), so taking the \(g\)-th power removes both Teichmüller factors and the logarithm is an isometry on increments there.
The coefficient multiplying the critical logarithm is \(gl\) in the displayed chart and \(-gk\) in the reciprocal chart for the same character \(U^l/V^k\), each a unit in its respective chart.
The rescaled ordinary function has scalar \(p^2\) and each of its \(m\) monic factors rescales by \(p^{-1}\), giving the stated additive \(m-2\) in the sum, including \(+1\) when \(p=m=3\).

At a branch point the distinguished quadratic places each critical root in an extension of degree at most two of the unramified base, with \(v_p(c_j-\beta)\ge1/2\).
The restricted cubic unit evaluates to a unit on this closed ball, and its scalar of valuation \(3/2\) cancels the three factors of valuation \(-1/2\) from rescaling.
The quadratic formal unit has unit value at each \(b_i\) in the open residue disk.
Summing the pairwise valuations \(v_p(c_j-b_i)\) in the two orders thus gives exactly the sum over the two critical values, with repeated roots counted in both sums.
Both coordinates at \(c_j\) differ from their Teichmüller lifts by elements of valuation at least \(1/2>1/(p-1)\).
Taking the logarithm of the \(g\)-th character power gives \(-gk B(c_j)\) there, including when the original character has nontrivial residue.
These evaluations require only the curve and slope equations.
If the character were torsion, Bézout's identity for the primitive pair would express the point as a torsion translate of the parametrized subtorus at a multiple zero, excluded by v852 even on an axis.

The elimination bound gives \([K:\mathbb Q]\le16\) and both absolute heights \(O_F(L)\), while the residue subgroup has \(g\mid p^f-1\) and \(f\le16\).
Yu's displayed bound on page 337 allows both signs and zero exponents.
If \(p^f\equiv1\pmod4\), Hensel lifting a primitive fourth root of unity puts it in the original completion, so adjoining it globally preserves \(e,f\) at a chosen extension of the place and gives global degree at most 32.
Dividing Yu's ideal valuation by \(e\) cancels exactly the factor \(e^{2-1}\).
Each height factor divided by \(f\log p\) costs \(O_F(1+L/\log p)\), and the exponent logarithm costs \(O(L+\log p)\) because \(g<p^{16}\).
This gives the first bound with \(p^{16}\) even after the global adjunction.

For independent coordinates with both exponents nonzero, independently inverting the bases as needed preserves their heights, generated field, independence and residue subgroup.
Dividing their power difference by the second power is division by a local unit.
Theorem 3 of Bugeaud and Laurent therefore applies with the original residue order \(g\) and positive exponents \(g|l|,g|k|\).
Taking equal logarithmic height majorants \(C_F(L+\log p)\) covers both the heights and \((\log p)/D\) since \(1\le D\le16\).
The theorem's maximum is \(O_F(L+\log p)\) including its \(\log\log p\) term, and its remaining factors cost \(O_F(g(L+\log p)^2/(\log p)^4)\).
On an axis the primitive exponent is a sign, and the local contribution to the height of \((W^g-1)^{-1}\) gives \(v_p(W^g-1)\log p\le16(g h(W)+\log2)\).

In rank one a lift \(\gamma\) of a free generator lies in the group generated by the coordinates, so its reduction has order dividing \(g\).
The identities \(h(U)=|i|h(\gamma)\) and \(h(V)=|j|h(\gamma)\), with at least one nonzero exponent, give the upper bound on \(h(\gamma)\), and Northcott finiteness in degree at most sixteen gives a uniform positive lower bound.
Thus \(0<|il-jk|\ll_F NL\), where nonvanishing follows from nontorsion of the character.
The torsion order \(m\) is absolutely bounded since \(\varphi(m)\le16\).
The valuation group in the ordinary field or the at-most-quadratic branch extension puts the principal unit \(\gamma^g\) in the logarithm isometry range.
Raising \(W^g\) to \(m\) can only increase the increment valuation and removes the torsion factors.
The resulting valuation equals \(v_p(\gamma^g-1)+v_p(m(il-jk))\), bounded by \(O_F((gL+1)/\log p+L/\log p)\) by the same local height inequality and the integer size bound.
This implies the claimed second bound since \(g\ge1\).

On each slope disk the individual valuations are nonnegative, so the estimate for their sum also bounds each term.
For fixed \(p\) the first bound is \(O_{F,p}(\log^3(3+N))\), giving the asserted lower bound on the absolute value.
At 8447 the horizontal disk has \(m=2\) and a compatible section has mutual root distance valuation \(v_p(H)/2\), so the prescribed value forces the sum to equal \(a+2\).
The fixed-prime estimate then gives the cube-root lower bound on \(\log(2+N_a)\).
The cited Hensel construction prescribes \(t\) modulo \(p^{a+1}\) and \(8448\), which are coprime.
Its least positive representative gives \(k=pt\le8448p^{a+2}\), with \(\gcd(pt,27)=1\) because \(t\equiv1\pmod3\) and \(p=8447\).
Since \(8421p\equiv27\pmod{8448}\), the residue compatibility holds for that representative.
This checks the application of the recorded Hensel witness and the two published inequalities at the level of their statements and hypotheses.
The published proofs and the finite-field search producing the witness were outside the scope of this review.
