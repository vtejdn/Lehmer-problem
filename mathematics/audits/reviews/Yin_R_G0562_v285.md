## Finding: the complete criterion and sharpness are valid

The whole stated result passes independent mathematical review.
This includes arbitrary finite order sets, the complete initial window, its least-length asymptotic, and irreducible counterexamples with any violating prime as exact denominator.

The triangular reciprocal-to-trace transformation preserves the specified integral prefix and full coefficient integrality.
Multiplication by the root-of-unity unit also preserves algebraic integrality of the tested value.
For orders at least three, the decomposition of the cyclotomic integer ring as \(\mathbb Z[\tau_m]+\zeta_m\mathbb Z[\tau_m]\) follows from the quadratic relation for \(\zeta_m\).
If an element of this ring is real, uniqueness over the real trace field forces its second component to vanish: conjugating \(a+\zeta_m b\) gives \(a+(\tau_m-\zeta_m)b\), so \((2\zeta_m-\tau_m)b=0\) implies \(b=0\).
Thus the trace integer ring and its monic presentation used in the proof are correct, including the immediate rational cases \(m=1,2\).

For every prime divisor \(p\) of the least common coefficient denominator \(A\), at least one coefficient of \(AS\) is nonzero modulo \(p\).
Otherwise \(A/p\) would still clear all denominators.
Its coefficients of degree above \(h\) vanish modulo \(p\), while each passed test puts its value in \(p\mathbb Z[\tau_m]\).
Reduction of the free monic quotient presentation therefore gives divisibility by the entire reduced \(\Psi_m\), with multiplicities retained.
The common least common multiple must have degree at most \(h\).
This proves the forcing direction for every denominator prime, rather than only for primes of good reduction.

Conversely, a monic lift \(H\) of a reduced least common multiple of degree at most \(h<n\) satisfies \(H\in p\mathbb Z[T]+\Psi_m\mathbb Z[T]\) for every tested order, by division by the monic integral \(\Psi_m\).
Consequently \(H(\tau_m)/p\) is integral.
The monic term of \(H\) ensures that \(S_0+H/p\) has exact denominator \(p\), and the degree bound leaves the full required prefix integral.
The inverse triangular transformation shows that the corresponding reciprocal polynomial has the same exact denominator.
This also covers the empty order set, whose least common multiple has degree zero, and gives the claimed single-order criterion because reduction preserves the degree of a monic polynomial.

The coefficient congruence at the auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne p\) is attainable because multiplication by \(p\) is invertible modulo \(\ell^2\) on every freely chosen nonleading coefficient of \(S_0\).
The leading coefficient is already the prescribed \(p\), since \(\deg H<n\).
Substitution gives \(pP(Z)\equiv p(Z-1)^{2n}\pmod\ell\), and its value at one is congruent to \(\ell\pmod{\ell^2}\).
Hence \(pP(Z+1)\) satisfies Eisenstein with leading coefficient prime to \(\ell\) and constant coefficient divisible by \(\ell\) exactly once.
The irreducibility conclusion is therefore valid at degree \(2n\), with the same exact denominator and prefix.
A torsion root of a monic irreducible rational polynomial would identify it with an integral cyclotomic polynomial, contradicting that denominator.
All the tested values are consequently nonzero.

For the window computation, reduction of a primitive root of order \(p^a t\), with \(p\nmid t\), has exact order \(t\).
The reduction fibers on primitive roots are uniform, and equality of traces identifies only inverse pairs.
Thus different prime-to-\(p\) parts have disjoint reduced trace sets, and the multiplicities within one such set are equal.
For \(t\ge3\), the number of distinct traces is \(\varphi(t)/2\); their common multiplicity is one for \(a=0\) and \(\varphi(p^a)\) for \(a\ge1\).
The exceptional groups also have nondecreasing multiplicities: for odd \(p\) and \(t=1,2\) they are \(1, (p-1)/2, p(p-1)/2,\ldots\); for \(p=2,t=1\) they are \(1,1,1,2,4,\ldots\).
These include the small orders and the coincident traces in characteristic two without an implicit separability assumption at the bad prime.
The terminal order of each group is exactly its member in \((J/p,J]\), so the sum of terminal degrees is precisely the degree of the reduced least common multiple.
The intervals contain \((J/2,J]\) for every prime \(p\), proving that the minimum occurs at two and that failure is witnessed by exact denominator two.

Finally, summing the displayed totient identity gives \(\sum_{m\le x}\varphi(m)=3x^2/\pi^2+O(x\log(2x))\).
Replacing \(\varphi(m)/2\) by \(\deg\Psi_m\) changes only the two initial terms, hence contributes \(O(1)\).
Subtracting the sums at \(J\) and \(J/2\) gives \(9J^2/(8\pi^2)+O(J\log(2J))\).
The integer threshold is at least \(n-j\), because \(h=n-j-1\); its least crossing therefore has the stated asymptotic \((2\sqrt2\pi/3)\sqrt{n-j}\).
Monotonicity also follows directly from inclusion of the tested orders and the least common multiple characterization.
