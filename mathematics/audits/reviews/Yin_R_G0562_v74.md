## Actual degrees, torsion translates and the two attained minima

At 2026-11-16T23:05 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the full C345v74 submission dated 2026-11-16T23:03.
The reviewer received the fixed proof without development dialogue, author explanation or an earlier verdict on it and took no part in its development.

For the unit \(\gamma\) the positive conjugate logarithms sum to half the sum of their absolute values, by the unit norm.
Uniform averaging on the splitting group gives each distinct image equal mass \(1/D\), with \(D\) the degree of \(\gamma\).
The two occupied exterior labels in a random permutation receive a uniform ordered pair of exponent positions because the projected group is \(S_m\).
In the balanced orientation, the two nonzero log moduli of the representatives have opposite signs and common absolute value \(\log R\).
The global orientation sign can depend on the permutation, but disappears inside the absolute value.
Thus the average absolute log is \(\log R\) times the mean of \(|b_i-b_j|\) over unordered pairs.
In the unbalanced group the even flips at fixed permutation give all four signs on the two designated coordinates with equal multiplicity, since a third coordinate allows parity correction.
The average over these signs is \(\log R\max(|b_i|,|b_j|)\).
Multiplying either average by \(D/(2L)=D/(4\log R)\) verifies both normalizations in (1) and (2).

In the connected balanced case the all-ones vector generates the full relation lattice modulo torsion.
A constant shift of exponents can change the actual monomial by \(\zeta^h\), so only distinctions proved modulo constant vectors are automatically distinctions of actual conjugates.
Within the positive permutation list, equal coordinate sums rule out a nonzero constant shift.
With a repeated entry, an odd transposition fixes the exponent vector.
Consequently the pure even permutations realize every permutation of the vector, and the presence of a negative global orientation realizes the whole negative list as well.
This works both for the direct product group and when orientation is prescribed by permutation parity.
All actual images belong to these lists since each \(g\) acts on the representatives by a permutation with one common sign.

For constant exponents the value is a root of unity.
For one exception of difference \(u\ne0\) from the common level, the minority difference is \(u\) in the positive list and \(-u\) in the negative list.
The multiplicities distinguish the levels, so these two lists stay disjoint modulo constants.
There are exactly \(2m\) images, and the sum of absolute differences in (1) is \((m-1)|u|\), giving \(|u|L\) for every base level \(h\).
For two equal exceptions of multiplicities \((m-2,2)\) the same distinction of minority difference proves that \(D=2\binom m2\), since \(m\ge8\).
For multiplicities \((m-2,1,1)\) even the positive list has \(m(m-1)\) members.
If the largest multiplicity is at most \(m-3\) and some value repeats, one can choose a union of complete levels of size \(3\le j\le m-3\).
One level suffices when a level has size at least three, and adding levels of size one or two to reach three or four handles the rest.
Every \(j\)-subset occurs as its set of positions, giving at least \(\binom m3\ge m(m-1)\) distinct images for \(m\ge8\).
All distinct entries instead give \(m!/2\) distinct images already from even permutations.

In every balanced case left after omitting constants and the single exception, the sum of absolute differences is at least \(2(m-2)\).
A level of largest size \(2\le k\le m-2\) gives \(k(m-k)\) cross-level differences of at least one, and all distinct entries give at least \(\binom m2\).
Combining with \(D\ge m(m-1)\) in (1) proves the lower bound \((m-2)L\).
A vector with two entries \(1\) and all others zero attains both lower bounds simultaneously, proving sharpness in the balanced orientation.

In the unbalanced case the even flips give independence modulo torsion as in C345v70, so distinct orbit vectors describe distinct values.
The zero vector is the only torsion case, and one supported exponent \(u\) has \(2m\) images of measure \(M(P)^{|u|}\) for its minimal polynomial.
For \(2\le s<m\), every \(s\)-subset of labels is realized, and a spare label permits all \(2^s\) signs on that support.
Every pair meeting the support contributes at least one to the sum in (2).
Counting incidences between \(s\)-subsets and label pairs gives the lower bound \(2^{s-2}(\binom ms-\binom{m-2}s)\) for \(\log M(\gamma)/L\).
For \(2\le s\le m-2\), the difference is \(\binom{m-1}{s-1}+\binom{m-2}{s-1}\), at least \((m-1)+(m-2)\) by the range of \(s-1\).
At \(s=m-1\) the difference is \(m\) and the sign factor suffices.
At full support parity still gives at least \(2^{m-1}\) vectors and every pair contributes at least one, hence a ratio at least \(2^{m-3}\ge2m-3\).
With two unit-magnitude exponents there are exactly \(4\binom m2\) signed images, and precisely \(2m-3\) pairs meeting their support.
This attains the unbalanced lower bound for each choice of the two signs.

The original polynomial meets the torsion-separation hypothesis of C345v68, so applying its two-height estimate to the one-block partition gives \(L\gg(\log(6m))^{-106}\) with an absolute constant.
The two attained lower bounds grow at least linearly in \(m\) times this quantity and therefore diverge uniformly over the respective nonexceptional classes.
The reviewer finds both sharp bounds, the exceptional measures and the uniform divergence proved as submitted.
