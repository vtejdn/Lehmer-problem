Write \(\epsilon=\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\) and \(u=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\).
Along the sequence \(d\to\infty\), since bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound the integer coefficients, and eventually \(\sigma\le2\).
In the reflected-root discriminant bound take \(t=1-(2d)^{-6}\).
The nonzero integer discriminant, the \(d\) diagonal terms and \(\log M(P)=\log\sigma\le\log2\) give energy \(O(d\log(2d))\).
Up to \(N=d^4\), \(t^k\) is bounded below, and subtracting the two identical reflected real-root contributions \(2\sigma^{-k}\) costs \(O(\log N)\) in squared weighted norm.
Division by \((d-2)^2\) gives \(\sum_{1\le |k|\le N}|\widehat\nu(k)|^2/|k|\ll\epsilon^2\) for the probability on the neutral conjugates. [Discriminant inequality (1)](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#energy).

For an arc of length \(\ell\), enlargement and contraction by \(\epsilon\) at both ends followed by uniform smoothing at that scale give upper and lower tests with errors at zero frequency at most \(2\epsilon\).
Their nonconstant coefficients are bounded by a constant times \(\min(\ell+2\epsilon,|k|^{-1},\epsilon^{-1}k^{-2})\).
The squared sum weighted by \(|k|\) is \(O(1+\log(2+\ell/\epsilon))\), and the absolute tail beyond \(N\) is \(O(1/(\epsilon N))\).
Pairing with the energy bound proves relative counts for \(\ell/\epsilon\to\infty\) and an \(O(\epsilon)\) maximal gap.
For \(g=U\) or \(|U|\), absolute continuity and Parseval give \(\sum |k|\,|\widehat g(k)|^2\le\|U\|_2\|U'\|_2\le K(2K+1)\|U\|_1^2\).
The absolute tail is \(O(N^{-1/2}K\sqrt{2K+1}\|U\|_1)\), which fits \(O(K\epsilon\|U\|_1)\) for \(1\le K\le d\).
Constants sample exactly.
These estimates hold uniformly before any coefficient or support bound.

For actual positive residues the empirical absolute mean is \(2(bj-W(T))/(d-2)\), since each interior trace has two neutral lifts and \(W(T)>0\).
On a bounded-ratio subsequence, absorbing the relative sampling error gives bounded circle \(L^1\) norm.
Jaming, Kellay and Saba's ordered-frequency inequality applies to every nonzero integral Laurent coefficient and gives \(\log(1+\#\operatorname{supp}U)\le52\|U\|_1\).
Each Fourier coefficient also has modulus at most \(\|U\|_1\), giving uniformly bounded absolute coefficient sum and angular derivative \(O(K)\).
Consequently \(\min U\ge-O(K\epsilon)\).
To test a purported infinite sequence of negative minima, extract fixed coefficients and a fixed support size, and diagonalize the integral relations among the positive exponents.
The eventual relation lattice \(\Lambda\) is saturated, since vanishing of a fixed nonzero multiple of an integer implies its vanishing.

The finite generation of \(\Lambda\) then places each sufficiently late parametrizing circle inside the group, contradicting any negative minimum.
The argument includes the trivial group for constant polynomials.

Evaluation of the trace transform at \(z=1\) is permitted because \(P(1)\ne0\), giving \(W(T)/u=b(q-1)+\sum_{x<T}W(x)/(2-x)\).
Voutier's bound applies to the nontorsion degree-\(d\) unit with \(d h(\sigma)=\log\sigma\).
Thus \(u=2\cosh(\log\sigma)-2\ge(\log\sigma)^2\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^6\), so \(\epsilon=o(u)\).
Taking \(h=\sqrt{\epsilon\min(u,1/(K+1))}\) gives \(h/\epsilon\to\infty,\ h/u\to0\) and \(Kh\to0\).
If \(W(2)>0\), integrality gives \(U(1)\ge1\) and the derivative bound gives \(U\ge1/2\) on the arc of radian radius \(h\).
Pairing the conjugates there leaves \(\gg dh\) distinct traces with \(0<2-x\le h^2\).
Their contribution to the exact endpoint identity forces \(W(T)/d\gg u/h\to\infty\), whereas \(W(T)\le bj\).
This excludes positive values at two, and the global sign excludes negative values there.

Since \(U(1)=0\), its mean \(B=-2\sum_{r>0}[z^r]U\) is even, and the nonzero nonnegative \(U\) gives \(B>0\).
In the remaining case \(B=2\), the integer polynomial \(1+\sum_{r>0}[z^r]U\,z^r\) has boundary real part \(U/2\), positive real part in the open disk and constant one.
The continuous-density case of the positive-real classification leaves the constant or \(1\pm z^s\), and evaluation at one leaves only \(U=2-z^s-z^{-s}\). [Positive-real classification](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar).
Its value at \(z=\sigma>1\) contradicts \(W(T)>0\).
Thus \(B\ge4\), and signed sampling gives \(bj/d=(d-2)(B+o(1))/(2d)+W(T)/d\) with bounded \(B\).
A subsequence violating the lower floor would have bounded ratios, and an infinite subsequence with \(B\ge6\) prevents convergence to two.
When the ratios converge to two this leaves \(B=4\) eventually, and then the branch formula gives \(W(T)/d\to0\).
The degree obstruction follows by applying the floor to any subsequence with \(K\epsilon\to0\).

For the zero-mass inverse take the first power \(\sigma^k\) above \(d\min(\log(d+2),u^{-1/2})\).
On the tail this target is \(d\) times a factor tending to infinity and is at most \(d\log(d+2)\), giving \(k=O(\log d/\log\sigma)\).
Voutier's lower bound then gives \((k+2)\epsilon\to0\).
The integral weight \((x^2-4)(D_k(x)-2)\) has that degree and takes the actual unscaled residue values by L462v129.
Its previously reviewed zero-mass limit gives \(j/d\to2\).

For a fixed initial input with \(\sigma>2\), division by the monic trace polynomial \(S=(x-T)H\) gives \(p\in\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(p(t)=g(t)S'(t)\) at every trace \(t\).
Partial fractions at infinity give \([x^{m-1}]p=\sum_t g(t)\), so \(q=1+\sum_t g(t)\to\infty\) as \(k\to\infty\).
Indeed \(g(T)=(T+2)(\sigma^k+\sigma^{-k}-2)\to\infty\), while every neutral lift has infinite order, giving \(0<2-D_k(t)\le4\) at the interior traces.
For the integral simplex parameter \(-p\), the required residues are \(p(t)/H'(t)=(T-t)(-g(t))>0\), and their endpoint sum is at most \(4\sum_{t<T}(T-t)\), independently of \(k\) at this fixed input.
Hence the sum is strictly below \(q+T/2\) for all sufficiently large \(k\).
The simplex yields the same-degree Pisot minimal polynomial with the prescribed reciprocal sum, and its trace transform \(1-q+(x-2)p(x)/S(x)\) has actual residues \((t-2)g(t)=W(t)\) with \(b=1\).
There are only finitely many such initial inputs, so these choices preserve both \(K\epsilon\to0\) and \(j/d\to2\).
