## The inverse test, the factor fields and the third iterate

At 2026-11-25T03:28 UTC an agent outside development of [C0901 v136](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v136.md#statement) independently reviewed for Yin its statement and all three proof sections against the fixed inputs below.
The reviewer had worked on other materials in this family and received the section names of v136 without its development discussion.
The inverse characterization, the two distinct fields and the quantitative third-iterate estimate hold with the stated smallness and strict-interior qualifications.

For a realizable \(A\) above the lower endpoint in the low interval, v120 gives \(A=\rho^{2e}\), \(Y=M(A)=M(\rho)^{2e}\), with full degree under powers.
Thus the positive square root \(R=\rho^e\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Q(A)\).
In each factorization of the original polynomial the nonempty group projecting nontrivially onto the leading constituent supplies powers of \(M(\rho)\) of product \(\sqrt Y\).
The complementary product is \(B=A/\sqrt Y\), including \(B=1\) at the upper endpoint, and it is itself a polynomial measure.
For \(A<Y<A^2\) it is strictly greater than one, so both groups have a factor of positive measure.

The descent of v120 gives a smaller polynomial value \(A_1=r_1^{2e}\), with all positive powers of \(r_1\) of full degree.
The chosen half stabilizer contains the root stabilizer, hence its product and the real signed representative \(r_1\) belong to \(\mathbb Q(\rho)\).
The squared-over-measure ratio at \(A_1\) is \((r_1^2/M(r_1))^{2e}=(\rho^2/M(\rho))^{2e}=A^2/Y\).
The new value is below the upper endpoint in the sense \(M(A_1)<A_1^2\), so it is either at the lower endpoint or permits the same step.
The uniform cutoff persists as the values decrease.
Degrees decrease strictly and the ultimate degree is at most the first descended degree \(2u<D\).
At the endpoint the invariant equals the endpoint itself, giving exactly the Salem number \(B^2\) in the first descended field.
The positive factor measures in \(B\) are strict Perron units, and a conjugate of their product attaining its maximum modulus must fix each actual positive factor by the exterior-product property.
Hence \(B\) also has full degree upon squaring.
Its conjugates then inherit the Salem moduli with multiplicity one off the circle and it is reciprocal by a neutral conjugate and its complex conjugate.
This gives \(\mathbb Q(B)=\mathbb Q(B^2)\) and \(d\le2u\ll(\log(3D))^{212}\).

Conversely a real Salem number realizes itself.
In the other branch \(A=R^2\) and \(R\in\mathbb Q(A)\) give equality of the two fields, so the same embeddings count once on both sides of \(M(R)^2=M(A)\).
Using the minimum of \(R\) and, when \(B>1\), the minimum of the Salem number \(B\), realizes \(A\) since \(M(R)B=A\).
The two nonempty groups already found show that two factors are necessary in the strict interior, counting multiplicities.

There \(Y=A^2/B^2\in\mathbb Q(A)\).
On the leading irreducible constituent the complementary product has zero projection, so an automorphism fixing \(Y\) fixes twice the projected class of \(A\).
The projection of a signed coordinate has orbit size exactly \(D=2ub\), including in the oriented model where the two signs and all labels are distinct for \(b\ge3\).
The original orbit also has \(D\) distinct classes, since the matched unit is compressed and its powers preserve this separation.
The equivariant projection is a bijection between these two finite orbits; fixing the projected vector forces fixation of the actual root \(A\).
This proves equality of the embedded fields of \(A,Y\).
All positive powers of \(M(\rho)\) generate this same field by its strict Perron property.

For a factor measure \(C\) of the complementary product, the stabilizer of \(B\) fixes \(C\) by the same maximum argument.
In the Salem field \(\mathbb Q(B)\) the unit \(v=C\tau C\) is totally real, and each conjugate has absolute value in \((0,B^2]\).
For sufficiently small \(m\), \(B^2<\sqrt2\), so all conjugates of the integral \(1-v^2\) have absolute value strictly less than one.
Its zero norm gives \(v^2=1\), and a complex place makes \(v\) positive there, whence \(v=1\).
The relative unit kernel in this degree has rank one modulo signs with a positive Salem generator.
Since \(C>1\), it is a positive power of that generator and has the full degree \(d\).
The two actual fields are distinct by \(d<D\), so these tests identify the factors in every realization and also force their individual measures when there are exactly two.

From \(Y=A^2/B^2\) the height triangle inequality and its reverse give \(|h(Y)-2h(B)|\le2h(A)\).
Multiplying by \(D=\deg A=\deg Y\) gives the claimed error \(2\log Y\), with main term \(2(D/d)\log B\).
Voutier's corollary and \(d\ll(\log(3D))^{212}\) bound that main term below by a positive constant times \(D/((\log(3D))^{212}(\log\log(3D))^3)\) as \(D\) grows.
The error is at most \(4m\).
Also \(B^2=A^2/Y<A\) makes \(\log B<m/2\), forcing \(d\) uniformly to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
Putting \(t=\log d\), the other Voutier bound yields \(t>(2m)^{-1/3}\log t\).
For small \(m\), \(\log t>1\), hence \(t>(2m)^{-1/3}\log((2m)^{-1/3})\).
After dividing by \(212\) in the block estimate, the coefficient of \(m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\) is \(1/(636\sqrt[3]2)\), up to a vanishing relative error.
The lower bound of order \(D\) divided by powers of logarithms gives \(\log\log(\log M(Y))\ge\log\log D-o(1)\), so two exponentiations give the asserted strict-constant estimate.
A bounded third iterate bounds the second by \(M(Y)\ge Y\); v120 then forces the low interval, and its strict interior becomes impossible by this growth.
At either endpoint the full degrees of the positive powers of \(A\) give the iterates claimed.
These implications supply no existence or exclusion of a shrinking endpoint sequence.
