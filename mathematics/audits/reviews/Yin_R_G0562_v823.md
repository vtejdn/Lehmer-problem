## Independent review of the prime equal to the delay and the square-root count

At 2026-11-20T23:34 (UTC), an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the whole submitted C345 v686 and found its exceptional-set estimate and all-delay density consequences sound.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and inspected its fixed text and mathematical inputs without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on v686.

For every \(n\ge7\), the odd-prime trace test covers \(p<n\), since its \(p^{e+1}\) divisibility falls on one of the three neighbors of the seed.
The dyadic argument only uses the trace indices \(1,2,3,6\), so its divisibility applies at every such delay.
At \(p=n\), the integral matrix attached to \(r^{1/p^e}\) has degree \(\deg P\) because this radical belongs to the original field and generates it.
The congruence for the consecutive matrix powers \(p^{e+1},p^e\) therefore compares \(P^{[p]}\) and \(P\) modulo \(p^{e+1}\).
Since \(p\) is odd, adjoining the same linear cyclotomic factors gives the claimed congruence for \(G\).
The first delayed trace difference is \(a(a^{p-1}-1-\epsilon p)\), of valuation exactly \(v_p(a)\) if \(p\mid a\).
In this case the required \(p^e\) divides \(a/\operatorname{rad}(a)\).

For \(x=z^p\) the root equation is \((x+\epsilon a)z=ax+\epsilon\).
If the coefficient of \(z\) vanished, the right side would be \(\epsilon(1-a^2)\ne0\).
Thus \(x\) determines \(z\), so the \(p+1\) simple roots give \(p+1\) distinct transformed roots and determine the stated monic polynomial.
For \(p\nmid a\), its coefficients at powers \(j=2,3,4\) must vanish modulo \(p^2\), since \(p\ge7\).
The exact coefficient is
\[
 \binom{p}{j-1}(\epsilon a)^{p-j+1}-\binom{p}{j}a^j\epsilon^{p-j}.
\]
Dividing by \(p\) and using the residues of the binomial coefficients shows that \(j\epsilon a^{p-j+1}+(j-1)a^j\) vanishes modulo \(p\).
Multiplication by \(a^{j-2}\) and Fermat's congruence give the displayed test in \(a^2\).
The three indices yield \(a^2=-2\epsilon\), \(8+3\epsilon=0\) and \(20\epsilon=0\) in the residue field.
The last congruence is impossible for \(p\ge7\).
This leaves only \(p>n\) as a possible prime responsible for failure of divisibility.

For such a prime the positive radical \(\beta=r^{1/p}\) lies in the same field as \(r\), has the same degree, and is a unit.
Its distinct conjugates map to the distinct conjugates of \(r\) under \(p\)-th powering, so it has one exterior root and neutral conjugates.
The inverse of a neutral conjugate is its complex conjugate, forcing reciprocity of the minimal polynomial and hence the Salem signature.
This uses the field-radical argument of v680 at its general algebraic scope.
The prime exclusion specific to the seed in v680 plays no role.
The logarithmic measure is \(\log r/p<z/p\) with \(z=\log(X+1)\), and Voutier's Corollary 2 applies to its own degree \(d\ge4\).
As \(d\le n+1\le p\), this gives \(p<z\log^3(3p)/2\).
The function \(v/\log^3(3v)\) is increasing for \(v\ge8\), and testing a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(z\log^3(2z)\) gives the stated upper range for \(p\).

For \(D=n+1\le p\) and \(B=e^{z/p}\), the cumulative Salem count in v685 allows every even degree up to \(D\) and has an absolute constant.
Its power \(B^{D/2}\) is at most \(e^{z/2}\).
The supplementary exponential has logarithm \(O(\sqrt{D\log(2DB)})\), where \(D\log(2DB)=D\log(2D)+Dz/p=O(z\log^4(2z))\).
The counting input follows at this scope from the reflected power-sum budget, Newton reconstruction through the half degree, and the signed integer vector count with weight \(O(D\log(2DB))\).
For fixed \(n,p,\epsilon\) the denominator \(r^n-\epsilon r\) in the seed formula is nonzero since \(r>1\).
Thus a candidate \(\beta\) yields at most one seed for those parameters.
There are \(O(z^2\log^6(2z))\) delay and prime pairs, and the additional factor \(D\) from summing degrees is also polynomial in \(z\).
Absorbing these factors yields precisely \(\sqrt X\exp(O(\sqrt{\log X}(\log\log X)^2))\), which is \(o(X)\).

Outside this exceptional set every index for every sign and delay divides \(H(a)\).
The factor measure and irreducibility formulas then apply for each \(h\ge1\), with the substituted torsion cofactor contributing only roots of unity.
Taking the infimum preserves the common lower bound in (2).
The inequality \(\log(a-1)\ge(\log a)/2\) holds from \(a=3\) onwards.
Thus the additional exceptional proportion at threshold \(t\log a\) is bounded by the limiting tail for \(H>1/(2t)\), which is \(O_s(t^s)\) by its finite \(s\)-moment.
For every positive sublogarithmic \(f\), the asserted convergence follows by fixing the multiplier of \(f\) before allowing \(t\) to decrease to zero.
Deleting the zero-density exceptional set from the seeds with \(H(a)=1\) preserves their positive product density and imposes the gcd-one irreducibility condition at every substitution and delay simultaneously.
