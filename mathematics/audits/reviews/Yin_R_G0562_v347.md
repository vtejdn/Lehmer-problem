### Torsion points and their multiplicities

The difference of two fast exponents has absolute value less than \(p\), and equality of the two diagonal exponents forces that difference to vanish modulo \(p\).
Thus each occupied power of \(x\) in the normalized pullback \(K(x,z)\) has just one coefficient monomial in \(z\), and both end coefficients are nonzero at every nonzero \(z\).
The monomial map has finite kernel and sends every torsion coset of dimension one to another of dimension one.
A torsion component of \(K\) would therefore have image equal to a torsion component of \(H\).
Section 4.1, p. 7, of the [Beukers–Smyth author edition](../literature/BS02/readme.md#editions) applies to the entire resulting plane curve, even if it is reducible.
I checked the theorem statement against the retained source and accepted its bound with ordinary Newton polygon area, without re-auditing its proof.
The \(p\) lifts of each torsion root of \(J\) are jointly distinct across all such roots because \(b\) is invertible modulo \(p\).
The containing rectangle gives the bound \(22d_0s_0/p\) for distinct roots.

Over each component fraction field the polynomial \(t^p-X\) is separable because \(X\) is invertible.
The monic extension of the reduced coordinate algebra injects into the product of these field extensions by using the basis \(1,t,\ldots,t^{p-1}\).
This gives a reduced torus pullback, and clearing monomials leaves no coordinate-axis factor.
The resultant in \(x\) is consequently nonzero and its Sylvester matrix gives degree at most \((2d_0-1)s_0\) in \(z\).
All \(p\) specialized polynomials retain degree \(d_0\) and differ from \(J\) only by a nonzero scalar and a scaling of \(x\).
The derivative also retains its degree in characteristic zero, so each specialized Sylvester matrix has corank \(e=\deg\gcd(J,J')\).
After constant row reduction, each of its \(e\) vanishing rows supplies a linear factor at that value of \(z\).
The \(p\) distinct values charge all excess multiplicities at most \((2d_0-1)s_0/p\), proving (4) when combined with the point count.

### The surviving factor and its degree

Every noncyclotomic factor of the common row gcd would give a polynomial in \(x^p\) whose irreducible factors all divide \(P\) and are nontorsion.
The single copy of \(P\) and the gcd-one hypothesis on its support differences preclude this.
Thus removing the common row gcd removes only padding and preserves every occupied class.
The residual diagonal has exactly one copy of \(P\), and width additivity and the collision test make the diagonal of every nonunit Laurent factor nonconstant.
Its extreme coefficients are units by multiplication of the extreme coefficients on the entire diagonal.
The factor carrying \(P\) occurs once, and reciprocity of the full lift and of \(P\) fixes its Laurent associate class.

A rational irreducible factor with a geometric torsion component is the cyclotomic polynomial of a primitive character, by taking all Galois conjugates of that component.
The character restricts nontrivially to the diagonal by the collision test, so this possibility is excluded for \(B\).
For a different factor without such a component, (4) would count all \(d_0\) diagonal roots with multiplicity and give strictly fewer than \(d_0\), since \(24s_0<p\).
It follows that \(B\) carries all independent measure, and that the \(d-D\) remaining roots of its diagonal consume part of its torsion bound.
Applying the projected-support gap to the full primitive lift proves \(m(B)\ge\eta_k\) independently of the number of rows of \(B\).
Collinear support would make \(m(B)\) equal to its diagonal measure by power substitution and hence exclude \(m(P)<\eta_k\).
In the latter range the determinant \(p\) scales a lattice triangle of area at least \(1/2\) into the \(d\)-by-\(s\) rectangle.
Consequently \(d\ge p/(2s)\), and \(D\ge d(1-24s/p)\) gives \(D\ge p/(2s)-12\).
The centered bound for \(h\ge s\) gives the stated exponent \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) on \(D+12\).

### The neutral-root contribution

For the reciprocal factor \(B_0\), the generic fiber is separable and its off-circle roots have the form \(Ru,R^{-1}u\).
The normalized Vandermonde row subtraction for each pair bounds the sum of off-circle grid discrepancies below by \(\log|\operatorname{disc}_Y B_0|-2(s-1)m_X-2s\log s\), where \(m_X\) is the fiber mean.
For a unit-circle root the discrepancy is exactly \(\log|u^p-X^b|\), and every root has discrepancy at most \(\log2\).
The nonzero diagonal gives an integrable conditional logarithm, while the leading coefficient and the bounded fiber polynomial control the independent mean.
The sum of the nonnegative discrepancies from the upper bound is therefore integrable, including its neutral-root subsum.
These facts give both inequalities with the specialized measure \(m(P)\).
For a discarded character factor every fiber root is neutral, and equality of its independent and diagonal measures at zero makes its integrated sum of discrepancies vanish.
Its contribution to the loss is exactly its width times \(\log2\), counted once for each occurrence.
Thus the loss identity continues to hold when the full discriminant vanishes from repeated factors.
Since \(s\log s=o(p)\), the coefficient of \(m(B)\) tends to one through positive values and its lower bound \(\eta_k\) proves the stated lower limit.

For the general reciprocal \(H\), inversion permutes primitive factors of a given multiplicity, so each squarefree layer has self-inversive generic fibers even when its factors are individually exchanged.
The row content has equal independent and diagonal measures and contributes no generic fiber roots.
Adding the squarefree comparisons with weights \(j\) accounts for all widths and all losses with their true multiplicities.
Nonnegativity of the integral Mahler measures and of \(m(\Delta_j)\), together with \(w_j\le w\) and \(\sum j w_j=w\), yields the weakened lower estimate and the additive upper estimate.
This review covers the conditional conclusions for the stated representations and the general squarefree-layer comparison.
The existence of suitable representations for arbitrary shrinking sequences remains open.
