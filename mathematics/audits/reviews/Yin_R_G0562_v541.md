## Outcome of the all-exponent and congruence review

The all-integer limit, the eventually exact obstruction for an original list, and the simultaneous congruences hold as stated for either sign.
The quantifier for existence fixes a finite list before taking the exponent large.

**Norm and collisions.**
The compositum of two degree-\(d\) conjugate fields has degree at most \(d^2\), giving \(\varphi(k)\le d^2\) for each torsion order.
The prime-power inequality in the submission then bounds \(k\le2d^4\).
If two \(M\)-th powers of conjugates have torsion ratio, the original ratio is torsion, so their \(M\)-th powers actually coincide.
The distinct conjugates of \(z\) therefore retain their number under every power, giving \(\mathbb Q(z^k)=F\).
In particular \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\) for every \(k\), and equality occurs at \(k=M\).
The relative norm satisfies \(\delta^M=z^s\), and \(z^{sk}\) generates \(F\) inside \(\mathbb Q(\delta^k)\subseteq F\).
Thus its absolute height is multiplied by \(s\) while the degree is divided by \(s\), preserving precisely the total measure also for nonintegral inputs.
Inversion of a reciprocal root acts on its generated field and on \(F\), and commutes with forming the relative norm, giving the reciprocal assertion.
For two such norm representatives a collision of powered conjugates forces equality of the two primitive powered measures, which are the same positive exponent times two distinct original values.
This rules out cross-seed torsion even when the two degrees agree.

**Original lists and the limit.**
On a qualifying list the common rational recovery map is defined at every powered conjugate and recovers each original root.
Two distinct original roots therefore cannot collide.
Conversely, avoiding all listed torsion orders makes the powers injective on the union of conjugates, so all powered minima keep their degrees and remain pairwise distinct.
For \(n\ge2\) the two exponent classes in the graph are disjoint, its derivative polynomial has nonzero least term, and power substitution is unramified at the prescribed nonzero roots.
Thus the exact derivative test, the finite coefficient search and the pointwise product estimate work for composite exponents as well.
In the direct product of the fixed seed fields the estimate uses only their degrees, the sum of their measures and the exponent bound \(n(b_0+1)\).
With fixed \(K\), the denominator correction costs \(O_D(t^2/l)=o(1)\) in the exponent divided by \(n\).
Both rows occur because a single row would already contain the product of the powered minima to order \(t\) and cost at least \(ntS\) in log norm.
The derivative product charges each gcd order \(c_i\) at \(h_i\), and yields \(\sum c_i h_i\le S+o(1)\), so the fixed choice of \(K\) preserves every target order after division.
The two signed projections have at most one forbidden slope per seed, and the logarithmic cost \(O(b_0+\log(1+s))\) for \(s\) fixed seeds is \(o(n)\).
The fixed slack \(S<B\) proves eventual realization on every admissible exponent.
Applying the norm construction to each distinct value removes all forbidden exponents for that choice of representatives.
The zero cardinality and unbounded cardinalities then give the stated subsequence equivalences by the pointwise upper bound and the fixed-budget gap dichotomy.

**Local multiplicities and simultaneous tests.**
For \(q\mid n\), the reduced gcd is taken before the separable substitution \(X^m\).
At a zero of \(V(X^m)\) the coprimality of \(U,V\) forces \(U(X^m)\ne0\), so the displayed \(R\) is coprime to its derivative also in characteristic two.
At a nonzero node the substituted gcd contributes exactly \(Q\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^m}J\), and the squarefree remainder supplies at most one further order even when both factors vanish there.
At a shared image the strongest individual bound is the maximum over the fiber, and Frobenius preserves this maximum.
For a binary field element of degree \(s\), powers of the Frobenius rotate its orbit of length \(s\).
The binary excess therefore occurs in a single class modulo \(s\) precisely when the indicated ratio is in that orbit.
In the example the seventh roots have degree three, and \(X^{2^j}+X\) has simple roots including them exactly for \(3\mid j\).

For every monic \(T\) coprime to the row, the same nonzero integral determinant is used at all primes.
The dimension of its reduced kernel is \(\deg\gcd(\bar T,\bar A)\), even for a nonsquarefree test polynomial.
This dominates \(\deg\gcd(\bar T,D_q)\), and the distinct prime powers divide that determinant jointly.
The root product gives the asserted upper bound including repetitions in \(T\).
Removing a common cyclotomic product leaves both row values nonzero at each qualifying node and preserves every original contact multiplicity, so the reduced width bounds its total degree including multiplicities.
For \(n=j!\), each indicated residue order divides \(j!\) and is coprime to \(q\), hence divides \(m\).
The maximum prime power removed from \(j!\) obeys \(Q\le q^{j/(q-1)}\le2^j\).
At the common image one the order is consequently at least \(k_j\) on both rows for every prime in \(C_{j,D}\).
The translated ordinary coefficients are integral Taylor coefficients, so the distinct primes divide each of the claimed coefficients jointly.
For fixed \(D\), every fixed prime eventually enters \(C_{j,D}\), while the superexponential growth of \((j!)^{1/8}\) relative to \(2^j\) sends \(k_j\) to infinity.
