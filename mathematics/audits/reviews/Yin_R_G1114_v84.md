## Whole argument, both offset regimes and both degree parities

At 2026-11-16T17:33 UTC a separately assigned reviewer for Yin inspected the complete fixed [L462v87 argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#statement) and the dependency interfaces listed below.
The reviewer developed the earlier [two cubic special cases](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v81.md#cubic), which use some of the same underlying trace and Fourier estimates.
The reviewer had no part in developing L462v87 and received the fixed text without its development dialogue, an author explanation or an earlier review conclusion about this proof.
The cubic classification and its finite tests are not steps in the fixed text's proof for a general primitive pair.
The absolute upper bound for \(q\) and positive lower bound for \(\theta-q\) hold throughout the stated branch-two family.

**Fourier coefficients and support.**
On an extracted subsequence with \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\), each nonzero fixed lattice vector eventually avoids the kernel of \((r,s)\mapsto rd+se\).
Simultaneous sign extraction on the countable lattice gives an order compatible with translation, and all fixed shifts of a positive mixed harmonic eventually lie between zero and \(h\).
The zeroth Taylor coefficient is one, whereas the doubled zeroth moment is two.
This gives precisely \(J+J^*=2\widehat\mu\) on the base and the integral transform \(A^*B+AB^*\) there.
On the other layers, expansion of \(A=2+ax+by\) includes the three doubled positive moments with the required factors.
Reflection supplies the opposite layers.
Sanders's locally compact result applies both to the positive weighted joint measure and to each complex projected row with integer Fourier transform.
On a discrete lattice every subgroup is open, and the inverse transform of its translated indicator is a character times Haar probability on its annihilator.
The component group of that annihilator is finite by the integer normal form for a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^k\).
A singleton on the Fourier side is the only kind of coset giving full-dimensional Haar density, so that density is an integer Laurent polynomial even for the complex rows.
After combining coincident connected components, two different components have mutually singular Haar measures, including when one is contained in the other.
Positivity thus applies separately on each component of the joint measure.

**Extinction.**
A proper component projecting onto the full two-dimensional base has codimension one, and its annihilating relation has nonzero coefficient on the fiber.
On powers divisible by every \(m\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\) its fiber harmonic is exactly the base character of its slope times that power.
Dividing by \(|A|^2\) leaves integrable projected densities because the weighted pieces are positive and arise by restriction of the probability away from one base point.
The extra fiber there projects to a point.
Distinct slopes separate to infinity in at least one Fourier coordinate.
After shifting by one slope, the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma kills the other terms, so every coefficient of \(A f_j\) is a limit of integers, hence integral and eventually zero at infinity.
The reality identity with \(A^*\) and coprimality of the primitive nonassociate Laurent factors \(A,A^*\) give an integral Laurent quotient \(f_j\).
Its nonnegative mean, if positive, must exhaust the probability.
An absolutely continuous probability has strict modulus less than one at each nontrivial character, so integrality would force the excluded Haar marginal.
This removes every surface projecting onto the full base.
The full-torus polynomial density has a finite Fourier range in the fiber, unaffected by division by a base function.
At the greatest surviving positive row \(k\), all conditional moments of positive orders \(2k,3k,\ldots\) vanish simultaneously almost everywhere on the part with positive base density.
The pushforward under the \(k\)-th power consequently has density \(1+2\Re(c_k z^{\pm1})\), forcing \(|c_k|\le1/2\).
The Mahler-measure estimate is strict even when the base density integrates to one, since equality in logarithmic Jensen would then make it identically one.
Together with \(M(A)=2\) this excludes the last row of any nonempty finite list.

**Offset scales and cutoffs.**
The one finite family of annihilators for the marginal is independent of the intermediate indices and of the further joint limits over it.
Each of its factors therefore annihilates its respective singular component, with either sign of the corresponding exponent.
The product kills the entire mixed row in the limit, and the resulting finite-input coefficient is an integer.
Failure of every fixed middle interval would give a subsequence with nonzero coefficient, \(t/e\to\infty\), \(t/h\to0\), contradicting this limit argument.
The same diagonal test applies to the first-offset interval when \(e/d\to\infty\) and to the fixed-pair interval after replacing \(e\) by \(g\).
The resulting constants can depend on the extracted sequence since each case is used to contradict existence of that sequence.

In the separated-offset case the rotated first marginal has the stated nine possibilities.
Its nonzero continuous part follows from the tabulated positive real part of its reciprocal on an arc.
The atomic contribution is periodic on both Fourier tails, while the continuous contribution decays.
The coefficient at the second offset consequently makes \(b\) times the atomic mass integral, forcing that mass to be zero.
The remaining density bound gives double vanishing of \((2-w)H-bV\) at every zero of \(V\).
First \(V\mid H\), and then \(2-\zeta\) must divide the unit \(b\) at each such root.
Its norm three at \(-1\) and at the primitive sixth roots excludes all but \(V=1,1-w\).
The two displayed model functions have positive real part and give respectively the integer products \(1,XY\).
The single-offset row proof applies at each fixed \(kt+ld\) inside the first prefix when \(d/t,t/e\to0\), and absence of atoms gives zero throughout the resulting intermediate interval.

The local reciprocal coefficient estimate sums at most \(1+\lfloor C\rfloor\) geometric-series terms of \(\ell^1\) norm at most two on \([0,Ce]\).
For bounded \(e/d\), the separated-offset support estimate or the \(e-d=u,2u\) initial models give the required absolute coefficient sums, including \(p=2(e-d)\le4u\) in the latter case.
For unbounded \(e/d\), the initial bound for \(J_i\) controls the signed Gram operator by uniform row sums over a block of diameter \(e/L_1\).
On any fixed \(K\) characters the positive-part correction in operator norm is at most \(K\) times the vanishing negative mass.
With the active multiplier fixed between offsets, \(K\) nonzero integer coefficients therefore violate Bessel for a single sufficiently large fixed \(K\).
Splitting at the finitely many offsets controls every block in location as well as the two ends.

In the first comparison, a shift of the representing sequence for \(1/S_i\) costs only the constant and the sum of the absolute values of its initial coefficients through that shift.
For \(WJ_i\), after the correction at indices \(0,\ldots,p\), the negative index \(-r\) has coefficient \(-[z^{r+p}]WJ_i\).
Multiplying by all shifts in the finite product \(U_i\) incurs bounded total correction by the local estimates for \((WB^\sharp)/S_i\), since both \(r\) and \(p\) are \(O(e)\) in this use.
The two-cutoff argument thus has a zero prefix comparable to \(h\), uniform variation on positive moments, bounded circle multipliers and a polynomial of degree \(O(h)\).
Writing \(X=az^d,Y=bz^e\) gives \(|W|\le2|X-Y|\) and \(4-|X+Y|^2\le4|2+X+Y|\).
These inequalities supply exactly the extra two copies of \(W\) in the integer polynomial whose \(L^1\) norm is bounded.

**Fixed primitive pair.**
For \(P=EF\) all factors with constant one have every root on the circle and are cyclotomic.
Irreducibility and reversal exclude a circle root of the single constant-two factor with unit leading coefficient.
The possible extra base fibers after division by \(|P|^2\) hence lie over finitely many torsion points.
The slope separation works over one base coordinate.
For \(H=P f_j\), the reality of the rational function \(H/P\) pairs any nonzero finite pole with its reciprocal conjugate.
Exterior poles would force interior ones, and a genuine circle pole has nonintegrable absolute value even when it is simple.
Clearing a monomial and dividing by the unit leading coefficient proves the Laurent quotient integral.
The previous extinction argument now uses \(M(P)=2\) and leaves only torsion atoms.
For \(g\to\infty\), the earlier integral offset, if present, samples an integral coefficient strictly inside the first fractional offset \(D_0\) at all fixed shifted seam tests.
Its contribution drops out modulo one, and at \(r=0\) the first fractional multiplier uses the Taylor constant one.
These are the seam hypotheses forcing the Haar first marginal, a zero initial block of length \(\kappa g\), and bounded local support by the same positive-part Bessel argument.
Bounded \(g\) needs only bounded coefficients over a bounded initial segment.
In the second comparison \(2/F\) has absolutely summable Taylor coefficients, its circle minimum denominator is positive, and each shift in \(E(z^g)(1-z^{Tg})\) costs a bounded initial coefficient sum of \(J_i\).
The same cutoff then bounds the circle \(L^1\) norm after retaining \(E(z^g)\) in the integer polynomial.
Both comparisons produce a nonzero cyclotomic multiple of \(B^\sharp\) of bounded support, whose measure remains strictly greater than one and tends to one.
The fixed-support gap excludes both sequences.
The trace factorization removes only an optional cyclotomic factor at \(-1\) in odd degree, so this return applies to both parities.
The fixed-branch derivative bound gives \(\theta-q\ge2/(q+3)\) for passage from bounded \(q\) to uniform positive excess.
The review checked the source and local estimates at their stated interfaces and did not independently reprove the external Fourier–Stieltjes or fixed-support theorems.
