### Trace factors and the prime sequence

An element \(\eta\) and its inverse are the two roots of \(X^2-(\eta+\eta^{-1})X+1\), and they are distinct at order \(3q\).
Frobenius returns the trace after \(a\) steps precisely when \(2^a\equiv\pm1\bmod 3q\).
The component modulo three fixes the sign to \((-1)^a\), giving least return time \(f=\operatorname{ord}_q(-2)\).
Each primitive inverse pair has a unique exponent representative congruent to one modulo three relative to a fixed primitive \(3q\)-th root.
In this representation squaring acts on its nonzero exponent modulo \(q\) by multiplication by \(-2\), proving the coset description.
The \(q-1\) traces give \(h\) orbits, and each orbit on traces lifts to one orbit of length \(2f\) or two inverse orbits of length \(f\).
Which case occurs is fixed by the sign of \(2^f\bmod 3q\), so the top orbit count is \(h\) or \(2h\).

Over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\bar P_l=(X^{3q}-1)/(X^q-1)\) is separable and contains every primitive \(3q\)-th root.
The inverse pairs over one trace orbit give a monic binary divisor of degree \(2f\) with coprime complement.
Its unique monic Hensel lift equals its reversed polynomial normalized by the constant term, by reciprocity of the parent and uniqueness.
That constant term squares to one and cannot be \(-1\), since an anti-reciprocal lift would vanish at one whereas \(P_l(1)=-1\).
Pairing the coefficients of its reciprocal Laurent form then expresses it as \(X^fT(X+X^{-1})\), using that \(X^a+X^{-a}\) is a monic integral polynomial of degree \(a\) in \(X+X^{-1}\) for \(a\ge1\).
Thus the trace lift is monic and reduces to the specified irreducible trace polynomial.

For each fixed \(H\), an integral primitive element of the splitting field gives a nonconstant monic polynomial \(A\), translated so \(A(0)=a\ne0\).
The identity \(A(aMz)/a\equiv1\bmod M\) and the unbounded absolute values give a prime outside any finite list at which \(A\) has a root.
Away from the fixed denominators, evaluation at this root maps the localized ring generated by the primitive element to \(\mathbb F_q\) and sends the full factorization of \(U^H+2\) to linear factors.
Excluding primes dividing its discriminant or \(2\) ensures distinct nonzero roots.
Their ratios to one of them supply \(H\) distinct \(H\)-th roots of unity, and \(-2\) lies in the subgroup of \(H\)-th powers of \(\mathbb F_q^*\).
This subgroup has index \(H\), yielding \(h\ge H\) for arbitrarily large primes and then a sequence with \(h\to\infty\).

### The simultaneous congruences

At each prescribed offset, a lifted trace of irreducible reduction of degree \(f\) is a unit modulo \((2^{b_k},G_k)\), since \(\deg G_k<f\) and an inverse modulo two lifts across the nilpotent ideal \((2)\).
Monicity of \(G_k\) gives exactly \(2^{b_ks_k}\) elements in the quotient ring.
Hence the product of the unit groups has order at most \(J\), including the stated adjustment for an extra finite abelian group.
Any \(J\) labels contain a nonempty block with identity product by equality of two initial products.
Successively setting such blocks aside until fewer than \(J\) elements remain gives an identity product in the original pool, since the group is abelian.
This selects the same binary labels for every offset, even though their lifted coefficients depend on the offset.
The product of their distinct monic Hensel divisors is the unique lift of the product reduction, and its trace polynomial is the product of their traces.

Since \(q\le 2^f+1\) and \(h\le q-1\), one has \(2j^2<f\) eventually.
At most \(j\) rings of degree \(2j^2\) and precision \(2^j\) give \(J\le 2^{2j^4}\le h^{1/4}\).
The discarded proportion in a pool of size \(\lfloor h/j\rfloor\) is \(O(jh^{-3/4})\), and the integer part costs at most one label.
Thus the degree is \((2+o(1))fh/j=(2+o(1))q/j\), uniformly for the prescribed pools and lists.
For each \(y\in\mathbb Z_2\), exactly \(j\) of the \(2j\) factors \(y-i\) are even, so \(A(y)\) is a multiple of \(2^j\).
In \(A(y+Z)^j\), each term of degree below \(j\) must use the constant term from at least one of the \(j\) copies.
Substitution therefore sends \((2^j,A(Y)^j)\) into \((2^j,Z^j)\), proving every claimed Taylor coefficient congruence.

### Numerical bound and rationality at the offsets

The gcd degree at period \(D\) is \(d\) for \(3q\mid D\) and zero otherwise, since the selected reduction is squarefree and supported entirely on primitive \(3q\)-th roots.
For positive contributions in C345 v166 the expression is then \(d(\log2)i/(2^iD)\).
The smallest eligible \(D\) is \(3q\) and \(i/2^i\) attains its maximum \(1/2\) at \(i=1,2\), verifying the exact supremum.
It is a numerical certificate for the reduction.
As \(j=O((\log q)^{1/4})\), the ratio of \(d\) to \(q(\log\log q/\log q)^3\) tends to infinity.

For large \(h\), the two nearest odd offsets to \(q/j^3\) and \(q/j\) are distinct, lie between zero and \(q\), and are coprime to \(q\).
They fit in the prescribed list since \(j\to\infty\).
The ratios of \(d\) to \(\sqrt{qk}\) tend to infinity and zero respectively, as follows directly from \(d\sim2q/j\).
Thus the whole selected divisor at the larger offset violates the absolute rational-divisor lower bound of C345 v247.
The smaller offset passes that bound and the simultaneous trace tests.
Its rationality remains unresolved by these results.
