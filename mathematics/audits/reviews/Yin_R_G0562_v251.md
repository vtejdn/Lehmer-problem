### Uniformity in the primitive direction

Rank two makes the gcd of the projected differences positive for every nonzero direction.
A primitive parametrization has image a one-dimensional subtorus with binomial defining equation, so absolute irreducibility and rank two exclude identical vanishing of the specialization.
Multiplication by a monomial preserves the support differences and the nonzero roots.
Comparison of coefficients on the normalized support gives precisely the twists \(\zeta^{g_v}=1\) as the proportional translates.
For each remaining translate, absolute irreducibility excludes a common component and Bézout bounds the number of geometric intersection points by \(D^2\).
Integers \(r,s\) with \(rp+sq=1\) recover \(\alpha=u^rw^s\), so the full relative degree counts distinct intersection points even with negative exponents.

The eliminant is nonzero over \(L(U)\) by coprimality and Gauss's lemma, and the determinant still vanishes when evaluated at a common root, including if a leading coefficient vanishes there.
The fixed determinant expressions in roots of unity bound the coefficient sum at every complex embedding and give integral coefficients.
For a root with local absolute value greater than one, division by its power one below the actual degree bounds the leading coefficient times that absolute value by the local coefficient sum, or by the coefficient maximum at a finite place.
At the remaining places the leading coefficient already satisfies the same bound.
The product formula cancels this nonzero coefficient even when twisting lowers the degree of the eliminant.
Interchanging the two variables bounds both coordinate heights uniformly in the twisting order.

I checked Theorem 1.3 on manuscript p. 3 of the [ESI edition](../literature/AD07/readme.md#edition) and the rational-base deduction in the source unit.
The fixed base \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) has discriminant five, its compositum with \(L\) is abelian over it, and adjoining that base preserves the upper bound \(D^2\) for the relative degree.
The theorem's positive right-hand side has a positive minimum over this finite range of integer degrees, uniform in \(L\).
I used the published height theorem as an input and did not re-audit its proof.
Applying \(h(\alpha^a)=|a|h(\alpha)\) to both coordinates excludes every nonproportional translate once \(\max(|p|,|q|)\) exceeds the stated threshold.
This argument handles roots on different rational factors in exactly the same intersection.

### The intersection of fields

Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^{g_v})\) extends to one of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
If the \(j\)-th powers of two images of \(\alpha^{g_v}\) agree, the lifts have quotient of order dividing \(jg_v\).
They are nontorsion roots of the same rational specialization, so the classification makes their \(g_v\)-th powers equal.
Counting distinct conjugates gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^{jg_v})=\mathbb Q(\alpha^{g_v})\).
The latter lies in every \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\), and the member at \(j=g_v\) gives equality in the intersection after adjoining any \(K\).
The prime-block comparison is qualitative here, with the threshold allowed to depend on the whole fixed curve.

### The five-term specialization

The no-torsion argument in [C345 v243](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts) applies to this entire range of \(n\).
Indeed the reduction at two is \((X^{3n}-1)/(X^n-1)\), squarefree because \(n\) is odd.
For a torsion root, its odd part of the order is \(3t\) with \(t\mid n\), since \(3\nmid n\), and squarefreeness bounds its two-part by two.
Its \(n\)-th power therefore has order three or six.
The trace equation would force its \(l\)-th power to have order four or six, impossible since \(l\) is even.
All roots are units by the monic polynomial and constant one.

Multiplication of \(P_l(x)\) by \(x^k\) gives the displayed quadratic in \(w=x^n\) and \(u=x^k\).
The coefficients \(u-2,u\) are coprime, and the discriminant has valuation one at \(u=0\), proving irreducibility over \(\mathbb C(u)\) and then in the Laurent ring.
The stated support differences include both coordinate directions, so the projected gcd is one for every permitted pair \((k,n)\).
The curve is fixed and \(n>k\), giving one threshold for all offsets and positive powers.

For equal \(k\)-th powers the ratio \(\xi=w'/w\) has the same nontrivial odd order as \(x'/x\).
Vieta gives \(w+w'=-u/(u-2)\ne0\) and \(ww'=u(1-2u)/(u-2)\).
Their quotient \(ww'/(w+w')^2=5-2(u+u^{-1})\) is a unit over two, so multiplying by \(2+\xi+\xi^{-1}\) and reducing forces the reduction of \(\xi\) to have order three.
Odd-order roots of unity retain their order under this reduction, whence \(u+u^{-1}=2\), forcing \(x^k=1\).
For equal \(n\)-th powers the quadratic in \(u\) has leading coefficient \(-2\) and constant \(-2w^2\), so the two distinct \(k\)-th powers multiply to \(w^2\).
This forces \(x^{2(n-k)}\) to be torsion, again impossible.
Equal \(l\)-th powers give equal \(n\)-th traces, so the \(n\)-th powers agree or are inverses.
The first case was settled above and the second gives a torsion \(2n\)-th power of \(x\).
Injectivity on all distinct roots implies injectivity on each conjugacy class, giving all claimed field equalities.
The proof supplies no measure estimate for an individual factor.
