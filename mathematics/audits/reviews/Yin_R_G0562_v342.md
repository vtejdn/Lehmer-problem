### The orbit product and the logarithmic tail

The two summands of \(Q_w\) have exponents congruent to \(0\) and \(S\) modulo \(D\), respectively.
These residues differ because \(\gcd(D,S)=1\) and \(D\ge2\).
Thus the actual term count is \(N\ge2\), and every coefficient of either row has modulus at most the common maximum \(L\).
On the circle \(g(z)=z^{\deg f}\overline{f(z)}\), so multiplying both rows by any complex \(H\) preserves equality of their moduli.
The triangle inequality for the shorter row gives \(|A|\le kL\).
Outside the finite zero set of the denominator, \(\log V_w=\log|Q_w|-\log|A(x^D)|\).
Both logarithms are integrable even at circle zeros, and cancellation of \(m(H)\) with power substitution gives the asserted difference of measures.

Rotations give all \(D\)-th roots of unity as phase increments, and the product identity bounds the product above by two.
The unregularized logarithms can be integrated because their exceptional zeros form a finite set.
For the lower estimate, if the nearest grid point has distance \(t\) from \(-1\), the quotient of the full product by one smallest factor is \(\sin(Dt/2)/\sin(t/2)\).
The concavity bound for sine on \([0,\pi/2]\) and \(\sin(t/2)\le t/2\) give \(2D/\pi\) as a lower bound.
The limiting quotient is \(D\) at a zero, while at \(t=\pi/D\) either tied point may be omitted.
Clipping restores a factor at least \(\varepsilon\) and increases or preserves all remaining factors.
Averaging the logarithm over the rotations gives (2).

For the strict sublevel set \(E=\{|Q_w|<vL\}\) of positive normalized arc measure, Theorem 2.1 in the retained BS13 source unit gives
\[
 L\le\|Q_w\|_\infty\le \bigl(c/\mu(E)\bigr)^{N-1}vL .
\]
The first inequality follows by extracting the Fourier coefficient of modulus \(L\).
All \(N\) frequencies are distinct and purely imaginary, so the source exponential prefactor is one.
The zero-measure case also satisfies \(\mu(E)\le c v^{1/(N-1)}\).
At level \(t\ge0\) the event \(V_w<\varepsilon e^{-t}\) implies \(|Q_w|<kL\varepsilon e^{-t}\) almost everywhere.
Integrating this sublevel bound gives (3), including its factor \(N-1\).
The choice \(\varepsilon=k^{-1}D^{-(N-1)}\) lies in \((0,1]\) and bounds the integrated loss by \(c(N-1)/D\).
In (2) it leaves \(-[(N-2)\log D+\log(\pi k/2)]/D\), as required even for \(N=2\).
Since \(1\le k\le N/2\), all errors vanish under \(N\log D=o(D)\) independently of the offset and coefficient sizes.
An unreduced pair has the same measures by the circle map \(x\mapsto x^{\gcd(d,s)}\), with (1) applying when its reduced denominator is at least two.
The external Turán–Nazarov and Smyth theorems were used at their inspected statements and hypotheses in the repository.
Their original proofs were outside this review.

### Shrinking sequences and multinacci seeds

For each nonconstant seed in (4), reciprocity up to sign would make the seed and its reversal have a common nonconstant divisor.
The whole-polynomial threshold from v337 therefore gives \(m(f_i)\ge\log\theta\), including reducible seeds.
The minimum defining \(\tau\) is attained by well-ordering of the positive integer term counts.
On any subsequence with \(\tau(f_i)\log D_i/D_i\) bounded, use attaining multipliers and \(k_i\le N_i/2\) to obtain \(N_i/D_i=O(1/\log D_i)\) and \(\log(\pi k_i/2)/D_i=o(1)\).
Rearranging (1) gives \(\tau(f_i)\log D_i/D_i\ge\log\theta-m(P_{w_i})-o(1)\) on that subsequence.
This proves (4), including the case of infinite lower limit.
For the v341 coprime integral two-residue input with subplastic measure, reversal exchanges the two rows, and \(s=d(n-a)+r\) has \(\gcd(s,d)=1\).
This puts the nonconstant row precisely in the setting of (4).
Constant unit rows yield the signed binomials already distinguished in v341.
The obstruction leaves open the case of sufficiently large \(\tau\) and the original unrestricted problem.

For the multinacci seed the two products by \(1-z\) have respectively the powers \(0,n,n+1\) and \(0,1,n+1\), all distinct for \(n\ge2\).
Their coefficients agree with the displayed trinomials, giving \(N=6,\ k=3\) uniformly.
The seed has one expanding Pisot root \(\theta_n\to2\) and is monic, so its logarithmic measure is \(\log\theta_n\).
Substitution in (1) proves the uniform \(O(\log D/D)\) error and the joint limit, also for \(S/D\to\infty\).
For the earlier v31 estimate, \(1-x^D\) is reciprocal up to sign with nonzero constant and multiplicative measure one.
The seeds \(f_n(x^D)\) and \(g_n(x^D)\) remain nonproportional, have bounded measure, and the clearing has three terms in each resulting row.
Taking \(j=D,\ k=-S\) and sign \(-\sigma\) in v31 produces the cyclic norm polynomial at \(x^D\), whose measure in logarithmic units is \(D\,m(P_\sigma)\).
Thus v31 already gives qualitative convergence for these signs.
The present estimate also controls every complex phase of modulus one and bounds the actual deficit left open by the disk inequality in v341.
