For \(r\mid q\) or \(r\mid q+1\), one works in the same degree-\(d\) power order for \(T\): its additive index in the order of \(\beta\) is \(2q+1\), prime to either modulus.
In the larger order the graph identity gives \(\beta^{v-u}=1\bmod q\) and \(\beta^{v+u}=1\bmod(q+1)\).

This uses divisibility, which holds even at the finitely many small heights where the full-order identities are not asserted.
For \(r\mid 2q+1\), the odd- and even-\(v\) calculations both identify the characteristic polynomial of the same \(T\) over the composite modulus as \((Z+1)^2D\) with \(D\mid Z^v-1\).
The evidence is the actual characteristic polynomial decomposition in [the odd proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v895.md#trace) and [the even proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v897.md#even).
Removing two occurrences above \(-1\) accordingly leaves a divisor with the claimed multiplicities even if there are further occurrences above that residue.

From the degree estimates one has \(u,q,r\ll(\log(3d))^3\) and \(m\ll d(\log(3d))^{C}\) for an absolute exponent.
For the lower bound, \(v\log\beta=\log Y=d\log(q+1)+O(\sqrt{d\log(2d)\log(2q+1)})\); the error is \(o(d)\) uniformly in the allowed heights.
Since \(\log\beta\le\log T<1/q\) and \(q\log(q+1)\ge2\log3>1\), subtraction of the polylogarithmic \(u\) still leaves \(v-u>d\) in sufficiently large degree.
Thus every prime \(\lambda\mid R\) exceeds \(r\), and at most \(O_\kappa(1)\) different primes enter \(R\).
Writing \(m=r^\nu m'\) with \(r\nmid m'\), every occurrence missing the full \(\lambda\)-part of the order is a root of \(Z^{m'/\lambda}-1\), with at most \(r^\nu\) copies in \(D\) or in the binomial divisor \(F\) at the other coefficients.
This gives the count \(m/\lambda\) even when \(r\mid m\).

Removal is Frobenius invariant as a removal of multisets.
Every remaining residue has order divisible by \(R\); its Frobenius degree, hence also its field degree over the local base, is a multiple of \(f\).
Their total multiplicity is \(tf=d-z\) with positive integral \(t\).
The convention at \(R=1\) is compatible with this count.

With \(b=m/R\) and \(j_0=\varphi(R)/f\), one has \(bj_0\le m/f\ll t(\log(3d))^C\).
The elementary totient product gives \(0\le m-b\varphi(R)\le m\sum_{\lambda\mid R}1/\lambda=\Delta\), summing over prime divisors.
Expanding \(tm-bj_0d=t(m-b\varphi(R))-bj_0z\) gives the claimed upper estimate.
For the binary period \(A\), the parity-specific exact identity \(kA=v-\epsilon u\) supplies a single \(k<2\log_2(2q+1)\); each of the three choices of \(m\) is within \(2u\) of this same integer.
The extra term \(2tu\) lies within the error already proved since \(0<\kappa<1\).
If \(t\) stayed under the proposed cutoff, take \(\eta=\kappa/4\) and choose the positive cutoff constant sufficiently small for both \(t\le d^{\kappa/4}\) and \(kt\le\exp(c_\eta\log d/\log^2(2q+1))\).

The upper error is then \(O_\kappa(d^{1-3\kappa/4}(\log(3d))^{C'})=o(d^{1-\eta})\), violating the repulsion at the integer multiplier \(kt\) and target \(bj_0\).
This proves the strict upper bound on \(f\); the reasoning bounds this divisor of the local degrees.
For \(R=m\), the retained residues are precisely primitive roots outside the reserved occurrences and each distinct orbit occurs once, since \(r\nmid m\).

Therefore the same \(t\) counts the asserted distinct irreducible factors in this case.

Finally take \(m=hP\) for fixed \(h\) and prime \(P\) with \((P-1)/j=s\) also prime for fixed \(j\).

The local prime \(r\le 2q+1\) satisfies \(1<r^j<P\) uniformly at large degrees.
Its order modulo \(P\) cannot divide \(j\) and must divide \(js\).
Since \(s>j\), this order is divisible by \(s\), hence at least a positive fixed multiple of \(d\).
The previous upper bound is \(o(d)\) uniformly in \(q\), ruling this out for all three choices.
This proof excludes the stated exponent families without giving a logarithmic upper bound on individual local degrees.
