## Prescribing the part of the order supported on the primes of \(m\)

On 2026-11-16T17:06 a separately assigned agent for Yin independently checked the [statement](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v85.md#supply) and both parts of its proof.
The reviewer took no part in developing the argument and received its fixed location without development discussion, author explanations or earlier review conclusions.
The review found the supply assertion valid for every Salem number, every \(m\ge3\), every finite exclusion set and every \(H\ge2\).

Since \(K/F\) has the involution \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\), reciprocity of \(\Phi_m\) puts the Laurent value \(c=A(\alpha)\) in \(\mathcal O_F\).
It is nonzero because \(\alpha>1\).
Reduction modulo \(bc\mathcal O_K\) works for both positive and negative powers of the unit \(\alpha\).
Consequently \(E_r-1\) is in \(b\mathcal O_K\cap F=b\mathcal O_F\), even when a prime of \(b\) already divides \(c\).
The determinant of multiplication by \(E_r\) on an integral basis of \(F\) is therefore \(1\bmod b\).
When it is negative, its absolute value is \(-1\bmod m\), unequal to \(1\bmod m\), and has no prime factor in the enlarged exclusion set.

At every \(\mathfrak q\mid E_r\) the residue characteristic is prime to \(m\) and \(c\) is a unit.
Thus \(\Phi_m(\bar\alpha^r)=0\) gives exact order \(m\), and the relative quadratic \(X^2-(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})X+1\) has distinct reductions of its roots since \(m>2\).
Its reduction is separable, so the completion splits or is unramified quadratic according to the two Frobenius permutations.
These permutations give \(N\mathfrak q\equiv 1,-1\bmod m\), respectively.
The negative sign in the product of prime-ideal norms forces odd total valuation over the inert factors.
For the residue order \(j\), the equality \(j/\gcd(j,r)=m\) gives \(j=mt\) with \(t=\gcd(j,r)\mid r\).
Since \(r\equiv1\bmod L\), the prescribed primes and the prime divisors of \(m\) do not divide \(t\).
If \(t=1\), then \(\Phi_m(\bar\alpha)=0\), violating the exclusion of primes dividing \(N(c)\).

For \(m=2^k,\ k\ge2\), an inert factor has odd absolute residue degree \(f\) by reduction modulo four.
Odd exponentiation is an automorphism of the unit group modulo \(2^k\), so its characteristic \(\ell\) is itself \(-1\bmod 2^k\).
Grouping the inert valuations by \(\ell\) leaves an odd sum for at least one such characteristic.
Each inert valuation in that group has odd weight \(f\) in the rational norm.
Every split factor above the same \(\ell\) which divides \(E_r\) has even residue degree, since its prime-ideal norm is \(1\bmod 2^k\).
Thus at least one of the required characteristics has odd valuation in the rational absolute norm as claimed.

To check independence of the circle angles, send a chosen representative \(u_j\) to \(\alpha\) by a normal-closure automorphism.
Its inverse is the unique root sent to \(\alpha^{-1}\), and it was omitted from the representatives.
Taking absolute values of any torsion relation yields \(a_j=0\) for every \(j\).
This makes every nontrivial Fourier character average to zero along \(r=1+Ln\), including after enlarging \(L\).
The exterior value of \(A\) is positive and the simple circle zeros of the real Laurent function give open arcs of each sign.
The product sign therefore has both values on open sets, with a null boundary, and division by \(N(c)\) preserves positive relative density of the negative sign.
For a power of two the centered cyclotomic polynomial is \(2\cos(2\pi(m/4)x)\), so either sign has measure one half in any one coordinate and the product has the same distribution.
These densities concern multipliers in the specified progression.
Infinitude of distinct rational characteristics follows by enlarging the exclusion set.

The progression imposes no upper degree window on the residue order.
Even with \(m,H\) and the initial exclusions fixed, the prescribed modulus can grow at fixed Salem degree.
Indeed the supply assertion for a single \(\alpha\) gives unbounded characteristics \(\ell\) at which \(\beta=\alpha^t\) has order exactly \(m\).
These powers have the same degree as \(\alpha\), since \(\alpha\) is its only conjugate of its absolute value.
At the chosen prime the valuation of \(\beta^m-1\) equals that of \(A(\beta)\), since the other cyclotomic divisors are units there.
The construction for \(\beta\) now puts \(\ell\) in \(b\), so \(\beta^L-1\in bA(\beta)\mathcal O_K\) forces \(\ell\mid L\) by expansion of the power \(L/m\) at \(\beta^m\).
Every positive multiplier giving negative norm is greater than one, so the least one in this progression is at least \(L+1\).
