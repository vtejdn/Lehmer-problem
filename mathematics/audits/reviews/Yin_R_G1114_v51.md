# Review of the finite exclusion for the leading-two Chebyshev path

<a id="finite-prefix-review"></a>
## Exact exclusion for \(b=1\) and \(74\le h\le90\)

On 2026-11-16T14:38 UTC an agent outside the development of [L462v53](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v53.md#exclusion) and [J162](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0162/README.md#prefix-certificate) independently reviewed the mathematical reduction and the complete enumeration for Yin.
The assignment supplied the fixed inputs and the requested checks without proof-development discussion or a prior verdict.
I inspected the program, reproduced both documented runs with Python 3.14.3 and compared its small-degree counts with a separate direct determinant calculation.
The evidence establishes the exclusion at the unit divisor for each of the seventeen stated degrees.

### Endpoint signs, coefficient bounds and positivity

At \(x=2,-2\) the numerator has values \(h+2,(-1)^h(3h+2)\), since \(U_l(\pm1)=(\pm1)^l(l+1)\).
Thus \(b=1\) fixes \(q=h+3\) and \(B(2)=-1\).
The [inverse test](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#endpoints) requires \(F(-2)=2B(-2)/C_h(-2)\le-2/(q+1)\), including the equality face.
For even \(h\) this reads \((q+1)B(-2)\le-(3h+2)\), and for odd \(h\) it reads \((q+1)B(-2)\ge3h+2\).
Since \(B^\sharp(-1)=(-1)^mB(-2)\), pairing the coefficients of the reciprocal polynomial gives exactly the alternating sum in the program's endpoint test, including the central term with positive sign.
The sum at \(1\) has twice each coefficient before the center and once the central term.

The \(h\) sign changes of \(C_h\) between the endpoint \(2\) and the nodes \(2\cos(k\pi/(h+1))\) give \(h\) simple interior zeros.
Every successful inverse has strictly positive weights \(w_i\), so the [signed reciprocal measure](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) has positive mass at the \(2h\) distinct points above them.
Its mass at \(1\) is \(-1/(2(q-1))\), its mass at \(-1\) is at least \(1/(2(q+1))\), and its total mass is one.
Adding \(c\delta_1-d\delta_{-1}\) as in the ledger makes both endpoint masses nonnegative for every \(q\) in the selected interval.
The \(2h\) strictly positive interior atoms therefore make each moment matrix through index \(2h-1\) positive definite, since a polynomial of that degree cannot vanish at them all.

Through index \(2h\) the [path identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#path) gives \(j_k=b_k+j_{k-1}/2\), with \(j_0=b_0=1\).
For \(k\ge2\) this gives \(b_k=\int(2v^k-v^{k-1})\,d\omega_q\).
The total variation is \(1+1/(q-1)\), bounding \(|b_k|\) by \(3(1+1/(q-1))<4\) for \(q\ge6\).
At index one the constant term of the Herglotz kernel has a different normalization, giving \(b_1=2\int v\,d\omega_q-1/2\), again of absolute value less than four.
Consequently the entire integer search interval \([-3,3]\) is sufficient at every index used.

### Determinant state and complete enumeration

For the scaled moments \(y_l=Ls_l\), consider \(T_{n+2}\) with the outer two indices removed.
Their coupling vectors to the middle matrix \(T_n\) are \(u=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)^{\mathsf T}\) and its reversal \(u^{\rm rev}\).
Reversal symmetry gives both diagonal Schur complements \(D_{n+1}/D_n\), and the off-diagonal complement is \(y_{n+1}-p^{\mathsf T}u^{\rm rev}/D_n=X/D_n\).
Hence positive definiteness is equivalent to \(|X|<D_{n+1}\) and the next determinant is \((D_{n+1}^2-X^2)/D_n\).
Solving \(T_{n+1}\) against the extended coupling vector gives final coordinate \(X/D_{n+1}\) and first \(n\) coordinates \((p-(X/D_{n+1})p^{\rm rev})/D_n\), because \(T_n^{-1}\) commutes with reversal.
Multiplication by \(D_{n+1}\) gives exactly the next predictor stored in [the implementation](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0162/prefix.py).

In the code \(k=n+1\), the stored series coefficient is \(2^n j_n\), and the paired list entry at index \(k-i\) is \(2^{k-i}y_{k-i}\).
Shifting that entry left by \(i\) gives \(2^ky_{k-i}\) in the predictor sum.
The constant part of \(2^ky_k\) is \(L(2^n j_n)/2+2^kL(c-(-1)^kd)\), and adjoining \(b_k\) adds \(2^kLb_k/2\).
Consequently \(X=o+ab_k\) with the implemented offset \(o\) and positive step \(a=D_nL/2\).
The program requires zero remainder in the offset, determinant and predictor divisions and starts with \(D_0=1,D_1=y_0>0\).
On the completed runs these divisions were exact at every visited state.
For integral \(X,o,a,D_{n+1}\) and \(a>0\), the integer bounds are precisely
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{-D_{n+1}-o}{a}\right\rfloor+1
 \ \le b_k\le\
 \left\lfloor\frac{D_{n+1}-1-o}{a}\right\rfloor .
\]
Python floor division implements these bounds for negative numerators as well, and the loop includes both endpoints of the intersection with \([-3,3]\).

The free traversal opens the branch for \(h=n-1\) at \(n=m=h+1\) after the moment test and both endpoint tests, before it can return at the last free index.
For each later \(k\) it intersects the same interval with the singleton \(b_{2m-k}\), which already belongs to the prefix.
The first element of the reflected count list records index \(m\), and a list of length \(r\) ending before the terminal index gives first empty index \(m+r\).
At the terminal index \(2h-1\) the tested matrix has size \(2h\), exactly within the strict positivity argument.
Only failure of a necessary condition removes a candidate.

### Reproduction and independent small-degree control

The default run at \(h=74,q=77\) gave 1,461 moment-positive prefixes at index 75 and 64 endpoint matches.
I compared all reflected counts with the published sequence, including four at index 98 and the first empty level at 99.
The common-correction run reproduced every free count, endpoint count and first empty index in the [seventeen-row certificate](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0162/README.md#prefix-certificate).
In degree order the first empty indices were \(99,101,102,103,105,106,107,109,110,111,113,114,115,117,118,119,121\).
Each is within its corresponding bound \(2h-1\), and every branch reported zero terminal survivors.

For a separate exact control I computed \(j_k=\sum_{i=0}^k b_i2^{i-k}\) directly with rational arithmetic.
I tried all seven integers at each free extension and tested the full Toeplitz determinant by fraction-free Gaussian elimination after clearing denominators, retaining earlier positive leading minors.
I formed each complete reciprocal array at its center, evaluated both endpoint sums directly and checked the successive reflected determinants in the same way.
At \(h=3,4,5\) the numbers of free prefixes through the center were \(44,74,105\) and the reflected lists were respectively \((10,7),(9,8,6),(23,14,7,5)\).
Every intermediate free and reflected count matched J162 for these three degrees, independently testing both endpoint parities and the interpretation of nonempty terminal levels.
The small-degree control compares implementations, while the two reproduced J162 runs give the stated finite exclusion.
