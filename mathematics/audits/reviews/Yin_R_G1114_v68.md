# Review of primitive resultant estimates with exceptions only for circle roots

<a id="circle-root-review"></a>
## Radial separation, the exceptional count and full resultants

At 2026-11-16T16:15 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin inspected the whole [circle-root estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v73.md#circle), its sharper bound for nonreciprocal irreducible factors and its full-resultant consequence.
The reviewer had used the earlier linear-window estimates as inputs in other work but did not develop this improvement and received the fixed result without development dialogue, author explanations or a previous verdict.
The claims pass uniformly at each fixed \(A>0\) for \(d\le AY\), with the stated count of distinct circle roots and the specified hypothesis \(s=o(Y)\) for the full lower estimate.

For each primitive irreducible factor \(F\ne z\), completion by the distinct factors among \(F,F^*\) gives a squarefree integer polynomial \(T\) of degree \(n\le2\deg F\), with \(m(T)\le2m(F)\).
The roots are closed under conjugation and \(\alpha\mapsto1/\bar\alpha\).
For off-circle \(\alpha\) the latter root is distinct, both reflect to \(w_\alpha\), and their normalized separation is exactly \(1-r_\alpha^2\).
Each \(\alpha\) supplies its own ordered pair, so the radial difference \(H_t(r_\alpha)\) occurs in a distinct entry of the off-diagonal sum.
All remaining entries have lower bound \(\tfrac12\log t\) by the [reflected distance and smoothing inequalities](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#energy).
The discriminant gives \(-\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}\log\delta_{\alpha\beta}=2(n-1)m(T)-\log|\operatorname{disc}T|\), including the leading coefficient.
Nonnegative squared Fourier energy gives \(\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}\log|1-tw_\alpha\bar w_\beta|\le-\sum_\alpha\log(1-tr_\alpha^2)\le n\log(1/(1-t))\).
The resulting estimate on \(T\) bounds its subsum on \(F\) since the radial differences are nonnegative.
Summation with factor multiplicity costs \(O_A(d(m+L))\), using \(m(F)\le m\), \(\sum \deg F\le d\) with these multiplicities, and \(d^2|\log t|=O_A(1)\).
Zero roots have zero radial difference.

For nonreciprocal irreducible \(F\ne z\), the resultant with \(F^*\) is a nonzero integer and there are no circle roots.
For a pair of roots on opposite sides, the normalized modulus \(|1-\alpha\bar\beta|/(A_\alpha A_\beta)\) equals \(|w_\alpha-w_\beta|\le|1-w_\alpha\bar w_\beta|\).
For roots on the same side it equals the last modulus.
Multiplication over all ordered pairs gives the sharper energy inequality because the normalized product on the left is \(|\operatorname{Res}(F,F^*)|\exp(-2D m(F))\), with \(D=\deg F\).
Subtracting the nonnegative smoothed energy and using the lower bound \(\tfrac12\log t\) on the off-diagonal differences leaves the claimed diagonal bound \(2D m(F)-\tfrac12D^2\log t\).

The [primitive-grid spacing](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#composite) allows one closest node per reflected nonzero root, with every other node at distance \(\gg Y^{-2}\).
A tie still leaves the other tied node at least half the minimal angular spacing away.
Only the closest nodes for the exact circle roots require deletion, and conjugate pairing costs at most \(s/2\) orders because the hypothesis on roots of unity excludes both real circle points.
All nonclosest nodes together contribute \(O(dY^2(1-t)Y^2)=O(dY^{-2})\) positive loss.
For the remaining closest off-circle node, the logarithmic loss is at most \(\log(1+(1-t)r/(1-r))\).
At \(r<1/2\) it is \(O(1-t)\), and at \(r\ge1/2\) the argument added to one is at most three times \((1-t)r^2/(1-r^2)\), giving the bound \(3H_t(r)\).
The radial estimate thus controls the total positive loss over all undeleted orders, with root multiplicity.

The [test Gram matrices](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#tests) depend only on the window, and \(d\le AY\) preserves the input energy bound with constants depending on \(A\).
In particular the [degree-dependent Mahler bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument) gives \(m(F)\gg_A L^{-3}\) for positive-measure factors, while positive measure arising solely from the integer content is at least \(\log2\).
Both the smoothed Gram exception count \(O_A(dY^{-5/8}L^{10})\) and the direct smoothing error in the upper estimates are therefore of the required order.
The [totient mean](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#exceptional-orders) removes \(O(Y/L)\) small-totient orders.
At the loss threshold \(Y^{7/8}m/L\), Markov's inequality costs \(O_A(d(m+L)L/(Y^{7/8}m))=O_A(Y^{1/8}L^5)=o_A(Y)\) further orders.
This yields relative primitive error tending to zero apart from the asserted \(s/2+o_A(Y)\).

If \(s=o(Y)\), the same relative estimate holds in density on each of the finitely many dyadic windows covering \([eY,2Y]\) for fixed \(0<e<1/2\), with degree constant at most \(2A/e\).
Each exceptional large divisor affects at most \(2/e\) indices of the original window.
The [small-divisor totient estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#actual) leaves \(\sum_{b\mid j,\ b<eY}\varphi(b)\le\sqrt e\,Y\) apart from \(O(\sqrt e\,Y)\) orders.
All \(\log D_b\) are nonnegative, so summing only over the other divisors gives the claimed lower bound for \(\log C_j/(jm)\).
Letting \(e\) tend to zero after the window limit and intersecting with the full upper estimate gives simultaneous convergence in density.
The nonreciprocal-unit and sublinear-circle-count examples satisfy these hypotheses.
For a Salem minimal polynomial the count still charges its \(d-2\) distinct circle roots.
