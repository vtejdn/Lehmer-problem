## Invariant factors and passage to divisors

At 12:21 UTC on November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the [trinomial power-suborder claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v549.md#statement) without participating in its development.
The additive invariant factors, index, exponent and conclusions for every monic divisor follow under the stated hypotheses.

The polynomial \(-F\) is monic of degree \(as+b\) with constant term one, and \(-B\) is monic of degree \(s\).
A common root of \(B\) and \(g\) would satisfy \(C=T^2+T+1=0\) by \(TB+(1+T)g=-C\), whereas the three possible residues of \(g\) there are \(-T,-1,2T^2\), all nonzero.
In the Laurent presentation of [v217](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v217.md#presentation), the exponents of \(1,X^b,\ldots,X^{(a-1)b}\) represent all classes modulo \(a\).
Writing \(Y=X^b\) gives \(Y^a=T^b\), and the products of \(BY+g\) with this basis generate the defining ideal as an \(R\)-module.
The image of \(R\cdot1\) is precisely \(O_0\), since the integral automorphism given by multiplication by \(x^a\) has characteristic polynomial with unit constant.
Quotienting by this image leaves the two end relations and all the middle relations displayed in the claim, including when \(O\) has nilpotents.

Multiplication by \(BU+gV\) on forms of degree \(r-1\) has entries \(B,g\) in consecutive rows of each column, so its transpose has exactly the stated relation columns.
An invertible substitution conjugates the multiplication map by the induced isomorphisms in degrees \(r-1\) and \(r\), and transposition preserves equivalence over the commutative Laurent ring.
The coefficient change from \((B,g)\) to \((g,C)\) has determinant \(T\), a unit.
Subtracting a polynomial multiple of \(C\) and scaling by a Laurent unit then yields \((1,C)\) or \((2,C)\).
The first pair kills the generators successively.

For the second pair, repeated substitution of \(ce_i=-2e_{i+1}\), with \(ce_r=0\), reduces every element over \(\mathbb Z[c]\) to an integral linear combination of the generators and gives \(2^ie_i=0\).
The action on \(\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb Z/2^i\mathbb Z\) given by \(cv_i=-2v_{i+1}\) and \(cv_r=0\) gives the inverse on these generators, proving exact orders and independence.
The monic quadratic extension sending \(c\) to \(C(T)\) doubles each cyclic summand as an additive group.
On this extension an explicit inverse for \(T\) is \(-(T+1)(1+c+\cdots+c^{r-1})\), since \(T(T+1)=c-1\) and \(c^r=0\).
Localization therefore preserves all the computed invariant factors.
Their orders multiply to \(2^{2\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}i}=2^{a(a-1)}\) and their maximum is \(2^{a-1}\) when \(a>1\).
At \(a=1\) the orders coincide and the zero group has exponent one.

Projection to any monic divisor of \(-F\) carries \(O_0\) onto the corresponding power suborder, giving the asserted additive quotient map even for reducible divisors or divisors with multiplicities.
It follows that the two orders coincide in the first two classes and that their quotient in the third is killed by \(2^{a-1}\).
Version 217 already supplies the general presentation and the resultant index, as well as a full cyclic decomposition for its particular quadratic seeds.
The additional conclusion here is the complete cyclic decomposition for the indicated trinomial seeds in every degree \(s\) and for all permitted \(a,b\).
The claim determines the whole parent quotient and bounds the possible quotients for individual divisors.
