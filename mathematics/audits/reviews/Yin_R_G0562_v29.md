## Excluding binomial limits for outputs of measure above one

At 2026-11-16T19:40 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the statement, argument and thirteen dependencies in the submitted decision of C345v29.
The reviewer took no part in developing this proof and received no development dialogue or prior verdict on it.
The [earlier review](Yin_R_G0562_v28.md#dyadic-limit) was consulted for its distinct, already proved dyadic concentration input.

For any sequence with output measures tending to one from above, that input gives the required sign, a Pisot unit, and a first coefficient discrepancy at \(n\to\infty\).
After division by \(z^n\), the centered quotient \(V\) has constant \(-\delta/2\).
The limit of \((z-2)/F-z\) is \(-2(1-z)^2\), so each compact subdisk is eventually free of poles of \(V\).
Canceling its possible single disk pole gives Schur compactness.
For any resulting limit \(G\), the integer polynomial \(2(1-z)^2G\) has a double zero at one, hence \(2G\) is an integer polynomial of norm at most two.
The three-term norm bound and the nonzero constant leave precisely the submitted list.
At discrepancy \(\pm2\), Boyd's interval recurrence makes all subsequent permitted intervals shorter than one for large \(n\).
The trinomial with \(t=-\delta/2\) realizes that signed choice, and its matched output before cyclotomic cancellation is \((1-z)^2(1+t z^{n+1})\).
This excludes both extreme constants from a positive-excess sequence.

For \(p=n+1\), the Gram matrix bound for \(n\) monomials gives a bounded sum of squares of the positive moments in each block of length \(n\).
Since \(T-2=O(n^{-2})\) and the inverse quadratic denominator has coefficients \(O_C(n)\) through \(Cn\), the convolution error from replacing the moments by the integer coefficients of \(K^0\) is \(O_C(n^{-1/2})\).
For \((x,y)=(\bar v,v^p)\) the coefficient at Fourier index \(r\) of \(2\mathbb E(y^l\mid x)\) therefore comes from index \(lp+r\).
The identity \(K^0=(1-z^pV)/(1+z^pV)\) gives the signed first row \(a+bx^k=-2G\).
For \(w=(b/a)x^k\), the second conditional moment of \(y/a\) equals one and has angular derivative \(ik\) at each point with \(w=1\).
If that moment has two terms, their exponents are multiples of \(k\) with sum \(2k\), and the variance bound from two independent conditional copies bounds their separation by \(2k\).
Thus the second moment is \(w\) or \((1+w^2)/2\).
In the first case \(y^2=abx^k\) almost surely, and in the second case the zero conditional variance of \(\operatorname{Re}((y/a)/\sqrt w)\) forces \(y=a\) or \(y=bx^k\).

For the support \(y\in\{a,bx^k\}\) take \(D=(1-a z^p)(1-bz^{p+k})\), and for \(y^2=abx^k\) take \(D=1-abz^{2p+k}\).
Each limiting support makes \(\int |D(\bar v)|^2\,d\mu\to0\) for its corresponding sequence of measures.
With \(h_0=2\), conjugation symmetry and the mass \(1+(T-2)/2\) extend the moment approximation across zero.
Cauchy–Schwarz and the larger window \(|j-m|\le Cn+2p+k\) make the integer \(\sum_m d_m h_{j-m}\) uniformly smaller than one in absolute value for \(|j|\le Cn\), where \(D=\sum_m d_m z^m\).
For the explicit seed with parameters \((-a,-b,c)\), taking \(c=ab\) gives the half-sum of the two uniform root measures, while \(c=-ab\) gives the uniform measure on \(v^{2p+k}=ab\) weighted by \(1+a\operatorname{Re}(v^p)\).
Their doubled bilateral moments reproduce the respective rational germs with \(h'_0=2\), including when the two root sets in the first case intersect.
The \(p-1\) initial zero coefficients and the first conditional row give equality with \(h'_j\) on \([-p-k,p+k]\) for large \(n\) at fixed \(k\).
Its length \(2p+2k+1\) exceeds \(\deg D=2p+k\), and \(d_0=1\) propagates equality through \(4n\).

The comparison-germ difference has exactly one extra factor of \(z\) times a unit at zero, giving common quotient coefficients through \(4n-1\).
After multiplication by both pole-canceling factors, the neutral quotient is a finite Blaschke product of degree at most \(2n+k+4\) and the other quotient is Schur.
Schur reduction uses one coefficient per step and reaches a unimodular common constant, so \(4n-1\ge 2n+k+4\) suffices for equality.
The common pole identifies the same minimal polynomial and hence the same matched output, contradicting positive excess.
The subsequence argument allows the discrepancy signs to alternate and needs no uniform threshold in \(k\).
At \(r=1/\theta\) the exact identity \(\theta-2=-r^nV(r)\) gives \(|2r-1|=O((3/5)^n)\), so \((2r)^n\to1\) and \(2^n(\theta-2)-\delta/2\to0\).

The whole claim passes independent review.
The explicit neutral seeds have one exterior root, only cyclotomic cofactors, and matched output of measure one.
Fixed \(k\) realizes every binomial, while \(k\to\infty\) realizes both unit constant profiles.
Existence of a positive-excess sequence remains open.
