### The integral identity and its nonzero norm

With \(z=\alpha^p\) of degree \(e=2m\) one has \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The inverse automorphism exchanges the two roots of each quadratic over its trace field and fixes \(s=z+z^{-1}\) and \(q_0=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\).
Both trace fields therefore equal its fixed field of degree \(m\).
For \(f=Y^{-2e}a\), the two equations in \(a\) from L229 yield \(f(z)=u\) and \(2pn(\mathcal E f)(z)=w+u\), since \(J-4pne=1\).
In particular both the value and the Euler derivative are fixed by inversion.

Each antisymmetric Laurent monomial difference is divisible by \(Y-Y^{-1}\) over \(\mathbb Z[Y+Y^{-1}]\) in the sense that its quotient is an integral trace polynomial.
For \(f-f^\iota\) the resulting quotient vanishes at \(s\), since \(z-z^{-1}\ne0\).
Monic division by \(T_p\) produces the unique polynomial \(Z\) over \(\mathbb Z\).
The derivative of \(f-f^\iota\) at \(z\) under \(\mathcal E\) is \(2(\mathcal E f)(z)\).
On the factored side the only surviving term is \((z-z^{-1})^2 T_p'(s)Z(s)\).
Multiplying by \(pn\) yields the claimed identity with exactly one factor \(pn\) and gives divisibility in the trace order without any localization at two.
Taking absolute norms supplies \((pn)^m\), \(|T_p(2)T_p(-2)|\) and \(|\operatorname{disc} T_p|\), leaving the exact integer quotient \(|N(Z(s))|\).
Both endpoints and the discriminant are nonzero.
The positive bound on a particular pair uses \(u+w\ne0\), which holds for both L229 witnesses because \(u\ne0\) and \(w/u\) is irrational.
The identity itself permits \(Z(s)=0\) for other pairs.
The odd-span derivative remainder at the original trace has its own representative derivative term, so the two norm divisors have distinct targets.

### Equal moduli and the range of powering exponents

Choose representatives of the inverse pairs with \(\lambda_j=\log|\rho_j|\ge0\) in increasing order.
Their sum counts the seed measure exactly, with neutral pairs contributing zero.
For a degree-preserving exponent \(p\), the \(m\) numbers \(s_j=\rho_j^p+\rho_j^{-p}\) are distinct.
Equality of two traces would identify two powered conjugates by equality of the two unordered roots of the corresponding reciprocal quadratics.
The elementary factorizations of \(s_j^2-4\) and \(s_j-s_i\) thus have no zero factors.
In a difference with \(i<j\), both \(\rho_i/\rho_j\) and \((\rho_i\rho_j)^{-1}\) have modulus at most one, even when both radii are exactly one.
An argument of modulus below one gives bounded logarithmic error.
A torsion argument has finitely many nonzero distances from one, whereas the audited logarithmic-form estimate gives \(O_{\xi}(\log(2p))\) for nontorsion unit-circle arguments.
Only finitely many algebraic arguments occur for a fixed seed.
This proves the error for all allowed integers \(p\ge2\), including tied radii.

The endpoint product contributes \(2p\sum_j\lambda_j\).
The discriminant contributes \(2p\sum_j(j-1)\lambda_j\), since each unordered difference is squared and its larger indexed radius gives the leading term.
Thus the total coefficient is \(2\sum_j j\lambda_j\), including \(m=1\) when the difference product is empty.
For a prime exponent, loss of degree means that a quotient of distinct conjugates has exactly that prime order.
There are finitely many such orders and the positive-integer asymptotic uses only degree-preserving exponents.

### Coefficient cost and the representative window

Each trace embedding evaluates \(U+V\) on one of the original \(\rho_j+\rho_j^{-1}\).
Using the maximum absolute coefficient in the basis \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{m-1}\) bounds every such value by a constant depending solely on the fixed seed times \(\max(\|U\|_C,\|V\|_C)\).
The product over the \(m\) embeddings therefore transfers the norm lower bound to the coefficient logarithm with an additive fixed-seed constant.
The resulting lower bound contains \(\log(pn)\) and \(p(2/m)\sum_j j\lambda_j\).
Pairing opposite indices in the nondecreasing radius list gives \(\sum_j j\lambda_j\ge(m+1)h/2\), and \(j\le m\) gives \(\sum_j j\lambda_j\le mh\).
For each fixed seed and every prime in the fourth-logarithmic window, \(\log(pn)=O(\log(ne))=o(p)\), and powering preserves the seed degree for sufficiently large representatives.
