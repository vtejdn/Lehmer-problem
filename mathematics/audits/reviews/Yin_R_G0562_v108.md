## Trace distances, short arcs and Salem factors

At 2026-11-17T01:15 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the fixed C345v108 submission of 2026-11-17T01:12 UTC.
The reviewer took no part in developing this argument and received the fixed submission without its development dialogue or earlier review findings for this version.
All five sections pass at the stated scope.

If \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) are conjugates of the reciprocal factor, substitution into the shifted parent and its reversal gives \(\alpha^{D-2k}=\epsilon\), since \(a\ne0\).
A nonzero exponent would make \(\alpha\) torsion, hence \(D=2k\), \(\epsilon=1\) and \(k\ge1\).
The parent after division by \(X^k\) is a symmetric Laurent polynomial with unit outer coefficients, and pairing terms at opposite indices gives its monic degree-\(k\) trace polynomial \(C\).
For any root \(c\) of \(C\), a solution of \(u^2-cu+1=0\) is nonzero and satisfies \(A(u)=0\), so \(c=u+\bar u\in[-2,2]\).
At all trace conjugates \(z_i\) one has \(C(z_i)=-a\).
The difference \(|z_i-c|^2-|z_j-c|^2\) is affine in a real variable \(c\) because the \(c^2\) terms cancel.
Since the endpoint distances are \(2(\cosh t_i\pm\cos\theta_i)\), a strict violation of (1) in either order makes the same conjugate strictly farther from every root of \(C\).
Multiplication then contradicts the common absolute value \(|a|\), even when there are repeated endpoint roots of \(C\).
With a neutral conjugate, (1) implies \(\cosh T\le3\).

For the circle estimate it suffices that the polynomial be monic and separable over the integers with constant coefficient of modulus one.
Reflection in the circle of an exterior root sends \(\alpha_i\) to \(1/\bar\alpha_i=\beta_i\) and preserves its angle.
For two exterior roots, dividing their distance by \(|\alpha_i\alpha_j|\) gives \(|\beta_i-\beta_j|\), bounded by \(|1-\bar\beta_i\beta_j|\) by the disk identity.
For two interior roots the same disk inequality applies directly.
For a mixed pair dividing by the exterior modulus gives equality with \(|1-\bar\beta_i\beta_j|\), including the endpoint convention for a neutral root.
Further smoothing costs at most the factor \(1/\rho\) since \(|1-\rho^2 z|^2-\rho^2|1-z|^2=(1-\rho^2)(1-\rho^2|z|^2)\ge0\).

The sum of the logarithms of distances over ordered unequal pairs is the logarithm of the absolute discriminant and is nonnegative.
Each exterior logarithmic modulus occurs \(2(d-1)\) times in the resulting estimate on the smoothed energy.
The \(d\) diagonal terms each cost at most \(-\log(1-\rho^2)\).
With \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\) the total bounds are \(O(d)\) for the off-diagonal smoothing and \(O(d\log(2d))\) for the diagonal.
Absolute convergence of the disk series permits summation over pairs to obtain the squared reflected power sums in (3).
Unit constant coefficient gives \(\sum|t_j|=2m\), and \(1-e^{-\ell|t_j|}\le\ell|t_j|\) gives the stated phase-replacement error.

The triangular test has normalized mean \(h/(2\pi)\) and its nonzero coefficients are \((1-\cos(\ell h))/(\pi h\ell^2)\).
Summing \(\ell h^2\) for \(\ell\le1/h\) and \(1/(h^2\ell^3)\) above \(1/h\) gives an absolute bound for its weighted square norm.
Cauchy–Schwarz through degree \(d\) costs \(O(\sqrt{(m+\log(2d))/d})\), since \(\rho^{2\ell}\ge e^{-2}\) in that range.
The phase replacement costs \(O(m\log(2d)/(dh))\), and the tail bound follows from \(\sum_{\ell>d}\ell^{-2}\le1/d\).
Both signs of the frequencies have the same estimates, and rotating the test leaves each coefficient's modulus unchanged.
For \(h\ge C\sqrt{(1+m)\log(2d)/d}\), the two errors in (4) are bounded by constants times \(h/C\) and \(h/C^2\), respectively.
Choosing \(C\) sufficiently large leaves a positive multiple of \(h\) in the normalized triangular average.
The test is between zero and one and supported in the stated arc, so this counts conjugates there with multiplicity, including repeated angles.

For a conjugate with \(|t_i|=T\), (1) and the angular Lipschitz bound for cosine force \(|t_j|\ge T/2\) whenever the angular distance is at most \(\min(1,T^2/4)\).
Indeed \(\cosh T-\cosh(T/2)\ge3T^2/8\) by the power series, exceeding \(T^2/4\) for \(T>0\).
The arc count therefore gives \(2m\gg dhT\) whenever the width meets its lower threshold.
In the neutral case \(T\le\operatorname{arcosh}3<2\), so \(h=T^2/4\).
Below the threshold \(T\ll q\) for \(q=((1+m)\log(2d)/d)^{1/4}\).
Above it \(T\ll (m/d)^{1/3}\ll q\), since \(m/d\le T\le2\) and \(d\ge2\).
Thus \(m\le rT\) gives (5).
Dobrowolski applies to these nontorsion algebraic integers at their irreducible degree \(d\).
The increasing function \(x/(1+x)^{1/4}\) evaluated at its lower benchmark is \(\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) for large \(d\), giving the exponent \(13/4\) of \(\log d\) in (2).

Without a neutral root, reciprocal pairing gives \(r=d/2\) and \(T\le m\).
If \(m\le B\), the short-arc threshold tends to zero uniformly, so \(h=1\) and \(T\ge2\) would force \(B\gg dT\) for large \(d\).
Once \(h=T^2/4\), the preceding two threshold cases apply with \(m/d\le B/d\).
Together with \(T\le B\) in bounded degree and the earlier bound in the neutral case, this proves the claimed house estimate.

The lower bound in (2) tends to infinity, bounding the degree for fixed \(R\).
With a neutral root every modulus is also at most \(e^{\operatorname{arcosh}3}\), bounding each integer coefficient by the elementary symmetric function estimate.
This gives finiteness of the represented polynomials, and in particular of their Salem roots under the stated convention.
The cubic-seed family supplies pairwise distinct Salem numbers whose degrees tend to infinity, each with the six-term multiple \(X^N(X^3-X-1)+(1-X^2-X^3)\) and a cofactor of measure one for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Consequently only finitely many members admit the given kind of parent.
The same finiteness excludes all sufficiently late terms of a sequence tending to one, since each represented Salem number is strictly larger than one.
For the positive example, \(\Phi_{10}-2X^2=X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1\) has trace roots \((1\pm\sqrt{13})/2\).
The larger exceeds two and the smaller belongs to \((-2,2)\).
The latter embedding makes the trace discriminant \(z^2-4\) strictly negative, ruling out a square in the real quadratic field.
The root above one thus has degree four over \(\mathbb Q\), with one reciprocal interior root and two neutral conjugates.

The external Dobrowolski theorem and the accepted Salem construction underlying the retained family were checked at their stated interfaces.
