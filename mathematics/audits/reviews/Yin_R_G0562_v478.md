## Independent finding on the three constructions

At 2026-11-18T19:43 UTC an agent outside the development of [L462 v223](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v223.md#least) independently checked its three sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the one-prime criterion L462 v220 and worked on earlier quartic inputs, and received the present constructions as fixed mathematics.
The simultaneous divisibility assertions over all integers in the stated ranges, the absence of the predicted rational divisors, and the lower bounds on the least good witness all hold.
The family with first exponent of order \(Y^\alpha\), \(1<\alpha<3\), gives a lower bound for witnessing the full odd degree regardless of whether the marked factor is Salem.

### Direction selection and the two logarithmic bounds

For primitive odd \(a\) and \(b\equiv2\bmod4\), none of the cyclotomic orders in the quartic table occurs.
The reciprocal parent is monic of degree \(4a+b\) with constant coefficient one, and its monic trace polynomial has degree \((4a+b)/2\).
Directly expanding the degree-six seed gives \(X^6+X^5-X^4-X^2+X+1\), whose trace is \(Y'^3+Y'^2-4Y'-2\).
In the finite algebra of this seed the class of \(X\) is a unit, and its order \(t\) is at most the cardinality \(L_Y^6\).
The lcm upper bound and the factor \(2^Y\) in \(M\) give \(Y\log2\le\log M\le C_1Y\) with an absolute constant for large \(Y\).

For every \(0\le k\le h\), the exponent of a prime \(p\) in \(\binom hk\) is the sum of the terms
\(\lfloor h/p^v\rfloor-\lfloor k/p^v\rfloor-\lfloor(h-k)/p^v\rfloor\), each zero or one and vanishing for \(p^v>h\).
Thus \(\binom hk\mid L_h\).
Using the largest term of the binomial sum gives \(\log L_h\ge h\log2-\log(h+1)\).
Subtracting the contributions of extra powers of primes \(p\le\sqrt h\) costs at most \(\sqrt h\log h\).
Together with \(\vartheta(h)\le\log L_h=O(h)\) this proves the two-sided Chebyshev estimate used here.
Choose \(K\) with \(cK>C'\) in those estimates.
For \(A/Y\to\infty\), the primes in \((A,KA]\) then have total log weight greater than \(\log M\), so at least one does not divide \(M\).
It is odd eventually, and \(b=M+2a\) has \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(b\equiv2\bmod4\).

For \(A=D(Y/\log Y)^3\), \(M\) dominates \(2a\) exponentially, so \(r\to\infty\) and \(\log b\asymp Y\).
In particular \(\log b\le C_2Y\) eventually with \(C_2\) independent of the chosen fixed \(D\), although the starting \(Y\) may depend on \(D\).
The lower bound for \(\log b\) gives \(\log\log b\ge \frac12\log Y\) eventually.
Taking \(D>C_0(2C_2)^3\) contradicts the necessary Salem bound in C345 v399 and ensures existence of a good certifying prime by L462 v220.
Since \(a\asymp D(Y/\log Y)^3\), one has \(a^{1/3}\log a\asymp Y\).
The choice \(A=(Y/\log Y)^3\log\log Y\) instead dominates any fixed necessary constant eventually, and satisfies \(a^{1/3}\log a/(\log\log a)^{1/3}\asymp Y\).
In either construction the cutoff below gives \(p_{\rm least}>Y\).

### Divisibility in the full finite algebra

Write \(R=\mathbb Z/L_Y\mathbb Z\) and \(x=X\bmod P_*\).
An inverse \(e\) of \(a\) modulo \(t\) gives \(z=x^e\) with \(z^a=x\) and \(z^M=1\).
Hence \(R[z]=R[x]\) and \(P_{a,b}(z)=-g(x)-x^2 B(x)=0\).
The monic characteristic polynomial \(Q\) of multiplication by \(z\) has degree six and vanishes on \(z\) by applying Cayley–Hamilton to the element \(1\).
Evaluation therefore maps \(R[Z]/(Q)\) onto \(R[x]\).
Both have exactly \(L_Y^6\) elements by monic division, so the kernel of evaluation from \(R[Z]\) is exactly \((Q)\).
Thus \(Q\) divides the parent over \(R\) even at bad primes and across their full powers in \(L_Y\).

Seed reciprocity makes the involution \(x\mapsto x^{-1}\) a ring automorphism and conjugates the multiplication operator \(U\) for \(z\) to \(U^{-1}\).
The companion determinant for \(x\) is \(P_*(0)=1\) in even degree, so \(\det U=1\).
It follows that \(Q(Z)=\det(ZI-U^{-1})=\det(I-ZU)=Z^6Q(Z^{-1})\).
The parent and \(Q\) have constant coefficient one, as does their monic quotient.
Reversing the product and cancelling the monic \(Q\), a non-zero-divisor over any coefficient ring, proves reciprocity of this even-degree quotient.
Pairing symmetric coefficients and using \(Z^k+Z^{-k}=(Z+Z^{-1})(Z^{k-1}+Z^{1-k})-(Z^{k-2}+Z^{2-k})\) produces monic trace polynomials for both factors.
Distinct trace polynomials remain distinct after substituting \(Z+Z^{-1}\), by the outermost Laurent coefficient.
Their product consequently equals the reduction of \(H_{a,b}\).

The resulting monic cubic modulo \(L_Y\) reduces compatibly modulo every \(2\le m\le Y\).
For each prime \(p\le Y\) its odd total degree forces an odd irreducible constituent of degree at most three, even with multiplicity.
Eventually \(3<r\), which excludes every such good prime from being a Salem certificate.
The fixed quartic graph satisfies the primitive degree theorem since \(g,B\) are coprime, its equation is linear and primitive in its second variable, and its support differences span rank two.
Here \(N=b\), the dilation is one, and its nontorsion degrees are \(\gg b(\log\log b/\log b)^3\).
A putative cubic rational trace divisor would give a degree-six divisor of the parent, contradicting this lower bound and the absence of torsion factors.
As a finite check on the transport, at \(Y=4,\ L_Y=12,\ a=5\) the order is \(312\), and the characteristic polynomial in trace coordinates gives the monic cubic with remaining coefficients \(9,8,2\bmod12\).

With \(A=Y^\alpha\) and \(1<\alpha<3\), the same prime selection and logarithmic estimate give \(a\asymp(\log b)^\alpha=o((\log b/\log\log b)^3)\).
The primitive lower bound forces the marked trace degree to tend to infinity, while the good primes up to \(Y\) have least odd degree at most three.
The maximum theorem guarantees a good prime attaining the full odd degree above this cutoff, independently of a decision about the Salem condition.

### A growing false degree nearest to half the slope

For the second seed, \(j=2Y+1\) and \(b_*=2j-4\equiv2\bmod4\) give a torsion-free parent of degree \(2j\).
The new exponents satisfy \(a=1+M\), \(b=b_*a+4M=2ja-4\), \(b\equiv2\bmod4\) and \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,4)=1\).
They reduce to \(1,b_*\) modulo \(t\).
Substitution in the finite algebra therefore gives divisibility by the seed parent.
Reciprocal descent gives the reductions of the very same integral polynomial \(H_*\) as divisors of \(H_{a,b}\) for all \(2\le m\le Y\) of this input.
The degree satisfies \(j-r/2=2/a\), strictly between zero and one-half eventually.
Moreover \(j<r\), so its odd degree forces an irreducible constituent of odd degree below the certificate threshold at every prime in the range.

The bound \(t\le L_Y^{2j}\) yields \(\log M=O(Y^2)\) and \(\log b=O(Y^2)\), while \(a>2^Y\) and \(r=2j-4/a\to\infty\).
Consequently \(a\) eventually exceeds the necessary Salem bound, which is \(O(Y^6)\) here.
The primitive lower bound for every rational nontorsion factor of the parent is at least a fixed positive multiple of \(b/Y^6\) for large \(Y\), hence exceeds \(2j\).
No rational trace divisor of degree \(j\) can exist since its lift would divide this entirely nontorsion parent.
L462 v220 supplies an actual certifying prime and the modular obstruction gives \(p_{\rm least}>Y\gg\sqrt{\log b}\).
The growing degree \(j\) is common to every modulus of this input and is the unique nearest integer to \(r/2\).
