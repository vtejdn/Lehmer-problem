## Independent finding on the uniform count and fixed-row certificate primes

At 2026-11-18T19:46 UTC an agent outside the development of [L462 v221](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v221.md#count) independently checked all three sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the one-prime exclusion and a different modular copying construction, and received the present argument in its written form without its development discussion.
The dyadic bound is uniform in every odd \(q\ge3\) and counts all eligible multiples of that \(q\) together.
The global fourth-power logarithmic bound and the lower bound for the least actual non-Salem certificate on every fixed odd row \(a\ge3\) both follow.

### Injectivity of the powered-root construction

For \(r>6\), the quartic real-root theorem gives precisely one negative real exterior root for odd \(a\) and either parity of \(b\).
Put \(u=\sigma_{a,b}^{a}\).
At large slope the real branch has \(u=\beta+O(\beta^{-r})\) uniformly, so \(\log u=L+O(\beta^{-r})\) with \(L=\log\beta>0\).
If \(q\mid a\), \(a/q\) is odd and \(z=-(-\sigma_{a,b})^{a/q}\).
For a root \(\alpha=-\sigma_{a,b}\), the section equation recovers \(\alpha^b=-g(\alpha^a)/B(\alpha^a)\) from \(\alpha^a\), and Bézout in \(a,b\) recovers \(\alpha\) in the same field.
Thus \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\alpha^{a/q})\), as also follows from the stronger integral-order equality in L462 v216.
Powers therefore preserve the number of distinct conjugates, and their absolute values preserve the single exterior root and the circle conjugates for Salem input.
The polynomial \(P_z\) is monic integral, is Salem and has \(m(P_z)=\log z=(L+O(\beta^{-r}))/q\).
The degree estimate \(d/r\to1\) is uniform for the one-exterior factor and is unaffected by taking the power or changing sign.

For a fixed \(q\), equality of two such polynomials identifies their unique exterior root \(z>1\), and hence identifies \(u=z^q\).
At an actual section root, both \(g(-u)\) and \(B(-u)\) are nonzero, since a zero of either would be common to both.
Taking absolute values gives \(u^r=|g(-u)/B(-u)|\), recovering \(r\) uniquely since \(\log u>0\).
Primitivity then recovers \((a,b)\) uniquely even across different first exponents.

### The discriminant bound is uniform in \(q\)

For a finite set of \(h\ge2\) directions at the fixed \(q\), the product \(Q\) of their minimal polynomials is monic and squarefree by the preceding injectivity.
Hence \(|\operatorname{disc}Q|\ge1\).
All \(h\) exterior roots have logarithm \(L/q+O(\beta^{-R}/q)\).
The mean value theorem gives \(|z_i-z_j|\ll\beta^{-R}/q\), while \(z_iz_j-1\ge L/q\) for large \(R\) by \(\log(z_iz_j)\ge L/q\).
Their ratio is \(O(\beta^{-R})\) with an absolute implied constant, and the interior pair \(z_i^{-1},z_j^{-1}\) has exactly the same disk ratio.

The reflected discriminant bound applies to squarefree \(Q\) regardless of its rational factor count.
After reflecting the exterior roots to \(w_i=1/\overline{z_i}\), the product \(\prod_{i<j}|1-w_i\bar w_j|^2\), using the original coordinates for the other roots, is at most \(D^D\) for \(D=\deg Q\).
Successive maximum modulus in each disk variable reduces this inequality to Hadamard on the unit-circle Vandermonde.
The normalization of the discriminant contributes \(2(D-1)m(Q)\) in logarithms.
There are \(h(h-1)\) ordered distinct exterior pairs and \(h(h-1)\) ordered distinct interior pairs.
Keeping both sets gives the left side \(2(RL-O(1))h(h-1)\) of (3).

Uniformity of the individual degree estimate gives \(D\le(2+o(1))Rh\) for every such finite set, and additivity of measure gives \(m(Q)\le h(L+O(\beta^{-R}))/q\).
Consequently \(2(D-1)m(Q)\le(4/q+o(1))RLh^2\le(4/3+o(1))RLh^2\).
Subtracting this term from the pair bound leaves a positive absolute multiple of \(RLh^2\), apart from \(O(Rh)\).
Bounding \(D\log D\ll Rh\log(2Rh)\) now gives \(h\le C(1+\log(2R)+\log h)\) with absolute \(C\).
Using \(\log h\le h/(2C)+O_C(1)\) proves \(h\ll\log(2R)\) for arbitrary finite subsets, hence for the whole set.
For \(q=1\) the row \(a=1\) already supplies \(\gg R\) Salem inputs.
Indeed the phase count gives one exterior root, and its growing-degree factor contains a circle root and is reciprocal by irreducibility, making it Salem after the sign change.

### Summing the count and applying it to the two exponent families

For a Salem direction, \(d\ll r\) while the primitive degree bound gives \(d\gg b(\log\log b/\log b)^3\) since \(N=b\) here.
Dividing by \(r=b/a\) gives \(a\ll(\log b/\log\log b)^3\) and hence \(\log a=O(\log\log b)\).
Thus \(\log r=\log b-O(\log\log b)\), so \(a\le A=C(\log R/\log\log R)^3\) uniformly in the dyadic window.
Every eligible odd \(a\ge3\) is counted for at least one odd prime \(q\le A\).
The prime-count bound \(\pi(A)\ll A/\log A\), with \(\log A\asymp\log\log R\), gives
\[
  \pi(A)\log(2R)\ll(\log R/\log\log R)^4.
\]
Overlap of directions with several prime divisors of \(a\) only increases this upper sum.
The claimed elementary prime-count bound follows from \(\vartheta(A)\ll A\) by counting primes above \(\sqrt A\) with weight at least \(\frac12\log A\), and the dyadic binomial-coefficient argument gives that bound for \(\vartheta\).

For fixed odd \(a\ge3\), choose \(q=a\) in (1) and sum the dyadic windows to get \(O_a((\log(2R))^2)\) up to slope \(R\), absorbing the bounded initial range on the fixed row.
At \(a=3\), the unique real exterior root and single nonreal exterior pair give precisely the previously proved correspondence between splitting of the nontorsion core and a Salem marked root.
In a dyadic window, the even progression \(b\equiv2\bmod2a\) supplies \(\gg_a R\) primitive choices.
For \(b=6a+c^2\) the interval of possible positive \(c\) has length \(\asymp_a\sqrt R\), and the subprogression \(c\equiv1\bmod2a\) gives \(\gg_a\sqrt R\) distinct primitive choices.
The fixed-row bound leaves only \(O(\log(2R))\) possible Salem exceptions in either family.
For all the other inputs, the marked odd trace factor exists and the prime maximum theorem supplies (5) in each prescribed reduced residue class.

### Existence of a non-Salem direction with a late first witness

For each fixed odd \(a\ge3\) one can take, for example, \(b_0=8a+4>6a\), which is divisible by four and coprime to \(a\).
The primitive cyclotomic table removes exactly \(\Phi_4\) throughout its class modulo \(4a\).
Thus the seed trace degree \(n_{b_0}=2a+b_0/2-1\) is odd and independent of \(Y\).
The residue of \(X\) is a unit modulo the monic \(A_{b_0}\) over each \(\mathbb F_p\), so its order \(t_p\) is at most the cardinality \(p^{4a+b_0}\), including when the quotient has nilpotents.
For the displayed \(M\), this and \(\vartheta(Y)\ll Y\) give \(Y\log2\le\log M\ll_a Y\).
If \(b\equiv b_0\bmod M\), then \(X^b=X^{b_0}\) in each seed quotient.
Consequently \(A_{b_0}\) divides \(A_b\bmod p\) for all \(p\le Y\).
Cancelling the common monic \(\Phi_4\) and the Laurent monomials preserves divisibility, even at \(p=2\).
In \(\mathbb F_p[X,X^{-1}]\) the basis \(1,X\) over \(\mathbb F_p[X+X^{-1}]\) follows from the monic relation \(X^2-(X+X^{-1})X+1=0\).
Comparing the two basis coefficients descends the divisibility to \(H_{b_0}\mid H_b\bmod p\).
The seed trace remains monic of odd degree, so every good \(p\le Y\) has \(\ell(p)\le n_{b_0}\).

The interval \([MY^2,2MY^2]\) contains \(Y^2+O(1)\) members of the specified residue class, all primitive even exponents.
At \(R=MY^2/a\), the entire window has only \(O(\log(2R))=O_a(Y)\) Salem exponents.
Consequently at least one, in fact \(\gg Y^2\), of the selected exponents is non-Salem for each large \(Y\).
Each has a good certificate by (5), while \(r>n_{b_0}\) excludes every prime up to \(Y\) from certifying.
Since \(\log b\ll_a Y\), the least good witness exceeds \(c_a\log b\) for a positive constant depending only on the fixed row.
The lower endpoints \(MY^2\) tend to infinity, so this supplies infinitely many distinct exponents on that row.
