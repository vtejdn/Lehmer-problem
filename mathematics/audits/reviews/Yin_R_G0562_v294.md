### Exact coefficient model and strict root counts

In the exact inverse expression from v293, substitute \(B(z)=D(z^2)\) and use reversal in degree \(2\ell\).
The even and odd coefficients give exactly \(f(z)=U(z^2)-zV(z^2)\), with the displayed formulas (4).
They cannot cancel each other, so their separate integrality is necessary and sufficient.
On \(|w|=1\), \(|D^*|=|D|>0\), making \(qD+D^*\) strictly stable by Rouché and giving zero disk roots to \(wD+qD^*\).
The same strict comparison excludes boundary roots of both polynomials.
Even when \(D(0)=0\), the formally reversed \(D^*\) has constant one and no disk roots; no degree assertion about its actual leading term is needed.

Since \(j\) is a positive integer, the assumed range gives \(1\le j\le q-2\).
The upper endpoint of the interval for \(S\) is \(q+1+j/(q-1)<q+2\), and its lower endpoint exceeds \(q-1\).
Thus its only possible integral values are \(q\) and \(q+1\).
Eliminating \(D(0)\) gives \(V(0)=qS-L\) and both constants in (5).
For the second branch, \(N-qj\ge2q-1>0\); the first constant is strictly negative, so neither is zero.

Conversely, \(qV-V^*\) has leading coefficient \(L\), and the strict comparison \(q|V|>|V^*|\) proves the monic rational \(D\) in (6) is stable.
Reversing in degree \(\ell\) yields \(qD+D^*=NV/L\) and the stated formula for \(U\).
Condition (3) is precisely the integrality of that \(U\); its leading coefficient is one and constant is \(q\).
The resulting integral \(f\) satisfies \(f^*-qf=LzD(z^2)-ND^*(z^2)\), so this \(A\) is integral as well as strictly stable.
Its leading coefficient is \(L>N\), constant is \(-N\), and value at one is \(jD(1)>0\).
Here \(D(1)>0\) follows from monicity and strict stability over the reals.
The inverse correspondence gives exactly one positive exterior root and no circle roots; the nonzero constant excludes a proper monic integral factor whose roots were all interior.
The accepted monic rational-factor theorem therefore supplies irreducibility.

The disjoint odd and even coefficient blocks in \(A\) show that every coefficient denominator of \(D\) divides both \(L\) and \(N\).
Their least common multiple consequently divides \(\gcd(N,j)\).
Using \(N\equiv-j\pmod L\) in the two expressions for \(ND(0)\) gives exactly the two scalar divisibilities (7).
No converse from these scalar tests is used.

### Forced content and arithmetic reduction

Formal reversal of \(F\) in degree \(\ell+1\) gives the displayed elimination identity with sign \(-N^2wV\).
Coefficientwise divisibility by \(L\) therefore forces \(L/\gcd(L,N^2)\) to divide every coefficient of \(V\).
Dividing out this integer \(h\) is exact and leaves (9) modulo \(M=L/h\).
Because \(M=\gcd(L,N^2)\), one has \(g=\gcd(M,N)=\gcd(L,N)\), and division by this greatest common divisor gives \(\gcd(n,m)=1\).
The relation \(gm=M\mid N^2=g^2n^2\) then forces \(m\mid g\).
Thus \(m\le g\le j\), \(M\le j^2\), and \(h\ge2\), since the integer \(h=L/M\) is strictly greater than one.
These arguments allow nonprimitive \(W\) and composite moduli.

Multiplication by the monic polynomial \(w-1\) is injective over \((\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z)[w]\), as comparison of the highest nonzero coefficient shows.
It is therefore legitimate to cancel that factor and obtain \(W^*\equiv qW\pmod g\).
The leading and constant coefficients give \(b\equiv qa\) and \(a\equiv qb\), making both \(a^2-b^2\) and every coefficient of \(aW-bW^*\) divisible by \(g\).
The latter polynomial has constant zero and leading coefficient \(a^2-b^2>0\).
Strict stability gives \(|b|<a\), so Rouché followed by division by \(w\) proves exactly the stated degree and stability of \(Y\).
When \(\ell=1\), this is the positive constant \(c\), with no omitted root condition.

### Formal resultant recurrence

The convention \(\operatorname{Res}_{r,r}(P,P^*)=a^r\prod_iP^*(\beta_i)\) agrees with the formal-degree Sylvester determinant even if the second polynomial has smaller actual degree.
For monic \(P\), the Schur reduction gives \(P=wZ+\tau Z^*\), and \(Z\) is monic and strictly stable.
When \(\tau\ne0\), switching the order of the resultants of degrees \(r\) and \(r-1\) introduces no sign, since \(r(r-1)\) is even.
Evaluation at the roots of \(Z\) gives \(\operatorname{Res}(P,Z)=\tau^{r-1}\mathcal R_{r-1}(Z)\).
Evaluation at the roots of \(P\), using \(\prod_i\beta_i=(-1)^r\tau\), gives the second identity in the proof with the stated sign and exponent.
Cancelling the nonzero \(\tau^{r-1}\) yields the recurrence.

When \(\tau=0\), the root zero of \(P=wZ\) contributes \(P^*(0)=Z^*(0)=1\), while the other factors give \(\mathcal R_{r-1}(Z)\).
This proves the same recurrence without division by zero or replacing formal degree by actual degree.
Starting from \(\mathcal R_0(1)=1\), every factor \((1-\tau^2)^r\) is positive and at most one.
For degree \(r\ge1\), this proves both positivity and the claimed upper bound, including \(r=1\).
Multiplying each input by \(a\) multiplies its formal resultant by \(a^{2r}\), giving exactly (13).
The estimate is proved locally; no unexamined resultant inequality is being imported.

### The full congruence and determinant divisibility

The leading coefficient of \(R=(W^*-qW)/g\) is \((b-qa)/g=-M/g=-m\), so the pair \((W,R)\) has the asserted exact degrees.
Substitution in (9) gives (14), and \(qn\) is invertible modulo \(m\), since \(m\mid g\mid N\) and \(\gcd(n,m)=1\).
Evaluation at \(q^2\) therefore makes \(W(q^2)\) divisible by \(m\).
Polynomial division by the monic \(w-q^2\) gives integral \(Q\) of degree \(\ell-1\); cancellation of that factor modulo \(m\) gives \(R\equiv qnQ\), with the sign in (15).

Use the Sylvester convention with shifted-polynomial coefficient vectors as columns, or transpose the usual row convention.
Every column of \(S_0\) is a multiple of \(Q\) of degree at most \(2\ell-1\).
Over \(\mathbb Q\), these multiples form a space of dimension \((2\ell-1)-(\ell-1)+1=\ell+1\).
Consequently a nonzero term in the multilinear column expansion of the determinant must use at least \(\ell-1\) columns from \(mS_1\).
All such terms are divisible by \(m^{\ell-1}\) as integers.
This proof requires neither a unit coefficient of \(W\) nor a rank argument over a ring with zero divisors.
It also covers \(\ell=1\), when the extra divisibility is the trivial power \(m^0\).

Replacing \(W^*\) by \(W^*-qW\) preserves the formal resultant by evaluation at the roots of \(W\); scaling that second input by \(g\) contributes \(g^\ell\).
The resulting positive integer is therefore at least \(g^\ell m^{\ell-1}\), while (13) bounds it by \((gc)^\ell\).
Cancellation gives precisely (17).
Nonvanishing follows from strict stability and the already established positivity, so no zero-resultant exception invalidates this comparison.
