Expanding \(z^{n-1}(v-1)+z^{1-n}(-v-1)\), for \(v=z-z^{-1}\), proves (3) including \(n=2\).
At the given \(n\) samples the trace values alternate between \(-2\) and \(2\), and remain strictly alternating in sign after addition of one.
The interval roots and the root on \((2,\infty)\) exhaust degree \(n\), hence they are simple in both traces.
At the larger exterior trace the shifted trace is positive, giving the strict order of the roots above two and therefore of their outer lifts.
The off-circle inverse pair must occur in the same irreducible factor, since each monic factor has unit constant coefficient and no other root can balance the exterior modulus.
The other factors are precisely cyclotomic factors by the cited statement of Kronecker.

For \(P_n\), \(T=t^2\ne1\) and \(v=(T+1)/(T-1)\).
An embedding sending \(T\) to its least positive primitive angle extends to the containing cyclotomic field, and \(|v|\le2\) at that embedding requires \(\cot(\pi/q)\le2\).
The largest seventh-root trace exceeds \(6/5\) by the cubic with opposite signs at \(6/5,2\).
This gives \(\cos(2\pi/7)>3/5\) and hence \(\cot^2(\pi/7)>4\), excluding every \(q\ge7\).
The norm test on \(v-1\) excludes 3 and 5, because for a prime-power root the absolute norm of \(1-T\) is its underlying prime and the numerator is 2.
Integrality descends to the subfield containing the quotient.
Order 2 forces \(\zeta^2=1\) and therefore \(T=1\).
The orders 4 and 6 force \(\zeta^2\) to have order 6 and 3 respectively, while \(T=(\zeta^2)^{n-1}\) cannot have the required order in either case.
This eliminates all possible cyclotomic source factors, so the entire lift and its trace are irreducible.

For the shifted polynomial \(t\ne\pm1\) and the quotient for \(v\) has integral value.
At a primitive angle within \(\pi/5\) of \(\pi\), the comparison is \(1+2\cos\delta>4\sin\delta\).
At the endpoint its squared difference is \(7\varphi-10>0\); monotonicity proves strictness throughout the interval.
The indices \((q-1)/2,\ q/2-1,\ q/2-2\) in the three specified parity classes are coprime to \(q\), giving respectively distances \(\pi/q,2\pi/q,4\pi/q\).
Thus the residual list is exactly \(3,4,6,8,10,14,18\).
The identity \((2+t)(v-1)+1=3/(t^2-1)\) excludes \(4,8,10,14\) by prime-power norms in the field of \(t^2\).
For orders 3 and 6 direct substitution gives respectively \(v^2=-4/3\) and \(v=0\), both impossible here.
For order 18, multiplying \(t^{-2}-t^2\) by \(t-t^{-1}\) gives \(t+t^{-1}-1\).
The two possibilities for \(\zeta\) are consequently \(t^{-2},-t^2=t^{11}\).
An element of order nine cannot yield \(t\) by taking a power, while \(11^{-1}=5\bmod18\) makes the second possibility equivalent to \(n=6\bmod18\).
Conversely \(t^{11}\) runs through every primitive eighteenth root, verifying the full optional cyclotomic factor with multiplicity one.
The other factor has even degree at least four and contains neutral roots as well as the inverse pair.
At degree \(2n-6\) the smallest permitted \(n\) is six.
Its trace degree is half its degree because a neutral real trace conjugate makes the discriminant of the reciprocal quadratic negative, excluding a square in the totally real field.
The optional trace factor is the irreducible cubic \(X^3-3X-1\).
Neither trace factor has a rational zero, and the exhibited constructions give both exceptional and ordinary degrees unboundedly often.

At the Pisot root the evaluations are \(-2\varphi\) and \(\varphi^n-2\varphi\), the latter positive for \(n\ge3\).
At fixed points greater than one on either side of \(\varphi\), the scaled polynomials converge to \(f\), bracketing both exterior roots.
The two root equations then bound the errors exponentially, so \(n\log(\sigma_n/\varphi)\) and \(n\log(\tau_n/\varphi)\) tend to zero.
The limiting constants after rescaling are respectively \(-f^*(\varphi)/f'(\varphi)=2\varphi/\sqrt5\) and \(1/f'(\varphi)=1/\sqrt5\).
Thus both families have the asserted positive limiting measure.

For the second step, the nonexceptional congruence ensures the whole intermediate trace \(S_n+1\) is the minimal polynomial, so the next level really is its own shift.
Expansion of \((z^h-1)(z^hf-f^*)\) uses \(f+f^*=-2z\) and gives \(P_n+2z^n\).
On the circle the ratio \(f^*/f=(1+2i\sin\theta)/(1-2i\sin\theta)\) has continuous periodic argument with derivative of absolute value at most 4.
Subtracting this argument from \(h\theta\) gives an increasing function with winding \(h\) for \(h=n-1\ge6\), counting exactly \(h\) distinct simple roots of \(A_h\) on the circle.
Direct differentiation gives \(A'_h(1)=4-h\), while evaluation in the factored identity gives \(A_h(\tau_n)=\tau_n^n/(\tau_n^h-1)>0\).
This places an exterior root in \((1,\tau_n)\), with its reciprocal accounting for the last available root.
Its trace is strictly between 2 and \(\sqrt5<3\), excluding a reciprocal quadratic with integral trace, so the noncyclotomic factor is Salem.
The fixed-point bracketing using \(z^{-h}A_h\to f\) gives the same positive limit.
The prior Pisot construction and the seven empirical examples are background only; the argument proves its assertions for every indicated \(n\) directly.
