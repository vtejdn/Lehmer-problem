# Review of the height bound for scalar congruences at reciprocal units

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of the two alternatives

At 2026-11-21T00:11 (UTC), an agent independent of the development of C345 v697 checked its complete submitted statement and proof for Sheng Yin and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer inspected the fixed Claim, the integral-congruence theorem, its registered numerical source unit and the Salem discriminant comparison.
The coordinator identified a clarification to the comparison sentence during the review.
This finding concerns the text explicitly using \(\Delta\ge1\) and growing degree, and the reviewer independently derived that specialization.

The reciprocal minimal polynomial makes evaluation \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\) an automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), since the image generates the same field.
It therefore takes the assumed algebraic integer \(G(\alpha)/q\) to the algebraic integer \(G(\alpha^{-1})/q\).
Their product \(y\) is integral, and it is zero exactly when \(G(\alpha)=0\).
This reasoning applies to divisibility in the ring of integers and hence also to the full power-order congruence in the statement.

For the Laurent estimate, the range condition on the unit circle bounds \(|T_k(V)|\) by \(\cosh(kB)\) even at both endpoints, using the cosine and hyperbolic-cosine formulas.
Multiplication of \(2T_k(V)\) by \(z^{kW}\) removes its pole at zero and preserves its boundary modulus.
Applying the disk maximum principle to this polynomial and then to the expression with \(z\) inverted gives the bound \(2\exp(kB+kW|\log|z||)\) everywhere off zero.
If a reciprocal quadratic root has modulus different from one, the modulus of the sum of its \(k\)-th power and its inverse \(k\)-th power has \(k\)-th root tending to the larger of the two moduli.
This proves the claimed bound with no restriction on the argument of either \(z\) or the quadratic root.
Unit modulus is immediate, including double roots, and the proof also works for \(W=0\).

Substitution of \(\theta=1+2Z\) in the lift equation gives \(Z^2+2yZ+y=0\).
Both reciprocal roots are algebraic integers and units, and this substitution proves the precise dyadic integrality required for the local height transfer at exponent one.
The transferred height is the absolute logarithmic height of the nontorsion lift itself.
In the recorded auxiliary normalization \(D=6005,\ A=11206\), so \(2A-D=16407\) and \(4D=24020\) in the source expression.
The published evaluation \(0.4278003111\ldots\) supports the adopted lower rounding \(0.427800\) at its registered scope.
This review uses the proved local extension to integral inputs and the accepted source evaluation, without independently recomputing the numerical supremum.

On the unit circle \(G(z)G(1/z)=|G(z)|^2\).
With \(x=H/q\), the interval for \(1-2|G(z)|^2/q^2\) is contained in \([-1,1]\) when \(x\le1\), and otherwise its lower endpoint is \(-\cosh(2\operatorname{arcosh}x)\).
Thus \(B=2b(H/q)\) is valid in both regimes.
For each embedding of the common number field, the conjugate lift satisfies the same quadratic relation at the corresponding conjugate of \(\alpha\).
At an algebraic unit the sum of the logarithms of the archimedean moduli over all embeddings, counting each complex embedding, is zero.
Half their mean absolute value is therefore the mean of their positive parts, the absolute logarithmic height.
Equal multiplicity of extensions of embeddings preserves this formula in the common field, even if the lift has a different degree.
Averaging the Laurent inequality and dividing by two gives \(h(\theta)\le Wm/d+b(H/q)\), using \(h(\alpha)=m/d\).
This proves the first alternative for all the stated signatures.

For a root of unity \(\theta\), every conjugate of \(y=(2-\theta-\theta^{-1})/4\) lies in \([0,1]\).
If \(y\ne0\), none of these conjugates vanishes and their product is a positive integer at most one.
Each is then exactly one, so \(y=1\) and the corresponding lift is \(-1\).
The zero case gives the lift \(1\).
Since \(G\) is nonzero, \(H>0\), and the second Laurent polynomial in the proof has circle range in \([-1,1]\).
At \(y=1\) its value at every conjugate of \(\alpha\) is the common number \(2(q/H)^2-1\).
For \(q>H\) this is \(\cosh(2\operatorname{arcosh}(q/H))\), so averaging the pointwise estimate gives \(b(q/H)\le Wm/d\).
For \(q\le H\) the right requirement is zero.
When \(q>H\), one neutral conjugate already places a conjugate of \(y\) strictly below one and eliminates that exceptional value.
The same reasoning excludes it at a negative real value of \(y\) without a condition on \(q/H\).
For an even alternating word the evaluations at the positive Salem root and its inverse have opposite signs by pairing consecutive ordered powers.
Using \(H\le r\) and monotonicity of \(b\) yields the stated \(r\)-coefficient bound, including a possible nonpositive lower bound.
The strict exact-detection condition, the arbitrary-signature minimum and all boundary conventions follow from the two alternatives.

For the comparison put \(d=2(n+1)\) in the earlier Salem discriminant inequality and \(A=H^2/q^2\).
At a false positive with \(q>H\) the bounded-interval case of that criterion is impossible on its common scalar-congruence domain.
The circle cap also gives \(|G(\alpha^{-1})|\le H\) by the maximum principle and \(|G(\alpha)|\le H\alpha^W\) by applying it to the reversed polynomial.
The left side of the determinant estimate is therefore at most \(A(e^{Wm}+1)\).
With \(\Delta\ge1\) its right side is at least \((2q/H)^{n-1}/(2\sqrt n)\).
For \(q/H\to1^+\) and \(n\to\infty\), this lower estimate grows exponentially, and its logarithm divided by \(d\) tends to \(\tfrac12\log2\).
The constant in the new bound is strictly larger.
This verifies the comparison after the stated specialization while preserving the possibility of gains from the individual trace discriminant.
