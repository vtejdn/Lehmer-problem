# Ramification in the trace field of a reciprocal unit

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T17:20 UTC I independently reviewed for Yin the complete [C345 v275 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v275.md#decision-quadratic-trace-ramification-submitted).
I used the fixed proof and the field description in C345 v274 without participating in development of C345 v275.
The local-degree and discriminant bounds, endpoint identity and prime-degree examples hold as stated.

### Relative inertia and the two discriminants

The distinct inverse conjugates \(x,x^{-1}\) have the same trace and generate the same field.
Thus \([A:F]=2\) and its involution interchanges them.
At a ramified \(\mathfrak p\) the local extension has ramification index two and residue degree one, so the involution fixes the residue of the unit \(x\).
It follows that \(x^2-1\) has positive valuation, at least \(1/(2e_\mathfrak p)\) in the normalization \(v(p)=1\).
Distributing this valuation over \(x-1\) and \(x+1\) gives a maximum at least \(1/(4e_\mathfrak p)\), including when their residues agree in characteristic two.
The \(2e_\mathfrak p f_\mathfrak p\) embeddings above each counted prime are distinct, and their images of \(x\) are distinct since \(x\) generates \(A\).
Assigning each image to a permissible sign leaves a cluster with \(r\ge d\delta_p(Y)/2\), with every pairwise difference of valuation at least \(1/(4Y)\).

The discriminant of the minimal polynomial sums twice the valuations over the unordered pairs, and every unused difference is integral.
The contribution of the cluster is at least \(r(r-1)/(4Y)\).
Hadamard's inequality on the Vandermonde matrix gives the claimed upper bound \(d\log d+2(d-1)m\) on the logarithm of the absolute polynomial discriminant.
Since \(r\le1+\sqrt{r(r-1)}\) for \(r\ge1\), multiplying the resulting bound on \(r\) by \(2/d\) gives (1), and an empty cluster is harmless.
When \(d\ge4/\eta\), \(\delta_p(Y)\ge\eta\) gives \(\delta_p(Y)-2/d\ge\eta/2\), explaining the constant \(128\) in the lower bound on \(m\).

The trace-pairing computation in (2) uses the field discriminant of \(F\), allowing an arbitrary index for \(\mathbb Z[t]\).
Modulo \(p\) the factor at \(\mathfrak p\) has \(e_\mathfrak p\) successive residue-field quotients.
The action of multiplication on each quotient is multiplication by the residue of the element.
Hence the trace of a product is \(e_\mathfrak p\) times its residue trace and the pairing has rank at most \(f_\mathfrak p\), or zero when \(p\mid e_\mathfrak p\).
The Smith factors of the integral trace matrix divisible by \(p\) account for its nullity modulo \(p\), proving \(\nu_p(D_F)\ge n-\sum_{p\nmid e_\mathfrak p}f_\mathfrak p\).

### Quadratic base change and endpoint values

In the oriented alternative of C345 v274, the stabilizer of \(t\) fixes its inverse pair as a set and preserves the block containing that pair.
The fixed field \(E\) of the block stabilizer therefore lies in \(F\).
An extension of the nonidentity automorphism of \(A/F\) sends \(x\) into the opposite selection and exchanges the selections in this block.
Its restriction to \(K/E\) is nontrivial, so \(K\cap F=E\) and \(FK=A\).
This specializes to a rational quadratic field when the block action has \(u=1\).
More generally, multiplicativity of ramification indices in \(A/K/E\) and \(A/F/E\) forces \(e(A/F)=2\) whenever \(e(K/E)=2\) and the index in \(F/E\) is odd.
Only the absolute indices enter (1).

For rational quadratic \(K\), all odd-index primes of \(F\) above a ramified \(p\) are therefore counted once the cutoff reaches their index.
Integrating against \(Y^{-2}\) divides each local-degree weight by its index, and \(\int_1^\infty Y^{-3/2}\,dY=2\) gives the coefficient \(8a_p\).
In the field discriminant bound an index \(e\ge2\) costs at least \((e-1)f\ge ef/2\), while the local mass at index one is at most \(\delta_p(1)\).
This proves the second inequality of (2).
At two the only ranks subtracted are those at odd indices, so the integrated bound gives the asserted stronger third inequality.
At bounded \(m\) the errors go to zero uniformly over \(p\) by \(\log p\ge\log2\).
A fixed deficit therefore bounds \(d\), after which the coefficient bound for monic integral minimal polynomials gives finiteness of the numerical measures.

Both \(x-x^{-1}\) and \(\sqrt{\Delta}\) span the anti-invariant \(F\)-line of the quadratic extension.
Taking their quotient and the norm down to \(\mathbb Q\) yields \(W(2)W(-2)=\Delta^{n}N(z)^2\).
The signs cancel because \((2-t)(-2-t)=t^2-4\).
Neither endpoint vanishes, since \(x\) is nontorsion.
This recovers \(K\) in odd trace degree and gives a rational square in even trace degree.

### Realizing the parity examples

For each prescribed prime \(p\), the local model has one rational root and \((n-1)/2\) distinct conjugate pairs in the same ramified quadratic field \(D\).
All its roots have nonzero derivative.
Close enough coefficients satisfy the strict Hensel inequality at every original root and give one root in each chosen disjoint disk, even if two original roots reduce to the same point.
The rational disk's lifted root is fixed by \(\operatorname{Gal}(D/\mathbb Q_p)\), while the roots in every exchanged pair of disks have degree two and generate \(D\).
An Eisenstein congruence at a different prime and a squarefree congruence at a third prime of factor degrees \(2,1,\ldots,1\) can be combined coefficientwise with this first neighborhood.
Taking \(L\) divisible by the combined modulus makes the needed correction to \(\prod_{j=1}^n(X-jL)\) independent of \(L\).
After rescaling, the correction tends to zero at the endpoints of fixed disjoint intervals bracketing the \(n\) simple real roots.
This preserves total reality while enforcing irreducibility and giving a transposition in the Galois group.
Primitivity in prime degree makes the graph of the conjugates of that transposition connected, and its edges generate \(S_n\).

Ramification of \(K\) at the auxiliary prime \(\ell\) makes it disjoint from the normal closure of this \(F\).
At \(p=2\) the choice \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2\ell})\) also ramifies at the prescribed prime.
Dirichlet's theorem gives relative norm-one unit rank at least \(n\) in the totally real field \(FK\).
Squares preserve rank and give totally positive units.
Each of the finitely many quadratic subfields contributes a rational subspace of rank at most one, so one may select a nontrivial unit \(x\) of degree greater than two outside them.
Its nonrational trace generates \(F\) by primality of \(n\), and \(x\notin F\) because relative norm one there would force \(x^2=1\).
Thus \(x\) generates \(FK\), and the normal closure has group \(S_n\times C_2\), where the second factor flips all reciprocal pairs.
Total positivity rules out torsion quotients between distinct conjugates.

The local discriminant valuation of \(D=\mathbb Q_p(\sqrt p)\) is one for odd \(p\), by its Eisenstein integral basis and discriminant \(4p\).
For \(D=\mathbb Q_2(i)\), \(1+i\) satisfies the Eisenstein polynomial \(X^2-2X+2\), so \(1,i\) is an integral basis of discriminant \(-4\).
Summing over the local factors gives exactly \((n-1)/2\) or \(n-1\), respectively, with a single degree-one summand at odd index.
As a separate dyadic check, \(X^3-31X+16\) is irreducible modulo three and has positive discriminant \(4\cdot28063\), with \(28063=7\cdot19\cdot211\equiv7\bmod8\).
It has a simple root modulo two at zero and its remaining quadratic factor over \(\mathbb Q_2\) has square class \(-1\) for its discriminant, since \(-28063\equiv1\bmod8\).
Thus even in a totally real \(S_3\) example the local algebra can be \(\mathbb Q_2\times\mathbb Q_2(i)\).
The submitted fraction \(1/n\) measures the trace-field embeddings at odd-index primes.
The examples allow ramification in \(FK/F\) at even-index primes and leave the Mahler measure unrestricted.
I found no defect in the submitted scope.
