## Independent proof finding

Both assertions hold over the ranges stated in v832.
At different poles, a valuation over 3 detects only the exponent involving 42, and a valuation over 2 then detects the exponent involving 56.
At one pole the corresponding valuation over 2 and the real logarithm of the absolute unit part give two linear equations in the relation exponents.
Their determinant, up to sign and a nonzero factor, is \(kn-tl\) for equal orientations and \(kn+tl\) for opposite orientations.
For fixed poles and conjugates a point in the source theorem's finite exceptional set fixes the powers of the rational constants by its 2-adic valuations, and then fixes both unit exponents by nontorsion.
The heights of all four pairs are bounded by a fixed multiple of \(T\), so arbitrary source tolerance gives a uniform little-oh bound.

The finite-place identity in the source unit is illustrated for ordinary units.
Here the two coordinates are algebraic integers and are units outside the selected places, which suffices for Proposition 2 and for the identity.
For integral differences the negative part of the local logarithm, taken with reversed sign, is the smaller ideal exponent times \(\log N\mathfrak p/[L:\mathbb Q]\).
The archimedean contributions after truncation have the same nonnegative sign.
Multiplying each difference by its conjugate-specific unit preserves every ideal exponent.
The rational norm is a product over the two conjugates, and the smaller of the two product valuations is bounded by the sum of the four pairwise minima, even if different branches contribute at the same rational prime.
The norm of a rational gcd extended to \(L\) is its \([L:\mathbb Q]\)-th power, including at ramified primes.

Each individual modulus divides its originating norm because it divides one factor resultant and their product is that norm.
A losing prime power can therefore be charged at its full exponent to the gcd of its originating norm with the winning norm.
At one prime and for one losing norm there is only one such charge, by within-norm coprimality.
The determinant separating distinct section slopes is \(a-b\), so with the batch fixed the sum of all charges is \(o_m(h)\).
Requiring \(h\) to be divisible by the finitely many residue orders reproduces each initial norm modulo \(p^{e_p}\) up to sign and fixes its valuation strictly below \(e_p\).
Removing the prescribed primes thus has bounded additional cost for the fixed past, and all surviving congruences descend by reduction.
The modulus one poses no obstruction, since the sole class modulo one satisfies the congruences and represents the unit group there.

For each fixed \(a\), the degree formula gives \(d/h\ge 2-o_a(1)\), so every factor eventually clears the search cutoff on the chosen progression.
The integer product test exactly compares the total loss with \(h\log(2)/m\), and the finite searches for factor and prime data, divisors and congruence witnesses terminate at each candidate.
Existence along the progression proves termination of the outer search for each fixed past.
Every individual loss is nonnegative and its ratio to degree is therefore at most \(\log(2)/m\) for the selected batch.
All its directions have \(a\ge m\) and slope at least \(h_m\), giving the hypotheses of the uniform archimedean estimate.
