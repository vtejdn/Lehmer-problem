For a circle conjugate \(\zeta_j\), the identity \(D_k(x_j)=2\Re(\zeta_j^k)\) gives \(0<2-D_k(x_j)\le4\), since a torsion conjugate would make the irreducible \(P\) cyclotomic.
The \(m\) trace roots are distinct, and reduction modulo the monic integral \(Q\) gives \(p(y)=g(y)Q'(y)\) at each such root \(y\).
Subtracting the corresponding simple fractions from \(p/Q\) leaves a polynomial tending to zero at infinity, so the coefficient \([x^{m-1}]p\) is the sum of the residues.
The \(m-1\) circular residues are \(-w_j\), with \(0<w_j<16\), while the exterior trace gives \((T+2)(X+X^{-1}-2)>4(X-2)\).
Minimality of \(k\) gives \(L\le X<\sigma L\le2L\), including the case \(X=L\).
Thus \(q>4X-16m+9>3L\) since \(L\ge32m\), and \(q<1+(9/2)X<10L\).

The Laurent expression \(z^{m-1}p(z+z^{-1})\) is an integral polynomial symmetric in formal degree \(2m-2\), with constant \(q-1\).
Multiplication by \(1-z\) gives an antisymmetric correction of degree at most \(2m-1\), so its contribution to \(zf+f^*\) vanishes, the degree and monicity of \(P\) are preserved, and \(f(0)=q\).
In the inverse criterion \(R=-p\) and \(Q'(x_j)=(x_j-T)H'(x_j)\), hence \(b_j=(T-x_j)w_j>0\).
Cancelling \(2+x_j>0\) in the endpoint sum leaves terms \((T-x_j)(2-D_k(x_j))<18\).
Their total is less than \(18m<q+T/2\).
These are the strict inward-motion and endpoint conditions of the criterion, so one root lies outside the unit circle and all others lie strictly inside.
Since \(f(1)=P(1)<0\), the unique exterior root is real and greater than one.
A monic integral factor missing that root would have nonzero integer constant of modulus less than one, proving irreducibility, and the product of the interior moduli gives \(q<\theta\).

At \(u=\theta+\theta^{-1}\) the root equation reads \(\theta(u-T)=(\theta-1)p(u)/H(u)\), where \(p/H=q-1+\sum_j b_j/(x-x_j)\).
Since \(\theta(u-T)=(\theta-1)^2-e\theta\), the excess over \(q+e\) is exactly \(e/(\theta-1)+\sum_j b_j/(u-x_j)>0\).
Every denominator exceeds \(q-2\), each \(b_j<72\) and \(e\le1/2\).
The resulting numerator bound \(1/2+72(m-1)<72m=36d\) verifies the strict uniform estimate.
With \(L=16d/\varepsilon\), the lower bound \(q>48d/\varepsilon\) gives \(q-2>36d/\varepsilon\) for \(d\ge4\) and \(0<\varepsilon\le1\).
The upper bound \(q<160d/\varepsilon\) also gives the claimed \(160d\) bound at \(\varepsilon=1\).

For \(L=16d\psi(d)\) the two norm bounds give exactly the stated window.
Along a Salem sequence tending to one the degrees escape every finite bound, since bounded degree and Mahler measure bound all the integer coefficients.
Thus \(e\to0\) and \(36d/(q-2)\to0\), with equal input and output degrees in each construction.
Conversely \(q\ge2\) permits the positive delay-one return of a Pisot seed.
Its unique real exterior root \(s>1\) satisfies \(s+s^{-1}-2\le(9/2)(\theta-q)\) by Boyd's inequality.
The exterior and reciprocal interior roots belong to the same irreducible factor because each factor has unit constant.
An integral reciprocal quadratic has trace at least three when its exterior root is positive, so convergence of the trace to two eventually forces Salem outputs.

Voutier's recorded theorem applies to the nontorsion \(\sigma\) of degree \(d\ge4\), for which \(d h(\sigma)=\log M(P)=\log\sigma\).
The bound \(e=2\cosh(\log\sigma)-2\ge(\log\sigma)^2>\tfrac1{16}(\log\log d/\log d)^6\) has positive right-hand side throughout this degree range.
With \(\varepsilon=e/d\in(0,1]\) the strict error divided by \(e\) is less than \(1/d\), and \(q<160d^2/e<2560d^2(\log d/\log\log d)^6\).
The cited earlier threshold applies to every prescribed integer \(q\ge(2d)^4(32\sqrt d)^d\) with relative error at most \(1/d\), whereas the trace-power construction selects one integer in each stated range.
