## The two sign matrices, trace generators, summed norms and family criteria

At 2026-11-25T01:12 UTC an agent outside the development of v105 independently reviewed its [full statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v105.md#statement), all three proof sections and the stated inputs for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed text without its development discussion.
Both collections have the claimed independent signatures, the height estimate is uniform on the moving index interval, and the norm budgets give both conditional positive gaps.

**Exact signs at distinct embeddings.**
The Salem conjugates consist of two positive real reciprocals and \(n-1\) inverse pairs on the unit circle.
The positive phases in \((0,\pi)\) give distinct trace embeddings because cosine is strictly decreasing there.
In the consecutive intervals for \(y_r\) the length is \(2\pi/[r(r-1)]>\pi/(2K^2)\), and the intervals for \(z_r\) have one quarter of that length.
Since \(1/K^2\ge c^{-2}\sqrt{\log(2n)/n}\), a single sufficiently small absolute \(c\) accommodates the short-arc half-width with room at both endpoints for every \(r\), in both collections.
Each such arc has an actual neutral phase since it avoids zero.

In fact the two independently chosen matrices can both be read explicitly with rows and columns in increasing order.
For the first matrix the interval at \(r\) lies below \(2\pi/j\) for \(j<r\), and above \(2\pi/j\) for \(j\ge r\).
Every interval remains below \(2\pi/K\le4\pi/j\), the earliest possible second crossing.
The monic trace polynomial is positive at \(2\), has simple roots and changes sign exactly once in the second case.
In the cosine matrix the same comparison uses the first crossing \(\pi/(2j)\), and \(j\theta<\pi\) throughout.
The negative-sign matrices thus both have entries \(1_{\{j\ge r\}}\) and determinant one over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Nonvanishing at all embeddings follows since a zero would make a conjugate of \(\alpha\) torsion.
The independence claims are separate for the two collections.

For a prime ideal dividing two of the cyclotomic trace values, lifting to the Salem root field gives a common reduced cyclotomic root, as the multiplicative factor relating the trace and ordinary cyclotomic values is a unit.
In residue characteristic \(p\), each root of \(\Phi_{p^a j}\) with \(p\nmid j\) has order exactly \(j\), including \(a=0\).
Indeed \(\Phi_j\) is separable modulo \(p\) with roots of order \(j\), and for \(a>0\) the cyclotomic quotient reduces to \(\Phi_j^{p^{a-1}(p-1)}\).
Equal prime-to-\(p\) parts for two indices would give an integral ratio at least two, excluded by the chosen interval.
This makes the principal ideals pairwise coprime at every finite prime ideal.

**Actual field of the power trace and uniform height.**
The power trace \(z_r\) is an integral polynomial in \(t\).
Fixing it requires the image of \(\alpha^r\) to equal \(\alpha^r\) or \(\alpha^{-r}\).
The unique conjugates of moduli \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) then force the image of \(\alpha\) to be one of the original reciprocal pair.
This fixes \(t\), giving \(\mathbb Q(z_r)=F\).
The discriminant estimate for reflected powers gives a normalized \(h\)-th Fourier coefficient bounded by \(O(\sqrt{h\log(2d)/d}+h/d)\) for \(h\le d\) when \(m\le1\).
For every fixed trigonometric approximation degree, all its nonconstant frequencies after multiplication by \(r\le2K\) therefore tend uniformly to zero in this estimate.
Approximation in supremum norm is valid for the continuous function \(\log^+|2\cos\theta|\) extended by zero at cosine zeros.
Its Haar mean is \(2\pi^{-1}\int_0^{\pi/3}\log(2\cos\theta)\,d\theta\) by reflection and half-period symmetry.
The two equal contributions per neutral trace are precisely its weight \(1/n\) after averaging over the \(2n\) phases.
At the real place the remaining correction is \(n^{-1}\log\cosh(rm)\), bounded by \(rm/n=o(1)\) uniformly.
There are no finite-place height terms for integral \(z_r\).

**Summing all finite norms.**
In the consecutive-power inequality at \(T\), the auxiliary weight satisfies \(E_a(T)/\binom T2\ge1/(4a)\) for \(a\le T/2\), since at least \(T/(4a)\) terms of its defining sum are at least \(T/2\).
The absolute height is \(m/(2n)\), giving \(n\sum_{a\le T/2}L_a/a\le4B_T\).
For a first-family value the relative degree two and the unit factor in \(\Phi_r(\alpha)\) give \(\log|N_F y_r|=nL_r\).
Since each \(r\le2K\), summation gives \(8K B_T\).
For the second collection \(\log|N_F z_r|=n\sum_{a\mid4r,\ a\nmid2r}L_a\) by the factorization of \(1+X^{2r}\).
A given \(a\) occurs at most \(\lfloor2K\gcd(a,4)/a\rfloor\) times, as \(a/\gcd(a,4)\) must divide \(r\).
All occurring \(a\) satisfy \(a\le8K\le T/2\), and positivity of the \(L_a\) yields \(32K B_T\).

At most \(s(F)\) members of the first independent family are units.
The other principal ideals have distinct prime-ideal divisors by coprimality, and selecting one for each charges at least \(\log Y\) against the first budget whenever its norm exceeds \(Y\).
This yields (3).
Pairwise coprimality is unnecessary in the second count because each \(z_r\) with any divisor of norm greater than \(Y\) incurs its own cost in the sum of logarithmic norms.
All remaining \(z_r\) are actual field generators, individually supported on the indicated primes, and every retained subset of their signatures stays independent.

**Asymptotics and the fixed-family implication.**
On a hypothetically shrinking sequence \(n\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness.
Writing \(L_0=\log(2n/m)\), the proposed integer \(T\) is asymptotic to \(\sqrt{nL_0/m}\), eventually exceeds \(16K\), and has \(\log T=O(L_0)\).
Both pieces of \(B_T\) are \(O(\sqrt{nmL_0})\).
Dividing by \(\sqrt{n\log(2n)}\) gives a quantity tending to zero, since \(m\log(2n/m)/\log(2n)=m+m\log(1/m)/\log(2n)\to0\).
For fixed \(\gamma,\delta>0\), the rank estimates then exceed \((1-\delta)K\), with heights at most \(H_*+\delta\) eventually for the second set.
Either eventual field condition excludes every shrinking sequence in that family, which establishes a positive uniform lower bound for its Salem measures.
The conclusion is conditional on the field hypothesis and does not give a numerical universal gap or exhibit a family satisfying it.
At the cyclotomic boundary the positive phase grid has spacing \(2\pi/p\) small enough for every selected interval.
A common residue root for \(\Phi_r,\Phi_p\) would require a prime-power integer ratio of their distinct indices, impossible for prime \(p\) and \(1<r<p\).
Thus all first-family ideals there may be unit ideals, consistent with both counts.
