## The cyclotomic compositum, local degrees and indexwise data

At 2026-11-24T21:08 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v33.md#statement) and all four proof sections of C0901 v33.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v33.
The inequalities (1), the splitting at every place over each prescribed prime, and the varying-data conclusion hold with the stated divisibility conditions.

For an arbitrary nonzero integer polynomial, factor out scalar content and the power of \(X\), and factor its primitive remaining part over \(\mathbb Z\).
Every integral factor has nonnegative logarithmic measure.
A scalar of absolute value at least two contributes at least \(\log2\), as does any remaining factor whose leading or constant coefficient has absolute value at least two, by the leading coefficient and root product bounds.
Cyclotomic factors and \(X\) have zero measure.
Thus any positive total measure below \(c/\varphi(N)\le c<\log2\) supplies a nontorsion unit factor of no greater measure, also for reducible or nonmonic initial inputs.
The all-power norm construction preserves this factor's total measure and unit property and gives a \(\delta\) with \(\mathbb Q(\delta^k)=K\) for every \(k\ge1\).
For \(\beta=\zeta_N\delta\), the element \(\beta^N=\delta^N\) generates \(K\), so \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) contains both \(\delta\) and \(\zeta_N\) and equals \(K(\zeta_N)\).
Its total measure equals \([K(\zeta_N):K]m(\alpha)\le\varphi(N)m(\alpha)\) because the twist preserves absolute height.

The strict threshold ensures both \(\delta\) and \(\beta\) have measures below the nonreciprocal bound \(c\).
Their reciprocity gives inversion automorphisms on \(K\) and \(L\).
Inversion on \(\beta\) sends \(\delta^N\) to \(\delta^{-N}\), so it restricts on \(K\) to inversion on \(\delta\) by equality on a generator.
Its action on \(\zeta_N=\beta/\delta\) is therefore inversion, irrespective of the intersection \(K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)\).
It fixes \(\beta+\beta^{-1}\) and is nontrivial, while \(\beta\) satisfies a quadratic over that trace field, proving \([L:F]=2,\ F=L^\tau\) and the norm-one identity.
The cyclotomic subfield and its maximal real subfield have the asserted inclusions because \(D\mid N\) and inversion restricts there to complex conjugation.
Taking a common multiple of conductors gives the finite list of abelian subfields by Kronecker–Weber, including containment in \(F\) for the prescribed totally real abelian fields.

The twelfth root \(w\) belongs to \(L\), its trace is \(\sqrt3\), and its difference from its conjugate under \(\tau\) is \(i\).
Both \(w\) and \(i\) generate the quadratic extension over \(F\).
For integral \(u\), the quotient \(y=(u-\tau u)/i\) is integral and fixed, and \(u-yw\) is fixed and integral as well.
Thus \(1,w\) form a relative integral basis of discriminant \(-1\), giving the unit relative discriminant ideal and unramifiedness at all finite primes.
This argument includes residue characteristic two, where the difference \(i\) remains a unit, and characteristic three.
At finite primes outside the prescribed set the theorem requires unramifiedness and leaves the splitting behavior unspecified.

For \(p\in S\), let \(t=3f_p\) and \(M=p^t-1\).
The factorization of \(X^M-1\) remains separable modulo every \(\mathfrak P\mid p\), so reduction is injective on \(\mu_M\).
If \(\tau\) fixed \(\mathfrak P\), its residue action on a primitive \(M\)-th root would be both inversion and a power of Frobenius.
Reducing that Frobenius exponent modulo \(t\) would force \(M\mid p^b+1\) for some \(0\le b<t\).
This is impossible since \(p^{t-1}+1<p^t-1\), including the smallest parameters \(p=2,t=3\).
The decomposition subgroup in \(L/F\) is therefore trivial at each \(\mathfrak P\).
In particular each \(\mathfrak p\mid p\) of \(F\) splits into two places and each completion \(L_{\mathfrak P}\) has degree one over \(F_{\mathfrak p}\).
The common residue field contains an element of exact order \(M\), and the order of \(p\) modulo \(M\) is exactly \(t\) since \(p^b-1<M\) for \(0<b<t\).
This proves \(3f_p\mid f(\mathfrak p/p)\) at every such \(\mathfrak p\), without an assumption that \(p\nmid N\) or a claim of exact absolute residue degree.

When \(a_p\ge1\), \(\Phi_{p^{a_p}}(1+X)\) is monic, is congruent to \(X^{\varphi(p^{a_p})}\) modulo \(p\), and has constant term \(p\).
Thus its local cyclotomic field is totally ramified of degree \(\varphi(p^{a_p})\), and it embeds in each \(L_{\mathfrak P}=F_{\mathfrak p}\) at the prescribed prime.
Multiplicativity proves \(\varphi(p^{a_p})\mid e(\mathfrak p/p)\).
At \(p=2,\ a_p=1\) this divisor is one, and \(a_p=0\) imposes no condition.
Increasing \(a_p\) can force an arbitrarily large numerical lower bound on the absolute index.
The asserted divisor is specifically \(p^{a_p-1}(p-1)\).
The exact absolute index and divisibility by an arbitrary additional prescribed integer go beyond this assertion.

Every member of the family supplies an integer polynomial for the unrestricted infimum, giving the upper inequality even if the family is empty.
Below \(c/\varphi(N)\), construction (3) bounds \(\lambda_{\mathcal D}\) by \(\varphi(N)\) times each positive input measure, while above or at that threshold the input already bounds \(\min(c,\lambda_{\mathcal D})/\varphi(N)\).
Taking the infimum over inputs gives the lower inequality, including the case \(\lambda_{\mathcal D}=+\infty\).
If \(\lambda=0\), at index \(j\) one may take a positive input below \(\min(c,\epsilon_j)/\varphi(N_j)\), which gives every required condition at that index and \(0<m(\beta_j)<\epsilon_j\).
The finite data may vary arbitrarily, including empty \(S_j\), with the fields and generators chosen separately for each index.
A restricted sequence with positive measures tending to zero conversely forces the unrestricted infimum to vanish.
