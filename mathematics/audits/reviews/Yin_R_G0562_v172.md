The two zero-mass energies are \(c_E-c_K\) and \(c_E\), respectively, since \(K\) and the unit circle lie in \(E\).
They are finite, and the nonnegative Dirichlet energy on zero-mass measures gives precisely the upper bound \(2c_E\).

Regularity of \(E\) suffices for the equilibrium potential identity even when its boundary has several components.

For an angular separation between \(1/(4s)\) and \(\pi/2\), the distance of the unit-circle point to the ray is at least \(\sin(1/(4s))\), and for obtuse separations it is one.
Thus the open disk of radius \(1/(8s)\) misses all the segments regardless of their lengths.
The test point on the outer circle is half a disk radius from its center, and Harnack's factor of three yields a center value at least \(1/(192s)\) on more than half the circle.
The resulting lower bound \(1/(384s)\) exceeds \(2/(1024s)\).

The comparison for the union uses a common exterior on which the logarithmic terms cancel at infinity.

The images in the proof have positive Lipschitz polar radii, so these regularity and exterior-domain hypotheses apply to their union as well.

Maximality of \(k\) excludes a collision of two squared roots, since opposite roots would force the irreducible unit polynomial \(A_0\) to be even.
