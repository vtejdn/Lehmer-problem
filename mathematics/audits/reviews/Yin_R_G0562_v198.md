For both signs and without comparability of the \(u_i\), the proof establishes the partition and rate (2), the uniqueness and estimates (1) when \(g=1\), and the cyclotomic degree bound with multiplicity.

On one root circle the minimum chord length is \(2p_i^{1/u_i}\sin(\pi/u_i)\) if \(u_i\ge2\).
For two different circles one may divide the logarithmic difference of the distinct integers \(p_i^{u_j}\) and \(p_j^{u_i}\) by \(u_i u_j\); integrating \(1/x\) between these integers supplies the bound \(1/(u_i u_j p^W)\).
Thus all centers are separated by at least \(\exp(-C_1W)\), and their logarithmic moduli are at least \((\log 2)/W\).
With \(0<c<(\log 2)/4\) fixed and \(n\ge CW^9\), the radius \(\delta=\exp(-cn/W^2)\) is less than half the separation and small enough that the closed disks lie in \(\log|z|\ge(\log 2)/(2W)\).
On a disk boundary every distance to a zero of \(A\) is at least \(\delta\), so \(\log|z^n A(z)|\ge n(\log 2)/(2W)-cnD/W^2\).
This exceeds the \(O(W)\) upper bound for \(\log|A^*(z)|\) on that boundary, uniformly after enlarging \(C\).
Rouché's theorem counts one root with multiplicity per disk, and the v194 upper bound \(D\) excludes any further exterior root.

In the disk of a given \(q_i\), the one nearby zero gives \(|q_i(z)|\le \delta\exp(O(W))\), whereas every other block has \(|q_j(z)|\ge\exp(-O(W^2))\) by the separation and its degree.
Since \(n/W^2\ge CW^7\), these bounds identify the unique minimizing block.
The radial error \(\big|\log|z|-(\log p_i)/u_i\big|\) is \(O(\delta)\), uniformly in the exponents.
The fixed primes bound \(L\), the blocks are distinct simple irreducible factors in \(A\), and \(A^*\) has no exterior zero, so all hypotheses of (2) of v194 hold.
Replacing each of the \(b_i\le u_i\) logarithms assigned to \(q_i\) by its center value and multiplying that estimate by \(d/(n\log p_i)\) gives the proposed bound on \(|b_i/u_i-d/n|\).

In particular \(d\le n+D\le2n\).
After multiplication by \(W^2\), the square of the middle error term is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(W^7(W^2+\log(4n))/n\).
Its maximum for \(n\ge CW^9\) is at the lower endpoint, where it equals \((1+W^{-2}\log(4CW^9))/C\) before the fixed multiple.
The other two terms after scaling are \(W^3\exp(-cn/W^2)\) and \(W^6/n\); all three tend uniformly to zero as \(C\) increases.
Every fraction is therefore within \(1/(3W^2)\) of \(d/n\), even if \(d\) is small.
Different fractions would be separated by at least \(1/(u_i u_j)>1/W^2\), so they coincide.
The denominator in lowest terms divides every \(u_i\), giving precisely the required form \(s/g\) with \(0\le s\le g\).

The constant term is a unit; Kronecker's theorem therefore forces an exterior root in each noncyclotomic irreducible factor.
Its part \(s\) is positive, and the simplicity of its exterior root excludes a repeated factor.
Each of the \(u_i\) disks belonging to \(q_i\) contains a noncyclotomic root, so summing the counts \(s u_i/g\) gives a partition of \(g\), allowing equal positive parts.
In one factor, replacing all exterior-root logarithms by their center logarithms costs \(O(D\delta)\) in total; the center sum is exactly \((s/g)\sum_i\log p_i\).
When \(g=1\), this gives a single factor and a positive uniform gap on enlarging \(C\).

For a torsion root of order \(m\), the exponents \(2\) for odd \(m\), \(m/2+2\) for \(m\equiv2\bmod4\), and \(1+m/2\) for \(4\mid m\) are units modulo \(m\) in the respective cases.
Applying the automorphisms to \(r(\zeta)=e\zeta^n\) gives the stated three equations, including the factor \(e(-1)^n\) in the negative squaring case.
The poles of \(r\) are simple, finite and nonzero, and composition with either signed square has only simple poles, whereas \(r^2\) has double poles.
Thus each of the first two equations yields a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(4D\) after denominators are cleared.
In the last case an odd \(u_i\) gives a positive pole whose negative is not a pole, since no other block has that radius.
This forces a nonidentity of cleared degree at most \(2D\).
If all \(u_i\) are even and their joint gcd with \(n\) is one, \(n\) is odd, and the required sign is impossible for the even function \(r\) at a nonzero finite value.
These counts give at most \(10D\) distinct torsion roots with joint gcd one.

On the circle \(z r_i'(z)/r_i(z)\) is the real number \(u_i(p_i^2-1)/|p_i-z^{u_i}|^2\), bounded by \(u_i(p_i+1)/(p_i-1)\le3u_i\).
A multiple root of \(z^n-e r(z)\) on the circle would require \(n\le3D\), excluded in the stated range.
For general \(h=\gcd(n,u_1,\ldots,u_k)\), the divided polynomial has block degree \(D/h\) and separating exponent \(n/h>3D/h\).
The torsion count for joint gcd one used only this last size condition for simplicity; it needs no ninth-power hypothesis after division.
Each torsion root downstairs has \(h\) distinct simple lifts, all torsion, and conversely every torsion root is such a lift.
Hence the cyclotomic degree is at most \(10D\), and at \(g=1\) it equals \(n+D-\deg S\).
