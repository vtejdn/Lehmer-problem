For a vanishing rational Laurent relation every complex embedding of the joint field preserves the equality.
Subtracting the vanishing sum divided by the largest term modulus, with coefficients kept outside this normalization, bounds the remaining sum of phases by \(\|c\|_1\min(1,T_\sigma)\).
Differences between logarithms of term moduli have the claimed span bound even for negative exponents.
Since \(\min(1,T)^2\le T\), averaging gives (4) from the two height formulas for \(x\) and \(x^{-1}\), including their nonnegative finite-place contributions.
Every pair ratio has height at most \(H\) and its own embeddings occur with equal multiplicity in the joint field.
For a nontorsion unit ratio L229 applies at frequency one and its increasing modulus can be evaluated at \(H\).
For a torsion ratio all primitive phases of its order occur equally often, giving the rational mean \(\mu(q)/\varphi(q)\).
Dividing the Gram estimate by \(\|c\|_2^2\) gives diagonal one and total absolute off-diagonal coefficient weight \(\rho-1\).

The prime-power factors of \(\varphi(q)^2/q\) give the lower bound \(1/2\), also for the unit order.

On the two rows of a graph, a same-row ratio is a nonzero power of nontorsion \(\alpha\).
Any cross-row torsion occurrence fixes the integer displacement \(j\), since two different displacements would give a nonzero torsion power of \(\alpha\).
There is at most one edge incident to each monomial, and all phase means on the paired edges have absolute value at most \(1/\varphi(q)\).
Summing both orientations via \(2|c_i c_j|\le c_i^2+c_j^2\) accounts for at most the entire diagonal weight.
Thus the normalized torsion contribution is at most \(1/\varphi(q)\), including an empty contribution if there are no edges.
Under strict (3) it exceeds one half, forcing \(q=1\) or \(2\) and \(\beta=\pm\alpha^j\), \(|j|\le e\).
For \(j<0\) multiplying by \(X^{-j}\) clears the equation, and for either sign of \(j\) the resulting polynomial has degree at most \(e+|j|\).
It is nonzero for the maps allowed in the statement.
Consequently \(d>2e\) rules out every such matching of order one or two, even when the coordinates are nonunits.

For a primitive degree-\(n\) minimal polynomial of positive leading coefficient \(a\), the discriminant contributes \((2n-2)\log a\) to the upper energy bound.
The radial factor from all ordered pairs is \(2(n-1)\sum_l t_l^+\).
Their sum is \(2(n-1)n h(\gamma)\) with the full primitive normalization.
The two reflection inequalities hold separately for every pair regardless of integrality.
The diagonal regularization contributes at most \(n\log(1/(1-r^2))\) for \(r=e^{-1/n}\), and the off-diagonal smoothing adds \(n(n-1)/n\).
Extracting the first Fourier term and dividing by \(n\) gives the square-root bound in (6), also when \(n=1\).
The average cost of replacing a reflected radius by one is at most \(2h(\gamma)\).
For a nontorsion unit the retained large-degree Dobrowolski theorem gives \(nu\gg1/\log^3(3n)\), with bounded degrees covered by finiteness and positivity.
If \(n<1/u\) substitute \(\log(3/u)\) in the denominator, and otherwise \(n\ge1/u\) already suffices.
In both cases \(n\gg1/[u(1+\log(1/u))^3]\).
The decreasing function \(\log(3n)/n\) then contributes \(O(u(1+\log(1/u))^4)\) under the square root.
The resulting bound (7) increases for sufficiently small positive \(u\), as required to use it at a common height \(H\).

Strict descent below \(\log2\) makes both minimal polynomials monic with unit constant term up to sign.
For \(j\ne0\) the degree loss from raising \(\alpha\) to \(j\) is at most \(|j|\), while absolute height grows by \(|j|\).
Thus signed powers cannot strictly decrease the positive total measure, and the exponent zero yields zero measure.
This exclusion holds for every rational-map degree.
For bounded effective support it forces \(H\) above a fixed positive threshold and, since \(h(\beta)\le L_\beta<L_\alpha\), gives \(e\gg_{s_0}d/L_\alpha\).
When \(e\le Kd\), the matching bound leaves one half to be supplied by the nontorsion and radial errors, with \(H<(K+1)L_\alpha\).
Using (7) gives precisely the displayed \(L_\alpha^{-1/2}/(1+\log(1/L_\alpha))^2\) lower bound up to a \(K\)-dependent constant.

For arbitrary coordinates a ratio of distinct graph monomials is \(S(\alpha)\) for \(S=X^j R^\delta\) of reduced degree at most \(2e\).
A rational constant ratio other than \(\pm1\) costs at least \(\log2\) in \(H\).
Otherwise each nontorsion ratio comes from a nonconstant \(S\), whose defining equation over the ratio field gives \(\deg S(\alpha)\ge d/(2e)\).
At \(d>2e\) inequality (6) applies with \(\log(3n)/n\le2(e/d)\log(3d/e)\).
Combining it with (4) and the torsion contribution at most one half gives (1) by squaring when \(H+(e/d)\log(3d/e)\le1\), and the other case follows by choosing the absolute constant at most one.
With \(L_\alpha\le B\) and \(t=e/d\to0\), the two extra costs in (1) tend to zero.
The same inequality forces a positive lower bound on \(t\) at the specified half-height threshold.
For bounded total measures, nonconstancy of \(R\) gives \(\deg\beta\ge d/e\) even for torsion output, hence \(H\le2Bt\).
On \(t<1/2\) the function \(t(2B+\log(3/t))\) is increasing.
At \(t=c_B/(\rho^2\log(2\rho))\) it is \(O_B(c_B(1+|\log c_B|))/\rho^2\) uniformly over \(\rho\ge1\).
Taking \(c_B\) small proves (2), also covering \(t\ge1/2\).
The reviewer finds the stated sparse criterion, strict descents in all degree ranges, and arbitrary-coordinate height and degree conclusions proved.
The two comparison results retain their respective bounded-total-measure and fixed-function hypotheses.
