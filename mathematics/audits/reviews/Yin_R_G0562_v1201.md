# Independent review of nonreal off-circle conjugates of a Mahler value

## Moved lines, two-label supports and the terminal coordinate

At 2026-11-25T03:38 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v141 independently reviewed for Yin its fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v141.md#statement) and all four proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or earlier findings on v141.
The terminal-coordinate conclusion for the divisible group and the exponential alternative for actual polynomial Mahler values follow, including the nonreciprocal scope.

For \(y=se^{i\theta}\ne0\), the imaginary part of \(y+y^{-1}\) is \((s-s^{-1})\sin\theta\).
Thus the trace count uses the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(y)\), with no hypothesis on the size of the trace field.
For a finite subfamily supporting \([x]\), individual smallness makes every attached chain degree uniformly large.
The combined decomposition uses these individual bounds for identifying coincident constituents; it does not require a bound on the sum of the input measures.
Projection from the conjugates of \([x]\) onto the orbit of an active component is equivariant and surjective.
Every real actual conjugate of \(x\) has class fixed by \(c\), and every neutral one has class negated by \(c\).
Hence every moved orbit vector needs a distinct nonreal off-circle conjugate.
This implication remains valid if torsion makes some actual conjugates project to the same vector.

For a component with some line moved, a scalar entry of \(v\wedge cv\) is a nonzero homogeneous quadratic evaluation on its orbit and vanishes on every unmoved vector.
Transitivity gives the orthogonal projector onto the quadratic evaluation space constant diagonal equal to its dimension divided by the orbit size \(N\).
A nonzero vector in that space has support at least \(N/\dim\), by applying the projected coordinate norm inequality at each nonzero entry and summing.
The dimension is at most \(t(t+1)/2\).
When every orbit line is fixed, the normal closure of \(c\) acts diagonally by signs on a basis selected from those lines.
Faithfulness on the attached leading space makes its normal image abelian, hence trivial in the label group with its unique nonabelian minimal normal subgroup.
The real-place and character argument of v140 then yields the independent signed case and just one coordinate: a full real oriented block is impossible, and the double flips in the independent model distinguish all coordinate characters.
This application uses only the given projection orbit.
Consequently any noncoordinate with \(N\ge\binom b3\) gives \(k(x)\gg b/u^2\) since \(t\le ub\).

For \(N<\binom b3\), a signed block with \(3\) to \(b-3\) supported labels contradicts subset transitivity, and support larger than \(b-3\) gives exponentially many sign patterns by the supported even flips.
In an oriented block lacking a color of multiplicity at least \(b-2\), a suitable union of color classes has size in \([3,b-3]\): either one class does, or classes of size at most two can be accumulated to reach three or four.
The independent ordinary alternating action again rules this out.
The resulting exceptional entries relative to a majority of size at least \(b-2\) are unambiguous at large \(b\), and their positions are invariant under sign reversal.
If there are at least three marks in total, variation in two blocks with two and one marks yields at least \(\binom b2 b\) position sets; variation in three nonempty blocks gives at least \(b^3\).
Zero marks mean the zero vector, and one mark gives a coordinate line, including after subtracting the block mean in the oriented model.
This leaves exactly two marks for a noncoordinate vector.

If any label \(i\) is moved, transport an occupied block to its block and put one mark at \(i\).
Within a single block double transitivity lets the other mark run through all positions except \(i,c(i)\) when present.
If the two marks are in different blocks, their positions can be prescribed independently by supported actions with at most the same exclusions.
The resulting at least \(b-2\) distinct position sets each contain \(i\) but not \(c(i)\), so changing the overall sign cannot undo their movement.
If no label moves, the model is independent signed by the real-block argument, and a real pair exists by nontorsion and reciprocity.
The total number of real labels is \(O(\sqrt{d\log(3d)})=o(b)\) for \(d=2ub\) at bounded measure.
In either position pattern one can fix a mark at a real label and place the second at \(b-o(b)\) neutral positions in its own block.
Their respective \(c\)-signs are opposite, proving movement of each line.

For \(m(z)\le m\) the degree inversion of v89 has \(t=\log d>A\log A\) for \(A=(4m)^{-1/3}\) at sufficiently small \(m\).
Since \(\log(b/u^2)=t-3\log u-\log2\ge t-O(\log(t+\log3))\), its lower asymptotic coefficient on the scale \(m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\) is still \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\).
The strict margin to the chosen \(\eta\) makes \(b/u^2\) divided by the threshold tend uniformly to infinity.
Below that threshold each active projection must therefore be a coordinate.
Its signed orbit represents all labels with two distinct orientations; different labels have different lines also in the sum-zero model for \(b\ge3\).
There are at most \(k(x)=o(b)\) moved labels per attached term.
In an oriented term a block exchange would move at least \(b\) labels, and a fixed block of positive sign would give \(b-o(b)\) real labels, contradicting the real-place estimate.
So \(c\) exchanges the halves of every block, making each actual half product conjugate to its inverse under \(c\).
All of their conjugates are such products, hence they are torsion by Kronecker.
This makes the oriented term terminal, as is already true in the independent model.

For distinct active terminal terms, first send the coordinate of some term other than a largest-degree one to a nonneutral pair.
If its label is \(c\)-fixed it is a real pair, giving a fixed nonzero coordinate with positive sign; otherwise its line is already moved.
The other terms are unmatched and have degree no larger than that largest degree.
The relative alternating theorem permits every within-block position for the coordinate of the largest term while fixing all the other active splitting fields, including at tied degrees.
These permutations lift also in a common larger field containing \(x\).
When the second line is moved all these conjugate classes are moved.
When it is fixed with positive sign, all but \(o(b)\) positions of the first coordinate either have moved line or have negative sign at a neutral pair.
In neither case can \(c\) fix or negate the full vector, and the first projection distinguishes the resulting classes.
This contradicts \(k(x)=o(b)\), proving the single active terminal line.

For \(A=M(P)\) sufficiently close to one all positive factors are monic units after removing signs, and \(A\) is a real positive unit in the combined group.
A terminal oriented coordinate cannot be fixed by \(c\): every block sign is negative, and no projected coordinate line of another label represents the same signed vector.
In the remaining independent case the coordinate match and the factor argument of v140 give \(A=\rho^k=M(\rho)^eB\), \(k=e,\ B\ge1\).
Since \(M(\rho)\ge\rho>1\), one has \(B=1,\ M(\rho)=\rho\); the small reciprocal primitive generator of large degree is Salem, as is its power \(A\).
The argument up to this conclusion requires neither reciprocity of \(A\) nor a bound on its second iterate.
Only the final conversion to a count of nonreal exterior conjugates alone uses reciprocity, by the bijection with interior conjugates under inversion.
