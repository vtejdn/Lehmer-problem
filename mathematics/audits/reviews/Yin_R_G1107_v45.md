## The integral cutoff, finite test and rational quartic

The integral bound holds uniformly in the parent parameter and in the height of the perturbation.
I checked that \(r=p-2s\) gives the factor \((-1)^{j+1}\sin(s\theta_j)\) in \(B_m(b_j)\) and that \(B_n'(b_j)\) has sign \((-1)^{j+1}\).
Thus \(W=\sqrt{2+t}\,t^2E_{s-1}^2\) is nonnegative, and a negative block of length \(\kappa\) costs at least \(\kappa-1\) distinct zeros of \(H\).
The zero polynomial cannot give a negative residue.
Under the proposed lower bound on \(p\), all nodes in \([-1,1]\) are initial, because \(j\le p/3\le n-h-1\).
The trace spacing within each parity is at most \(\delta=8\pi/p\), and \([\pi/3,\pi/2]\) contains a node of each parity within one parity spacing of \(\pi/2\); neither trace equals zero.

On this central interval the bound \(W(t)\le(4/\sqrt3)t^2\) follows from the lower bounds on the two sine factors in the denominator.
The cosine coefficient bound and \(|\sin(k\theta)|\le k\sin\theta\) give the claimed first and second derivative bounds, also for \(h=0\).
Near a maximizer of \(|H|\), the even-node upper bound on \(H\) or the odd-node lower bound on \(H\), according to its sign at the maximizer, gives \(\|H\|\le3+\delta A_h\|H\|\) and hence \(\|H\|\le6\).
At the two small positive nodes the opposite one-sided bounds imply \(|H(0)|\le3\delta^2+6A_h\delta<1\).
After integrality gives \(H(0)=0\), division by the positive trace and the Taylor remainder give \(|H'(0)|\le3(1+A_h^2)\delta<1\), hence \(t^2\mid Q\).
Both strict estimates follow already from \(\delta\le1/[16(1+A_h)^2]\).

I checked the root-count interface separately: the coefficients \(1,1,4\) of the neutral lift give zero quotient shift and \([t^{n-1}](Q-tB_n)=4-(n+1)+(n-1)=2\).
Here \(h\le n-2\), so \(H\) changes neither coefficient, and the remainder is integral.
The strict cut and strict endpoint therefore meet the hypotheses of the integral all-cut theorem.
Its \(n-1\) disjoint real-root intervals and residual nonreal pair exhaust the degree including multiplicities; in particular a double root at zero is impossible.

For the finite test the recession signs at \(b_c,b_{c+1}\) coincide, and the required sign at \(-2\) is opposite to the one at \(b_n\).
After omission of \(b_c\) the \(n\) interpolation denominators alternate in sign, including when \(c=1\) or \(c=n-1\).
The coefficient of degree \(n-1\) thus vanishes as a sum of like weak signs, forcing a recession polynomial of degree at most \(n-2\) to vanish identically.
Normalizing an unbounded feasible sequence would give such a polynomial of coefficient norm one, so the closed feasible set is bounded or empty.
Its real algebraic inequalities suffice for exact coefficient bounds and finite strict testing in the stated \(h\le n-2\) domain.
No enumeration of survivors is asserted or needed for this conclusion.

For the rational construction, coprimality and odd \(p\) exclude every grid zero of \(D\).
I checked \(2\sin(\pi/(2p))<4/p<2\sin(3\pi/(2p))\) and \(4/p<1/s\); the last node alone then has negative residue and the endpoint is \(-4s+4s^2(4/p)<0\).
At \(s=2\) this includes \(p=13\), gives \(D=t^4\) and leaves \(t^3\mid Q\).
The integral root-count theorem was not applied to these rational polynomials.

## The real degree obstruction and its sharp cubic

The lower bound uses the strict residues alone.
At \(b_n=-2\cos u\), \(u=\pi/p\), the expression for \(W\) has denominator \(\sin u\cos(u/2)\le u\); \(su<\pi/4\), \(\sin(su)\ge2su/\pi\) and \(4\cos^2u\ge1\) give \(W(b_n)\ge4s^2/(\pi p)\).
For fixed \(s\), the same expression is asymptotic to \(4s^2\pi/p\).
If \(s\) grows, the \(h+1\) chosen interior sine zeros approach separated angles, and the two adjacent initial nodes at each are of opposite parity even when the zero is itself a grid node.
The one-sided bounds then give an intermediate trace with \(|H|\le C_hs^2/p^2\) by continuity.
Their separated limits bound all Lagrange weights at \(b_n\) uniformly, in contradiction with the last-node lower bound.

For fixed \(s\) and a zero \(x\) of multiplicity \(m_x\), the separated pairs at distance \(O(1/p)\) from \(x\) produce \(h+1\) intermediate traces where \(H=O(p^{-m_x})\).
The derivative of \(2\cos\theta\) is nonzero near each relevant angle, so the scaled interpolation points \(p(t-x)\) remain bounded and mutually separated.
The coefficients of the Lagrange basis at these scaled points are bounded, so \(H^{(k)}(x)/(k!p^k)=O(p^{-m_x})\), with constants permitted to depend on \(s,h\).
The largest coefficient norm of \(H\) is at least a constant times \(1/p\) by the last-node inequality.
Any unit-norm subsequential limit consequently vanishes to order at least \(m_x-1\) at every such \(x\).
Counting the \(s-1\) simple zeros of \(E_{s-1}\) and its overlap with zero exactly for even \(s\) gives \(h\ge s\) for odd \(s\) and \(h\ge s+1\) for even \(s\).
Since an unbounded set of conductors with bounded \(s\) contains an unbounded constant-\(s\) subsequence, the universal lower threshold is three.

For \(p=8a+1\ge25\), \(s=2\) and \(H=-7t^3/p\), division of the residue sign expression by \(|b_j|^3\) leaves a baseline \(f(b_j)=|b_j|\sqrt{2+b_j}\).
The final even negative trace satisfies \(f(b_n)<2\pi/p\); the only earlier nodes needing a strict lower bound on \(f\) are odd positive traces and even negative traces.
On the positive side the smallest relevant trace is \(2\sin(5\pi/(2p))\ge10/p\), and the extra square root exceeds one.
On the negative side \(-t\sqrt{2+t}\) has its only stationary point at \(-4/3\), a maximum; its values at \(b_{2a+2}\) and \(b_{n-2}\) are at least \(14/p\) and \(10/p\), respectively.
These estimates hold already at \(p=25\).
The common endpoint identity gives \(-8+56/p<0\).
The separate degree-\(2s\) construction also has the claimed endpoint, and \(p\equiv1\bmod 2s\) supplies arbitrarily large admissible coprime conductors.
I found no gap in either ledger's stated result; the exact minimum for each \(s>2\) and the existence of Lehmer counterexamples are not decided.
