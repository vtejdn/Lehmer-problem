## Atom test and circle-norm bound

The atom test and the circle-norm lower bound hold with the stated strict inequalities.
The objects are actual square-constant returns of increasing even degree for one fixed Salem number.
The full neutral lifts are positive, and the fixed-input limit theorem gives the positive singular part \(\eta\) on the indicated finite set of atoms, with mass \(\delta<1/4\).
Removing the old conjugates leaves a positive measure, so for any sequence of twists of the doubled new lift every weak limit \(\theta\) satisfies \(|\theta|\le h+2\eta\).
Indeed integration against a continuous function \(f\) bounds \(\left|\int f\,d\theta\right|\) by \(\int|f|\,d(h+2\eta)\).
This gives both \(|H|\le1\) for the Haar density and \(|\theta_{\rm sing}|\le2\eta\).

Put \(q_*=z^{-s}P_0\).
Inversion symmetry of the untwisted lift gives an integer for \(\int z^n q_*\,d\lambda_d\) at every \(n\), including \(n=0\) with the doubled constant moment.
An integral Laurent multiple of \(P_0\) is \(q_*\) times an integral Laurent polynomial, by monicity after clearing powers of \(z\).
Thus all its shifted tests are integer linear combinations of those moments, even for a nonsymmetric multiplier.
They remain integers on taking a weak limit of twists.
For each \(S_i\), the singular part of \(S_i\theta\) has variation at most \(2\int |S_i|\,d\eta<1\).
The Fourier coefficients of its absolutely continuous part tend to zero, so integrality forces the Fourier coefficients of the whole measure to vanish in both tails.
Fourier uniqueness gives \(S_i\theta=D_i h\) for an integral Laurent polynomial \(D_i\).

The rational Laurent Bézout identity combines these equalities to give \(P_0\theta_{\rm sing}=0\) and \(P_0H=D\) almost everywhere with \(D\) rational Laurent.
The bound \(|D|\le |P_0|\) extends to the whole circle, forcing \(D\) to vanish at a neutral Salem conjugate.
Clearing a monomial and using rational irreducibility gives \(D/P_0\in\mathbb Q[z^{\pm1}]\), and \(S_1H=D_1\) with \(S_1\) primitive clears all scalar denominators by Gauss's lemma.
Parseval and \(|H|\le1\) now force \(H=0\) or a signed Laurent monomial.

If infinitely many degrees had even one frequency for which the required rounding failed, selecting one such frequency at each degree and taking a weak limit of the twists would give a contradiction.
In fact every fixed shift \(u\) then has a real limiting moment within \(2\delta<1/2\) of the integer \(\int z^uH\,dh\).
The same extraction gives the correct nearest integers eventually for all the finitely many shifts in the support of \(P_0\) at once.
If (2) failed along selected frequencies, its integer left side would converge on this extraction to \(\int P_0\,d\theta_{\rm sing}=0\), contradicting the failure.
This argument uses the signs and joint limits of the errors and requires no bound on their sum of absolute values.
The resulting convolution is invertible on the finite group of each return because \(P_0\) has no torsion zero.
All coefficients of \(\lambda_d\) are therefore in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
The support is nonempty for sufficiently large \(d\), so positivity gives total mass one.
The modulus-one Fourier coefficients detect the annihilator of the subgroup generated by support ratios, giving exactly uniform probability on one coset.
Inversion symmetry and exclusion of both \(1\) and \(-1\) give \(z^{2k}=-1\).
Restoring the trace normalization gives \(1/(2k)\) at each new trace, and \(k=d/2-s\to\infty\), so only the fixed old conjugates can carry the excess mass in this limit.

For the remaining parity the new-node interpolation congruence reduces to \((k-1)+k\equiv0\bmod2\), which fails at every \(k\ge2\).
A liminf below \(1/2+1/(2B)\) gives a bounded subsequence with a full weak limit of mass strictly below \(3/4\) because \(B\ge2\).
Each test satisfies \(\int |S_i|\,d\eta\le B\delta<1/2\), contradicting this congruence.
This also yields the infimum bound over the stated families of fixed inputs, with the onset allowed to depend on the input.
For \(S_1=P_0\), alternation of \(C_s\) shows that the trace norm of any monic polynomial of degree \(s\) is at least two on \([-2,2]\).
In the example the trace discriminant is \(33\) and the smaller real trace gives a negative quadratic discriminant, proving degree four with exactly one exterior root.
The constant \(-6\) is two modulo four and the linear coefficient \(-3\) is odd.
The coefficients of \(P_0\) are \(1,-3,-4,-3,1\), and the trace values at \(-2,3/2,2\) are \(4,-33/4,-8\).
They give coefficient length \(12\), circle norm \(33/4\), and the improved bound \(37/66\).
Strictness in the atom test and the fixed-input weak limit are both used in this review.
