# Review of integral positive averages and signed averages of Salem power traces

## Fiber estimates, loss of measure under degree descent and the signed convex hull

At 2026-11-25T02:47 UTC an agent outside the development of v127 independently reviewed its [full statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v127.md#statement), all three proof sections and their fixed inputs for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed version without its development discussion.
The uniform lower bound on positive integral averages, their actual occurrence over each specified subfield and the exact signed rational representation all hold.

**Arithmetic and Fourier bounds on individual fibers.**
A Salem number has distinct conjugates modulo torsion, because transporting a pair with torsion quotient to its unique exterior root would violate strict dominance.
The fibers of the trace embeddings above the \(u\) embeddings of \(E\) are ordinary systems of blocks of size \(b\).
A minimal nontrivial block inside one fiber exists by finite descent and its translates cover that fiber by transitivity of the fiber stabilizer.
The unique canonical system therefore refines these fibers.
The bound on the number of canonical blocks gives \(u\ll_M(\log(3n))^{212}\), so \(b=n/u\) tends to infinity uniformly over the eligible subfields.

Adjoining both inverse lifts of every trace in a fiber gives a monic polynomial with coefficients obtained by embedding algebraic integers of \(E\).
Consequently the product of its nonzero absolute discriminants over all \(u\) embeddings is at least one, even if individual discriminants have absolute value less than one.
Using the reflected two-point inequality and expanding the logarithm after scaling the unit disk by \(\rho=e^{-1/(2b)}\) leaves, within each fiber, the negative log of that discriminant, a radial error \(2(2b-1)\sum_{j\in\tau}t_j^+\), a term \(2b(2b-1)\log(1/\rho)\) and a diagonal error bounded by \(2b\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\).
Summing over fibers therefore gives (3), because the global positive radial sum is \(m\) and the discriminant terms now have nonpositive sum.
For \(h\le b\) one has \(\rho^{2h}\ge e^{-1}\), making each reflected fiber sum \(O(\sqrt{h b(m+u\log(3b))})\).
Only the distinguished exterior conjugate changes upon reflection, causing the exact difference \(\alpha^h-\alpha^{-h}=2\sinh(hm)\).
The sum of the actual powers over \(2b\) roots is \(b\tau(J_h)\), without a factor of two in that normalization.
This gives (4).
At arbitrary \(h\) all nondistinguished averages lie strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), and the distinguished one exceeds \(e^{hm}/b-2\) since the other \(b-1\) pair traces are at least \(-2\).

**Positivity, growth and actual attainment.**
For \(hm\le2u\log b\), Voutier's bound on the original Salem number gives \(h\ll u(\log(3n))^4\), which is eventually at most \(b\).
The error in the nondistinguished averages is at most \(O_M(b^{-1/2}u(\log(3n))^{5/2})=b^{-1/2+o_M(1)}\).
This estimate is uniform in the choice of \(h,E\).
At larger exponents the distinguished coordinate exceeds \(b^{2u-1}-2\).
If \(Y^*\le b^u\), positivity and the weight budget at most one consequently bound the total weight at the larger exponents by \((b^u+2)/b^{2u-1}=O(b^{1-u})\).
The remaining nondistinguished coordinates of the mixture are bounded by \(b^{-1/2+o_M(1)}\), since \(u\ge2\).
For a nonzero integral mixture its nonzero integer norm then implies \(|Y^*|\ge b^{(u-1)(1/2-o_M(1))}\).
The distinguished value is greater than \(-2\), so this bound gives the required positive growth.
The case \(Y^*>b^u\) directly exceeds the same threshold.
In both cases the other coordinates have absolute value strictly less than two.
The sole exterior trace embedding forces generation of \(E\).
The discriminant \(Y^2-4\) is negative at another real place, giving a quadratic extension of \(E\), a Salem root of degree \(2u\) and \(\log\eta\ge\log Y^*-\log2\).
As \((u-1)\log b\to\infty\) uniformly, the last constant is absorbed by the stated error.

For the separate existence assertion the unit \(\alpha\) has a finite positive period modulo \(b\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)}\).
For every multiple of that period \(s_h=(\alpha^h+\alpha^{-h}-2)/b\) belongs to \(\mathcal O_B\), by integrality and intersection with the trace field.
Then \(J_h=2+\operatorname{Tr}_{B/E}(s_h)\) is integral.
Along these multiples the unique distinguished value tends to infinity and the others stay strictly within the trace interval.
This gives infinitely many distinct integral Salem traces in the original fixed subfield, even without the asymptotic estimates.

**Signed representations with unrestricted exponents.**
One element from each neutral inverse pair gives an independent family modulo torsion.
In a putative relation, sending one selected root to \(\alpha\) keeps every other selected root neutral since they are on distinct labels.
Absolute values then force that exponent to vanish, and this applies to every index.
For every nonconstant integer character the arithmetic mean of its values on successive powers tends to zero by the finite geometric sum formula.
Trigonometric approximation gives uniform distribution of the powers on the full torus.
The nondistinguished trace fibers use disjoint groups of these angles, and twice the average of their cosines maps their product onto the whole closed box \([-2,2]^{u-1}\).
Every open target box is visited at arbitrarily large exponents, while the distinguished coordinate independently tends to positive infinity.

To reach a prescribed real distinguished coordinate \(s\) in the closure, take \(v_h\) with other coordinates approaching the chosen box point and take a much larger distinguished coordinate \(v_k^*\) with \(v_k^*/v_h^*\to\infty\).
The convex combination of \(v_h\) and \(-v_k\) with weight \((v_h^*-s)/(v_h^*+v_k^*)\) on the second point has first coordinate exactly \(s\).
This weight tends to zero, so all the other coordinates converge to the target.
The convex hull of the signed vectors and zero consequently contains the interior of the full slab, by approximating vertices of a simplex strictly containing each interior point.
No finite combination reaches either boundary in a nondistinguished coordinate.
For a target coming from \(E\), reduce a finite representation to at most \(u+1\) affinely independent vertices.
The real Minkowski embedding is an invertible change of coordinates from any rational basis of \(E\), so solving for these unique barycentric weights in the rational basis gives rational coefficients.
Combining signs retains the total absolute-weight budget at most one and proves (2) in both directions.
The construction does not bound the exponents or independently produce small lower-field units.
