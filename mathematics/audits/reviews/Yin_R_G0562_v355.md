### Interpolation and division

At any zero \(\alpha\) of a witness, vanishing of \(V(\beta)\) would force \(U(\beta)=0\) and give a common irreducible factor.
Thus the quotient \(-U(\beta)/V(\beta)\) recovers \(\alpha\).
Conversely, under field equality the \(D\) conjugates of \(\beta\) are the \(p\)-th powers of the \(D\) conjugates of \(\alpha\).
They occur in inverse pairs, with no fixed point because a conjugate equal to \(1\) or \(-1\) would make \(\alpha\) torsion.
Their monic polynomial \(Q\) consequently has constant coefficient one and equals its reversal.
Clearing denominators in the rational power basis of \(\beta\) supplies \(F(\beta)=a\alpha\) with \(\deg F<D\).
Conjugating gives the same identity at the inverse pair, so both the forward and reversed copies of \(H\) vanish at \(\alpha\).
The exponents in \(H\) lie between \(p\) and \(\max(pD,p+1)<T\), leaving the endpoints of \(Q(x^p)(1+x^{2p+1})\) unaltered.
Reflection about \(T\equiv1\bmod p\) exchanges the two occupied classes even at \(p=2\).

The row of residue zero is \(U_0(X)=Q(X)+XF(X)-aX^{D+1}\).
Its value at \(\beta\) is nonzero because \(pD-1>0\) and \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
The opposite row is monic of degree \(D+2\), hence its monic rational gcd with \(U_0\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z[X]\) by Gauss's lemma.
This gcd has constant term \(\pm1\) by integral division of \(U_0(0)=1\).
At each nonzero \(z\), the common multiplicity of the rows is the minimum of their orders of vanishing, and reversal preserves that minimum at \(z^{-1}\).
The gcd is therefore reciprocal up to sign.
Dividing both rows by it retains the factor \(P\), since the divisor is nonzero at \(\beta\).
The resulting rows are integral, nonzero and coprime, and the quotient is signed reciprocal, monic, of constant term \(\pm1\) and degree \(T-p\deg A\).
For the record application, distinct \(p\)-th powers of the conjugates give degree \(D\) for \(\beta\), which suffices for field equality.

### Cost for any witness in the degree range

Writing \(\deg J=bp+1\) gives \(b\le D+2\).
The reversal identity is \(V=\sigma X^{b-n}f^*\) for \(f=U\) and \(n=\deg f\), and \(f(0)=\pm1\).
Thus \(\gcd(f,f^*)=1\), and the offset \(S=(b-n)p+1\) is positive and prime to \(p\).
If \(f\) were constant it would be a unit and every zero of the resulting signed binomial would be torsion.

For the [angular inequality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#two), the circle map \(\Phi=(-\sigma f/f^*)^p/X^S\) has no poles or zeros on the circle.
Each unit target has at most \(pn+S\) preimages, by lifting to the roots of its degree-\(pn+S\) twisted diagonal using \(\gcd(p,S)=1\).
The absolute angular derivative has logarithmic mean \(m(K)-2m(f)\) with \(K=pX(f'(f^*)-f(f^*)')-Sff^*\).
Since \(|K(0)|=S|\operatorname{lc}f|>0\), Jensen's formula gives \(m(K)\ge\log(S|\operatorname{lc}f|)\), and the isolated circle zeros of \(K\) have integrable logarithms.
Angular change of variables against \((2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\), followed by Jensen's inequality, bounds the loss \(L=\int_{\mathbb T}\log(2/|1-\Phi|)\,d\mu\) by
\[
 L/2\le \log\!\big(C_{1/2}(pn+S)\big)+2m(f)-m(K).
\]
The product on each \(x^p=X\) fiber gives \(p(m(J)-m(f))=\log 2-L\).
The nonconstant coprime seed cannot equal its signed reversal, so the [whole-polynomial consequence of Smyth's theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) gives \(m(f)\ge\log\theta\).
The cited [source statement](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) has the required unnormalized measure and irreducible monic hypotheses, which the factor reduction supplies whenever \(M(f)<\theta<2\).
This review used that established theorem and its recorded source inspection.

The coefficient of \(m(f)\) is \(1-4/p>0\).
Using \(pn/S\le p(D+2)\), \(|\operatorname{lc}f|\ge1\) and \(m(J/P)=m(J)-m(P)\) gives precisely the claimed finite inequality.
The error \(\log(1+p(D+2))/p\) vanishes when \(p\to\infty\) and \(\log(2D)=o(p)\).
The resulting positive lower limit assumes \(m(P)\to0\) and applies to the product of the extra factors.
