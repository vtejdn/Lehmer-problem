## Four relative embeddings determine every iterate without requiring disjoint trace fields

At 2026-11-25T04:44 UTC a reviewer outside the development of [C0901 v157](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v157.md#statement) checked all three sections for Yin.
The fixed SHA256 was `D5053A752970FF63B61E6BEF41C94A857E5D861B5311DABD91608F1F65E97D52`.
The formula for arbitrary positive exponents, every orbit alternative and the conditional near-one exclusions follow at the stated scope.

Every nonzero power of a Salem number generates the same marked root field, since any collision of powers of distinct conjugates could be transported to a second conjugate of maximal modulus.
In one common root field the Salem involutions fix the same maximal totally real subfield, and both positive generators are positive integral powers of the unit on its primitive norm-one line.
Their product is again Salem and fixed.
For different root fields, each remains quadratic on extending its trace base to the totally real compositum \(F\); the original field with a nonreal embedding cannot lie in a totally real field.

For a fixed marked Salem trace field, the split places after this base change are precisely the embeddings of \(F\) restricting to its marked embedding.
This follows also by the unique positive conjugate of the trace discriminant.
If the two relative quadratic extensions over \(F\) coincided, these real-place sets would be identical.
Pulling them back to the absolute Galois group gives equality of the pointwise stabilizers of the two original embedded trace fields.
Taking fixed fields then gives equality of the trace fields themselves, in which case there was no base change and equality of the two quadratic extensions contradicts the hypothesis.
Hence the distinct relative quadratic extensions give degree four, including for nontrivial intersections and for equal trace fields with distinct root fields.
Above each embedding of \(F\) their four signed branch choices are independent because the relative Galois group is the full product of the two order-two groups.
The field generated by \(x^sy^t\) is the whole compositum: fixing its positive maximal modulus forces the individual maxima and hence both individual powers, which still generate the two root fields.
Thus the four lifts are counted with the correct absolute multiplicity in computing the measure of the actual product.

The only embedding simultaneously marked for both trace fields fixes their generators and hence the whole compositum \(F\).
There are \(k_x-1\) further embeddings marked only for the first, and \(k_y-1\) marked only for the second.
On each single-mark fiber the four possible logs are the signed marked log repeated twice; on the double-mark fiber the positive parts of the four sums add to twice the larger marked log.
All unmarked fibers have zero log modulus.
Summing proves (1) without any use of the degree of an intersection as a replacement for either actual relative index.

If both indices are at least two, every positive log contribution in the two-factor iteration at least doubles at each step, so the orbit diverges.
If both are one, the next value is a single Salem power and is fixed.
For a proper inclusion with indices \(1,k\), the branch \(U\le V\) immediately leaves only the power \(y^{2kt}\).
On \(U>V\) the update divides the ratio \(U/V\) by \(k-1\) while both exponents remain positive integers.
For \(k=2\) that ratio stays fixed and growth persists; for \(k>2\) it reaches the other branch within the indicated ceiling number of steps.
This covers all iterates including the last update after the ratio threshold, and exchanging the roles gives the other inclusion.
An equality of powers between the two distinct-field factors cannot occur by the full-degree observation; the formula still handles the boundary without ambiguity.

When both indices are at most two the two power-stable inputs have absolute degrees \(2n/k_x,2n/k_y\) and joint degree \(4n\), for \(n=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
They are multiplicatively independent since equality of nonzero powers would equate the root fields.
The joint two-unit bound then gives a product of logarithmic measures at least an absolute constant times \(n/(\log(12n))^{212}\).
On a putative shrinking sequence bounded-degree finiteness forces \(n\to\infty\), contradicting this estimate.
Conversely the putative low second-iterate inequality in the explicit formula would give \((k_x-1)U+(k_y-1)V\le\min(U,V)\).
It forces both indices at most two, so that alternative is impossible uniformly at sufficiently small first log measure.
Applying v120 to the product of the two minimum polynomials proves the stated exponential lower bound.
For distinct root fields, eventual stabilization at such small values requires and suffices that one marked trace field be properly contained in the other with index greater than two.
Even in those cases an individual orbit may stabilize at a large Salem power without a common bound on the eventual values across different inputs.

Finally each small individual Salem value, being the measure of its own irreducible minimum, has only one atom in v156.
For a primitive Salem value this atom must be the value itself: extraction of a positive power of an atom preserves the real Perron field, and primitive positive norm-one units have no proper positive root on that line in their own field.
Even without this normalization, two Salem values supported on the same atom would have equal nonzero powers, which is impossible in the distinct-field case.
Thus their product has two different local atoms and cannot be the measure of a single irreducible input.
All near-one consequences are uniform implications; they require no existence of a sequence of Salem numbers tending to one.
