### Multiplicities and valuations at two

An equality of two powered conjugates can be transported by a Galois automorphism so that one base conjugate becomes \(\sigma\).
The other transported conjugate has the same modulus and therefore equals the unique exterior conjugate.
Thus powering preserves the degree, and two powered traces coincide only when their powered circle-coordinate roots are equal or reciprocal.
Consequently \(Q_k(X)=\prod_{Q(t)=0}(X-C_k(t))\) with precisely \(s\) factors before reduction.
This permits use of the fixed-input theorem for \(\sigma^k\) in exactly the same degree, including degree four.

In characteristic two the polynomial over a residue trace \(t_0\) is \(z^2-t_0z+1\).
For \(t_0\ne0\) it has two distinct reciprocal roots of the same odd order, each contributing the multiplicity of \(t_0\) to \(\bar P\).
At \(t_0=0\) it is \((z-1)^2\), so the single preimage contributes twice that multiplicity.
This proves integrality of each half-count \(w_n\) and \(\sum_n w_n=s\).
For a preimage of order \(n\), the powered trace vanishes precisely if \(z^k=z^{-k}\), equivalently \(n\mid k\).
The product formula for \(Q_k\) thus gives zero of multiplicity \(u_k\) even when different original traces coalesce or their reductions already have multiplicity greater than one.
The cases \(u_k=0\) and \(u_k\ge2\) give an odd constant and a double or higher zero modulo two, respectively, exactly as the fixed-input parity test requires.

If \(u_k=1\), exactly one residue trace contributes, with multiplicity one.
The contributing set is Frobenius stable, so this trace is in \(\mathbb F_2\) and has a unique simple Hensel lift \(t\in\mathbb Z_2\).
All other powered traces have unit reductions, making \(v_2(Q_k(0))=v_2(C_k(t))\) and \(Q_k'(0)\) odd.
For even \(t\) the original zero is simple, \(w_1=1\), and all other original traces are units, giving \(v_2(Q(0))=v_2(t)\).
For odd \(k\) the linear term of \(C_k\) is an odd multiple of \(X\) and its higher terms have degree at least three, so \(v_2(C_k(t))=v_2(t)\).
For even \(k\), \(C_{k/2}(t)\) is even and \(C_k(t)=C_{k/2}(t)^2-2\) has valuation one.
For odd \(t\), the residue trace is \(1\) and its inverse roots solve \(z^2+z+1=0\), of order three.
Then \(3\mid k\) and \(t^2\equiv1\bmod8\) gives \(v_2(C_3(t))=v_2(t(t^2-3))=1\).
Both parity cases for \(C_{k/3}\) preserve valuation one on this input.
These simple-zero cases prove exactly the second alternative in (1).

### Least exponents and attainment

An initially inadmissible even constant must be \(2\bmod4\) with odd derivative, so \(w_1=1\).
The least successful exponent is then the smallest nontrivial order of positive weight, since any one such divisor adds a second trace and the initial simple zero alone never suffices.
For an odd constant \(w_1=0\), an order of weight one would have a Frobenius-fixed nonzero trace, necessarily \(1\), forcing that order to be three.
Thus at most one order has weight one, and \(s\ge2\) ensures that some order has weight at least two.
The least such order is the least successful exponent in this case.
Every candidate order is odd.
For a trace of residue degree \(f\), its reciprocal pair is preserved by the \(2^f\)-power map, which yields \(n\mid 2^f-1\) or \(n\mid 2^f+1\).
The conjugate orbit uses \(f\) degrees, and a separate simple zero uses one more in the even-constant case, giving the two stated bounds.

For \(n=2^f+1\), an element of order \(n\) exists in \(\mathbb F_{2^{2f}}^\times\) and its \(2^f\)-power is its inverse.
Its trace is nonzero and has residue degree \(f\), since \(0<j<f\) gives \(2^j+1<n\) and \(0<2^j-1<n\).
All its Frobenius conjugates give the same order.
For \(f=1\), the trace is \(1\) and \(n=3\).
The two prescribed monic coefficient systems modulo four are compatible by the Chinese remainder theorem with monic irreducible reduction of degree \(s\) modulo three.
Subtracting \(12M C_{s-1}\) preserves both congruences and monicity, hence irreducibility.
Simple real zeros of \(C_{s-1}\) give \(s-1\) distinct bounded interior roots for large \(M\) by sign changes, while the remaining real root tends to \(+\infty\) since the sum of all roots is \(12M+O(1)\).
The trace field is totally real, and \(T^2-4\) is negative at an interior embedding, so it cannot be a square there.
The larger root above \(T\) therefore has degree \(2s\), one reciprocal contracting conjugate and all other conjugates on the circle.
For reduction \(D_s\) all \(s\) weights occur at \(2^s+1\).
For reduction \(X D_{s-1}\) there is one weight at order one and \(s-1\) at \(2^{s-1}+1\), with constant \(2\bmod4\) and odd derivative.
At \(s=2\) these yield least exponents five and three, respectively, with a genuine interior embedding in both constructions.

### Changing inputs

For every admissible power \(k_j\), \(e(\sigma_j)\log\sigma_j\le\log(\sigma_j^{k_j})\).
Conversely, if the left side tends to zero then the minimal powers themselves approach one and each is admissible.
For each such powered input the appropriate even- or odd-grid construction has unbounded degrees, mass tending to \(1/2\), and Pisot excess tending to its trace minus two.
Choose a degree larger than the preceding one with each of the two errors below \(1/j\).
The traces tend to two, so this single sequence has both required limits without any uniform convergence rate in the input.
This proves the equivalence for individually admissible powers of a supplied sequence.
The sharp examples have large exterior traces and leave the behavior of the least-exponent entropy near one open.
