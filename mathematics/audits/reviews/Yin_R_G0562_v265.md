### Integral annihilators of proper selections

In the real cyclotomic field of conductor \(3q\), Frobenius at two acts on trace labels through the class of \(-2\bmod q\).
For \(h=r\) there are \(r\) primes, each of residue degree \(f\), and each lies over a different split prime of \(K\).
At a primitive trace \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) the full trace polynomial has value \(-2(\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l})\), with the term in parentheses a unit for \(0<l<q\).
Indeed \(1+\zeta^{2l}\) is a unit since \(\zeta^{2l}\) has odd order greater than one.
The squarefree reduction assigns valuation one at exactly the selected primes to the sampled value of any monic trace factor.
Norming its principal ideal to \(K\) gives \(A(\sigma)z=0\), and the product of the split primes in \(K\) gives \(\Phi_r(\sigma)z=0\).
These are the two relations required from [C263](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v263.md#module).

The action on the subgroup generated by the conjugates of \(z\) factors through \(\mathbb Z[\xi]\), so any rational integer in \((A(\xi))\) annihilates \(z\).
A proper, nonempty selection gives \(A(\xi)\ne0\) by the degree and coefficients of \(\Phi_r\).
The absolute norm belongs to this ideal, and the sum of the squared absolute values at all \(r-1\) conjugates is \(re-e^2\) by orthogonality.
Their geometric mean is bounded by their arithmetic mean, giving exactly the exponent \((r-1)/2\) in (1), including \(r=2\).
Translation and complementation preserve the ideal.
At odd \(r\) the pair sums are units, as the two nonzero conjugates of \(1-\xi\) in their quotient have equal norm.
At seven the same argument deals with three-term progressions, including \(\{0,1,4\}\) with step four.
Each other triple is affinely equivalent to \(\{0,1,3\}\), whose six distinct nonzero differences give \(A\overline A=2\).
Rescaling indices conjugates this identity, preserving the rational integer on its right.
As an independent finite check, norms of the \(6\) and \(30\) proper nonempty selections at three and five were all one.
At seven, \(98\) had absolute norm one and \(28\) had norm eight.
For the latter selections the identity with the complex conjugate yields the stronger integer annihilator two.

### Arithmetic of the cubic field

Trial division through 41 and modular powering at the prime divisors of \(1830\) and \(610\) reproduce the primality and the two order certificates.
The cosets of the subgroup generated by 27 are stable under negation.
Their period sums are permuted cyclically and differ by a polynomial which cannot be a multiple of \(\Phi_{1831}\).
They therefore realize the unique cubic subfield of the cyclic real field.
I multiplied the four indicator functions of \(\{0\},H_0,H_1,H_2\) by additive convolution and retained the entries at \(0,1,3,9\).
For the second and third powers of the indicator of \(H_0\) these gave \((610,195,202,212)\) and \((118950,124383,123766,123756)\).
After evaluation the tuple \((a,b,c,d)\) has coordinates \((a-d,b-d,c-d)\) on \(1,\eta_0,\eta_1\).
Using multiplication matrices in this rational basis, I obtained the characteristic polynomials of \(\eta_0,u,\alpha\) respectively as
\[
 T^3+T^2-610T+4408,\qquad
 T^3+2832745T^2-78919577T-1,\qquad
 T^3+648T^2-4681T-128 .
\]
The period square also gives \(10\eta_1=398-17\eta_0-\eta_0^2\).
Both displayed elements are integral sums of periods, so norm one makes \(u\) a unit and norm \(128\) confines \((\alpha)\) to dyadic primes.
Since two splits completely, the reduction of the characteristic polynomial is the product of three linear polynomials with roots the three residues of \(\alpha\) in \(\mathbb F_2\).
Only one of these residues vanishes, with multiplicity one in that list, so \((\alpha)\) is the seventh power of that prime.
This reasoning uses the full split local algebra and requires no integral-basis assertion about the periods.

In the three stated finite fields the derivatives at \(0,5,46\) reduce to \(28,26,145\), respectively, giving three Hensel embeddings.
Reducing the two other periods through the square identity and the period sum, then evaluating \(u,\sigma u,\alpha\), gives respectively \((8,12,8),(12,13,14),(279,117,15)\).
Exponentiation by \((p-1)/7\) and discrete logarithms to the stated order-seven roots give the rows \((3,0,3),(0,4,6),(3,6,1)\).
The determinant is \(-132\equiv1\bmod7\).
Both unit columns are independent, and the rank-two global unit group modulo torsion has image of dimension at most two, since \(\pm1\) have zero character values.
Thus the image of \(\alpha\) is outside the image of all global units.
A generator of the dyadic prime would be a local unit in the three embeddings, and its seventh power times a global unit would equal \(\alpha\).
This excludes principality and proves exact order seven for all three conjugate prime classes.
Multiplication by \(610\) preserves that order.
Schoof's Main Table lists conductor \(1831\), factor size seven and degree three, with the scope of its class-number divisors given in the introduction.
The identification of the specific dyadic classes follows from the norm and character computation. [Schoof, Main Table and introduction](https://reneschoof.github.io/realcyc.pdf)

### All offsets and possible complementary factors

The [reciprocal lifting proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v263.md#lift) uses its older class criterion only to force a full or empty primitive selection, so the new integral annihilators supply exactly its input.
The binary reduction of \(P_l\) is squarefree with roots of orders three and \(3q\).
Exactly one rational irreducible trace factor takes the primitive labels, and at most a single odd integer root can remain from order three.
For an odd integer trace of absolute value at least three, the exterior reciprocal lift has modulus at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
The leading-term inequality excludes it when \(q-l\ge2\), while for \(q-l=1\) the signs at \(x<-1\) and the grouped terms at \(x\ge2\) exclude the two real possibilities.
Evaluation at \(-1,1\) then leaves the simple trace root one precisely for \(\gcd(l,6)=1\).
For the full irreducible trace polynomial the norm of the quadratic discriminant is \(5\) or \(-3\) when this linear factor is absent.
When it is present, the alternating signs at successive multiples of \(\pi/l\) furnish a second trace root in \((-2,2)\) for \(l>1\).
At \(l=1\) two consecutive grid points \(j\pi/q\) in \([\pi/3,\pi/2]\) give a root strictly inside this interval of angles, distinct from the trace root one.
The corresponding real embedding gives a negative discriminant.
In each case the discriminant is nonsquare, so the quadratic lift is irreducible over the trace field and has the full asserted degree over \(\mathbb Q\).
This excludes splitting into complementary nonreciprocal factors as well.
Finally \(P_l(1)=-1\) and \(P_l(x)>0\) for large positive \(x\), giving the asserted nontorsion root in the factor \(S_l\).
