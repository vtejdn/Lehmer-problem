The one nonreal pair of exterior conjugates and reciprocity give radial rank one.
Write \([\alpha]=e[\rho]\) and \(\beta=\eta^h\) as in v99.
The roots of \(\alpha\) are distinct modulo torsion, so an absolute automorphism fixing any positive power of \(\rho\) fixes the class of \(\alpha\) and then \(\alpha\) itself.
As \(\rho\in K\), each such power generates \(K\), and \(\lambda=dh(\rho)=m(\alpha)/e\le m(\alpha)\).
The power-field conclusion for \(\eta\) in v95 gives \(\mathbb Q(\eta)=F\), so \(m(\eta)=D h(\eta)\) where \(D=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
The joint field of the two actual units is \(KF\), contained in \(E\) and in fact equal to \(E\) since \(KF\) contains \(\alpha\) and \(\bar\alpha=\beta/\alpha\).
The relative degree two and the two product orbits from v94 give \([E:\mathbb Q]=2D\), with \(D=d(b-1)\) or \(d(b-1)/2\), respectively.

The two classes in the height inequality are multiplicatively independent even modulo roots of unity.
In the independent sign action the vector of \(\beta\) has two nonzero coordinates, and the vector of \(\alpha\) just one.
For the oriented action in the distinguished block, any relation between the images of \(e_1\) and \(e_1-e_2\) would give a block constant supported on at most those two positions.
A third position makes that constant zero, and then both coefficients vanish.
Passing to the two primitive generators only rescales these classes by nonzero rationals.

In the accepted product theorem of Amoroso and David at the fixed rank two the exponent is \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\), and the constant \(c_0>0\) depends only on this fixed rank.
The input is the product \(h(\rho)h(\eta)\) of absolute logarithmic heights and its degree is the joint degree.
After multiplication by \(dD\) the right-hand side is \(c_0d/(2(\log(6D))^{212})\).
Since \(D\le d^2\) and \(6d^2\le(3d)^2\), one may choose \(c=c_0/2^{213}\) in (1).
The source supplies existence of \(c_0\), so this identifies the dependence without requiring its numerical value.
For \(m(\alpha)\le H\) with \(H>0\) fixed, it follows that \(m(\eta)\ge(c/H)d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), which diverges as \(d\to\infty\).
The least unit greater than one at the chosen embedding is precisely \(\eta\) by saturation of its line in \(F\).

From v95, \(m(\eta)=t\log\eta=t\lambda/k\), with \(t=2b-3\) or \(b-1\) as appropriate, so \(t/d\le1/u\) for \(d=2ub\).
Substitution in the first inequality gives the second with \(C=1/c\).
Both constants are absolute while the original large-block threshold uses a fixed upper bound for \(m(\alpha)\).
From v99 the index \(k=h/e\) is a positive integer dividing \(b\) in the oriented action and dividing two in the independent action.
In the oriented action a prime dividing any \(k>1\) would divide \(b\) and be at most \(k\), contradicting the stated strict prime-factor bound.
For prime \(b\) the sufficient inequality after multiplying by \(2u\) follows from \(d>2CH^2(\log(3d))^{212}\), eventually true for fixed \(H\), independently of \(u\).
The prime-\(b\) conclusion in the statement belongs to the oriented case.
When \(k=1\), \(\rho\in D(\alpha)\) has \(M(\rho)=\eta\), and degree preservation and homogeneity give \(M(\rho^j)=\eta^j\) for every \(j\ge1\).
Every other nontrivial value belongs to the same positive integer power set by v95 for the entire divisible group, including twists and radicals.
Thus the claimed spectrum under that condition has no upper cutoff.
