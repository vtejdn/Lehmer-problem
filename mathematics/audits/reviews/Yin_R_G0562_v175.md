## Whole example

Multiplying the three integer factors gives coefficient \(+1\) at degrees \(0,28\) and \(-1\) at degrees \(4,5,23,24\), with all other coefficients zero.
These are the six terms of \(X^{23}Q+X^5Q(1/X)\), and the exponent \(1\) in \(Q\) ensures \(\gcd(23,5,1)=1\).
For the trace quintic \(H\) in the cited ledger, I expanded \(X^5H(X+X^{-1})\) to recover \(L\), and reduced \(u^{32}\) modulo \(H\bmod 2\) to obtain \(u\).
Since \(H\bmod 2\) has no root in \(\mathbb F_2\), its irreducible factors all have degree five, so the quintic is irreducible.
Exact substitution gives opposite signs across each of the five cited brackets, placing four roots in \((-2,2)\) and the fifth in \((2,2.1)\).
At an interior real embedding \(u^2-4<0\), so the quadratic lift has degree two over \(\mathbb Q(u)\).
This proves that \(L\) is irreducible and that its sole exterior root is the positive root \(\lambda>1\).

For the seed \(Q\bmod 5\), every root \(z\) satisfies \(z^{5^j}=z+j\); the least positive return time is five.
Thus \(Q\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
For any root \(w\) with \(|w|\ge1\) other than \(\rho\), the triangle inequality for \(w+1\) is strict, so \( |w|^5-|w|-1<0\).
The strict increase of \(x^5-x-1\) for \(x\ge1\) then gives \(|w|<\rho\).
I verified the opposite endpoint signs for \(Q\) on \([1.16,1.17]\) and \(L\) on \([1.17,1.18]\) by rational substitution, so \(0<\lambda-\rho<0.02\).

I recomputed the coefficients of \(1,w,w^2\) at both Gaussian rational centers and checked all six tabulated bounds by exact comparison.
For the tail estimate the coefficient of order \(j\ge3\) from a monomial of degree \(m\) is counted with multiplier \(\binom j3\ge1\) in \(r^3\binom m3(|z_F|+r)^{m-3}\).
The squared center moduli are \(11988/10000\) and \(12281/10000\), each below \(1.11^2\).
Using \(1.12\) for \(|z_F|+r\) therefore bounds the two tails by \(49/3906250\) and \(70704454969197/47683715820312500\), respectively.
The tabulated values leave positive margins \(197/25000\) and \(27/625\) for the linear term over the sum of all the other terms on the respective boundaries.
Rouché's theorem gives one zero in each open disk, counted with multiplicity.

The imaginary parts throughout the disks exceed one and the moduli are less than \(1.12\).
The distance between the two zeros is less than \((2+\sqrt5)/100<0.043\).
The first zero is a conjugate of \(\rho\) by irreducibility, while the second is a nonreal exterior zero of \(C\), which rules out membership in \(L\).
This verifies the full finite example without requiring \(C\) to be irreducible; the argument gives no conclusion about the factor distribution along a sequence of growing gaps.
