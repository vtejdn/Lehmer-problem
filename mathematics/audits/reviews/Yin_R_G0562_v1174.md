## Projection constant, transitions and the full first-level dichotomy

At 2026-11-25T01:45 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v114 reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v114.md#statement) and all three proof sections at SHA-256 6C1C60F1B4A3FD73C5CA061098960BD0518EDBBC233850D498EDC72B53806C61.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or earlier findings on v114.
The reviewer previously developed v95 and checked its application to real Perron measure values, including the fixed Salem branch.
The projected bound (2), the fixed, doubling and remaining growth cases for strict records, and the two alternatives for all sufficiently small positive unit inputs in \(U_1\) hold as stated.

For a nontorsion unit \(y\), evaluation of linear forms on its \(D\) conjugate classes embeds the dual of their real span into \(\mathbb R^D\), giving image of dimension \(t>0\).
The group acts transitively on the indices of the actual embeddings even if some classes repeat.
It preserves the evaluation image and commutes with its orthogonal projection \(P\) for the ordinary Euclidean product.
The diagonal is constant and sums to \(t\), and \(P_{ij}=\langle Pe_i,Pe_j\rangle\) gives \(|P_{ij}|\le t/D\).
The function taking a class to its log modulus at the fixed embedding is well defined and rational-linear and extends to the real span.
Thus the log vector \(f\) lies in the image, and \(|f_i|\le(t/D)\|f\|_1\).
The norm-one absolute product of the conjugates of a unit gives \(\|f\|_1=2m(y)\), proving (2) with the maximum of the absolute log moduli and no injectivity assumption on the classes.

The two absolutely irreducible record models give \(t=ub\) or \(t=u(b-1)\) for every nontorsion member of the divisible group.
The lower orbit bound for any off-coordinate class is \(ub(b-1)/2\) in either model, and the orbit of its actual representative surjects onto that class orbit.
Hence \(D/(2t)\ge(b-1)/4=C\).
All iterates stay in the group because a unit's measure is a signed product of actual exterior conjugates.
From the first step they are positive real units greater than one, so their distinguished log supplies the required coordinate in (2).
These estimates impose no degree or height cutoff on \(x\) or on later iterates.

If a measure value \(y>1\) has \([y]=t_0[a']\) with \(t_0\ne0\), complex conjugation fixes \([a']\) and therefore fixes \(a'\) itself because the source is compressed.
The absolute stabilizer of \([y]\) fixes the actual \(y\): all its other distinct conjugates have strictly smaller modulus by the exterior-product characterization.
The stabilizer of \([a']\) likewise fixes \(a'\).
These class stabilizers coincide since the vectors are nonzero rational multiples, proving equality of the actual fields as well as their degrees.
Taking a common denominator in the classes gives both \(\log y=t_0\log|a'|\) and \(h(y)=|t_0|h(a)\).
Consequently \(m(y)=|t_0|m(a)\) and \(M(y)=M(a)^{|t_0|}\) as positive real numbers, which establishes the class identity in (3).
For all embeddings the conjugate log moduli scale by \(t_0\), with sign reversal permuting the original list by reciprocity, so \(\log y=|t_0|\log H\).

For a real Salem source up to the stated changes, its divisible group equals that of its positive exterior Salem representative \(\sigma\).
This group has radial rank one and \(M(\sigma)=M^2(\sigma)=\sigma>1\).
The common iteration exponent of v95 therefore equals one, and every first measure in the entire group is fixed, also for the bounded-measure record formulation without any further smallness assumption.
In the exceptional oriented pattern the positive record measure is \(H^2\) on a conjugate coordinate.
Thus every positive coordinate iterate has next logarithm exactly \(2\log y\) by (3), and that successor remains on a coordinate.
Off a coordinate (2) gives at least \(C\log y\), with \(C\ge2\) since \(b\ge9\).
For \(x=a\) the first measure is already on the specified coordinate, so the logarithms double throughout and their asymptotic root growth is exactly two.

The strict-record classification of v109 leaves \([M(a)]\) off every coordinate in the remaining case.
Any positive coordinate iterate then has an off-coordinate successor by (3).
In each consecutive pair of steps at least one therefore expands by \(C\), and the other is noncontracting since \(M(y)\ge y\).
This gives (1) at all \(j\ge1\), including a possible coordinate value at the first step.
With no real source conjugate there is no positive Perron coordinate iterate at all, giving the one-step form.
Iteration separately on even and odd indices proves the stated lower limit \(\sqrt C\) from \(L_1,L_2>0\), or \(C\) in the one-step case.
The block-size estimate makes these factors diverge at large source degree for fixed upper total measure.

For the version over the rational \(U_1\), one can fix a common measure ceiling before decreasing the absolute smallness threshold.
Compression preserves the entire divisible group and the actual first value \(M(w)=M(a)\), and forces the compressed degree uniformly to infinity as \(m(w)\to0\) by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness.
Smyth makes \(a\) reciprocal in this range, and membership in the rational field \(U_1\) gives exactly one block by v107, hence \(b=d/2\).
In the even-inversion case the full signed model is independent.
In the oriented case the half product has degree at most two and total measure at most \(m(w)\), forcing it to be torsion by bounded-degree finiteness.
All half products are then torsion and the only relations are the block constants, giving exactly the full sum-zero model.
A real conjugate would give a real embedding of its quadratic orientation field, making that field totally real.
Its ordinary complex conjugation would be the identity and differ from the nontrivial half interchange, so the relative measure bound in v274 would give \(m(a)\ge\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\).
Taking the threshold below this number excludes every real conjugate in the oriented case.

In the one-block independent model, if the first measure is on a coordinate, the distinct formal coordinates of the exterior product show that only one inverse pair is exterior.
Its exterior root is real, and large degree and reciprocity leave neutral pairs, giving precisely the signed Salem branch.
The square pattern needs both a real coordinate and the oriented action, excluded by the preceding argument.
In all remaining cases \(M(a)\) is off-coordinate and each of the two models satisfies the single-input replacement hypotheses of v103, without first attainment of a degree record.
Applying that theorem to \(M(a)\) gives \(m(M(w))m(w)\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\).
The projection, orbit and stabilizer arguments require only these same models and compression, so they give the two-step bound with \(C=(d/2-1)/4\) for every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\).
The product estimate concerns the source \(w\), while the iteration bound holds simultaneously for every such \(x\).
