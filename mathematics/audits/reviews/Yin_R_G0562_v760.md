## Integral rounding and the limiting phase measures

The numerator bound (1), the sequence assertion (2) and the resulting positive infimum on each family with bounded normalized residue sum follow under the stated hypotheses.

For an interior root, convolution with \(Q=\sum_{i=0}^q Q_i X^i\) contributes \(\sum_i Q_i\rho^{k+i}/P'(\rho)\) to coefficient \(-k-1\).
For an exterior root the contribution to coefficient \(q+k\) is \(-\sum_i Q_i\rho^{-q-k-1+i}/P'(\rho)\).
No pole on the other side contributes at these indices.
The exterior geometric norm supplies the factor \(r_\rho/\sqrt{r_\rho^2-1}\), giving precisely \(r_\rho^{-q}b_\rho|Q(\rho)|\).
Conjugation makes the middle coefficients real, so rounding changes each to a residual of magnitude at most \(1/2\).
The three ranges of indices are disjoint, and subtracting \(PB\) preserves the class of \(Q\) modulo \(P\).
These facts give the claimed explicit bound by Parseval, also when \(q=0\) and the middle range is empty.

For \(Q=X^q-1\) with \(q\ge1\), hyperbolicity guarantees admissibility.
On the exterior, \(r_\rho^{-q}|\rho^q-1|=|1-s_\rho^q u_\rho^q|\) by conjugation and invariance of modulus under rotation.
The normalized geometric vectors have the claimed Gram matrix on either side, with conjugate angular conventions but identical absolute bounds.
Taking absolute values of the cross terms separately on the two disjoint tails therefore gives (6).

On a subsequence of bounded normalized masses, compactness supplies a single weak limit on each side before \(q\) is chosen.
The unit norm identity bounds every \(|\log r_\rho|\) by \(m_n\).
At chordal separation at least \(\eta\), the kernel is at most \((1-e^{-2m_n})e^{m_n}/\eta\), since \(|1-a u\bar v|^2=(1-a)^2+a|u-v|^2\) for \(a=s_\rho s_\sigma\).
For fixed \(q\), both radial phase factors can thus be replaced by their circle limits in the cutoff estimate with vanishing error.
Product weak convergence applies to the continuous cutoff times these two phase factors.
Dominated convergence as the cutoff shrinks leaves diagonal mass \(\sum_u |1-u^q|^2\nu_J(\{u\})^2\) on side \(J\).

The direct dependency applies to every admissible Laurent numerator with the same normalized circle measure.
Its triangular compression on the first \(k\) occupied Taylor positions has determinant \(e^{-km_n}\) and operator norm at most one, giving energy at least \(k e^{-2km_n}\).
Taking the infimum over numerators and then \(k=\lfloor1/(2m_n)\rfloor\) for large \(n\) supplies the required lower limit \(1/(2\mathrm e)\) for \(m_n\mathcal E(P_n)\).
The fixed-\(q\) middle contribution \(m_nq/4\) tends to zero.
I checked this quotient estimate and its use here independently of the periodic Fourier assertions in the same dependency.

Inequality (7) consequently holds for every integer \(q\ge1\) for the same pair of limiting measures.
Their squared atom masses are summable, so one finite union of phases leaves arbitrarily small tails on both sides.
The box argument then supplies one positive \(q\) making every retained factor \(|1-u^q|\) arbitrarily small, with the discarded factors bounded by two.
This contradicts (7), excludes every subsequence with bounded normalized mass, and gives the family assertion by choosing a sequence if its infimum were zero.
All substantive inputs to this implication are present in the indicated proof and quotient argument.
Its scope is the stated class of hyperbolic algebraic units.
