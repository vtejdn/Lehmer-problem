**Circuit inputs and ratios.**
In [the projection proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection), complex conjugation acts on rational exponents by \(+1\) at the real pairs and \(-1\) at the neutral pairs.
The resulting conjugate projections preserve the rational relation space, and saturation gives its integral decomposition.
The [minimal-dependence argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#circuit) forces the entire exterior set to be one block, and transitivity and primitivity give the signed generators.
Each generator has value a sign because some conjugate of its block is real.
Consequently the oriented Galois action uses only a common sign on a block, and the lattice of vectors constant on each block intersects the space of blockwise zero sums trivially.
This gives both distinctness and nontorsion for the ordered ratios.
Exactly one ratio for each unordered pair in the exterior block is exterior, since a repeated absolute logarithm would give a dependent proper subset.
The opposite-sign portion of the sum of pair differences is \(e\sum_{t_i>0}t_i\), and a pair of distinct nonzero entries of the same sign makes both bounds in (4) strict.

**Conjugates in the radical saturation.**
Moving a nonconstant exponent block to the exterior block leaves each other block neutral throughout its stabilizer orbit.
The logarithm distinguishes classes modulo the all-ones vector by the unique circuit relation.
For a coordinate multiset with permutation orbit of size \(h=e!/\prod n_i!\), the signed orbit thus gives at least \(h\) exterior conjugates when the negative orbit is disjoint and \(h/2\) when it agrees.
When the two orbits agree and there are exactly two levels, the levels have equal multiplicity, giving \(\binom{e}{e/2}/2\).
For three or more levels the multinomial coefficient satisfies \(h\ge e(e-1)\).
The exceptional value is three at \(e=4\).
A root of \(P\) attains the minimum in the other cases.
Distinct exterior conjugates of a positive power require distinct exterior preimages, so clearing torsion preserves the lower bound for arbitrary radicals.
When there is a neutral block, a conjugate of complex conjugation fixing that block inverts the entire exterior block and fixes its labels, which supplies the required sign for any lift of an unsigned permutation.

**Four exterior roots.**

Equality between products from different blocks would give a nonconstant vector on each of two blocks, and equality within a block would give a constant difference of zero sum.
This verifies the orbit size \(6m/4\) and leaves exactly three exterior products, all positive and real.
Taking \(t_1=\max |t_i|>0\) up to simultaneous sign, the three positive pair sums through label \(1\) add to \(2t_1\).
Their doubles give the exterior logarithms and yield the cost \(4t_1\le2\log M(P)\), with equality precisely when one sign occurs only once.

With block product \(+1\) the unsquared orbit already realizes complementary inverses and has half this cost, with the same equality criterion.
