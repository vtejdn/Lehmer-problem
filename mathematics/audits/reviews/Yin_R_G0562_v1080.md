# Review of uniform atomic masses at all forbidden cyclotomic orders

## The finite Fourier transform detects every nonconstant even orbit component

At 2026-11-24T20:30 an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed the four fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without development discussion, author explanations or prior findings on this proof.

The available atom-exclusion argument applies without regularity.
An allowed torsion atom would force its own logarithmic integral to be minus infinity.
At an irrational atom, powers can approach a primitive root of a prime order \(p\) not dividing any forbidden order.
The pullback tests \(\log|\Phi_p(z^j)|\) are bounded above by \(\log p\) and are allowed by factorization, giving the same contradiction.
Thus the atomic part \(A\) has finite support on the forbidden orbits.
The empty forbidden set needs no orbit classification.

For \(j\equiv1\bmod L\), every forbidden point is fixed, so substituting \(X^j\) in an allowed cyclotomic polynomial introduces only allowed factors.
All resulting log moments are finite and nonnegative.
On the progression the powers of every infinite-order point equidistribute by the geometric sum for each nonconstant Fourier mode.
Bounded convergence on continuous tests therefore gives the weak limit \(A+(1-A(\mathbb T))\eta\), retaining the original atomic weights.
Each allowed logarithmic moment is upper semicontinuous, as follows by truncating from below.
The limit remains feasible, and its Haar contribution is zero, proving feasibility of \(A\) by itself.

Given a unit \(u\bmod L\) and an allowed \(n\), CRT gives \(j\equiv u\bmod L\) avoiding every prime dividing \(nL\), also when \(n\) shares factors with \(L\).
The quotient \(\Phi_n(X^j)/\Phi_n(X)\) is a monic cyclotomic product because \(j\) is coprime to \(n\).
It avoids all forbidden orders because \(j\) permutes each forbidden primitive orbit.
After any further substitution \(X^{j^t}\) its factors are still allowed for the same reason.
The resulting integrals against \(A\) give a nondecreasing chain, periodic since \(j\) is a unit modulo \(L\) and the support consists of \(L\)-th roots.
Hence \(\int f_n\,dA\) is invariant under each unit power.
The signed \(v=A-\bar A\) consequently annihilates every allowed logarithm and has mass zero on each primitive orbit.
It is even when the original measure is even.

For \(j\) coprime to \(L\), a forbidden order dividing \(sj\) already divides \(s\).
The polynomial \((1-X^{sj})/(1-X^s)\) therefore has only allowed factors.
At \(z^s=1\) its value \(j\) contributes zero to the integral of its logarithm against \(v\), since this subset is a union of whole orbits.
For every remaining point of the support, both \(z^s\) and \(z^{sj}\) differ from one, so its log modulus is exactly \(g_s(z^j)-g_s(z)\).
The two \(g_s\) integrals are consequently equal.
The unit-class average of \(g_s(z^j)\) is constant on each orbit, giving \(\int g_s\,dv=0\) for all \(s\).

Choose a maximal order \(b\) under divisibility among the nonzero restrictions of \(v\).
In the additive transform on \(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\), a primitive frequency \(j/b\) detects a summand periodic modulo \(c\mid L\) only when \(b\mid c\).
Thus the only surviving restriction is that of order \(b\), including when other nonzero orders are incomparable with \(b\).
Changing the summation variable from \(s\) to \(as\bmod b\) gives the term \(\widehat g(ja^{-1})\) with the stated \(1/b\) normalization.
The equations at all such frequencies give (2).
The principal multiplicative coefficient of \(v_a\) vanishes by its orbit mass, and every odd coefficient vanishes by \(v_{-a}=v_a\).
Orders \(1,2\) already have single-point orbits.

For an even nonprincipal character \(\chi\), regularizing \(g\) by \(g_r\) produces \(g_r(0)=\log(1-r)\) in every additive coefficient, with the same factor \(1/b\).
This term cancels identically on summing against \(\chi\).
All other terms converge individually on this finite group.
Finite additive orthogonality applied to the absolutely convergent log series gives
\[
 \widehat g_r(j)
 =-\tfrac12\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{r^\ell}{\ell}
  \left(\mathbf 1_{\ell\equiv j\bmod b}+\mathbf 1_{\ell\equiv-j\bmod b}\right).
\]
Consequently the multiplicative sum over \(j\in G_b\) is \(-\sum_{\ell\ge1}\chi(\ell)r^\ell/\ell\), with \(\chi\) extended by zero at nonunits.
No primitivity or Gauss-sum inversion enters this calculation.
The nonprincipal character has bounded partial sums, so the Abel limit equals its convergent series \( -L(1,\chi)\).
The retained nonvanishing theorem includes imprimitive nonprincipal characters at their actual modulus.
Equivalently their missing Euler factors at one are nonzero since each has the form \(1-\chi^*(p)/p\).
Multiplying (2) by \(\chi(j)\) and putting \(j=ah\) gives the product of this nonzero multiplier and \(\sum_a v_a\chi(a)\).
The product vanishes, hence so does that coefficient.
All multiplicative character coefficients vanish, impossible for a nonzero orbit restriction.
It follows that \(A=\bar A\).
The units modulo \(L\) act transitively on each primitive \(b\)-th orbit by surjectivity of the reduction map to \(G_b\), proving uniform masses.

The allowed logarithms are invariant under conjugation, so symmetrization preserves feasibility for a general probability.
For even probabilities the residual measure is even, and the earlier divisor-weighted summability theorem identifies it with its mass times Haar under the stated additional hypothesis.
Conversely each uniform forbidden-orbit probability is feasible by the nonzero integral resultants at all allowed orders, and Haar is feasible by Jensen.
The even feasible set is weakly compact, and the real-polynomial objective is upper semicontinuous with a finite Haar value.
It therefore attains the dual coefficient rate.
The maximum among the classified mixtures is the maximum of the Haar and forbidden-orbit values, with each individual measure available.
Any maximizing measure yielding strict excess must consequently fail the summability condition on its nonatomic part.
Existence or impossibility of that residual behavior remains open.
