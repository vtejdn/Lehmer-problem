## Real embeddings and reflected pairs

At 2026-11-20T18:51 UTC an independent Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the full argument of C0345v616 and the quadratic-order calculation it uses from C0345v612.
The reviewer worked from the fixed claims, had no part in developing C0345v616 and received no proof-development discussion or earlier verdict on this claim.
The mixed-signature estimates (1) and (2), their sequence consequences and the ordered-pair estimate (5) hold on the stated domains.

For (3), each exterior root supplies its own Blaschke factor, even when a root occurs several times or in both polynomials.
Cancellation against that factor changes the denominator to \(1-\bar p z\), leaving a numerator bounded by one.
For an interior or circle root the unchanged denominator already has parameter of modulus at most one.
Parseval on circles and Cauchy–Schwarz therefore give \(\sqrt j\) per summand, including \(j=1\).
The logarithmic derivative of the quotient has first term \(jwz^{j-1}\), so only the Blaschke product's constant term enters this coefficient.
For (4), exterior and interior roots give the numerator and denominator products respectively, with moduli \(e^{-u}\) and \(e^{-v}\) at zero.
Averaging a product over the \(j\)-th roots of unity bounds its \(j\)-th coefficient by one minus the squared modulus of its constant term, including an empty product.
After normalization, the two bounds add to \(2\sinh u+2\sinh v\).

The coefficient field equals \(F\) by irreducibility and the tower law, so two distinct embeddings differ on some coefficient.
Each embedding of \(K\) accounts for one root in the norm polynomial, and inversion carries the relative polynomial to its monic reciprocal.
For a moved integral coefficient \(c\), the quadratic order on \(1,c\) has discriminant \((c-\tau c)^2\).
Its local index in the maximal order gives the asserted discriminant divisibility, and the relative norm of \(c-\tau c\) differs from this square only by sign.
Real embeddings are permuted by \(\tau\) and admit (4), while (3) applies at every other embedding counted separately.
Their total exponential weight is \(e^{2m}\), and the real pairs have positive mean arguments for \(\log\sinh\), giving (1) by concavity.

For the nontorsion constant, the unit norm bounds the sum of the absolute logarithms by \(2m\).
An embedded real value of modulus one would force the constant to be \(1\) or \(-1\), so the real logarithms in absolute value are positive.
With their sum \(X\), the function \(r\log\sinh(X/r)-X\) is increasing, justifying replacement of \(X\) by \(2m\) in the product bound and giving (2).
The two necessary conditions for vanishing measure follow uniformly from boundedness of \(\sinh x/x\) near zero.
The stated upper bound on \(s\log(2b)/r\) gives \(r(2b)^{-s/r}\ge e^{-C}\), which also verifies the example with bounded \(s\) and polynomial growth in \(r\).

On an ordered-pair orbit the first unequal coefficient has a common index by Galois transitivity, and the orbit product of its nonzero differences is a rational algebraic integer.
The reflection \(\sigma\mapsto\bar\sigma\circ\tau\) is injective, so the reflected pairs use at most \(m\) in each coordinate.
On the full orbit both projections have constant fiber size \(|\Omega|/e\), giving the weight \(e^{2om}\) even when there are no real embeddings.
On the graph of \(\tau\) the reflected pairs are precisely those with real first embedding, as claimed.
