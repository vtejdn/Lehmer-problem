## The nine profiles and the branch-two gap for \(2\pm2w+w^2\)

The classification, extinction and positive gap in L462v76 pass for both signs, including bounded \(d\) and \(2d=h\).
For \(A=2+2w+w^2\) the mod-two reduction at a torsion pair gives \((\lambda-w^2)/2\) integral, with every conjugate of modulus at most one.
If it is nonzero its norm forces equality in the triangle inequality, giving \(\lambda=-w^2\).
Factoring \(A\mp w^2 A^*\) yields exactly the orders \(1,2,3,5\), all realized, and \(w\mapsto-w\) preserves the sign-rotated probability problem.

Here \(K=A/B\) is an integer power series because \(B(0)=1\).
The doubled Herglotz measure has integer Fourier coefficients also at zero and at negative indices, so the integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem gives eventual periodicity and rationality.
Bounded integral \(B\) then has a finite recurrence alphabet and a simple cyclotomic denominator.
Its reduced numerator has constant one and no zero in the open disk, so the monic reciprocal has roots in the closed disk and Kronecker's theorem makes them torsion.
The lower radial bound for \(\Re J\) excludes multiple numerator roots on the circle.
Since the simple Herglotz atom kernels have zero real boundary part off their poles, the regular doubled density \(\rho\) is a Laurent polynomial.
It cannot vanish identically, as evaluation at a root of \(A\) would force \(K\) to vanish at the reciprocal point in the disk.
Parity of the circle zeros of the reciprocal bracket therefore determines the union of factors of the coprime \(U,V\).

I enumerated all \(3^4\) disjoint assignments of the four signed factors to \(U,V\) or neither.
For each assignment I used its degree difference and leading signs to form the cleared bracket, and divided successively by every candidate factor over the integers.
The parity test leaves ten assignments.
Direct cancellation of \(K(w)+K(1/w)\) and substitution of \(w^l+w^{-l}=2T_l(x/2)\) give the nine displayed densities and the rejected \(-(x+2)^2\).
The factorizations give nonnegativity of the nine on all of \([-2,2]\).
Reducing \(-AU/(2wV')\) modulo each individual factor of \(V\) reproduces all displayed masses.
At the fifth roots the residue reduces to \((3+x)/10\), yielding the stated two values at the stated exponents.
After subtraction of the positive atom kernels the remaining harmonic real part has nonnegative boundary values, which gives \(\Re K>0\) and realizes every retained row with \(J(0)=1\).
The poles of \(J\) come only from \(U\), so the fifth roots in the \(K\)-mass column do not enlarge the period of the eventual coefficient relation.

The regular envelopes are bounded since a rational Herglotz transform has an analytic remainder after its simple atom kernels are removed.
The identities \(\sigma=2\rho/|K|^2\) and \(M(K)=M(A)=2\) give \(M(D)=M(\rho)/2\).
The separated-block inequality applies to the seven small envelopes, including \(M(x+3)=(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
For \(K=A\), \(D\le2\), with equality only at \(\pm1\), so its mean square is strictly less than four.
A unit trinomial with distinct positive gaps has fourth moment \(15>4\cdot3\), and arithmetic-progression exponents admit a phase giving modulus three or \(\sqrt5\).
Thus only monomials and binomials remain and the finitely many alignment points bound the binomial gap.
For \(K=A(1-w)\), the inverse coefficients in each block are \(-1/4\) times those in the preceding block, giving \(\int |A|^{-2}=3/5\).
The mean of \(4D^2=|A|^2+2+|A|^{-2}\) consequently gives \(\int D^2=29/10\).
Parseval and \(D(-1)=1\) bound both the term count and the exponent difference up to a common shift.

Every surviving shape has measure below two, so it cannot contain the irreducible \(A\).
Its rational phase has an exterior pole, which precludes the squaring identity by iteration.
For a sufficiently large common prime the specialized nonzero moments are therefore nonreal, and finite moment-matrix positivity extends by continuity at the chosen regular node.
The two-sided sequence has finite range, so its level indicators are polynomials of a Fourier–Stieltjes transform and belong to the same algebra.
Their eventual periodicity, followed by Cesàro averaging on the period multiples and the other residue classes, forces the entire late tail to vanish.
Avoiding all shape zeros at this node transfers the vanishing to the Laurent rows.
The highest surviving row would have \(|c_k|\le1/2\) by pushforward under the \(k\)-th power, contradicting \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(D)/2<1\).

Below \(2d\) the normalized numerator is integral and its coefficient at \(2d\) is \(1/2\), giving \(2d=o(h)\) along a hypothetical vanishing-excess sequence.
In the seam test \(s j_{d+ru}\) is integral for \(u=o(d)\) and fixed \(r\), including \(r=0\).
The Taylor constant one supplies the half-odd coefficient and all remaining one-seam congruences have the required signs.
The Haar marginal forces a zero initial interval, after which the positive-part Gram bound on 26 indices has limiting right side \(25\) and left side \(26\).
The three seam coefficients are counted separately.
The nine profiles leave only atoms at \(\{\pm1\}\) in each remote weighted projection, so integer coefficients at indices \(t,t+2d\) coincide eventually.
The diagonal window, summable progression corrections and cutoff on the original denominator give a bounded-support cyclotomic multiple of measure \(>1\) tending to one, the requisite contradiction.

## The perturbed criterion, exhaustive quadratic family and cubic consequence

The whole claim in C345v18 passes, including its uniform neighborhood statement with variable \(j\) and arbitrary integral \(G\), the absolute bounds on branch two, the cyclotomic factors and the reducible primitive cubics.
Failure of the neighborhood statement for fixed \(M,\eta\) supplies excess \(<1/i\) and distance \(<1/i\) from a single center after extraction.
The actual signed inverse measure has mass one and negative mass \(j/[4(q-1)]\to0\) by the derivative identity.
The trace construction in either parity only removes a possible cyclotomic factor \(z+1\) from the delay-one associate, so \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tends to one and \(h\to\infty\).
If \(d/h\) stayed bounded below on a subsequence, the integral lower prefix through \(\min(d-1,\lfloor\eta h\rfloor)\) would contradict the divisibility obstruction.
Thus \(d=o(h)\) even when a nearby coefficient has become exactly integral.

The formal inverse of \(G\) is integral, hence so is \(X\), and all needed indices lie in the range where \(X=S_i(z^d)J_i\).
At the centers with \(|\lambda|=1/2\) the residue at \(d\) comes from \(j_0=1\).
At the other centers all \(j_n\) for \(d\le n<2d\) are within \(O(|\lambda_i-\lambda|)\) of integers because \(j_{n-d}\) is a bounded integer.
Thus \(\lambda_i j_{d+rt}\) differs from an integer by \(o(1)\) uniformly for each fixed \(r\), allowing the half coefficient at \(2d\) to yield the same mixed-row test.
The limiting first marginal is Haar, so any positive \(t=o(d)\) has eventually zero integer coefficient \(x_t\).
An extracted counterexample would violate this if no fixed initial proportion of \(d\) vanished.
For bounded \(d\) the needed short-window count holds by length alone.
For unbounded \(d\) the signed Gram matrix on each short block is the identity, with correction norm at most \(K\gamma_i\) on \(K\) characters.
A fixed \(K\) larger than the bounded squared norm of the appropriate partial multiplier suffices by Bessel's inequality.

For \(2+w+w^2\) I checked the seven nonidentity torsion tests by cyclotomic division of the displayed degree-at-most-eight polynomials.
The complete list of orders with \(\varphi(m)\le8\) follows by the prime-power formula, which permits only primes \(2,3,5,7\).
The tests give precisely the four allowable factors.
Repeating the disjoint-assignment calculation for these factors gives twelve assignments passing the bracket parity test, one with negative density \(-(x+1)^2\).
The other eleven densities and every individual pole mass agree with L462v74 by Laurent cancellation and reduction modulo the individual factors.
The exceptional identity for \(D^2\) at \(K=A(1-w)\) holds on multiplication by \(|A|^2\), giving mean \(11/4\).
For \(K=A\) the supremum bound and trinomial test again bound the shapes.
For the other nine rows the only additional nontrivial factor is \(x^2+x+1\), whose measure is less than \(5/2\) by the trace-root calculation.
The same exterior-pole, finite-range and largest-harmonic tests therefore apply.
The listed \(U\) and their sign rotations give twelfth-root support.
The [second quadratic finding](#coefficient-two-review) checks the other two centers, whose residual atoms have orders dividing two.

At \(kt+ld\), each of the three indices needed for a fixed \(k>0,l\in\mathbb Z\) is positive and \(o(h)\) when \(d/t,t/h\to0\).
The reflected weighted projection gives the limits of integers, even with varying multiplier coefficients since the raw moments are uniformly bounded.
Singleton Fourier coefficients give an integer Laurent density while congruence classes give finite torsion measures.
Disintegration supplies the same conditional probabilities for every harmonic.
Extinction leaves equality at shifts \(0,12\), and integrality followed by the diagonal counterexample argument gives the entire middle interval for a single \(L\) with \(1/L<\eta\).
Truncating \((1-z^{12d})X\) there yields degree at most \((L+12)d\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
All four limiting quadratics have their roots at modulus \(\sqrt2\), so any fixed \(1<R<\sqrt2\) gives a uniform inverse-coefficient bound \(O(R^{-n})\) for sufficiently late inputs.
Each progression correction costs \(O_L(1+n)\) in variation and the series is summable.
The two cutoffs use only the zero prefix of positive fractional length, the bounded measure on positive indices, the bounded multipliers and degree \(O(h)\) of the resulting polynomial.
This last identity uses \(B^\sharp\) itself, and the uniform lower bound on \(|S_i|\) allows division on the circle without evaluating the formal \(G\).
The resulting integer polynomial \((1-z^{12d})B^\sharp\) has bounded support and the same Mahler measure as \(B^\sharp\), contradicting the fixed-support gap.

On branch two the derivative identity makes \(q\to\infty\) equivalent to \(\theta-q\to0\).
The four-term result covers either missing coefficient, including both missing.
With both present, the fixed primitive pair \((1,2)\) gives \(|b|=1,\ |a|\le3\) and no open-disk root.
For \(b=-1,\ |a|\ge2\) the opposite signs at \(0,-\operatorname{sgn}(a)\) force an interior zero.
For \(b=1,\ |a|=3\) or \(b=-1,\ |a|=1\), expansion of \((1+s w)(2+t w)\) gives \(a=2s+t,\ b=st\) and covers all four choices.
The remaining four signed pairs are exactly the stable centers after division by two.

For a general \(A\) of the stated type its monic factors up to sign have integer constants whose absolute values multiply to two.
Every factor of absolute constant one has all roots on the circle by the product formula, hence is cyclotomic and can be normalized to constant one in \(G\).
Kronecker's step also follows here by taking the monic polynomials formed from powers of their roots, whose bounded integer coefficients leave only finitely many possible roots of all powers.
There is exactly one factor left with absolute constant two.
After sign normalization a linear factor or a quadratic with zero middle coefficient gives the formal unit criterion with \(|\lambda|=1/2\), or an integral prefix of positive fractional length if its offset exceeds \(h\).
For the remaining quadratic the leading coefficient is \(\pm1\), and strict middle-term dominance excludes \(|a'|>3\) by Rouché's theorem.
The preceding sign tests and factorizations exhaust the rest uniformly.
For a reducible primitive cubic at either pair \((1,3),(2,3)\), the fixed-pair restrictions give the necessary root, leading-coefficient and height bounds.
Its remaining irreducible factor has degree at most two and the lift agrees with the lower prefix through \(h\), proving the stated exclusion.
Neither finding supplies an explicit gap or excludes an irreducible cubic.
