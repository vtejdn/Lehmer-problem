At 2026-11-21T06:32 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the full [C345 v779](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received its fixed text and dependencies without drafting discussion, author explanations or prior findings on this Claim.

The degree of the pullback \(L\) is \(D\), so it is big on the curve, and pullback preserves semipositivity of the canonical sup metric.
All its point heights are nonnegative, and roots of unity have nonempty disjoint fibers of height zero, giving both minima zero on the full curve.
Eventual avoidance of every finite set is precisely the genericity required here.
I checked Proposition 6.4, Theorem 4.22 and Corollary 6.12 in the retained edition of Ballaÿ and Sombra, and the definitions and formulas on pp. 14–16, including the new point-intersection passage in the source note.
The general equidistribution and integrability theorems are accepted inputs at the scope of their statements.
With \(d=1\) and minimum zero the directional limit is \((\bar L\cdot\bar Q)/D\), even for an arbitrary continuous metric on the test divisor.
In (4.3), intersecting the underlying divisor \([Q]\) with \(C\) gives \(Q\) with multiplicity one.
Its height is \(h(U(Q))\), and the local measures have mass \(D\) before division.
Applying (4.4) to a one-place continuous invariant test gives convergence of the corresponding orbit mean to its normalized integral.
Invariant tests suffice for these invariant probability measures, by averaging a test over the local Galois group, in particular over conjugation at a real place.

At a finite place \(\lvert V\rvert_v=1\) defines a closed subset of the compact analytic curve and contains each sampled orbit.
Its complement has zero limiting measure.
At an infinite place apply the bounded continuous test \(\min(1,\lvert\log\lvert U\rvert_v\rvert)\), extended by one across zeros and poles.
The sum of the absolute logarithmic means over all places with their weights is \(2h(U(P_i))\) once the finite zero and pole fibers are avoided.
Positivity at each place forces the limiting test integral to vanish, so \(\mu_v\) is supported on \(\lvert U\rvert_v=1\).
The closures of the chosen neighborhoods are disjoint from that support, hence their orbit frequencies tend to zero without any regularity assumption on their boundaries.

Proper smooth reduction of the curve, extension of the morphism \(V\), and extension of the section \(Q\) and its horizontal Cartier divisor all hold after deleting finitely many finite places.
On these models specialization commutes with \(V\).
Any analytic point reducing to the section has reduction of its \(V\)-image equal to \(0\) or \(\infty\), whereas \(\lvert V\rvert_v=1\) excludes both reductions.
Off the reduction of the divisor a local defining section is an integral unit, and its model norm is one.
Thus every good finite place contributes exactly zero to both sides of the Green-function comparison for every index.
At each remaining finite place the compact unit locus misses \(Q\), so the continuous restriction of \(g_v\) has a finite upper bound.
At infinity the complement of the given neighborhood likewise misses \(Q^\tau\).
A common \(T\) works because these are finitely many compact sets.
The positive logarithmic singularity makes \(\min(g_v,T)\) continuous after extension by \(T\) at \(Q\).
It equals \(g_v\) on all the limiting supports and at all the finite-place samples under consideration.
Subtracting the finitely many convergent truncated means from the global limit leaves precisely \(h(\lambda)/D\) in the sum of archimedean excesses.

In a complex parameter \(w\) at the individual branch, \(g_v=-\log\lvert w\rvert+O(1)\) and \(\lvert\log\lvert V\rvert\rvert=-e\log\lvert w\rvert+O(1)\).
The discrepancy between \(\mathbf1_{B_\tau}\lvert\log\lvert V\rvert\rvert\) and \(e(g_v-T)^+\) is bounded and supported in \(B_\tau\), since the closure contains no other zero or pole of \(V\).
Its orbit mean tends to zero, proving the asserted factor \(e\).
The coefficient \(n_v\) counts the two embeddings of a complex place and the single embedding of a real place in the normalization by \([K:\mathbb Q]\).
Every embedding of \(K\) has \([E_i:K]\) extensions with equal multiplicities on the relative orbit, giving exactly the average in (1).
This calculation also justifies finite extension of the field of definition of the branch and distinguishes different points above the same \(U\)-value.

If all conjugates of \(\lambda\) are exterior then \(h(\lambda)>0\) by the archimedean contribution to height.
If all are interior, apply the same argument to \(\lambda^{-1}\), which has the same height.
At a pole, choose neighborhoods where \(\log\lvert V\rvert>0\) and \(\log\lvert U\rvert\) has the sign of \(\log\lvert\tau\lambda\rvert\).
An incompatible value of \(k_i/l_i\), including zero, prevents any visit there because the exact powered relation holds at every embedding.
An infinite subsequence of such indices contradicts the positive limit.
Inverting \(V\) reverses the inequalities for a zero, and the two indicated types of pole together exclude every possible sign.
