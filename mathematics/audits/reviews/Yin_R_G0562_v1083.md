## The simultaneous inequalities and the failing conductor

At 2026-11-24T20:42 an agent outside proof development independently reviewed for Yin all five sections of the [radial model](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v26.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed claim and scope without development dialogue or a prior verdict on this version.
The real polynomial family, both thresholds, the conductor and dilation quantifiers, the asymptotic at the matching conductor and the bound for integer norms all hold as stated.

The phase set is closed under conjugation, and inversion exchanges the two radii and inverts each phase.
The roots are distinct, their product is one, and exactly \(d_Q\) lie on the outer circle of radius \(\exp(A/Q)\).
This gives \(m(P_{Q,A})=A\varphi(Q)/Q\to0\) at fixed \(A\) through primorials.
The sum of primitive phases equals \(\mu(Q)=\pm1\) here, making the magnitude of the penultimate coefficient \(2\cosh(A/Q)\), strictly between two and three eventually.

Multiplying the distances from \(e^xu,e^{-x}u\) to a unit-circle point \(v\) gives \(|u-v|^2+4\sinh^2(x/2)\).
For a monic integral circle polynomial coprime to \(\Phi_Q\), the value at \(x=0\) of the full paired product is the square of a nonzero integral resultant.
This proves the floor for \(X^n-1\) at \(Q\nmid n\) and for the geometric quotient unless \(Q\mid kn\) but \(Q\nmid n\).
When \(Q\mid n\), the bare pair factor is \(4\sinh^2(nx/2)\), with its least value at \(n=Q\), giving exactly the bare threshold.
In the remaining quotient case \(nx/2\le knx/4\), so the pair ratio is at least
\(\sinh^2(knx/2)/\cosh^2(knx/4)=4\sinh^2(knx/4)\).
Here \(knx\ge A\).
At \(n=Q/2,k=2\) all primitive phases have \(n\)-th power \(-1\), so every pair realizes that ratio with \(knx=A\).
This proves sharpness of \(4\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)\) for the simultaneous floors and monotonicities, including \(Q=2\).

The sole possible collision with \(\Phi_h(X^n)\) occurs at \(h=Q/(n,Q)\).
At that order the phases project uniformly onto the primitive \(h\)-th roots, and the subsequent \(h/D\)-th powers onto the primitive \(D\)-th roots.
Applying Möbius inversion before averaging gives the stated signed sum of \(L_D(v/D)\), with \(v=An/(n,Q)\ge A\).
For \(D>1\), monotonicity and \(|\Phi_D(1)|\ge1\) give \(L_D(y)\ge0\).
For \(y\ge1\) the error \(L_D(y)-y\) lies between \(2\log(1-e^{-1})>-1\) and \(\log4\), and nonnegativity supplies the lower bound \(-1\) at smaller \(y\).
The \(D=1\) term is evaluated only at \(v\ge1\) under (2).
There are \(2^{\omega(h)}\) terms and their main terms sum to \(v\varphi(h)/h\).
For \(s=\omega(h)\le r\), the ordered-prime bound yields \(\varphi(h)/h\ge1/(s+1)\), also for \(h=1\).
Thus the given choice of \(A\) gives a nonnegative logarithm uniformly for every positive dilation, without any size bound on the conductor.

For \(\Phi_Q\) itself the \(d_Q\) diagonal pairs give \(4\sinh^2(x/2)\) each, and the off-diagonal *ordered* pairs give the discriminant squared times the stated correction.
After division by \(2d_Q\) the discriminant term is \(\log|\operatorname{disc}\Phi_Q|/d_Q\).
For each fixed nonzero signed difference \(j\) the fraction of allowed shifts among primitive residues is the product of \((p-2)/(p-1)\) over \(p\mid Q,\ p\nmid j\), by counting the available residues modulo each prime.
This tends to zero, including the immediate zero when \(j\) is odd.
Each logarithmic correction is bounded by \(O_A(j^{-2})\) for \(0<|j|\le Q/2\), counting the possible tie once, so dominated convergence proves that the averaged correction is \(o_A(1)\).
The derivative of the Möbius product contributes \(Q\) at each primitive root.
Among the other squarefree divisors only primes contribute a nonzero log geometric mean, with Möbius sign minus, giving the displayed \(\log Q-\sum_{p\mid Q}\log p/(p-1)\).
The diagonal term is \(\log(A/Q)+o_A(1)\), establishing (3).
The normalized logarithm tends to \(-\infty\).
The absolute resultant is nonzero because the radial roots are off the unit circle, and is eventually less than one, which is impossible for coprime monic integer polynomials.
Choosing \(A\) separately at each \(r\) and then \(Q\) with the three stated inequalities uses only fixed-\(A\) convergence and proves the diagonal assertion.

The Vandermonde determinant on the \(N+1\) powers of \(z\) gives each difference \(j\) weight \(N+1-j\) on the circle.
Hadamard gives \((N+1)^{(N+1)/2}\), with equality exactly when the Fourier rows are orthogonal.
Orthogonality of the first two rows gives \(z^{N+1}=1\), and distinctness of all \(N+1\) points forces exact order \(N+1\), which conversely gives orthogonality.
The maximum modulus principle extends this polynomial bound inside the disk.
Reversal outside the disk adds the factor with exponent \(\sum_j j(N+1-j)=N(N+1)(N+2)/6\).
Taking products across all \(d\) embeddings and using divisibility of the nonzero integer \(R_{kj}(\alpha)\) by \(R_k(\alpha)\), then dividing by \(\sum_j(N+1-j)=N(N+1)/2\), gives exactly \(d\log(N+1)/N+(N+2)k\,m(\alpha)/3\) as the upper bound.
The equality case proved for the circle determinant is used only at its pointwise scope.
