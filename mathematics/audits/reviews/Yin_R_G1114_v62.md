## Classification, specialization and stability near an irrational ratio

At 2026-11-16T15:41 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin checked all four sections of [L462v67](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v67.md#profile) and the real-parameter identities and moving-prefix comparison they use from [L462v66](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#measure).
The reviewer did not develop either proof and received the fixed targets without development dialogue, author explanations or a proposed verdict.
The reviewer had independently checked the preceding [eight-profile and extinction argument](Yin_R_G0562_v14.md#all-branches-review) at integer denominators.
The classification, extinction of every continuous harmonic and positive-fraction agreement near each fixed irrational \(r\in(0,1)\) pass for both signs.

For \(l\ge2\), the two Taylor coefficients \(B_l,B_{l+1}\) involve strictly positive index moments, so \(B_l-rB_{l+1}\) equals the \(l\)-th moment of \(2|1-rv|^2\mu\), including the factor two.
The integers \(B_l\) are bounded by \(2(1+r)\), and the finitely many indices outside the formula preserve finite range by symmetry.
Polynomial interpolation gives each level indicator as a Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a finite complex measure.
The [integer-transform theorem](../literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) on \(\mathbb Z\) gives a common period on the positive tail.
Writing \(d_l=B_l-rB_{l+1}\) gives \(B_l=\sum_{a\ge0}r^ad_{l+a}\) because \(B_l\) is bounded, transferring this period to \(B_l\).
The denominator \(U\) of the reduced series therefore divides \(1-w^T\).
Its numerator \(V\) is integral, with constant one and no root in the open unit disk.
Kronecker's theorem on the reciprocal and the radial lower bound for \(\Re J\) give simple torsion roots for \(V\), since \(1-rw\) is regular and nonzero there.
For \(K=(1-rw)U/V\), the atomic kernels cancel from \(K(w)+K(1/w)\) on regular circle points, and the regular continuation is nonnegative.
At \(w=1/r\) its value is \(K(r)>0\), excluding an identically zero density.

At a zero of \(UV\), coprimality and squarefreeness give an odd change of order in \(\rho=(U/V)P\).
Since \(\rho\) has only even-order circle zeros, the zeros of \(UV\) are exactly the odd-order zeros of \(P\) on the circle.
Each rational conjugate of such a \(\xi\) also belongs to this set.
If \(\xi+\delta\xi^b\ne0\), evaluating the displayed rational quotient for \(r\) at each conjugate leaves the same value.
Then \(r\in\mathbb Q(\xi)\) is fixed by the whole Galois group, a contradiction.
If both numerator and denominator vanish, subtracting \(\xi\) times the denominator from the numerator gives \(1-\xi^2=0\).
The signed crossing count for winding \(b+1\) thus gives \(-3\le b\le1\) without a phase monotonicity hypothesis.
The real factorizations exclude the indicated signs and require \(r\le1/2\) in the \(1/(1-w^2)\) row and \(r\le1/3\) in the \(1/(1-w)\) row, since outside these ranges the quadratic has a simple nonendpoint crossing.
Degree, parity and the nonnegative sign at \(b=-1,\delta=-1\) give the stated eight candidate series.

For this normalization, \(M(K)=M(1-rw)=1\) and \(\sigma=\rho/|K|^2\) off the atoms, so \(M(D)=M(\rho)\).
These are the ratios in the [real-parameter table](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#measure).
The exceptional table rows give \(M(D)=1+\sqrt{1-r^2}\) and \(1+r\), both strictly below two.
Their pointwise envelopes give finite shapes by the trinomial fourth-moment and binomial alignment bounds.
In the second case this argument applies after division by \(1-w\), with the quotient still vanishing at one.
The other six rows satisfy \(M(D)\le4/3\), including the quadratic row where the squared comparison reduces to \(4(r-2/3)^2\ge0\).
Thus a single finite shape list suffices at the chosen \(r\).

For distinct odd primes the cyclotomic compositum has the product of their field degrees, so an irrational \(r\) belongs to at most one of the fields, also when \(r\) is transcendental.
Only finitely many further primitive nodes are zeros of shapes or are singularities or zeros of the nonzero rational density.
At any remaining \(\xi\), a nonzero row value \(h\in\mathbb Q(\xi)\) would give \(h-\bar h=r(h\bar\xi-\bar h\xi)\) if the corresponding moment were real, since the density at \(\xi\) is positive and real.
A nonzero coefficient of \(r\) forces \(r\in\mathbb Q(\xi)\), and a zero coefficient would make \(h\) real and \(\xi=\bar\xi\), also impossible.
All continuous moment representatives give one probability at this node by continuity of every finite Toeplitz matrix.
Its two-sided transform takes finitely many values because each monomial shift takes one of \(p\) possible values and the common divisor at \(\xi\) is fixed.
Interpolation and the integer-transform theorem therefore apply directly to this complex-valued transform.
The mean on positive multiples of a common period equals the mass on the corresponding root group, hence is zero because every nonzero eventual value there is nonreal.
The other progression means are integrals over the same null group.
The eventual vanishing of specialized moments gives vanishing of the late rows, because all chosen shapes are nonzero at \(\xi\).
A last nonzero harmonic would have \(|c_k|\le1/2\) by positivity of the power image, contrary to \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(D)/2<1\).

In the neighboring-ratio statement, before the first discrepancy every Taylor error for \(J_i-J_0\) is at most \(2|r_i-r|/(1-r_i)\).
At each later tested moving index the change of multiplier contributes \(o(1)\) by bounded total variation.
The [two-circle comparison](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#comparison) consequently recovers the atomic phases and leaves integral continuous rows with a nonzero first discrepancy, contradicting extinction.
This checks the existence of a neighborhood, negative-mass threshold, starting length and positive prefix fraction at each fixed \(r\).
The rotation \(w\mapsto-w\) preserves the measure assumptions and integrality.
The external Fourier–Stieltjes theorem is used at its recorded statement and application scope.
