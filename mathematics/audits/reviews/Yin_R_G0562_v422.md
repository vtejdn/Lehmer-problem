# Square-root measure bounds for cubic factors under power substitution

## Independent review of the finite estimate, asymptotic constant and dilation

At 2026-11-18T15:28 UTC an agent outside proof development independently reviewed the [finite estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v188.md#finite), [power-lift bound](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v188.md#limit) and [allocation consequence](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v188.md#allocation) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed manuscript and scope without development dialogue or earlier findings on this argument.
All three estimates hold, including the constants, uniformity in the substitution exponent and the stated comparison with the logarithmic degree floor.

Let \(h\) be the actual number of occupied exponents of \(F\), so \(2\le h\le s\).
For an exterior root, the nonleading terms have total modulus at most \(L|z|^{D-G}\), while the leading term has modulus at least \(|z|^D\).
Reversal has the same coefficient norm and its leading coefficient is the original nonzero constant coefficient.
Thus every \(u_j=\log|z_j|\) satisfies \(|u_j|\le\log L/G\), including the neutral roots.
The sum of the \(u_j\) is zero for a monic unit factor, giving \(\sum|u_j|=2m(P)\).
Part A of C855v2 applies to every nontorsion algebraic unit with all \(d\) complex embeddings counted, including both embeddings of a complex place.
Its square gives \(1/10\le d\sum u_j^2\le 2d(\log L)m(P)/G\), with no requirement on reciprocity or the degrees of powers of a root.

The reviewer checked Lemma 1 on printed page 203 of [Dobrowolski's published article](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83526).
The support count is \(h\) for the multiple \(F\), and the divisor must be monic integral with nonzero constant term and have no cyclotomic factor.
The given \(P\) meets all these hypotheses, and \(1/h!\ge1/s!\).
Writing \(x=d/D\in(0,1]\) and \(c=0.31/s!\), concavity gives \(\log(1+cx)\ge x\log(1+c)\).
Multiplying this lower bound by \(G/(20d\log L)\), the bound on \(m(P)\) from the square norm, proves (1).
For the cubic polynomial the six possible exponents are \(0,a,3a,b,b+2a,b+3a\).
When \(b>a\), \(a\) is the least positive exponent and \(b+2a\) is next to the leading one.
The endpoint gaps are therefore \(a\), including \(b=3a\) when two interior terms combine, and multiplication of exponents by \(q\) scales both gaps and the degree by \(q\).
Collecting terms can only decrease the support and coefficient one-norm from six.

Write \(C=\log\theta>0\).
The exterior power coordinates are \(O(e^{-cr})\) from the fixed poles of modulus \(\sqrt\theta\), so each exterior logarithmic modulus is \((C+O(e^{-cr}))/(2a)\).
A nontorsion base factor receives \(2k\) exterior roots and \(2k\) reciprocal interior roots with \(k\ge1\).
The other roots have zero logarithmic modulus.
Consequently \(m(P)=(k/a)(C+O(e^{-cr}))\) and \(\sum_j u_j^2=(k/a^2)(C^2+O(e^{-cr}))=(1+o(1))m(P)^2/k\).
Both relative errors are uniform in the number of exterior pairs.
The uniform degree comparison \(d=(1+o(1))kr\) gives \(d\sum_j u_j^2/m(P)^2=(1+o(1))r\).

For a root \(\gamma\) of an irreducible lifted factor, \(\alpha=\gamma^q\) is again nontorsion and both are units.
The tower \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)/\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q\) is separable, so every embedding of the base root field has exactly \(t\) extensions.
Their images of \(\gamma\) all have logarithmic modulus \(u_j/q\), whether or not \(X^q-\alpha\) is irreducible.
This gives degree \(td\), measure \(tm(P)/q\), and squared logarithms summing to \((t/q^2)\sum_j u_j^2\).
The degree-weighted sum of squares and the square of the measure both acquire the factor \((t/q)^2\).
Their quotient is unchanged exactly, so the same uniform error applies however large \(q\) is and independently of \(t\).
Part A applied to \(\gamma\) gives \(\sqrt r\,m(S)\ge(1-o(1))/\sqrt{10}\).
The identity \(m(S)/\deg S=m(P)/(qd)=(1+o(1))C/(qb)\) then yields the degree constant \(1/(\sqrt{10}C)\), since \(b/\sqrt r=\sqrt{ab}\).

Taking all \(q\)-th roots preserves the full log measure because each base root contributes \(q\) copies of its positive logarithm divided by \(q\), counted with multiplicity.
The exterior partition gives \(m(A)=C+O(e^{-cr})\) by summing the uniform contribution per pair over exactly \(a\) pairs.
Additivity over irreducible factors, with torsion contributing zero, bounds their nontorsion count by \((\sqrt{10}C+o(1))\sqrt r\).
Substitution of the base measure in the first part of (2) gives \(bk^2/a^3\ge(1-o(1))/(10C^2)\), and the exact measure scaling replaces \(k\) by \(kt/q\) in the lifted case.

For the fixed graph \(g(U)+VB(U)\), coprimality of \(g,B\) gives irreducibility and its support differences have rank two.
The earlier primitive degree theorem with \(N=b\) gives \(d\gg b\lambda_b\), where \(\lambda_b=(\log\log b/\log b)^3\).
The degree comparison converts this to \(k/a\gg\lambda_b\).
For bounded \(k\), it forces \(\log b/\log\log b\gg a^{1/3}\), already giving growth faster than every fixed power of \(a\).
For \(r=o((\log b/\log\log b)^6)\) with \(r\to\infty\), the ratio \(r^{-1/2}/\lambda_b\) tends to infinity, so the new estimate is stronger in order there.
For \(r/(\log b/\log\log b)^6\to\infty\) the logarithmic floor is stronger in order, while the lower bound on \(m(S)\) derived from (2) decays as \(r^{-1/2}\).
The existence of a uniform positive measure gap for these individual factors remains open.
