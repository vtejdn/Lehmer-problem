All three integral congruences, the norm factors and the existence of primitive coprime rows with \(z\ne0\) and \(D=0\) hold under the stated hypotheses.

Inversion is a nontrivial automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) by reciprocity and nontorsion.
Its fixed field is \(\mathbb Q(q)\) since \(\alpha\) satisfies \(X^2-qX+1\), so \(H\) has degree \(m\).
The unit constant term makes \(\alpha^{-1}\) integral in \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), giving both inclusions for \(R+\alpha R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and the quadratic degree makes this a direct sum.
The same reduction using \(x^{-1}=t-x\) and \(x^2=tx-1\) works in the integral Laurent ring.
Inversion of \(x\) fixes \(t\) and exchanges the two distinct roots of the quadratic over \(\mathbb Q(t)\), proving uniqueness of \(L=U+xV\).
The local coordinate \(t-q\) has a simple zero at \(\alpha\) because \(\alpha\ne\pm1\).
Thus a double zero of a polynomial in \(t\) at \(\alpha\) forces its value and derivative at \(q\) to vanish.
Dividing twice by the monic \(H\) gives divisibility by \(H^2\) in \(\mathbb Z[t]\).

For \(J=2k+1\), direct reversal gives \(x^{-k}F=(1+\sigma x)(U+\sigma V)\).
Neither \(1+\sigma\alpha\) nor \(1-\sigma\alpha\) vanishes.
Modulo \(H(t)^2\) one has \(L=(1-\sigma x)U(t)\), and at \(\alpha\) the operator \(2x\,d/dx-1\) on \(L\) gives \(\alpha^{-k}W\).
Applying it to the right gives \(-(1+\sigma\alpha)u+2(1-\sigma\alpha)\delta U'(q)\).
The identity \((1-\sigma\alpha)\delta=(1+\sigma\alpha)(2\sigma-q)\) establishes \(v=-u+2(2\sigma-q)U'(q)\) in \(R\).
Subtracting the monic reduced representative of \(u\) changes the derivative by \(H'(q)R\).
These operations prove (1) including the factor two at dyadic primes.
The norm factor follows from \(|N(H'(q))|=|\operatorname{disc}H|\) and \(P(\sigma)=\sigma^mH(2\sigma)\).
At the powering prime the \(x\,d/dx\) derivative of \(F\) leaves only \(\sigma J\alpha^J A(\beta^{-1})\) modulo \(p\), which equals \(-J A(\beta)\) by the value equation.
As \(J\) is invertible modulo \(p\), both \(A(\beta)\) and \(W\) are in \(p\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
The two direct-sum coordinates of \((1\pm\sigma\alpha)u\) or \((1\pm\sigma\alpha)v\) give the stated divisibilities in \(pR\).

For \(J=2k\), the difference of the two reflected Laurent polynomials is \((x-x^{-1})V\), and their sum is \(2U+tV\).
In the negative-sign case \(H^2\mid V\).
Thus \(2x\,dL/dx\) at \(\alpha\) equals \(2\delta U'(q)\), giving an integral value for the displayed quotient even where \(2\delta\) is a nonunit.
In the positive-sign case \(H^2\mid 2U+tV\), so \(2L\) agrees with \((x-x^{-1})V\) through its first derivative at \(\alpha\).
The Euler derivative gives \(qb+\delta^2 V'(q)\), with \(\delta^2=q^2-4\).
Taking the derivative difference with the reduced representative now gives (2) and (3) in the original order.
The absolute norm of \(q^2-4\) is \(|H(2)H(-2)|=|P(1)P(-1)|\).
None of these deductions requires a derivative, trace difference or discriminant to be invertible at a finite prime.

For the construction, \(Q\) is reciprocal of the same even degree and has constant term one, by degree preservation and inversion on the conjugate powers.
The two-layer substitution has finite additive cokernel, and the retained denominator estimate annihilates it by \(ps^3\).
This gives the positive integer \(b\) as the order of the class of \(x\).
Differentiating its integral representation \(G(x^p)-bx\in(P^2)\) in characteristic \(p\) shows that \(\overline P\) divides the constant \(b\), forcing \(p\mid b\) even if \(\overline P\) is inseparable.
For any integer \(t\), membership of \(t x^{pd}(1-\sigma x)\) in the powered subring is equivalent to membership of \(tx\): multiply by the inverse of \(X^d\) in that subring, subtract the integer and multiply by the sign.
The least positive \(t\) is consequently the same \(b\).
Monic division gives its unique representative \(T\) of degree less than \(2d\).

The congruence to \(b x^{pd}(1-\sigma x)\) is unaffected by addition of \(MQ^2\).
Substitution of \(x^{-1}\) preserves the Laurent ideal \((P^2)\), and the signed reflected expression cancels the original one at span \(2pd+1\).
The ordinary polynomial is divisible by \(P^2\) since \(x\) and \(P\) are coprime.
The value and centered derivative therefore give \(u=b,\ v=-b\), and the derivative of the reduced representative is zero.
At the same time \(W=-b\alpha^{pd}(1+\sigma\alpha)\ne0\), so the denominator in the centered-derivative formula gives \(z\ne0\).

For \(M\ne0\) and \(M\ne-T(0)\), the row has degree \(2d\) with nonzero constant term.

No zero of \(Q\) is among the zeros of \(T\) since \(Q\) is irreducible and \(T(\beta)\ne0\).

The two rows lie in disjoint residue classes zero and one, so the diagonal has exactly this same content and degree \(2pd+1\), within \(p(16d+1)\).
