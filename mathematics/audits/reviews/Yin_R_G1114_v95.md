## One growing prime for the original minimal polynomials

For each seed, the nonproportional polynomials \(f,f^*\) are coprime since \(f\) is irreducible, \(f(0)\ne0\) and their degrees agree.
The circle modulus comparison with \(s f^*\) for \(0<s<1\) leaves one exterior zero, and any strictly exterior zeros of the limiting polynomial persist with multiplicity for nearby \(s\).
The real sign change arbitrarily close to the simple Pisot root then identifies the exterior zero and proves convergence.
The reciprocal symmetry gives its unique inverse inside the disk, which must belong to the same irreducible factor because that factor has unit constant coefficient.
The other roots of this factor occur in nonreal conjugate pairs on the circle, and Kronecker's theorem applies to every remaining factor.
This argument also covers linear and nonunit seeds, since the constant of the delay polynomial itself is \(-\sigma\).

I checked the torsion-root count using \(V=f^*/f\).
The exponents \(2,m/2+2,m/2+1\) are coprime to the respective orders \(m\) in the three cases, including \(m=1,2\).
Applying the corresponding conjugation to \(V(\eta)=\sigma\eta^N\) gives the stated rational equations.
At \(\theta\) the right side has a pole, and the left side is finite since \(f\) is nonzero at \(\theta^2,-\theta^2,-\theta\).
Clearing denominators gives degrees at most \(4r,4r,2r\), with just finitely many choices of coefficients across the two signs and all delays.
Thus the orders are bounded for each seed and each single delay has at most \(10r\) distinct torsion roots.
Differentiation at a repeated root forces the unique possible delay \(N=\eta V'(\eta)/V(\eta)\).
Eventual simplicity gives \(N-9r\le d_N\le N+r\), hence Salem degree at least four eventually and logarithmic measure \(\log\alpha_N\).
At \(1\), substitution gives a simple factor eventually for \(\sigma=1\) and no factor for \(\sigma=-1\).

For \(p\) beyond the torsion orders, the norms of all \(\zeta^j-1\), \(j\mid m<p\), equal \(p\) in absolute value.
The Möbius exponents sum to zero for \(m>1\), proving that the corresponding cyclotomic values are units.
Equality of the two numerator values at a primitive \(p\)-th root therefore permits cancellation of the same power of \(\zeta-1\) when \(N\equiv a\bmod p\).
The Salem values are nonzero and their trace normalization contributes the integral power \(\zeta^{(d_a-d_N)/2}\).
Both the resulting trace ratio and its inverse are real elements of \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]\).
Writing such an element as \(b+c\zeta\) over \(\mathbb Z[\zeta+\zeta^{-1}]\) and subtracting its conjugate forces \(c=0\).
Equality of the principal trace ideals thus lifts through the common kernel \((S_p)\).
Evaluation at every integer gives equal gcds, including their full prime powers, and the absolute norms of the trace values give equal absolute resultants.
The nonzero resultant bounds all achievable gcds by its divisors, so taking the maximum is legitimate.

At each fixed \(k\), the \(2k\) short delays can be chosen to meet all constraints because the seed and sign list is finite.
If the largest prime divisor of \(u(u-1)\) were bounded on a subsequence, the distinct pairs \((u,u-1)\) would belong to the square of a single finitely generated group of positive rationals.
The retained Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem applies in characteristic zero with coefficients \(1,-1\), finite tuple rank and both proper nonempty subsums nonzero.
Its finiteness conclusion gives growth of the largest prime throughout the class and hence throughout the finite union of classes.
That prime divides \(N-a_\rho\) or \(N-a_\rho-k\), and \(p\le u=(N-a_\rho)/k\le\min_{f,\sigma}d_{f,\sigma,N}/k\) by the choice of \(a_\rho\).
The all-prime theorem applies to each fixed short Salem factor with reciprocal parameter \(b=2\), giving both required limits since \(\log(B_p/G_p)=o(p)\).
Taking the maximum of finitely many onsets and transferring both quantities gives error at most \(1/k+1/(2k)<2/k\) against the half logarithmic seed measure.
The equivalence with the varying half measure follows by convergence of the exterior roots, uniformly on any one finite list.

Choosing strictly increasing integer thresholds also enforcing \(p\ge k\) gives growth, convergence of each rate and vanishing degree ratio through all sufficiently large delays.
For the first \(k\) members of a countable enumeration, the thresholds may change with the whole prefix.
The bound for the first seed already gives \(p_N=o(N)\), and each fixed seed belongs to all sufficiently late prefixes, proving both pointwise limits along this same sequence.
The logarithmic-window examples have \(G_p=1\) for every odd prime through \(c\log d\) with fixed \(0<c<1\), although the Salem logarithmic measures have positive limits.
They justify the stated limitation on prescribing an arbitrary sublinear window.
The argument gives a seed-dependent onset and makes no estimate uniform over a changing seed.

I found no defect in either fixed target.
The fixed-base rate and the unit-equation theorem were used at their retained statements and applicability, without a new review of the original external proofs.
