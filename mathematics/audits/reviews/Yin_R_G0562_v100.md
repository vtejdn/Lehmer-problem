## Euclidean clearing, ternary blocks and monic interpolation

At 2026-11-17T00:39 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin inspected the fixed C345v100 submission of 2026-11-17T00:32 UTC.
The reviewer received the completed argument without development dialogue, exploration notes or an earlier finding on this version, and took no part in its development.
The review covered every statement and proof section, including the general two-step case, and found the conclusions valid at their stated scopes.

For \(n>1\), each divisor \(e\) with nonzero exponent in the Möbius product corresponds to a squarefree divisor of \(\operatorname{rad}(n)\).
Exactly half have each sign, so both numbers of binomials are bounded in terms of \(\omega(n)\) without a restriction on prime powers.
In the inverse series the denominator exponents have \(\mu(n/e)=1\).
For every bounded-ratio denominator step a contributing nonnegative multiplicity is less than \(\ell/e\le C\).
Expansion of the numerator and those steps therefore has the stated bound before collecting equal exponents.
At total degree \(j<\ell\) the exceptional step produces a single progression, whose product with \(1-X^b\) telescopes even when the progression has length one.
At \(\ell=0\) the truncated series is zero.

In the exterior interpolation \(ET\) sparse implies that \(EX^NT(1/X)=\pm X^{N+\deg E}(ET)(1/X)\) is equally sparse for a binomial product \(E\).
Also \(EX^\ell W\) has bounded term count, since the number of binomials and the degree of \(W\) are bounded.
This uses only the prefix and its reflection as necessary properties of the actual quotient, so overlapping additional congruences at \(k>\ell\) cause no loss.
The Möbius numerator of the inverse series clears the denominator of \(\Phi_n\).
Multiplying the actual reconstruction by it and by \(E\) produces an integer polynomial with bounded support after removing the monomial \(X^k\).
This polynomial is nonzero since \(P\) and the multiplying factors are nonzero.
The interior case uses the bounded-degree quotient of the original \(P\) minus its centered pair, also allowing the zero quotient and coincident exponents.
The sign, monomial shift, and every binomial \(1-X^h\) used here have Mahler measure one.
The accepted DS17 statement thus applies to the original full logarithmic measure, with no restriction on the resulting coefficient heights.

For the two exceptional steps \(p\le q\), fixing a row \(j\) gives first exponent \(qj\) and first omitted exponent \(t+[qj-t]_p\).
There are exactly \(\lceil t/q\rceil\) nonempty rows for \(t>0\).
This gives the displayed difference after multiplication by \(1-X^p\), and the initial progression clears with \(1-X^q\).
For \(0<b<P\), the change \(r_j\mapsto r_{j+1}\) crosses at most one multiple of \(P\), with exactly \(\lfloor(c+hb)/P\rfloor\) crossings through the last landing.
Crossing \(vP\) contributes \((1-X^P)X^{r_{j+1}}\) to the telescoping remainder because \(r_j+b=P+r_{j+1}\).
There \(0\le r_{j+1}<b\) and \(r_{j+1}\equiv c-vP\bmod b\).
Reversal of the crossing order therefore gives the claimed initial residue \([c-wP]_b\) and forward step \([P]_b\).
The first and last endpoints are \(r_0,r_h\), including an exact landing at zero on the last step.
If no crossing occurs the smaller sum is empty, and if \(b=0\) the entire sum is \(h X^c\).
These observations also cover \(h=0\), repeated residues and noncoprime steps.
In particular \(p\mid q\) terminates at once after the row calculation.

At each recursive level the next clearing factor is determined by the modulus and step.
Multiplying the two endpoint monomials by the deeper product costs at most twice the number of terms in that bounded binomial product, and multiplying the smaller cleared sum by \(1-X^P\) at most doubles its term count.
The same full list of factors works if a particular cutoff terminates early.
Induction thus gives uniform bounds in the Euclidean length alone, independent of the partial quotients, initial residue and row count.
It applies simultaneously to every shifted prefix left after the other Möbius steps are expanded, including \(t\le0\), and to each of the four numerator shifts in the binary cyclotomic formula.

For radical \(pqr\), dilation by \(s=n/(pqr)\) identifies an exponent \(js<\ell\) exactly with \(j<\lceil\ell/s\rceil=L\), preserving the number of binomial factors and monomials.
The inverse denominator steps at the radical are \(p,q,r,pqr\).
Consequently \(L\le Cq\) leaves at most one short step, also when \(k\le Csq\) after the ceiling adjustment.
The identity \(\Phi_{pqr}=\Phi_{pq}(X^r)/\Phi_{pq}(X)\) follows from the Möbius product at \(r\nmid pq\).
Expansion of \(1/\Phi_{pq}(Y)\) gives disjoint positive and negative residue intervals of length \(p\) in each period \(pq\).
When \(r>D\), full blocks end strictly before the next block begins.
The remainder \(u\) contributes precisely degrees less than \(u\) in block \(J\), so \(u=0\) gives no cut terms whereas \(u=D+1\) already includes its leading coefficient.

Writing \(pqr-\varphi(pqr)=2h+1\), the interpolation inequality \(2\ell\le s(2h+1)+1\) yields \(L\le h+1\), since \((s+1)/(2s)\le1\).
Therefore \(2L\le r(p+q-1)+D+1\le r(p+q)\) when \(r>D\).
This gives \(J<q\), preventing every negative block in the full prefix, and \(b_J=0\) when \(p\le J<q\).
The full-block progression clears with \(1-X^r\), and \((1-X^p)(1-X^q)\Phi_{pq}=(1-X)(1-X^{pq})\).
At cutoffs \(u\le D\), the reflected complement has length \(D+1-u\), including the leading term at the edge \(u=D\).
Thus either bounded end ratio gives the asserted gap, as does \(u>D\).
If a shrinking sequence remained at \(r>D\), the exclusions apply on every subsequence and for each fixed \(C\), forcing the two stated inequalities on \(J,u\) eventually and the end-distance ratio to tend to infinity.

For overlapping blocks, the starting indices \(j\ge0\) satisfying \(0<L-jr\le D\) lie in an interval of length at most \(\min(L,D)/r\), up to inclusion of endpoints.
Hence the stated upper bound on the number of cut blocks also applies when \(L=0\) or \(L=D\).
Every lower index is included in full.
The complete periods in the \(b_j\) prefix clear using \(1-Y^{pq}\) and \(1-Y\), and the two possible partial residue intervals clear with \(1-Y\).
Substitution \(Y=X^r\) and clearing the binary factor gives the required uniform bound for the full contribution.
When \(\min(L,D)\le Cr\), only boundedly many shifted binary prefixes remain, and each uses the same Euclidean factors for \(p,q\).
This covers arbitrary cutoffs for \(r>D\) with bounded Euclidean length by taking \(C=1\).

At full degree \(\varphi(n)=2m\), the allowed \(k\) is positive, \(N=2k\) and \(\ell=k\).
The quotient ends are fixed by the inverse prefix, and the central coefficient is its only undetermined coefficient.
Relative to the inverse series, a central coefficient \(w\) gives \([X^k](\Phi_n Q)=w-c_k\), since \(\Phi_n(0)=1\).
The constant of the reconstructed \(P\) is therefore \(\epsilon(c_k-w)\), and setting it equal to one forces \(w=c_k-\epsilon\).
The two ends of \(Q\) are one, hence it has exact degree \(2k\).
The full lower congruence gives an integer \(P\) of formal reciprocal degree \(2m\), and its constant one forces actual degree \(2m\) and monicity.
Uniqueness also follows since two monic polynomials of degree \(\varphi(n)\) with the same primitive value have a difference of smaller degree divisible by \(\Phi_n\).
An empty range of \(k\) is vacuous, and different sign or exponent descriptions of a value need not yield distinct polynomials.
The ratio order of the specified inverse pair governs the side of \(4/\pi\) by C345v96, independently of the coefficient \(c_k\).
The resulting construction asserts neither irreducibility nor small positive measure at unrestricted cutoffs.
