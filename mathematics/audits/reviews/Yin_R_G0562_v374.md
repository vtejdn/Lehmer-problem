# The least Pisot excess at the square of every large even degree

## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T11:46 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin examined the full [prescribed-constant construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#decision-prescribed-square-inverse-submitted) and its [extension to every sufficiently large even degree](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v370.md#decision-all-even-square-inverse-submitted).
The reviewer took no part in either construction, received no development discussion or earlier verdict on them, and checked the dependency passages at their uses.
The two constructions, their rates, attainment at each sufficiently large even degree, and the equality of the limit with the Salem trace infimum all hold.
The value of that infimum remains undetermined.

**Rounding at fixed constant.**
The recurrence for \(z^j+z^{-j}\) gives monic integer trace polynomials of degree \(j\), bounded by two on \([-2,2]\).
Together with \(1\) these form an integral triangular basis.
The leading coordinate is the integer \(1-q\), so precisely the \(h\) lower coordinates are available for rounding, including when \(h=1\).
At each node the \(h\) independent centered errors have interval lengths at most two, including zero for an already integral coordinate.
The second derivative of each log moment is the variance under the tilted distribution and is bounded by the squared half-length of its interval.
Integrating from zero gives the exponent \(ht^2/2\).
The two tail estimates at \(t=\pm B_h/h\) and the union bound give probability at most \(1/2\) of any bad node, regardless of dependence between nodes.
Since \(R_0(x_i)=-2B_h\operatorname{sgn}H'(x_i)\), a successful rounding gives positive residues bounded above by \(3B_h/|H'(x_i)|\) and the strict endpoint inequality.
The elementary Bernoulli choice is the [recorded randomized-rounding step](../literature/RT87/readme.md#independent-bernoulli-rounding-attribution).
The concentration bound proved here is the two-sided specialization of Hoeffding's Theorem 2, printed p. 16, equation (2.6), with the single-summand estimate on p. 22, equation (4.16). [Original journal article](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf)

**Strict stability and the excess.**
The added trace expression has degree at most \(d-1\), is antisymmetric in formal degree \(d-1\), and has constant coefficient \(q-1\).
It therefore gives the specified monic integer inverse, its constant \(q\), and the exact reciprocal identity.
For the starting quadratic \(p_q\), the signs at \(0,1,q\) are \(+,-,-\), giving simple roots in \((0,1)\) and \((q,\infty)\).
At an upper-half-circle neutral point \(z=e^{it}\), the logarithmic radial velocity is minus the real part of the displayed quotient in v369.
The sign follows from \(R(x_i)/H'(x_i)<0\) and \(\Re(e^{-it/2}p_q(e^{it}))=(x_i-T)\cos(t/2)<0\).
All neutral pairs thus initially enter the disk.
Along the path a circle zero would also annihilate \((z+1)P\).
The original neutral nodes have nonzero perturbations, and positivity at \(-1\) follows at both ends of the linear path from \((-1)^hH(-2)>0\) and \(2q+T-2\sum_i b_i/(2+x_i)>0\).
This works for reducible \(P\) with the stipulated simple roots.
The negative value at \(1\) locates the unique exterior root on the positive real axis.
Any monic integral factor missing this root would have nonzero constant of absolute value less than one, so the degree is precisely the Pisot degree.
The constant gives \(\theta>q\).
Substitution of the partial fractions of \(R/H\) into the root equation yields the stated excess identity, whose correction is strictly positive.
Since \(2+x_i<4\), the weighted residue bound gives \(\sum_i b_i\le12B_h\Gamma(P)\), and \(x_\theta-x_i>q-2\) gives the error in (3).

**The two padding estimates.**
At a neutral root, differentiation gives \(|P'(e^{it})|=2\sin(t)(T-x)|H'(x)|\).
Together with \(2+x=4\cos^2(t/2)\) this proves (5), including its weight near \(-1\).
For a fixed Salem polynomial, differences of sufficiently widely separated exponents of a convergent phase subsequence give positive \(J\to\infty\), each divisible by four, with all old phases approaching one.
The added grid is simple and avoids the old nontorsion roots and both endpoints.
Its endpoint and \(i\)-evaluations give the factor two.
At old roots the derivative factor \(1+\zeta^J\) stays bounded away from zero.
At grid roots the derivative contributes \(J\), while the reciprocal of \(|(z+1)P_0(z)|\) is controlled by simple poles at the fixed circle zeros.
The nearest distance to each of those zeros is at least \(1/J\) eventually, by the power-difference inequality.
Ordering the other grid distances from that zero gives a bound by a constant times \(J\sum_{k=1}^J1/k\) for their reciprocal sum.
Thus \(\Gamma=O_{P_0}(\log J)\), and with \(q=d^2\) the error is \(O_{P_0}((\log d)^{3/2}d^{-3/2})\).

For the changing unpadded family, the six-term torsion bound applies to \((J,1)\), with the stated signs, and gives orders dividing \(360\).
Since \(360\mid12600\mid J\), the only possible torsion root of the numerator is \(-1\), by \(g+g^*=-z(z+1)\).
Its derivative there is \(J+1\), and the quotient at \(1\) equals \(-1\).
The plastic cubic has one real root \(\tau>1\) and its two conjugates have modulus \(\tau^{-1/2}\).
Rouché's comparison followed by continuity gives at most one exterior root of the numerator, and the signs on either side of \(\tau\) give one such root tending to \(\tau\).
Reciprocity, the unit constant and the exclusion of further torsion give the irreducible Salem quotient of degree \(J+2\).
At its neutral nodes the term \(J\zeta^{J-1}g(\zeta)\) in the numerator derivative dominates both bounded derivative terms.
Consequently every summand of \(\Gamma\) is \(O(1/J)\), the whole sum is \(O(1)\), and (3) gives the claimed smaller error and limiting trace excess \(\tau+\tau^{-1}-2\).

**Every even degree.**
In the compact closure of the phase powers, recurrence to the identity makes negative powers limits of nonnegative powers.
The finite subcover by nonnegative translates of the return neighborhood therefore gives an admissible backward shift \(0\le k\le K(P_0)\) at every \(n\ge K+1\).
The remainder in \(d-d_0-4n\) is zero or two, so \(L=4k+(d-d_0-4n)\) is nonnegative, even and bounded by \(4K+2\).
All zeros of \(G_L\) are nonidentity roots of odd order, disjoint from the even-order grid and the old nontorsion roots.
At \(L=0\) this factor is one.
The factors at \(1,-1,i\) give the stated endpoint ratio, including its dependence on \(L\).
Across the finite set of fillers, all powers of any filler root stay uniformly away from \(-1\).
The old Salem phases have the same needed separation by the choice of \(J\), and \((-1)^J=1\).
The preceding harmonic estimate thus applies uniformly to the finitely many old factors \(P_0G_L\), and \(J=d-O_{P_0}(1)\) gives (2) at every sufficiently large even degree.

**Converse, minima and order of quantifiers.**
The positive delay-one return is monic reciprocal, negative at one, and has at most one exterior root by the unit-circle comparison with \(zf+t f^*\) as \(t\uparrow1\).
Its exterior and interior roots are reciprocal and belong to the same irreducible factor, since each monic factor has unit constant and the interior root needs an exterior conjugate.
The other factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem, including their possible multiplicities. [Recorded measure-one characterization](../literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization)
Schwarz–Pick at zero and the reciprocal exterior root gives the cited \(T-2\le(1-1/\theta)(\theta-d^2)\), with positive denominators because \(\sigma>1\) and \(\theta>d^2\).
If the exterior factor has degree two, its integer trace is at least three, whereas the unpadded Salem family gives a trace excess tending to \(0.079596\) to six decimal places.
Thus every input bounds the same Salem infimum from above by its excess.
The fixed-factor construction gives an upper bound on the liminf in v369 and on the limsup in v370, separately for each Salem factor before taking their infimum.
One fixed factor ensures eventual nonemptiness.
At a fixed admissible degree the coefficients of every candidate below a chosen witness are bounded by its measure times the corresponding binomial coefficients.
Finiteness gives an actual minimizer at that degree, so a zero limit yields a sequence of minimizers through every sufficiently large even degree.
Conversely, vanishing excess forces vanishing trace excess and eventually excludes the quadratic alternative.
