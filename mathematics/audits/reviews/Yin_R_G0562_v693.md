## The marked factor and its complementary order

At 12:49 UTC on November 20, 2026, a separate agent reviewing for Sheng Yin checked the fixed statement and both arguments of version 556 directly against versions 553 and 549.
The reviewer had no role in developing this proof and received no development discussion or previous finding on version 556.
Both conclusions hold for each indicated offset and all stated size ratios.

Write \(C(T)=T^2+T+1\) and \(D=C(X^a)\bmod 2\).
Version 553 gives an irreducible \(D\) of degree \(2a\), a single marked rational factor containing it on reduction, and multiplicity one for that factor.
Restoring \(X+1\) leaves \(D\) coprime to the reduction of the complete complement, since \(D(1)=1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
The roots of \(D\) have order \(3a\).
For odd \(m\), reduction of the factorization of \(X^m-1\) is squarefree and separates the roots according to their exact order, so the roots of \(\bar\Phi_m\) have order \(m\).
Reduction of \(\Phi_{2^h m}\) for \(h\ge1\) repeats the same roots, each with multiplicity \(2^{h-1}\).
A cyclotomic marked factor could therefore have only conductor \(2^h3a\).
At a section root neither seed vanishes because their simultaneous vanishing forces \(C=0\) and there \(g=2T^2\ne0\).
Thus \(x^j=-g(x^a)/B(x^a)\) lies in \(\mathbb Q(x^a)\), and \(x\ne0\) and \(\gcd(a,j)=1\) put \(x\) in the same field by an integral Bézout combination of the two powers.
A primitive root of the candidate conductor has degree \(\varphi(2^h3a)/\varphi(2^h3)=a>1\) over its power field, excluding all these conductors.

Put \(r=a-1\).
The module relations of version 549 give \(2^rD_G=C(T)^rD_G=0\) and two cyclic summands of each order \(2^i\) for \(1\le i\le r\).
For \(H(T)=\prod_z(T-z^a)\), taking the roots of the monic \(Q\) with multiplicity, each occurrence of \(z\) contributes a factor divisible by \(X-z\) to \(H(X^a)\).
Monic division therefore gives \(Q\mid H(X^a)\) integrally, even for a repeated root.
The same product formula after reduction shows that a common root of \(\bar H\) and \(C\) would be the power of a root of \(\bar Q\) annihilated by \(D\), contradicting the complement property above.
Lifting a Bézout identity for \(\bar H,C^r\) gives \(uH=1+2w\) on \(D_G\), whose right side is a unit since \((2w)^r=0\).
The operators commute, so \(H\) acts invertibly on \(D_G\).
Its action on the surjective image \(D_Q\) is both surjective and zero, forcing \(D_Q=0\) and the same conclusion for every divisor of \(Q\).
The kernel of reduction to \(D_P\) is the image of \(P O_G\), since a polynomial in \(x_P^a\) lifts to the same polynomial in \(x_G^a\).
Multiplication by \(H(x_G^a)\) kills this ideal by \(PQ=G\), so invertibility also gives the claimed isomorphism with \(D_P\).
Its order is \(2^{2\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}i}=2^{a(a-1)}\), and squaring the index gives the stated absolute discriminant ratio in the common number field.
At \(j=as^3\pm1\), coprimality follows by reduction modulo \(a\), and \(a(s^3+s)\) is even, so both offsets satisfy the hypotheses.
The conclusion determines the additive index and leaves the marked factors' degree shares open.
