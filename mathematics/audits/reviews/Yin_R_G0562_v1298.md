## Finding

Both inequalities hold with the stated normalizations.
For fixed \(p,\eta\), integrating the indicator for an ordered pair of different embeddings gives the minimum of their two depths.
The difference of their \(B\)-images has at least this valuation since \(\eta(u)\) is a unit.
Summing over pairs therefore costs at most \(v_p(\eta\Delta)\).
On the archimedean side each row of the Vandermonde matrix contributes at most \(\tfrac12\log n+(n-1)\log^+|y|\) to the log of its absolute determinant.
After squaring and averaging over the base embeddings, the sum of the \(\log^+|y|\) terms before multiplication by \(2(n-1)\) is \(nh(B)\).
Separability gives \(\Delta\ne0\), and integrality allows all finite contributions to be summed by the norm formula.

For \(Q\mid\mathfrak p\mid p\), write \(d_{\mathfrak p}=[K_{\mathfrak p}:\mathbb Q_p]\) and \(q_Q=v_p(z)\).
Exactly \(d_{\mathfrak p}\) base embeddings induce \(\mathfrak p\), and \(s_Q\) extensions of each induce \(Q\).
Thus the first-moment contribution is \(d_{\mathfrak p}s_Qq_Q\log p/D=w_Q\), and the contribution of ordered pairs inside this group to the normalized second moment is \(s_Qw_Q/n\).
The diagonal contributes \(H/n\) to that second moment, while the discriminant bounds the off-diagonal terms by \((\log n)/n+2(1-1/n)h(B)\).
Cauchy–Schwarz on the capped integration intervals has second factor \((\sum_{p\in S}t_p\log p)^{1/2}\).
This also gives zero for empty support or zero caps.

On the designated rows the norm limit and the relative-degree divergence apply to the same fields and exponent.
Since \(n\le d_B\) and \(h(B)=\log B/d_B\) for Salem \(B\), the first inequality gives \(nU\le H+\log n+2\log B\).
The probability weights \(w_Q/H\) are defined for sufficiently late rows since \(H\to\log 2\).
With fixed positive mass \(\delta\) over \(S\), Markov's inequality at \(4nU/\delta\) costs at most \(\delta/4\).
The constant cap \(\delta^2/(16UV_S)\) likewise costs at most \(\delta/4\) on all places of depth below or equal to that cap.
The union bound leaves the stated \(\delta/2\) with both properties.
Intersecting with the selected split-quartet places when both records are Salem loses at most their total discarded weight \(o(1)\).

For the example, both trace minima are irreducible quadratics with exactly one root above two and one in \((-2,2)\).
Their distinct quadratic fields are linearly disjoint, so both real sign choices for each radicand can be imposed independently.
Each radicand and their product thus has a negative real image and cannot be a square in the real trace compositum.
This gives total degree \(4\cdot4=16\), the product of the two quartic degrees, so the chosen embeddings of the individual coordinate fields into \(\mathbb Q_{17}\) extend jointly.
Direct reduction gives derivatives \(7,1\bmod17\) at \(10,5\) and Hensel lifts \(61,192\bmod289\), respectively.
I obtain \(10^8\equiv-1\bmod17\) and \(61^{16}\equiv103=1+6\cdot17\bmod289\).
The binomial valuation step gives order \(16\cdot17^{k-1}\) modulo \(17^k\) for the first lift, which suffices to reach \(2/\beta\) with a positive exponent.
At such an exponent the two coordinates reduce to \(14,5\) and the traces to \(8,12\).
The four signed products reduce to \(2,13,4,9\), so exactly one factor carries all of the valuation in the trace norm.
The derivative \(8T_k-10S\) reduces to \(12\), confirming smoothness.
These computations establish unbounded depth at one fixed place as the exponent varies.
Whether positive mass persists at a fixed prime along shrinking adjacent records remains open.

The cited determinant estimate and four-factor trace identity agree with the computations above.
The existing record limits and quartet estimate are used at their stated hypotheses and scope.
