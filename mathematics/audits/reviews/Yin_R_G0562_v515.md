## Outcome of the computation review

Up to \(X\mapsto-X\), precisely the 17 tabulated seeds occur.
Eleven have one exterior root and \(M>7/5\), and six have two and \(M>17/10\).
Every distinct-root ratio of every seed is nontorsion.
I executed the fixed program's census in memory, obtaining a full match with its recorded JSON, and independently reconstructed the table as follows.

### Exhaustion and strict comparisons

The bound \(|[X^{6-i}]P|\le\binom6i M(P)\) follows by expanding the elementary symmetric function.
The sign involution acts by \((a,b,c)\mapsto(-a,b,-c)\), preserving moduli and root ratios.
Choosing \(a>0\) or \(a=0,\ c\ge0\) together with the three coefficient bounds gives \(61(12\cdot81+41)=61\,793\) triples.

For an independent exact measure test I started with each sextic and iterated Graeffe squaring.
Writing the current polynomial as \(E(X^2)+XO(X^2)\), the new monic sextic \(E(Y)^2-YO(Y)^2\) has all squared roots with multiplicities.
After \(j\) steps, for descending coefficients \(c_i\) of the current polynomial, elementary symmetric bounds and the circle norm bound give
\[
        \max_i |c_i|/\binom6i \ \le\ M(P)^{2^j}\ \le\ \sum_i |c_i|.
\]
I compared the two endpoints against \(R^{2^j}\) by integer cross-multiplication, accepting an upper endpoint at most the threshold and rejecting a lower endpoint strictly above it.
Every threshold-two comparison resolved by step ten, leaving 60 triples.
SymPy 1.14.0's rational irreducibility and cyclotomic tests found 19 irreducibles, including exactly \(X^6+X^3+1=\Phi_9\) and \(X^6+X^5+\cdots+1=\Phi_7\) as the cyclotomics.
The independent list of remaining triples matched every table entry.

Dividing by \(X^3\) gives the cubic in \(X+X^{-1}\) with coefficients \(1,a,b-3,c-2a\).
Each trace in \((-2,2)\) contributes two circle roots, and each remaining trace contributes one exterior root via its reciprocal quadratic, including for nonreal traces.
Irreducibility of a sextic in characteristic zero rules out repeated roots and traces at either endpoint.
I counted the real trace roots on this interval by exact rational root counting and obtained the displayed exterior counts for each seed.
Graeffe comparison at its corresponding rational lower threshold resolved every case strictly above the threshold within eleven steps.

I also checked why the implementation's different measure test is exact.
The exterior product of a real reciprocal sextic is a real number of absolute value \(M\), with at most three exterior roots.
When \(M>R\ge1\) at least one exterior root occurs, so one of the two shifts for its subset size has a strictly positive real zero.
A monic polynomial with nonnegative coefficients cannot have such a zero.
When \(M\le R\), every subset product has modulus at most \(R\), and pairing its nonreal conjugates gives nonnegative coefficients after either shift, including at equality.
Newton's identities for the second and third elementary symmetric functions of the powered roots give the two product-power formulas in the code.
The Horner loop translates the polynomial after multiplying its roots by \(q\) or \(-q\), giving an integral test at \(N\) for \(R=N/q\).
Repeated indexed roots are allowed in this stage.
In the trial-factor stage any reducible capped polynomial has a monic integral factor of degree at most three, with unit constant term and measure at most two by multiplicativity.
The code enumerates all such coefficient bounds before applying the Sturm and strict-threshold tests to the surviving nontorsion inputs.

### A separate modular certificate for all distinct-root ratios

The ordered ratios with \(i\ne j\) form a Galois-invariant multiset of size 30, so any torsion order \(n\) among them has \(\varphi(n)\le30\).
The prime-power product for \(\varphi(n)^2/n\) gives \(n\le1800\).
Independent totient enumeration gives 57 eligible orders, the largest 90, with least common multiple \(L=144403552893600\).
For each seed I worked in \(\mathbb F_{1000003}[X]/P\) and computed \(h=X^L\) by binary exponentiation.
In the ascending power basis \(1,X,\ldots,X^5\), the determinants of the six coordinate rows \(1,h,\ldots,h^5\) modulo this prime, in lexicographic order of \((a,b,c)\), are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 85333,\ 941842,\ 538280,\ 757601,\ 423115,\ 391091,\ 767364,\ 4973,\ 956354,\\
 724475,\ 947942,\ 369103,\ 687701,\ 268334,\ 794707,\ 723484,\ 797864.
 \end{gathered}
\]
These are reductions of ordinary integer determinants since \(P\) is monic.
Every nonzero residue proves that \(X^L\bmod P\) has degree six over \(\mathbb Q\).
A torsion ratio would identify two conjugates of this element, which is impossible.
In the program's ratio-polynomial check, the inverse-root multiset equals the root multiset, so its off-diagonal power sum is \(s_n^2-6\).
Newton's identities therefore give the correct monic ratio polynomial.
The cyclotomic recursion includes every proper divisor of an eligible order since \(d\mid n\) implies \(\varphi(d)\le\varphi(n)\).

### Scope of the prime-exponent consequence

In the sum of absolute log-modulus differences, positive-negative pairs contribute \(2gh\), and all pairs with exactly one zero contribute \(2(6-2g)h\).
The other summands are nonnegative, giving the stated \(D\ge2(6-g)h\).
The weakest row-width bound in the specified range is at \(w=9\).
For \(g=1,2\), the respective strict integer margins \(7^{13}-64\cdot5^{13}\) and \(17^9-64\cdot10^9\) are \(18764010407\) and \(54587876497\).
Thus the expression in J178 exceeds \(\log2\) for each sextic through width nine.
When the specified fixed-seed asymptotic coefficient inequality applies, finiteness makes the eventual exclusion uniform over this list and these widths.
The computation provides no numerical prime onset, and the full test at widths eight and nine also requires other seed degrees.
