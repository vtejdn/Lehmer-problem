## Full extraction, the exact invariant degree and its vanishing limit

At 2026-11-24T21:18 an agent outside the development of this proof independently checked the three fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer had worked on the cited irreducible substitutions of Lehmer's polynomial and received this new tower result without its development discussion, author explanations or previous findings.

For a general family member \(\beta\), under the explicit assumption of irreducibility of \(X^n-\beta\) over \(L=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), each root \(\gamma\) generates \(L(\gamma)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) because \(\gamma^n=\beta\).
Its degree is \(n[L:\mathbb Q]\), exactly the degree of the composed monic minimum, so the composition is irreducible.
It is reciprocal and \(\gamma\) is a nontorsion unit.
The conjugate \(\gamma^{-1}\) generates the same field, so inversion gives an involution restricting to the original inverter on \(L\).
Its fixed field is generated by \(\gamma+\gamma^{-1}\), since the trace equation gives relative degree at most two and the involution is nontrivial.
It fixes \(\sqrt3\) and negates \(i\), proving the new generator has relative norm one and lies in the same quadratic family with the stated relative integral basis.

At a fixed embedding of \(L\), all \(n\) roots of the image binomial occur, each with logarithmic modulus \(1/n\) times that of the image of \(\beta\).
The sum of the positive logarithms is therefore unchanged, while the number of off-circle embeddings is multiplied by \(n\).
For the invariant choose the unique lift of each base trace-field embedding fixing \(i\).
Its \(n\) extensions also fix \(i\) and yield \(n\) distinct embeddings of the new trace field because adjoining \(i\) recovers the whole field.
At a nonreal embedding with log modulus \(a_s\) these lifts give \(a_s/n\), and the conjugate trace embedding gives the negative values under the lifts fixing \(i\).
At a real trace embedding all lifted roots have modulus one and their traces are real.
Thus there are \(n r\) nonreal trace-embedding pairs and \(D(\gamma)=4n\sum_s\sinh^2(a_s/n)\).
Multiplying by \(nr\) and taking an unbounded sequence of such full extractions gives \(4r\sum_s a_s^2\).
The base family has \(r\ge1\), and \(m(\beta)=2\sum_s|a_s|\).
Cauchy–Schwarz yields the claimed inequality with equality precisely when all \(|a_s|\) are equal.

In the positive real embedding of Lehmer's Salem field \(K\), its intersection with \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\) lies in \(E\cap\mathbb R=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
Compatibility of the Salem inverter with complex conjugation on this intersection makes it pointwise fixed.
The fixed trace field inside \(K\) has degree five because \(K\) has degree ten and inversion has relative degree two.
The intersection cannot be quadratic by the degree tower, hence it is \(\mathbb Q\).
It follows that \([KE:K]=4\).
The Salem root's powers generate \(K\) by uniqueness of its conjugate of maximal modulus, so \((w\alpha)^{12}\) already generates \(K\) and \(w\alpha\) generates all of \(KE\).
The four extensions of each \(K\)-embedding give \(m(w\alpha)=4h\).
The only underlying off-circle moduli are \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\), each with four lifts, yielding precisely two nonreal pairs in the trace field, both with \(|a_s|=h\).

For the indicated positive \(n\) coprime to six, \([K(\alpha^{1/n}):K]=n\) by the irreducible-substitution result, including the trivial \(n=1\) case.
Since \(\gcd(4,n)=1\), the compositum over \(K\) has degree divisible by \(4n\) and at most \(4n\).
The same hypothesis permits \(nj\equiv1\bmod12\).
The element \(\beta_n=w^j\alpha^{1/n}\) then has \(n\)-th power \(w\alpha\), so its field contains \(KE\), and dividing by \(w^j\) recovers \(\alpha^{1/n}\).
This proves degree \(40n\) over \(\mathbb Q\) and full irreducibility of \(X^n-w\alpha\) over \(KE\).
The general tower result now gives \(m(\beta_n)=4h\), \(r_2(F_n)=2n\) and \(D_n=8n\sinh^2(h/n)=2nV_n\).

Write \(v=\alpha^{2/n}\) in its positive real embedding.
Its \(n\)-th power \(\alpha^2\) generates \(K\), so \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q(v)\).
The Bézout identity for \(2,n\) then recovers \(\alpha^{1/n}\) from \(v\) and \(\alpha\), giving \([\mathbb Q(v):\mathbb Q]=10n\).
The degree-ten minimum \(P_2\) of \(\alpha^2\) is reciprocal, so \(P_2(X^n)\) is the reciprocal minimum of \(v\) by its degree.
Inversion on \(\mathbb Q(v)\) is nontrivial since \(v>1\), and the quadratic equation over its trace gives \([\mathbb Q(v):\mathbb Q(v+v^{-1})]=2\).
Translation by \(-2\) and multiplication by the nonzero rational \(2n\) do not change the trace field, giving \([\mathbb Q(D_n):\mathbb Q]=[\mathbb Q(V_n):\mathbb Q]=5n\).
Here the trace field of \(v\) has its own degree and is distinct in degree from the \(20n\)-degree field \(F_n\).
The original polynomial has \(P(1)=-1\), \(P(-1)=1\).
Since the ten squared conjugates are distinct, \(P_2(1)=\prod_i(1-\alpha_i^2)=P(1)P(-1)=-1\).
The norm of \(1-v\) equals \(P_2(1)\) by the just-identified minimum.
Consequently \(1-v\) and \(V_n=(v-1)^2/v\) are algebraic units, and \(V_n>0\) at the chosen embedding.

Every conjugate of \(V_n\) is obtained by extending its embedding to \(\mathbb Q(v)\).
Any resulting \(z=e^{a+i\theta}\) above it satisfies \(|a|\le2h/n\) because \(z^n\) is a conjugate of \(\alpha^2\).
Comparing \(z+z^{-1}-2\) with \(2\cos\theta-2\in[-4,0]\) gives distance at most \(|e^a-1|+|e^{-a}-1|\le2(e^{2h/n}-1)\).
Finally \(V_n=4\sinh^2(h/n)\) implies \(n^2V_n\to4h^2\), \(D_n\to0\), and \(r_2(F_n)D_n\to16h^2=m(\beta_n)^2\).
These examples keep the total measure strictly positive while the invariant tends to zero through the specified extractions.
