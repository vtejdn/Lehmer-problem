### Arbitrary normal algebraic bases

If the torsion-quotient class of \(x\) has size \(f\), its product is \(x^f\) times a root of unity.
The distinct class products are precisely the conjugates of \(\gamma\), and the degree drops by \(f\) while the absolute height increases by \(f\).
This verifies the measure identity for nonunits as well.
The label fibers of \(\gamma\) have size at most two.
At bounded measure the lower bound on the minimal block size tends to infinity with the compressed degree, uniformly in the base.
One may therefore impose both \(b\ge5\) and \(b>q\) at a threshold depending only on \(M,q\).

The finite rational label field \(P\) is Galois and \(P\cap F\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\) even when \(F\) has infinite degree.
Restriction identifies \({\rm Gal}(PF/F)\) with the subgroup of \({\rm Gal}(P/\mathbb Q)\) fixing \(P\cap F\), since \(PF/F\) is Galois with fixed field \(F\).
If \(P\not\subset F\), that subgroup is nontrivial and normal, so it contains the unique minimal normal subgroup \(D=(A_b)^u\).
In particular it has \(A_b\) as a section.
Solvable groups and subgroups of \(S_q\) exclude this section, the latter by order.
The exclusion passes to subgroups, quotients and extensions, since the image of a normal intersection under a surjection onto the simple \(A_b\) is either trivial or all of \(A_b\).
Successive normal closures over \(F\) of the finitely many tower steps therefore exclude it.
Normality over \(\mathbb Q\) ensures every rational conjugate tower starts over the same \(F\).
The compositum of one such normal closure for each conjugate of \(x\) still excludes the section, while containing \(PF\) would give a quotient with that section.
Thus \(P\subset F\) beyond the uniform degree threshold, and the orbit over \(F\) of \(\gamma\) stays within its label.

For compressed degrees below the threshold the coefficients of a primitive minimal polynomial satisfy \(|a_j|\le\binom{d}{j}\exp M\), giving a single finite set of possible representatives independently of \(F\).
The inclusions of spectra apply even to \(q=1\), since a finite compositum of quadratic extensions over \(F\) is solvable.
The largest and smallest positive spectra on \((0,M]\) differ only at finitely many values, so finite accumulation points, bounded-interval finiteness and gaps at zero transfer in both directions.

### Local degrees, nonunit places and uniform finiteness

For \(K=\mathbb Q(y)\), calculate the height using the weights \([K_v:\mathbb Q_p]/[K:\mathbb Q]\) at \(v\mid p\).
They sum to one and the hypothesis gives \(e_v,f_v\le S\) at all these places.
Each nonunit contributes at least \((\log p)/S\) per unit of weight to the sum of the positive and negative logarithmic parts, bounded globally by \(2h(y)\).
Hence either \(h(y)\ge(\log p)/(4S)\), or the unit places have total weight greater than \(1/2\).
Raising a local unit to \(p^{f_*}-1\) gives \(1+z\) with valuation at least \(1/S\) for \(v(p)=1\), since \(f_v\mid f_*\).
In \((1+z)^p-1\) every intermediate binomial term has valuation at least \(1+v(z)\), and the last term has valuation \(p v(z)\ge v(z)+1/S\).
This includes \(p=2\) and uses no absence of cancellation.
After \(4S\) iterations the valuation of \(y^N-1\) at the unit places is at least four.
This difference is nonzero by nontorsion, so its inverse height is at least \(2\log p\).
The usual power and sum estimates then give the second term of (2), positive even at two.

If \([F(\gamma):F]\le2\), normality gives the same degree bound for each rational conjugate of \(\gamma\).
After the chosen embedding, its polynomial over \(F\) works over any local field \(E\) containing that image, giving degree over \(\mathbb Q_p\) at most \(2[E:\mathbb Q_p]\).
The field obtained by adjoining the root to \(E\) may vary with the conjugate.
Formula (2) with \(S=2s\) bounds the compressed rational degree at fixed \(M,p,s\).
Together with the exceptions depending on \(M,q\) this gives finiteness for the union over all allowed \(F\) and all local \(E\) of the indicated degrees.
The totally \(p\)-adic field is a normal base with image in \(\mathbb Q_p\), so \(s=1\) suffices there.
The finite-place lower bound used in this reasoning follows directly from (2).

### Congruences and the split fraction

Substitution \(X=z+z^{-1}\) gives \(C_p(X)\equiv X^p\bmod p\) as a polynomial and hence \(C'_p\equiv0\bmod p\).
For \(v,w\in\mathbb Z_p\) with \(v-w\in p^k\mathbb Z_p,\ k\ge1\), the linear term of the difference lies in \(p^{k+1}\mathbb Z_p\) and all higher terms in \(p^{2k}\mathbb Z_p\).
Starting with \(C_p(v)-v\in p\mathbb Z_p\) and using \(C_p\circ C_n=C_{pn}\) gives the asserted divisibility for all \(j\ge1\).
The factorization after multiplying by \(\gamma^a\) excludes zero for the algebraic integer \(\delta=C_a(t)-C_c(t)\).

Compute the inverse height of \(\delta\) over \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
A trace embedding into \(\mathbb Q_p\) is integral there and contributes at least \(j\log p/[\mathbb Q(t):\mathbb Q]\).
Equivalently each local place above \(p\) has as many embeddings into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) as its local degree, so the split fraction has exactly the normalization in (3).
There is no condition on the local degrees of the reciprocal lifts in this calculation.
For the upper estimate \(\delta\) is integral at every finite place.
At an archimedean embedding the logarithmic bound is \(\log4+a|\log|z||\), where \(z\) is the image of \(\gamma\).
Averaging over its conjugates gives \(\log4+2a h(\gamma)\) because \(\gamma\) is a unit.
This proves (3) for each prime, even when its right side is nonpositive.

Along a shrinking positive-measure sequence the uniform finite exceptions are eventually absent and \(d_i\to\infty\).
Small measure gives a reciprocal unit class product, and the absence of torsion quotients makes its labels the inverse pairs, whose field is the splitting field of its trace.
Containment of the label field in the totally \(p_i\)-adic base therefore gives \(\rho_{p_i}=1\).
Finiteness at each fixed prime excludes an infinite subsequence with \(p_i\) in any finite set.
Once \(p_i\to\infty\), (3) at \(j=1\) bounds \(p_i/(d_i\log p_i)\) below by \((1-\log4/\log p_i)/(2m(\gamma_i))\), which tends to infinity.
This reasoning needs fixed \(q\) and the base-independent threshold, and uses the local height gap separately for each fixed prime.
