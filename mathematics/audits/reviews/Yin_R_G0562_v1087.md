## Sparse splitting controls the derivative loss without the original degree

At 2026-11-24T20:55 an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed the three fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer had developed the earlier divided-derivative bound and received this new gap argument without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on it.

Removing a monomial preserves the one-norm, support size and both relevant multiplicities because the roots of \(P\) and \(\Phi_b\) are nonzero.
In a split \(G=A+X^vB\), both parts are nonzero with disjoint support and \(P^a\mid A\) implies \(P^a\mid B\).
Otherwise \(t=\operatorname{ord}_P A<a\) is the exact order of \(A\) at each conjugate of the unit, by irreducibility and separability.
For \(t>0\), the value of \(T^tA\) there is \(\alpha^t A^{(t)}(\alpha)\ne0\), since every lower derivative vanishes.
For \(t=0\) one uses \(A(\alpha)\ne0\).
At the same roots \(T^tG=0\), so either side of \(T^t A=-T^t(X^vB)\) can be used in the archimedean estimate.

Every coefficient multiplier \(j^t\) is bounded by \((D+2)^t\), including the identity operation at \(t=0\).
At a root of modulus at least one, the lower part has exponents at most \(u\), giving the factor \(|\alpha|^u\).
At an interior root all exponents of the upper part are at least \(v\), giving \(|\alpha|^v\).
Both parts have one-norm at most \(H\).
The product of those bounds is \(H^d(D+2)^{td}\exp(-(v-u)h)\) because the absolute norm of the unit is one and its exterior logarithms sum to \(h\).
The product of the absolute values of \(T^t A\) over the conjugates is a nonzero integer in absolute value, by monic integrality.
Thus the strict gap in (3) forces \(P^a\) to divide both pieces, also when there are roots on the circle or the factor is nonreciprocal.

Both branches of every split contain fewer terms than the parent, and shifting the upper part back to exponent zero keeps its constant coefficient nonzero.
The process terminates in nonzero integral pieces still divisible by \(P^a\), whose monomial shifts sum to the initial polynomial.
Summing the negations of the strict gap condition over at most \(s-1\) terminal gaps gives (4) with the terminal degree \(D\) and original norm bound \(H\).
For \(y=D+2,\ K=2+(sd/h)(a+\log H)\), the inequality implies \(y\le K(1+\log y)\).
The function \(y/(1+\log y)\) is nondecreasing for \(y\ge1\).
At \(y=4K\log(4K)\) its value is at least \(K\), since \(3\log(4K)\ge1+\log\log(4K)\) for \(K\ge2\).
On any subsequence with \(\log H\le M a\), one has \(\log K\le\log s+O_{P,M}(\log(a+2))\).
Integrality gives \(\log s\le\log H\le Ma\), so the additional term \(\log\log(4K)\) is also \(O_{P,M}(\log(a+2))\).
This proves (5) for every terminal piece without any restriction on the original degree.

If all terminal pieces were divisible by \(\Phi_b^{c+1}\), so would be every shifted summand and hence \(F_a\).
A terminal piece with exact order \(t'\le c\) must therefore exist.
Its \(P^a\)-cofactor is integral, and \(P\) has no cyclotomic factor.
At order \(t'\) the divided-derivative resultant bound yields \(aA_b\le\log\|G\|_1+\log\binom{D}{t'}\).
Here \(t'\le D\) and the binomial is at most \((D+2)^c\), also for \(c=0\).
Inserting (5) proves (2), with its allowed dependence on \(P,c,M\) and no dependence on the input degrees.
For any finite liminf of \(\log H_a/a\), a bounded subsequence realizes it, so \(s_a\le H_a\) gives the lower bound \(A_b/(c+1)\).
An infinite liminf satisfies the bound directly.
Separately, the integral cofactor has nonnegative measure, giving \(\log H_a\ge ah\).
This proves both sides of the maximum for arbitrary integral cofactors.

For the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial \(L\), \(|L(i)|=|L(-i)|=3\) gives \(A_4=\log3\).
The sole exterior conjugate is its positive Salem root.
Direct integer substitution gives \(L(1)=-1\) and \(5^{10}L(6/5)=6762031\), putting that root strictly below \(6/5\).
Since \(6^6=46656<46875=3\cdot5^6\), one has \(m(L)<\log3/6\).
This verifies the strict conclusion for every integer \(0\le c\le5\), including the endpoint five.
For a general fixed input with \(A_b>0\), approach to \(h\) implies \(c+1\ge A_b/h\), and at \(b=1\) the hypothesis \(|P(1)|>1\) indeed gives \(A_1\ge\log2\).
