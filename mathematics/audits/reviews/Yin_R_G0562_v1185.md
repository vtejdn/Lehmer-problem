# Independent review of the residue signs at the two long indices

## The pure-power exclusions and both cofactor bounds

At 2026-11-25T02:26 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v125 independently checked for Yin its fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v125.md#statement) and all three proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or previous findings on v125.
The pure-power exclusion holds at either specified endpoint when \(h=1\) and at least one of \(3,5\) divides its own coefficient \(c\).
The local orbit divisibilities, the dyadic valuation and the two uniform asymptotic lower bounds follow.

For a nontorsion unit root of \(F(X^g)\), its \(g\)-th power is a nontorsion unit of the primitive polynomial \(F\).
Its minimum contains the unique exterior root \(\alpha\), and the sign and trace bounds give a reciprocal minimum of degree \(d=2n\), \(1<\alpha<(q+1)/q\), and \(d<v/K_q\) with the specified \(K_q\).
The complete degree-44 classification at short exponent one leaves only \(q=2\) in its range, so \(n\ge23\) for these reduced roots.
The square from the odd-index graph is \(\gamma=(p-q\alpha)\alpha^{(v-1)/2}\) in the trace field, obtained as the inverse of the trace unit whose square is \(\delta^{-1}\) in v110.
At \(c=q\) the first factor is one modulo \(c\); at \(c=p\) it is \(\alpha\) modulo \(c\).
This proves both congruences \(\gamma\equiv\alpha^{N/2}\pmod c\) with their indicated choices of \(N\).
The congruences persist at every embedding.

The quadratic extension of the trace field gives each trace embedding two root embeddings, both restricting to the same \(\gamma\).
In odd characteristic \(\alpha^N\equiv1\bmod\ell\), so the negative trace sign corresponds to root residues satisfying \(\alpha^{N/2}=-1\).
The square of \(1-2d_-(\ell)/d\) is therefore the sign imbalance squared in v124.
Keeping one nonnegative term of its weighted inequality gives (2), including the factor \(v_\ell(c)\).
The trace coefficient gives \(q(q+1)<4n\) and \(\alpha<4/3\).
At \(n=23\) the strict inequality required for nonemptiness reduces after exponentiation to \(4n(36n)^2\,16<9\cdot3^n\), whose two sides are \(1009179648\) and \(847288609443\).
The derivative of the difference of the logarithms in favor of the right side is \(\log3-3/n>0\) thereafter.
Thus both signs occur at each tested odd prime already for the smallest allowed degree.
As \(q\to\infty\), also \(n\to\infty\), and the upper bound for the absolute imbalance is \(O(\sqrt{\log(2n)/n})\), uniformly in the prime and its valuation.

An odd-characteristic residue in the negative class has order dividing \(N=2^ah\) and not dividing \(N/2\), hence order with two-part \(2^a\).
Its local residue degree is a multiple of \(\operatorname{ord}_{2^a}(\ell)\), also at \(a=1\) under the stated convention.
The class is invariant under the local Galois group, so it counts all embeddings belonging to each selected completion, with multiplicity its ramification index times residue degree.
For \(a\ge2\) these residues have order divisible by four and avoid both exceptional centers.
In the notation of v123, take short exponent one, coefficients \(-q,p\), and sign \(-1\), which recovers precisely \(F\).
When \(v_\ell(N)>v_\ell(c)\), each selected local factor therefore also has ramification index divisible by \(\ell^{v_\ell(N)-v_\ell(c)+1}\), giving the claimed product divisibility.

For \(e=v_2(c)\ge3\), the sign from v124 uses valuation at least \(e-1\), and its discriminant weight is \((e-2)\log2\).
The same uniform decay of the imbalance applies with that positive weight.
At a negative embedding \(v_2(\alpha^{N/2}+1)\ge e-1\ge2\), so subtraction of \(2\) gives \(v_2(\alpha^{N/2}-1)=1\).
Put \(z=\alpha^h\); its residue satisfies \(z^{2^{a-1}}=1\) and hence \(z=1\) in the residue field.
If \(0<v_2(y-1)<1\), then \(v_2(y+1)=v_2(y-1)\) and \(v_2(y^2-1)=2v_2(y-1)\).
If instead \(v_2(y-1)\ge1\), that doubled-exponent valuation is at least two and remains greater than one at subsequent doublings.
Working back from the final valuation one thus gives \(v_2(z-1)=1/2^{a-1}\), also for \(a=1\).
The value group forces \(2^{a-1}\) to divide the ramification index of each selected completion and therefore the number \(d_-(2)\) of its root embeddings summed over the negative class.

For \(\ell=3,5\) the order modulo eight is two, \(v_2(\ell^2-1)=3\), and subsequent squarings increase this valuation by exactly one.
Thus \(f=2^{a-2}\) for \(a\ge3\).
When \(h=1\), \(v\le2^a+1\), and \(1/K_q\le 2\log(4/3)/\log12<4/17\), the strict comparison following from \(4^{17}=17179869184<18596183472=144\cdot3^{17}\).
For \(a\ge4\), \((4/17)(2^a+1)\le2^{a-2}\), including equality at \(a=4\); the strict upper bound on \(d\) contradicts \(d\ge d_-(\ell)\ge f\).
For \(1\le a\le3\), \(v\le9\), inconsistent with \(d\ge46\) and the same degree bound.
Taking \((N,c)=(v-1,3)\) yields the stated \(q=3\) family without a ramification hypothesis at three.

Finally the sign proportions imply \(d\ge(2-o(1))2^{a-2}\) at three or five and \(d\ge(2-o(1))2^{a-1}\) when \(8\mid c\), with negative classes nonempty for sufficiently large \(q\).
Since \(v>K_qd\), \(N\ge v-1\) and \(K_q\sim q\log q\), division by \(2^a\) gives the constants \(1/2\) and \(1\) respectively.
The additive endpoint difference contributes at most \(2^{-a}\), uniformly negligible on this scale.
All degrees and local counts refer to the primitive unit and hence cover arbitrary dilations without a bound on the lift degree.
