### The two height estimates and exact vanishing

I checked [Xiao's source unit](../literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations) against Definition 1.2, Theorem 2.1 on printed page 8 and Lemma 2.11 on printed page 11 in the [author's arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.01751v3).
Theorem 2.1 allows fixed forms over an arbitrary fixed number field at each place of a finite set and uses projective height.
The almost-unit definition and Lemma 2.11 use affine tuples, including the coordinate \(1\) in the local maxima.
I applied both statements at these scopes.

For \(x_i=a_i(t)\theta_i^t\), the absolute sum of the outside-\(S\) logarithms of the \(i\)-th coordinate is at most \(2h(a_i(t))\) by the product formula.
It also bounds the corresponding contribution of \(\log\max_i|x_i|_v\) after summing over coordinates.
Thus the sum of all coordinate proximities over \(S\) differs from \(u h([x])\) by \(o(t)\).
At the chosen archimedean place the modulus of every base is one, so \(|\log |x_1|_v|\le h(a_1(t))\).
Replacing the first coordinate form there by the sum preserves linear independence and gains a positive constant times \(t\) if the nonzero sum is exponentially small in the given complex embedding.
The local-degree normalization of the archimedean place only changes that constant.
Since \(h([x])\le \sum_i h(x_i)=O(t)\), this gain dominates \(\epsilon h([x])+O(1)\) for some fixed \(\epsilon>0\).
The finite union of exceptional hyperplanes yields a fixed relation infinitely often.
Exact zeros instead give the all-ones relation directly.

Discard zero coordinates of the relation and choose a minimal vanishing subsystem pointwise, extracting one support occurring infinitely often.
Dividing by a distinguished term gives \(p\) nonzero coordinates with sum one.
A vanishing subsum of those coordinates would contradict minimality, and a single coordinate equal to one is already incompatible with the height growth below.
The outside-\(S\) affine height of the tuple and its inverse is bounded by a fixed multiple of the sum of the heights of the \(a_i(t)\) and of the fixed coefficients, after putting the latter's exceptional places into \(S\).
Each surviving coordinate has height at least \(t h(\theta_i/\theta_j)-o(t)\), since its ratio of moving coefficients has height \(o(t)\).
The coefficient of \(t\) is positive by the height-zero criterion and the nontorsion-ratio hypothesis.
The affine height of the tuple is therefore at least a fixed positive multiple of \(t\).
For \(p\ge2\) choose \(0<\delta<1/[p(p+1)]\), exactly the range in Xiao's Lemma 2.11.
The eventual almost-unit condition and the absence of proper vanishing subsums give finitely many tuples, contradicting their height growth.
The one-term initial power sum is covered by the individual local height bound.
When coefficients are allowed to vanish, an infinite sequence with a nonempty surviving support has a fixed such support after extraction, so the same argument applies.
These checks establish (1) for sublinear-height coefficients on the specified infinite sequence.

### Transfer through coincident exterior radii

Admissibility depends only on the divisibility of \(n\) by finitely many original collision orders.
In each determinant choose the progression modulus divisible by these orders and by the orders of all torsion ratios of the determinant bases.
On any retained class take a positive representative \(a_0\) between one and the modulus inclusive.
Its phases at each radius are distinct, including for representative one.
The full double-node coefficient matrix at formal derivative parameter zero is invertible, since each component of degree at most \(m-1\) vanishes at the same \(m\) distinct points.
Its determinant therefore retains a nonzero polynomial coefficient after collecting the classes with equal powered bases.
The bases have modulus one and the remaining ratios have infinite order, as required by [Xiao's Lemma 4.1](../literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch).
This proves the lower bound for the determinant up to a factor \(\exp(o(n))\) in its reciprocal on each of the finitely many admissible classes.

The finite cutoff for the coefficient envelope can be chosen with the minimum log radius over both the double nodes and the additional simple node.
Its tail remains exponentially small even with the factor \(nj\) and without a prescribed width bound.
Thus the full double list charges all its exterior logarithmic moduli.
For the weakened list a trial slope at most \(S_i+o(1)\) forces equality in the integrated jump bound.
This gives \(\gamma_0=0\), exactly \(m\) indices of jump at each radius with \(m\) double nodes, and no other positive breakpoint.
Successive extreme-line equalities give \(\gamma_k=\sum_{R'>R}m'\log R'\) at the start of the block at \(R\).
Consequently the scalar \(g/(c^{-n})^k\) in the normalized equations has exponential slope \(-L(\log R)\le0\).
All discarded finite indices have a fixed negative slope relative to this normalization, and the geometric tail still decays exponentially.
Using a real factor \(g\) common to the block is valid, since the node's phase at index \(k\) has already been removed by division by \((c^{-n})^k\).
Its update to \(gR^{-nm}\) makes the last vector of one block exactly the first vector of the next.

Prescribing the constant pair \(y_0\) leaves \(2m\) unknown coefficients and \(2m\) double-node equations.
At formal \(X=0\) both polynomials must be multiples of the monic polynomial \(L_R\) of the \(m\) phases.
Its nonzero constant term fixes their scalars uniquely.
This verifies invertibility of the coefficient determinant in this second, prescribed-constant system.
The same residue argument bounds its inverse by \(\exp(o(n))\).
All determinant numerators have polynomial size at the actual phases, so finitely many applications of Cramer's rule and final clearing of denominators preserve a bound \(e^{-\rho' n}\) for some \(\rho'>0\).
At \(X=0\) each completed block sends \(y_0\) to \(y_0/L_R(0)\).
The last simple equation multiplies \((1,-c_*)\) by the nonzero factor \(L_R(z_{c_*})/L_R(0)\), because the original roots separate at \(a_0\).
All determinant factors cleared there are nonzero.
This establishes (3) and (4), including a last radius without a double node, a simple node above every double radius and an empty double list.

### Descent and simultaneous realization

The polynomial row \(p\) expands into fixed algebraic polynomial coefficients times powers of products of the algebraic phases, all of modulus one.
After grouping on a further fixed admissible class, each nonzero coefficient \(r_\theta(n)w_0\) is in one fixed number field and has height \(O(\log n)+o(n)=o(t)\).
Here the moving pair \(w_0\) is rational integral of size \(\exp(o(n))\) and \(n=a_0+qt\) with fixed \(q\).
Estimate (3) and (1) therefore force every grouped scalar coefficient to vanish on the tail of the extracted sequence.
For \(r=\sum r_\theta\), applying any automorphism of a fixed normal field to \(r(n)w_0=0\) preserves the same nonzero integer pair.
Hence \(\det(r(X),\tau r(X))\) is a polynomial vanishing at infinitely many integers.
At zero its value is \(\kappa\,\tau(\kappa)(c_*-\tau c_*)\) by the verified specialization.
This forces the exterior algebraic unit \(c_*\) to be rational, which is impossible.
A failure of an eventual positive uniform excess for the weakened list would yield a sequence with smaller and smaller excess and increasing admissible exponents, the case just excluded.

For the [two-jet interpolation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) the counts multiply over the separate seed-field embeddings even when their fields meet, since this gives an upper bound on the joint number of tuples.
In the direct product the leading logarithmic term is \(2n(w+1)S_0\), with \(J=2\sum_i\deg P_i\) fixed.
Division by \(2(w+1)-J\) gives the stated leading slope and lets one choose a fixed width for each excess.
Admissibility preserves every powered degree and separates the powered minima.
Dividing two rows by any one of those minima loses just one contact order at its orbit, preserving all other double factors.
The strict weakened-list lower bound and the loss at most \(w\log2\) on converting the larger quotient row's one-norm to Mahler measure contradict the original upper bound after restoring \(nh_i\).
Thus neither row vanishes at any powered node.
For either specified sign, the two reciprocal projections have at least one nonzero candidate value on each orbit; one integer coefficient among \(0,\ldots,s\) avoids all forbidden values.
Their fixed norm cost and the subsequent reciprocal padding factor of norm at most two yield the claimed limit along every diverging width sequence.
At an inadmissible exponent, evaluation of the graph expresses each original root as the same rational function of its powered node, excluding a collision.

Every numerical value in the strict range admits a reciprocal unit minimum by the small-measure reduction.
For the all-power norm representative the degree lost under the relative norm exactly compensates the factor by which the absolute height increases.
The resulting representatives retain injective powering in their own orbits, and a powered collision across two of them would identify their measures \(nh_i\) and \(nh_j\).
Thus the joint realization holds at all sufficiently large integers for these representatives, and on the admissible exponents for the prescribed ones.
The positive slack in \(2S_0<(\log2)/4\) absorbs both the width cost and the interpolation excess.
