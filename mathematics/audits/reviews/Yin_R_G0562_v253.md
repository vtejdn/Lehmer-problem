### Height along a primitive direction

After normalization, a proper subsum has degree at most that of \(F\) and cannot be a scalar multiple of \(F\).
A common absolute factor would give a common rational factor by taking the product of its distinct monic conjugates with respect to a fixed term order.
Thus Bézout gives a finite intersection for each of the finitely many subsets.
At a point \((\beta^p,\beta^q)\) with \(\beta\) nontorsion the exact lattice of multiplicative relations is the kernel of \((i,j)\mapsto ip+jq\).
It determines the primitive direction up to sign, even when one coordinate of the direction is zero.
Projection collisions likewise exclude only finitely many primitive directions.
Identical vanishing along a subtorus would make its rational binomial equation a divisor of \(F\), contradicting irreducibility and rank two.

Two independent support differences give \(N\ll_F W\) by inverting their two-by-two matrix, and \(W\ll_F N\) follows from the fixed support.
For any surviving cut \(i<j\), summing the local bounds for the nonzero initial sum gives \(0\le\log L+(i-j)h(\beta)\).
Here the positive and negative local logarithms of \(\beta\) cancel by the product formula, including the finite places of a nonunit.
Summing the gaps gives the upper bound for \(Wh(\beta)\).
For a unit the effective support in C345 v186 is bounded by the number of terms of \(F\), giving the stated positive lower bound for width times height.
For a nonunit either the primitive minimal polynomial or its reversal has leading coefficient of absolute value at least two, and its degree is at most \(W\).
The finitely many exceptional primitive specializations contribute only finitely many nontorsion heights, all positive.
The identity \(h(\alpha^a)=a h(\alpha)\) gives (1) for every branch.

### Twists and power fields

Rational irreducibility makes the absolutely irreducible factors conjugate after scalar normalization.
Their common support has rank-two differences, since otherwise every exponent sum in their product would lie on one line.
Choose a fixed number field \(K\) containing their coefficients.
For nonproportional translated components, the \(K(\zeta)\)-orbit of the intersection point has at most \(D^2\) elements and its coordinates recover \(\beta\) by primitivity.
The tower law gives relative degree at most \([K:\mathbb Q]D^2\) over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\).
The positive height bound at this degree over an abelian field contradicts (1) for large \(N\).
For proportional components the two independent characters prescribed by coefficient comparison have finite joint kernel and hence finite fibers.
The orders of the torsion coordinates that occur are therefore jointly bounded, and \(\gcd(p,q)=1\) passes their common exponent \(B_F\) to \(\zeta\).
This treats components interchanged by a twist as well as those it preserves.
Lifting two embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\beta^{B_F})\) verifies that any collision of \(j\)-th powers would already be equality.
Counting embeddings and adjoining \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) gives the necessary equality at every exponent.

### The height theorem over the maximal abelian extension

I checked the definition of \(e(\gamma)\) and the statement and proof of Amoroso–David, Proposition 2.4, on manuscript pp. 4–6, and the relative-conjugate argument in [the source unit](../literature/AD22/Yin_U001.md#power-field).
Put \(A=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) and let \(E/A\) be the normal closure of \(A(\gamma)/A\), of degree \(D_0\).
The torsion in the group generated by the \(A\)-conjugates is killed by the exponent defined using all rational conjugates.
Equality of the power fields implies that the powered \(A\)-conjugates generate \(E\) over \(A\).
Consequently \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/A)\) acts faithfully on their free multiplicative group of rank \(r\), giving \(D_0\le3^{r^2}\) by reduction modulo three.
For large \(D_0\) this supplies \(t\asymp(\log\log(16D_0))^{1/3}\) independent unpowered conjugates inside \(E\), all of height \(h(\gamma)\).
Their joint degree over \(A\) is at most \(D_0\).

I checked the product-of-heights inequality and the dimension dependence of its constants against Delsinne's Theorem 1.6, printed pp. 983–984. [Published theorem](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.2114.pdf#page=4)
Writing \(x=\log\log(16D_0)\), the logarithm of each error term \(\log c_2(t)\) and \(\kappa_2(t)x\) is \(O(t^2\log(2t)+\log x)=o(x)\).
Their sum is therefore \(o(\log(16D_0))\), giving \(-\log h(\gamma)\ll\log(16D_0)/t\).
The one-coordinate bound handles bounded \(D_0\).
This verifies Proposition 2.4 at its stated relative normal-closure degree, including the choice of conjugates needed in its proof.
Delsinne's proof and the proofs of the underlying one-dimensional literature bounds were outside this inspection.
Now \(h(\gamma)=B_Fh(\beta)\ll_F N^{-1}\) gives first \(\log(16D_0)\gg_F\log N\), then the extra factor \((\log\log N)^{1/3}\) by substitution.
All \(A\)-conjugates of \(\alpha^{aB_F}\) lie in the normal closure of \(A(\alpha)\), giving (3).
The five-term curve meets these assumptions for \((p,q)=(k,n)\), has no torsion roots at the specified offsets, and is independent of \(k,n\).

### Dilation and the criterion for small measure

At primitive degree \(b\ll_F N\), Voutier's lower bound and the positive bounded-degree floor give \(m(\beta)\gg_F\lambda(N)\), since \(\lambda(CN)\asymp_C\lambda(N)\) for each fixed \(C>0\).
Nonunits already have \(m(\beta)\ge\log2\).
An integral relation after projection has length bounded by the original coefficient length even after terms combine.
Thus C345 v210 applies with a fixed \(R_L\) and gives \(m(\alpha)\ge m(\beta)/R_L\) for every branch, independent of \(a\).
Together with (1) this proves (2) with the logarithmic loss evaluated at \(N\).
On a rank-one component the surviving roots are radicals of a fixed finite set of seeds or their inverses, so the same estimate gives a uniform positive gap for the nontorsion roots.
From any shrinking sequence one can therefore select a single rank-two component and pass to its primitive roots while preserving convergence to zero.
Bounded directions would leave only finitely many such roots, so a subsequence has \(N\to\infty\), and (1) makes the degrees \(o(N)\).
In the converse direction only finitely many primitive directions can make another factor vanish identically, since each such direction forces the corresponding subtorus binomial to divide it.
Discarding these exceptions leaves the actual factors on the original specializations, with measures tending to zero by (1).
Existence of the required sequence of factors remains open.
