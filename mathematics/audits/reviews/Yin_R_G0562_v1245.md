## Discriminant divisibility, real roots and the count in a degree interval

At 2026-11-25T11:06 UTC a reviewer outside proof development checked the whole submitted C G0345 v899 for Yin, including the count over all heights.
The inspection used the submitted claim, the cited mathematical passages in v891, v892 and v108, and Voutier's theorem in the retained author edition.
The reviewer received no development discussion, author explanation or prior finding on v899.

The pole and strict root signature give the irreducibility obstruction in v891.
A complementary factor with negative roots would have positive pole norm greater than \(M^2>K\) if its degree were at least two.
A linear factor \(x+a\) would require \(1+aM\mid4-a\) for \(1\le a\le3\).
The strict grid signs exclude degree two, and the negative trace embeddings make the quadratic lift irreducible since \(v(v+4)<0\) at each such embedding.
The trace is recovered from either lifted root, so distinct trace polynomials yield disjoint lifts.

For \(n_i\le n_j\), the equal normalized pole values imply \(H_j-x^{n_j-n_i}H_i=(Mx-1)B\) with \(B\) integral by primitive division.
The product over the roots of \(H_i\) gives \(\operatorname{Res}(H_i,H_j)=(-1)^{n_i}K\operatorname{Res}(H_i,B)\).
Taking each reciprocal pair over a trace root squares this resultant in the lift.
The factorization of \(S_i\) into quadratics gives a factor \(v(v+4)\) for each trace root and a factor \((v-u)^4\) for each distinct pair of trace roots in its discriminant.
The pole congruence at \(-4\) gives \(K\mid H_i(-4)\), since \(4M+1=K\) and \(M\) is a unit modulo \(K\).
Multiplication of the discriminants and squared cross-resultants therefore supplies \(k+4\binom{k}{2}=2k^2-k\) factors of \(K\).

The nonzero integral constant coefficient gives \(\prod_{v<0}|v|\ge1/w\), which yields \(0<1/M-w<K/M^{n+1}\) from the pole product.
Here \(n\ge3\) and \(2K<M^3\) give \(w>1/(2M)\).
The trace at \(1+1/(2q)\) is \(1/(2q(2q+1))<1/(2M)\), so \(T>1+1/(2q)\).
The identity \(1/M-w=(s-T)(1-1/(sT))\) implies \(0<s-T<pK/M^{n+1}\), and reciprocal differences are smaller.
All exterior roots lie below the same \(s\) and all interior real roots above \(s^{-1}\), so the larger of the two pinning bounds controls each same-side separation.

The two disk inequalities of v108 apply to individual pairs in the squarefree product and allow any regularization \(0<t<1\).
For both real same-side pairs the reflected product is \(1/(T_iT_j)\), so the regularized factor exceeds \(1-1/T_i>1/L\).
The other factors in the pointwise bound are at least one.
Comparison with the direct separation thus saves at least \(A_{ij}\) in each of the four ordered terms, including the case \(A_{ij}=0\).
The absolutely convergent logarithmic series over all ordered pairs gives \(-\mathcal E\), and deleting the diagonal costs at most \(d\log d\) when \(t=1-1/d\).
The radial sum equals \(2(d-1)\sum_i\log T_i\), and \((d-1)\log(d/(d-1))\le1\).
These calculations prove (1) with the energy included.

For \(N\le n_i\le2N\), the pair saving is at least \((N-3)\log M\) and \(d\le4kN\).
After division by \(2kN\), the radial cost is at most \(4k\log s\).
Since \(\log M\ge\log6\) and \(\log s\le\log(3/2)\), the stated \(\gamma>0\) leaves at least \((\gamma/2)k\log M\) on the left after moving the radial cost and allowing the additive \(\log M\), for \(N\ge6/\gamma\).
Absorbing \(2\log k\) into \((\gamma/4)k\log M\) up to an absolute constant gives (2).
In the remaining bounded degrees all coefficients belong to finite integer intervals determined by the uniform root bound four.
Applying the estimate to every finite subfamily permits all grids at once, and summing over dyadic blocks gives the cumulative count and density zero.

For the sum over heights, the Salem root \(T>1\) is nontorsion, its degree is \(2n\ge6\), and its unique exterior conjugate gives \(\log M(T)=\log T<1/q\).
I checked the normalization on p. 1 and the theorem on p. 3 of Voutier's arXiv:1211.3110v1, as identified in Vou96 U001.
I used this established theorem at statement scope and did not audit its determinant proof.
Its lower bound gives \(q<4(\log(2n)/\log\log(2n))^3\) since \(\log\log(2n)>0\).
The ratio is increasing once \(\log(2n)>e\), and the smaller admissible degrees have a finite maximum in the same bound.
Thus one may take \(Q=C(\log X/\log\log X)^3\) for large \(X\) with \(C\) absolute.
The lower and upper pieces cut at \(\sqrt Q\) contribute \(O(\sqrt Q)\) and \(O(Q/\log Q)\) respectively to the sum of \(1/\log(q(q+1))\).
Since \(\log Q\sim3\log\log X\), the total is \(O((\log X)^5/(\log\log X)^4)=o(X)\), which proves density zero for the union of degrees.
Finally \(K(q+2)<2M^3\le2M^n\) gives \(h_q-w<2/(M(q+2))=h_q-h_{q+1}\).
The intervals \((h_{q+1},h_q)\) are disjoint, so the positive trace identifies \(q\) uniquely.
