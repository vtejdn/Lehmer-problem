## Independent review of the product resultant, common factors and the seed threshold

At 2026-11-21T01:11 (UTC), an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin inspected the complete submitted C345 v707 and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no part in developing v707 and checked the fixed proof and its applicable inputs without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on v707.

For two of the given monic polynomials the identity \(F_j-F_i=M H_{ij}\) gives exactly \(M^d\) times an integral resultant by evaluation on the complete root list of \(F_i\).
This latter resultant is nonzero because any common root of \(F_i,H_{ij}\) would also belong to \(F_j\).
The product polynomial has \(kd\) distinct roots by squarefreeness and pairwise coprimality.
In its squared Vandermonde, pairs within blocks yield the individual absolute discriminants, and each unordered pair of blocks yields the square of the corresponding resultant.
Thus the combined power of \(M\) is \(2d\binom k2=dk(k-1)\).
Writing \(D=kd\), the norm bound \(\sqrt D\max(1,|z|)^{D-1}\) for each Vandermonde row gives \(D^D(\prod_i b_i)^{2(D-1)}\) after squaring.
This includes zero roots and \(d=1\), with the one-by-one determinant equal to one for \(D=1\).
The earlier prime-power resultant in C219 is a two-polynomial comparison, and the discriminant here is the polynomial discriminant of the full product.

Since every \(\Delta_i\ge1\), division of the logarithmic bound by \(kd\) gives \((k-1)\log M\le\log(kd)+(2-2/(kd))\sum_i\log b_i\).
This implies \(k\delta\le\log(dM)+\log k\) at \(\delta=\log(M/B^2)>0\).
The inequality \(\log k\le k\delta/2+\log(2/(\mathrm e\delta))\) then gives the stated effective upper bound, including the range where its logarithmic correction is replaced by zero.
Distinct monic irreducible polynomials are pairwise coprime, so this bounds the entire irreducible class with no signature or constant-term condition.
For \(M=2\) and \(B_d\to1\), eventually \(\delta\ge(\log2)/2\), uniformly in the chosen class.
The effective estimate first gives \(k=O(\log(2d))\), and reinsertion into the first inequality gives the upper leading coefficient \(1/\log2\), since \(\log k=o(\log d)\) in a nonempty class.

For two distinct squarefree polynomials with a common factor, the monic rational gcd \(G\) and both monic quotients are integral by Gauss's lemma.
The quotients are coprime, squarefree and of the same positive degree \(e\le d\).
Over \(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\), the leading coefficient of a nonzero polynomial persists under multiplication by monic \(G\), which proves cancellation for every composite modulus as well.
The \(k=2\) bound for these quotients gives \(M\le2e(\mathcal M(U)\mathcal M(V))^{2-1/e}\) after taking the \(2e\)-th root.
Multiplicativity and \(\mathcal M(G)\ge1\) bound both quotient measures by \(B\).
As \(e\le d\) and \(B\ge1\), this yields \(M\le2dB^{4-2/d}\) for every distinct pair and hence the strict uniqueness threshold in (3).

For fixed \(n,p,\epsilon,a\bmod p\), the monic cofactor \(U\) depends only on \(n,\epsilon\), and the degree \(d=n+1-\deg U\le p\) is common to all candidate seeds in (4).
The field radical generates the same field as \(r\), so raising its distinct conjugates to the \(p\)-th power gives all conjugates of \(r\) once.
Frobenius preserves the elementary symmetric residues, hence fixes the full residue polynomial at \(P_a=G_a/U\bmod p\).
The radical is Salem, its polynomial has measure \(\beta\), and its unique exterior root determines the seed through \(r=\beta^p\) and the displayed rational expression for \(a\) with positive denominator.
Thus the count of polynomials bounds the count of seeds without an additional multiplicity factor.
Taking \(M=p,\ B=c\sqrt p\ge1\) gives \(\delta=-2\log c\) and \(\log(dp)\le2\log p\), yielding \(O_c(\log(2p))\) for all \(0<c<1\).
For \(B<1\) the seed range is empty.
The earlier residue analysis is used here only to fix the arithmetic parameters, polynomial signature and injectivity.

The finite-coincidence theorem in v701 disposes of the exact-seed alternative of v698, including the possible smaller seed two.
For the other alternative \(j_p=1+\lfloor(\sqrt{2p-1}-1)/2\rfloor\), hence \(p/(4j_p)=\sqrt{p/8}(1+O(p^{-1/2}))\).
The effective radical bound in v689 gives \(p\le C'd^3\) after an effective enlargement, since \(\log^5(3d)/d\) is bounded.
In particular \(\log(p/(4j_p))/(n+1)=O(\log p/p^{1/3})\) for large \(p\), uniformly over seeds, delays and signs.
Since \(0\le n+1-d\le2\), raising \(p/(4j_p)\) to \(d/(n+1)\) incurs a multiplicative factor tending effectively to one.
This proves the lower limit \(1/\sqrt8\), and substituting \(\log r=p\log\beta\) yields the second estimate of (5).
For a fixed strict margin \(c<1/\sqrt8\), these effective errors and the finite coincidence list bound \(p\), and then \(n<p\) and \(a<1+(c\sqrt p)^p\) bound the remaining parameters.
Factoring the substituted minimal polynomial and isolating its positive \(p\)-th radical tests whether its degree equals \(d\), which decides membership in \(\mathbb Q(r)\).
The range \(p\log p\ge 2\log r+tp\) is exactly \(\beta\le e^{-t/2}\sqrt p\), giving finiteness when \(t>\log8\).
Likewise \(p\log p\ge(2+\eta)\log r\) forces \(\beta/\sqrt p\le p^{-\eta/(2(2+\eta))}\), and at bounded \(p\) it bounds \(r\) and the seed.
