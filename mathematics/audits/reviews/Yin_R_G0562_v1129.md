# Independent review of the obstruction to concentration near rotating rays

## Torsion quotients, both averages and the normalized ordered-pair count

At 2026-11-24T22:58 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v72.md#statement) and both proof sections of C0901 v72.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v72.
The strict alternative (1) holds for either definition of \(p\) in the statement, including nontrivial torsion quotients, nonintegral inputs and zero arc width.
The finite covering bound and the necessary growth in the number of rays along shrinking positive-measure unit sequences hold at the given scales.

For an algebraic number \(z\ne0,1\) let \(p\) be its positive fraction in the indicated angular neighborhood of the positive ray.
Under the assumed narrow-width inequality put \(L=\log(8/p)\) and \(N=\lceil16L/p\rceil\).
A primitive root of unity of order \(q\ge2\) and all its conjugates have angular distance at least \(2\pi/q\) from the positive ray.
If \(q<N\), then \(2\pi/q>2\pi/N\ge2\pi p/(17L)>p/(100L)\), incompatible with \(p>0\).
If \(q\ge N\), none of the positive powers with exponent less than \(N\) is one.
Thus \(A_N(z)\) is nonzero in every case, including \(q=N\), and the product formula is legitimate.

At \(v=\sigma z\) the factor divided by \(\max(1,|v|)^j\) equals \(|1-v^j|\) for \(|v|\le1\) and \(|1-v^{-j}|\) otherwise.
In either case it is at most \(1-e^{-js}+|1-e^{ij\theta}|\le j(s+|\theta|)\), where \(s=|\log|v||\) and \(\theta\) is a principal argument.
No selected embedding has \(s+|\theta|=0\), because that would give \(v=1\).
The weights \((N-j)/B\) sum to one and their weighted mean of \(j\) is \((N+1)/3=J\).
Concavity of the logarithm in the first average therefore bounds the local normalized log product divided by \(B\) by \(\log(J(s+|\theta|))\).
In the second average the conditional mean of \(s\) is at most \(2h(z)/p\), since the unconditional archimedean mean is at most \(h(z)+h(z^{-1})\), with complex embeddings counted individually.
Every selected argument has \(|\theta|\le\varepsilon\).
Jensen on this same subset gives its full contribution at most \(p\log(J(2h(z)/p+\varepsilon))\).
If \(h(z)=\varepsilon=0\) with \(p>0\), all archimedean log moduli vanish and a selected value would be one, which is excluded.

On the complementary embeddings the normalized archimedean bound \(N^{N/2}\) contributes at most \((1-p)\log N/(N-1)\) after division by \(B\).
Every finite-place normalized factor is bounded by one through integrality of \(A_N\), while the factors extracted at all places supply \(Jh(z)\) since \(\deg A_N=BJ\).
Assuming also \(h(z)\le p^2/(100L)\) gives \(J(2h(z)/p+\varepsilon)\le18/100=9/50\).
Thus the left side of the product-formula inequality is at least \(p\log(50/9)>p\), and the right side is at most \(2p/15+6p/100<p\).
This contradiction establishes the strict height bound under the width condition without an integrality or nontorsion requirement on \(z\).

For two distinct original conjugates \(a,b\), \(z=a/b\) is nonzero and different from one, with \(h(z)\le h(a)+h(b)=2h(w)\).
Angular distance modulo \(2\pi\) of the transported pair is exactly the absolute principal angle of the transported quotient.
Restriction from the splitting group counts each embedding of the quotient field with the same multiplicity, so its angular fraction is the prescribed-pair probability and the factor two in the height denominator is required.
Summing the indicated probabilities over ordered distinct pairs and assigning zero to the diagonal reproduces the original fixed-embedding count after dividing by \(d^2\), since every automorphism permutes all roots.
Some off-diagonal pair has probability at least the positive aggregate \(p\).
Both \(x/\log(8/x)\) and \(x^2/\log(8/x)\) increase on \(0<x\le1\), so either strict alternative for that pair yields the corresponding alternative for the aggregate.

Assigning each selected root once resolves overlaps among arcs with arbitrary centers.
Within one cell any two points have angular distance at most the arc length.
The ordered off-diagonal count is at least \(\sum_i(n_i^2-n_i)\), where \(\sum_i n_i=\sigma d\).
Cauchy's inequality divided by \(d^2\) gives \(\sigma^2/T-\sigma/d\), with the same normalization as the quotient argument.
At fixed positive fraction and \(T\le C\sqrt{d/\log d}\), the subtraction is negligible and the lower bound is \(\gg_{\sigma,C}\sqrt{\log d/d}\).
The logarithm of eight divided by this lower bound is \(O_{\sigma,C}(\log d)\).
Hence the two thresholds are respectively \(\gg_{\sigma,C}1/\sqrt{d\log d}\) for the width and \(\gg_{\sigma,C}1\) for total measure after multiplication by \(d\).
Any subsequence with the stated upper bound on the number of arcs contradicts little-oh widths and shrinking positive measures, since bounded-degree finiteness forces \(d\to\infty\).
Setting the width to zero proves the claimed little-omega growth in the number of rays, with the centers allowed to depend on the input.
