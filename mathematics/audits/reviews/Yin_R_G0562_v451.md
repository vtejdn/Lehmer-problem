### Uniform exterior counts

Write \(n=sa+b\), \(R=e^{T/a}\) and \(w=Rz\).
In \(-F_{a,b}(w)/w^n\) the two leading terms give \(1-w^{-b}\), and the other four have reciprocal exponents \((s-1)a+b,sa+b,sa,a\).
They contribute at most \(4e^{-T}\) in modulus, including when terms coincide.
Since the absolute leading coefficient is one, averaging the logarithm of the normalized polynomial gives exactly \(\sum_\alpha(\log|\alpha|-\log R)_+\), with multiplicity.
This follows by averaging each \(\log|1-\alpha/(Rz)|\), whose logarithmic singularity is integrable also at \(|\alpha|=R\).
The leading binomial has mean logarithm zero since \(u=R^{-b}<1\) and \(z^{-b}\) preserves Haar probability.
The excess is therefore bounded above by the mean of \(\log(1+\epsilon/|1-ue^{i\theta}|)\), with \(\epsilon=4e^{-T}<1\).
For \(u<1/2\) this is \(O(\epsilon)\).
For \(u\ge1/2\) the denominator dominates a constant times \(|\theta|\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\).
Rescaling by \(\epsilon\) on the inner interval and integrating \(O(\epsilon/|\theta|)\) on the outer interval gives \(O((1+T)e^{-T})\), uniformly as \(u\uparrow1\).

For bounded \(r\), the separated-block and endpoint bounds in v391 give \(c_{s,R_0}\le m(P)\le\log6\) for every \(r\le R_0\), including nonprimitive pairs.
The entire tail above \(\log R\) bounds the corresponding tail of \(P\), hence \(m(P)\le N(P)T/a+O((1+T)e^{-T})\).
Choosing \(T\) once for the fixed slope bound gives \(N(P)\gg_{s,R_0}a\).
Since \(N(P)\le d\le(s+R_0)a\), both comparisons in (1) follow on this range.
For sufficiently large primitive \(r\), the allocation formula in v391 gives \(m(P)\asymp_s N(P)/a\).
The height limit in v198 applies to the coprime reversal pair with unit endpoints and rank-two support, and gives \(m(P)\asymp_s d/b\).
Choosing the cutoff in terms of \(s\) joins the two ranges with constants depending only on \(s\).
Primitive directions beyond this cutoff preserve irreducibility of all nontorsion factors under every common dilation.
In a dilation by \(q\) the degree and exterior count are multiplied by \(q\), while the Mahler measure stays the same.
This accounts for all factors, since roots over torsion are torsion.
An infinite sequence of distinct pairs with bounded slope has \(a\to\infty\), so bounded exterior count forces the slopes to escape every bounded interval.

### The real root

The exterior disks can be chosen disjoint from the real line at every nonreal pole.
For \(u>1\), \(B(u)<0\), while \(B(-u)>0\) for odd \(s\) and \(B(-u)=-V_s(u)\) for even \(s\).
The unique zero \(\beta_s>1\) in the latter case is simple since \(u^{s-1}(u-1)\) increases strictly from zero to infinity.
Thus a real exterior root is possible only for even \(s\), odd \(a\), and negative \(X=-\sigma\).
Substitution with \(u=\sigma^a\) gives exactly \(V_s(u)=(-1)^b u^{-r}G_s(u)\).
On a fixed interval about \(\beta_s\) in \((1,\infty)\) the right side and its derivative tend uniformly to zero.
The difference increases strictly there with opposite signs at the endpoints, giving exactly one solution.
Its displacement from \(\beta_s\) has sign \((-1)^b\) since \(G_s>0\).
The initial exponential bound gives \(r|u-\beta_s|=o(1)\), hence \(u^{-r}/\beta_s^{-r}=1+o(1)\).
The equation and \(V_s'(\beta_s)>0\) then yield \(u-\beta_s=O_s(\beta_s^{-r})\).
Substituting this into \(u^{-r}\) gives relative error \(O_s(r\beta_s^{-r})\), and Taylor expansion of \(G_s,V_s\) gives (3).

The full primitive section has only \(2at_s\) off-circle roots, whereas each nontorsion factor has degree \(\gg_s a\sqrt r\).
At large \(r\) each such factor therefore contains a circle root, whose complex conjugate is its inverse, forcing reciprocity by irreducibility.
A lone exterior root is real by conjugation, and the sign change of its minimal polynomial gives a Salem number with its positive reciprocal and at least one unit-circle conjugate.
Conversely the minimal polynomial of the negative of a Salem number has exactly one exterior root.

### Accumulation points and the quartic exception

Every unbounded sequence of distinct pairs admitting a one-exterior-root factor has slopes tending to infinity.
The dilation formula then forces the common divisor to be one eventually, so the real-branch restrictions apply.
Along \(a\to\infty\), (3) gives \(\sigma=u^{1/a}\to1\) from above, hence one is an accumulation point of distinct Salem numbers.
Along fixed \(a\), \(\sigma\to\beta_s^{1/a}\), and the increasing factor degrees exclude a finite list of Salem numbers.
The inequality \(\theta^5(\theta-1)=\theta>1\) gives \(\beta_s\le\beta_6<\theta\) for even \(s\ge6\).
For \(s=4\), \(\beta_4<2<\theta^3\) suffices for every odd \(a\ge3\).
An infinite sequence supplies a fixed-\(a\) or an \(a\to\infty\) subsequence, which proves the conditional finiteness outside \(s=4,a=1\).
On printed page 455 Boyd attributes the least Pisot number to Siegel and presents \(T'=S\) as a suggested equality.
Only \(T'\cap[1,\theta)=\varnothing\) enters the conditional finiteness proof. [Boyd, published edition, p. 455](../../literature/pdf/Boy81--published.pdf#page=3)

For \(s=4\) the single real critical point of \(g\) and its signs give two real roots, \(-1/\beta_4\) and \(\gamma>1\).
From \(g(\beta_4)=\beta_4^3-\beta_4>0\) one obtains \(\gamma<\beta_4\), so the remaining conjugate pair has squared modulus \(\beta_4/\gamma>1\).
Thus \(B\) has exactly one exterior root, \(-\beta_4\), and all its other roots are strictly interior.
This also gives the Pisot property of \(\beta_4\) after sign change.
The exactly \(a\) exterior roots at large primitive slope are simple and each nontorsion factor must receive at least one.
For \(a=1\) this gives one factor, and for \(a=2\) the two nonreal exterior roots must belong to the same factor by conjugation.
For \(a=3,\ 3\nmid b\), the unique real exterior root and the nonreal pair exhaust the exterior set.
Splitting must therefore separate the real root from that pair, and the Salem property just proved is equivalent to precisely this separation.
There are exactly two simple factors in the split case.

At \(7/5\) the value of \(u^3(u-1)\) is \(686/625>1\), and \(7/5<(9/8)^3\), so the limit \(\beta_4^{1/3}\) is strictly below \(9/8\).
The Lehmer polynomial evaluates at \(9/8\) to \(-941480999/8^{10}\).
Its unique real root above one consequently exceeds \(9/8\), by the positive leading coefficient.
Hence a sufficiently late split gives the stated strict comparison, and infinitely many splits would produce the forbidden accumulation point under Boyd's proposed equality.
No exclusion of the quartic partitions with several exterior roots per factor for odd \(a\ge5\) follows from this argument.
