### The abelian power and the output module

Put \(B=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
If \(u^j\in B\), every root of \(X^j-u^j\) belongs to \(B(u)\), so the extension over \(B\) is finite Galois even though \(B/\mathbb Q\) is infinite.
Its action on \(u\) identifies the group with a cyclic subgroup of \(\mu_j\) of order \(n\), and \(u^m\in B\) holds exactly when \(n\mid m\).
Putting \(w=u^n\), the inequalities \(n=[B(u):B]\le[\mathbb Q(u):\mathbb Q(w)]\le n\) give the degree factor that cancels the height factor in \(h(w)=nh(u)\).
Thus \(M(w)=M(u)\) for the least exponent.

For an abelian unit \(w\), every conjugate of \(w\overline w\) is the squared modulus of the corresponding conjugate of \(w\).
A neutral conjugate would force \(w\overline w=1\), and then Kronecker's theorem would make \(w\) torsion.
Taking \(a=\sqrt{w\overline w}>0\) produces a totally real unit, since every image of \(w\overline w\) is positive.
On a common number field all embeddings give \(|\sigma a|=|\sigma w|\), so equal embedding multiplicities give \(h(a)=h(w)\).
For nontorsion \(w\), all \(l_\sigma=|\log|\sigma a||\) are positive and their mean is \(2h(a)\).
The norm of \(a-a^{-1}\) and concavity give \(1\le\exp(\operatorname{mean}\log(2\sinh l_\sigma))\le2\sinh(2h(a))\).
This proves the unit case of [Schinzel's height bound](../literature/Sch73/Yin_U001.md#linear-height) within the argument.
Reciprocity survives taking powers and forces even degree by pairing distinct inverses.
The height bound gives \(M(w)\ge\varphi^2\) from degree four on, and in degree two the integer trace has absolute value at least three, attaining equality at trace three.
Both the strict measure cutoff and the alternative neutral-place hypothesis therefore exclude abelian powers, including powers of a radical with an initial torsion multiplier.

For \(w=y^j\in F\), clearing denominators reduces the kernel of \(\log|\cdot|\) on rational unit classes to the identity \(|z|=1\iff z\overline z=1\).
If the class of \(z\overline z\) vanishes, this positive real number is a root of unity and hence equals one.
The kernel is precisely the negative eigenspace, so projection onto the fixed space separates the absolute logarithms of its nonzero projected classes modulo sign.
Reciprocity supplies the positive sign in each pair, and extension of embeddings through \(y^j=w\) gives distinct exterior conjugates for distinct positive logarithms.
Their number bounds \(\dim W^\tau\) from below.

The irreducible input is separable, its Galois group acts transitively on inverse pairs, and the signs and label permutation determine each automorphism of its splitting field.
The odd prime \(p\) divides the group order exactly once.
For an order-\(p\) subgroup \(S\), changing pair representatives removes the signs from its cycle.
The normalizing label permutations are affine, and a diagonal sign change normalizing the pure cycle must be constant.
Thus \(N_G(S)/S\) embeds into \(C_2\times\mathbb F_p^\times\).
If the representation kernel \(K\) contained \(S\), Sylow conjugacy inside \(K\) would give \(G=K N_G(S)\), making \(F^K/\mathbb Q\) abelian.
The finite set of torsion ratios \(k(w)/w\) would then put a common power of \(w\) in \(F^K\).
The abelian-power exclusion forces a nontrivial rational \(S\)-constituent of dimension \(p-1\).
For \(t\) conjugates generating a transitive action on labels, their subgroup contains such an \(S\).
Their common negative eigenspace is stable under each generator, which acts there by a scalar, so the odd-order subgroup acts trivially there.
Comparing codimensions gives \(p-1\le t\dim W^\tau\le t b(y)\).

### The two matching estimates

The transpositions of a label involution are exactly the edges of its matching.
The orbits of any selected involutions are the connected components of the union, since an edge can be traversed by applying its own involution to its endpoint.
For translations along a \(p\)-cycle, the \(p\) shifts of a single edge of difference \(d\ne0\) form the simple cycle of step \(d\).
Every proper nonempty component of size \(a\) has at least two cut edges in this cycle and at most \(a\) incident crossing edges in one shifted matching.
Linearity of expectation gives a touching probability at least \(2c/(pa)\), without independence of the shifts of different edges.
If \(s\) components are touched, their images after merging have at most \(s/2\) components.
Consequently a shift can decrease the count \(k\) by at least \((c/p)\sum_A |A|^{-1}\ge ck^2/p^2\).
With \(K_0=\lceil p/\sqrt c\rceil\), the steps before reaching \(k\le K_0\) each increase \(1/k\) by at least \(c/p^2\).
If there are \(m>0\) such steps, immediately before the last one \(1/p+(m-1)c/p^2<1/K_0\).
Adding the at most \(K_0-1\) subsequent merges proves the stated \(T_1\), also when \(m=0\).
In particular \(T_1\le 2p/\sqrt c+1\), which suffices for divergence without a growth-rate condition on \(c\).

Burnside's theorem applies to the transitive unsigned image of prime degree.
In its affine alternative the image contains the translations, and a nonidentity involution has the form \(x\mapsto d-x\).
Two distinct translates of this reflection generate a nonzero translation in their product.
Two conjugates suffice, and \(T_2\ge1+2\log 2>2\) even at the smallest prime.
In the doubly transitive alternative each original edge becomes uniform among unordered pairs.
A component of size \(a\) thus receives a crossing with probability at least \(2c(p-a)/(p(p-1))\), by dividing its expected crossing count by the maximum \(a\).
Summing over the components and halving for merges shows that \(k-1\) can be reduced to at most \((1-c/(p-1))(k-1)\).
Starting with \(p-1\), any integer number of steps strictly greater than \((p-1)\log(p-1)/c\) suffices for the residual integer to vanish.
The floor-plus-one choice gives \(t\le T_2\) also when the logarithmic expression is an integer.
All selected label conjugations lift to \(G\), so each of the two step estimates applies to the same representation inequality.

### The five-term family and the scope of the obstruction

In v306 the characteristic-\(p\) Frobenius orbits of \(X^p-X-1\) have length \(p\), and a real trace in \((-2,2)\) forces the quadratic lift to double the degree.
Counting the neutral traces by alternating signs and interlacing leaves \(p/3+O(1)\) exterior roots and exactly one real exterior root.
Pairing the others by complex conjugation gives \(c=p/6+O(1)\).
The specialization parameter of \((1,p)\) is \(p\), so the fixed Laurent polynomial meets the hypotheses of the recorded Lawton theorem.
The Jensen integral is positive, and the concavity estimate in v306 puts its exponential \(L\) strictly below the plastic number.
Since the input measures are eventually strictly below Smyth's threshold, every nontorsion output \(y\) with \(M(y)\le M(P_p)\) is a subplastic algebraic integer and hence reciprocal.
Substitution gives \((p-1)/T_2=c/(\log(p-1)+c/(p-1))=(1/6+o(1))p/\log p\), uniformly in \(y\).
The obstruction consequently applies on every input interval \((1,A)\) with \(A>L\).
An interval ending below \(L\) and the existence of inputs approaching one with increasing \(c\) remain open here.
The source checks used the recorded statements of Burnside, Smyth and Lawton at their stated scope.
Their original proofs fall outside this review.
