# Nested disk contributions to the two-row resultant

## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T11:40 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin checked the complete [November 18 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#decision-nested-row-resultant-submitted) and the two dependency passages bound below.
The reviewer had no role in developing this proof and received no development dialogue, author explanation or earlier verdict on it.
The additive resultant estimate holds on every finite tree in the statement.
The comparison with the full local power index holds when every mixed disk in the complete collision tree satisfies (2).

**A single pair of rows on the tree.**
Stripping the two monomials preserves coprimality since the remaining polynomials have nonzero constant terms.
At a specified root neither row can vanish, so two roots in one power fiber would have quotient annihilated by the two coprime exponents \(p\) and \(r_1-r_0\).
The specified powered roots are therefore distinct.
After removal of common content, some coefficient pair has nonzero reduction.
It excludes at most one pencil parameter by requiring that coefficient of the row combination to be nonzero.
The top coefficient pair of each scaled disk gives the same type of exclusion.
A single finite residue extension contains two distinct nonzero parameters outside the finite union of exclusions, and their lifts give a unit determinant.
For each resulting scaled row, reduction of a normalized factor \(Y-z\) contributes one to the degree when \(z\) is integral, while a factor at a nonintegral root can be written as \(1-Y/z\) up to scalar and contributes zero.
The product of primitive factors is primitive, giving exactly \(n_D\) roots of either row in \(D\), with multiplicities.

**What the local length measures.**
Use the absolute ramification index of the final splitting field to convert uniformizer valuation to \(v(\ell)=1\).
At an original residue \(b\), factors with roots outside \(b\) are units in the local power series ring after normalization.
For a primitive second row the remaining monic factor reduces to a power of \(T\).
In the quotient by that factor the powers of \(T\) below its degree are a free basis by distinguished division.
Over the splitting field the determinant of multiplication by the first row is the product of its values at the roots of the second factor, also for repeated roots by triangularization on each local factor.
The ideal length thus includes the first row's scalar content once for each root of the second row in \(b\), besides the sum of the pairwise valuations in \(b\).
The global resultant includes these scalar contributions and all these normalized linear-factor determinants, with the other normalized determinants integral.
The unit change of generators preserves the local quotient, and primitivity of both new rows now expresses its normalized length solely by their root pairs.
Each such pair is charged on an ancestor chain, whose increments telescope to a depth at most its actual separation valuation.
Thus even sibling disks in a common original residue are covered by the single local length there.
Content restoration adds its valuation times the sum of the original degrees, and degree loss in the pencil has no effect on the local ideal.
For a constant distinguished factor the local length and the corresponding original pair count are zero.

**Higher jets on the same scaled residue.**
The inverse branch of \(z^p=X\) at a unit has integral coefficients and unit linear term since \(\ell\ne p\).
Negative powers of this branch also have integral coefficients.
Translation to \(y_i+Z\) preserves integrality of the divided rows, and positive depth makes the multiplier \(f_i\) constant after reduction.
This checks the use of the ledger's single-node Taylor calculation even when different nodes have the same scaled residue.
Two different original residues in the disk yield different constants since their quotient has order \(p\) and \(p\nmid k_0\).
If \(n_D<e\), the corresponding two row combinations must both vanish as polynomials, contrary to the joint normalization.
For the stronger count put \(h=a+f_i b\) locally.
Substituting \(a=h-f_i b\) in \(K_D\) gives \(pX(h'b-hb')-\tau_D k_0hb\), using \(pXf_i'=\tau_D k_0 f_i\).
Its order at \(y_i\) is at least \(e-1\).
The points \(y_i\) are distinct in characteristic zero, so these orders add before reduction.
For a separable nonconstant quotient the reduced Wronskian is nonzero, including the square of the common factor, and its degree is at most \(2n_D-2\).
The integral root count therefore gives (2), including for \(e=1\).
In the inseparable nonconstant case the quotient lies in \(k(Y^\ell)\), so its degree is at least \(\ell\), which gives the stated numerical alternative.

**Passage to the index.**
Evaluating the integral change of power basis at all conjugates gives the two Vandermonde determinants in the cited power-index identity.
Degree preservation makes their quotient nonzero and its absolute value is the index of the two orders.
Although the cited Claim assumes nontorsion for its combined results, this determinant calculation applies verbatim to every algebraic unit in the present degree-preserving statement.
When two original residues agree, the power-difference quotient reduces to \(p\bar\alpha_i^{p-1}\), a unit.
For distinct original residues the denominator is a unit.
Every positive numerator valuation for such a pair is the sum of the depth increments through the disk where the two powered nodes first separate on rescaling.
Pairs already separated modulo the maximal ideal contribute zero.
This proves the identity with \(\sum_D\delta_D J_D\).
Only mixed disks have \(J_D>0\), and \(2J_D\le m_D(m_D-1)\le m_D^2\).
Where (2) holds, this gives \(n_D^2\ge (e-1)^2J_D/2\), yielding the claimed conditional factor, including \(1/2\) for double roots.
The unconditional result weights mixed depths by \(e^2\).
A lower bound by a fixed positive fraction of the index on arbitrary mixed disks remains open here.
