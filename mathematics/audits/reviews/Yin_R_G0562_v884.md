# Repeated Frobenius powers in a height bound for a subset of conjugates

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:00 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the submitted [C345 v744](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v744.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing this argument and received the fixed claim without its development discussion or a prior verdict.
The review included the finite inequalities, their conditional asymptotic, the application at \(29\) and the actual uses of the supporting field construction.

For a uniform lift of the \(k\)-th power of residue Frobenius, the conditional image of \(b\) is uniform over a translate of its inertia orbit.
That orbit has size \(e=e(P\cap\mathbb Q(b)/p)\).
When the image \(a\) is possible and \(b\) is integral at \(P\), lifting its residue in the local root field to a root of \(X^{p^f}-X\) gives the comparison with \(b^{p^k}\) to valuation at least \(1/e\), as in the [single-level calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#edges).
For local integral elements of difference valuation \(s\), their \(p\)-th powers differ to valuation at least \(\min(ps,1+s)\) by binomial expansion around either element.
As long as \(s\ge1/e\), both expressions are at least \(s+1/e\), including \(p=2\).
Iteration gives valuation at least \((i+1)/e\) between levels \(i\) and \(i+k\).
Inversion above absolute value one preserves the ramification index of the root field and turns the difference into the normalized chordal factor used at finite places.
Outside a hit that factor is at most one.
After averaging, a gain of \((i+1)\log p\) times the conditional hit probability follows at each ordered pair across the two levels.

Heights \(p^i h>0\) distinguish the levels.
Equality within an earlier level implies equality after powering up to the last level, contrary to its injectivity.
For Salem conjugates a collision of positive powers would give two conjugates of the same modulus as the unique exterior conjugate after a Galois automorphism, which is impossible for two distinct elements.
Each cross-level pair occurs once in the ordinary Vandermonde product and each row maximum has total exponent \(rJ-1\).
Normalized local degrees in the splitting field over \(p\) are equal, so the valuation average is the one required by the product formula.
Archimedean row norms cost \(rJ\log(rJ)/2\) before division by \(r\).
The height sum before multiplication by \(rJ-1\) is \(rh\sum_{i<J}p^i\).
The gain for a given difference \(k\) sums \(1,\ldots,J-k\), yielding the displayed binomial weight and (1).
Its total is \(J(J^2-1)/6\), giving (2) since the coefficient left after division by \(J\) is at most \(r\sum_{i<J}p^i\).
For the stated \(J\) the positive part grows like a positive constant times \(J^2\).
Taking logarithms in \(rh\gg J^2p^{-J}\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\) gives the constant \(\sqrt{3\log p/\eta}\).

The primitive trace-field construction permits the single split prime \(29\), with distinct auxiliary split primes larger than \(n\) to force the Galois group.
The completely split local pattern at \(29\) requires distinct roots over \(\mathbb Q_{29}\), which the [construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#product) obtains even for \(n>29\) by sufficiently close approximation to a polynomial with distinct integral roots.
Its auxiliary permutations generate the full triple symmetric-group kernel, and adjoining a coordinate rotation yields the required full action.
The stabilizer giving the index \(n^3\) is maximal by the single-coordinate transposition argument.
The [trace realization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#salem) supplies a Salem number in the specified sense, while for every other generator of the same trace field the embeddings again lift equivariantly to entire inverse pairs.
Restriction of a uniform prime of the larger Galois extension gives a uniform trace prime, and all inertia permutations fix the trace field closure at these unramified trace primes.
Thus the trace-group overlap determines the full-pair overlap regardless of the lift orientations.

Conditional on the constant third coordinate being the fixed coordinate of the Frobenius action, the other two coordinate permutations for \(n\nmid k\) are independent uniform \(n\)-cycles after taking the \(k\)-th power, since the power map permutes the \(n\)-cycles when \(n\) is prime to \(k\).
Otherwise the third coordinate changes and the triangle cannot intersect itself.
Among all ordered pairs of triangle points, the same-row and same-column counts are each \(\sum_{j=1}^n j^2\) and their overlap is \(u\).
Dividing \(u^2-2\sum j^2+u\) by \(3u(n-1)^2\) gives \((3n-2)/(18(n-1))\).
When \(n\mid k\) all trace points are fixed, giving overlap one.
The different sizes of rows and of columns prevent a nontrivial action on the triangle by its setwise stabilizer.
Finally \(r=n(n+1)\) and \(rh=(n+1)\log\alpha/(2n^2)\), so the change from \(\sqrt{\log r}\) to \(\sqrt{\log n}\) multiplies the constant in (4) by \(\sqrt2\).
Taking \(\eta=1/6\) gives \(6\sqrt{\log 29}\) with the asserted sign.
