# Coefficient length and the density of sparse evaluations

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T06:34 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed [C345 v783](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v783.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of its finite bound and both shrinking-sequence conclusions.
The reviewer had no part in developing the proof and received the fixed text and its inputs without development discussions or earlier findings about this claim.

A common exponent shift multiplies each row by a nonzero scalar, preserving its relative residual even for a negative shift.
For \(s\ge2\) and \(C=Wh(\alpha)\), the mean logarithmic cost of the nonzero normalized determinants in C345 v740 is bounded by precisely \(A_s(C)\).
For \(s\) rows with relative residual at most \(\epsilon_*\), the unitary change of basis gives normalized determinant at most \(s\epsilon_*\).
The cost of \((\eta d/2)^s\) independent tuples would therefore exceed the mean bound, since \((\eta/2)^s\log(1/(s\epsilon_*))=A_s(C)+(\eta/2)^s\).
The resulting subspace yields, by the Galois partition of C345 v740, a relation at \(\alpha\) over a subfield of its own field, with one coefficient one and heights at most \(2(\log(s!)+sC)\).
This argument uses only the existence of the relation and applies to all complex columns of norm one.
Dividing each dimension threshold by eight turns the downward recursion into successive squaring, giving \(\tau_1=8(\eta/16)^{2^{s-2}}\), also for \(s=2\).
If \(T=(16/\eta)^{2^{s-2}}\), then \(T\ge16\) and \(\lceil2/\tau_1\rceil=\lceil T/4\rceil\le T\).

The product over all embeddings of the coefficient field is a rational polynomial by Galois invariance.
All its factors are nonzero because a coefficient equals one and the powers are distinct, and the identity factor vanishes at \(\alpha\).
At \(p\), the maximum of the rational coefficient norms is bounded above by \(\prod_{v\mid p}\max_i|b_i|_v^{[E_v:\mathbb Q_p]}\) by the ultrametric inequality on the product over embeddings into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\).
Every factor in this bound is at least one, so it bounds the \(p\)-part of the least common denominator even if every rational coefficient is \(p\)-adically small.
Only finitely many primes can contribute.
Each archimedean factor has coefficient length at most \(s\max_i|\tau b_i|\), and coefficient length is submultiplicative.
Taking positive local logarithms coefficient by coefficient with these local degrees and counting both complex embeddings yields \(\log\lambda(\alpha)\le M\log s+M\sum_i h(b_i)\le M B_s(C)\).
Cancellation can only lower the length.
The nonzero \(\alpha\) admits no monomial annihilator, so \(\log\lambda(\alpha)>0\).

For a positive measured proportion put \(\eta=p_\epsilon\).
If \(\epsilon\le\epsilon_*\), monotonicity of the measured set gives \(\log\lambda(\alpha)\le(16/\eta)^{2^{s-2}}B_s(C)\), yielding the first density bound.
Otherwise \(\log(1/(s\epsilon))-1\) is positive by hypothesis and strictly smaller than \(A_s(C)(2/\eta)^s\), yielding the second bound.
At \(\epsilon=0\) only the first case occurs, and a zero proportion satisfies the inequality directly.
This also gives the stated necessary condition for an exact hyperplane of density at least \(\eta\).

C345 v183 supplies a single shrinking sequence with \(\lambda(\gamma_n)\to\infty\), with power stability and the stated Salem specialization.
Substitution in any integer annihilator of \(\gamma_n^{q_n}\) preserves the length and produces a nonzero annihilator of \(\gamma_n\).
Thus the denominator in the first term is at least \(\log\lambda(\gamma_n)\) simultaneously for every chosen positive power.
For each of the finitely many allowed term counts, \(A_s(u_n)\) and \(B_s(u_n)\) are \(O_s(1+u_n)\).
The length assumption sends the first ratio to zero, and the threshold assumption sends the second to zero, with exact zero thresholds already covered.
A single normalized term has relative magnitude one at all embeddings.
In particular a positive-density test of bounded height cost at shrinking thresholds on a subsequence of every shrinking sequence would contradict this representative at \(q_n=1\).
The proof uses no bound on the measures of the selected powers.
