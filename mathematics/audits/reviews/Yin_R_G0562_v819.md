## Verification of the two signed families

Both factorizations, the one-sided limits at two, and irreducibility under every positive power substitution follow for all \(n\ge4\).

The circle map is \((z-1/2)/(1-z/2)\) up to the constant sign, so its lifted phase increases by \(2\pi\) and its derivative is \(3/|2-z|^2\le3\).
The phase difference is strictly increasing through \(n-1\) turns.
This gives simple roots, including at either endpoint of an angular period when counted only once.
At \(n=4\) one has \(G_{4,+}(3/2)=-17/32\) and \(G_{4,-}(9/4)=2977/1024\), and increasing \(n\) strengthens each sign.
The values at two are \(3\epsilon\).
Reciprocity up to the factor \(-\epsilon\) supplies \(1/r\), exhausting the degree.
The minimal polynomial of the exterior unit must contain the sole interior root, by its unit norm.
The inequalities \(2<r+1/r<9/4+4/9<3\) preclude a quadratic minimum.
Thus the minimum has a circle conjugate and \(r\) is Salem.
The remaining monic factors have nonzero constant coefficient and all conjugates on the circle, as required by the [checked Kronecker classification](../literature/MV226/Yin_U001.md#mv226-kronecker-algorithm).
The uniform bound \(|r-2|\le(7/2)(3/2)^{-n}\) yields \((2/r)^n\to1\), and multiplying the root equation by \((2/r)^n\) gives \(2^n(r-2)\to-3\epsilon\).

For a root of unity, if \(\zeta^n-\epsilon\zeta\ne0\), its norm and the given equation force \(|\zeta^{n+1}-\epsilon|=2\).
Hence \(\zeta^{n+1}=-\epsilon\), and substitution gives \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=1\).
The two primitive sixth roots satisfy the equation exactly at \(n\equiv2\bmod6\) for the positive sign and \(n\equiv5\bmod6\) for the negative sign.
The zero-difference branch gives \(\zeta^2=1\), and direct evaluation at \(1,-1\) gives \(\epsilon-1,-3((-1)^n+\epsilon)\).
Simplicity therefore gives the complete cofactors.

If \(\theta=r^{1/p}\) lies in \(K\), the inclusions \(\mathbb Q(r)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\theta)\subseteq K\) are equalities, so the embeddings give \(d\) distinct \(p\)-th powers.
For its monic integral minimum \(S\), \(P=S^{[p]}\equiv S\bmod p\) by Frobenius.
Thus \(G(\theta)\in p\mathcal O_K\), and \(1<\theta<r\) ensures this value is nonzero.
Its absolute norm lies between \(p^d\) and \(6^d r^{(n+1)/p}\), by the coefficient sum six and the embedding moduli of \(\theta\).
This recovers the precise specialization of v206.
The cofactor degrees give \((n+1)/d\le3/2\) in both families and \((n+1)/d\le5/4\) in the negative family outside \(n=5\).
Direct integer arithmetic gives \(7^{14}-8\cdot6^{14}=51309760081>0\) and \(4^5 7^{28}-9^5 6^{28}=108409716599880829745136640>0\).
These inequalities exclude \(p\ge7\) in their respective ranges, and at the exceptional negative index \(6(9/4)^{3/22}<6(9/4)^{1/2}=9<11\).

The coefficient map taking \(S\) to \(S^{[p]}\) is given by integer polynomials, each congruent modulo \(p\) to the \(p\)-th power of its respective coefficient.
Applying the map to the congruent coefficient vectors of \(S\) and \(P\) thus gives \(P\equiv P^{[p]}\bmod p^2\), including at \(p=2\).
For that prime I checked the two coefficient comparisons after dividing each signed polynomial by its cofactor.
For the positive family, first divide \(G_{n,+}\) by \(X-1\) to obtain \(F_n\) of v680.
When no further cofactor occurs, the first comparison has right side minus left side equal to \(2\bmod4\).
In the odd and sixth-root cases the first four coefficients after the leading one are \((-2,1,-2,1)\) and \((0,-2,-3,-2)\), respectively, and the second comparison fails by \(2\bmod4\).
For the negative sign the first comparison fails by \(2\bmod4\) when \(U=1\).
For \(U=\Phi_6\) the tuple is \((-1,-2,-1,1)\), and for \(U=X+1\) it is \((-3,3,-3,3)\) with final entry \(1\) at \(n=4\).
All three tuples fail the second comparison by \(2\bmod4\).

Expanding \(-\log(1-2z+\epsilon z^n(2-z))\) through order \(2n-1\) gives the claimed Newton sums, since the quadratic correction starts at \(2n\).
For \(p=3\), the difference at \(j=1\) is six on either side without a sixth-root cofactor, including after removing any linear factors.
With sixth roots, those roots contribute \(2-(-1)=3\) at \(j=2\).
The remaining difference is \(60-3=57\) except at the negative index \(5\), where it is \(60+18-3=75\).
Both have residue three modulo nine.
For \(p=5\) all removed roots are permuted by powering, and the difference for \(n>5\) is \(30\).
For \(n=5\) its two values are \(20,40\), and for \(n=4\) they are \(15,45\), all nonzero modulo \(25\).
At \(p=7,n=5,\epsilon=-1\) the difference \(128+7\cdot6-2=168\) has residue \(21\bmod49\).
This checks the positive small-prime inputs directly and exhausts the exceptions on the negative side.

A positive field radical would be integral, with unique exterior conjugate and a neutral conjugate inherited from \(r\).
The complex conjugate of that neutral conjugate is its inverse, forcing reciprocity of the radical's minimum and justifying the Salem specialization in v680.
A proper monic factor of \(X^q-r\) over the real field \(K\), of degree \(0<t<q\), has real constant coefficient of absolute value \(r^{t/q}\).
Taking this absolute value in \(K\), then applying Bézout to \(t,q\), puts \(r^{1/(q/\gcd(t,q))}\) in \(K\), contradicting the exclusions for any prime divisor of \(q/\gcd(t,q)\).
The tower degree is \(qd\), including the immediate case \(q=1\).
Exactly the \(q\) roots over \(r\) have modulus greater than one and their moduli multiply to \(r\).
Every root over a root of \(U\) is again a root of unity.
