## Primitive mass, residue rank and the same-seed criterion

A separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently inspected the fixed statement and four argument sections of C345 v509 on 2026-11-20, without participation in its development or access to the developers' mathematical discussion.
The argument establishes both mass inequalities, exact residue order and degree, the two integer divisibilities and the asymptotic same-seed criterion.
Whether the unrestricted Lehmer infimum is positive remains open.

For a prime of the seed field above a divisor of \(d\), the order of a residue unit is prime to the residue characteristic and therefore differs from \(d\).
Stewart's bound at such an ideal and the norm of \((d)\) bound the entire discarded rational factor by \(d^e\) past the stated degree threshold.
A fixed seed has positive logarithmic measure, so the missing main term from short orders is at most \(hn/\lceil\log_2(2n)\rceil\), and the total absolute error from the long orders is \(O_P(\tau(n)^2\log(2n))=o(n)\).
At order \(n\) alone the error is also \(o(\varphi(n))\) by the stated totient bound.
The strict positive right side of the full-order inequality forces an actual primitive prime divisor there.
The source inputs used here are Stewart's Lemma 4 and the modulus-one interval count at their registered statement scopes.

At fixed \(j\), the numbers whose divisors enter the exclusion tests are bounded by \((2L)^{2j}\).
For each candidate divisor the complementary factor is bounded by \(\max_{2L<m<4L}T(m)=L^{o(1)}\), and fixing their product and \(p\) determines \(q\).
There are at most \(jL^{1-1/j}\) small-characteristic tests and another \(j\) tests depending on \(p\).
Indeed \(q^f\equiv1\pmod d\) implies \(p^{2f}\equiv1\pmod d\), as does \(p^f\equiv1\pmod d\).
Division by the number of second primes proves the required per-first-prime density uniformly.
Every fixed budget eventually passes the mass tests on its finite seed list, so choosing the largest successful budget requires neither nested success sets nor an effective convergence threshold.
The row tangency bounds place every qualifying seed on this list.

For each rational prime the retained cyclotomic factors are pairwise coprime modulo that prime, even when it divides the complementary part of the period.
Projection from the full period ring onto their product is surjective since the monic product divides the period polynomial.
On the lifted components of positive valuation the distinct residue factors divide the reduction of the monic seed, so their degrees have sum at most \(e\).
The order valuation of the projected quotient is \(k\sum_i f_i a_i\), while its exponent valuation is \(k\max_i a_i\).
The integral ideal containment on the sum period gives a surjection onto this quotient, which is killed by \(B\), and \(\sum_i f_i a_i\le e\max_i a_i\) gives the claimed divisibility at each supporting prime.
For a common residue root \(t\) of the seed and the cyclotomic factor, raising to \(p\) permutes the primitive \(d\)-th roots and preserves the Frobenius orbit length.
Reciprocity and \((t^{-1})^q=t^p\) put the resulting row zero on both powered minima.

Interpolation with one fixed seed gives degree at most \(4e+2\) and normalized logarithmic cost at most \(2h+O_P(\log n/n)\) for two sufficiently large primes.
For a vanishing infimum one may choose seeds with \(2h\) below successively smaller targets and increasing index thresholds beyond which each corresponding width, degree-preservation and cost test holds along the prescribed sequence.
Using that seed until the next threshold gives actual admissible rows on the sequence tail, with the same repeated factor at the two exponents of each pair.
The reverse direction follows from the original weighted inequality \(n\lambda\le nh\le\log H\).
