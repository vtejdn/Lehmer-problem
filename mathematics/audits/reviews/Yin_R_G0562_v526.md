## Outcome of the full review

For each fixed seed satisfying the inverse-pair independence hypothesis and each fixed \(w\ge e\), the product of the successive minima of either saturated signed lattice has logarithmic rate \(F(P)+(w-e)h\).
At \(w=e+1\) the uncapped cost of genuine coprime pairs has upper rate \((F(P)+h)/2\).
For a quadratic seed the limit equals \(3h/2\).

**Common divisors and extremal monomials.**
For \(k=w-e\), multiplication by \(g=(1\pm Y)^k\) intertwines the two signed matrices with the sign change dictated by \(g_k^*/g\).
The differentiated product contributes \(NY_i g'(Y_i)\) times the value row, so each node contributes determinant \(g(Y_i)^2\).
By Cauchy–Binet a common irreducible factor of the width-\(w\) minors divides every minor of the composition.
The width-\(e\) minors have polynomial gcd one for each resulting sign.
Thus the factor would divide both \(\prod_i(1+Y_i)^{2k}\) and \(\prod_i(1-Y_i)^{2k}\), which have disjoint irreducible factors in characteristic zero.
For \(k=0\) the products equal one.

The \(2s\) largest distances of the columns from \(w/2\) occur in the pairs \(l,w-l\), \(0\le l<s\), also when the midpoint is a half-integer.
For strictly decreasing positive absolute weights, rearrangement assigns exactly one pair to each node in order.
In that pair the sign of the weight fixes both exponents, and the two column orders sum with their determinant signs to the coefficient \(-c_i\) or \(c_i\).
The product survives in the same extremal minor, so continuity gives the asserted support function also at ties and zero weights.
After specialization, ratios of distinct monomial bases are nontorsion.
The fixed-place estimate therefore applies even when the largest complex modulus is shared.
The almost-unit theorem applies to one nonzero minor and a linear combination coprime to it, because the outside-archimedean cost is \(\log p\), the height is at least \(ph/e\), and the sum of coordinate heights is \(O_P(p)\).
At every fixed tolerance, a nonzero polynomial vanishing on the exceptional locus specializes to nonzero values eventually.
The Galois permutations of the inverse pairs preserve the ordered absolute weights, while the unit norm makes the linear term average to zero.

**From rational subspace to minima.**
Reversal identifies the inverse-node conditions, so the specialized kernel agrees over the splitting field with the solution of rational remainder equations.
Its integral lattice is saturated.
The primitive exterior product of a basis has projective height equal to the logarithm of its largest coordinate and Euclidean norm equal to its covolume \(\Delta\).
Projection off a shortest primitive vector gives a lattice of covolume \(\Delta/\lambda_1\).
Every nonzero projected vector can be lifted with parallel component at most \(\lambda_1/2\), so minimality forces its projected length to be at least \(\sqrt3\lambda_1/2\).
The lift has length at most \(2/\sqrt3\) times its projection.
Adjoining the shortest vector to successive independent lifts proves the induction bound \(\prod\lambda_j\le(2/\sqrt3)^{d(d-1)/2}\Delta\), including \(d=1\).
Independent minimizing vectors give the reverse bound \(\Delta\le\prod\lambda_j\).

**Short coprime pairs.**
At width \(e+1\), the two inherited directions in each signed plane have primitive integral first coordinates of rate \(F(P)\).
Their rational lines contain only integral multiples of these primitive coordinates as lattice points.
Since \(F(P)\ge(s+1)h>h\), a vector at upper rate \((F(P)+h)/2\) eventually avoids both directions.
If \(s\ge2\), divisibility by \(Q=\min(x_1^p)\) would leave a linear quotient whose simple-contact equations at two nodes are independent.
In the generic determinant the two ratios vary nontrivially with separate variables because \(x_i^2\ne1\), and specialization preserves nonvanishing eventually.

For \(s=1\), C411 applies to a signed row outside \((Q)\) because its reversal is nonzero at the chosen powered root.
Here \(D=2h\) and \(w=3\), giving the lower rate \(3h/2\) uniformly in the row.
For a nonzero row \(QC\), the simple-contact equation bounds \(|\varepsilon\alpha c_0+c_1|\) by \(O_P(\|C\|_1|\alpha|^{-p})\).
This form is a nonzero algebraic integer with conjugate \(O_P(\|C\|_1)\), which gives \(\|C\|_1\gg_P|\alpha|^{p/2}\).
The Mahler measure of the linear or constant \(C\) is at least \(\|C\|_1/2\), yielding the same lower rate after multiplication by \(Q\).
The volume formula now forces both plane minima to have rate \(3h/2\).
The simple-contact equation cuts the \(Q\)-divisible directions down to at most one line, so two independent vectors at the second minimum suffice to select a short row outside that line.

At each sign the selected row uses both inherited directions.
The mixed determinant is nonzero because \((1+Y)^2\) and \((Y-1)^2\) are independent and the width-\(e\) determinant is nonzero.
The sum and the swapped reversal recover both signed parts over \(\mathbb Q\), which excludes a common factor \(Q\) and gives the nonzero Laurent expression.
Removing a primitive polynomial gcd therefore preserves \(P^2\) in the graph, and the fixed-degree Mahler bound controls the coefficient one-norms of the integral quotients.
For quadratic seeds C411 also supplies the matching lower rate for every resulting genuine pair.
