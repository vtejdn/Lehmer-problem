# Review of small nonzero trace remainders for reciprocal inputs

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T16:51 UTC a reviewer outside proof development examined the [fixed statement](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v199.md#obstruction) and all three parts of its argument for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer used the fixed text and its dependencies without development dialogue, author explanations or a previous finding on this ledger.
The simultaneous coefficient and norm bounds hold for a single primitive coprime row for each input and each sign.
At index one the minimum nonzero norm on the stated nonproportional branch is \(p^m N_0\).

### The lift and the box

Degree preservation identifies \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) with \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and the two multiplicity layers of \(B_2\subset O_2\) have indices \(I\) and \(p^dI^3\).
The trace order \(R\) is free of rank \(m\), since \(H\) is monic of degree \(m\), and choosing representatives of degree less than \(m\) is additive.
Thus reduction to \(O_2/B_2\) defines an additive map on \(R^2\) whose image has size at most \(p^dI^4\).
Negative powers of \(x\) in this map are integral in \(O_2\) because \(P(0)=1\).
On its kernel the target has a unique representative \(T(X)\) of degree below \(2d\) in \(B_2\).

Write \(k=pd\), and put \(G=U+HW\).
The lifted expression \(x^k(1-\sigma x)G(x+x^{-1})\) and its signed reversal about \(2k+1\) sum to zero identically.
Laurent congruence to this expression modulo \(P^2\) therefore gives the required polynomial divisibility, as \(x\) is coprime to \(P\).
After centering at \(x^k\), applying \(2x\,d/dx-1\) evaluates to the specified derivative row at \(\alpha\).
The same operator on \((1-\sigma x)G(t)\) gives \((1+\sigma\alpha)(-u+2(2\sigma-q)(U'(q)+H'(q)w))\) there.
Consequently \(D=\gamma w\), and \(|N\gamma|=2^m|H(2\sigma)N(H'(q))|=N_0>0\).
In integral coordinates the map to \((u,v)\) is triangular with identity and multiplication by \(\gamma\) on the diagonal.
Its image of the kernel therefore has index at most \(J_*\), including when that upper bound is strict.

The polynomials \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{m-1}\) are an integral basis of \(R\), by triangular change of basis with diagonal entries one.
Writing \(b=B+1\), the strict inequality \(b^{m-1}>3J_*\) guarantees one coset containing more than \(3b^{m+1}\) box points, also for \(m=2\).
Subtraction from a fixed box point is injective and gives at most \(b\) choices in each coordinate.
The fiber \(u=0\) has at most \(b^m\) differences, and \(D=0\) fixes \(v\) for each of the at most \(b^m\) possible \(u\).
For a fixed nonzero \(u\), choose its first nonzero coordinate once.
A rationally proportional \(v\) is determined by that coordinate of \(v\), so at most \(b^{m+1}\) further differences are excluded in total.
Their union cannot fill the coset, and the coordinate differences have absolute value at most \(B\).

### Coprimality, primitivity and the cofactor

Inversion fixes \(u\) and exchanges \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\).
Applying it to \(T(\beta)=\beta^d(1-\sigma\alpha)u\) gives
\[
 T(\beta)-\beta^{2d}T(1/\beta)=-\sigma\beta^d(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})u\ne0 .
\]
As \(Q^2\) is reciprocal, every common root of \(A_M\) and its reversal is among the finitely many zeros of \(T-X^{2d}T(1/X)\).
No root of \(Q\) can be a root of \(T\) since \(T(\beta)\ne0\) and \(Q\) is irreducible.
Each other candidate excludes at most one \(M\).
The leading coefficient is \(M\), the constant coefficient is \(M+T(0)\), and every common coefficient divisor divides both \(M\) and the content of \(T\).
The infinitely many integers coprime to that content still leave infinitely many choices after the finite exclusions.
The two nonempty rows then occupy classes \(0,1\), so the primitive diagonal has degree \(2pd+1\le p(16d+1)\).

Holding \(P,p,u,w\) fixed and letting \(|M|\) grow through those choices, \(F_M/M\) tends to \(Q(x^p)^2(1+\sigma x)\) with the same degree.
Continuity of Mahler measure at this nonzero polynomial gives \(m(F_M)-\log|M|\to 2m(Q)=2ph\).
Primitivity and the definition of the cofactor give \(m(R_{F_M})=m(F_M)-2h\), including when there are additional factors \(P\).
The traces therefore remain fixed on witnesses of arbitrarily large cofactor measure.

### Archimedean bounds

For each inverse pair one may choose \(z_i\) with \(|z_i|\ge1\), including on the circle, and the sum of their logarithmic moduli is \(h\).
The \(q_i=z_i+z_i^{-1}\) are the distinct conjugates of \(q\).
The identity \(C_j'(q_i)=j\sum_{s=0}^{j-1}z_i^{j-1-2s}\) gives the derivative bound without any separation assumption.
At \(a_i=\log|z_i|\), the two reduced traces together contribute at most \(4mB_A e^{(m-1)a_i}\) to \(|D_i|\).
The derivative term contributes at most \(8B_A\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j^2 e^{(m-1)a_i}\).
These estimates imply the factor \(12m^3\) in (2).
Multiplying over the \(m\) embeddings gives the exponent \((1-1/m)h\) after division by \(m\), and rearrangement gives the claimed coefficient threshold on the same row.

The determinant of evaluation in the trace basis has square of absolute value \(|\operatorname{disc}H|\).
Hadamard's inequality with row lengths \(2\sqrt m e^{(m-1)a_i}\) yields \((4m)^m e^{2(m-1)h}\).
The elementary bound \(|\sigma-z|\le2\max(1,|z|)\) at all \(d\) roots gives \(|P(\sigma)|\le2^d e^h\).
Together these yield \(\log N_0/m\le\log(32m)+(2-1/m)h\).
Applying the same determinant bound in the power basis of \(\beta\), of degree \(d\) and measure \(ph\), gives \(\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|\le d\log d+2(d-1)ph\).
The discriminant ratio is \(I^2\) and \(|\operatorname{disc}P|\ge1\), giving the claimed bound for \(\log I\).
Since \(m/(m-1)\le2\), substitution in the box bound proves (1) with absolute constants even in degree four.
In the prescribed window \(\log(dp)/p\to0\), and Voutier's bound at degree \(d\) gives \(ph/\log d\to\infty\) uniformly.
Consequently \(\log N_0/(pm)=o(h)\), while the full upper bound for the normalized remainder tends to zero along a sequence with \(h\to0\).

### The index-one minimum

When \(I=1\), Frobenius surjects on \(\mathbb F_p[x]/\bar P\), so \(\bar P\) is squarefree.
A repeated root of \(\bar H\) would give a repeated root of \(\bar P\) upon substituting \(x+x^{-1}\), and a root of \(\bar H\) at either endpoint \(\pm2\) would give a double factor at \(x=\pm1\).
Thus \(p\nmid N_0\).
For every admissible row, the odd-span congruence gives \(u,v\in pR\) and \(D\in\gamma R\).
The reduced representative of \(u\) has coefficients in \(p\mathbb Z\), so \(D\in pR\) as well.
Multiplication by \(\gamma\) is invertible on \(R/pR\) by its determinant \(N\gamma\).
It follows that \(D\in p\gamma R\), giving the lower bound \(p^mN_0\) for \(|ND|\) when \(D\ne0\).

For attainment, \(p(O_2/B_2)=0\) implies that every \((u,w)\in pR^2\) has an integral lift.
Fixing \(w=p\) makes \(D=p\gamma\).
For irrational \(\gamma\), \(u=p\) gives irrational \(v/u=-1+\gamma\).
For rational \(\gamma\), \(q\) has degree \(m\ge2\), so \(q+c\) is irrational and nonzero for either \(c=0,1\).
At least one choice makes \(4\sigma+\gamma+2c\) nonzero, giving the irrational ratio \(-3+(4\sigma+\gamma+2c)/(q+c)\) for \(u=p(q+c)\).
Both pairs admit the primitive coprime lifts just checked.
The prescribed-prime construction supplies reciprocal index-one examples in its stated window.

The minimum concerns all admissible primitive coprime rows with the given nonvanishing and nonproportionality conditions.
The coefficient estimate is an existence statement from the box argument, and the proof gives no cofactor upper bound for its chosen row or for a row attaining the exact norm minimum.
The prior arithmetic, source and existence results were checked at the interfaces used here, without a fresh audit of their full proofs.
