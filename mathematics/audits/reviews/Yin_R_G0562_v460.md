### Integral pole signs and the trace resultant

At each root \(\alpha\) of the monic \(D\), substituting \(\alpha^{12}=1-\alpha^9\) gives \(H_b(\alpha)=\alpha^3(1+\alpha^6)\).
The product of the twelve \(\alpha^3\) is \((-1)^3=-1\).
For \(d(t)=t^4+t^3-1\), \(\prod_{d(t)=0}(1+t^2)=d(i)d(-i)=(-i)i=1\).
The map \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^3\) gives three lifts of each \(t\), so the second factor in the norm is \(1^3\).
This proves the integer resultant is \(-1\), and multiplicativity forces each monic integral divisor to have resultant of absolute value one.

The two real zeros of \(D\) are the real cube roots of the unique negative and positive zeros of \(d\).
The negative one is \(\xi=-\beta_4^{1/3}<-1\), and the positive one \(\eta\) is less than one.
At real arguments, each nonreal conjugate pair of roots of a real monic factor gives a positive quadratic factor.
The two real roots of the nontorsion factor isolating \(-\sigma\) in a split give \((X+\sigma)(X+\sigma^{-1})\).
This is positive at \(\eta\) and negative at \(\xi\) exactly for even \(b\), by the strict position of \(\sigma^3\) relative to \(\beta_4\).
The nonreal conjugate pairs of zeros of \(D\) also give positive products of evaluations, proving the sign \((-1)^{b+1}\).
For odd \(b\), after removal of the simple root \(-1\) there is no real circle root and the remaining real inverse pair lies entirely in \((\xi,0)\).
A reciprocal divisor includes both members or neither, giving sign \(+1\) at the poles in both cases.

Direct multiplication gives \(D(X)D(X^{-1})=3+C_3-C_9-C_{12}\).
Also \((X-\alpha)(X^{-1}-\alpha)=-\alpha(Y-\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\).
Multiplication over all twelve poles therefore gives the monic trace polynomial \(W\) in the ledger, with the minus sign accounted for by \(\prod\alpha=-1\).
Evaluation of \(P_f(\alpha)=\alpha^k f(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) then gives exactly \((-1)^k\operatorname{Res}(W,f)\).
This polynomial identity persists under reduction and is multiplicative in groups of trace factors, with their multiplicities.
