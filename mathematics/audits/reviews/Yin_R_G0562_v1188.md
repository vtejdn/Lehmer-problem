## The examples in all degrees \(4b\), the delay bound and the intrinsic power sums

At 2026-11-25T02:46 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v128 independently reviewed for Yin its fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#statement) and the five proof sections, including the clarified scope of the neutral labels.
The reviewer received the fixed mathematics and its inputs without development discussion or previous findings on this claim.
The actual irreducible examples exist for every \(b\ge3\), the canonical orientation follows for the stated large primes, and the two general estimates and the normalized power-sum test hold under their given hypotheses.

I independently obtained \(c^2-4d=13+8\sqrt3\), negative at the other embedding because \(192>169\).
Both values of \(d\) are positive and their product is one.
The real quadratic seed takes the signs \(+,-,+\) at \(0,1,+\infty\), so \(a>1>a'>0\), and both nonreal conjugates have modulus \(\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}<1\).
The relative norm-one unit \(a/a'\) has trace \(c^2/d-2=4+3\sqrt3\), which generates \(E\), with its other value strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\).
This proves it generates the quartic field and is Salem, with \(a<a/a'<10\).
For a positive primitive relative norm-one unit \(s>1\), its trace \(m_0+j\sqrt3\) in \(\mathcal O_E\) is \(>2\), with conjugate in \((-2,2)\).
Indeed its power \(a/a'\) has this complex signature and the relative unit kernel has rank one.
This gives \(j\ge1\); for \(j=1\) one needs \(m_0\ge1\), and for \(j\ge2\) the trace exceeds \(4\sqrt3-2\).
In particular \(s>9/4\), so its third and all higher powers already exceed ten.
If \(a/a'\) were a square in \(K\), then \(a^2/(a/a')=d\) would be a square there.
Writing \(d=(x+y\sqrt3)^2\) in \(E\) requires \(x^2=1/2\) or \(3/2\), both impossible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The resulting totally real quadratic extension of \(E\) cannot be \(K\), which has complex embeddings.
Thus \(a/a'\) itself is a generator and gives the strict lower threshold for nontrivial norm-one units at the distinguished real place.

For all \(b\ge3\), the squared-modulus difference on the circle is \((a-a')(a+a'-2\Re X)>0\).
Rouché gives \(b-1\) interior roots, and evaluation at \(1,a\) gives a unique simple exterior root \(1<r_b<a\).
The leading term \(x^{b-1}(x-a)\) at every fixed \(1<x<a\) tends to minus infinity, proving the asserted limit.
The product of the interior moduli is \(1/r_b\), and each interior modulus strictly exceeds that product since there are at least two.
Consequently the root sets of the chosen polynomial and its negative reciprocal are disjoint, with every inverse interior root of smaller modulus than \(r_b\).
At each complex place the equation is a disk automorphism times \(X^{b-1}\) equal to one, and conjugate reciprocal symmetry gives only neutral roots.
A proper integral monic factor omitting \(r_b\) would have unit constant \(h\) with \(1/r_b\le |h|<1\), and \(|\iota h|=1\) at a complex place.
The latter equality forces \(h\tau h=1\) exactly by injectivity of that embedding.
This contradicts the primitive generator bound \(1/(a/a')<1/r_b\).
Gauss integrality of the monic factors here follows from integral closedness of the coefficient ring of integers.
The complex coefficient pair is the transport of the disjoint real pair under one absolute embedding, and circle versus off-circle distinguishes the real and complex pairs.
All four relatively irreducible conjugates therefore form one rational orbit of \(4b\) simple roots.
Reciprocity permutes the four polynomials and the product has constant one.
There are \(2b\) circle roots, and the exterior product of moduli equals \(r_b/(1/r_b)=r_b^2\).
Its strict dominance excludes two distinct conjugates differing by torsion.
The four sets provide actual invariant halves and two corresponding blocks of inverse pairs.
At prime \(b\) the transitive stabilizer of such a block admits no proper nontrivial partition of it into equal parts; any strictly contained block of the full action would give such a partition.
For large prime \(b\) the canonical block theorem applies since \(m(r_b)<2\log a\).
The marked halves exclude a supported two-label flip, which would switch only two of their labels.
The supported alternating group preserves the halves by perfection and is transitive on the labels, identifying the canonical halves with these very root sets.

As an independent exact calculation, put \(U=X^b-1,\ V=X^{b-1},\ W=(X-V)U,\ H=X^2+V^2,\ J=XV\).
Multiplying the two real conjugate factors before taking their quadratic norm gives the rational formula
\[
 S_b=(U^2+3W+2H-17J)^2-3(2W+H-10J)^2 .
\]
For \(b=3\) I obtained the ascending coefficients \((1,-6,7,-32,2,-10,53,-10,2,-32,7,-6,1)\).
Reduction modulo five gives \(X^{5^{12}}\equiv X\bmod S_3\).

These exact polynomial divisions verify irreducibility at this smallest allowed degree independently of the norm-one proof used for every \(b\).

For the general monic seed, if all roots at one nonsplit embedding are neutral, conjugating and inverting the roots preserves the multiset with multiplicity.
The leading coefficient of the reflected conjugate there is the image of \(\tau_0(h)\), giving \(Q=\tau_0(h)P\) in \(K_0[X]\) and an actual nontrivial factorization at every delay.
Otherwise \(d_0=h\tau_0(h)\) has values strictly between zero and one at every nonsplit place.
Using the nonzero integral norms of \(d_0,1-d_0\) gives \(4^{u-1}\le|d_0(1-d_0)|\), with all embeddings counted even if \(d_0\) has smaller degree.
At the split place \(|h|\le M(P)=\beta\) and \(|\tau_0(h)|\le M(Q)\le\beta\) by the circle mean.
It follows that \(4^{u-1}\le\beta^2(1+\beta^2)\), giving precisely the square root \(B_u\).

Rouché gives exactly one exterior root for either delayed sign at this same place.
The exterior maximum principle applies to \((X-\beta)Q(X)/((\beta X-1)P(X))\), since removal at the simple exterior zero leaves no pole there or at infinity.
Unless the root is \(\beta\), one obtains \(R^k|r-\beta|\le|\beta r-1|\).
For \(r=-R\) the right side divided by \(R+\beta\) is strictly less than \(R\) for \(R>1\), impossible.
For \(1<R<\beta\), subtracting one from the resulting upper bound for \(R^k\) and using \(R^k-1\ge k(R-1)\) yields \(R\ge\beta-(\beta+1)/k\).
Circle factors cancel with equal multiplicities at the complex places, leaving a finite Blaschke product with its \(X^k\) factor.
The delayed polynomial there is conjugate reciprocal up to sign, and the opposite real polynomial is the reciprocal up to sign.
Neither possible reducibility nor any roots from other embeddings invalidate the lower bound \(M(r)\ge R\).
The comparisons \(B_2>11/9,\ B_3>5/3,\ B_4>2,\ B_5>3\), monotonicity in \(u\), and positivity of \(1-1/k\) at the stated cutoffs give the four claimed uniform gaps.
