## The slope estimate and its consequences

The proof establishes both statements and the two-dimensional consequence.
In a good residue slope both coordinates are units and the smooth critical point has both partial derivatives nonzero, by the tangency relation.
Restriction of the second equation to the graph of the first identifies its simple zero with a unit derivative of the slope function.
Integral implicit series and their compositional inverses therefore interpolate each of the sixteen geometric points on the whole slope disk over its own unramified coefficient ring.
For a compatible residue point the unique section coordinate follows by the character relation for the primitive vector, and the binomial exponents \(1/l,k/l\) are in \(\mathbb Z_p\).
The section and graph equations differ locally by units after the coordinate change since the graph is cut out by a unit partial derivative.
On translation to the critical point the first surviving variable term is quadratic with a unit coefficient.
Its square-root change has an integral inverse on every open residue disk, including over ramified extensions.
Consequently the unique pair of roots has distance valuation half the valuation of the critical value, since \(2\) is a unit.
The critical value is in the unramified principal-unit logarithm domain, where the logarithm preserves its depth.
The pair accounts for precisely twice its mutual distance valuation in the discriminant, with all pairs of distinct residue classes contributing zero.

Differentiation of the two logarithms cancels the terms involving the motion of the critical point because \(Vf'/f=r\).
The remaining second derivative is integral with unit constant, so the normalized quadratic coefficient on substituting \(r=r_0+pT\) is a unit.
For \(n\ge4\) the coefficient valuation lower bound \(n-2-v_p(n(n-1))\) is positive and tends to infinity, since at most one consecutive factor is divisible by \(p\).
The cubic bound at three is zero, which permits degree three in the reduction.
The two nonzero residues at three really are good: specializing the octic at \((k,l)=(1,1),(2,1)\) and dividing modulo three gives \(\gcd(E,E')=\gcd(E,G)=1\) in both cases.
No assertion about occurrence of a cubic is required.
In the shallow case all slope values have depth one, while in the deeper case successive quotient and remainder corrections lift the scalar unit and monic polynomial through all powers of \(p\) in the restricted series ring.
Its unit evaluates to a unit everywhere on the closed disk and the monic factor has integral roots, yielding the valuation constant \(2-m\) after rescaling.

For two rational slopes too close to the same algebraic center, the ultrametric inequality gives a determinant of valuation strictly greater than \(\log_p(KL)\), since both denominators are units.
The positive primitive representatives are unique, and a nonzero determinant has ordinary absolute value at most \(KL\).
Thus each center removes at most one direction, even for a center with fractional distances or coinciding conjugate centers.
There are at most \(32(p-1)\) centers for \(p\ge5\) and 96 for \(p=3\), within the common allowance \(32(p+1)\).
The remaining deep disks each cost at most \(m\log(KL)\), using \(2-m\le0\), and the shallow cost is at most \(\log(KL)\).
Sixteen disks give the stated constant 48.
Summing the omitted counts up to \(Y\) costs \(O(Y\pi(Y))\), and one may intersect that exceptional set with any primitive graph.

On the steep graph, small good primes cost \(O_F(\sqrt M)\) after these omissions.
At larger good primes the earlier low-order estimate counts individual low-order contacts even when another contact keeps the full prime power in the certificate.
The bad-prime result gives the corresponding distinction at bad primes, and the derivative sums dominate the requisite discarded weights in each case.
The two exceptional sets from earlier cutoffs and the rectangle omissions total \(O_F(M/\log M)\).
The loss \(O_F(M\log M)\) is negligible compared both with the minimum factor degree and with the reducibility threshold for the full certificate.
In the box use \(K=2M,L=2Mh,Y=M\sqrt h\), so the union costs \(O(M^2h/\log M)\) directions and the remaining weight is \(O_F(M\sqrt h)\).
Here \(l/k\ge h/2\) and \(k\ge M\), so every sequence in the box satisfies the steep degree hypotheses.
The uniform lower bound is \(d\gg M(\log M)^3(\log\log M)^3\), which absorbs the weight even though \(\sqrt h\asymp(\log M)^3\).
