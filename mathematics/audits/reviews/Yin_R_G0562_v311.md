### Specialization, degree and reciprocity

Equation (2–2) in BM05 gives the constant, linear and quadratic coefficients \(1+x,\ 1+x+x^2,\ x(1+x)\) at \((a,b)=(2,3)\).
The journal text explicitly associates these parameters with the reported multiplicative measure \(1.255433\ldots\) on printed p. 406. [Boyd and Mossinghoff, §2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38338596_Small_Limit_Points_of_Mahler%27s_Measure)
Substituting \(y=-z^n\) gives all seven terms of \(H_n\), and \(\mathbb Z(n,-1)\) is the full lattice of relations for \((1,n)\).
Lawton's theorem for a fixed polynomial, including torus zeros, consequently gives the limit \(m(P_{23})\) after the Haar-preserving sign change.
Dividing \(H_n\) by \(z^{n+1}\) pairs its outer terms with their inverses and gives the trace polynomial in (6).
Frobenius gives \(C_p(X)\equiv X^p\bmod p\) by substitution at \(X=z+z^{-1}\).
The additional monic term \(C_{p-1}\) survives in (7), so the earlier prime-family irreducibility argument supplies no irreducibility test here.

For the separated-block theorem the high block starts at exponent \(2n+1\), the low block ends at \(n+2\), and the high coefficient polynomial \(1+z\) has its sole zero at \(-1\).
The two coefficient lengths are \(2\) and \(5\).
The required inequality \(n-3\ge(2n+2)/4\) holds exactly from \(n=7\) onward.
Since the monic factors have constant coefficient \(\pm1\), Kronecker's theorem gives positive logarithmic measure for each noncyclotomic factor.
C170 gives a positive lower bound for this measure and a lower bound proportional to \(2n+2\) for the degree of each such factor.
Summing the degrees with multiplicity gives the bounded count.
For the alternative C171 application the six stated boundary segments are primitive lattice edges, each with two coefficients of modulus one, also after the sign change in \(y\).
The six edge polynomials thus have their roots in \(\{1,-1\}\).

Additivity of the nonnegative logarithmic measures of monic factors gives \(m(S)\le m(H_n)\).
The positive limiting integral below guarantees that \(H_n\) eventually has a noncyclotomic factor, and its strict upper bound gives \(m(S)<6/25\).
The inequalities \(3/10-(3/10)^2/2>6/25\) and \((13/10)^3-13/10-1<0\) put \(e^{6/25}\) strictly below the plastic number.
Smyth's theorem applies to the algebraic integer represented by each of these factors and gives reciprocity.

### The two strict inequalities

Expanding \(P_{23}(x^2,-x^{-1}Y)\) on the degree-two torus cover gives, after removal of the monomial, the fiber equation \(Y+Y^{-1}=t-1/t\) for \(t=2\cos\vartheta\ne0\).
The leading fiber coefficient \(x+x^{-1}\) has logarithmic measure zero since \(x^2+1\) is cyclotomic.
For \(t>0\), \(t-1/t\) is increasing, ranges up to \(3/2\) at \(t=2\), and equals \(-2\) at \(a=\sqrt2-1\).
The absolute value of this trace is even in \(t\).
The density \(1/(\pi\sqrt{4-t^2})\) on \((-2,2)\) therefore yields (12) by Jensen's formula.
Its integrand is positive on \((0,a)\) and of order \(-\log t\) at zero.

On this interval \(1-3t^2>0\), and squaring bounds the discriminant square root in (13) because the difference of the squared expressions is \(8t^4\).
The resulting upper integrand \(-\log t-2t^2\) is decreasing and stays positive up to \(b=5/12\), since \(-\log b\ge1-b>2b^2\).
Increasing the integration endpoint to \(b\) and replacing the reciprocal square root by its value at \(b\) are thus both upper estimates.
Substituting \(\sqrt{4-b^2}=\sqrt{551}/12\), the lower bounds on \(\pi\) and \(\sqrt{551}\), and the degree-five exponential bound for \(\log(12/5)\) gives the rational upper bound \(2376350/9948933\).
