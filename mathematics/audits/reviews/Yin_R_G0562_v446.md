### Sampled trace values and the norm threshold

The inclusion \(\mathbb Q(T_\tau)\subseteq\mathbb Q(T_\sigma)\) follows from the integral polynomial identity, and every embedding of the smaller number field has exactly \(h\) extensions in characteristic zero.
The original exterior trace supplies one of the \(h\) copies of \(T_\tau\), so precisely \(h-1\) neutral input traces, or \(2(h-1)\) circle angles, must be removed.
At least one neutral output trace exists even for a degree-four Salem number, and all its preimages remain in the sample.
The polynomial \(H(X)-T_\tau\) also gives \(h\le k\).

On the \(2k\)-point extrema grid, multiplying \(U\) by \((-1)^l\) shifts the two top frequencies to zero, giving mean \(2a_k\) after normalizing the leading sign.
The other positive and negative frequencies are distinct modulo \(2k\), with the constant term shifted to index \(k\).
This also covers \(k=1\), where there is only this last lower coefficient.
The nonnegative grid function \(M-V_l\) has mean \(M-2a_k\), which bounds every nonconstant Fourier coefficient in modulus.
When \(M<3\) this forces \(a_k=1\) and all lower integer coefficients to vanish.
The example \(C_k+1\) has norm exactly three.

For the sampling envelope, a strict violation at \(0<s<\pi/k\) persists under a sufficiently small positive phase shift of the comparison cosine and the stated factor \(1+\delta^3\).
At zero the perturbed cosine is less than \(M\), since its quadratic decrease dominates the cubic change in amplitude.
The \(2k\) intervals between consecutive extrema have strictly opposite endpoint signs, and the first contains three separate sign changes.
A nonzero trigonometric polynomial of degree \(k\) has at most \(2k\) zeros per period, by clearing its negative powers.
Reflection and the endpoint norm bound complete the envelope, including \(|s|=\pi/k\).
At a nearest retained angle this gives \(2M/3\) under \(kG\le2\arccos(2/3)\), with a strict inequality below two for the sampled value.
For the unpruned alternative the strict angular cutoff gives \(M\cos(kG_0/2)\le T_\tau\) with \(\cos(kG_0/2)>T_\tau/3\).
In either test the threshold then applies, and evaluation at \(T_\sigma\) excludes the negative Chebyshev choice.
The positive choice yields \(\tau=\sigma^k\) by strict monotonicity of \(t+t^{-1}\) for \(t>1\).

### Discriminant energy, radial tents and the constants

Smoothing all \(d\) conjugates at radius \(\epsilon=1/(2d)\) gives self-energy \(\log(1/\epsilon)/d\).
Off-diagonal self-interactions are bounded above using the nonzero integral discriminant, and the mixed term with Haar is at most \(2\log\sigma/d+2\epsilon\) by the \(1\)-Lipschitz potential \(\log^+|z|\).
This gives \(I\le L/d\), with the constant one in \(L\).
For a test of normalized Dirichlet energy \(E=(2\pi)^{-1}\int|\nabla\psi|^2\), Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its integral against the smoothed orbit minus Haar by \(\sqrt{EL/d}\).
A unit-height disk tent has normalized energy \(1/2\), by integrating \(R^{-2}\) on its disk, and Haar integral at least \(R/(2\pi)\) by chord distance at most angular distance.
At the midpoint of the empty arc the nearest retained point has chord distance \(2\sin(G/4)\).
For positive radius \(R=2\sin(G/4)-\epsilon\), smoothing leaves all of these points outside the tent interior.
The two real roots together with the discarded angles account for at most \(2h/d\) of the smoothed integral.
These estimates give exactly (4), including its trivial case of nonpositive proposed radius.
For \(k<\sqrt d\), the right side of (4) is at most \((1/2+4\pi+\sqrt2\,\pi)\sqrt{L/d}\).
A nonpower has \(G>2\kappa/k\), so the left side exceeds \(2\sin(\kappa/(2k))\ge2\kappa/(\pi k)\).
For \(k\ge\sqrt d\) the claimed degree floor already holds since \(L\ge1\).
When \(1<\sigma\le2\) one has \(L=O(\log(2d))\), uniformly in the output trace.

For the bounded-norm floor, when \(a_k=1\) a nonzero lower Fourier coefficient gives \(\mathbb E|V_l-2|\ge1\) and zero mean gives \(\mathbb E(V_l-2)_+\ge1/2\).
For \(a_k\ge2\) the corresponding positive-part mean is at least two.
Splitting the mean at \(1/4\) bounds it above by \(1/4+MJ/(2k)\), hence \(J\ge k/(2M)\).
Bernstein's inequality with angular coordinate in radians excludes all retained angles within \(1/(8kM)\) of each selected center.
The chord distance exceeds \(1/(16kM)\) at the edge of that excluded interval, by \(2\sin(u/2)\ge 2u/\pi\).
The grid spacing gives disjoint tent disks of radius \(R=1/(32kM)\), and \(d\ge16kM\) gives \(\epsilon\le R\).
Their total smoothed integral is at most \(2h/d\) since their sum has height at most one even on the exceptional circles.
The Dirichlet pairing gives
\[
           JR/(2\pi)\le 2h/d+\sqrt{JL/(2d)} .
\]
Multiplication by \(kM/J\) gives the displayed \(1/(64\pi)\) bound in L203.
For \(y=kM^3L/d\le1\), the right side is at most \(5\sqrt y\), giving \(y\ge(320\pi)^{-2}\), stronger than the claimed \((400\pi)^{-2}\).
For \(y>1\) or \(d<16kM\), (5) follows directly using \(M\ge3\) and \(L\ge1\).
An upper bound on the indicated coefficient sum bounds \(M\) and therefore can replace it in this lower estimate.

### Least admissible degree

An admissible Chebyshev transformation has degree at least the finite least admissible power \(e(\sigma)\).
Every other admissible transformation obeys the absolute degree floor just proved, giving the stated minimum as lower bound.
The polynomial \(C_{e(\sigma)}\) and its remainder modulo the monic trace minimal polynomial give the two upper bounds.
The remainder is integral and nonconstant, since its value at \(T_\sigma\) is the irrational trace of the Salem number \(\sigma^{e(\sigma)}\).
This gives exact equality of the minimum degree with \(e(\sigma)\) under the stated comparison, including the equality endpoint.
The proof gives no identification of a general representing polynomial outside the sampling or degree restrictions.
