### Primitive rows, random slices and sweeping their zeros to the circle

Width in \(X\) adds in a product over the Laurent domain in \(Y\), so the rows of \(F_0\) can be shifted together into \([-d,d]\).
Their common integral content is one, and a common nonzero complex zero would be algebraic and would yield a common primitive polynomial factor by Gauss's lemma.
For \(n>2d\), the specialized bands are disjoint, so the leading coefficient of the diagonal comes from a single nonzero integer coefficient times a phase of modulus one.
This proves \(\mu_\phi\ge0\), and the same inductive leading-coefficient Jensen bound gives \(I\ge0\).

The two analytic Laurent combinations for each row recover \(2a_j\) and coincide on the real line with twice its real and imaginary parts.
They have Gaussian integer coefficients and cannot all vanish at any complex argument \(z\), since \(X=e^{iz}\ne0\).
Integrating first in the phase of a nonzero coordinate replaces the logarithm by the log maximum of its modulus and the modulus of the sum of the other terms.
Choosing a maximizing coordinate proves the lower bound in (3), and choosing a locally nonvanishing coordinate proves continuity by uniform boundedness after that first integration.
Conjugation of all the phases preserves their law, giving real-axis symmetry.
On every compact disk the mean of the negative logarithmic part is bounded by the positive-part bound and the lower bound in (3).
Fubini against a Laplacian test function then identifies \((2\pi)^{-1}\Delta W\) with the expected zero-counting measure in the \(z\) plane.
The event of an identically zero slice has measure zero by evaluating at a single point with nonzero coordinate.

The common monomial shift has harmonic logarithm in \(z\) and unit boundary modulus.
After shifting to occupied degrees \(0,\ldots,s\), the coordinate polynomials have no common root, including zero.
A first slice of full degree has finitely many roots and each excludes a proper parameter hyperplane for the second slice, which proves nonvanishing of the cross-resultant.
Its Gaussian integer coefficients give nonnegative Mahler measure by iterated Jensen, and the same argument with bounded positive part gives logarithmic integrability on the parameter torus.
For \(s=0\) reality forces the common frequency of the coordinates to be zero, yielding the exact comparison for constant rows.

For independent full-degree slices the reflected pair estimate gives
\[
 |1-qw\bar w'|\ \ge\ \sqrt q\,
 \frac{|\alpha-\alpha'|}{\max(1,|\alpha|)\max(1,|\alpha'|)} .
\]
Multiplying over the \(s^2\) pairs recovers both leading coefficients of the resultant to power \(s\), including multiplicities.
The Haar mean of the slice measure is \(\int W\,d\nu\).
Independence turns the absolutely convergent logarithm series at \(q<1\) into the squared moments of \(\sigma\) with weights \(q^\ell/\ell\).
Letting \(q\) increase to one leaves at most \(2s\int W\,d\nu\), since the integral of the resultant logarithm is nonnegative.
The fiber coefficient inequality gives \(\int W\,d\nu\le I+\log(2^{m+1}p)\).
This verifies the multi-parameter use of the [v331 energy calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v331.md#energy).
Poisson sweeping preserves the \(\ell\)-th complex moment \(w^\ell\) of every reflected root and its unit mass, including for a boundary point mass.
Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz against \(\tau-s\nu\) therefore gives (5), with Fejér approximation valid uniformly for the real Lipschitz tests.

### Packing and the local zero count

An overfull radius satisfies \(Kdr\le s\le2d\), hence \(r\le2/K\), and the fallback radius is at most \(\delta\).
Closed arcs have upper semicontinuous mass as functions of their radius, so a largest eligible radius exists whenever there is one.
Every larger radius has mass at most \(Kd\) times its radius, including when no eligible radius exists.
The greedy selection keeps disjoint doubled arcs, and a discarded center is at most \(6r_i\) from the selected center because its radius is at most \(2r_i\).
The positive minimum radius ensures termination and \(\sum r_i=O(1)\).
Fallback centers contribute \(O(d)\) to \(\sum M_i^2\), since \(M_i=\delta\) there.

Each larger-radius tent has excess \(\int\psi_i\,d(\tau-s\nu)\gg KM_i\), by its unit plateau and \(s\nu(B(x_i,2r_i))\ll dr_i\).
The Fourier bound \(\min(O(r_i),O((r_i\ell^2)^{-1}))\) gives bounded squared homogeneous \(H^{1/2}\) seminorm, by splitting the sum at \(1/r_i\).
For two distinct tents the polarized difference product equals \(-\psi_i(t)\psi_j(u)-\psi_i(u)\psi_j(t)\le0\), because their supports are disjoint.
The positive difference-quotient kernel representing the seminorm thus gives \(\|\sum_i M_i\psi_i\|_*^2\ll\sum_iM_i^2\) for these centers.
Testing (5) with this sum yields \((\sum_i M_i^2)^2\ll_m d(1+I)\sum_i M_i^2\), which proves (6), also when there are no larger radii.

The exponential map is injective on each radius-\(256r_i\) disk and preserves every local zero multiplicity.
For each root counted there the projected angular distance from \(x_i\) and the reflected logarithmic depth \(a\) are both at most \(256r_i\).
If \(a>0\), the Poisson density on the subarc of radius \(a\) about the root direction is \(\gg1/a\), giving absolute positive mass in \(B(x_i,512r_i)\).
At depth zero the point mass lies in the same arc.
Taking expectations and applying the upper mass bound at radius \(512r_i\) gives (7).

### Green normalization, a fixed real rotation and chart thresholds

On a real-centered disk, the harmonic extension \(H\) of \(W\)'s boundary values satisfies \(H-W=\int G_R\,d\omega\) by the disk Green formula for continuous subharmonic functions.
The Green potential is nonnegative, and \(H\)'s symmetry makes its harmonic conjugate, normalized at the center, zero on the real diameter.
Thus \(e^{-h_R}\) is positive real there and all normalized coordinates have modulus at most one on the entire disk.
The kernel obeys \(0\le G_R(t,\zeta)\le\log(2R/|t-\zeta|)\) on the diameter.
After projecting the pole onto that diameter, its integral in \(t\) is \(O(R)\), uniformly up to the real singularity.
On any subset of length \(\gamma R\) the average deficiency \(H-W\) is therefore at most \(C_\gamma\omega(B_R)\).
The upper inequality in (3) then supplies a point where the largest normalized coordinate has modulus at least \(p^{-1}e^{-C_\gamma\omega(B_R)}\).

For \(R=256r_i\) and the central interval of length \(2r_i\) this gives a real vector at \(t_0\) with norm at least \(e^{-C_mM_i}\), using \(M_i\ge\delta\).
A single real orthogonal matrix sends it to equal positive coordinates and preserves its norm.
This same matrix applies at all real and complex arguments of the disk, so the rotated coordinates remain holomorphic, uniformly bounded on it and real on its diameter.
Every denominator at \(t_0\) has modulus at least \(e^{-C'_mM_i}\).
Cauchy's inequalities on the inner disk bound scaled derivatives of each rotated coordinate, of any order \(k\le m\), by \(O_m(1)\), since \(nr_i=DM_i\ge1\).
The quotient-rule numerators have the same type of bounds.
For each signed threshold interval the two endpoint tests differ at \(t_0\) by its width times \(|u_b(t_0)|^{k+1}\), so one endpoint yields modulus at least \(e^{-C''_mM_i}\).
The radius-\(50r_i\) disk about \(t_0\) contains \(J\), and the radius-\(120r_i\) disk about it lies inside the radius-\(160r_i\) region of the upper bound.
Hence Jensen counts \(O_m(M_i)\) zeros for all the finitely many analytic level functions together, including the order-zero tests.

On each interval where order \(k\) is the first excessive order for a particular ratio, its preceding scaled derivative has bounded range and signed slope of magnitude at least \(\eta n\).
This gives length \(O_m(1/n)\) and \(O_m(M_i)\) flagged periods in \(J\), also counting threshold endpoints.
A bounded chart cannot lose its denominator through a zero without a threshold crossing because the vector has no common zero.
On each good period the inverse of the fixed rotation takes the real ratio vector to a vector of twice the complex coefficient coordinates divided by the same denominator.
Its norm is between one and \(3\sqrt p\) before the factor \(1/2\), and positive derivatives in the fast variable through order \(m\) are \(O_m(\eta)\).
The denominator and the original maximum coordinate are comparable in modulus after the common normalization, by the same norm inequalities.
Freezing the coefficients, applying the fixed Vandermonde bound including derivative order zero, and using a finite real or imaginary derivative partition with the divided-difference bound gives (9).
In particular at least half of each good period has \(|G|\ge c_mA\), with no absolute amplitude assumption.

### The second disk and the total comparison error

There are finitely many marks, so each marked singleton belongs to a maximal dyadic interval with marked fraction above \(1/4\).
Every parent of such an interval has marked fraction at most \(1/4\) and above \(1/8\).
Keeping inclusion-maximal parents preserves these two bounds, disjointness and coverage of the marks.
Their total length is \(O_m(M_i/n)\), hence every retained interval has length \(l\le r_i/100\) for all sufficiently large \(D\).
For blocks meeting the radius-\(8r_i\) cover arc all their periods lie inside \(J\).
They consequently contain a set \(S\) of length at least \(3l/8\) with the good relative amplitude bound.

The second disk is centered at the block midpoint and has radius \(16l\), staying inside the large disk in (7).
It has mass \(O_m(M_i)\), and \(|S|/(16l)\ge3/128\), independently of \(r_i/l\).
The diameter estimate applied on this disk gives an anchor in \(S\) for which \(\log|\widetilde G(\xi)|\ge-C_mM_i\).
Its own analytic normalization bounds the coordinate moduli by one on this disk, giving \(\sup\log^+|\widetilde G|=O_m(1+nl)=O_m(M_i)\).
The disk of radius \(8l\) about \(\xi\) remains inside it.
Jensen counts \(O_m(M_i)\) zeros within \(5l\) and division by the interior Blaschke factors on a circle of radius \(4l<R'<5l\) preserves the upper boundary modulus while increasing the anchor modulus.
Harnack bounds the negative logarithm of the zero-free quotient by \(O_m(M_i)\) on the interval.
Each removed factor costs \(O(l)\) there by integration of its logarithmic singularity, including a real or repeated zero.
Since \(e^{-H}A\le1\), (10) bounds the relative clipping loss on the block by \(O_m(M_i l)\).
Summing per center costs \(O_m(M_i^2/n)\), and the remaining good periods cost \(O_m((r_i+1/n)u^{1/m})\).
The losses are nonnegative, so summing over the cover and using (6) proves (11).

At fixed base point the normalized coefficient vector has bounded norm and a coefficient of modulus at least \(1/2\), giving independent clipping cost \(O_m(u^{1/m})\).
At fixed fast angle the normalized clipped modulus has \(O_m(d)\) monotone pieces, since the equations for squared coordinate ties, clipping thresholds and extrema of each squared ratio have degree \(O(d)\) after denominators are cleared.
Persistent ties admit a fixed choice, constant ratios cost no variation and \(A>0\).
The shifted grid therefore compares the clipped averages within \(O_m(D^{-1}\log(2/u))\).
Taking \(u=D^{-m}\) proves (1).
The lower inequality absorbs its \(C_mI/D\) term for \(D\ge2C_m\), giving the version with \(\mu_\phi\).
The common gcd adds the same nonnegative measure to both averages, proving the stated uniform convergence with the bounded original diagonal measure.

### The arithmetic alternative

For the primitive row, a nontorsion \(Y\)-dependent geometric component gives \(I\ge c_m>0\) by the partial-degree theorem, since the actual \(Y\)-width is at most \(m\).
For sufficiently large \(D\), the coefficient of \(I\) in the lower comparison is at least \(3/4\) and its additive loss at most \(c_m/4\), so \(\mu_0\ge c_m/2\).
Otherwise the horizontal factorization gives cyclotomic polynomials in primitive characters with positive \(Y\)-exponent and a base factor.
Their lowest \(Y\)-coefficients are units up to monomial factors, so the base factor is the row gcd up to a unit and is a unit for \(F_0\).
By additivity of \(X\)-width each character satisfies \(|a|\varphi(q)\le2d<n\), hence \(a+bn\ne0\).
The phase-zero specialization of each horizontal factor then has zero logarithmic measure.
A single occupied row of \(F_0\) is a Laurent unit, and the same conclusion holds there.
Restoring the gcd proves the gap above \(m(h)\).
If at least two rows survive, specialization of \(F_0\) has at least two terms by band separation, so irreducibility of the original diagonal forces its other factor \(h\) to be a Laurent unit.
The arithmetic alternative is at phase zero and concerns the full specialization.
