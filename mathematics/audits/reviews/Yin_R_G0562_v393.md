# Factor degrees in power substitutions of the reciprocal cubic seed and in the moving Lehmer family

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of the two degree bounds

At 2026-11-18T13:22 UTC an agent outside proof development independently checked the whole [fixed argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v168.md#degree) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed input and review scope without development dialogue, author explanations or a previous finding on this argument.
The bounds hold for every pair of positive integers \(n,q\) in the seed family and for every odd \(n\ge3\) in the moving family.

For \(n\ge2\) the six exponents before substitution are \(0,1,n,2n+1,3n,3n+1\), in increasing order.
The end coefficients of the monic normalization are both one, so every monic irreducible factor has constant coefficient \(\pm1\) and its roots are algebraic units.
The two blocks have norms \(2\) and \(4\), and all zeros of \(B=1-X^q\) lie on the unit circle.
Writing \(D_0=(3n+1)q\) for the complete degree, the gap after subtracting twice the block width is \((n-3)q\ge D_0/5\) at \(n\ge9\), where \(D_0>20\).
In the capacity proof the shift is \(\lfloor(n-6)q/2\rfloor\) and the auxiliary polynomial has degree \(3q\).
Putting \(e=nq\bmod 2\) gives \(D=(nq-e)/2\) and \(\lambda D=((n-3)q-e)/2\).
The latter numerator before division by two is at least \((n-4)q\), and \(nq-e\le nq\), giving the stated lower bound for \(\lambda^2D\).
The dyadic rotation parameter \(k\) belongs to the irreducible factor and may vary independently of \(q\).
The inequality \(\log(2\cdot4^{10k})\le21k\log 2\) cancels this parameter and yields \(384\cdot2\cdot21=16128\) in (2).
For \(n\ge1000\), using \(0.694\) for the upper bound on \(\log2\) gives \(16128\cdot3.001\cdot0.694/(0.996)^2<33861<34000\).

For \(2\le n<1000\), the base factor \(P\) has \(0<m(P)\le\log\sqrt6<1\) by multiplicativity, nonnegativity, Jensen and Parseval.
For a root \(\gamma\) of the lifted factor, \(\alpha=\gamma^q\) is nontorsion and \(\eta=N_{\mathbb Q(\gamma)/\mathbb Q(\alpha)}\gamma\) is an algebraic unit satisfying \(\eta^q=\alpha^t\), where \(t=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\).
Thus \(\eta\) has positive height, whereas the only rational algebraic units are \(\pm1\), giving \(2\le d_\eta\le d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\).
Using \(h(\eta)=t\,m(P)/(qd)\), Corollary 2 of Voutier gives
\[
 \frac{dt}{q}>\frac{2d^2}{d_\eta\,m(P)(\log(3d_\eta))^3}
              \ge \frac{2d}{(\log(3d))^3}.
\]
Differentiation gives the sign of \(\log(3d)-3\) for the derivative of \(d/(\log(3d))^3\) on \(d\ge2\).
Consequently \(dt/q>2\exp(3)/81>1/3\), which exceeds \(2998/34000\) throughout this range.
At \(n=1\) the polynomial is \(-A_1(X^q)=X^{4q}+1\) and all roots are torsion.
Dobrowolski's Lemma 1 applies to each monic nontorsion irreducible divisor with nonzero constant coefficient, using at most six terms in its multiple.
Taking logarithms gives the claimed measure floor, and the degree sum with multiplicity bounds the positive-measure factor count.

At odd base exponents, the fixed two-variable specialization theorem applies directly to \(g(Y)+Xb(Y)\).
The upper bound \(m(P)\le C\deg(P)/n\) from the base height comparison then implies a linear lower degree bound, and removal of the exact cyclotomic part leaves positive degree for odd \(n\ge3\).
For \(q=2^k\), the established nonsquareness of both signs gives the irreducibility of each substituted nontorsion factor, and distinct base factors stay coprime by substitution in a Bézout identity.
The single possible simple cyclotomic factor becomes \(X^{4\cdot2^k}+1=\Phi_{2^{k+3}}(X)\).
For odd \(q\), each primitive \(8d\)-th root with \(d\mid q\) has \(4q\)-th power \(-1\), so the number of cyclotomic factors can grow without bound.

For the moving family the fixed Laurent expression specializes \(Y-X-1\) and \(1-X^{-1}Y-Y\) to the two required degree-\(n\) polynomials.
Their leading coefficients in the homogeneous expression give \(P_L(-1)=1\), hence degree \(10n\).
The denominator has no common zero with its reversal, and a torsion root would map to a conjugate of the Lehmer number with all conjugates on the circle.
All irreducible factors therefore have positive measure and receive the same positive specialization gap \(c_{\mathcal F}\).
The fixed Möbius height bound (M9) applies after choosing a conjugate root above the fixed Lehmer number \(\lambda\).
Using \(m(S)=\deg(S)h(\beta)\) and \(n-2\ge n/3\) gives \(\deg S\ge c_{\mathcal F}n/(3C)\), whence the number counted with multiplicity is at most \(30C/c_{\mathcal F}\).
The inclusion \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\beta)\) gives the exact factor of ten in converting relative degree to absolute degree and excludes relative sublinear degrees as well.
Individual irreducibility in this family remains open in the reviewed argument.
The inspection used the cited project results and source statements at their indicated scopes and did not re-audit the original published proofs.
