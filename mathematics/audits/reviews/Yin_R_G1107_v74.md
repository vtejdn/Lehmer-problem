## An arbitrary fixed number of moving frequencies

The [moving-frequency exclusion](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v32.md#moving-frequencies) holds for arbitrary integral coefficients and frequencies, including collisions modulo an odd composite conductor.
Reflection preserves the error against the real residues because the coefficients are real, and the undefined nodes and the adjoined zero have vanishing relative mass.
After merging collisions the full-grid second moment is exactly \(S=\sum|c_\ell|^2\) and the pointwise squared modulus is at most \(HS\).
For \(S>0\) the fraction with squared modulus at least \(S/2\) is therefore at least \(1/(2H)\).
Tightness forces bounded \(S\), so integrality gives a subsequence with fixed coefficients.
The identically zero case already violates the positive tail.

For fixed coefficients, diagonal extraction gives an eventual value for the average of every character.
The characters of eventual average one form a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^r\), and Smith normal form shows that its annihilator has exactly these trivial characters.
The resulting moments identify the limit as Haar measure on that annihilator by uniform trigonometric approximation.
Convergence in probability of the approximation error identifies the law of the limiting Fourier sum with the normalized marginal.
Full Haar support forces the finite Fourier sum to take real nonnegative values everywhere.
Each component has equal positive mass, so absence of atoms forces positive torus dimension and nonconstancy on every component.

In Smith coordinates the coefficients on a component are sums of integer multiples of roots of unity.
The full Galois group of the common cyclotomic field permutes the component labels and acts on each coefficient at its fixed torus exponent.
Choose an integer direction separating all exponents within each component, including zero.
Galois conjugation preserves the support on an orbit of labels, and hence its positive largest projected exponent \(N\).
The product of the corresponding leading coefficients over all distinct labels in that orbit is a nonzero algebraic integer fixed by the full Galois group, even if some coefficient values repeat.
Consequently the geometric mean of their absolute values is at least one.
The average of the nonnegative restriction over the \(N\)-th roots gives \(c_\omega\ge2|a_\omega|\) on each component.
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and the global mean bound force equality in every orbit, then \(|a_\omega|=1\) and \(c_\omega=2\) for every label.
At a zero of the root average all \(N\) rotated values vanish, and nonnegativity makes all these distinct zeros double.
They exhaust the degree \(2N\) of the polynomial obtained by multiplying by \(z^N\).
The resulting constant and opposite top frequencies have range \([0,4]\) for every complex phase of \(a_\omega\).
Separation of exponents transfers this description to each whole component, contradicting the positive marginal mass above four.
This verifies the ledger's exclusion using the [proved tail bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#threshold) in the stated shrinking chamber.

## The stationary law for every consecutive dyadic pair test

The [stationary example](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v32.md#relaxation) satisfies every ordered inequality of C898v1 and fails the arithmetic marginal logarithm bound.
The uniform measure on the two alternating phases is shift invariant, as is the constant label sequence, and independent draws conditional on the labels preserve stationarity.
The marginal is a mixture of two continuous uniform laws with weights \(3/4,1/4\), while the oriented pairs \(II,IJ,JI\) have respective weights \(1/2,1/4,1/4\).
The two upper endpoints give mean less than two and mean logarithm at most \(\tfrac14\log((3/10)^3(601/100))<0\).

For \(m=1\) the absolute difference is \(|v-(u-2)^2|\), and for \(m=2\) it is \(|(v-2)^2-((u-2)^2-2)^2|\).
Using \((I-2)^2=[2.89,3.0625]\) and \((J-2)^2=[16,16.0801]\) verifies both rows of lower bounds.
The products with the \(II\) bound squared exceed \((2m)^4\) by factors greater than \(19\) and \(6\), respectively.
At \(m=4\), monotonicity on the external interval and the trace formula give the two lower bounds \(192,37632\) on the mixed pairs.
On \(II\), \(T_4(v-2)\) is monotone with derivative of magnitude greater than six, giving density at most \(20/6\).
The integrated tail bound for the negative logarithm applies conditionally at every \(u\), by independence of the draws given \(II\).
The resulting weighted lower estimate exceeds \(2.49>\log 8\).

For \(m\ge8\) the density of \(\theta=\arccos((v-2)/2)\) on \(I\) is \(40\sin\theta<22\) and the support length is less than \(0.052\).
At most \(1+0.052m/(2\pi)\) translates contribute to the wrapped density, each with a factor \(1/m\), giving total density less than three.
The two sine terms in the cosine difference each have expected negative logarithm bounded by \(1+\log(3\pi)<3.25\), uniformly in the phase determined by \(u\).
On the mixed pairs, \(3^m-2\) and \(3^{2m}-2\) suffice, yielding the stated lower estimate \(-3.25+(3m\log3-2\log2)/4\).
This exceeds \(\log16\) at \(m=8\) and its slope exceeds \(1/m\) thereafter.
Together these checks give strict inequality at every dyadic order for this single stationary process.
The failure of the marginal test excludes it as a normalized residue law.
