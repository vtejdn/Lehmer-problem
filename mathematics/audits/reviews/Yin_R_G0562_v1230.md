## Uniqueness on each fixed grid and the scope of the moment obstructions

At 2026-11-25T08:54 UTC a reviewer outside the development checked J0185's reconstruction using the residue weights \(r_i\), its node-moment and equally spaced grid exclusions, and the accompanying Python trials for Yin.
The reviewer inspected the mathematical inputs directly, derived the coefficient bounds, and reproduced the experiments without development dialogue or earlier findings on these inputs.

At each simple grid point the alternating sign gives a positive weight \(r_i=-H(t_i)/Q'(t_i)\).
For any specified positive endpoint \(u\) with \(H(u)>0\), evaluating the partial fractions at \(u\) gives \(\sum r_i/(u-t_i)<1\) and hence \(s=\sum r_i<4+u\).
One can use \(u=1/M\) at a positive pole value and \(u=2/q\) at a signed pole value when the positive root must lie below \(2/q\).
For \(j\ge2\), the coefficient \(c_j\) enters the weighted moment of the shifted monic Chebyshev polynomial of degree \(j-1\) with coefficient \(-1\).
Each extremal level omits at least one of the \(n\) nodes by the degree bound, so the interval for \(c_j\) has open endpoints and length \(4s\).
At \(q\ge4\) even \(4(4+2/q)<M\).
The known residue of \(c_j\) modulo \(M\) then gives at most one integer at each step, and the pole value fixes the last coefficient exactly.
The resulting strict signs give \(n\) roots in disjoint intervals by the intermediate value theorem.
This establishes uniqueness for arbitrary fixed real grids, with rational arithmetic sufficient on rational inputs.

For the degree bound, the exact first node sum and \(K=L^2=1+4M\) force \(s=2n-4-MA\) to be an even integer.
Writing \(\sum t_i^2=6n+2+2\varepsilon\), the weighted Cauchy inequality and the endpoint bound \(\mu>-4s\) constrain the integer \(b=\mu+\varepsilon\) to \((-4s,s/M-s^2+1/2)\).
This eliminates \(s=4\) and leaves \(-7\le b\le-4\) at \(s=2\).
Substituting \(n=3+MA/2\) into \(b\) gives \(b\equiv A-4-MA/2\bmod M\).
The least allowable positive \(A\) is at least \(M/2-3\), giving \(n\ge669943\) at \(q=40\).
This argument requires the exact first sum and the specified one-sided square-sum bound, including when the knots approximate Lobatto nodes.
For the third-moment test, reflection about \(-2\) and the exact square sum \(6n+2\) give \(d_3=8\binom n3-2(n+1)(n-2)\).
At \(n=423,\ q=5\) I obtain \(d_1=846,\ d_2=356588,\ d_3=99844360,\ A=28,\ B=11831\).
The variance is strictly positive and every interior node satisfies \(t_i(t_i+4)<0\), forcing \(18<\nu<24\), while the coefficient recurrence forces \(\nu\equiv11\bmod30\).
This excludes every symmetric grid satisfying that exact moment identity at these parameters.

For equally spaced nodes and \(K=4M+1\), an integer root at \(-2\) would force \(2M+1\) to divide \(4M+1=2(2M+1)-1\).
Hence \(|H(-2)|\ge1\), while interlacing bounds it by \((2+1/M)U_m\).
The two new outer intervals and the rescaling of all \(m\) older intervals give \(U_{m+2}/U_m=4(m/(m+2))^m<1\) for \(m>2\).
Both recorded base fractions for \(n=16,17\) are below one at \(M=30\), proving the exclusion for both parities at every \(q\ge5,\ n\ge16\).

The program implements Newton's identities over rational numbers, chooses the first residue representative strictly above the lower bound, checks for a second representative where needed, and verifies the signs at the grid and positive endpoint.
The identity \(K=1+\sum_{j=1}^n c_jM^j\) immediately rules out every target outside \(1\bmod M\), and every documented trial meets this initial congruence.
The four undeformed grids have first failures \(2,2,4,3\), in the documented order.
For the quintic control I independently obtained the node values \((-1,461/1024,-1,3,-1)\) and pole value \(6762031\).
For the cubic control, shifting the degree-six witness's trace by two gives \(x^3+5x^2+5x-1\), with signs \((-,+,-)\) on the three nodes, positive value at one, mass one and pole value \(-5\).
The first width is five and the subsequent widths are four, each below the modulus six in this example.
The rational deformation is strictly increasing since its derivative lies in \([3/4,5/4]\), and its node sum gives the prescribed \(c_1\) and mass.
The full run reproduced all first-failure counts, with \(107,86,7\) exclusions at \(q=5,6,40\), respectively.
The finite trials concern those particular grids, and the two general exclusions cover precisely their stated classes of grids.
