### Affine actions in both characteristics

If a transitive group of permutations of a block contains a regular normal elementary abelian group \(V\), identify the block with \(V\) by the orbit map at an origin.
Conjugation by each permutation induces a linear automorphism of \(V\), giving its affine action after the image of the origin is specified.
When the characteristic is odd, \(2b_0-a\) is fixed by every affine transformation fixing an ordered pair \(a,b_0\) and differs from both points, including in characteristic three.
At the ordinary pair of C345 v266 the required orbit size tends to infinity, uniformly for \(0<m\le M\), excluding this case.

In characteristic two, all translations lift from the induced permutation group to automorphisms preserving the group generated by the actual roots in the block.
On its rational vector space modulo torsion these lifts preserve ranks, including when they interchange the two possible roots above a label.
The distinct projective labels span a space of rank at least four for large \(d\), uniformly in the block.
On projecting modulo \(L_0\), no other point vanishes, and merging coincident projected points preserves a span of rank at least three.
Choose the two distinct points of an ordinary line in this projected set, and take any labels \(a,b_0\) lifting them.
The three lines \(L_0,L_a,L_{b_0}\) have rank three.
The plane \(\{0,a,b_0,a+b_0\}\) in \(V\) has four elements, and its translations permute transitively the four triples obtained by deleting one element.
Thus each of these triples also has rank three in the rational multiplicative representation.
In particular \(L_{a+b_0}\) can project to neither of the original two projected points, since otherwise one of the triples containing \(L_0\) would have rank two.
Its projection lies outside their ordinary line.
This proves rank four for the lifted four lines even if other, unused labels coincide after projection.
The proof requires no transitivity on triples by an affine point stabilizer.

In each translated block fix one transporter from the original block.
After this identification, possible images of the four labels are affine images, so the fourth is uniquely specified by the first three as their affine sum.
With \(u=d/(wb)\) translated blocks and \(w\le2\) roots per label, the absolute joint degree is at most \(ub^3w^4=d b^2 w^3\le8d^3\).
The relative degree over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is no larger.
Delsinne's product theorem at four multiplicatively independent coordinates of equal height \(m/d\) now gives \(m^4\gg d/\log^{K_4}(3d)\), impossible at bounded \(m\) for large \(d\).

### Normal orbits and the splitting fields

For the faithful action \(H\) on a block \(B\), a nontrivial radical has a nontrivial characteristic abelian subgroup given by its last nontrivial derived term.
Taking elements killed by a prime divisor of the order gives a nontrivial elementary abelian normal subgroup \(E\) of \(H\).
Some \(E\)-orbit \(B'\) has more than one point by faithfulness.
Every element of \(H\) permutes the \(E\)-orbits by normality, so \(B'\) is a block in \(B\), and an element of the full label group moving \(B\) moves \(B'\) to a disjoint set.
The image of \(E\) on \(B'\) acts faithfully and transitively.
An element of this abelian permutation image fixing one point fixes the whole orbit, proving regularity.
Any element of the full group stabilizing \(B'\) preserves \(B\), since \(B'\subseteq B\) is nonempty, and thus normalizes the image of \(E\) on \(B'\).
The result for regular normal elementary abelian subgroups applies to \(B'\) at the same threshold in \(d\).
Taking \(B\) to be all labels gives \(R(G_0)=1\).
The number of labels is \(d/w\) and is at least two for the asserted large degrees.

The kernel from \(G_\gamma\) to \(G_0\) embeds in a product of copies of \(S_w\) by its faithful action on the actual roots.
It is elementary abelian of exponent at most two.
Every solvable normal subgroup maps trivially to \(G_0\), so this kernel is the entire radical of \(G_\gamma\).
For the original compression write \(L_\alpha,L_\gamma\) for the two rational splitting fields.
The class products give \(L_\gamma\subseteq L_\alpha\), and restriction is surjective by normality.
If \(f\) is the class size, \(x\) a member and \(y\) its class product, then \(y/x^f\) is the product of within-class root-of-unity quotients.
Taking \(N\) divisible by their orders gives \(x^{fN}=y^N\) for every class simultaneously.
Over \(L_\gamma\), the extension obtained by adjoining \(\mu_{fN}\) and all roots of every \(X^{fN}-y^N\) is Galois.
The cyclotomic step is abelian, and the relative group of the second step injects into a product of copies of \(\mu_{fN}\) by acting on one chosen radical per class.
The field contains \(L_\alpha\), so \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_\alpha/L_\gamma)\) is a quotient of a solvable Galois group.
The kernel of \(G_\alpha\to G_0\) is therefore an extension of two solvable groups.
It coincides with \(R(G_\alpha)\) because \(R(G_0)=1\), giving the canonical restriction and label-action quotient.
Finally, when a block radical is nontrivial the preceding theorem bounds \(d\) in terms of \(M\).
The coefficient bound \(2^d e^M\) for the primitive minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\) leaves finitely many such \(\gamma\), hence finitely many original measure values by the exact height compression.
The corrected statement presents this finiteness as a consequence of the degree bound.
