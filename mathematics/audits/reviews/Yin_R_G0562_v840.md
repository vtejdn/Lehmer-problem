## Independent review of the trace code and the all-delay count

At 2026-11-21T01:00 (UTC), an agent independent of the development of C345 v703 checked its complete fixed proof for Sheng Yin and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had contributed to the earlier inputs v687 and v698 and checked their use here at their respective prime-localization and Frobenius scopes.

For a Salem minimal polynomial the reflected list used in the discriminant estimate consists of the \(d-2\) neutral conjugates and two copies of \(\beta^{-1}\).
At \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\) the weight \(\rho^{2j}\) is at least \(e^{-1}\) through \(h=d/2\).
Removing \(2\beta^{-j}\) costs \(O(\log(2d))\) in the squared harmonic norm by the triangle inequality for squares.
Thus v685 gives the claimed absolute bound for \(\sum_{j\le h}|u_j|^2/j\) over the entire range \(1<\beta\le B\).

For fixed coefficient residues and previously decoded values, Newton's identity fixes the single residue
\[
              -j c_j-\sum_{i<j}a_i s_{j-i}\pmod{jM}
\]
for \(s_j\), where the sum starts at \(i=1\).
The predictor of \(s_j\) for \(j\ge2\) only uses \(s_{j-1}\) and the common bound \(B\).
The tie convention and this known residue therefore determine \(b_j\) before reading the next correction.
At \(j=1\) the predictor zero and the prescribed first residue initialize the same reconstruction.
From \(b_j+jMz_j\) one obtains \(s_j\) and then the integral coefficient \(a_j\).
This proves injectivity because the reciprocal polynomial is determined by the coefficients through its center, and each Salem polynomial has one exterior root.

Nearest-point rounding in the specified progression gives \(jM|z_j|\le2|s_j-y_j|\), also when the integer correction vanishes.
Clipping \(s_{j-1}\) to \([1,B^{j-1}]\) decreases or preserves its distance to \(\beta^{j-1}\).
The derivative of the prediction function at the upper endpoint is \(jB/(j-1)\le2B\), which also bounds it throughout that interval.
Since \(\beta^{-j}\le1\), this yields the asserted adjacent-trace error with no accumulated decoding error.
The first error is bounded separately by \(B+1+|u_1|\).
Squaring and summing uses \(1/j\le1/(j-1)\) for the shifted neutral terms and \(B\ge1\) for the constant terms.
Consequently the code has \(\sum j|z_j|\le\sum jz_j^2\ll (B/M)^2d\log(2dB)\) with an absolute implied constant.

For a budget \(E>0\), the signed partition product has logarithm at most \(vE+2\sum_{l\ge1}1/[l(e^{vl}-1)]\le vE+O(1/v)\) for any \(v>0\).
One may take \(v=E^{-1/2}\) even for \(E<1\), when only the zero vector has such small weight.
Accordingly the count has precisely the asserted exponential dependence on \(B/M\), without a prefactor that would affect large-modulus uniqueness.
Choosing the absolute modulus threshold to make the upper bound strictly less than two gives at most one.
For \(M=1\) and fixed \(B>1\), summing over the at most \(D\) degrees costs only \(\log D\) in the logarithmic count, preserving subexponential growth through degree \(D\).
At \(B=1\) the interval is empty.

For a counted tuple, the cofactor \(U\) depends only on the delay and sign, so division by this fixed monic polynomial determines \(P_a\bmod p\) from the seed residue.
The positive in-field radical generates the field and has Salem signature.
Its distinct conjugates power bijectively onto those of \(r\), and Frobenius fixes the reduced multiset of roots of its integral minimal polynomial, including multiplicities.
Hence \(S\equiv P_a\bmod p\) coefficientwise, at the exact common degree \(d=n+1-\deg U\le p\).
The bound \(\beta<5p\) holds for all these tuples by v687, regardless of whether the seed fails the divisor bound for its radical index.
Likewise the prime size estimate in v686 only needs \(p\log\beta=\log r<Z\), \(d\le p\) and the degree-dependent lower bound on \(\log\beta\).
These hypotheses hold for every tuple counted here.
They give \(p\ll Z\log^3(2Z)\), extending to \(X\ge3\) by enlargement of the absolute constant on bounded intervals.

When \(p\log p\le2Z\), the bound \(B\le5p\) makes \(B/p\) bounded and \(d\log(2dB)\ll Z\).
In the other range \(Z/p<(\log p)/2\), giving \(B\le\sqrt p\).
Here the exponent in (1) is \(O(\sqrt{\log(2p)})=O(\sqrt Z)\) by the polynomial range for \(p\).
There are polynomially many joint choices of \(p,n,\epsilon,a\bmod p\) in \(1+Z\), including the residue choices for each prime.
Their logarithmic cost \(O(\log(1+Z))\) is absorbed by \(O(\sqrt Z)\).
For fixed such data and \(\beta\), the formula for \(a\) has positive denominator \(r^n-\epsilon r\) because \(r=\beta^p>1\) and \(n>1\).
Thus the argument counts the tuples themselves, even when a seed has multiple witnesses.
Finally v686 covers every prime at most the delay, including \(p=n\) when prime.
Failure of \(R\mid H(a)\) hence forces a larger prime dividing \(R\), and taking a power of the positive \(R\)-th root gives the required first field radical.
Projecting the tuples onto the seed coordinate proves the distinct-seed assertion.
