# Independent review of the occupation of seed poles by a critical factor

## Two logarithmic means and the exterior fraction

On November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the [occupation theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v543.md#statement) from the fixed candidate and its cited inputs, without participating in its development or receiving its development discussion.
Both estimates in (1) and the exterior fraction in (2) follow uniformly under the stated hypotheses.

For the truncated logarithm, convolution in the two variables costs at most twice the Lipschitz constant times the first absolute Fejér moment, giving \(O(s^{-3/16}\log s)\).
Only the constant frequency survives integration on the Haar graph, since \(i+sj=0\) in this box forces \(i=j=0\).
For each remaining frequency the power has height \(O(H/s^2)\) and degree \(N\ge d/((s+1)H)\).
The critical lower bound on \(a\) makes \((\log N)/N\) smaller than any fixed inverse power of \(s\), uniformly in the box.
The compact phase tests therefore give discrepancy \(O(\sqrt H/s)\) before the omission of off-circle roots, whose mass is \(2k/d=O(s^{-2})\).
This omission also accounts for the constant frequency.
Summing the coefficient bounds gives \(O(s^{-3/8}\log s)\) for the nonconstant character error.
The two simple angular zeros of \(|1+z|-1\) bound the small-value probabilities on both continuous measures by \(O(v)\), so the integral against \(dv/v\) up to \(\delta\) is \(O(\delta)\).
For the discrete mean, the lower cutoff \(c/s\) makes the additional probability error \(O(s^{-1/2})\) cost \(O(s^{-1/2}\log s)\).
Jensen's formula in the second torus variable gives \(\mu\), and the unit constant term equates the graph mean with the sum of the interior logarithmic radii.
The integral norm bounds the difference between the discrete mean and \((1-s/r)h/D\) by \(O((a+k\log(2s))/d)\).
This is \(O(s^{-2}\log s)\), and \(s/r=O(s^{-2})\), giving the second comparison with \(\mu\).

For every divisor \(q\mid a\), the projection is a nonzero integer power of a unit and has \(d\) distinct conjugates and logarithmic measure \(h/q\).
In a single branch the numerator of the disk ratio is \(O(e^{-rL_j}/q)\), since \(rs^{-4}=O(1/s)\) and the branch derivative is bounded by \(O(1/q)\) on the convex localization disk.
The denominator is at least \(1-\exp(-cL_j/q)\gg L_j/q\), leaving an additive \(O(\log s)\) in the logarithmic ratio even at \(q=a\).
For a cluster of size \(n\), the squared Vandermonde counts each of its \(\binom n2\) pairs twice, and reflection \(u\mapsto1/\bar u\) gives another disjoint set of the same size with identical disk ratios.
The loss is therefore at least \(2n(n-1)(rL_j-O(\log s))\).
All remaining same-side ratios are at most one, and the folded product is bounded by \(d^d\), giving (4) with its stated factor two.
With \(D=d/r\), its measure term after rescaling is \((1-1/d)h/D\), while its degree term is \(q\log d/(2D)\), before the relative \(O(\log s/s)\) correction.
The diagonal contributes \(qH_1/D^2=O(q/D)\), and \(|H_1-h|/D\ll se^{-cs}\) by localization of all \(k\) exterior roots.
After centering, the leading contribution is \(\sum_j L_j-h/D=O(s^{-1/16})\).
This argument also covers divisors where the resulting error exceeds one.

For \(sL_j\le u\) the seed lies within \(Cu\) of a primitive cube root for an absolute \(C\).
At either center \(|g|\ge1\), while the logarithm of the maximum on the disk of radius \(2Cu\) is \(O(1+su)\), so Jensen bounds the number in the smaller disk by \(O(1+su)\).
On each dyadic interval with lower endpoint \(u\gg 1/s\), the sum of \((sL_j)^{-1}\) is thus \(O(s)\).
The radial lower bound permits \(O(\log s)\) intervals, giving \(\sum_jL_j^{-1}\ll s^2\log s\) after including the deeper seeds.
Cauchy–Schwarz over the \(qt_s\) branches now gives mean absolute error \(O(\sqrt{e_q\log(2s)})\) since \(t_s=s/3+O(1)\).
When this bound tends to zero, Markov's inequality gives convergence outside a vanishing fraction of branches, even with a sufficiently slowly decreasing tolerance.
At \(q=1\), the signed mean is \(k/(Dt_s)-1\) and \(\log(2d)/D\) decays faster than any fixed inverse power of \(s\), giving the claimed relative error and \(k/d\sim s/(3r)\).
The review uses the recorded critical scales, localization, power-field theorem and discrepancy estimate at their stated scopes.
