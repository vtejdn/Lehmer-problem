## Finitely many logarithmic tests, degree and coefficient cost

At 2026-11-24T19:54 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v15.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v15, including the clarified varying-degree example.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v15.
The proof gives (1) with an absolute constant for all nonzero real polynomials of positive degree, all \(M\ge2\) and all \(a\ge1\).

The finite lower bounds on the cyclotomic integrals guarantee absolute integrability for every factor used when \(kj\le M\).
Each factorization involves at most \(M\) distinct factors, giving losses at most \(\xi=M^{-1}\) for \(A(k)\) and for \(A(kj)-A(k)\).
For the smoothed logarithm the identity \(|1-e^{-s}z|^2=e^{-s}|1-z|^2+(1-e^{-s})^2\) gives the lower estimate in (2).
When \(|1-z|\ge1/L\), the upper smoothing error is at most \(sL\).
On the smaller arc, \(F_L-f\le\log L\) and \(b_s\le\log(1/L+s)\), whose sum is at most \(sL\).
The exceptional zeros have measure zero for the powers under consideration.
The finite divisibility bounds then justify integration of the two pointwise cases and give the upper estimate in (2).

For the arc at one, the average \(F_L(z^k)\) is at most \(-\log2\) on the chosen arc while its integral is at least \(-\xi\).
The condition \(k/\delta\le M\) ensures \(kL\le M\) at the specified \(L\).
For a radius \(x\) about any center, Dirichlet approximation maps the arc within chord distance \(O(\sqrt x)\) of one using a multiplier \(k\le2/\sqrt x\).
On the \(j\)-th power image measure this requires \(jk/\delta\ll j/x\le M\) after fixing the absolute lower cutoff on \(x\).
The term \(\xi\) is absorbed there, and the total mass handles larger radii where the small-arc estimate no longer applies.
The Fejér integral at order \(2N\) uses only radii at least \(1/N\).
Thus \(jN\le c_0M\) suffices for the inner arc and every annulus, giving (3) without imposing a mass estimate on finer scales.

For any fixed \(R\), \(\sum_{m\ge1}\tau(m)e^{-m/R}/m<\infty\), so the doubly indexed damped series can be integrated and regrouped.
The damping at frequency \(km\) is \(e^{-m/R}\) independently of its divisor \(j\), yielding the Möbius cancellation in the displayed identity.
For \(k\le R,\ j\le Y\) the choices of \(R,Y,L\) give \(kjL\le M\) and \(j/R\le1\).
Applying (2) and summing with weights \(1/j\) gives the stated bound for these terms.
In the remainder the divisor count bounds the absolute coefficient after grouping by \(m\).
On dyadic blocks from \(Y\) through \(R^2\), the condition \(2kR^2\le c_0M\) holds eventually, so (3) and the divisor square-sum estimate give the summable contributions \(O(N^{-1/6}(1+\log(2N))^{3/2})\).
For \(m>R^2\) the damping bounds the tail geometrically using only \(|\widehat\mu(km)|\le1\).
Since \(Y^{-1/6}\ll M^{-1/48}\), evenness gives the claimed ordinary Fourier coefficient bound through \(R\).

For each reflected root \(w\) with \(|w|\le1\), the radial majorant loses \(O(R^{-1/2})\) uniformly up to the unit circle.
The convergent series for the smoothed root logarithm costs at most a harmonic sum times the low-frequency bound and an exponentially small tail above \(R\).
The logarithmic factors multiplying \(M^{-1/48}\) are absorbed by \(M^{-1/64}\).
The leading coefficient and exterior-root constants sum to \(m(P)\), giving (4) independently of all root locations and coefficient sizes.
The pointwise bound \(m(P)+d\log2\) covers bounded \(M\).

For fixed \(P,M\), the continuous truncations can all be made at a single level.
Otherwise weak compactness at every fixed truncation followed by monotone passage to the original logarithms would produce a measure meeting the cyclotomic constraints and the higher \(m(P)+2E\) bound, contrary to (4).
Strict separation of the compact moment set from the closed translated orthant gives a nonnegative normal with positive first entry, since all truncated Haar cyclotomic moments are nonnegative.
Symmetrized point masses give the pointwise upper bound, and integration against Haar after dropping the truncations bounds \(\sum w_n\) by \(2M^2E\).
This last estimate uses the negative threshold \(-M^{-2}\) in each coordinate.
Rounding upwards costs at most \(M^2\log2\) in log supremum and \(M^2\) in degree, in addition to the weighted degree bound \(2aM^3E\).
The coefficient conversion costs at most \(\tfrac12\log(1+\deg(P^a H))=O(\log(2+adM))\), proving both inequalities.

The normalized log coefficient sum is bounded below by \(m(P)\) since \(m(H)=0\), giving the asserted limit when \(m(P)\to0\).
These choices give total degree \(O(d^{642})\), and the only noncyclotomic irreducible factor is the given minimum.
