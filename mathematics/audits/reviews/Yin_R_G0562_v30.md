## Both signs and every Pisot seed

At 2026-11-16T19:57 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the whole submitted claim C345v30 and its twenty direct dependencies.
The reviewer had no part in the proof development and received the fixed mathematical texts without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict on this claim.
The reviewer used the three previously proved input theorems at their stated scopes and consulted their independent reviews of the larger-delay gap, the dyadic limit and the first discrepancy. [Larger delays](Yin_R_G0562_v27.md#uniform-delay-gap), [dyadic limit](Yin_R_G0562_v28.md#dyadic-limit), [first discrepancy](Yin_R_G0562_v29.md#centered-limit).

Failure of an absolute gap would supply inputs with \(1<M_i<1+1/i\), even with the degree, seed, sign and delay all free to vary.
The larger-delay gap then makes \(N_i=2\) eventually, and the next two input theorems give the matched sign \(z^2f+e f^*\), \(e=f(0)=\pm1\), and a first discrepancy \(a=\pm1\) from \(1/(1-2z)\) at \(n\to\infty\).
We may fix \(a\) on a subsequence independently of \(e\).
For \(F=e f/f^*\), the identity \(K^0-1=z((1-2z)F-1)/(1+z^2F)\) yields the initial zero block and coefficient \(a\) at \(p=n+1\).
The reciprocal root \(\tau=1/M_i\) satisfies \(F(\tau)=-\tau^{-2}\) by coprimality, and the numerator of \(K^0\) at \(\tau\) equals \((1-\tau)(1-\tau^{-1})\ne0\).
For the positive representing measure of \(K\), canceling the pole at \(\tau\) leaves boundary residues of positive Herglotz weight because the finite Blaschke map fixing \(\tau\) has angular derivative greater than one at every boundary fixed point.
Its mass is \(1+t/2\), where \(t=M_i+M_i^{-1}-2\).
Reflection and the value \(h_0=2\) therefore give the bilateral moment approximation at zero with error \(t\).

The initial bound \(t=O(n^{-2})\) bounds the row sums of the Gram matrix of \(n\) consecutive monomials and hence the squared moments on every translate of such a block.
This gives the uniform approximation on fixed multiples of \(n\) by convolution with the inverse quadratic denominator.
A coefficient of absolute value two at a nonzero moving \(j=O(n)\) would concentrate \(v^{2|j|}\) at one, giving the single-binomial recurrence contradiction checked below.
A purely atomic marginal of \(v^p\) is excluded by the same argument because its integral doubled moments give a common torsion period.
The integral Herglotz classification for a marginal with a continuous part, and its first coefficient \(a\), then give exactly the two stated laws.

For \(j=O(n)\) with \(p\nmid j\), take a joint limit of \((x,X)=(v^p,v^j)\).
Integrality at every \(j+kp\) supplies the Fourier coefficients of the projection to \(x\) weighted by \(2X\).
In the density case, the bound by \(W(x)=2+a(x+x^{-1})=ax^{-1}(x+a)^2\) and the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma give an integer Laurent polynomial divisible by \(W\).
Indeed the pointwise bound forces a double zero, and the divisor differs from a monic square by a Laurent unit.
Parseval makes the quotient zero or a signed monomial.
The latter forces the doubled moment at \(2(j-bp)\) to tend to two for some fixed integer \(b\), an excluded nonzero frequency because \(p\nmid j\).
For the half-atom marginal the continuous Fourier coefficients tend to zero on the even tail, so the atomic conditional mean is integral and the continuous Fourier coefficients are integral separately.
Any nonzero continuous coefficient has modulus one, forcing a signed binomial on that component and the binomial \(1-av^p\) on the atom.
Their product falls under the recurrence exclusion at scale \(n\), since its second exponent \(|j+kp|\) is nonzero and \(O(n)\).
With the continuous coefficients zero, a shift into \(0<|j-lp|<p\) kills the atomic contribution as well.
The bounded possible multipliers at multiples of \(p\) are determined by the marginal itself.
These arguments apply to any proposed sequence of offending indices, which gives exact agreement on each full linear window.

The first discrepancy \(q\) from the corresponding model exists by the interior pole, and the exact agreement just proved gives \(q/p\to\infty\).
Writing \(A(s)=s+\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}(s-k)H_k\), coefficient comparison up to \(q-1\) gives \(K_j=H_j-t(A(j)-1)\) for \(1\le j<q\).
Fejér positivity gives \(t(\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}(q-j)(A(j)-1)-q/2)\le A(q)\), including the mass correction at zero.
For the two polynomial signs \(A(s)=s\pm(s-p)_+\), so the negative sign reaches the positive plateau \(p\).
For the inverse models \(A(s)\) is comparable to \(s^2/p\) for \(s\ge2p\) with the positive sign, and lies between \(s/2\) and \(s\) with the alternating sign.
Thus \(A(j)-1\) is bounded below by an absolute multiple of \(A(q)\) on \(q/2\le j\le3q/4\) eventually in all four cases, proving \(t=O(q^{-2})\).
On \(1\le j\le Cq\) the block estimate now gives \(\sum_j|K_j|=O_C(q/\sqrt p)\), and \(q\log M_i=O(1)\) bounds each coefficient of the inverse quadratic denominator by \(O_C(q)\).
Convolution and multiplication by \(t/2\) give the required \(O_C(p^{-1/2})\) error throughout the larger window.

In a joint limit of \((x,y)=(v^p,v^q)\), fix the unequal actual and model coefficients at \(q\) by extraction.
For each fixed integer \(r\), the integer at \(q+rp\) tends to \(m_r=2\int x^r y\), with eventual equality to the model coefficient when \(r<0\).
In the density case the model coefficient at \(q\) vanishes, so \(m_0\ne0\).
The same Laurent division forces the conditional mean of \(y\) to be \(c x^b\), with \(c=\pm1\) and \(b\) fixed.
Its unit modulus forces \(y=c x^b\) almost surely, giving concentration for \(1-cv^{q-bp}\) with \(q-bp\sim q>0\).
In the half-atom case write \(m_r=a^r A+\int x^r B(x)\,dm(x)\) using normalized arclength \(m\) and conditional means on the two components.
If the model coefficient at \(q\) is \(u\), its values on the negative tail are \(u a^r\), both when \(p\nmid q\) and when \(u=a^{q/p}\).
Taking \(r\to-\infty\) in the limiting law identifies \(A=u\).
The continuous Fourier coefficients are integers, and the squared modulus of their nonzero constant \(m_0-u\) attains the upper bound \(\|B\|_2^2\le1\).
Thus \(y=c\) on the continuous component for a fixed \(c=\pm1\), and \(\int|(1-av^p)(1-cv^q)|^2\,d\mu\to0\).

Each support polynomial \(D\) has real coefficients of bounded absolute sum, unit ends, all roots on the circle and degree \(d=O(s)\) at its respective scale \(s=n\) or \(q\).
Cauchy–Schwarz uniformly bounds the convolution residual by \(2\sqrt{\mu(\mathbb T)}\|D\|_{L^2(\mu)}\) plus the enlarged-window approximation error times the coefficient absolute sum.
Its integrality forces exact recurrence on \(|j|\le Cs\) for any fixed \(C\) eventually.
Both unit ends propagate the data to a bilateral sequence \(b\) agreeing with \(h\) on \(|j|\le4d+12\).
Reciprocity gives the same recurrence for the reflection, whose agreement on \(d\) initial values forces \(b_{-j}=b_j\) everywhere.
For \(S=1+\sum_{j>0}b_jz^j\), \(DS\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(d\), and the Laurent polynomial \(D(z)(S(z)+S(1/z))\) has all coefficients zero by the recurrence with \(b_0=2\).
The inverse quotient \(F'=(1-z-S)/(z^2S-z(1-z))\) is analytic at zero with value one because \(S'(0)=0\).
Reciprocity gives \(F'(1/z)F'(z)=1\), and clearing \(D\) bounds its rational degree by \(d+2\).
The comparison difference loses one coefficient on inversion, leaving equality of \(F,F'\) through degree \(4d+11\).
Canceling all disk poles of \(F'\) and the single pole of \(F\) uses at most \(d+3\) common Blaschke factors.
The resulting Schur functions agree through more than the degree \(2d+5\) bounding the finite Blaschke product for \(F'\), so Schur reduction forces equality.
This would give \(K^0=S\), although \(S\) is analytic at \(\tau\) and \(K^0\) has a pole there.

Both possible marginals thus contradict strict positive excess tending to zero.
The uniform gap follows for all integer delays \(N\ge2\), and an escaping sequence among positive construction delays must eventually have \(N=1\).
Boyd's delay-one representation applies to every Salem number and imposes no upper bound on the Pisot seed, as reported in Smyth's Section 3.1. [Delay-one source inspection](../literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#closedness-and-delay-one).
The full submitted claim passes independent review.
The review used Sanders's Fourier–Stieltjes theorem and Boyd's representation at their recorded statement and applicability scopes.
Their original proofs were outside this review.
