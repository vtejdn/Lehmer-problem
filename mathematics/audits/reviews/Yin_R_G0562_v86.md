## The cyclotomic mask, its exact loss and its binomial multiple

Write \(A_c(Y)=(Y^c-1)/\Phi_c(Y)\), a monic integer product over the proper divisors of \(c\).
Since \(q=cm\) is squarefree and \((c,m)=1\), the order of \(x^m\) at a \(q\)-th root \(x\) is exactly the part of its order supported on \(c\).
If a prime factor of \(c\) is missing, \(A_c(x^m)=0\).
If all of \(c\) occurs and a prime \(l\mid m\) is missing, \(x^{m/l}\) has order \(c\), killing the corresponding \(\Phi_c\) factor.
At a primitive \(q\)-th root the respective orders are \(c\) and \(cl\), so the mask is nonzero there.
Every factor is an ordinary integer polynomial whose roots lie on the circle.
Substitution of any positive exponent preserves the supremum on the circle, so the bounded number of factors and the finite range of \(c\) bound the supremum uniformly.

The degree of the first factor is \(m(c-\varphi(c))\) and the remaining degrees sum to \(\varphi(c)m\sum_{l\mid m}1/l\), with the sum over primes.
Subtracting \(q-\varphi(c)\varphi(m)\) gives precisely the asserted excess \(E\).
It vanishes for zero or one prime in \(m\), while for two primes it equals \(\varphi(c)\).
At three primes with \(c=1\), the pairwise terms in the product expansion contribute \(u+v+w\) and the triple intersection subtracts one.
In general the bracket equals the mean of \(J-{\bf1}_{J>0}\) for independent events of probabilities \(1/l\).
Its lower bound is zero and the upper bound follows from \(J-{\bf1}_{J>0}\le\binom J2\) and the pairwise probabilities \(1/(lt)\).

At \(\deg P\le\varphi(n)+H\), the primitive-component result of C345v83 permits at most \(K(B,r)+H+1\) nonzero integral blocks, each with normalized primitive \(q\)-grid average at most \(B\).
For every such block the disjoint residues modulo \(s\) give \(\deg P_j\le\lfloor(\deg P-j)/s\rfloor\).
The additional floor bound thus gives \(\deg(ZP_j)\le q+J_0\).
The full-grid average of \(ZP_j\) is at most \(\|Z\|_\infty\varphi(q)B/q\) by vanishing at the imprimitive roots.
The cyclic coefficient argument applies at any \(q\) once the full-grid mean and this degree bound hold.
In the ordinary representative, each bounded-norm coset term is a finite progression cleared by one binomial, and at most \(J_0+1\) excess positions fold back modulo \(q\).
Each excess coefficient contributes at most two monomials, irrespective of its size.
This supplies a bounded product of integral binomials making \(ZP_j\) sparse.

Multiplying \(Z\) by \(\Phi_c(X^m)\prod_{l\mid m} A_c(X^{m/l})\), with prime indices, gives \((X^{mc}-1)\prod_{l\mid m}(X^{cm/l}-1)\) exactly.
The complementary multiplier has bounded term count even if factors coincide, since the substitutions leave each fixed polynomial's term count unchanged.
For \(c=1\), \(A_1=1\) and \(\Phi_1(Y)=Y-1\).
When \(m=1\) both prime-indexed products are empty, and the first factor alone is completed to \(X^c-1\).
Thus each block has an actual binomial multiplier with positive exponents and sparse integral output, up to a global sign which does not affect sparsity.
Substitution of \(X^s\) in the product of all block multipliers preserves integrality and the upper bound on the number of binomials.
On each shifted block the other binomials multiply its term count by at most two per factor.
Summing the bounded number of blocks therefore proves the asserted sparse completion.
The binomials have measure one, so the Dobrowolski–Smyth gap for this nonzero integral multiple is a gap for \(P\) itself.

For the fractional margin, the primes remaining in \(m\) exceed \(L\), so \(E\le\varphi(c)m\binom r2/L^2\).
Since \(\varphi(q)\ge\varphi(c)m2^{-r}\), the specified choice of \(L\) gives \(E\le\epsilon\varphi(q)\).
The identity \(\varphi(n)=s\varphi(q)\) then supplies the floor bound with \(J_0=H\), also for \(s=1\).
With at most two primes outside the fixed set \(\mathcal S\), there are at most \(|\mathcal S|+2\) primes in total and finitely many possible products \(c\) of primes from \(\mathcal S\).
The exact two-prime bound \(E\le\varphi(c)\) gives \(J_0=H+\max_c\varphi(c)\), including the empty set or empty product.

On the proposed sequence, bounded-degree polynomials of bounded Mahler measure have bounded integer coefficients, so positive measures tending to one force the degrees, then \(\varphi(n_i)\), to tend to infinity.
The upper bound on degree gives a limiting upper ratio at most one.
A subsequence bounded away below one would satisfy a fixed fractional margin, contradicting the first gap.
Likewise any subsequence with bounded third-largest prime would satisfy the second gap by taking the fixed set of all primes below or equal to that bound.
This also covers conductors with fewer than three prime factors.
