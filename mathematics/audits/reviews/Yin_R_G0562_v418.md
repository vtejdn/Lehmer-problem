# Ramification and discriminants of factors of the primitive cubic family

## Independent review of the critical resultant and the prime-size bounds

At 2026-11-18T15:18 UTC an agent outside proof development independently reviewed the [submitted C385 decision](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v385.md#decision-cubic-ramification-submitted) and its entire argument for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed manuscript and scope without development dialogue or earlier findings on this argument.
The resultant identity, the joint discriminant bounds at every finite prime, the alternating-group restriction and the necessary prime sizes hold under the stated hypotheses.

For a root \(x\), write \(t=x^a\).
Substituting \(x^b=-g(t)/B(t)\) in the derivative gives \(xB(t)A'(x)=a t(g'B-gB')-bgB\).
The polynomial identity \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\) yields \(B(t)^{-1}=x^{a+b}+1-x^a\), and \(x^{-1}\) is integral by the constant coefficient of \(-A\).
Their products over the full root set are rational units by Galois invariance.
For \(au+bv=1\), the map \(t\mapsto t^u(-g(t)/B(t))^v\) inverts the power map on the eliminant, which has degree \(3a+b\) and leading coefficient \((-1)^a\).
Thus its resultant with \(R\) has absolute value \(\prod_x|R(x^a)|=|\operatorname{disc}(-A)|\), positive by simplicity.
Expanding \(T^3L(T+T^{-1})\) gives the reciprocal coefficient list with first four entries \(b,b-a,-2a-b,-a-3b\) in ascending degree, as also obtained by differentiating \(g,B\).
As supplementary checks the reviewer computed both integer discriminants and resultants directly at \((a,b)=(1,3),(1,9),(2,23),(3,17),(3,31)\) and obtained equality of their absolute values in all five cases.
Ramification passes up from the trace field to the root field, whose discriminant divides the polynomial discriminant by the integral index formula.
The product discriminant formula then puts each ramified prime in \(D\).

In residual characteristic \(\ell\mid a\), the term \(b x^{b-1}B(x^a)\) survives in the derivative at every root.
For \(\ell\nmid a\), the coordinate \(x^a\) is separable at these units and separates distinct roots by the two-power inverse.
At each residual root of multiplicity \(m\), the polynomial derivative identity gives an order at least \(m-1\) in \(R\), also when \(\ell\mid m\).
The constant term of \(R\) survives if \(\ell\nmid b\), and otherwise the coefficient of \(T\) survives, so the total excess is at most six.
Across the nontorsion factors every distinct characteristic-zero trace labels exactly two distinct roots.
For \(m_s\) labels reducing to \(s\), the quadratic \(X^2-sX+1\) leaves at most two distinct residues for their \(2m_s\) roots, and every residue determines \(s\).
In characteristic two or at a repeated residual quadratic the same upper bound on the number of residues applies.
Additional roots of \(A\) can only increase the sum of the excesses in these residue classes.
Thus \(\sum_s(m_s-1)\le3\), and passing from each residue class to its separate inertia orbits can only decrease the total deficit.

For a decomposition orbit in the joint Galois extension, let \(G_0\) be the decomposition group, \(I\) its inertia, and \(H\subset G_0\) the stabilizer of one local embedding.
The tower of local fields gives \([G_0:H]=ef\) and \([I:I\cap H]=e\).
Normality of \(I\) gives the same orbit length at every point in \(G_0/H\), hence exactly \(f\) inertia orbits even when the trace field is non-Galois.
This proves the joint bound \(\sum f(e-1)\le3\) with fields counted by their factors.
Each ramified field uses at least one unit, and a field using only one has one two-point orbit with all other embeddings fixed by inertia.
An element interchanging those two embeddings acts oddly, so every ramified alternating factor uses at least two units.

The reviewer checked Theorem 4.8 on page 11 and equation (4.12) on page 18 of [Conrad's notes](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/gradnumthy/different.pdf).
With prime-ideal valuation normalized to one on a uniformizer, the extra wild term is bounded by \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(e)=e v_\ell(e)\), and the ideal norm weights the whole different exponent by \(f\).
Only \(e=2,3,4\) can ramify under the deficit bound.
At two, \(e=4\) forces \(f=1\) and uses the whole deficit, adding eight, while the indices two add at most six in total if no index four occurs.
At three, a wild index three forces \(f=1\), adds three, and leaves room for at most one further unit of tame deficit.
All indices are tame above larger primes, giving the joint bounds \(11,6,3\).

Reciprocity and simplicity give trace-field degree \(n_i=\deg(P_i)/2\), since the two roots of its defining quadratic over the trace field are conjugate.
For an exterior root \(z\), the imaginary part of its trace is \((1-|z|^{-2})\operatorname{Im}z\), which is nonzero by the exterior partition.
Its inverse has the same trace, its conjugate gives the conjugate trace, and every unit-circle pair gives a real trace.
Thus the signature has exactly \(k_i\) complex places.
The convex body with the weighted sum of the archimedean absolute values bounded by \(t\) gives \(\sqrt{\Delta_i}\ge(\pi/4)^{k_i}n_i^{n_i}/n_i!\) by taking \(t\uparrow n_i\), since a nonzero algebraic integer has absolute norm at least one.
The tower discriminant gives \(\Delta_i^2\mid|\operatorname{disc}P_i|\).
Each Vandermonde row for a root \(z\) has norm at most \(\sqrt d\max(1,|z|)^{d-1}\), where \(d=2n_i\).
The resulting square root of the discriminant bound is \(d^{d/2}M(P_i)^{d-1}\le(2n_i)^{n_i}8^{n_i-1/2}<(16n_i)^{n_i}\).
Every prime of \(\Delta_i\) is consequently below the cutoff in (2), with extra weights beyond three bounded by \(8\log2+3\log3\) in total.

For \(r\to\infty\), the cyclotomic degree is at most twelve and \(\sum k_i=a=o(b)\).
The fixed graph is irreducible because \(g,B\) are coprime and the equation is primitive of degree one in its second coordinate, and its support differences have rank two.
The individual degree theorem therefore applies in the primitive direction \((a,b)\) with \(N=b\) and dilation one.
Its lower bound makes the minimum half-degree tend to infinity, so \(\sum_i\log(2n_i)=o(\sum_i n_i)=o(b)\).
This bounds the uniform Stirling error and gives \(\sum_i\log\Delta_i\ge b-o(b)\).
Using the joint local bounds once per prime gives \(3\log\operatorname{rad}(D)+8\log2+3\log3\) as an upper bound on this sum.
For an individual shrinking factor the degree theorem gives \(b\lambda_b\ll n_i=o(b)\), and \(n_i\log(16n_i)=o(b\log b)\) gives the cutoff for each fixed positive \(\varepsilon\).
On escaping directions the allocation estimate gives \(k_i/n_i\sim2a/b\), so the left side of (2) is \((2-o(1))n_i\).
Even the uniform signature bound \(k_i\le n_i/2\) suffices for the asserted lower order of prime weight, since \(2+\log(\pi/4)>0\).
The sum of log primes up to \(y\) is \(O(y)\) by the dyadic binomial coefficient bound, hence removing all primes up to \(y=o(b\lambda_b)\) costs \(o(n_i)\).
The argument supplies necessary conditions for shrinking and leaves existence of such factors open.
