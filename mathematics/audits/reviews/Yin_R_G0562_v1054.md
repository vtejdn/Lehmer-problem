# Review of the six-term measure gap under the row degree bound

## The gap is uniform in the exponent parameter and both rows

At 2026-11-24T18:48 an agent outside the development of this proof independently checked all five fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the earlier curve construction and received the present claim without its development dialogue, author explanations or prior findings on this proof.

Let \(k=2r+2\), including \(k=6\) when \(r=2\).
After translating the exponents and dividing their differences by \(g\), reciprocity and the unramified power map give all \(2r\) normalized Euler equations at the projective point \(y\).
The rational solution set in the torus has total component degree at most \(D_r=(k-1)^r\) by successive intersection with \(r\) hyperplanes and \(r\) forms of degree \(k-1\).
Retaining a component already contained in the next hypersurface is compatible with this upper bound, and omitting coordinate-boundary components can only decrease it.
The \(B_i\) have gcd one, so \(y\) in the chart \(z_0=1\) generates \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
If it is an isolated component, all its conjugates are such components and \(e\le D_r\).

The fixed-node interpolation gives \(Q_\pm\) of degrees at most \(r+1\) with product \(S_0+\lambda D\).
Both the product's values and the pivot values fixing each factor's scale are nonzero.
Even when the product has multiple roots or lower degree, there are only finitely many monic divisors, so the fibers of \(\lambda\) are finite.
On each curve component \(\lambda\) is nonconstant, the generic product has degree \(k\), and each interpolant must have degree exactly \(r+1\).
For a selected root \(s\) over a finite value of \(\lambda\), the ratio \((B_i-s)/(B_j-s)\) is a unit at every place of the splitting curve.
Indeed a finite limiting root cannot be a node because \(S_0(B_i)\ne0\), and a root with a pole gives a ratio with value one.
This handles the degree drop over \(\lambda=0\) as well.

Over infinity the reduction of the monic \(D+uS_0\), \(u=1/\lambda\), is the separable \(D\).
At every place above infinity each root is on a unique simple branch through a node \(B_j\), with first displacement \(-uS_0(B_j)/D'(B_j)\).
This coefficient is nonzero, so the order of that displacement is exactly \(\operatorname{ord}(u)\) even at a ramified place.
The \(r+1\) selected roots of \(Q_+\) approach distinct nodes there.
Their product formula for coordinate ratios consequently gives order \(\operatorname{ord}(u)\sum_{i\in I}v_i\) for a projective character with \(\sum_i v_i=0\).
Finite and infinite places exhaust the smooth projective splitting curve.
Zero orders at all places are equivalent to constancy over the algebraically closed constant field, giving exactly lattice (2) on the original component.
The stabilizer of that component defines a field of degree at most \(D_r\), as its index counts its conjugate components.
A constant rational character has value in that field, hence a nonzero pairing with \(B\) gives an actual power of \(\alpha\) of degree at most \(D_r\).
With negative exponent one can equally take its positive opposite.
In the remaining case every such pairing is zero.

The rational row space of the balanced sign vectors has dimension \(s=k-1-\operatorname{rank}\Lambda\), because adjoining the constant vector gives the full rational orthogonal complement of (2).
Two identical columns of a sign-row basis would give \(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_j\in\Lambda\) and \(B_i=B_j\), contradicting distinct occupied exponents.
For \(k=6\), six distinct binary sign columns force \(s\ge3\).
When \(s=3\), the omitted two of the eight cube vertices have opposite signs in every coordinate by balance of each row.
The remaining six therefore come in opposite pairs.
For \(k=8,\ s\le3\) all eight vertices occur, and for \(k>8,\ s\le3\) there are too few vertices.
Since \(B\) lies in the span of the sign rows and the constant vector, opposite columns pair distinct exponents with the same sum.
This produces a single reflection of the full support.

A reflection preserving residues \(0,1\bmod p\) must swap them at every integer \(p\ge3\), because fixing each requires \(p\mid2\).
For six terms this requires a \(3+3\) split.
If the polynomial were proportional to its reflection the scalar would be \(\pm1\) by involution, and the rows would be monomial-shifted reversals of one another up to sign.
This contradicts the nonzero reflected determinant.
The six-term reflection estimate thus gives \(m(\alpha)\ge(\log2)/24\).
For general \(r\), canceling one occupied coefficient in a rational linear combination with the reflection leaves a nonzero polynomial with at most \(2r+1\) terms.
Both order-\(r\) contacts persist, so finite interpolation and norm descent bound the measure of \(\alpha\) itself.

In the counting case \(s\ge4\), independently choose the least exponent of each row.
There are \(h=k-2\) independent differences from those two pivots, also when one row is a singleton.
Every within-row difference is \(p(j_i-j_{i_h})\) and is divisible by \(g\).
As a mixed difference is one modulo \(p\), \(\gcd(g,p)=1\), so \((j_i-j_{i_h})/g\) is a positive integer bounded by \(x=b/g\).
In particular \(x\ge1\).
The box gives \((T+1)^h\) distinct vectors and at most \(hxT+1\) values after division of the pairing by \(p\).
Intersection of a coset of \(\Lambda\) with this box has at most \((T+1)^{k-1-s}\) points, by injective coordinate projection on the affine intersection with the span of these differences.
Since \(h-(k-1-s)=s-1\ge3\), \(T=\lceil\sqrt{hx}\rceil\) gives \((T+1)^{s-1}>hxT+1\).
Two members of a larger fiber thus have difference \(v\notin\Lambda\) with \(B\cdot v=0\).
The homogeneous binomial \(z^v=1\) has degree at most \(hT\), since each basis difference has total positive and negative parts one.
It contains \(y\) and cuts its curve properly.
This same rational binomial cuts every conjugate curve properly, and those curves together have degree at most \(D_r\).
Counting the entire rational orbit in their union yields \(e\le hTD_r\), without an extra factor for the field of definition.

For six terms this gives \(D_2=25,\ h=4,\ T=\lceil2\sqrt{x}\rceil\), so \(e\le100T\le300\sqrt{x}\).
If \(b\le2d\), then \(d\le ge\) by the binomial equation over the power field and \(x\le2e\).
Hence \(e\le180000\) in the counting case.
Norm descent and the house bound give \(m(\alpha)\ge(\log2)/(4D)\) for any power of degree \(D\).
Together with the bounded-power branch and the reflection branch this proves the denominator \(720000\).
For fixed \(r,K\), \(b\le Kd\) gives \(x\le Ke\) and \(T\le(\sqrt h+1)\sqrt{x}\).
Squaring \(e\le hD_r(\sqrt h+1)\sqrt{Ke}\) gives exactly \(e\le K h^2D_r^2(\sqrt h+1)^2\).
The other branches have the asserted denominators \(D_r\) and \(\binom{2r}{r}\).
The individual graph estimate \(\log H\ge p\,m(\alpha)\) uses coprimality for power-field equality, reciprocal multiplicity two for the reflected determinant, and integrality for the measure of the quotient.
It holds for composite \(p\) as well.
Thus the six-term bound is uniform over all \(p\ge3\), all three splits in either order and arbitrary coefficient heights at the specified width.
The higher-multiplicity constant is uniform for each fixed \(r,K\).
The unrestricted Lehmer gap remains open.
