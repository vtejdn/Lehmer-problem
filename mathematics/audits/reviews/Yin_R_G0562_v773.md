## Sparse prefixes and energy growth for scalar multiples

The finite sparsity bound (2) holds for every nonzero integer multiplier and every Laurent representation specified in the claim.
It implies the lower limit (1) with the displayed constant, including the stated inverse-polynomial and reciprocal strict-record applications.

For \(k\ne0\), removal of a zero-valued subset preserves the nonzero value \(kx\) and strictly decreases the number of remaining terms.
It therefore leaves a nonempty set with no vanishing prefix, of mass at most the original \(H\) and size \(t\le s\).
After factoring out \(X^{n_i}\), the \(i\)-th prefix is an ordinary integral polynomial of degree \(\ell_i=n_1-n_i\), even when the original exponents are negative.
Its nonzero value has absolute field norm at least one.
The other \(d-1\) embeddings contribute at most \(H^{d-1}\exp((m-\lambda)\ell_i)\) to that norm, with each complex embedding counted separately.
Restoring \(X^{n_i}\) gives the lower bound \(H^{1-d}\exp(\lambda n_1-m\ell_i)\) for the prefix.

The tail has size at most \(H\exp(\lambda n_{i+1})\), so its ratio to that prefix bound is at most \(H^d\exp((m-\lambda)\ell_i-\lambda g)\).
The stopping inequality makes this at most \(1/2\), including equality at the stopping threshold.
Reaching the final term gives a zero tail.
At each failed test the width grows to less than \(b\ell_i+(d\log H+\log2)/\lambda\), where \(m\ge\lambda+\nu>\lambda\).
Iteration from width zero through at most \(s-1\) failed tests gives \(\ell_i\le L_s\), also when the initial term is the only term.
The triangle inequality at \(\tau\) gives \(\lambda n_1\ge r(\log|k|+\log|x_\tau|-\log H)\).
Substitution into the prefix estimate after allowing for the tail gives exactly \(mL_s+(d-1+r)\log H+\log2+\log|x_\rho|-r\log|x_\tau|\) as an upper bound for \((r-1)\log|k|\).
Both embedding values of \(x\) are nonzero.

For an attaining numerator, integrality and Parseval give \(s\le H\le \sum_j a_j^2\le D^2E(kx)\) with the circle maximum \(D\) used in (1).
The finite bound implies \(\log |k|\le C b^s(1+\log H)\) with fixed \(C\).
A bounded-energy subsequence would bound its right-hand side and hence \(|k|\).
Taking logs for \(|k|\) large and substituting the bound on \(s\) leaves the error \(\log C+\log(1+\log H)=o(E(kx))\).
Dividing by \(E(kx)\) gives an upper limit at most \(D^2\log b\) for the reciprocal ratio and hence (1).

For the inverse generator, \(A(1/X)\) represents the same element as \(A\) did at the original generator.
The monic reciprocal polynomial has the same circle maximum and the same measure since the original polynomial is monic with unit constant term.
Reflection on the circle also preserves the quotient energy.
Two distinct contracting radii therefore yield two expanding radii in the inverse problem.
At a reciprocal hyperbolic input, a single expanding radius would place every conjugate on two reciprocal circles.
The [degree descent](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v587.md#degree) excludes this possibility at exactly the stated strict records of degree greater than four.
The quadratic comparison uses the bounded sequences in C345 v633 and imposes no additional hypothesis on the finite sparsity bound.
