## Whole-proof finding

Equation (1), its uniform gap, and both families satisfying (2) hold as stated.
The dense family has measure between \(2\) and \(52\).
The irreducible family has unbounded degree and measure at most \(52\).
The proof establishes neither full support nor a uniform separation from one for those extracted factors.

### Dyadic resultants

Monicity gives an integral free quotient of rank \(d\).
Over a splitting field the multiplication determinant for \(B\) is \(\prod_\alpha B(\alpha)\), with every occurrence of each root counted, including when \(P\) has repeated factors.
Over \(\mathbb F_2\) the cokernel of the square matrix has dimension \(\deg\gcd(\bar P,\bar B)\), so at least that many Smith entries are even when the determinant is nonzero.
This verifies the exact calculation used from the [companion proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0513_v1.md#nonreduced-layer-capacity-proof), including its extension here to reducible \(P\), nonunits, and general \(B\).
Each plus factor at index \(i\) reduces to \((X^n-1)^{2^i}\), even for even \(n\), and every characteristic-zero factor has only roots of unity.
Thus all \(j+1\) determinants are nonzero and divisible by \(2^{r_P(n)}\), including the single determinant when \(j=0\).
Their product is at most \(2^d M(P)^{2^j n}\) in absolute value.
With \(j=\lceil1/\delta\rceil\) the logarithmic numerator is at least \(\delta d\log 2\), also at \(\delta=1\).
The hypotheses allow unbounded support even inside the positive-gap class, as \((X+3)^d\) with \(d=n\) a power of two has \(r_P(n)=d\) and all \(d+1\) coefficients nonzero.

For the alternative qualitative argument restricted to irreducible nontorsion units, the same determinant at \(n\) gives normalized log norm at least \(\delta\log 2\).
The parameter \(h=nm(P)/d\) is at most \(Cm(P)\), so the vanishing envelope in the [accepted cyclic norm theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#statement) excludes \(m(P)\to0\).
Its [existing review](Yin_R_G0381_v1.md#cyclic-loglog-whole-independent-finding) covers the original-field norm, powered-field multiplicities and the small-\(h\) domain.
The present inspection uses that accepted theorem only for this comparison.
The [bounded-residue-degree comparison](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0205_v1.md#bounded-residue-mass-near-one-escape) counts the full multiplicity of bounded-degree factors, which differs from the gcd at one specified exponent.

### Reciprocal reduction and the integral lifts

The degree-\(q\) trinomial and its reciprocal have constant and leading coefficient one, also when \(q=2\).
Their product, the central monomial \(X^q\), and the coefficients of \(XD D^*\) are symmetric about degree \(q\).
The latter summand has extreme exponents \(1\) and \(2q-1\), so neither sign changes monicity, degree or the unit constant term.
Both corrections vanish modulo two.

The \(q\) roots of \(\bar f\) are nonzero and simple, and applying \(q\)-power Frobenius twice fixes each root.
For \(c=a/b\) from distinct roots the identity \((c^q-c)b=1-c^q\) forces \(c^q\ne c\) and determines the ordered pair from \(c\).
Thus \(s\) selected roots yield \(s(s-1)\le |\mu_n|-1\le n-1\), giving the stated estimate also for \(s=0,1\) and \(n=1\).
Inversion preserves \(\mu_n\) as a set.
An overlap of the two squarefree factors contributes at most twice to the gcd, regardless of the multiplicities in \(X^n-1\).
At \(q=2\) the two factors coincide completely, so this check includes the extreme overlap.
Reduction preserves divisibility by each monic integral factor, giving the identical bound there.
For fixed \(C>0\) and all sufficiently large \(d\) the window is nonempty, and its normalized maximum is at most \(2/d+2\sqrt{C/d}\).

The [earlier translation construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0130_v2.md#translation-root-subgroup-bound) uses a different count on the same line and applies it to nonreciprocal units.
The distinct-ratio property itself is the classical quadratic Bose–Chowla Sidon property.
Indeed, for a generator \(\theta\) of \(\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times\), \(u=(\theta^q+\theta)^{-1}\) lies in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) and \(L=u(\theta+\mathbb F_q)\).
Scaling preserves ratios, and uniqueness of unordered pair products on that affine line gives uniqueness of the nonidentity ordered ratios.
The source identifies the finite-field step, for which v166 gives its own proof. [M. B. Nathanson, *The Bose-Chowla argument for Sidon sets*, §2, Theorem 1, pp. 136–137](https://www.theoryofnumbers.com/melnathanson/pdfs/nath2022-198.pdf).

### Support, Mahler measures and sign changes

The coefficients of \(D^2\) are positive in each degree from \(0\) to \(2q-2\), and \(ff^*\) has nonnegative coefficients and both endpoint coefficients one.
On the circle, conjugation gives \(z^{-q}ff^*=|f(z)|^2\) and \(z^{1-q}D D^*=|D(z)|^2\).
The plus polynomial therefore has modulus at least two everywhere, excluding all torsion and giving \(M(P_q^+)\ge2\) by the logarithmic integral.
Since \(D^*=D\), multiplication by \((X-1)^2\) changes the last term to \(2X(X^q-1)^2\).
The three coefficient norm bounds are \(36,8,8\), by convolution and the triangle inequality, independently of the sign of the central term.
Hence the product has circle supremum at most \(52\), and \(M((X-1)^2)=1\) gives the claimed upper bound on both original polynomials.
Any circle zeros of the minus polynomial have integrable logarithmic singularities. [Mahler measure and its normalization](../literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization).

At the selected \(q\)-th roots \(z\) the condition on \(\cos\theta\) excludes \(z=1\), so \(D(z)=0\) and the normalized minus value is \(3+4\cos\theta<0\).
At the following midpoint \(z^q=-1\), \(|f(z)|=1\) and \(|D(z)|=2/|z-1|\ge1\), giving a value at least one.
The normalized polynomial is real and continuous along each arc.
The number of selected grid points is \(q\arccos(3/4)/\pi+O(1)\), and their forward half-intervals have pairwise disjoint interiors.
The strict endpoint signs give that many distinct interior zeros even if some other zeros have multiplicity greater than one.

### Torsion orders and irreducible factors

For any order \(b\) represented by a root, integral divisibility by \(\Phi_b\) gives a common monic divisor of degree \(\varphi(b)\) after reducing both \(P_q^-\) and \(X^b-1\).
Even \(b\) may give repeated factors after reduction, and the gcd counts their full common multiplicity.
In \(\varphi(b)/b^{3/4}\) the prime-power factor for \(p^e\Vert b\) is \(p^{(e-1)/4}(p-1)/p^{3/4}\).
It is at least one for \(p\ge5\), and the two smaller primes together cost at worst \(2/6^{3/4}\), also a valid lower bound for \(b=1\).
Comparison with (2) bounds \(b\) uniformly in \(q\).
Consequently only finitely many distinct circle roots over the entire sequence are torsion, while the distinct circle roots on each polynomial grow in number.

The minimal polynomial \(A\) of each remaining root and its complementary factor are monic integral.
Their constant terms are units and the complementary factor has measure at least one, whence \(M(A)\le52\).
Complex conjugation sends the selected root to its inverse, so \(A^*\) and \(A\) have a common root and have the same degree.
The proportionality is \(\pm1\) since \(A(0)=\pm1\).
The negative sign would give \(A(1)=0\), impossible for an irreducible polynomial with a nontorsion root.
For each fixed degree the elementary symmetric sums bound every coefficient by \(52\binom{\deg A}{i}\), leaving finitely many integral coefficient vectors and hence finitely many roots.
Unboundedly many distinct nontorsion circle roots force unbounded degrees, and one may select a sequence of these actual factors with degrees tending to infinity.
The proof concerns the reduction at two and supplies no sequence whose measures tend to one.
