## Controlled rational reconstruction, pole evaluation and effective finiteness

I checked the use of the existing trace-pole realization, the two published arithmetic inputs at the scope of their source records, the dyadic height calculation and the comparison with the bounded-support result.
All estimates follow with the stated scope and effective constants.

Write \(N=2D\).
On a window of length at least \(2r\), the first \(2r\) coefficients determine any rational approximant of type \((r-1,r)\) uniquely as a rational function.
Indeed for two such candidates, cross multiplication gives a polynomial of degree at most \(2r-1\) with zero of order at least \(2r\), since the denominators are units in \(\mathbb Q[[X]]\).
Existence on the whole window gives consistency of the first \(r\) tail equations after normalizing the denominator constant to one.
A nonsingular minor on a spanning set of columns solves them with all other unknowns zero, including when the system has deficient rank.
The rank-zero case needs no denominator increase.
With integral entries and right side bounded by the stated \(L\), Cramer's rule bounds all numerators and their common denominator by \(r!L^r\).
The equations for the first \(r\) terms give the numerator bounds before division by a common polynomial factor.
Cancellation can be done by primitive integral division, and \(M(W)\le M(V)\) for an integral divisor \(W\) of a nonzero integral polynomial \(V\); the coefficient length of \(W\) is at most \(2^{\deg W}M(W)\).
Thus cancellation keeps the claimed logarithmic order of magnitude.
The zero series is represented by \(0/1\).
The rational function with controlled coefficients agrees with the original on the remaining window by uniqueness.

Including the original prefix below \(w\) produces integral \(U,Q\) such that \(QC-U\) is divisible by \(X^j\).
Any zero of \(Q\) is nonzero, and at it \(U=X^wP\ne0\).
The products in the two homogenizations cost at most \(\max(t,u)^{w+r}\) times a coefficient length.
The resulting \(H\) bounds both \(A\) and \(d_0K B\) as in the claim, keeping the coefficient denominator rather than suppressing it for rational models.
At the dyadic place \(u/t\) is integral, with positive valuation, and multiplication by the odd \(t^{N+w+r}\) gives the exact congruence for \(d_0KB-t^NA\).

If the two integers are equal neither can be zero, so their size is at least \(t^N\) and the height bound alone gives \(j\ll H\).
If only one term vanishes, or their valuations differ, the smaller finite valuation of the nonzero terms is at most \(H/\log2\): the factor \(t^N\) has valuation zero.
In the equal finite valuation case their ratio separates the dyadic unit \(A/(d_0KB)\).
Its absolute logarithmic Weil height is at most \(H\) by the displayed bound on its two integer representatives.
Yu's theorem in the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\) applies to the units \(t,A/(d_0KB)\), exponents \(N,1\), and the nonzero multiplicative difference.
That field contains the required dyadic cube root of unity and is unramified of residue degree two at two.

The weaker estimate absorbs \(\log(2q+1)\) into \(O(\log(3D))\) in (5) and gives \(\log(2q+1)\ll\log\log(9D)\) in the prefix and partition estimates.
