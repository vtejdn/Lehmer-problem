### Exact source domain and torsion-character obstruction

The primary Theorem5.1 concerns any Zariski-closed subset of the torus over Qbar, without an irreducibility requirement.
It removes all contained torsion subvarieties and gives a positive height infimum whenever the remaining set is nonempty.
The source's tuple height includes every normalized place and local degree, so the maximum-coordinate lower bound and sum-coordinate upper bound are correct.
The fixed fiber X_a may therefore be defined using any algebraic a, not only rational a.
Neither Galois-stability of that individual fiber nor strictness of the chosen input sequence is required by this direct application.

On a torsion translate, equal restrictions of two coordinate characters make their coordinate ratio a fixed root of unity.
An admissible-base point consequently forces the first k characters to be pairwise distinct.
Their linear independence in the Laurent coordinate ring holds with arbitrary algebraic scalar coefficients and includes the trivial character.
A zero-dimensional translate has only the trivial character and cannot contain an admissible point when k>=2.

For X_+, the output character can match at most one of the k distinct input characters.
At least one input term remains a singleton with its nonzero coefficient c_i zeta_i, so the restricted equation cannot vanish identically on that translate.
For X_0 every input character is a singleton, giving the same exclusion without an output coordinate.
For X_a the additional constant uses the trivial character; at most one input character can match it, and another singleton remains because k>=2.
Thus the asserted admissible points lie in the respective complements X_+*, X_0* and X_a* in every case.
This does not assert that arbitrary proper subsums cannot vanish at arbitrary heights.

### Nonempty graph, uniform constants and sequence limit

The polynomial sum_i c_i Q^i is nonzero since its powers are distinct and every coefficient is nonzero.
There exists an integer q>=2 outside its finite zero set.
The point z_i=q^i has pairwise nontorsion ratios and nonzero output, proving X_+* nonempty by the preceding argument.
Hence eta_+ is positive and depends only on the fixed coefficient vector and k.

When X_0* is empty, the character argument already rules out every admissible zero-output point.
Otherwise its positive infimum eta_0 excludes zero output as soon as the sum of input heights is at most eta_0/2.
The harmless assignment eta_0=1 in the empty case does not invoke a nonexistent infimum.
The displayed delta_c=min(eta_0,eta_+)/(2k) is positive, treats both cases, and includes equality at the asserted small-height threshold.
For the remaining graph point the tuple-height upper bound gives h_abs(y)>=eta_+-sum_i h_abs(z_i)>=eta_+/2=b_c.

### Rational denominator cost and full degree divergence

Write c_i=b_i/B with B positive and all b_i nonzero integers.
At each infinite place, the projective maximum for(sum_i b_i z_i:B) is bounded by C times the product of max(1,|z_i|), with C=max(B,sum_i|b_i|).
At finite places the integral coefficients and ultrametric inequality give the same estimate without an extra C factor.
The normalized infinite-place weights sum to one, and projective rescaling by B uses the product formula.
This proves precisely h_abs(y)<=log C+sum_i h_abs(z_i), retaining all denominator and finite-place costs without output integrality.
Along the sequence the right side is bounded on its eventual tail.

For every fixed algebraic a!=0, infinitely many outputs equal to a would give points of X_a* with H_k tending to zero.
That contradicts its positive infimum if it is nonempty; if empty there were no such points to begin with.
Zero output was separately excluded eventually.
There is no need for a constant uniform over a in this fixed-fiber step.

Failure of own degree tending to infinity would supply an infinite bounded-degree subsequence.
The common height upper bound on that subsequence bounds every coefficient of its primitive minimal polynomials by2^D exp(DH).

This proves the full limit of own degree, including the possibility that degrees are unbounded on some other subsequence.
Together with the strictly positive height lower limit it gives m_own(y_n)=deg(y_n)h_abs(y_n) tending to infinity.
The own measure includes the primitive leading coefficient for nonintegral outputs, and no ambient or input degree replaces the actual output degree.

### Salem specialization with varying supports

Accepted C165 gives independence modulo roots of unity for one representative from every original inverse pair, including the real pair.
The difference of two distinct effective exponent vectors is nonzero, so the ratio of the corresponding monomials is nontorsion.
Multiplying either term by any root of unity preserves this conclusion: otherwise the untwisted ratio would itself be torsion.
The torsion factors may lie outside the original normal closure; absolute height remains unchanged.

The exact C165 height is A_i m_n/d_n after combining inverse-pair exponents.
Its proof averages over the full original Galois group: at most one selected inverse pair maps to the real expanding/contracting pair in each embedding, preventing cancellation between nonzero radial logarithms.
This applies to every integer vector, with the zero vector handled directly as the term1 of height zero.
At most one zero vector occurs under pairwise distinctness; with torsion prefactors it remains one height-zero torsion term and the same ratio test applies.

The explicit maximum A_i m_n/d_n tending to zero is therefore exactly the small-input hypothesis for this consumer.
The number of algebraic root variables may grow, but the varieties in the finite-sum theorem use only the fixed k term coordinates, or k+1 including the output.
Neither an inversion-kernel estimate nor C165's all-support total-measure bound is needed here.
The result does not assume or infer that increasing original degree alone makes the term heights small.
