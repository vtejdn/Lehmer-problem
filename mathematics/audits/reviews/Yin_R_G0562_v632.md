# Independent review of the single-seed ordinary resultant

<a id="finding"></a>
## Local contributions, integral divisibility and the sharp-cost degree obstruction

On November 20, 2026, an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete [single-seed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#statement) without participating in its development.
The divisibility, the fixed-seed asymptotic, both Salem degree lower bounds and the asserted existence of sequences at sharp coefficient cost follow under the stated hypotheses.

Full degree ensures distinct nodes in each list, and equality across lists would contradict equality of the original conjugate heights since the positive exponents differ and the height is positive.
The Galois action permutes the common labels simultaneously, so each of the two products is a nonzero rational integer.
At an equal label the two simple zeros give a monic quadratic factor of the row combination, and the first derivative equation gives divisibility of the value of the second row by the node difference \(d\).
Adding the quadratic factor to the two squared-node relations gives exactly \((t^2,dt,d^2)\), by subtracting the quadratics, even in residue characteristic two.
In the resulting module of length \(3v(d)\) the row ideal is cyclic over the valuation ring since both generators of the square-zero ideal \((d,t)\) annihilate the remaining row.
That row is killed by \(d\), so the quotient has length at least \(2v(d)\).
For the affine interpolant in the claim the slope is integral, both derivative equations hold, and multiplication of its values at the two roots of the monic quadratic gives resultant of valuation \(2v(d)+v(mn)\).
Away from \(mn\) the local row quotient also has length at most this resultant valuation since the quotient by the monic quadratic row and the affine row surjects onto the two-jet row quotient after replacing the quadratic row by \(U=G-cV\) in the row ideal.
Here the example realizes the coefficient two for the single pair of labeled local contacts.

At unequal labels the two first-order equations of [v495](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v495.md#labels) use only the units, their different labels, and the graph contacts.
Their determinant has constant term \(mn(a_j-a_i)+ma_jd/w_j\), and the square of this constant annihilates the row ideal in the two-generator module of length \(4v(d)\).
If \(v(d)>v(n)+v(a_j-a_i)\), its valuation is \(v(mn(a_j-a_i))\), which bounds the loss by four times that valuation by elementary divisors.
In the complementary range the asserted lower bound is nonpositive.

The Hermite interpolation denominator \(H_i(z_i)^2\) divides the squared discriminant in the splitting ring because its fourth powers of differences occur among those of all pairs.
The component lift therefore works on any prescribed linear jet as well as on the identity.
Tensoring over the polynomial ring gives one simultaneous map whose image contains the product of the clearing factors times the full direct sum, and this containment descends to the row quotients.
The image cokernel needs at most two generators per ordered pair, giving the stated length loss even when several node differences have positive valuation at once.
After removing the common integer content, one row survives at each residue prime, and the degree of the reduced common gcd bounds the number of consecutive monomials needed to generate the row quotient modulo that prime.
The Sylvester target at the actual degrees supplies these monomials, so Nakayama's lemma and the vanishing of row combinations give a quotient of the ordinary Sylvester cokernel as in [v496](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v496.md#length).
Its length is bounded by \(v(R)\), including if a row is constant.
Monic division preserves contact under normalization, and restoration of content only increases the valuation.
The ordered unequal-label differences contribute the discriminant once before the fourth power, and there are \(e(e-1)\) factors of \(mn\) before that power.
Restriction of valuations to rational integers divides both sides by the same ramification index.

The index-overlap and archimedean parts of [v497](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#statement) admit identical seeds under the degree and disjointness hypotheses already verified.
In the subgroup exception argument an equation fixes the rational exponent ratio since the class of the second seed conjugate modulo torsion is nonzero, even if both seed labels coincide.
For \(b=v(B),d'=v(D),i=v(I_m I_n)\), the index-dependent loss is at most \(\min(4b+2d',8e^2 i)\le 8e^2\min(b+d',i)\).
The discriminant is fixed and the remaining exponent factors cost logarithmically.
The absolute diagonal norm agrees with the norm of \(a^{|m-n|}-1\) since the extra power is a unit, so the [conjugate norm estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#aggregate) supplies the claimed subtraction with an error logarithmic in the exponent difference.

For Salem data and \(p<q\), summing over rows with signs positive, negative and zero gives respectively \(((e-1)p+q)h\), \((q-p)h\) and \((e-2)qh\).
The lower coefficient after division by \((p+q)h\) is \(4e-4+2(q-p)/(p+q)\).
Hadamard's inequality and integrality of the degree sum give the first eventual bound, and the second follows when the last fraction stays bounded away from zero.
A Salem seed has full degree at every power because over its power field the exterior conjugate can only be sent to itself, by its unique modulus.
The construction of [v491](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#threshold) therefore applies to this very seed in separated comparable prime intervals, with both interval prime counts tending to infinity.
For a sequence of positive excesses decreasing to zero one can choose successive intervals past each fixed-excess threshold and retain a pair in each, while the reciprocal tangency lower bound for the same seed gives the other side of convergence.
This verifies attainability of the normalized coefficient cost; equality in either degree bound is not claimed.
