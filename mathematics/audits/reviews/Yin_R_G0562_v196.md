## Collision probability, descent and structured supports

At 2026-11-17T09:07 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v196.md#decision-iterated-sumset-gap-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the statement and proof without development discussion, author explanations or prior conclusions about this submission.
The proof establishes (1) with absolute constants for the full polynomial and proves both structured-support consequences.

For \(k\ge2\), Bertrand's postulate at \(2D\) gives the stated prime since \(4D\) is composite; the interval theorem is stated in [Erdős, §5, pp. 197–198](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1932-01.pdf#page=4).
Reduction modulo \(p\) cannot increase the support of the sum distribution, even when it identifies two elements of \(E\).
Taking the Fourier transform of the convolution of the original uniform choices therefore gives \(\sum_{t\ne0}|\phi(t)|^{2n}\ge p/D-1\).
Division by \(p-1\) gives a nonzero character with \(|\phi(t)|^{2n}\ge1/(2D)\), so its mean phase is nonzero.
Taking logarithms and using \(1-u\le-\log u\) bounds the total squared deviation from the mean direction by \(s\log(2D)/n\).
The phase at zero controls the distance of that direction from one, bounding each phase's distance from one by twice the square root of this total.
The chord inequality bounds the largest absolute residue, and doubling that bound gives the width estimate (2).
The width cannot be zero, since otherwise \(p\) divides every exponent of the normalized support of gcd one.

Distinct exponents \(e\) lift to distinct pairs \((x_e,y_e)\), since specializing the pair recovers \(e=p x_e+N y_e\).
For \(L=\log(2D)\), the multiplicity-vector bound gives \(L\ll k\log(Ak)\), uniformly in \(k\ge2\) and \(A\ge2\).
Thus (2) makes \(R<p\) for one sufficiently large \(A\), and the cyclic specialization estimate applies with \(H=p\), at most \(k\) terms, and \(\log(2p)\ll L\).
The component bound applies to an integral Laurent polynomial with \(Y\)-width \(R\) and a horizontal component that is not a torsion translate.
Its right side dominates a constant times \((\log(2+L)/L)^3\): the two numerators compared at \(R\) have bounded positive ratio, \(\log(2R)\ll L\), and \(\log(2+u)/u\) decreases.
Multiplying the specialization error by \(L^3\) costs at most a constant times \(A^{-10}k^{-4}\log^{9/2}(Ak)\), using \(n\ge(Ak)^{20}\).
Its supremum tends to zero as \(A\) increases since \(k^{-4}(\log k)^{9/2}\) is bounded and \(A^{-10}(\log A)^{9/2}\to0\).
The numerator \(\log(2+L)\) is bounded below, so the choice of \(A\) absorbs the error uniformly.

If all horizontal components are torsion translates, the cited factorization leaves an integral base factor times cyclotomic polynomials in characters.
For a horizontal character \(X^aY^j\) one has \(0<j\le R<p\), whence \(pa+Nj\ne0\).
The cyclotomic factors thus have zero specialized measure, with any multiplicities, and the top coefficient is the base factor up to a unit monomial.
On its row \(y_e=y\), the \(n\)-term sums of the new exponents are obtained from sums of \(n\) original exponents on that row, with repetitions allowed, by subtracting \(nNy\) and dividing by \(p\).
This affine map is injective, so there are at most \(D\) such sums.
Normalizing by translation and common dilation again preserves the sumset cardinality and the measure.
The positive width discards a term, so the descent terminates with the same \(n,D,p\) throughout and pays the approximation error at most once.
A single integral monomial has measure zero or at least \(\log 2\), covering descent endpoints and \(k=D=1\).

For the multivariate substitution, nonzero differences inside the two fixed finite sets \(S,nS\) give finitely many exceptional values of \(b\).
All later one-variable supports therefore have exactly the same \(k,D\); the specialization limit for logarithmic measures applies to this sequence.
Its terms are nonnegative, and a positive limit makes them eventually positive, so (1) passes to the limit without changing its right side.
The general bound \(L\ll_A k\log(2k)\) gives \(\log(2+L)/L\gg_A 1/k\) by monotonicity.
For \(D\) polynomial in \(k\), the same comparison at \(L\ll\log(2k)\) gives the stated stronger estimate, with small \(k\) absorbed into the constant.
Each progression coordinate has at most \(1+nL_j\) possible sums, whether or not different tuples represent different exponents.
For \(V=S-S\), maximal disjointness of the translates gives both \(|X|\le K\) and \(2V\subset X+V\).
Iterating gives \(nV\subset(n-1)X+V\); translating \(S\) into \(V\) and \(V\) into \(3S-2S\) bounds \(D\) by \(Kk(n+1)^K\).
In the width comparison the two copies of the fixed polynomial have disjoint support when \(j>\deg Q\), while the noncyclotomic degree is constant and widths add under multiplication.
The normalized absolute Fourier sum is at least one by its constant term, so passing to simultaneous multiples of the whole input does not remove that loss.
The review checked the stated interfaces of the fixed specialization, component-height, top-row and convergence results; it did not independently reprove their underlying analytic and arithmetic estimates.
