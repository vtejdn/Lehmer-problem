## Independent review of the radical and exceptional-seed bounds

At 2026-11-20T23:47 (UTC), a reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the whole argument of C345 v687 and recommends acceptance of the stated bounds.
The reviewer took no part in developing v687 and received its fixed proof and cited inputs without development dialogue, author explanations, exploration notes or previous findings on this Claim.
The reviewer had contributed to the separate counting theorem v685 and checked its application here at its accepted scope.

The torsion classification for \(G\) removes exactly the linear roots common to \(X^{n-1}-\epsilon\) and \(X^2-1\), each with multiplicity one.
Thus \(D=n-1-\deg U=d-2\ge4\).
At each remaining grid point \(\zeta\), direct substitution gives \(P(\zeta)=\epsilon(\zeta^2-1)/U(\zeta)\).
For the three nontrivial cofactors \(U=X^2-1,X-1,X+1\), the products have absolute value one, using respectively the constant value \(1\), \(A(-1)=1\), and \(A(1)=1\).
When \(U=1\), \(A=X^L+1\) for even \(L\) and the product has absolute value \(A(1)A(-1)=4\).
Every prime \(p>n\) is coprime to \(2L\) and permutes the grid while fixing the removed real points.
The quotient \(A(X^p)/A(X)\) is therefore integral by monic division.
The positive radical in \(K\) generates that field, has the Salem signature, and satisfies \(A(\beta)\ne0\).
Taking norms in \(A(r)=A(\beta)\,(A(X^p)/A(X))|_{X=\beta}\) proves both bounds in (3).

The argument of \(1-e^{it}/a\) has a continuous periodic branch of absolute value at most \(\arcsin(1/a)\).
The derivative of the lifted phase in the proof is bounded below by \(n-(a+1)/(a-1)>0\), with total increment \(2\pi L\).
Each grid point therefore has a unique partner, within angular distance \(2\arcsin(1/a)/L\le\pi/(aL)\).
At \(1,-1\) in the grid the argument is zero, so deleting the common real points gives precisely the claimed neutral bijection.
Because the \(p\)-th roots have mutual angular spacing \(2\pi/p\), the corresponding lift of each neutral radical conjugate is unique within \(\pi/(apL)\).
Complex conjugation preserves that choice.

The coefficient sum of \(A\) is bounded in absolute-coefficient norm by \(L\) in each case, and the angular derivative by \(L^2\).
A lift at a root of \(A\) thus costs a factor at most \(\pi L/(ap)\) twice, at two distinct conjugate embeddings.
Using (3) and \(r=\beta^p<a+1\) gives
\[
 \beta^{2p-D}\le L^{D+2}\left(\frac{\pi\beta^p}{ap}\right)^2
                 < L^{D+2},
\]
since \(\beta^p/a<4/3\) and \(p>n\ge7\).
The inequalities \(2p-D\ge D+2\) and \(p\le2p-D\) yield \(\beta<L\) and \(p\log\beta<(D+2)\log L\), respectively.
These imply the asserted seed bound in this case because \(a-1<r\) and \(D+2\le n+1\).

In the other case \(\epsilon\rho^L\) is a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity at each chosen lift.
A deleted linear root cannot occur as a lift, since its \(p\)-th power is itself.
The grid separation \(2\sin(\pi/p)\ge4/p\) loses at most \(\pi/(ap)\) on passing to \(w\), leaving a distance greater than \(2/p\).
Division by \(|U(w)|\le4\) gives \(|A(w)|>1/(2p)\) at all \(D\) neutral embeddings.
For \(\beta\ge2\) the reciprocal polynomial identity and \(|A(\beta^{-1})|\ge(1-2^{-L})/(3/2)^2>1/3\) give \((\beta/(2p))^D<36\) by the upper norm bound.
This gives \(\beta<5p\) even at \(D=4\), and \(\beta<2\) already satisfies the conclusion.
Together the two cases give \(a<1+(5p)^p\).

For \(a\le X\), the degree \(d\le N+1\) and \(\log M(\beta)=\log r/p<\log(X+1)/p\) permit the stated application of Voutier's Corollary 2.
At a fixed delay, full coefficient expansion bounds each free reciprocal coefficient by \(2^d\beta\), and their number \(d/2\) is at most \(\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor\).
The unique exterior root identifies each Salem number in the polynomial count.
For each sign, delay and prime, the formula for the seed has nonzero denominator \(r(r^{n-1}-\epsilon)\).
This gives the asserted logarithmic power after summing the \(O_n(1+\log X)\) prime choices.
Across varying delays, the bound through degree \(N+1\) has logarithm \(O(N\log Y+N+\log(NY))\) after all multiplicities, which is \(o(\log X)\) in the stated range.
The previously established necessity of \(p>n\) applies to failure of the divisor condition on the denoted range.
Voutier's estimate was used at the recorded theorem-statement depth.
