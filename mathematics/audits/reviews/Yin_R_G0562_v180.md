## Independent review of the degree bounds

At 2026-11-17T07:45 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the complete [C345 v180 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v180.md#decision-local-degree-salem-endpoints-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and review scope without development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or prior findings on v180.
The argument proves all four local estimates and the three stated lower limits.

Over the chosen finite unramified extension \(K/\mathbb Q_p\), the ring of integers is complete, its uniformizer is \(p\), and its residue field contains all the chosen residual roots.
Hensel factorization separates the full power \((X-\bar\zeta)^m\) from its coprime complement even when \(p\mid m\).
The complementary lift evaluates to a unit at \(\zeta\), so valuation one for the whole polynomial gives valuation one for the constant term of the translated degree-\(m\) factor.
Its remaining nonleading coefficients are divisible by \(p\), as its reduction is \(X^m\).
Eisenstein irreducibility over \(K\) then gives \(d\ge m\) for every rational factor sharing a root of that local factor.
This uses the usual [coprime factorization form of Hensel's lemma](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GG).

After the substitution \(Y=X^{p^w}\), a deleted linear factor centered at \(\xi=\zeta^{p^w}\) reduces to \((X-\bar\zeta)^{p^w}\).
The same identity holds for the negative center at two.
All roots of a deleted factor have finite multiplicative order, so each prescribed nontorsion root remains a root of the quotient.
Subtracting \(p^w\) per deletion thus counts the residual multiplicity with its repetitions.

For the binomial at two, \(2^{k-w}\mid L+A\) with at least one of \(A,L\) odd forces both odd.
All odd-order lifts satisfy \(\xi^{L+A}=1\).
For the plus sign, failure of valuation one is exactly \(\bar u^2+\bar u+1=0\), giving \(u\) of order three and \(u+u^{-1}=-1\).
Consequently \(B(-\xi)=0\) and \(B(\xi)/(2\xi)=2/\xi\), leaving \(2^k-2^w\) copies.
For the minus sign the exceptional class has \(u=1\) by injectivity of reduction on odd-order roots of unity.
At the two roots \(\pm\xi\), removal of \(Y^2-\xi^2\) gives quotient value \(B'(\xi)/(2\xi)\) at \(\xi\).
Since \(\xi B'(\xi)=4A-(L+A)\equiv4\pmod8\), its valuation after division is one, leaving \(2^k-2^{w+1}\) copies.
The nonexceptional classes retain \(2^k\), so the uniform bound holds for either sign.

For the binomial at three, let \(F\) be the monic integral representative of the given divisor.
If its reduction were coprime to \(X^N+\sigma\), divisibility of \(\bar F\) into \((X^a+1)(X^N+\sigma)\) would give \(d\le a\).
Thus for \(d>a\) a root of \(F\) reduces to a class with \(\zeta^N=-\sigma\), of multiplicity at least \(3^k\).
This argument allows a common root of the two displayed residual factors.
At the lifted center \(B(\xi)=3\sigma(1-u)\) has valuation one unless \(u=1\).
In that exception \(\xi B'(\xi)=\sigma(L-3A)\) has valuation one because \(9\mid L\) and \(3\nmid A\).
Deletion of \(Y-\xi\) therefore suffices and leaves at least \(3^k-3^w\) copies.

For equally spaced exponents, the same divisibility test uses the complementary factor of degree \(2b\), giving a class with \(\zeta^N=1\) when \(d>2b\).
Its multiplicity is at least \(2^k\) even at a common residual zero.
The plus sign gives the value \(-2\zeta^b\) and the full lower bound for every \(k\).
For the minus sign, \(2(\xi^{2A}-1)\) has valuation one except at \(\xi^A=1\).
There \(8\mid L\) and \(A\) is odd, both \(\pm\xi\) are roots, and \(\xi B'(\xi)=4A-L\equiv4\pmod8\).
Division by the same quadratic consequently leaves an Eisenstein factor of degree at least \(2^k-2^{w+1}\).

For both endpoints the disjoint upper and lower blocks have lengths three and three, so only their given split can be the split in the [reviewed length-six reduction](Yin_R_G0562_v174.md#length-six-salem).
The quadratic lower bound for \(d\) and the upper bound for the block width there imply \(d/r\to\infty\).
The estimate for \(Q(e^t)\) forces \(e^{rt}\to2\) in the binomial case and \(e^{rt}\to\phi\) in the equally spaced case, since \(-1/\phi\) is the other root of \(U^2-U-1\).
The associated endpoint measures are \(\log 2\) and \(\log\phi\), both invariant under the relevant power substitution.
The [reviewed degree balance](Yin_R_G0562_v176.md#degree-balance) therefore gives \(N/(rd)\to1\) after division by \(rt\), with \(\deg Q/(rd)\le2/d\).
In the binomial case replacing \(N\) by \(N+a\) adds \(1/d\) to this ratio.
Along any subsequence with \(M_{p'}/r\le C\) one has \(p^{k-w}\ge M/(Cr^2)\to\infty\), using \(p^w\le r\) and \(M/(rd)\to1\).
The local cutoffs eventually hold and the discarded multiplicities are \(o(p^k)\), giving \(M_{p'}/r=(M/(rd))(d/p^k)\ge1-o(1)\).
Every subsequence that could violate a claimed lower limit has such a bounded subsequence.
The two earlier results were used at these interfaces with their existing proof reviews.
The existence of a shrinking Salem sequence is outside the conclusion.
