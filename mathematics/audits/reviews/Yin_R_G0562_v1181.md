# Review of the three ramified degree tests for the positive pole equation

<a id="finding"></a>
## Congruences at coefficient and pole primes, the dyadic exceptional disk and simultaneous exclusions

At 2026-11-25T02:23 UTC an agent outside the development of v121 independently reviewed its [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v121.md#statement), all three proof sections and the cited fixed inputs for Yin.
Before receiving the fixed v121, this reviewer developed a separate general tetranomial argument in [v123](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v123.md#polygon).
Its odd-prime calculation at the two coefficient endpoints overlaps part of v121.
The reviewer did not take part in the construction of v121, and the present finding covers the fixed v121, including an independent derivation and finite checks of its pole-prime and dyadic calculations.
The simultaneously permitted residue classes, the pure-prime-power exclusions and the mixed-index example hold as stated.

**The three local equations.**
At a primitive nontorsion unit input the signed pole theorem supplies the unique exterior root, its reciprocal even-degree minimum, \(v>L\), and unit \(E(x)=p-qx\) at every conjugate.
A unit at a common dilation reduces to exactly such a primitive unit by powering.
The known trace-degree estimate gives (1), since \(L\log(1+1/q)<(2q+1)/q\le7/3<\log12\).
Starting from \(x^v=(px-q)/(p-qx)\), direct subtraction of \(1,x,x^{-1}\), with the appropriate powers of \(x\), gives all three numerators \(L(x-1),q(x^2-1),p(x^2-1)\) in (2).
The denominators are units.
For \(\ell\mid L\) the residue \(-1\) cannot occur because it would make \(E(x)\) vanish modulo \(\ell\).
Every permitted residue is an \(m=N/\ell^a\)-th root of unity.
Its Teichmüller lift \(\xi\) thus satisfies \(\xi^N=1\).
At odd coefficient primes the special lifts are \(1\) and, precisely when \(N\) is even, \(-1\).
For the pole prime only the simple zero at \(1\) remains.

**Odd primes and ordinary dyadic disks.**
The binomial coefficient formula \(v_\ell\binom Nk=a-v_\ell(k)\), \(1\le k\le\ell^a\), follows directly from \((1+Y)^{N-1}\equiv(1+Y)^{-1}\pmod{(\ell,Y^{\ell^a})}\).
After clearing the unit denominator, remove the factor \(Y\) at an odd simple endpoint and put \(s=1\) there, \(s=0\) elsewhere in (3).
After this removal the perturbation has constant coefficient of valuation exactly \(b\), whereas the remaining binomial constant vanishes or has valuation \(a>b\).
The binomial entries at indices \(\ell^{a-b+1+j}-s\) have successive valuations \(b-1-j\).
Earlier coefficients have strictly greater valuation than each new endpoint, so multiplication by an integral polynomial with unit constant cannot cancel it or create a lower intermediate point.
Each remaining degree above the last endpoint has a nonnegative coefficient valuation.
This gives the claimed negative slopes and widths.
On an ordinary dyadic disk a repeated slope at the first two widths has denominator \(A\) even when the two edges coalesce.
At each odd exceptional disk the first slope accounts for exactly \(A-1\) roots with that valuation.
The extension is initially unramified, so a root with valuation \(1/(A-1)\) has local degree divisible by \(A-1\); hence these \(A-1\) roots cannot split further there.
All remaining widths have valuation denominators divisible by \(A\).
Summing the factor degrees of the separable minimum even after unramified base change gives the table, with at most one first factor per actual sign disk.

**The dyadic endpoint disk.**
Eligibility gives \(a\ge b+2\), so \(N\) is even and \(v\) is odd.
Both \(x=1,-1\) are exact roots of the cleared equation in the one residue disk and neither belongs to the nontorsion minimum.
Divide by \(h=x^2-1=Y(Y+2)\), with \(Y=x-1\), and put \(N'=N/2\).
The perturbation left after this division is a constant of valuation \(b\), and \(v_2(N')=a-1>b\).
For \(0<t<1\) the initial term of \(h\) at weight \(t\) is uniquely \(Y^2\).
The initial monomials of its powers have distinct \(Y\)-degrees, so multiplication by the denominator with unit constant and substitution in the binomial polynomial preserve the lower edges, with horizontal lengths doubled.
For \(N'\) and the divided binomial in \(h\) the first length is \(2^{a-b}-1\); doubling gives \(A-2\).
Its drop is one and the later widths are multiples of \(A\).
For \(v_2(Y)\ge1\) one has \(v_2(h)\ge2\), and for every \(k\ge2\)
\[
   v_2\!\left(\binom{N'}k h^{k-1}\right)
        \ge (a-1)-v_2(k)+2(k-1)>a-1 .
\]
The divided binomial has valuation \(a-1\), also at \(h=0\) by evaluation, so it cannot cancel the perturbation.
The sole first factor in that disk has degree \(A-2\); all other degrees are multiples of \(A\).
This proves both alternatives at two and \(D\ge A-2\).

For a separate exact check the reviewer translated the original integer polynomial and constructed lower convex hulls from its actual coefficient valuations.
For \(q=8,v=33,\ell=2\), division by \(X^2-1\) and translation at \(1\) gave three negative edges of widths \(6,8,16\), each of drop one.
For \(q=3,v=95,\ell=2\), translation at a primitive cube root using integer pairs in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_3]\) gave one edge of width \(32\) and drop two in that ordinary disk, hence reduced denominator \(16\).
The valuations were the minima of the two coefficient valuations because \(X^2+X+1\) is irreducible modulo two.
These computations distinguish the exceptional branch from the coincident ordinary slopes.

**Archimedean and cross-prime exclusions.**
If \(N=v=\ell^a\) and \(\ell\mid L\), then \(v>L\ge\ell^b\) gives \(a>b\).
Here \(D\ge A-1\ge3v/L-1>2v/L\).
If \(N=v\pm1=\ell^a\) at the corresponding coefficient \(c\), one has \(N>2c\) from \(v>L\), so the test remains eligible even for \(\ell=2\).
Its lower bound is at least \(2N/c-2\).
At \(v=L\) this equals \(2v/L=2\) in both rows, and its slope \(2/c\) exceeds \(2/L\).
Thus all three pure-power cases contradict (1) throughout their range.

For \(q=3,v=784\), \((4/3)^8<12\) gives \(D<196\).
Exact translation at \(1\) after division by \(X-1\) gave first widths \(48\) at the pole prime seven and \(26\) at the coefficient prime three, each with drop one.
The permitted positive even residues below \(196\) from \(7^2\) are \(48,98,146\).
Their residues modulo 27 are \(21,17,11\), none of them \(0\) or \(26\), so all are excluded without either tested exponent being a prime power.
Finally for \(v=q(q+1)(1+(2q+1)j)\) all three gcds in the inertia test are one, leaving a necessary full symmetric label action and no new existence assertion.
