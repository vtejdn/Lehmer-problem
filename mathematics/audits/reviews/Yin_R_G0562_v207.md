## Power congruences and irreducibility for the blocks 2 and 3

At 2026-11-17T10:25 UTC an agent outside the development of v207 reviewed the [fixed submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v207.md#decision-prime-block-index-sieve-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed arguments and their dependencies, including v206, without development dialogue, author explanations, exploration notes or prior findings about v207.
The restrictions on every prime divisor of \(r\) and simultaneous irreducibility for the blocks 2 and 3 follow.

For monic polynomials of a fixed degree the coefficient functions of the root-power transform are integral by the fundamental theorem on symmetric polynomials.
In the universal coefficient ring in characteristic \(q\), the substitution \(X=Y^q\) identifies the transformed coefficients with the \(q\)-th powers of the original coefficients by Frobenius.
All their first partial derivatives vanish modulo \(q\).
Taylor expansion therefore lifts an input congruence modulo \(q\) to an output congruence modulo \(q^2\).
For an in-field radical of \(\beta\), equality of fields gives \(\Phi_q(T)=S\) with multiplicity one.
Frobenius over \(\mathbb F_q\) gives \(T\equiv S\bmod q\), whence \(\Phi_q(S)\equiv S\bmod q^2\).
Multiplicativity gives (2) without division by \(U\) in the residue ring.

By v202 an admissible odd \(q\) is \(O(1+\log^3(2N))\), using \(B\le\log(2\prod_i(1+p_i))\) and \(d\le2N\).
The same bound includes \(q=2\), and \(D\le(N/C)^{1/9}\) makes \(N>12qD\) uniform on enlarging \(C\).
The product at the \(q\) rotations has overall sign \((-1)^{(q-1)(N+D)}\).
For each number \(j\) of high terms the sum is fixed by rotation and Galois action, so its coefficients are rational algebraic integers and all its \(Y\)-exponents are divisible by \(q\).
After multiplication by \(U\), its \(X\)-exponents lie in \([jN/q,jN/q+11D]\).
These intervals are pairwise disjoint, and only the two end intervals can meet the support of \(P_N\Phi_q(U)\).
The highest group comes from the monic polynomial \(Y^NA\) alone and is \(X^N\Phi_q(A)\), with the same sign convention.
This gives (3) at that endpoint.

At zero, \(U(0)\) and \(\Phi_q(U)(0)\) are units in \(\mathbb Z\).
If \(q\mid M\), the two sides of (3) have respective \(q\)-adic valuations \(q\) and one, which are incompatible modulo \(q^2\), including \(q=2\).
For odd \(q\nmid M\) their unit signs agree, and cancellation gives \(M^{q-1}\equiv1\bmod q^2\).

For each of the interior groups its product by \(U\) vanishes modulo \(q^2\).
The monic leading term of \(U\) permits cancellation over \(\mathbb Z/q^2\mathbb Z\).
The product of the rotated low terms has constant term \(\pm1\), so formal power series division shows that the corresponding elementary symmetric functions of the rotations of \(R\) vanish modulo \(q^2\).
Newton's identities give the same divisibility for each power sum of order \(1,\ldots,q-1\), with no division by its order.
The coefficient at \(Y^{qm}\) in the \(j\)-th power sum is \(q[Y^{qm}]R^j\).
Consequently every such coefficient of \(R^j\) is zero modulo \(q\).

In the rational decomposition of \(\bar R^j\) on the basis \(1,Y,\ldots,Y^{q-1}\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_q(Y^q)\), the component of index zero must vanish by its Laurent expansion at zero.
After dividing by \(Y\), each remaining term \(Y^{i-1}f_i(Y^q)\) is a rational derivative, with primitive \(Y^if_i(Y^q)/i\).
At a point \(a\ne0,\infty\) where \(\bar R\) has signed order \(s\) prime to \(q\), choose \(1\le j<q\) with \(js\equiv-1\bmod q\).
The Laurent series of \(\bar R^j/Y\) at \(a\) has a nonzero coefficient of degree \(js\), but differentiation of a Laurent series kills every coefficient that could contribute to that degree.
This proves multiplicity divisibility for zeros and poles, including after cancellation of common block roots.
Removing all blocks with \(q\mid b_i\) changes each signed order by a multiple of \(q\).
The remaining simple blocks give a signed order in \([-k,k]\), forcing it to vanish when \(q>k\).
All remaining zeros are nonzero and \(I\) is nonempty by normalization, so the two polynomials with identical zeros and multiplicities differ by a nonzero scalar.
Comparing both endpoint coefficients gives \((\prod_{i\in I}p_i)^2\equiv1\bmod q\).

For \(M=6\) the primes 2 and 3 are excluded at zero, while the nonempty subset products give only 5 and 7 as remaining candidates.
Directly, \(6^4=1296\equiv21\bmod25\), and \(6^3\equiv20\bmod49\) gives \(6^6\equiv8\bmod49\).
Both candidates fail, so \(r=1\).
The v205 degree formula gives full degree \(h\deg S\) for the positive \(h\)-th radical, hence irreducibility and logarithmic measure \(m(S)\), and \(U(X^h)\) contributes only torsion roots.
The review checked the stated uses of the earlier factorization and degree estimates, without a new audit of their complete proofs.
