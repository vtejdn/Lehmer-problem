## The fixed-space minimum and its realization at prescribed rank

At 2026-11-24T22:37 UTC an agent outside the development of v62 independently reviewed its [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#statement) and both proof sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and review scope without the development dialogue or earlier conclusions about v62.
The constituent formula, monomial attainment and examples for every \(n\ge5\) and \(1\le t<n\) hold.

**Logarithms and rational representations.**
If \(x\in D(w)\), a positive integer multiple of its class modulo torsion belongs to the integer span of the original conjugate classes.
The same holds for every conjugate of \(x\), and thus their rational span \(V_x\) is a \(G\)-submodule of \(V\).
An automorphism of the algebraic closure fixing the splitting field of \(w\) acts trivially on \(V\) because it fixes the generating classes.
This justifies use of the finite group even for \(x\) outside that field.
Over \(\mathbb Q\), \((1+c)/2\) projects onto the \(c\)-fixed space, so \(V_x^c\) is spanned by the classes of \(|\sigma x|^2\).
On the multiplicative subgroup generated by these positive real numbers the logarithm is injective, and passing to classes modulo roots of unity is also injective there.
After clearing denominators this identifies its rational rank with the dimension of the span of \(2\log|\sigma x|\).
Repeated conjugate classes or torsion multiples do not affect that argument.
It follows that \(r(x)=\dim V_x^c\) with no faithfulness or torsion-class compression assumption.

Semisimplicity of a finite group representation in characteristic zero ensures that any nonzero \(V_x\) contains an irreducible submodule and that each constituent of \(V\) has a representative submodule.
The first inclusion gives the lower bound in (1).
For the reverse inequality a nonzero rational vector of a minimizing representative generates it under \(G\).
Clearing the denominators of that vector in the original conjugate classes realizes a multiple as an integral-exponent monomial.
Its orbit spans the chosen constituent, and the monomial is nontorsion because its class is nonzero.
Even nonnegative exponents can be obtained by adding the same integer to all exponents since the product of all conjugates is the unit norm \(\pm1\).
Kronecker's theorem gives at least one nonzero logarithm for this monomial, so all constituent fixed dimensions are positive.
When \(V\) is irreducible, \(V_x=V\) for every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\).
For reciprocal \(x\), circle conjugates contribute zero and inversion pairs positive and negative logs.
Each pair of complex conjugate exterior roots contributes only one absolute value, proving the stated upper bound on \(r(x)\).
Both real and complex Salem numbers have rank one in these conventions.

**Realization of the prescribed rank.**
Choose the three distinct primes larger than \(n\).
Merging the three prescribed squarefree finite-field patterns by coefficient congruences allows arbitrary bounded rounding within the resulting residue classes.
The irreducible reduction gives transitivity, and the two other reductions give an \((n-1)\)-cycle and a transposition by the Frobenius factorization rule.
The stabilizer of the letter fixed by the \((n-1)\)-cycle is transitive on the remaining letters, so the action is doubly transitive.
Conjugating the transposition then gives all transpositions.
This proves that the trace group is \(S_n\).

At the fixed endpoints bracketing the \(n-t\) interior targets, the comparison values are nonzero of order \(R^t\) while coefficient rounding changes them by \(O(1)\).
At the two endpoints of the \(j\)-th expanding interval all \(n\) factors have absolute values bounded below by a positive multiple of \(R\), while the total perturbation there is \(O(R^{n-1})\).
For large \(R\) the resulting \(n\) disjoint sign brackets therefore account for every root, with exactly \(t\) traces above two.
The largest trace root grows without bound as \(R\) tends to infinity.
In the totally real field generated by a trace \(s\), the discriminant \(s^2-4\) has a negative conjugate because \(t<n\).
Thus \(X^2-sX+1\) is irreducible there, giving a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(2n\).

The pair-permutation kernel acts on the formal \(n\)-dimensional rational signed permutation space by diagonal signs.
It contains complex conjugation, which is positive at the \(t\) real off-circle labels and negative at all \(n-t\) circle labels.
Equality of the kernel characters is invariant under the trace action since the kernel is normal.
Both sign types occur, so primitivity of \(S_n\) forces the classes of equal characters to be singletons.
For each label, averaging the kernel action weighted by that sign character gives the rational projection onto its coordinate line.
Every invariant subspace is therefore a sum of coordinate lines, and transitivity forces a nonzero one to be the full space.
The map taking the basis to the oriented root classes is an equivariant surjection onto \(V\), with nonzero image because the constructed unit is nontorsion.
It is an isomorphism, and its fixed dimension under complex conjugation is \(t\).
Consequently every nontorsion \(x\) in this divisible group has rank \(t\).
For \(t>k\) this excludes every reciprocal witness with at most \(k\) exterior conjugates, including the neutral-place subclass in v59.
The construction allows unbounded largest roots and gives the claimed rank realization with no small-measure conclusion.
