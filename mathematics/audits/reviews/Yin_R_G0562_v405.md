## Independent review of the finite bound and the cofactor estimate

At 2026-11-18T14:28 UTC an agent outside proof development checked the [complete C382 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v382.md#decision-double-house-submitted) for Sheng Yin.

Inequality (1) holds at every nonzero double root for arbitrary integral \(p\ge2\), and (2) and its uniform consequence hold under the stated prime and degree hypotheses.

Translation to lowest exponent zero gives the constant coefficient \(U(0)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\).
The other class has a unique representative \(1\le r<p\), independently of whether \(x^r\) actually occurs.
Laurent row coprimality excludes simultaneous zeros on the circle, so \(f=\log\max(|U|,|V|)\) is finite and continuous there.
Its Poisson extension majorizes both row logarithms by subharmonicity.
Exponentiating the analytic integral gives a zero-free \(W\) with \(W(0)=e^I\), and Cauchy's inequalities give coefficient bounds one for both normalized rows.

At an interior marked root \(a\) the division by \(W(x^p)\) preserves both vanishing jets.
On the series in the two classes, \(1-(x/r)d/dx\) gives weights \(1-jp/r\) and \(-jp/r\) for coefficients indexed by \(j\).
Thus the \(j=0\) term of the second row disappears and the constant of the first survives.
For \(q=|a|^p\le1/2\), summing \(q^j\) and \(jq^j\) gives \(|U(0)|e^{-I}\le8pq/r\).
For \(q>1/2\), the claimed right side of (1) is negative and \(I\ge\log|U(0)|\ge0\).
Reversal takes an exterior double root to the interior without changing the row integral, since it inverts the circle parameter and multiplies the rows by monomials up to exchange.
The unit-circle case follows directly from \(I\ge0\).
The jet argument uses no inverse of \(r\) modulo \(p\), so it also covers nonprimitive residue differences at composite moduli.

For the cofactor inequality \(p>4\) gives a positive coefficient on \(I\) in the row comparison.
Removing two copies of \(P\), monomials and cyclotomic factors from primitive \(F\) removes exactly \(2h\) of logarithmic measure.
Dividing by \(p\) after substituting (1) gives the displayed finite error, including \(2\log(CD)/p^2\).
Stripping row monomials preserves the circle integral and leaves coprime ordinary polynomials, so the cited identity gives \(\log B+\mathcal D=2kI\) when \(k>0\).
The identity and its resultant convention were checked against the separately reviewed fixed input.
For a unit, the positive logarithms and the absolute negative logarithms each sum to \(h\), giving \(b/h\le1\) with equality when one open side contains just one conjugate.
A span \(D<p\) would allow only two terms and hence only simple nonzero roots, so \(\log(8p)=O(\log(2+D))\) here.
The fourth-logarithmic prime window and the cap give \(\log(2+D)/(ph)\to0\) uniformly by the cited finite estimate.
These facts absorb every error after dividing (2) by \(h\), including \(4b/(ph)\), and give the asserted lower limit for Salem inputs.
This is a relative bound even when the input measure varies.
