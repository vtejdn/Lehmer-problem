For nonproportional quadratics the gcd has degree one if a common root exists, and the substitution \((X,Y)\mapsto(X^{-1},Y^{-1})\) gives \(RR^*=1\).
Subtracting the two equations after multiplying by \(A^*,B^*\) yields \(T=-cE^*R\).
The zero case forces equality of the shifted binomials and hence \(j=0,\ A=B\).
A nonzero constant \(T\) would force equal binomial degrees and coefficient products, and \(EE^*\) constant would give \(E\) a single exponent.
Two distinct two-element supports have at least two unmatched indices, so the supports would coincide and one coefficient would agree, forcing agreement of both.
Otherwise the outer term of \(T\) has exponent \(w\), so \(E\) has width \(2w\).
For \(|j|<w\) the union of the two supports is too short.
For \(j\ge w\) the width is \(a+j\), and for \(j\le-w\) it is \(b-j\), including equality at either endpoint.

Both displacements are multiples of \(g=\gcd(a,b)\).
The gcd descends to \(\mathbb Q(Z)\) by polynomial division, where \(Z=X^g\).
In a reduced numerator and denominator for \(R\), reversal identifies the denominator with the reversed numerator up to a Laurent unit.
The numerator divides both end binomials.
Any common zero of those binomials is determined by its two rational powers of coprime exponents and is therefore unique and rational.
The binomial zeros are simple, and a zero at \(1\) or \(-1\) would violate coprimality with the denominator.
This gives the claimed signed monomial or the expression (5) with a single simple zero \(z\) away from those two points.

In (6) both coefficient strings are nonzero throughout their intervals because \(z\ne0,\pm1\).
Every unmatched exponent must be \(e\).
Reversal maps \(Y\) to \(X^jY\) and interchanges the two equations, so one can take \(p\ge q\).

For \(p=2\), the two overlaps in the left and right deletion cases force \(z^2=1/2\) and \(z^2=2\), respectively, both impossible over \(\mathbb Q\).
For \(p=3\), comparison of the two indicated coefficients forces \(B_1/A_1\) to take both values \(-1,1\) or both values \(z,-z\), respectively.
The remaining possibility is \(p=q=1\).
Equal supports then give \(e=-1,k=1\) or \(e=0,k=-1\).
