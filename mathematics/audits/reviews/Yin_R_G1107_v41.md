## All-cut positive-sum theorem

**Finding:** the full statement and proof of C881 v10 are valid under the stated simultaneous hypotheses.
No mathematical gap was found.
The conclusion is a conditional theorem for every allowed cut, without an integer existence assertion.

The recurrence makes each \(B_n\) monic of degree \(n\), with exactly the distinct ordered roots \(b_1>\cdots>b_n\) in \((-2,2)\).
The polynomial \(Q=tB_n+C\) is monic of degree \(n+1\), and its lift is monic, reciprocal, integral, of degree \(2n+2\), and has constant coefficient one.
Since \(\deg C=n-1\) and its leading coefficient is two, polynomial division and the expansion at infinity give \(Q/B_n=t+\sum_jr_j/(t-b_j)\) and \(\sum_jr_j=2\).
The v3 remainder identity \(C=\sum_i a_iB_{n-i-1}\) is triangular over the integers and identifies its leading coefficient with \(a_0\).
Its last basis remainder also supplies the inverse congruence, so the stated original-coordinate identification introduces no additional integrality premise.

For \(s=n-\kappa\), the pivot \(c=b_s\) lies at the sign boundary.
On \(t>b_1>0\), both \(t\) and \(2/(t-c)\) are positive.
The bracket \((t-b_j)^{-1}-(t-c)^{-1}\) has the sign required to make its product with \(r_j\) nonnegative on each block, with strict positivity on the negative block.
No zero of \(B_n\) lies in this range.
This verifies the upper-root exclusion without a restriction on either block length.

The strict endpoint and the negative last residue give opposite signs at the two ends of \((-2,b_n)\), so at least one trace root lies there.
For a primitive angle representing any torsion trace in this interval, the strict inequality is \(0<m-2a<m/p\).
It forces \(m>p\), and cyclotomic irreducibility gives \(\Phi_m\mid P\), hence the conjugate trace \(2\cos(2\pi/m)>b_1\) is a root of \(Q\).
This contradicts the upper-root exclusion.
The argument applies to every root in the endpoint interval, and uses no primeness assumption on \(p\).
The accepted measure-one characterization applies because the lift is monic integral with nonzero constant coefficient one; its conclusion would make all roots torsion, contrary to this endpoint root.
Orders one, two, \(p\), and every order larger than \(p\) are excluded respectively by the upper interval, the strict endpoint, the nonzero residue at \(b_1\), and primitive-root conjugacy.
Thus the exact surviving cyclotomic range is the claimed necessary range \(3\le m<p\).

There are \((s-1)+(\kappa-1)=n-2\) same-sign pole gaps, including zero gaps in either block when that block has length one.
Removing one root from each such gap and one from the endpoint interval removes exactly \(n-1\) distinct real roots and preserves all other multiplicities in a real monic quadratic.
If its two roots are real, the upper-root exclusion and nonzero residues put both below \(b_1\), while the endpoint product sign forces both below \(-2\) or both above \(-2\).
The latter possibility would put every trace root in \((-2,2)\), contradicting the already established strict measure.

The right-endpoint matching pairs the positive-block gaps with \(b_1,\ldots,b_{s-1}\), the negative-block gaps with \(b_{s+1},\ldots,b_{n-1}\), and the endpoint interval with \(b_n\).
They omit exactly \(b_s\), and each selected root is strictly smaller than its matched pole.
The degree bound on \(C\) makes the sum of all trace roots equal the sum of all grid poles.
Consequently the residual sum is \(b_s+\sum(b-u)>b_s>-2\), which is incompatible with both residual roots being below \(-2\).
The residual roots are therefore nonreal conjugates.
Each associated reciprocal quadratic has one exterior and one interior root, and no unit-circle root; conjugation identifies the two exterior moduli.
This proves exactly two nonreal conjugate exterior roots, two interior roots, \(2n-2\) unit-circle roots, and the complete measure \(M(P)=\rho^2>1\), with multiplicity retained throughout.
The argument covers \(n=2\) and both extreme allowed cut sizes.

For a proposed product \(H(z)F(\varepsilon z^k)\), the sole exterior root of the Salem factor yields exactly \(k\) exterior roots after substitution, while \(H\) contributes none.
The count forces \(k=2\).
For positive substitution sign, the positive exterior square root has trace above two and is excluded.
For negative substitution sign, a nontorsion endpoint lift cannot be a root of \(H\), so it is a root of \(F(-z^2)\).
Its negative is then also a root of the whole product and has trace greater than \(b_1\).
This verifies both signs using the actual product, without cancellation or a statement about an extracted factor.

The v9 upper-bound hypotheses hold with shift zero, the same arbitrary cut, and the same endpoint.
I checked its relevant derivation directly: prefix agreement determines the formal logarithm coefficients, reciprocity converts inverse powers to the full power sums, and the unit-modulus numerator and denominator roots give \(|s_j(P)|\le H_0\).
The range \(j\le L\le n+1<p\) makes the grid sum exactly minus one.
The disjoint matching costs total variation \(4j\), with one unpaired Chebyshev value of magnitude at most two, yielding \(4j+H_0+3\).
For a nonreal residual pair, one of the frequencies \(q,2q\) has cosine magnitude at least one half and gives the claimed bound \((8q+H_0+3)^{2/q}\) for the complete measure.
The v9 real-residual branch also checks: at the even frequency, the other real trace contributes at least minus two, giving the intermediate constant \(8q+H_0+5\) and its stated weakening to the common bound.
Constants in \(U,V\), cancellation between them, repeated roots, and \(L=2\) cause no exception, because degrees count all specified multiplicities and \(q\ge1\).
The condition \(\log(L+H_0)=o(L)\) makes the logarithmic upper bound tend to zero, while v10 supplies strict positivity independently of the reference.
This establishes the conditional obstruction to a uniform positive Mahler gap if an actual integer sequence exists.

The accepted Bell input supplies exactly primitive-root minimal-polynomial conjugacy, and the Smyth input supplies the complete integral measure-one characterization with products and multiplicities.
Their recorded source statements and the local applicability are sufficient for these uses; this finding does not enlarge the original source-proof inspection.
The historical v8 proof and v9's separate single-cut positivity subcase are not logical premises of the all-cut proof checked here.

## Parent localization and complete fixed-parent exclusion

**Finding:** all statements and arguments in the final L457 v5 are valid at their stated scope.
This includes the rejection of \(A=\Phi_4\Phi_{60}\Phi_{84}\) for every allowed odd \(r>43\) and every strict terminal cut.
No mathematical gap was found.

The v4 family has an integral reciprocal trace representation and the exact parameter relation \(p=r+2d-2\).
The coefficient profile \(A=1+3z^2+O(z^4)\), together with the permitted central index, gives the first coefficients \(1,1,4,4\) of the perturbed lift.
Comparison of the top two trace coefficients with \(tB_n\) makes the quotient exactly \(t\) and gives remainder degree \(n-1\) and leading coefficient two.
Thus the all-cut theorem applies whenever the signs and endpoint actually hold.

For a parent order \(q>p\), its primitive trace \(\beta\) lies strictly between \(b_1\) and two, and \(Q(\beta)=-1\).
All roots of \(A\) come in nonreal conjugate pairs, so \(A(1)\) is a positive integer.
Hence \(Q(2)=rA(1)-1>0\), and the intermediate value theorem contradicts the all-cut upper-root exclusion.
This proves \(q\le p\) for every parent order; it does not exclude \(q=p\), since perturbation need not retain a parent root.

I checked the last-pole dependency including its zero case.
When \(n\) is even, a coincident parent trace gives positive last residue immediately; otherwise a negative last residue requires \(W_A(\theta_n)<-1\).
The signs of \(C_A(b_n)\) and the sine factor make the sign of \(W_A(\theta_n)\) equal to \((-1)^e\), where \(e\) counts parent trace roots in \((-2,b_n)\).
It follows that \(e\) is odd and positive.
Each such primitive angle forces an order \(q>p\), contradicting localization.
Therefore an admissible input must have odd \(n\), so \(N\) is even, and the strict endpoint is precisely \(A(-1)-1>0\).
These deductions use every terminal cut only through its required negative last residue, so they are uniform in the cut length.

For the specified parent, the degrees sum to \(42\), so \(d=21\) and \(d-1=20\).
The low coefficients of all three factors and the value \(A(-1)=2\) check exactly; the identities \(\Phi_{60}(z)=\Phi_{15}(-z^2)\) and \(\Phi_{84}(z)=\Phi_{21}(-z^2)\) give the two nontrivial factors' asserted values and coefficients.
Every allowed odd \(r\) gives \(p=r+40\ge85\), greater than all three parent orders.

On \(1/40<u<1/20\), the only parent primitive trace angles already crossed are \(1/84\) and \(1/60\).
The next admissible primitive numerators give \(5/84\), \(7/60\), and \(1/4\), all beyond the upper endpoint.
The trace factor is positive and \(\sin(40\pi u)\) is negative, hence \(W_A<0\).
An interval of length \(p/40>2\) contains an odd integer \(j\), and the positive sine prefactor in the exact residue formula then gives \(\rho_j<0\).

On \(3/40<u<1/10\), the additional crossed trace angle is \(5/84\), with the next angles \(11/84\), \(7/60\), and \(1/4\) all beyond the upper endpoint.
The trace factor and \(\sin(40\pi u)\) are both negative, hence \(W_A>0\).
This interval has the same scaled length and contains an even integer \(\ell\), giving \(\rho_\ell>0\).
The two open intervals enforce \(1\le j<\ell<n\); their strict endpoints avoid both phase zeros and trace roots.
For any initial-positive, terminal-negative strict sign pattern, the negative residue at \(j\) puts that index in the terminal block and forces the later index \(\ell\) into the same negative block, a contradiction.
Thus the proof covers all allowed odd \(r\) and every cut, including the smallest conductor \(p=85\), without a finite scan or an amplitude estimate.

The derivative normalization and parity thresholds used here were checked directly from v4's sine formula: odd indices have threshold one, even indices have threshold minus one, and the prefactor is positive for all grid indices from one through \(n\).
No nonnegative-square residue formula from another construction is used.
The fixed-parent resultant calculation elsewhere in v4 is unnecessary for these findings and was not audited in this review.
The conclusions remain necessary restrictions on other moving parents; they do not give their existence or nonexistence, a whole-measure bound outside these chambers, or a general Mahler gap.
