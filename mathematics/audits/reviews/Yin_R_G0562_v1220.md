# Review of the all-place exterior-power bound and the normality obstruction

## The matrix bound holds for the specified local scalings, and the high-degree obstruction holds over the half field

At 2026-11-25T05:08 UTC a reviewer outside the development checked the fixed [C0901 v160](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v160.md#statement) for Yin, including all five sections and their direct project inputs.
The initial review used the fixed text and its mathematical dependencies without author explanations, development notes or a previous verdict on this argument.
Both the general matrix inequality and the asserted endpoint applications follow in the stated ranges.

For each exterior order, the product of the two complementary entries of the compound matrices of a matrix and its inverse is independent of diagonal similarities.
Their sum on a fixed row is one.
At infinity the Euclidean row and column norms are bounded by the largest compound norms of the two inverse matrices, which coincide since the singular values have product one.
At a finite place the equality of largest norms follows instead from complementary minors and the unit determinant.
Isolating one summand and applying the triangle inequality to the rest gives the upper bound \(T_v^4/4\) for the modulus of \(B(1-B)\) at an archimedean place.
Both factors have modulus at most \(T_v^2\) at each finite place.
Since the archimedean weights add to one, the strict trial bound \(\mathcal T<\sqrt2\) indeed forces \(B(1-B)=0\) by the product formula.
At order one the column identity follows from the opposite matrix product, so the zeros and ones specify a permutation.
For a fixed row set \(I\) the principal minor with the diagonal indeterminates is a single squarefree monomial.
Its derivative at the identity is the trace of the pertinent rank-one perturbation on \(I\), recovering precisely the image set \(\pi(I)\).
Thus the global minor used for any subset of matching edges is nonzero.

The finite product over places of the local scaling ratios is well defined because almost all scalings are trivial.
For a nonmatching edge the inverse entry with transposed indices vanishes.
The corresponding entry of the adjoint-minus-inverse has modulus equal to that of the nonmatching matrix entry, giving the singular-value difference bound in the text.
The scaled entry stays nonzero at every infinite place, so Jensen's inequality for \(\log(2\sinh t)\) is used only at positive \(t\).
After multiplication by the finite factors its bound is \(\mathcal T-\mathcal T/T_\infty^2\le\mathcal T-1/\mathcal T\).
Strong connectivity closes the edge along a simple directed cycle by taking a shortest path back to its starting vertex, including the possibility of a loop.
The initial vertices of its matching edges form one subset to which the global minor estimate applies, including the empty subset.
Every other edge ratio on the cycle is at most the same strictly subunit bound, and there is at least one.
Multiplying the ratios around the cycle proves the contradiction with strict inequality and hence the non-strict lower bound claimed.

For an irreducible half, proper block triangularity would factor the relative characteristic polynomial.
Single-cycle monomial support instead gives a binomial with unit-modulus marked constant, incompatible with the exterior eigenvalue.
The half-field norm supplies simple disjoint root sets, the reciprocal half, two split real embeddings and neutral roots at the nonsplit embeddings.
The exterior-modulus products at the two split places are both \(R\), the second equality using the unit absolute value of the product in the first half.
All finite spectral factors are one by integrality of the roots and their inverses.
Thus the infinite spectral product has the normalization \(R^{1/u}\).
Normality gives equality with the strongest compound norm locally, and integral matrices with identity finite scalings have finite product one.
This yields \(R^2\ge2^u\) for the integral normal-model case.
Simultaneous archimedean approximation of the local normalizing similarities by one matrix over \(K\) is possible because the rational coefficient lattice with rational denominators is dense in its Minkowski real vector space.
Simple spectrum allows the local real rotation-dilation form at real places and a diagonal form at complex places.
Taking the spectral lower bound before approximation and the all-place bound along an approximating sequence gives exactly the asserted infimum and finite lower limit.

For the final obstruction the unique strict largest conjugate makes all torsion-quotient classes singletons by Galois transport.
Reciprocity then makes the canonical multiplicative labels precisely the actual inverse pairs.
The disjoint half root sets pair across the two extensions of each embedding of the totally real coefficient base, so their unions project to a genuine partition into blocks of labels.
The field generated by the coefficients of the chosen half equals \(K\), since otherwise two coefficient embeddings would have the very same root set.
The canonical partition refines this partition because any original block contains a minimal nontrivial block, and translating it by the original block stabilizer covers that block.
The accepted canonical action bounds the number of original blocks by a power of the logarithm of the rational root degree.
If every marked root were real, inversion would give at least the fraction \(1/u\) real among rational conjugates, exceeding the upper bound from the bounded total measure and the real-place estimate at large degree.
Hence complex conjugation moves an inverse-pair label, as every marked root is off the unit circle.
The image of its normal closure contains the unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of the canonical permutation image.
A lift there of a supported three-cycle preserves the original trace block; its fourth power restores the chosen half and still acts by that three-cycle.
It fixes the half field by the coefficient observation and acts on roots of the half exactly as on their labels.

Under the local diagonal normality hypothesis, column scalings and diagonal coordinate changes cancel in each eigenvector minor times its inverse minor.
At each embedding one may calculate this quantity in a unitary eigenbasis, so all its conjugates are real and nonnegative and every conjugate of complex conjugation fixes it in the splitting field.
Field-fixing automorphisms transport the spectral indices without changing the coordinate indices.
This gives equality of the order-one weights over a canonical block and equality of the order-two weights on its unordered pairs.
For order one, a zero would propagate to every spectral index by transitivity over the half field, contradicting the unit row sum.
The independent columns on a canonical block give a nonzero minor on a pair of rows, and then every two-column minor on that block for those rows has the same positive modulus.
Dividing the two row entries at each column by the entry of the second row gives planar points with constant positive mutual distance, because the denominator moduli are constant and nonzero.
After one point is subtracted the positive definite Gram matrix has rank the block size minus one.
The plane bounds that rank by two, inconsistent with the canonical block size at large degree.
No integrality of the representing matrix was used in this last step.
The eigenvalue measure of arbitrary endpoints remains outside the lower bound on the all-place matrix product in the absence of further control of its excess over the spectral product.
