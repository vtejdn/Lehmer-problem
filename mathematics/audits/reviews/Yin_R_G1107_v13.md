### Norm costs and the general signature argument

For the sum of two full-support squares, multiplying the weighted arithmetic-geometric-mean inequality cancels both normalized geometric-mean products.
This gives the sum of the two geometric means as a lower bound for the geometric mean of the sum.
Each individual resultant is a nonzero integer, so the displayed lower bound two is valid.
The argument requires both summands to be nonzero on every node and a nonvanishing integral base.
The text retains the distinction between this hypothesis and a sum with zero components.

For the specified top-layer patch, \(B=B_0F_m\) and \(F_m=1+T_{3^{m-1}}\).
Its value at every lower node is three, giving \(|\operatorname{Res}(F_m,B_0)|=3^{n_0}\).
The irreducibility of the top layer makes a nonzero residue class of \(v\) nonvanishing at all of its conjugate nodes.
Multiplying \(1+B_0(b)^2v(b)^2\ge B_0(b)^2v(b)^2\) over the top layer proves the norm bound \(3^{2n_0}\), and \(2n_0/n\to2/3\).
If \(F_m\) divides \(v\), the patch is the identity on both parts of the grid.
These calculations exclude only the two stated constructions and do not prove a lower bound for arbitrary integral gluing.

I checked the application of the full-order unit-signature criterion and the positive-unit square theorem.
The sign of a signed layer norm is exactly the parity of the negative values on that layer.
A rectifier therefore exists precisely when those layer parities agree.
The quotient of two rectifiers is a totally positive unit and hence an integer unit square.
For the latter theorem, the logarithmic unit image is discrete of rank at most \(n-m\), and its kernel is exactly the global signs: the layer congruences modulo three force the component signs to agree.
The already established signature image has \(2^{n-m+1}\) elements, which exhausts the possible unit square classes.
This checks the assertion in the original order, without replacing it by the product of its field orders.

The signed-unit columns used by the finite program also justify the stated deterministic construction at general conductors.
From the full cosine-sign basis, replace each non-pure-power column \(v_a\) by \(v_a+v_{3^{v_3(a)}}\), retaining the pure-power columns.
This invertible column change leaves \(n-m\) independent quotient columns, each with zero parity on every layer.
They span the kernel of all \(m\) layer-parity maps.
Adding the constant unit \(-1\), whose parity is one on every odd-degree layer, gives exactly the \(n-m+1\)-dimensional unit-signature image.
The corresponding quotients are integral units by the odd trace quotient construction and their layer norms.
Thus the general paired-supply assertion does not extrapolate from the observed rank 37 at conductor 81.

Multiplication by a coprime Galois index permutes each primitive layer, so all \(V_k\) have the same signed norms \(A_i\).
The conflicting signs of \(A_1\) and \(A_6\) obstruct every single-filter rectifier when \(m\ge6\), independently of subsequent signs.
A pair has signed norms \(A_i^2>0\) on every layer, giving an integral positive rectification for every conductor.
The diagonal pair is already a square, and the text makes no assertion that distinct labels yield distinct square classes.

The full cyclic resultant identity and its fixed-root asymptotic are applied at their previously checked interface.
The seed's non-torsion unit-circle conjugates satisfy the adopted Dim16 bound, while its two exterior/interior conjugates supply the linear main term.
The resulting \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(F,z^p-1)|=p\log\mu+O(\log p)\), together with \(2n=p-1\), gives \(N_p^{1/n}\to\mu\).
Unit rectification changes no absolute norm.
The exact retained bounds \(7/6<\mu<19/16\) put \(\mu^2,\mu^4\) below the strict shrinking-shell ceiling and \(\mu^6\) above two.
These are necessary norm-budget comparisons, not whole-measure bounds.
The original proof of the external logarithmic estimate and the previously reviewed whole-measure ceiling were not re-audited.

### Square-multiple boundary and the proposed next test

The original unqualified sentence about square multiples required narrowing because the old complete energy-one and energy-two domain includes zero-component vectors.
A concrete overlap illustrates the issue.
For the single positive multiplier \(u\), its restriction to the lower conductor-27 order is a totally positive unit of norm one.
Thus \(u=a^2\bmod B_0\) for an integral \(a\) in that lower order.
Taking any integral representative of \(a\) and the top factor \(F_4\) gives
\[
                  uF_4^2=(aF_4)^2\pmod{B_0F_4}.
\]
These are nonzero elements supported on the lower layers, so the two square-multiple families need not be disjoint.

The author revised the actual L462 v7 paragraph after this scope finding.
The final paragraph says that the old exclusion does not by itself settle the entire new family, explicitly permits overlap for zero components, and proves separation for the single input's multiples nonzero on every component by
\[
             |\operatorname{Norm}(uv^2)|
                =163|\operatorname{Norm}(v)|^2.
\]
This is not an integer square when the norm of \(v\) is nonzero.
Cancellation of the componentwise nonzero base justifies comparing multipliers.
The original two supplied inputs are therefore outside the old family; their positivity and nonsquare certificates are unaffected by the clarification.
