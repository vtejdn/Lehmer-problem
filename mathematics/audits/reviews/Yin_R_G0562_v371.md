**The analytic multiplier.**
The complex-conjugate reciprocal \(A^\#\) restricts to \(\overline A\) on the circle.
Consequently the Laurent polynomial \(S\) is strictly positive there, even at a zero of either individual row, by coprimality.
For nonconstant \(S\), its symmetry forces equal positive and negative extreme orders.
The \(2q\) roots of \(z^qS\) are nonzero and pair off under reciprocal conjugation with equal multiplicities, with one exterior root in each pair.
Using exactly the exterior roots gives \(W_0 W_0^\#\) with the same divisor and pole order at zero as \(S\).
Their ratio is a positive constant on the circle.
The resulting \(W\), also for constant \(S\), admits a holomorphic \(k\)-th root \(w\) on a disk containing the closed unit disk.
The displayed boundary identity gives \(|U/w|,|V/w|\le1\).
The recorded scalar Fejér–Riesz statement matches this use over \(\mathbb C\), and the proof supplies the strictly positive case directly.

**Sylvester columns and the exact coefficient.**
There are \(\deg V\) columns from \(U\) and \(\deg U\) from \(V\), all supported in powers \(0,\ldots,N-1\).
Multiplication modulo \(X^N\) by \(1/w\) has determinant \(w(0)^{-N}\).
Its image on each column consists of the first \(N\) Taylor coefficients of the corresponding shifted bounded analytic function.
The squared norm of this truncation is at most the squared boundary \(L^2\) norm by Parseval and hence at most one.
Hadamard gives exactly \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\le|w(0)|^N\).
Since \(\log |w|\) is harmonic on a neighborhood of the closed disk, its boundary mean is its value at zero.
Replacing the sum of \(2k\)-th powers by the maximum inside that mean costs at most \(\log2/(2k)\), uniformly on the circle.
Letting \(k\) increase proves (1) for arbitrary complex coefficients, including the endpoint with one constant row.
Scaling both rows by \(\lambda\ne0\) adds \(N\log|\lambda|\) on the left and \(\log|\lambda|\) to the boundary integral, forcing the stated coefficient.

Thus \(N>0\), and (1) bounds \(I\) from below by \(B/N\) for every \(B\le\log H\).
The offset \(s-r\) is prime to \(p\), and the common shift \(x^{-r}\) preserves \(D\) and the measure.
The row comparison consequently loses \(2\log(CD)/p\), and primitivity gives \(m(R)=m(F)-eh\) even if \(P^{e+1}\) divides \(F\).

Its right side exceeds that of (2) by \(2(B/N+\log(CD))/p\), which is positive for the nonnegative arithmetic choices since \(C>1\) and \(D\ge1\).
This checks the revised comparison at its stated scope.
