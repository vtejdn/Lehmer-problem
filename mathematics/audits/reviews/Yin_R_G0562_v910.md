# Newton-support heights on dependent points of a plane curve

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T05:34 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin completed an independent review of [C345 v770](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of the complete result for plane curves irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The reviewer took no part in developing either the proof or its preceding individual-factor theorem.
The [growing-section limit](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) is used here as a separate mathematical input.
The reviewer prepared the cited Habegger source unit and checked its comparison with the new claim.

The rank-two support condition excludes a geometric component on which a nontrivial torus character is constant.
Indeed such a component would be a torus coset, and its coefficient conjugates would have the same single exponent direction, contradicting the support of their rational product up to a monomial.
The finite torsion intersection can therefore be discarded along distinct points.
For any remaining dependent pair the free quotient of the group generated by its coordinates has rank one.
A representative of its generator yields coprime exponents, unique up to simultaneous change of sign, with the remaining factors roots of unity.
At every place their absolute values are one.
The extremal exponents in the projected support therefore determine the two branches of the local projective height, and the product formula yields exactly \(w_B(p,q)h(z)\).
This calculation applies to negative exponents, singleton supports, and coordinates of absolute value one.
Since the Newton support spans the plane, its width is strictly positive for every nonzero direction.

For subsequences with primitive sup norm tending to infinity, the preceding theorem applies even when the phases have unbounded orders.
For a bounded subsequence one can pass to a constant primitive direction.
Over a finite normal coefficient field the geometric components form one conjugacy orbit.
Placing the points on one chosen component preserves their absolute heights and their primitive dependence modulo torsion.
They still eventually leave every finite set, as the union of the conjugates of a finite set of algebraic points is finite.
Lifts to the smooth projective normalization are consequently generic, including when finitely many singular points were originally present.

The character \(f=x^q y^{-p}\) is nonconstant and hence defines a finite morphism to the projective line on this normalization.
The pullback of the canonically metrized bundle has positive geometric degree and is semipositive.
Its height is \(h(f)\) and is nonnegative even at zero or infinity in the target.
The torsion values on the original generic sequence force both absolute and essential minima to be zero.
The monomial projective map defining \(h_A\) extends over the nonsingular projective curve by properness, and its metrized pullback provides an admissible test height.
The [global limit-height theorem](../literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#bs26-u001) gives one common limit for this test on every generic sequence with \(h(f)\to0\) on the chosen component.

For a primitive nonzero vector \(w=(p,q)\), choose \(w'\) with \(\det(w,w')=1\).
All vectors \(nw+w'\) are primitive and give distinct directions tending after normalization to that of \(w\).
The [untwisted torsion-fiber estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#calibration) leaves a nontorsion root for every sufficiently large such direction.
On the transported powered points the height \(h(f)\) is the absolute determinant of the two directions times \(h(z_n)\).
Here the determinant is one and \(h(z_n)=O_F(1/\|nw+w'\|_\infty)\), by the growing-section theorem and the positive minimum of the Newton width on the unit sphere.
This verifies the smallness and genericity needed for calibration, while the same growing-section theorem identifies the limiting test height with \(m(F)>0\).
The division into unbounded and bounded primitive directions covers every sequence by the subsequence criterion for convergence.

Each joint height limit belongs to the displayed locus by compactness and continuity of the width ratios, including zero widths for some of the other supports.
Conversely any irrational direction can be approximated by distinct primitive lattice directions, and the determinant construction covers rational directions and axes.
The available nontorsion roots give distinct untwisted points along distinct directions because one nontorsion pair cannot have two independent character relations modulo roots of unity.
Thus every point of the stated joint locus is attained.
For \(X+Y=1\) the width of the triangular support runs through \([1,2]\) on the sup-norm unit sphere, giving the entire interval claimed for the maximum of the two separate heights.
This agrees with [Habegger's Theorem 1 and Corollary 2](../literature/Hab09/Yin_U001.md#hab09-u001) under the standard logarithmic projective height.
Habegger's Theorem 4 supplies a rate on this particular line.
