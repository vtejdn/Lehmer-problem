The estimates, all three exact exceptional counts and the dyadic and irreducibility conclusions hold for every monic rational divisor and both signs on the same set of pairs.

For each of \(2,3,5\) the multiplicative order is the order of a fixed rank-one group satisfying the hypotheses on printed p. 207 of [Pappalardi's article](../../literature/pdf/Pap96.pdf).
In Theorem 2.1 on p. 211 take \(L=\log\log p\), \(\psi=L^{1/4}\) and \(\tau=(1-\log 2)/2>0\).
The gain above \(\sqrt p\) has logarithm at least \(\exp(\tau L-L^{3/4})\), which eventually exceeds \(5L\).
This is the unconditional theorem, whose proof is accepted at the statement level.
The three exceptional sets together remove \(o(x/\log x)\) primes from \([x,2x]\) by comparison with their count up to \(2x\).
The fixed-field Chebotarev density and the prime number theorem leave \(M\gg_h x/\log x\) split primes after this deletion, also for the rational field when \(h=1\).
Forming \(m=\lfloor M/2\rfloor\) disjoint pairs in increasing order gives total gap at most \(x\), so at least \(m/2\) pairs have gap at most \(2x/m\).
Complete splitting gives \(r,q\equiv1\bmod 2h\), and \(r\ge x\) eventually dominates every fixed multiple of \((2g+1)^9\).

In v198 the substitution \(n=r,\ u_1=u_2=g,\ W=2g+1\) gives equal integer counts \(s\) in the two disk families for each irreducible factor.
The [torsion classification and reciprocity argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#degrees) apply since \(q=r+g\) is prime, \(q\nmid g\), and \(r\) satisfies the delay bound.
Thus each constituent of \(Q\) has \(s>0\), the shares sum to \(g\), and each reduction is stable under inversion as well as Frobenius.
The unsimplified degree estimate, multiplied by \(r\), has middle term \(O_h(\sqrt x(\log x)^{5/2})\) because \(d\le r+2g\) and \(W^2+\log(2d)=O_h((\log x)^2)\).
The exponential and \(W^4\) terms satisfy the same bound.

On each simple primitive grid the two actions multiply exponents by \(\ell\) and \(-1\) modulo the endpoint prime.
Their subgroup acts freely there, giving a common orbit length \(t\ge\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)\), with \(p=q,q,r\) respectively.
Since \(t\mid N\) and \(t\mid d-\delta_\ell\), the integer \(g(d-\delta_\ell)-Ns\) is divisible by \(t\).
Its absolute value is bounded by \(g|d-rs/g|+g\delta_\ell+|r-N|s=O_h(\sqrt x(\log x)^{7/2})\), using \(\delta_\ell\le2g\) and \(s\le g\).
For sufficiently large \(x\) the chosen order at each endpoint exceeds this upper bound, forcing the integer to vanish.
The two equal grid sizes give \(\delta_2=\delta_3\), and their difference from \(r-1\) is \(g\), giving \(\delta_5=\delta_2+s\).
The equality \((d-\delta_\ell)/t=J_\ell s/g\) gives the claimed orbit-index divisibility.
Summing over constituents preserves the exact equations and integrality for every divisor, including \(1\) and \(Q\).

The parity input from v220 applies to these reciprocal divisors with \(r>6g\).
At two the exceptional residues have multiplicity \(2^v\) whenever selected, and only the residue \(1\) is fixed by inversion.
Whether it is selected is detected by \(\kappa=v_2(S(-\varepsilon))\), since \(Q(-\varepsilon)=2(r-5g)\) has valuation one.
This gives \(\delta_2/2^v\equiv\kappa\bmod 2\).
The other parity can also be read from the quadratic character of the binomial resultant ratio at five.
From the two absolute norms in v220, that character is \((-1)^{\delta_3+3\delta_2-\kappa}=(-1)^\kappa\), since both degrees are even, \(2,3\) are nonsquares, and either rational sign is a square modulo five.
The [oriented product at five](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#quartic) gives \(2^v\mid u\) and character \((-1)^{u/2^v}\) for \(u=\delta_5/2\), using odd \(r\) and odd \(g/2^v\).
Thus \(u\equiv\delta_2\bmod 2^{v+1}\), and \(s=2u-\delta_2\equiv\delta_2\bmod 2^{v+2}\).
These checks cover the parity interface of the submitted v220, without a separate verdict on its stronger signed formula modulo four.

The valuations of the integral constituent values at \(-\varepsilon\) sum to one, so precisely one constituent has odd \(s/2^v\).
The reduced denominator of \(s/g\) is odd, divides \(g\), and divides \(q-1\) since \((q-1)s/g=d-\delta_2\).
When \(\gcd(r-1,q-1)=\gcd(g,q-1)\) is a power of two, each positive share is therefore the full \(g\).
The sum of the shares permits exactly one constituent.
This last conclusion is conditional on the stated gcd for the pair.
