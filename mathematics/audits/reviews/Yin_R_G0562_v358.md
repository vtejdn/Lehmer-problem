**Degree of the power and common factors.**
For the prime input, the trace polynomial reduces to \(t^p-t-1\), whose roots have Frobenius orbit length \(p\).
A real trace in \((-2,2)\) makes the quadratic discriminant negative in one embedding, so the reciprocal polynomial has degree \(2p\) and is irreducible.
The real trace greater than two gives an exterior root, and irreducibility excludes torsion for every conjugate.
Over \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) a second root conjugate to \(\alpha\) would be \(\alpha^{-1}\), forcing \(\beta=\beta^{-1}\).
Thus \(\beta=\alpha^p\) has degree \(2p\).
Each of its \(2p\) conjugates has \(p\) distinct nontorsion preimages under the power map.
If the common row divisor vanishes at \(\beta\), then all these preimages occur in \(F\), and division by one copy of \(P_p\) costs exactly \(2p\) degrees.
The resulting \(2p^2-2p\) dominates both lower estimates and is attained for one class by the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) evaluated at \(x^p\).

**The norm and the span.**
In the other case the coprime rows still give a multiple of \(P_p\) after allowing Laurent monomials and rational scalars.
The four simple branch points of \(Y+Y^{-1}=X+X^{-1}-1\) make its function field quadratic over \(\mathbb C(X)\).
An invariant power \(Y^r\) would satisfy \(Y^{2r}=1\), contradicting the nonconstant trace for either nonzero sign of \(r\).
The quadratic norm is therefore nonzero and has all \(2p\) conjugates of \(\beta\) as roots.
Its span is at most \(2b+2s\), yielding \(b\ge p-s\).
Terms above the two extreme row indices survive because their two possible offsets are distinct modulo \(p\).
Their separation gives \(d\ge pb-s\), with \(p(p-s)-s=(p^2+1)/2\) at \(s=(p-1)/2\).

**Torsion with multiplicity.**
The Laurent polynomial \(U+YV\) is primitive in \(Y\), is linear over \(\mathbb Q(X)\), and has two nonempty rows.
A Laurent-monomial cyclotomic factor would have to be the whole polynomial up to a unit, incompatible with its nonzero diagonal having a nontorsion root.
The strip bound therefore applies with width one, diagonal \((p,r)\), and \(\gcd(p,r)=1\).
In its rank-two argument, passage to the support lattice divides the Newton area by the lattice index \(I\), while return to the original parameter multiplies the torsion count by \(g\mid I\).
In the full lattice the sign translates are distinct and none divides the squared transform, since all four would divide it and their total width exceeds its width.
For the three order classes the maps \(-t,t^2,-t^2\) preserve the multiplicity at the relevant conjugate.
Restriction to the primitive parameter curve makes this multiplicity a lower bound on the local intersection length.
The recorded [mixed-area bound](../literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#intersection-multiplicities) then supplies the three constants \(2,4,4\).
Diagonal projection puts the Newton polygon inside a rectangle of area \(d\) after scaling its area by \(p\).
A rank-one noncyclotomic factor has zero torsion count under the same primitive narrow specialization.
Hence the torsion count is at most \(10d/p\) in either case.
The \(d\) nonzero roots counted by span include all multiplicities, and the discarded common factors can only increase the noncyclotomic degree.
Subtracting the distinguished \(2p\) degrees and using \(p>10\) proves both lower bounds.

**Existence and scope.**
The kernel for the two degree-\(p\) rows has dimension at least two since the target field has dimension \(2p\).
Evaluation at \(\beta\) is injective on either row alone, so a nonzero kernel vector yields both classes and an integral multiple upon clearing denominators.
The degree cap \(p^2+s\) gives the upper bound.
Translations by nonnegative powers of \(x\) realize the prescribed pairs in both directions, preserving the degree after discarding factors \(x\).
Dividing the two bounds by \(p^2\) gives the common limit one when \(s=o(p)\).
The separate Lucas bound applies to the same primitive cofactor under \(m(R)=o(p)\).
The fixed-family measure limit is positive in logarithmic scale by the integral in v306, and field equality meets the hypothesis for reciprocal unit interpolation in v354.
These comparisons make no assertion that the dimension construction bounds height or measure.
