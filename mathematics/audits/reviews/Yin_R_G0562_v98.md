For a trigonometric polynomial with finitely many integral coefficients, reduction of its support modulo any sufficiently large prime is injective, also for negative indices.
The normalized transform of the resulting integer function takes the values of the original polynomial on the grid divided by the prime, up to the inversion permutation.
Its Fourier algebra norm tends to the circle integral of the absolute value by continuity and Riemann sums.
One can therefore choose a prime large enough at once for injectivity, for the norm bound \(M+1\), and for its order to exceed the support size plus \(S(M+1)\).
On this prime cyclic group every signed coset in Sanders's representation is a singleton or the full group.
Some point outside the coefficient support is also outside all singleton cosets, so evaluating there makes the signed full-group contribution zero identically.
Summing the remaining absolute coefficients costs at most the number of singleton summands, including repetitions.
Only the bound \(S(M+1)\) passes to the conclusion, uniformly in degree and support size.

For the lowest group \([D,E]\), an active divisor \(d\mid t\), \(1\le t\le2E\), has \(t/d\le\lfloor2E/D\rfloor=K\).

The full divisor sum \(\sum_{j\mid t/d}\mu(j)\) eliminates every term except \(d=t\), leaving \(-b_t\) for the cosine coefficient, with zero mean.
The Fejér difference has multiplier one through \(E\), zero from \(2E\) onward, and norm at most three by positivity and the unit integrals of the two kernels.

Thus its output is exactly the negative of the group's cosine polynomial \(f\), since frequencies of absolute value at least \(2E\) are killed.
