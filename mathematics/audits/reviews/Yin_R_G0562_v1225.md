# Independent review of relative norms near integer targets on adjacent degree records

## Exterior potential, one-sided targets and four neighboring evaluations

At 2026-11-25T05:17 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v165 independently reviewed for Yin the fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#statement) and its three proofs.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its inputs without development discussion or previous findings on v165.
The reviewer independently audited the earlier row and relative-degree theorems v163 and v159.
The potential estimate, norm asymptotic, one-sided equivalence and the four-choice restriction follow with the shrinking hypothesis for the last restriction.

For positive Perron units with strictly smaller moduli at every other conjugate, an automorphism fixing \(A^aB\) attains maximal modulus separately in both factors.
It therefore fixes both actual numbers, giving \(\mathbb Q(A^aB)=\mathbb Q(A,B)\) for every positive integer \(a\).
The degree of the first measure is constant within the row and each embedding of the first source value field has exactly \(q\) lifts.
The \(q\) images of \(B\) are the simple zeros of its transported monic relative minimum.
The coefficients and discriminant are algebraic integers in the base field, and the discriminant is nonzero by separability.

In each fiber the scaled roots \(v_j\) have \(|v_j|\le1\).
Absolute convergence at \(0<\rho<1\) justifies expanding the kernel and interchanging the finite sums with the Taylor series.
The real part of the double sum of powers gives the squared modulus of the power sum, including its diagonal.
For distinct labels the disk inequality gives a factor \(\rho e^{-b}|y_j-y_l|\) under the negative logarithm.
The product of the ordered distinct-pair differences in absolute value is \(|\Delta_\sigma|\), accounting for the full discriminant without an extra factor two.
Each of the \(q\) diagonal terms costs at most \(-\log(1-\rho^2)\).
Dividing by \(q^2\) gives the stated coefficients \(1-1/q\), \(1/q\) and \(1/q^2\).
Taking the average over all embeddings of \(K\) makes the discriminant contribution nonpositive, since its absolute norm is a positive integer.
With \(u=\min(s/2,1/q)\) one has \(u\le1/q\) and \(1-e^{-2u}\gg u\), giving \(\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\ll\log(2q)+L\), including \(q=1\).

For the fiber potential, \(|w|\le e^{-s}\) follows from strict dominance and the radial choice of \(z\), while \(\rho\ge e^{-s/2}\).
Using the factors \(\rho^k/\sqrt{k}\) and \((|w|/\rho)^k/\sqrt{k}\) in Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the squared deviation by \(E_\sigma[-\log(1-e^{-s})]\).
The last factor is \(O(1+L)\) both near and away from zero.
All complex embeddings count with equal weight, hence the mean of the normalized fiber potential is precisely the normalized logarithm of the actual minimum evaluated at \(z\), minus \(\log|z|\).
Bounding the square of the mean by the mean square proves (2) with absolute constants and no restriction on the degree of \(K\) or the height of \(A^a\).

For the later record of source degree \(d\), v159 gives \(q\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), while Voutier's Corollary 2 gives \(b=m_{i+1}>2/(\log(3d))^3\).
Eventual decrease of \(\log(2x)/x\) therefore gives \(\log(2q)/q=o(b^N)\) for every fixed \(N>0\), also in rows with large degree jumps.
If \(L<c/b\), the square of the right side of (2), with its implied constant included, is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(b+c+o(1)\).
Indeed the relative-degree terms are bounded using \(\log(2q)/q=o(b^N)\) and \(1/q=o(b^N)\), for instance with \(N=3\).
For any fixed absolute deviation choose \(c>0\) sufficiently small relative to its square.
A deviation of that size forces \(L\ge c/b\), hence \(s\le\exp(-c/b)\), in either direction of the normalized potential error.

In the zero-gap case the first-log progression spacings \(h=m_i\) go to zero and the upper endpoints tend to infinity by v163.
Thus every sufficiently late row allows the two strictly lower exponents nearest any fixed \(t>1\).
For an integral \(t\ge2\) equality with a row value is impossible because a product of units is a unit.
For a real target the ceiling prescription handles an exact row hit as well.
The second strictly lower choice always has \(h<s\le2h\), so \(L\le\log(1/h)\) at large rows.
Since \(b\le h\), both the \(b(1+L)\) contribution and the relative-degree terms tend to zero.
This proves the convergence of \(|P_{i,a}(t)|^{1/D_i}\) in (1), with the root evaluated at the actual first-measure field.
The second log measure diverges by v159 because \(a h\to\log t>0\) and the later block size diverges.
At an integer target the absolute norm of \(t-C_{i,a}\) is \(|P_{i,a}(t)|\), and its congruence modulo \(t\) is a sign because the unit minimum has constant coefficient \(\pm1\).
Here \(D_i\to\infty\) by the same growing relative degree.
For the converse implication, finite rows give finitely many inputs.
An infinite record list with positive limit \(\ell\) gives only bounded exponents in a bounded first-log interval, so a proposed sequence from strictly below has a constant-exponent subsequence of increasing row indices.
Its limit is \((a+1)\ell\) in log scale, and each of its first logs is strictly greater than that limit.
This excludes approach from below even at the lattice points where two-sided accumulation is possible.

For the four closest choices set \(h=m_i,\ k=m_{i+1}\) as in the statement.
The first-row errors belong to \((0,h]\) and the next-row errors to \((0,k]\).
The exponent at 2 in the first row and the positive difference of the exponents at 3 and 2 there are each comparable to \(1/h\), and the second row exponent difference is comparable to \(1/k\).
Eliminating \(h,k\) from the two spacing identities and the first 2-target identity gives (3), including the terms with coefficients \(-pv\) and \(-u\).
The logarithms \(\log 2,\log(3/2)\) are rationally independent by the prime factors 2 and 3, so this linear form with \(uv>0\) is nonzero.
The degree-one two-logarithm theorem applies with fixed arguments and an integer coefficient vector of maximum modulus \(O((hk)^{-1})\).
Its primitive-vector height is no larger than the logarithm of that bound, giving the asserted absolute polynomial lower bound on the form.
Each coefficient multiplying an error on the right of (3) is also \(O((hk)^{-1})\).
Consequently the largest error is \(\gg(hk)^{C_0+1}\ge k^{2(C_0+1)}\) up to an absolute positive factor.
For the maximizing choice, \(L=O(1+\log(1/k))\).
If it lies in the second row, its later log \(b'=m_{i+2}\le k\) makes \(b'(1+L)\) negligible and the inverse relative degree is smaller than every fixed power of \(b'\).
The same argument applies to the first row with \(b'=k\).
This proves a uniform \(o(1)\) error for at least one of the four choices.
At an integer target at least two, convergence to its positive log is incompatible with absolute norm one or a fixed normalized deficit at that choice.
The conclusion constrains norms of the translates while the neighborhood boundedness in the Lehmer test remains open.
