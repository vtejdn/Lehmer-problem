## The global count and the index-three restrictions

At 2026-11-24T20:13 an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the full [exceptional-parameter count](../../claims/Yin_C_G0902_v6.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed proof and scope without development dialogue or a previous verdict on this version.
The earlier independent reviews of the [trace construction](Yin_R_G0562_v1040.md#finding) and the [relative units](Yin_R_G0562_v1062.md#finding) apply to their respective inputs.
Both restrictions on the index-three case and the asserted bound on the number of integer parameters hold.

In the completion \(B_2\), the powers of the uniformizer have distinct valuation classes modulo \(n\), proving the claimed integral basis and allowing use of the derivative formula for the different.
In characteristic two the doubling identities for the shifted Chebyshev recurrence give \(C_d'=B_h^2\) with \(h=(n-4)/2\).
An even-index \(B_j\) has nonzero constant, and the odd-index formula yields \(\operatorname{ord}_x B_h=2^{v_2(h+1)}-1\).
Consequently the first odd coefficient of \(H'/2\) has exponent \(j=2^{v_2(n-2)}-2\), including \(j=n-4\) when \(n-2\) is a power of two.
The additional term of degree \(n-1\) cannot change this order.
At \(w\), lower powers have coefficient valuation at least \(n>j\) and higher powers also have valuation greater than \(j\).
This gives different exponent \(n+j\).
If \([K:F]=3\), the restriction of \(\iota\) is nontrivial on \(F=\mathbb Q(u)\), with fixed field \(E=\mathbb Q(u+u^{-1})\).
The tower degrees give \([B:E]=3\), and total ramification of \(B_2/\mathbb Q_2\) gives the same relative degree locally.
The tame cubic upper step contributes different exponent two, making \(n+j\equiv2\bmod3\).
Together with \(3\mid n\) this forces \(v_2(n-2)\) even.

The field \(F\) has the two real embeddings determined by \(u,u^{-1}\), with unit-circle values at the others.
Exactly one real embedding of \(K\) extends each of these real embeddings of \(F\).
At the distinguished one, the remaining two extensions cannot be neutral because the common value of \(u\) has modulus different from one.
They form a conjugate pair, so the values of \(T\) must be \(\zeta^s,\bar\zeta^s\) with the same sign.
The other real place receives the inverse triple and all remaining triples consist of neutral places.
Thus the unit \(W\) is positive in the chosen embedding, inverted in the other real embedding, and of modulus one in every nonreal embedding.
The identity
\[
 \log W=s(n-1)\log T+2(n-1)\log r-\log Y-2s\log|Z(\zeta)|
\]
gives the constant \(\log(2^{n-2}/(2n-5))\) and correction \(s(n-3)/\sqrt M\) from the cited estimates.
The remainder is \(O(n^3/M)\), uniformly also for the continuous real parameter used in the final counting argument, whose estimates only require the trace equation, signature and fractional linear identities.
The admissible range bounds \(n^2/\sqrt M\) absolutely, and \(n/\sqrt M\to0\) uniformly as \(q\to\infty\).
For the possible cubic degrees \(n\ge6\), this gives \(W>1\) uniformly.
The noninteger \(A/b\) cannot equal this algebraic integer, and all other conjugate factors of \(bW-A\) are bounded by \(A+b\).
The norm inequality gives \(\log M\le2(2n/3)(n-2)\log 2+O(\log(2n))\), in particular the stated bound (2).

The norm descent supplies one actual integral unit of total measure at most \(C\).
The squarefree coefficient estimate applies to its monic irreducible polynomial with input \((\log(2e)+m(u))/e\to0\) as \(e\to\infty\) for fixed \(C\).
Each of the \(e\) free integer coefficients has \(O_C(\sqrt e\log(2e))\) logarithmic cost.
Allowing all degrees up to \(2n\) and all roots establishes the count for embedded fields, as needed when comparing the specified real values.
For a candidate full field \(K\), the norm \(-2(2n-5)\) restricts \(1-T\) to the indicated prime support, and \(T\) is a unit.
Taking \(S\) to consist of precisely the indicated places gives rank \(O(n\log(2n))\) for the tuple group.
Amoroso–Viada, Theorem 6.2, applies over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) with both coefficients one and both terms nonzero.
Its bound is \(16^{64(r+3)}\) for tuple-group rank \(r\).
I checked the theorem at statement scope against the Torino author postprint, together with the cited HAL source record. [Theorem 6.2, internal p. 26](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/handle/2318/1944876/1212223/Duke5.pdf)
Each tuple determines \(w>0\), then \(M\) by the displayed trace equation since \(C_d(w)>0\), and finally at most one positive integer \(q\).

For a fixed embedded cubic-case field \(F\), its second real embedding and its nonreal embeddings are the same for all candidate units.
The norm of \(1-W_1/W_2\) compares the two real factors \(4\sinh^2(\delta/2)\) with at most \(2^{D-2}\) from the nonreal ones, giving separation \(\gg2^{-n/3}\).
The admissibility condition makes the interval length \(O(n2^{-n})\).
The resulting count of units is bounded absolutely, and at most one survives per field for large \(n\).

For each sign the symmetric product over triples has direct parameter degree at most \(3N\) and root degree at most \(3nN\).
Writing its symmetric coefficients in elementary symmetric functions with weights \(1,\ldots,2n\) bounds their ordinary degree by \(3nN\).
Every elementary symmetric function becomes a polynomial of parameter degree at most two, giving the bound \((3+6n)N\).
The leading coefficient in \(z\) survives because none of the denominators vanishes on the admissible interval.
There are \(n-3\) distinct negative real roots and one positive root of \(H_t\) there, and its negative second Newton sum forces the remaining two to be nonreal.
Simplicity and the lack of endpoint traces give the stated continuous, interior real-analytic branch of triple products.
For fixed \(n\), the nonzero first correction in (4) makes each sign branch nonconstant.
Dividing off the largest constant linear factor power \((z-W_0)^k\) leaves a nonzero specialization at \(W_0\) by the polynomial remainder theorem and cannot increase the degree in \(t\).
The remaining polynomial still vanishes at the analytic branch off \(W_s=W_0\), a dense subset of the interval, and hence on the full interval by continuity, including any endpoint.
The degree bound therefore counts all preimages for either sign, even if a different triple takes value \(W_0\) identically.
Combining at most \(O(n^4)\) parameters per candidate unit with the field count, and adding the bounded set below the absolute cutoff, gives (1) over the whole unbounded range.
Finiteness at every fixed \(C,n\) proves divergence of the positive minimum at fixed \(n\), and summing over \(n\le N(X)\) gives the stated \(X^{o(1)}\) bound under its precise growth assumption.
