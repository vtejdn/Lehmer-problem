## From binary prime sums to distinct lifted evaluation maps

On 2026-11-20 a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the complete [prime-field claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#statement) independently of its proof development.
I found the statement supported, with the three cited analytic theorems used at their recorded scopes.

On the minor arcs the denominator range bounds each of the two denominator-dependent terms in Vaughan's theorem by \(O(LD^{-1/2})\).
Its seventh logarithmic power leaves a saving of eight powers at both endpoints, including the removal of higher prime powers.
Squaring this supremum and multiplying by \(\int |F|^2\ll L\log L\) gives the stated fifteenth-power saving in Bessel's bound.
On each major arc the total partial-summation loss across classes is at most \(v+2D\), and squaring the approximation and integrating over all arcs costs \(O(LD^2/(\log L)^{110})=o(L)\).
The smooth integral is independent of the numerator once its phase has been summed into \(c_v(n)\).
Its extension error uses the integrable square tail, and completing the modulus sum uses \(J(n)\le L\).
Both errors are bounded by \(O(LD^{-1/3}A(n))\).
The squarefree expansion gives a bounded mean for \(A(n)\), since a prime dividing the index contributes \(O(u^{-2/3})\) with mean frequency at most \(1/u\).
Markov's bound thus discards \(O(L/(\log L)^{10})\) indices, within the stated allowance.
For even indices the local factor at two is two and the remaining factors have product at least \(\prod_{i\ge2}(1-i^{-2})=1/2\).
The triangle has length at least \(L/2\), and subtracting the errors and the single diagonal weight leaves at least \(L/16\).
Dividing by \(m^2\) counts ordered pairs, each uniquely determined by its first prime for fixed sum.

For fixed \(P,j\), the difference of Chebotarev counts is asymptotic to \(jL/([K:\mathbb Q]\log L)\).
The cyclotomic subfield forces even \(n\), and reduction of the integral radical units exhibits every seed root as a \(j\)-th power in the prime field.
Removing the discriminant primes makes those roots distinct.
The map from a splitting prime to its sum is injective, so the exceptional-set bound survives restriction to split primes.
A remaining prime with a root of order at most \(T\) divides the displayed nonzero product of resultants, even if other roots have different orders.
Its logarithmic size is \(O_P(T^2+T) = o_{P,j}(L)\) for the stated cutoff, including the empty product.
Dividing by the minimum logarithm of a candidate prime leaves \(o_{P,j}(L/\log L)\) further deletions.
Thus the survivors eventually exceed the integer quota for each seed in any fixed trial.

The exact coefficient, factorization, order and representation tests suffice at every scale without a rate of convergence in the seed or multiplier.
The largest passing multiplier tends to infinity because each fixed trial eventually passes whenever available, and the upper limit diverges.
The tangency bounds place every actual seed on its trial list.
The same pair also qualifies at the larger budget of C513, so its deletion bounds apply simultaneously with their original multiplier.

For distinct summands, both are coprime to \(n\), and their power maps differ by inversion on \(n\)-th roots.
Reciprocity and degree preservation therefore give the same squarefree split minimum for both exponents.
Applying the integral inclusion with \(F=P^k\) to an \(n\)-th root lift gives the two congruences at its powered image.
For the opposite ordering one can apply the inclusion at the inverse lift, since \(q\equiv-p\pmod n\) and the inverse residue is again a seed root.
A fiber of the map \(p\mapsto z^p\) lies in one class modulo \(t\), so its size is at most \(1+L/t\).
All its images have order \(t\), and \(L/(1+L/t)\ge\min(t,L)/2\) gives the stated second bound since the trial cap implies \(\sqrt\ell/g(L)\le L\) eventually.
Evaluation kills both rows modulo \(\ell^k\), hence kills the integer \(B\) there.
Distinct images have lifts differing by units, so the corresponding evaluation ideals over \(\mathbb Z/\ell^k\) are comaximal.
The simultaneous evaluation map onto the full product factors through the stated period quotient.

Finally, for each fixed reciprocal seed of sufficiently small positive logarithmic measure the interpolated pairs eventually satisfy both the width and height cutoffs, with normalized cost at most twice the seed measure plus a term tending to zero.
Taking successively smaller seeds on successive tails gives genuine pairs when the infimum vanishes.
Conversely the positive seed measure is bounded by the normalized cost for every qualifying pair, giving the reverse implication.
