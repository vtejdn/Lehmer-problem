## The Salem factor and the orders that survive modulo two

At 2026-11-19T06:41 UTC a reviewer outside proof development examined the [complete submitted theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v478.md#decision-half-mass-powers-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and its cited inputs, with no development dialogue, author explanations or prior findings on this submission.
The stated criterion holds for every \(m\ge3\), \(n\ge1\) and \(k\ge1\), including the prime sections below the golden ratio.

### Root count, monotonicity and full measure

The identity \(Xf_m-f_m^*=X^m-1\) and the finite geometric sum give \(F_N=(X^m-1)A_{m,n}\), where \(F_N=X^Nf_m-f_m^*\).
The blocks in \(A_{m,n}\) have length \(m\), so all \(N\) coefficients are signs, including the terminal leading one.
Both \(F_N\) and \(X^m-1\) are anti-reciprocal, giving a reciprocal quotient with constant one.
For \(m-1\ge2\) the cited multinacci result gives a simple root \(\beta_m\in(1,2)\) and \(m-2\) interior roots.
Thus on the unit circle \(X^Nf_m\) strictly dominates \(\lambda f_m^*\) for \(0\le\lambda<1\).
Rouché counts \(N+m-2\) interior roots of the perturbed polynomial of degree \(N+m-1\).
Any exterior roots of the limit at \(\lambda=1\) persist with total multiplicity in small disks outside the unit circle, so at most one survives.
Since \(N\ge2m+1\), the derivative \(F_N'(1)=-N(m-2)+2(m-1)\) is strictly negative even at \(m=3,n=1\).
Also \(F_N(\beta_m)=\beta_m^m-1>0\), yielding a simple positive exterior root \(\sigma_{m,n}\).

Reciprocity gives one interior root.
The monic integral factor containing the exterior root must contain the interior root to have constant coefficient of absolute value one.
A quadratic factor with this pair would have the integer trace \(\sigma_{m,n}+\sigma_{m,n}^{-1}\) strictly between \(2\) and \(5/2\).
Hence the minimal polynomial has unit-circle conjugates and degree at least four, and Kronecker's theorem applies to every remaining irreducible factor.
Both the full polynomial and the Salem factor have measure \(\sigma_{m,n}\).
The strict sign \(f_m(y)<0\) for \(1<y<\beta_m\) gives \(F_{N+2m}(\sigma_{m,n})<0\), hence strict increase of the roots.
At each fixed such \(y\), \(F_N(y)\to-\infty\) forces the unique exterior root eventually above \(y\), proving the limit.

### Cyclotomic conductors and admissible powers

The reduction of \(A_{m,n}\) is the geometric sum of length \(N\), with simple roots of every order \(d\mid N\) except \(1\).
For \(d\) odd, the reduction of \(\Phi_{2^a d}\) has precisely the primitive \(d\)-th roots with multiplicity \(\varphi(2^a)\).
This follows from the separability of \(X^d-1\) in characteristic two and, for \(a\ge1\), the substitution \(X\mapsto X^{2^{a-1}}\) in \(\Phi_{2d}\), whose reduction equals that of \(\Phi_d\).
Consequently a cyclotomic divisor has conductor \(d\) or \(2d\) for \(1<d\mid N\), and occurs once.
For a primitive complex root \(\xi\) of either order, \(\gcd(m,N)=1\) excludes \(\xi^m=1\), so testing \(F_N(\xi)\) also tests \(A_{m,n}(\xi)\).
The two signs of \(\xi^N\) give the conditions \(\xi^{m-1}=1\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}\xi^j=0\), respectively.
As \(\xi\ne1\), the second is equivalent to \(\xi^{m-2}=1\).
The coprime consecutive integers \(m-1,m-2\) exclude simultaneous removal at a given \(d>1\).
These tests account for the entire cyclotomic cofactor and remove exactly one full residue order for each conductor found.

Every remaining order has weight \(\varphi(d)/2\), while the weight at \(1\) is zero.
The criterion for actual half-mass returns in the cited power theorem therefore reduces to total weight at least two among the divisors of \(k\).
The only odd \(d>1\) with \(\varphi(d)=2\) is \(3\), and this single order cannot contribute twice.
Thus exactly the surviving divisors \(d\ge5\) can individually certify admissibility, and one of them must divide each admissible \(k\).
The order \(N\ge7\) always survives.
For \((m,n)=(4,1)\), the cofactor is \(\Phi_3\) and the least successful order is \(9\).
For \((8,2)\), the cofactor is \(\Phi_6\) and that order is \(11\).
For \((5,2)\), order \(3\) survives in degree \(20\) with weight one and the least successful order is \(7\).

### Prime sections and the published family

For a multiple \(t\) of \(2m\) and the previously listed primes, \(\Phi_{2m}(t)\equiv1\bmod t\) and is greater than one for sufficiently large \(t\).
Each prime divisor \(\ell\) is therefore new and coprime to \(2m\).
The factors of \(X^{2m}-1\) are disjoint modulo \(\ell\) by separability, forcing \(t\bmod\ell\) to have exact order \(2m\).
This supplies infinitely many prime parameters \(N\equiv1\bmod 2m\).
Their sole nontrivial order is \(N>m\), so the whole polynomial is irreducible of degree \(N-1\) and exactly the multiples of \(N\) are admissible.
At \(m=3\) the first such section has \(N=7\), and along these sections the measures increase to \(\beta_3=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
Powers preserve the Salem degree and retain a unique exterior conjugate, so the smallest powered logarithmic measure is \(N\log\sigma_{3,n}\), which grows asymptotically as \(N\log\beta_3\).

The review checked the published BDM07 original on printed pp. 363–365.
Formula (6.1) and the proof of Theorem 6.2 establish the lower sequence for \(m=3\), including irreducibility for every \(n\) and increase to the golden ratio.
Page 365 displays the exact general \(A_{m,n}\) and proposes its approximation from below.
The general root and cofactor conclusions above use the submitted proof.
Mukunda's one-sided approximation result, mentioned in the source unit at abstract scope, supplies no premise here.
This review determines neither priority for those conclusions nor the least-exponent behavior for sequences tending to one.
