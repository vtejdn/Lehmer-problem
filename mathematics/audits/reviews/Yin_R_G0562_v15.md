## Finding

Every positive-constant Pisot input in C0345 v15 with at most five nonzero coefficients in its trace lift has an absolute positive lower bound on \(\theta-q\).
L0462 v68 also proves its stated uniform neighborhood of coefficient ratio one.
The arguments allow unbounded \(j,h,q,d\), both signs of the interior coefficient and either parity of the Pisot degree.
The constants are qualitative and the conclusion concerns the specified trace family.

### Reduction and interior ratios

The delay-one associate has a real root \(\sigma>1\) because it is monic and negative at one.
Its measure equals that of \(B^\sharp\), since their only possible extra factor is \(z+1\), and Boyd's upper bound gives \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\le9(\theta-q)/2\).
Thus a shrinking sequence has \(M(B^\sharp)\downarrow1\) after subselection, and bounded degree is impossible by the fixed-support gap.
The exact derivative inequality \(j\le(\theta-q)(q+3)\) makes the sole negative mass \(j/[4(q-1)]\) tend to zero uniformly in \(j\).
The trace zeros are simple and interior, so the lift has only circle roots and has measure \(j\).
Anti-reciprocity removes the midpoint and pairs the remaining coefficients, leaving either the two endpoints or one additional pair with smaller index \(1\le d\le h\).
The geometric-sum formula for \(U_{h-d}\) gives the stated Chebyshev representation, including \(d=h\), and the single-term case includes \(h=0\).
The sparse-height bound gives \(|a|/j\le4\), endpoint dominance excludes \(2|a|/j\to0\), and C0345 v13 excludes divisibility of the entire lift or a first nondivisible coefficient at a positive fraction of \(h\).
Hence \(d=o(h)\) on the remaining sequence.
For \(r>1\) and \(1/r<b<1\), the roots of the initial binomial lie strictly inside \(|z|=b^{1/d}\) eventually and the reflected tail costs \(O(b^{(N-d)/d})\).
Rouché's count contradicts the circle root locations.

For \(0<r<1\), the indices \(d+lt\), \(t=o(d)\), are eventually between zero and \(N-d\) for each fixed \(l\).
At zero shift the Taylor constant is one, whereas nonzero shifts use doubled moments.
The mixed transform in \(l\) has finite range even for irrational \(r\), since the first marginal has bounded integer moments and the mixed transform itself is bounded.
Its interpolated level indicators are integer Fourier–Stieltjes transforms, so the negative tail transfers integrality to the positive tail with one common period.
A pure point first marginal would have finite torsion support by the cyclotomic description and would also transfer integrality to zero, contrary to \(M(0,1)+sr\in\mathbb Z\).
In the remaining classification the reciprocal models have arbitrarily late unit coefficients, contradicting \(sr M(l,0)\in\mathbb Z\).
For the polynomial model put \(g=1+\epsilon x^u\).
The reflected density \(F\) has finite Fourier support by finite range and decay, and satisfies \(P=F+sr g\in\mathbb Z[x^{\pm1}]\) with \(|F|\le|g|^2\).
At each of the simple roots of \(g\), \(P\) vanishes and \(P'/g'=sr\).
Division by the binomial therefore gives an integral Laurent quotient whose remainder of degree less than \(u\) takes the value \(sr\) at all \(u\) roots.
It must be the constant \(sr\), which is impossible for either sign.
The Haar conclusion gives a vanishing first block by diagonal extraction and a bound on the number of nonzero coefficients through \(Ld\) by the Gram estimate with multiplier of modulus at most four.
For bounded \(d\), this last bound follows from the length of the interval.

In the far range the first marginal has an integral series after multiplication by \(1+srw\), since fixed multiples of \(d\) are inside the prefix.
For rational \(r=m/n\) in lowest terms, \(n\ge3\), the identity \(n(tw-1)=m(t-w)\) forces the torsion crossings to be endpoints by integrality and the strict conjugate bound \(2/n<1\).
For irrational \(r\), the weighted finite-valued transform gives eventual periodicity of the integer series, and a nonendpoint odd crossing with nonzero denominator would express \(r\) as a cyclotomic-field element fixed under every conjugation.
A zero denominator in the same bracket forces \(w^2=1\).
The resulting eight profiles have possible atoms at \(\pm1\), and the separate half-ratio table adds only roots of order dividing six, also after sign rotation.
For \(d/t\to0,\ t/h\to0\), all shifts \(kt+ld\) and \(kt+(l-1)d\) are strictly inside the prefix for fixed \(k\ge1,l\in\mathbb Z\).
Bounded total variation permits replacing \(r_i\) by \(r\) at both indices.
The integer-transform decomposition separates the singleton terms, which give integer Laurent densities, from the periodic terms, which are atomic.
All the continuous rows come from the same conditional probabilities.
For the smaller envelopes the separated-block inequality gives finitely many shapes up to shifts, and the two exceptional envelopes give the same finiteness by their norm and alignment bounds.
The rational specialization uses a pole at \(n/m\), since the primitive linear factor there has measure \(n>M(H_k)\), with the corresponding measure-two argument at \(r=1/2\).
The irrational specialization uses a prime with \(r\notin\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\), available since distinct prime cyclotomic fields intersect in \(\mathbb Q\).
In each case a nonzero specialized coefficient is nonreal, while the average on multiples of an eventual period is a nonnegative mass.
Its vanishing forces the other progression averages to vanish, giving a last nonzero harmonic if any.
Its conditional moment has modulus at most \(1/2\), contradicting \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(D)/2<1\).
Thus the atoms alone give \(b_{t+6d}=b_t\) eventually by integer convergence.
This argument uses only the limiting profiles at \(r\), so the original ratios can vary through rational numbers with unbounded denominators.

A diagonal choice then gives one window \(Ld\le t\le h/L\).
With \(U=1-z^{6d}\), the truncated \(Q\) has bounded coefficient sum and degree at most \((L+6)d\).
For a term of degree \(e\) in \(Q\), expansion of \(1/(1+sr_i z^d)\) introduces translates \(e+md\) of a progression of step \(6d\).
Extending each progression to a Fourier measure costs \(O_L(1+m)\), summable against \(r_i^m\) uniformly after taking \(\sup r_i<1\).
The comparison series therefore matches on a positive fraction of \(h\) and has a bounded representing measure at positive indices.
The two cutoff kernels apply with degree \(O(h)\) and bounded circle factors.
Division on the circle by \(1+sr_i z^d\) is uniformly bounded.
The resulting bounded \(L^1\) norm of the integral \(UB^\sharp\) bounds its support by the prime-grid result, contradicting its strictly positive shrinking logarithmic measure.

### Ratios tending to one

For unbounded \(d\), the moving one- and two-index tests in C0345 v13 use only differences \(o(d)\) in the integral prefix.
Multiplication of a putative annihilator by a character difference eliminates the negative atom, so its squared norm is an integer tending to zero.
The specialized polynomial has degree \(o(d)\) and cannot vanish at the \(2h\) charged interior lifts.
The mass-two classification and local sign rules thus supply an exact model on \(L_0=\lfloor cd\rfloor\).
The polynomial models have bounded density, and in the geometric model with \(u>L_0/4\) the first \(\lfloor L_0/4\rfloor\) positive moments vanish.
For smaller \(u\), weighting by \(1-\epsilon v^{-u}\) kills all the geometric model's atoms and leaves doubled density bounded by four.
On a block of diameter at most \(L_0/2\) the Gram entries only use differences shifted by \(0,\pm u\).
For fifty selected indices the sole correction to their doubled model Gram has norm at most \(400\alpha\), where \(\alpha\) is the negative mass.
The squared-moment bound tends to eight, while fifty nonzero integral signed moments would give a lower bound tending to fifty.
The same argument with unweighted characters controls the truncated ends on indices \(1,\dots,u-1\) and \(d-u+1,\dots,d-1\), since differences there have absolute value less than \(u\).
Separating \(0,u,d,d+u\) also covers \(u=1\).
This proves the required support bound for \(UP_d\), with \(U=1\) allowed also for bounded \(d\).

With \(p=N-d>d\), division by \(z^p-s\) folds at most two terms into each coefficient.
The errors of \(I_l-(M_l+sM_{l-d})\) at \(l=0,d\) are respectively \(r_i-1,s(r_i-1)\), and the remaining errors are uniformly \(O(|r_i-1|)\).
On the support of \(\omega_i\), the polynomial identity gives \(|1+s v^d|\,|1-s v^p|\le 2|r_i-1|\).
This controls projection even on tied nearest nodes, for which the angular displacement may reach \(\pi/p\).
Conjugate-symmetric tie splitting makes the transform real, and both the transform and the rounded integers extend with the same sign to the second half of \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\).
The rounding theorem applies at a fixed Fourier norm, independent of the grid size.
Every coset restricted to the first \(p\) representatives gives one finite progression, including singleton and empty cases.
Multiplication by a binomial of its step telescopes the progression even when its length does not divide \(p\).
In the identity \(B^\sharp=I+(z^p-s)z^dP_d(1/z)\), the reversal of \(U\) clears the reversed prefix, including the two endpoints.
The resulting nonzero integer multiple has bounded support and the exact measure of \(B^\sharp\).
This contradicts the fixed-support gap for every sequence with \(r_i\to1\) and \(\theta_i-q_i\to0\).
Such a sequence would follow from failure of L0462 v68 by taking both proposed constants to be \(1/i\).
Together with compactness of the bounded ratio this proves C0345 v15.
