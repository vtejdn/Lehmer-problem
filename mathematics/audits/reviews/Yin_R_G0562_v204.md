## All exceptional orders and the sharp length-four examples

At 2026-11-17T10:04 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed v204 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v204.md#decision-cyclotomic-resultants-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the statement, proof and scope without development discussion, author explanations, exploration notes or earlier findings on this submission.
The complete argument supports both bounds in (1), the two refinements and the assertions about the family in (2).

In the gap estimate the tail of a shortest relation is a nonzero algebraic integer at the chosen unit, since dropping a vanishing proper subsum would strictly decrease length.
Multiplication over all conjugates bounds its nonzero integral norm by \(L^d\exp(-g m(P))\) for each consecutive gap \(g\).
Thus all the positive support exponents are bounded by \(E\).
At a primitive \(n\)-th root where the relation is nonzero, its normalized absolute norm is at most \(L\), and the integral quotient by \(P\) has absolute norm at least one.
In a minimal vanishing block, normalization by one term leaves a group of order \(r\).
For each \(p^a\Vert r\), the class of the identity and a second class modulo the subgroup of order \(r/p\) are occupied.
Nondegeneracy makes the constant class sum nonzero.
The relative cyclotomic degree is \(p\) if \(a>1\) and \(p-1\) if \(a=1\), giving the contradiction in the first case and requiring all \(p\) classes in the second.
This works for coincident roots with their given nonzero rational weights.
The block through the constant term therefore supplies \(n\mid K e_j\) from a single fixed list of at most \(L-1\) exponents.
Its covering integers have least common multiple \(K\operatorname{lcm}(e_1,\ldots,e_{s-1})\le K E^{L-1}\), which also bounds the empty exceptional set.

For \(t=n/\gcd(n,K)\), the blocks in each of the first \(h\) weighted derivative sums have all exponents congruent modulo \(t\).
Multiplication of the root by any \(t\)-th root of unity preserves every vanishing sum, including a sum in which differentiation removes terms.
All roots over \(\omega=\zeta^t\) have multiplicity at least \(h\), and Galois conjugacy gives the same conclusion over every primitive conjugate of \(\omega\).
The resulting monic factor \(\Phi_g(X^t)^h\), with \(g=n/t\), has exactly multiplicity \(h\) at \(\zeta\), so the remaining factor after division by it and \(P\) has nonzero integral norm there.
In the normalized absolute norm of the divided derivative, the integer \(t\) contributes precisely \(t^h\), and \(\Phi'_g(\omega)\) contributes at least one, including \(g=1\).
The conjugatewise bound \(L\binom D h\) gives the stated pointwise estimate using \(h\le s-1\) and the maximum with one.
In particular \(R_1(P)=|P(1)|\).
At fixed \(L,x\), the least common multiple and \(R_1(P)\) are polynomially bounded in \(d\), and \(R_n(P)\) is uniformly bounded for \(n\ge cd\).
Unboundedness on a subsequence excludes bounded lengths there, while divergence along the sequence rules out any subsequence of bounded lengths.

Within each occupied class, the first \(u\) derivative identities are incompatible with \(u\le h\) exponents, since the falling factorial polynomials of degrees zero through \(u-1\) give an invertible evaluation matrix.
For a single occupied class the constant term forces \(F=G(X^t)\), and full degree of \(\alpha^t\) gives \(t\,m(\alpha)=m(\alpha^t)\le m(G)\le\log L\).
The strict threshold in the statement ensures \(t>\log L/x\), hence gives the sharper exponent.
For four signed terms at an odd exceptional order, a chord midpoint yields the opposite pairing even with repeated points, with the zero-midpoint case paired directly.
Oddness forces opposite signs in each pair, so \(X^n-1\mid F\) and \(n\mid F'(1)\).
If the sum of the two positive exponents equalled the sum of the two negative ones, the displayed Laurent factorization would make every nonzero root torsion or the entire polynomial zero.
Thus the same positive integer \(|F'(1)|\le2D\) works for all the odd exceptional orders.

For the family, reversal of the geometric sum at a reciprocal pair forces \(z^{(b-1)q+2}=1\), of odd exponent.
If \(u=z^q=1\), then \(bz=1\) contradicts \(|z|=1\).
For \(u\ne1\) the equality of chord lengths forces \(u^b=u\) or \(u^b=u^{-1}\).
The first forces \(z=1\), contradicting \(u\ne1\), and the second forces \(z^{q-1}=-1\), contradicting odd order.
In a factor reversal the coefficient square sum four and the unit endpoints force exactly four signed terms.
The six distinct positive lags exhaust the six available pairs.
After orientation by the lag \(N-1\), only \(q-1,q\) are consecutive among the three remaining lags, since \(q\ge3\) and \(b\ge6\).
This recovers the interior points \(1,q\), and the correlations with the constant term determine all signs up to an overall sign.
Cancellation makes one factor reciprocal up to sign, which for a split of \(H\) would contradict the reciprocal-pair exclusion even if the factors repeated.

Equality in the triangle inequality at the smallest root modulus forces \(z\) itself positive real by the monomial \(X\), giving a unique conjugate there and hence full degree at every power by separability.
The irreducible nonreciprocal unit meets Smyth's hypotheses, while the squared coefficient sum of \(F\) gives \(m(H)=m(F)\le\log2\).
For any hypothetical relation of length at most three, divisibility by \(H\) forces its value at one to be a multiple of \(b-1>3\), hence zero.
Its positive and negative coefficient weights are then equal, leaving only a single term of each sign and thus no nontorsion root.
The explicit four terms attain the minimum.
At every \(n\mid q\), inversion permutes the primitive roots and the absolute norm of \(b\zeta-1\) is exactly \(|\Phi_n(b)|\), including \(n=1\).
For fixed \(b\) the odd exceptional least common multiple is at least \(q=(d-1)/(b-1)\).
When both parameters tend to infinity, \(b-1\le R_q(P)\le b+1\) and \(d/q=b-1+1/q\), which rules out an exponent below one even allowing fixed constants at \(L=4,x=\log\theta\).

The reviewer checked the statement and logarithmic normalization of [Smyth's theorem](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) in the source unit, accepting its established bound without repeating the original proof.
[Evertse's page 3](../../literature/pdf/Eve99.pdf#page=3) confirms the rational-coefficient formulation attributed to Mann, and [Dubickas's Theorem 2](../../literature/pdf/Dub01.pdf#page=6) has \(M(Q_d)=p(d)>d\) and leading coefficient \(p(d)\) from the displayed derivative.
The local arguments for root-of-unity ratios and factor reversal were checked directly.
The original Mann proof, the full Ljunggren classification and the proof of Dubickas's comparison theorem were outside this inspection.
