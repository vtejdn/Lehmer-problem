The angular derivative of the circle graph is \((B^2-1)/|T+B_0|^2\) for both signs of \(b/a\).
The graph has one zero inside and its pole outside, and the angular derivative of the quotient is at least \(v-u(B+1)/(B-1)>0\).
Its argument therefore crosses exactly \(v-u\) multiples of \(2\pi\) on a half-open period, transversely each time.
Reciprocity of the degree-\(W\) polynomial pairs the remaining roots with multiplicity, giving exactly \(u\) exterior roots.
Summing their individual errors divides each by \(u\) before summation.
Since \(D>2u\) eventually, the chosen minimal polynomial has a circle root and shares that root with its reciprocal by complex conjugation.
Irreducibility gives reciprocity of the factor, and summing its positive logarithms gives the claimed relative error for \(Dh(\beta)\).
Nontorsion gives positive height and hence \(J>0\).

Bilu's accepted one-dimensional theorem applies to \(T=\beta^u\), whose height tends to zero and which, being nontorsion, belongs to no proper algebraic subgroup of the one-dimensional torus.
Every embedding of the power field extends equally often to the field of \(\beta\).
Reciprocity and the exterior estimate confine all conjugates of \(T\) to a fixed compact annulus approaching the circle.

For each fixed lower truncation their logarithmic maximum therefore has the limiting Haar mean, by weak convergence using a bounded continuous extension from the annulus.

The limiting untruncated logarithm has integral zero by the unit-circle linear-factor integral, giving a nonpositive limsup for \(vh(\beta)-\log H\).
