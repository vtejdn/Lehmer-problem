## Orbit weights, equality for two labels and the degree-eight example

At 2026-11-16T22:26 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the full C345v62 submission dated 2026-11-16T22:24.
The reviewer received the fixed proof without development dialogue, author explanation or an earlier verdict on it and had no role in its development.

All roots are algebraic units by monicity and reciprocity.
Irreducibility and \(M(P)>1\) exclude a torsion root and hence a singleton inverse pair.
Taking the quotient of a torsion relation by its image under the individual flip at \(i\) makes \(v_i^{2b_i}\) torsion, forcing \(b_i=0\).
Thus equality of two monomial values implies equality of their integer exponent vectors.
The finite Galois orbit of the vector therefore counts the distinct conjugates of \(\gamma\) exactly once, even when different conjugates have equal absolute value.
The signed-permutation orbit argument of C866v1 applies at this point after establishing independence directly for the present \(P\).
The rest of the estimate uses the stated individual flips of \(P\).

For each absolute exponent vector \(w\) in the orbit, products of individual flips realize all \(2^s\) sign choices on its support.
Since global negation pairs every logarithm with its negative, the logarithmic measure of the monic minimal polynomial is half the sum of absolute logarithms.
The signs of the real numbers \(\log|v_i|\) can be absorbed into the independent choices, including when some are zero.
This gives the factor \(2^{s-1}\) multiplying the sum of sign expectations in the submission.
With all other signs fixed and \(x=w_j a_j\ge0\), the mean \((|T+x|+|T-x|)/2=\max(|T|,x)\) is at least \(w_j a_j\).
Taking the maximum of these bounds and then the arithmetic mean over the \(s\) supported labels gives \(s^{-1}\sum_i w_i a_i\).
The permutation part of the Galois group acts transitively on labels and preserves the set of distinct absolute vectors.
Hence \(\sum_w w_i=d\) is independent of \(i\), and moving a maximal original exponent in absolute value to \(i\) gives \(d\ge\max_j|b_j|\).
Summation yields the stronger lower bound \(2^{s-1}dL/s\).
It is strictly larger than \(L\) when \(s\ge3\).

For support one, every label contributes the two distinct opposite exponents of the same absolute value.
There are \(2m\) conjugates, with one exterior conjugate for each label of positive weight, just as for \(P\), and the logarithmic measure is \(|b_i|L\).
More generally any nonzero integer scaling of an exponent vector has the same stabilizer, since integer multiplication is injective on vectors.
Thus taking a nonzero power of the supported monomial preserves the orbit degree, and sign symmetry gives the corresponding scaling of logarithmic measure by the absolute exponent.
For support two with at least one exponent of magnitude greater than one, the stronger bound is at least \(2L\).
This applies in particular when the two magnitudes differ, regardless of how the permutation group orders the pair in its orbit.

When both magnitudes are one, each absolute vector is exactly one edge of the orbit of the unordered pair.
The four sign choices on that edge have positive-part sum \(a_i+a_j+|a_i-a_j|\).
Summing \(a_i+a_j\) counts each vertex with the common valency \(\Delta\), giving the exact formula \(\Delta L+\sum_{\{i,j\}}|a_i-a_j|\).
Because \(L>0\) and \(\Delta\) is a positive integer, equality with \(L\) holds precisely at valency one with equal weights on every edge.
Transitivity then gives a matching covering all \(m\) labels, whose \(m/2\) edges supply four distinct conjugates each.
Every edge with common positive weight gives one exterior monomial conjugate and accounts for two exterior roots of \(P\).
Every edge of weight zero gives no exterior conjugate on either side, proving the exact halving with unchanged degree.

In the cited L462v20 example, the two fibers of \((t-1)^2+2=\pm\sqrt5\) partition the four root labels into two Galois-conjugate edges.
The positive fiber has two real traces in \((-2,2)\), while the other has two nonreal conjugate traces and hence two equal positive exterior weights.
The distinct roots \(-2,4,-3,5\) of the trace polynomial modulo \(29\), together with \(T(-2)=4\cdot29\), give a valuation odd at just one of the four quadratic discriminants \(t_i^2-4\).
Transitivity moves the odd valuation to each discriminant, establishing the independent square classes and hence the required individual flips.
The matched two-coordinate monomial thus has the original measure and degree eight, with one exterior conjugate in place of two.
The reviewer finds the full bound, equivalence, degree and exterior counts proved as submitted.
