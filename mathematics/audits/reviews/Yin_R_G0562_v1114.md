# Review of Salem detection by norms in the real root field

## The projection detects precisely the stated subfields with nontorsion norm

At 2026-11-24T22:22 UTC an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed the three fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and the questions to check without proof-development discussion or other findings on this argument.
The equivalence holds in the original real exterior embedding, with the given primitive Salem number in its positive real embedding, and the measure inequality holds throughout the stated class.

A nontorsion algebraic unit has an exterior conjugate by Kronecker's theorem.
If \(z+z^{-1}\) is real, its imaginary part is \(\operatorname{Im}(z)(1-|z|^{-2})\), so every conjugate off the circle is real.
In particular the specified embedding exists even if the exterior conjugate chosen is negative.
Reciprocity gives an automorphism \(\tau\) of \(K\) taking \(w\) to its distinct inverse, and \(X^2-tX+1\) gives \([K:F]=2\) and \(K^\tau=F\).
There is also a direct check on the labels at this embedding.
Ordinary complex conjugation fixes \(w\) and sends each nonreal Salem conjugate \(\gamma\) to \(\gamma^{-1}\).
Simultaneous invariance of the inner product and \(f_{\gamma^{-1}}=-f_\gamma\) therefore give zero at every such label.

For the proof using overlap, a nonzero inner product of the two real log functions implies that at some automorphism neither function vanishes.
Their images are then \(w'\) real and off the circle, and \(\alpha\) or \(\alpha^{-1}\) for the Salem input.
Simultaneously applying that automorphism preserves the inner product, and switching the orientation of the Salem input changes only its sign.
The fields \(F'=\mathbb Q(w'+w'^{-1})\) and \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), hence their compositum \(T\), are totally real.
A nonsplit real place of \(B\) for \(K_\alpha\) extends to a real place of any finite totally real compositum containing \(B\).
The negative discriminant there ensures that adjoining \(\alpha\) still gives degree two.
In the case of totally real \(w'\), apply this to \(\mathbb Q(w')B\).
Its quadratic inverter in \(\mathbb Q(w')K_\alpha\) fixes \(w'\) and restricts to the inverter of \(K_\alpha\).
Norm equivariance makes \(N_{\mathbb Q(w')K_\alpha/K_\alpha}w'\) invariant under the latter, and the conditional norm formula gives zero.

Otherwise a nonreal conjugate of \(w'\) gives an interior trace place of \(F'\) at which its quadratic discriminant is strictly negative.
That place extends to a real place of \(T\), so \(T(w')/T\) is quadratic as well.
If the two quadratic extensions over \(T\) were distinct, their compositum would be biquadratic, with an involution fixing \(T(w')\) and inverting \(\alpha\).
This compositum equals \(\mathbb Q(w')K_\alpha\), since it already contains both trace fields.
Norm equivariance again contradicts nonzero projection.
These arguments use the norm from the full compositum to \(K_\alpha\) even when that extension is nonnormal.

The equality of the two quadratic extensions implies that their nonzero radicands differ by a square in \(T^\times\).
Consequently their signs agree at every real embedding of \(T\).
In the displayed embedding \(d_w=(w'+w'^{-1})^2-4>0\), since \(w'\) is real and off the circle.
In the normal closure of \(T\), an automorphism fixing \(F'\) preserves this value, so it sends \(d_\alpha=(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})^2-4\) to a positive number.
The unique trace conjugate giving a positive value is \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) itself, since every other conjugate is strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\).
Thus every such automorphism fixes \(B\) pointwise, giving \(B\subseteq F'\) in this embedding.
The quadratic equality over \(T=F'\) yields \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q(w')\).
Transporting back sends \(\alpha\) to \(\gamma\) or \(\gamma^{-1}\) according to the orientation, hence puts \(\gamma\) in \(K\).
This field is real in the stipulated embedding, so \(\gamma\) must be one of \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\), yielding the literal inclusion \(K_\alpha\subseteq K\).

Given that inclusion, \(K\) has a nonreal embedding because an embedding of its Salem subfield with nonreal image extends to \(K\).
At such an embedding \(\sigma\), the nonreal value \(\sigma(w)\) lies on the circle, so \(\bar\sigma=\sigma\tau\) on the generator \(w\) and hence on \(K\).
If \(\sigma(\alpha)\) were real then \(\tau\alpha=\alpha\), which would put the entire Salem field in the totally real field \(F\).
It is therefore on the circle and \(\tau\alpha=\alpha^{-1}\).
Thus \(\tau\) restricts to the Salem inverter, and for \(\rho=N_{K/K_\alpha}w\) norm equivariance gives \(\tau\rho=\rho^{-1}\).
This integral unit belongs to the rank-one relative norm kernel, so the primitive positive \(\alpha\) gives \(\rho=\pm\alpha^k\) for an integer \(k\), including in Salem degree four.
With the positive orientation the conditional norm formula gives coefficient \(k/[K:K_\alpha]\), since \(\rho/\tau\rho=\alpha^{2k}\).
In particular the two real coefficients vanish together exactly for \(\rho=\pm1\).
If \(K\) is totally real it has no such Salem subfield and all tests vanish.

The product over the \([K:K_\alpha]\) embeddings defining the relative norm gives \(h(\rho)\le[K:K_\alpha]h(w)\) for the absolute logarithmic height.
Multiplication by \([K_\alpha:\mathbb Q]\), with \(h(\rho)=|k|h(\alpha)\), gives \(|k|\log\alpha\le m(w)\).
All arguments apply independently of measure, degree, and the number of real exterior roots.
