# Independent review of the fixed-exponent cost with all prescribed rows present

## The complementary rank ratio and simultaneous nonzero values

At 2026-11-24T19:26 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v11.md#statement) and all five proof sections of C0901 v11.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v11.
The argument establishes the exact rate \(m(\beta)\rho_*\) when every prescribed row is present.
It also establishes the stated necessary and sufficient rank condition for attaining this rate with every row value nonzero.

The rotations coming from embeddings over \(K\) have contact order at least \(t\) at \(\alpha\).
Their coefficient space \(L\) has restriction rank \(r(T)\) on each set \(T\), since dividing the power-evaluation columns by their fixed nonzero factors preserves rank.
If removing \(i\) lowers rank, the restriction to the other slots has a nonzero kernel in \(L\), so \(L\) contains \(\mathbf e_i\).
This proves the forced order on \(U_i\) by unramified power substitution.
Otherwise \(\mathbf e_i\notin L\), and adding it raises full rank by one.

For disjoint \(C,J\), the vectors annihilating the \(C\)-coordinates restrict on \(J\) with dimension \(r(C\cup J)-r(C)\), by taking the kernel of the projection of \(L|_{C\cup J}\) onto \(L|_C\).
The same identity holds after adding \(\mathbf e_i\).
The projected spaces are nested, so a single invertible constant change of Wronskian columns uses first the combinations of order \(t\), then the additional combinations of order \(u\).
Only the rows in \(J\) enter this Wronskian, and their distinct leading exponents make its highest coefficient nonzero.
Each derivative loses at most \(|J|-1\) orders, so the multiplicity at \(\beta\) of its integral polynomial factor is at least \(t(v(C\cup J)-v(C))-k(k-1)\).
The determinant bound in terms of the original columns loses only \(O(\log(2+b))\) in (2), after accounting for at most \(b+1\) coefficients per row.
Applying this to each complementary set for a graph with all rows present yields the asserted lower rate under \(\log(B+2)=o(a)\).

In the packing argument, any next addition of smaller incremental density would combine with the preceding addition to beat the preceding minimum, so the slopes are nondecreasing.
After fixing later blocks, the difference between distinct projections is supported on earlier slots and a nonempty subset of the current block.
Inequality (2) bounds the norm on that subset itself, and its incremental density is at least that of the chosen whole block.
The resulting separation is independent of the unexposed coefficients.
Packing in the coefficient cube and multiplying the conditional counts gives the sum of positive parts in the proof.
This sum telescopes to \(|D_j|h-v(D_j)\) on the initial segment of slopes below \(h\), including the empty segment.
The errors total \(O((b+1)\log(2+b))\) since the number of slots is fixed, giving (3) also with a fixed row deleted.

With \(h_0>\rho_*\), the integral jet count for all \(k\) rows has \(r(S)\) independent field coordinates per derivative.
After normalization by \((b+1)t m_0\), the binomial contribution is \(O(\log(2+f)/f)\), and the coefficient-box contribution beyond the main terms is \(O(\eta)\) for fixed \(h_0\).
This gives (4).
For every proper \(T\), the difference of the two leading expressions is at least \((k-|T|)(h-\rho_*)\ge h_0-\rho_*>0\).
Thus no row can be constant on the largest fiber.
One difference for each slot suffices, since a combination using successive powers of one integer has a nonzero coefficient polynomial of degree at most \(k-1\) on each slot.
At most \(k(k-1)\) integers are excluded in total, so the chosen bounded combination has all rows present and preserves the rate.
For \(k=1\) the projection gives the single-row lower bound and the same construction supplies a nonzero row.

When no deletion lowers rank, adding \(\mathbf e_i\) leaves every proper-set packing exponent bounded above by its previous value and lowers the full-set exponent by \(u/t\).
Taking \(u=\lceil t\sqrt{\eta}\rceil\) makes this full-set loss larger than the normalized \(O(\eta)\) counting errors, while proper sets retain the fixed margin.
Hence for each \(i\) a difference has finite row order \(c_i<u\).
The integer divided-derivative operator preserves each surviving exponent, makes this row value nonzero, and loses at most \(c_i\) orders of contact for the graph.
Writing \(x=c_i/t\), its normalized log cost for \(c_i>0\) is at most \(x\log(\mathrm e q(b+1)/(tx))\).
Here \(x\le\sqrt{\eta}+1/t\), and \(\sqrt{\eta}\log(2+f)\to0\) since \(f\to\infty,\ f\le t\), and both \((\log(2+f))^{3/2}/\sqrt f\) and \((\log(2t))^{3/2}/\sqrt t\) tend to zero.
Also \(x\log(1/x)\to0\), so the cost is \(o(t)\).
Fixed positive multiplicity slack absorbs every \(c_i\), and the bounded scalar combination now avoids zeros of one nonzero row-value polynomial per slot.
Taking limits first in \(a\), then in the two fixed positive margins, proves the simultaneous nonzero-value conclusion.

In the example, \(\alpha^4=7+4\sqrt3\) generates \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
The identity \(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}=\sqrt2\notin K\) puts slot \(1\) outside the common \(K\)-line of slots \(0,2\).
The minimum subset density is \(1/2\), and deleting slot \(1\) gives complementary density one, the largest possible.
This proves the two different rates and the forced order \(a\) on the odd slot.
