### Specialization and the finite choice of an original prefix

After removing the integral gcd a single occupied row is a signed Laurent monomial.
With at least two rows, every nonzero fixed relation \((c,e)\) on the original coordinates is excluded eventually.
When \(e\ne0\), rounding contributes \(O_e(1)\), and the leading term \(n^{h+1}e\cdot\beta\) dominates the base polynomial \(c\cdot p_n\), also for empty base coordinates.
When \(e=0\) and \(c\ne0\), the base polynomial in \(n\) has finite zero set.
Only finitely many candidate relations have sup norm below a given bound, so the shortest relation tends to infinity.
This both prevents collisions among the finite original support and gives convergence of the full Mahler measures, including in the presence of zeros on the torus. [Boyd–Lawton theorem](../literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002)

The finitely many fast centers have a positive minimum separation in the scalar products with \(\beta\).
All slow offsets are \(O_F(n^{\max(h-1,0)})\), whereas separation after rounding is of order \(n^{h+1}\).
In the earliest row each pair of base exponents is eventually ordered by one fixed sign, so a single original support point is first for every sufficiently large \(n\).
A fixed lattice translation taking it to zero makes all these specializations ordinary polynomials with nonzero constant term and makes the earliest fast row a base polynomial.
The strict inequality \(m(F)<\eta(k,1)\) passes to the specializations for all sufficiently large \(n\).
The initial polynomial from v335 has degree at most \(C_kW_n\), which lies before the first exponent of every other row for large \(n\).
Its coefficients and support therefore come from a subset of actual terms of the first row, and normalization introduces no changing scalar multiplier.
After extraction this subset is constant, giving \(Q_n=Q(Z^{p_n})\) for one fixed nonzero integral \(Q\) in the base variables.
This reasoning also covers a zero-width support and \(h=0\). [Short-interval factorization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v335.md#statement)

The number of cyclotomic factors is at most \(C_k\), since every totient is at least one.
For each \(e\), the prime-power contribution to \(e/\varphi(e)^2\) is \(p^{2-a}/(p-1)^2\) for \(p^a\Vert e\).
Only \(p=2\) can exceed one, and its contribution is at most two.
Thus \(e\le 2\varphi(e)^2\le 2C_k^2\), giving finitely many choices of the list with repetitions and of the overall sign.
The positive exponents in the substitutions can remain unbounded.

### Equality patterns and descent to the original lattice

Expand the fixed left-hand polynomial and every factor on the right before collecting exponents.
The two lists of terms, their coefficients and their indexing are finite and fixed.
The remaining \(n\)-dependence is solely in the evaluated integer exponents.
Select one subsequence with the same equivalence relation on the disjoint union of the two indexed lists.
In each equivalence class the signed sum of coefficients from the two sides is zero at every selected index, by the univariate polynomial identity.
Two exponent expressions in different classes differ at every selected index, so they represent different sequences.
This proves the identity in the group ring, including all cancellations within the expanded right-hand side.
No claim about collisions of other group elements at one index is required.

The finitely generated group of exponent sequences \(\Gamma\) is torsion-free, and the original coordinate sequences generate an isomorphic copy \(\Delta\) of the original full exponent lattice by the eventual exclusion of each relation.
This preserves integer coefficients and the distinction between base and fast variables.
For every coset of \(\Delta\) in \(\Gamma\), its finitely supported coefficient vectors form a monomial translate of \(\mathbb Z[\Delta]\).
Multiplication by \(Q\ne0\) is injective on each such summand because the Laurent ring of \(\Delta\) is a domain.
In (2) the left-hand side has zero components on all nonzero cosets, so the whole product \(T\) has zero components there.
This argument permits torsion in \(\Gamma/\Delta\) and imposes no requirement that an individual character factor descend to \(\Delta\).
Consequently \(F=\pm QT\) already in \(\mathbb Z[\Delta]\), and coefficient comparison at every original fast exponent shows \(Q\) divides every row in the base Laurent ring.
Their unit gcd forces \(Q\) to be a signed Laurent monomial.

In Smith coordinates the embedding of \(\Delta\) into the free group \(\Gamma\) dualizes to projection and nonzero power maps on circles.
These give a surjection carrying normalized Haar measure to normalized Haar measure, including when the index in the saturation exceeds one.
The logarithmic measure thus agrees in the two lattices.
Each positive sequence \(\lambda_\nu\) is a nonzero element of \(\Gamma\) and evaluates at a Haar-uniform circle coordinate on its character torus, so its cyclotomic factor has zero logarithmic measure.
With \(Q\) a unit this proves \(m(F)=0\).
Injectivity of the group embedding also preserves coefficient length, while convolution gives the product upper bound and the elementary symmetric expansion gives \(\|\Phi_e\|_1\le 2^{\varphi(e)}\).
Thus the primitive equality cases have coefficient length at most \(2^{C_k}\).

To identify irreducible factors in the original ring, choose a primitive separating fast direction and use it as a coordinate functional in a unimodular basis.
The new leading coefficient in that coordinate is a single original row times a Laurent monomial in the remaining fast variables.
Any factor independent of the chosen coordinate divides that leading coefficient and hence is a base divisor up to a monomial.
Comparing all the separated coefficients forces it to divide every row.
There is no such nonunit for the primitive row.
At zero measure the partial-degree result then excludes a nontorsion horizontal component, and rational torsion components give cyclotomic polynomials in primitive monomials with their full multiplicities.
This supplies the factorization in the original lattice even if individual factors in the enlarged lattice split differently.
Multiplying by \(H\) adds \(m(H)\) to the result, and changes of lattice basis preserve measure and transport the projection. [Partial-degree theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) · [Horizontal factorization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal)

### Exactly two occupied fast exponents

For a fixed Laurent polynomial \(D\), use any sequence of univariate specializations with shortest relation tending to infinity.
Its finite support eventually evaluates injectively.
If infinitely many specializations are reciprocal up to sign and a monomial, choose a subsequence with the same sign, the same extreme support points \(e_-,e_+\), and the same finite matching \(e\mapsto e'\) of reflected terms.
Then the lattice vector \(e+e'-e_--e_+\) evaluates to zero infinitely often, forcing it to vanish.
The matched integer coefficients agree up to the fixed common sign, so the original polynomial is reciprocal in its own lattice.
It follows that a nonreciprocal original polynomial has eventually nonreciprocal specializations.
Univariate monomial translation does not affect the measure, and v337's product consequence of Smyth gives a lower bound \(\log\theta\) on each.
Lawton's theorem gives the same bound in the limit. [Univariate rigidity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity)

For a primitive two-row polynomial \(A(X)+Y^vB(X)\), reciprocity exchanges the two fast points because a central reflection fixing both would imply \(2v=0\), impossible for a nonzero lattice vector.
Thus \(B(X)=\pm X^u A(X^{-1})\).
For each base point outside the zero set the two coefficients have equal modulus, and the nonzero fast character \(Y^v\) has uniform circle distribution even for nonprimitive \(v\).
Linear Jensen and averaging over the base give \(m(F)=m(A)\).
If this value were strictly between zero and \(\log\theta\), both \(F\) and \(A\) would be reciprocal by the threshold just proved.
Then \(B\) is associated to \(A\) and both are units by the gcd, giving the contradiction \(m(F)=0\).
The threshold is attained in one base and one fast variable by \(A=x^3-x-1\) and \(B=1-x^2-x^3\).
The only possible integer roots of the monic cubic are \(1,-1\), neither a root, and \(B\) is not an associate, so the two rows are coprime.
The discriminant is \(-23\), and the real root \(\theta>1\) and the nonreal conjugate pair have product one.
Both nonreal root moduli equal \(\theta^{-1/2}\), hence \(m(A)=\log\theta\), and \(B=A^*\) gives exactly this value for the two-row polynomial.
This proves optimality for the class over unrestricted base and fast dimensions.
