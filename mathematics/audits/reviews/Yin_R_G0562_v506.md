### Both divisor arguments

For any qualifying pair, the [C408 value identity and reflected elimination](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) give \(V(z)\ne0,\ \alpha=-U(z)/V(z),\ [\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]=e\) and \(Q^2\mid UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) in the Laurent ring.
The nonzero reflected polynomial has span at most \(2\max(d_U,d_V)\), so \(e\le\max(d_U,d_V)\).
For a reduced factor \(\phi^m\parallel\overline P\), remove \(\phi^s\) from both reduced rows, where \(s\) is their smaller order, treating a zero row as having infinite order.
Frobenius gives residual graph \(A^p+xB^p\) after division by \(\phi^{ps}\).
If \(B\) vanishes modulo \(\phi\), \(A\) is a unit there, and otherwise the derivative \(B^p\) is a unit there.
Either way the order of the residual graph is at most one, so the inequality \(ps+1\ge2m\) gives the specified ceiling, including equality cases.
Both zero reductions satisfy the resulting divisibilities immediately.
Each ceiling is at most \(m\), so \(t\le e\le d_U+d_V\).
Multiples of the monic \(D_p\) in the target polynomials of degree \(<d_U+d_V\) have codimension \(t\).
The fixed-size integral Sylvester matrix therefore has mod-\(p\) corank at least \(t\), including if either reduced degree drops.
Its nonzero determinant is divisible by \(p^t\) by Smith normal form.
Squarefree reduction puts exponent one on every factor, giving \(t=e\).

Ordinary differentiation of \(U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\) at its double root gives \(V(z)=-p\alpha^{p-1}(U'(z)+\alpha V'(z))\) in \(p\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Its nonzero field norm is divisible by \(p^e\) without a discriminant hypothesis.
In the polynomial resultant the product of the \(e\) conjugates \(U(z_j)+TV(z_j)\) is the norm of \(V(z)\) times the monic minimum \(P(T)\).
This proves the displayed sign and the exact positive content.

### The positive example and the Hensel neighborhood

For a fixed seed, all sufficiently large primes preserve its degree, give separable reduction, and avoid \(P(1)P(-1)\).
The powered minimum has the same reduction as \(P\) by Frobenius on its symmetric coefficients, so the \(z_j\) have distinct residues outside \(0,\pm1\).
I checked [the two centered formulas](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#rows) at \(w=L=\sigma=1\).
They give first-row value \(pD(z_j)^3\delta(z_j)\) and Euler derivative \(D(z_j)^2\) at every powered conjugate.
The slope choice made elsewhere in C407 for two independent columns is unnecessary for the single graph in C408.
Multiplication by \(Y^{2e}\) adds a derivative term divisible by \(p\), and the [endpoint addition by a multiple of \(Q^2\)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#bounds) preserves both jets.
Thus \(v(V(z_j))=1,\ v(V'(z_j))=0\) for \(v(p)=1\).
The shifted reversal \(U_0\) has degree \(<4e\) because \(b_0(0)=0\), while \(V\) is monic of degree \(4e\) and also has constant term one.
The graph before adding \(cQ^2\) is \(x\) times the C407 graph.
The addition retains the double roots since \(P\mid Q(x^p)\), and retains the strict degree inequality since \(\deg Q^2=2e\).
Every other distinct residue root of \(\overline V\) rules out at most one of \(0,\ldots,4e\) modulo \(p\), and \(p>4e\) makes these choices distinct.
At each protected residue root \(\overline U=0\) by the exact value identity and \(p\mid V(z_j)\).

In a finite complete splitting extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\), [the simple-root Hensel theorem](../literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001) gives one root \(y_j\) of \(V\) in each protected residue class and \(v(y_j-z_j)=1\).
Taylor expansion now gives \(y_j-z_j\equiv-V(z_j)/V'(z_j)\pmod{p^2}\), including in a ramified extension with the valuation normalized at \(p\).
Expansion of \(U\) gives \(U(y_j)\equiv -V(z_j)(U'(z_j)+\alpha_j V'(z_j))/V'(z_j)\pmod{p^2}\).
By the exact derivative identity the parenthesized factor is \(-V(z_j)/(p\alpha_j^{p-1})\), a unit.
Each protected root therefore contributes exactly one to the valuation of the resultant.
Monicity keeps all other roots integral, the simplicity of the protected roots in \(\overline V\) accounts for their full multiplicities, and the choice of \(c\) makes \(U\) a unit at every remaining root.
The resulting valuation is \(e\), so in particular the rows are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\).

The C407 bound hence gives \(\log H\le3ph+O_P(\log(2p))\).
For each fixed seed with \(3h<\log2\), this lies below \(p\log2\) eventually, and every diverging degree cap eventually includes \(4e\).
