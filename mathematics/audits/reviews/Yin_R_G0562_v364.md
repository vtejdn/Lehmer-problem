### Prime powering and the determinant

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(L=\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)\) and \(e=[K:L]\).
For arbitrary nonzero algebraic \(\alpha\) and prime \(p>d+1\), the norm gives \(N_{K/L}(\alpha)^p=(\alpha^p)^e\).
Since \(e<p\), Bézout gives a \(p\)-th root of \(\alpha^p\) in \(L\).
Its ratio to \(\alpha\) lies in \(K\), where a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity would give a subfield of degree \(p-1>d\).
Thus \(L=K\), all powered conjugates are distinct, and the monic integral unit case gives \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\).
The degree hypothesis holds uniformly once \(p^{1-\delta}<p-1\).

Each term in a column of the Euler derivative determinant keeps its original exponent class.
The top coefficient is the product of the leading coefficients times \(\prod_{i<j}(n_j-n_i)\), up to sign, because other selections have lower total degree.
Distinct classes give distinct \(n_j\), including when one is zero.
Extracting \(A=(x^p-\beta)^c\) from the functions inside the determinant contributes \(A^l\) by the lower triangular Leibniz transformation.
After replacing one quotient column by their sum, the smallest guaranteed vanishing order in this column is \(\max(0,t-c-l+1)\).
The monomial prefactor is nonzero at \(\alpha\), and \(p\alpha^{p-1}\ne0\), so the resulting order bound passes to \(W\) at \(\beta\).
All conjugates of \(\beta\) have that multiplicity in the integral \(W\), giving divisibility by the stated nonnegative power of the monic \(Q\).
The residual nonzero integral factor has nonnegative logarithmic measure.

The determinant expansion has at most \(l!\) products, with the derivative indices in each product summing to \(l(l-1)/2\).
The coefficient sum is submultiplicative and is preserved by undoing the monomial and power substitution.
In the stated domain \(t\ge\max(1,l-1)\) every such \(F\) vanishes at a nonzero root and has \(T\ge2\) terms, even for \(l=1\).
The [Dobrowolski–Smyth height bound](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) then gives \(\|F\|_1\le T\,2^{T-2}M(F)\).
With \(M(W)\le\|W\|_1\) this proves (2), including the zero left side when \(l=2,\ t=1,\ c=0\).

### Construction and uniformity

The \(k(b+1)\) monomials used in the construction are distinct and have exponents at most \(pb+p-1<D\).
At order \(j\), all divided derivative coordinates are algebraic integers and the stated bound follows termwise from \(\binom ej\le(1+D)^j\) and \(0\le e-j\le D\) for nonzero terms.
A box of side \(1/2\) bounds every real conjugate difference by \(1/2\) and every complex conjugate difference by \(1/\sqrt2\).
The integral norm therefore distinguishes unequal algebraic integers.
Multiplying the counts over all \(d\) real coordinates, with two coordinates for each conjugate pair, and over the \(t\) derivative orders gives exactly the stated bound.
If \(B\) denotes the exponent in the choice of \(H\), then \(e^B<H\le2e^B\).
The first inequality forces a collision when \(n>dt\), and the second gives (3) from the coefficient sum of the nonzero difference.
All first \(t\) divided derivatives of that difference vanish, giving \(P^t\mid F_0\) by separability and monicity.

With \(t=a+1\) put \(q=dt/n\).
The floor estimates give \(q=O_k(p^{-\delta/2})\) and \(tq=O_k(p^{-\delta/4})\).
Since \(D/n=p/k\), the first term in (3), divided by \(ph\), is \(t/[k(1-q)]=t/k+O_k(p^{-\delta/4})\).
The remaining numerator terms after division by \(n-dt\) are \(O_k(q(1+t)\log p)\), and \(\log(2n)=O_k(\log p)\).
[Voutier's Corollary 2](../literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) applies to \(h=\log M(P)\) for all the possible degrees \(d\ge2\).
It gives \(1/h=O((\log p)^3)\) and makes these errors \(o(1)\) in units of \(ph\).
The error from (2) in the same units is \(O_k((b+\log D)(\log p)^3/p)=o_{k,\delta}(1)\), uniformly over all witnesses and all choices of the classes.

Both \(a\) and \(a+1\) eventually exceed \(k-1\).
For \(l<k\), the difference \((t-l+1)/l-t/k=t(k-l)/(kl)-(l-1)/l\) tends uniformly to infinity over the finitely many possible \(l\).
Thus \(F_0\) has all \(k\) rows, and the two inequalities with \(l=k\) force \((k-1)c\le k-1+o(1)\).
Since \(k\ge2\), integrality gives \(c\le1\).
The primitive integral gcd of the row polynomials divides each integrally and has order exactly \(c\) at \(\beta\).
After power substitution it removes exactly \(c\) copies of \(P\) from the multiplicity at \(\alpha\), so the quotient retains at least \(t-c\ge a\) copies.
Dividing this factor and the scalar content can only decrease degree and measure and leaves every prescribed row nonzero.
The remaining rows are coprime even in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).

In either family of witnesses, normalization by scalar content leaves \(R\) unchanged and gives \(m(F)=ah+m(R)\), including any further copies of \(P\) in \(R\).
The coefficients of primitive \(R\) of bounded degree and bounded measure are bounded by the elementary symmetric function estimates, so a minimum exists.
The lower estimate for every witness and the upper estimate for the constructed one enclose \(\min_F m(R)/(aph)\) within \(O_{k,\delta}(1/a+1/p)\) of \(1/k\), which proves (1).

### Finite comparison and external inputs

[Bertrand's postulate](../literature/Erd32/Yin_U001.md#erd32-u001) at the integer \(d^2\) gives an admissible prime throughout the required range.
For \(k=2,\ \delta=1/2\), one has \(d\le\sqrt p\), \(p^{-1/8}=O(d^{-1/4})\) and \(D=O(p^{15/8})=O(d^{15/4})\).
Consequently \(C(P)/m(P)\) is bounded above and away from zero uniformly at large degree and tends to \(1/2\).
The bounded-degree case follows by finiteness below any fixed measure.
For the unrestricted integer-polynomial question, content of absolute value at least two already gives measure at least two.
The same bound holds for a primitive irreducible factor whose leading or constant coefficient has absolute value at least two, and the factor \(x\) has measure one.
Multiplicativity reduces all remaining positive measures to the nontorsion unit case.
The finite equivalence leaves the positive lower bound itself open.
The [earlier multiplicity-one result](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement) fixes a reciprocal unit before taking its prime limit.
Only its single-value box argument is used in the present proof.
The three cited external inputs were checked against their recorded source statements at the points of use.
