## Independent review of the finite and asymptotic bounds

At 2026-11-20T23:48 (UTC), a reviewer outside the development of the submitted argument inspected the full Claim for Sheng Yin.
The initial review used the fixed Claim and its needed mathematical inputs, without its proof-development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or previous findings on it.
I recommend acceptance of the finite bounds, their shrinking-measure consequences and the Gram comparison as stated.

For the inverse-carry recurrence, the prefix sum at the expanding embedding and the negated suffix sum at the contracting embedding give respective radii \(1/(\alpha-1)\) and \(1/(1-\alpha^{-1})\) on every zero-to-zero path.
Every carry lies in the order, and the absolute norm of a nonzero difference is at least one.
The two real coordinates of a difference can each cost twice the indicated radius, accounting for the factor four in \(r^s\).
For the complex pairs the squared Euclidean separation is \(2\sum_l w_l^2|\sigma_l(x-y)|^2\).
The product bound and \(\prod_l w_l=1\) make this at least \(s r^2\).
The separated support is thus injective under the scaled projection and retains the complete discrete entropy at each positive-probability past.

A ball of radius \(\sqrt{s}\,r/2\) can be used at every cut and for every allowed scaling.
The interiors of its translates are disjoint, so the differential entropy after adding uniform ball noise equals the discrete entropy plus the logarithm of ball volume.
The covariance of the noise is \(\tau I_s\), and its positivity permits the Gaussian entropy estimate and the concavity step even for singular conditional carry covariances.
Averaging with the bridge probabilities for the pasts and uniform weight for the cuts produces precisely \(d\mathcal H\) and \(DKD^{\mathsf T}\), including the deterministic terminal cut.
Raising the resulting determinant inequality to power \(1/s\) supplies the multiplier \(a^2V_s^{2/s}/(2\pi\mathrm e)=c_s r^2\), where \(a=\sqrt{s}\,r/2\).
The two diagonal entries from an unscaled complex pair sum to \(2E_l\).
Consequently the normalized trace after scaling and smoothing is \(\tau+b^{-1}\sum_l w_l^2 E_l\).
Optimization at fixed product of the weights yields the geometric mean in the statement, including by limits if a pair error vanishes.

For a sequence with \(m\to0\), bounded degree would allow only finitely many monic integer polynomials at bounded measure.
Thus \(d\to\infty\).
The cited lower bound on \(m=\log M(\alpha)\) implies \(\log(1/m)=O(\log\log(3d))\), and \((\alpha-1)(1-\alpha^{-1})\sim m^2\), giving \(r\to1\).
The Euclidean ball volume gives \(s V_s^{2/s}\to2\pi\mathrm e\), so \(c_s,\tau\to1/4\).
The cited bridge entropy estimate applies to the same complete digit past and the same \(T=Lkd\), with \(k=\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor\) and \(\log(4T)=o(L)\).
It gives \(\liminf m\exp(2\mathcal H)\ge\pi/(2\mathrm e)\).
Because \(d/s\ge1\) and discrete entropy is nonnegative, each lower bound follows with \(\pi/(8\mathrm e)\), and \(m\tau\to0\) allows removal of the additive term in the pair-error estimate.

Iteration of the carry recursion gives the digit coefficient \(\zeta_l^{j-N}\) for position \(j\) within a block.
The contribution of the previous carry is a rotation measurable from the past.
For each fixed real direction the error of this predictor is therefore one deterministic linear form in the full digit word, using zero coefficients away from that block.
The endpoint event in the moment estimate for integer columns is precisely the zero event for the power-basis sum, since multiplication by \(\alpha^T\) takes the final carry to that sum.
Sign symmetry and the second derivative of the exponential-moment bound at zero give the predictor's bridge mean-square error at most \(u^{\mathsf T}W_Nu\).
Minimization by conditional expectation under the same law gives the asserted matrix bound after averaging.
The argument needs no independence after endpoint conditioning.

For \(d\) consecutive powers starting at zero, the full real evaluation matrix has volume \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}P|}\ge1\).
The realification of a conjugate pair by \(\sqrt2\) times its real and imaginary parts has unit determinant in absolute value as a change of rows.
Appending the two real rows multiplies the neutral volume by at most the product of their norms, bounded by \(d\alpha^{d-1}\).
Thus the neutral Gram determinant is at least \((d\alpha^{d-1})^{-2}\).
The \(k\) consecutive blocks of \(d\) columns in \(W_N\) are rotations of the same full-row-rank neutral matrix, so concavity of \(\log\det\) on their positive definite Gram matrices gives the factor \(k\) after taking the \(s\)-th root.
The remaining factor tends to one because \((\log d+(d-1)m)/s\to0\), while \(mk\to1/2\).
This establishes the stated deterministic limitation.
The established finite bridge and logarithmic Mahler bounds were used at their cited scopes.
These conclusions are conditional on a shrinking Salem sequence for their asymptotic application.
