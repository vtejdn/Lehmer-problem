### Exterior and interior roots and the torsion-free progression

The cubic \(f\) has no rational root and has discriminant \(-23\), so it has a single real root \(\theta>1\) and a conjugate nonreal pair of modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}\) by the product of its roots.
Reversal gives \(g\) one real interior root \(1/\theta\) and a simple exterior pair of modulus \(r=\sqrt\theta\).
The identity \(f+g=-X(X+1)\) and \(f(0)=f(-1)=-1\) exclude common roots.
When \(n>3\), the three exponents of each block occupy disjoint width-three intervals, and reversing the degree-\((n+3)\) polynomial exchanges the blocks.

On the unit circle the moduli of \(f,g\) agree and are strictly positive.
For \(0\le t<1\), Rouché thus counts \(n+1\) interior roots and two exterior roots of \(X^ng+tf\), with multiplicity.
Every strictly exterior root of its \(t=1\) limit retains its multiplicity count in a sufficiently small exterior disk under a small perturbation, so \(G_n\) has at most two for every \(n>3\).
On a fixed exterior disk isolating \(\beta\), convergence of \(g(z)+z^{-n}f(z)\) to \(g(z)\) is uniform and the boundary values of \(g\) are bounded away from zero.
There is eventually exactly one simple zero \(\beta_n\) there, tending to \(\beta\) by shrinking the disk.
Its conjugate uses the second exterior root and reciprocity provides exactly two strictly interior roots.
The leading coefficient has modulus one, so \(m(G_n)=2\log|\beta_n|\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). [Salem–Boyd setting](../literature/Hir06/Yin_U001.md#hir06-u001)

On the forward progression \(n'\equiv9\bmod120,\ n'\ge9\), the complete cyclotomic table in the forward-family argument gives no factor.
In particular \(n'\) is odd, \(n'\equiv1\bmod8\) and \(n'\equiv9\bmod30\), excluding the odd-index alternatives \(\Phi_8,\Phi_{30}\) as well as the even-index cases.
If a torsion point of order \(t\) vanished for the reverse polynomial, choose \(n'=j\operatorname{lcm}(120,t)-n\) with \(n'\ge9\).
For \(n\equiv111\bmod120\), this has the required forward residue, and \(\zeta^{-n}G_n(\zeta)=\zeta^{n'}f(\zeta)+g(\zeta)\) gives a contradiction.
Hence torsion exclusion holds at every positive index in the reverse progression for which \(G_n\) is defined. [Forward progression](../../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v1.md#argument)

Once the exterior nonreal pair has been established, a real monic irreducible factor containing one member contains both.
The two roots are simple, so no additional factor, including a repeated copy, can have a root outside the circle.
Any remaining monic integral factor has nonzero constant term and roots in the closed unit disk, hence has torsion roots by Kronecker.
The torsion exclusion proves that \(-G_n\) is the minimal polynomial.
The two interiors and two exteriors leave unit-circle conjugates because \(n+3>4\), verifying the specified complex Salem convention. [Complex Salem numbers](../literature/PT21/Yin_U001.md#pt21-u001)

### First-order displacement and the limiting law

Simplicity of \(\beta\) bounds \(|g(\beta_n)|\) below by a constant times \(|\beta_n-\beta|\) for large \(n\).
Given \(1<q<r\), the equation \(g(\beta_n)=-\beta_n^{-n}f(\beta_n)\) gives \(\Delta_n=O(q^{-n})\).
This implies \(n|\log(\beta_n/\beta)|\to0\) on the local branch, hence \(|\beta_n^{-n}|=O(r^{-n})\), and the root equation now gives \(\Delta_n=O(r^{-n})\).
Exponentiation on this sharper bound yields \(\beta_n^{-n}=\beta^{-n}(1+O(nr^{-n}))\).
Expanding the two cubics gives \(g'(\beta)\Delta_n=-\beta^{-n}f(\beta)+O(nr^{-2n})\).
Taking twice the real part of the logarithmic displacement divides the linear term by \(\beta\) and gives (1) with \(c=-f(\beta)/(\beta g'(\beta))\ne0\).
The exterior count used here is eventually valid for every delay, regardless of torsion factors.

The image of \(\beta/\bar\beta=e^{2i\psi}\) therefore has modulus \(1/(\theta r)<1\), so \(\psi/\pi\) is irrational.
