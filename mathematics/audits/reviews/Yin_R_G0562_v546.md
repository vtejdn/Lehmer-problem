## Conclusion on exact kernels and squarefree valuations

All three sections hold at the stated scope.
The quotient parameters describe reductions of solutions to the exact integral equations, and the search at 223 decides each supplied pair.

**Rank over the integers and over the residue field.**
In an occupied disk the characteristic-zero roots of the different irreducibles are distinct.
The factor \(P_i^{2t_i}\) in the graph supplies \(2t_iE_i(\xi)\) contacts counted with multiplicity regardless of any cancellation in the row values.
The radius from the monic factor above the disk is at least the required valuation \(1/e_\xi\).
For a row with nonzero reduction, \(s<k_\xi\) would give both \(2s+1<p-e_\xi\) and \(2s+1<2k_\xi\), contradicting C422's zero count.
The inequality \(s\le\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^p}\bar A\) and Frobenius invariance thus put the image of the integral kernel inside the multiples of \(\bar F\).
Scalar multiples reducing to zero already belong to this image bound.
The original remainder map takes values in the free abelian group of remainders of degree below \(2d\).
Its kernel is consequently saturated, giving \(K_b\cap p\mathbb Z^{b+1}=pK_b\), so its reduction dimension is its integer rank.

On the characteristic-zero quotient, inversion of \(X\) is defined because the constant term of \(F\) is a unit.
Inversion preserves the ideal of \(F^2\), and multiplying it by \(X^{pb+1}\) gives a linear involution whose square is the identity.
A graph has its prescribed eigenvalue under this involution even if its actual degree is smaller than \(pb+1\).
Each conjugate pair \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) consists of two distinct roots and has two local summands of dimension \(2t_i\).
The involution gives inverse isomorphisms between these summands, which contribute exactly \(2t_i\) dimensions to each eigenspace.
The eigenvalue constraint therefore gives image rank at most \(d\), matching the reduction bound for \(b\ge d\).
For \(b<d\) the reduction bound already forces zero rank and hence zero kernel.

Monic division commutes with reduction, and equality of ranks makes its quotient map on the kernel an isomorphism modulo \(p\).
On an integral kernel basis the resulting square matrix has unit determinant in \(\mathbb Z_p\).
It is therefore invertible over every \(\mathbb Z/p^j\mathbb Z\), and integer combinations of the basis supply all parameter residue classes.
The same applies to top coefficients because their map to quotient coefficients is triangular with diagonal one.
The determinant can have prime divisors away from \(p\), so finite residue parametrization allows a proper image on ordinary integer quotients.
Solving the graph congruence alone only imposes finite-precision equations and can admit additional classes.

**Squarefree nonvanishing and its residual scalar.**
The nonzero residue of the specified \(C_0\) at unit nodes uses the exclusion of \(-1\).
It is its own reversal at formal width \(r\), so the exact lattice lift of \(\bar F\bar C_0\) satisfies \(\bar V=\varepsilon\bar A\), with \(V=\varepsilon A_b^*\).
There is also a direct check of the digit calculation.
At each root \(\alpha\) of \(F\), the exact graph and derivative equations, with \(z=\alpha^p\), give
\[
                 A(z)=p z\big(A'(z)+\alpha V'(z)\big).
\]
This identity only uses two graph zeros counted with multiplicity, so it applies before nonvanishing has been established.
Reducing the quotient by \(p\) gives \(\bar z\bar F'(\bar z)\bar C_0(\bar z)(1+\varepsilon\xi)\).
All four factors are nonzero by the unit, squarefreeness, chosen quotient and exclusion of both endpoints.
The claimed Teichmüller congruence follows as well since the roots lift in an unramified extension and raising a unit congruence modulo \(p\) to the \(p\)-th power preserves it modulo \(p^2\).
At \(b=d\) a primitive generator reduces to \(c\bar F\) with \(c\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), by saturation, and the same formula is multiplied by \(c\).
Distinct conjugate powers have distinct residues, so the root product for the monic powered resultant has exactly \(\deg P_i\) factors of valuation one.
For fixed squarefree \(F\) only finitely many primes divide its discriminant or endpoint values.

As an exact check, I reduced the monomial columns \(X^{pi}+\varepsilon X^{p(b-i)+1}\) modulo \(P^{2t}\) for \(P=X^2-3X+1\), \(p=11\).
For \(t=1\) the rational nullities at widths \(1,2,4\) are respectively \(0,1,3\) for both signs.
At width two the primitive generators with positive leading coefficient give \(A(X^{11})/11\) with reduced values represented by \(10+2X\) and \(10+4X\) in \(\mathbb F_{11}[X]/(\bar P)\), respectively, by reducing \(A(X^{11})\) modulo \(P\) before division by 11.
Both evaluations are nonzero at each residue root \(5,9\).
For \(t=2\) the nullities at widths three and four are zero and one for both signs, independently checking a nonsquarefree input to the rank statement.
