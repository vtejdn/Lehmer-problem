## Rotated derivatives and the finite degree window

Both estimates hold with the stated degree caps and normalizations, for every degree-preserving prime in the interval and every prescribed set of the given cardinality.
To check the determinant estimate, let \(B\) consist of the selected \(l\) columns of the full Fourier matrix.
Since \(B^*B=pI_l\), the average squared modulus of its \(l\)-row minors is \(p^l/\binom pl\ge1\).
For a minor \(A\) reaching at least this average in squared modulus, the Euler derivative matrix of the chosen rotations equals the row-polynomial derivative matrix multiplied by \(A^{\mathsf T}\).
The latter derivative matrix has nonzero determinant by its top-degree coefficient.
Thus division by \(|\det A|\) costs nothing in the integrated upper bound.

For a root \(z\), the integral of \(|e^{it}-z|^{-1/2}\) is uniformly bounded, including for \(|z|=1\) since \(|\sin(t/2)|^{-1/2}\) is integrable.
The derivative of a product and the expansion of \(E^i\) give the claimed bound for each logarithmic derivative.
With \(S\) the sum of reciprocal distances to the roots with multiplicity, integration of \(\sqrt{1+S}\le 1+\sum_z |e^{it}-z|^{-1/2}\) and concavity of \(\log\) bound the integral of \(\log(1+S)\) by \(2\log(1+CD)\).
Factoring one rotated \(F\) from each column therefore costs \(l\,m(F)\), and the remaining integrated upper bound is \(O_l(\log(2+D))\).
All determinants are nonzero rational functions or polynomials at this integration step, so their logarithmic singularities are integrable.

Writing \(h=\log M(P)\), degree preservation gives \(m(Q)=ph\) by powering each conjugate exactly once.
The common row multiplicity contributes \(lc\) to the determinant and the column sum contributes at least \(\max(0,t-c-l+1)\) more.
For the integer upper bound, the divided derivative values are integral and their embedding boxes of coordinate width \(1/2\) separate unequal values by the nonzero integer norm of a difference.
This gives the cited estimates with \(n=k(b+1)\) and \(D/n=p/k\), including for classes with positive least representative.
At either cap \(\log(2+D)=O(\log(3d))\), and Voutier's lower bound on \(h\) gives \(ph/\log(2+D)\gg(\log\log d)^3\) uniformly.

For \(k=2,\ b=16d,\ t=3\), the leading upper coefficient in units of \(ph\) is at most \((3/2)/(1-3/32)=48/29\), and the other terms are \(o(1)\) in these units.
The lower estimate forces both rows to survive and gives \(2+c<4\).
Dividing by the primitive polynomial row gcd loses exactly \(c\le1\) copies of \(P\), because \(x\mapsto x^p\) is unramified at \(\alpha\ne0\).
Division preserves the two residue classes and cannot increase measure or degree.
Scalar normalization of any witness preserves its cofactor \(R\), with \(m(F)=2h+m(R)\) after normalization, since cyclotomic factors and powers of \(x\) have logarithmic measure zero.
The lower coefficient for \(m(R)/(ph)\) is at least \(1/2-o(1)-2/p\) and the constructed upper coefficient is below \(7/4\) for large \(d\).
These bounds imply (1).

For \(b=dp^3,\ t=p+1\), the denominator correction to \(t/k\) in units of \(ph\) is \(O_k(1/p)\).
The box terms and \(\log(2n)\) together contribute \(O_k(\log(2+D))=o_k(ph)\).
The determinant lower coefficient for \(l<k\) exceeds \(t/k\) by an amount growing linearly in \(p\), forcing \(l=k\).
It then gives \((k-1)c\le k-1+o_k(1)\), so gcd division retains at least \(p\) copies.
For every primitive witness the lower estimate is \((p-k+1-o_k(1))ph/k\), and the constructed upper estimate is \(((p+1)/k+o_k(1))ph\).
Subtracting \(ph\) from each bound and dividing by \(p^2h\) gives (2) for both choices of the row condition.
Existence of the constructed witness and the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds for bounded-degree primitive polynomials of bounded measure ensure that both minima are attained.

For \(p\nmid d\), the relative degree \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)]\) is coprime to \(p\).
The relative norm and Bézout identity give a \(p\)-th root of \(\alpha^p\) in the smaller field, so \(e>1\) places a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity in the larger field.
For primes of the latter type the product of the numbers \(p-1\) divides \(d\) by taking the compositum inside \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Prime divisors of \(d\) contribute at most \(\log d\) in logarithmic weight, and the other exceptions contribute at most \(2\log d\).
Each prime in the interval contributes at least \(\log y\), whereas the total prime weight there is \(\gg y\) by the cited binomial estimate.
This proves both the exceptional count and unconditional existence.

For even a single chosen admissible prime and the fixed classes \(0,1\), a lower bound \(\min_F m(R)/p\ge\eta>0\) would give \(h\ge\eta/2\).
Conversely \(h\ge\epsilon>0\) gives \(\min_F m(R)/p\ge\epsilon/3\).
Finite-degree coefficient bounds handle the remaining bounded degrees.
After removing powers of \(x\), either a nonunit leading or constant coefficient or nonunit scalar content gives \(M\ge2\), and all remaining polynomials of positive logarithmic measure have a nontorsion unit factor.
This checks the equivalence with a Lehmer gap and its normalization in the closing paragraph.
The claim establishes a comparison only, so a uniform positive lower bound for this normalized minimum remains open.
The Voutier theorem was used at the recorded statement level.
