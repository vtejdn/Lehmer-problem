# Normal closures of intermediate fields and the prime-degree trace gap

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T15:17 UTC a reviewer outside proof development checked the whole [C345 v260 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v260.md#decision-intermediate-normal-closures-submitted) for Yin.
The review used the fixed proof and its mathematical dependencies, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings on v260.
The finite-list statement, both quantitative degree estimates, the sharp eventual gap for solvable prime-degree traces and the application to all powers hold with the stated hypotheses.

### Normalization and relative rank

Compression \(S(X)=U(X^t)\) preserves the coefficient content and leading coefficient even for a nonmonic minimal polynomial.
Rational irreducibility descends to \(U\) because any factorization substitutes back into \(S\).
Thus the absolute degree of \(\gamma=\alpha^t\) is \(d_0=d/t\), and \(d_0h(\gamma)=dh(\alpha)\) includes all finite-place contributions.
The support of \(U\) has gcd one by construction, so C345 v254 gives \(F(\gamma^j)=F(\gamma)\) for every \(j\).
Extension of embeddings shows that the image of the group generated by the \(F\)-conjugates of \(\alpha\) under the \(t\)-th power map is exactly the corresponding group for \(\gamma\).
The finite kernel preserves rank.
Raising the latter group to its torsion exponent gives a free group of rank \(r\).
Its generators still generate the normal closure \(N_0\) over \(F\) by power-field equality at each conjugate.
Consequently the Galois action is faithful, and reduction of the finite matrix group modulo three gives \(D_0\le3^{r^2}\).

In Delsinne's one-coordinate bound \(A_1=16\) and \(\kappa_2(1)=48\).
Replacing the relative degree \([F(\gamma):F]\) in its denominator by \(D_0\) weakens the lower bound.
If \(D_0<\sqrt{d_0}\), then \(D_0(\log(3D_0))^{48}\le\sqrt{d_0}(\log(3d_0))^{48}=o(d_0)\), contradicting \(h(\gamma)\le M_0/d_0\) beyond an effective threshold depending on \(M_0\).
Thus \(r\gg\sqrt{\log(3d_0)}\) with an absolute constant past that threshold.

### The intermediate field and choice of dimension

Choose \(k\le r\) independent elements among the actual relative conjugates of \(\alpha\).
For each chosen embedding the image of \(E\) lies in \(\widetilde E\), so its conjugate of \(\alpha\) satisfies a degree-\(b\) equation there.
It follows by a tower of \(k\) adjunctions that the joint degree \(J\) over \(F\) is at most \(Db^k\), regardless of the intersections between the conjugate fields.
The left side of the product-of-heights theorem is \(h(\alpha)^k\).
The contrapositive of Delsinne's Theorem 1.6 gives the stated logarithmic inequality in \(J\) for these multiplicatively independent coordinates.
The published constants have \(\kappa_2(k)=3kA_k^k\) and \(\log c_2(k)=k\log(2k^2)+64k^2 k!A_k^{2k}\), with \(\log A_k=O(k\log(2k))\).
Both costs are at most \(\exp(C_0k^2\log(2k))\) for an effective absolute \(C_0\).
I checked the theorem and constants against their published statement and used the theorem as an external input at that scope. [Delsinne, pp. 983–984](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.2114.pdf#page=4)

With both polynomial bounds in (1), choose an integer \(k>C/\epsilon\).
When \(r\ge k\), subtraction of the \(\log J\) bound leaves \((\epsilon k-C)\log d\) on the left and at most a fixed constant times \(1+\log\log(3d)\) on the right.
All the constants are effective and the coefficient of \(\log d\) is positive, so \(d\) is effectively bounded.
When \(r<k\), the rank bound already bounds \(d_0\) effectively.
At bounded \(d_0\), the elementary symmetric functions of the roots, multiplied by the leading coefficient, have absolute values at most \(\binom{d_0}{i}\exp(M_0)\).
This gives an enumerable finite set containing every \(U\).
At mass cutoff one its positive measures give a uniform gap, also covering all higher measures by taking the minimum with one.

For (2), put \(L=\log\log(16d)\) and \(k=\lfloor\eta\min(\sqrt{\log(3d_0)},\sqrt{L/\log(2+L)})\rfloor\).
The rank estimate ensures \(k\le r\) for sufficiently small absolute \(\eta\).
Since \(\log(2k)=O(\log(2+L))\), we can also enforce \(C_0k^2\log(2k)\le(\log\log d)/3\) for large \(d\).
The two dimension costs are then each at most \((\log d)^{1/3}\).
Assuming \(D<d^{\epsilon k/2}\) gives \(\log J\le k\log A+(1-\epsilon/2)k\log d\), and hence \(\log\log(3J)=O_A(L)\).
Using the original height \(h(\alpha)\le M_0/d\) in (4) leaves
\[
 \tfrac{\epsilon}{2}k\log d\le k\log(A M_0)+O_A((\log d)^{1/3}(1+L)).
\]
The right side is \(o(k\log d)\) uniformly for \(k\ge1\) at fixed \(A,M_0\).
The resulting contradiction and the harmless floor loss give (2) with an absolute coefficient and an effective threshold depending only on \(M_0,A,\epsilon\).
Along a shrinking sequence one may eventually use \(M_0=1\).
Bounded \(d_0\) on a subsequence would leave only finitely many positive measures for \(U\), so both entries of the minimum tend to infinity.
All these bounds are uniform in \(E\) for fixed index parameters.

### Solvable traces and the attaining polynomials

Reciprocity gives an automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) exchanging \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\), distinct because \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
The quadratic over the trace therefore has degree exactly two, and the absolute trace degree is \(q\).
For the faithful solvable transitive group of prime degree the action is primitive.
A minimal nontrivial normal subgroup is elementary abelian, and its orbits force transitivity by primitivity and faithfulness.
An abelian transitive subgroup is regular, since a point stabilizer fixes every point, so it is cyclic of order \(q\).
Its permutation normalizer has order \(q(q-1)\) by the affine description.
The trace normal closure over \(F\) is contained in the compositum of \(F\) with the rational trace splitting field.
Thus \(D\le q(q-1)\), and \(b\le2\) by the quadratic equation, verifying (1) with fixed \(A=2,\epsilon=1,C=2\).
Since bounded \(d_0\) divides \(2q\), only \(1,2\) remain for large \(q\).
The first would give \(X^{2q}+1\), and the second gives \(X^{2q}+aX^q+1\), with \(|a|\ge3\) by nontorsion.
The measure is \(\log((|a|+\sqrt{a^2-4})/2)\), minimized at \(|a|=3\).
Taking the fixed cutoff equal to this minimum establishes the eventual gap for unrestricted measures in the stated solvable class.

For the attaining family, write \(u=(3+\sqrt5)/2\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
As a direct check for odd prime \(q\), a \(q\)-th root \(w\in K\) of \(u\) would be a positive algebraic unit of norm one with \(1<w<u\).
Its integral trace \(w+w^{-1}\) would lie strictly between 2 and \(u+u^{-1}=3\).
Thus \(u\) has no such root in \(K\), in particular for the sufficiently large primes of the claim.
A proper irreducible factor of \(X^q-u\) would give a root of degree \(e<q\) and a norm \(v\in K\) satisfying \(v^q=u^e\).
The identity \(\gcd(e,q)=1\) then constructs a \(q\)-th root of \(u\) in \(K\), proving irreducibility over \(K\).
Since \(u\) generates \(K\), the degree over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(2q\).
The field \(K(\zeta_q,u^{1/q})\) contains every root over \(u\) and its inverse over \(u^{-1}\), is normal, and is cyclic over the abelian extension \(K(\zeta_q)\).
Its trace splitting group is solvable, and its \(q\) exterior roots all have modulus \(u^{1/q}\), giving measure exactly \(\log u\).

### All powers in the prime-index five-term family

For the normalized minimal sparse relation, C345 v254 gives \(d_0=d\) and \(F(\beta^j)=F(\beta)\) for every \(j\).
The quadratic for \(\beta^j\) over \(E_j\) bounds its index by two independently of \(j\), so the second term of the minimum applies for large \(d\).
For prime \(n\), the five distinct support exponents have gcd of differences \(\gcd(n,l)=1\).
A proper vanishing subset would split off a monomial or binomial, impossible at a nontorsion unit.
The coefficient length seven bounds every factor measure by \(\log7\), and C345 v250 gives \(d\gg n\lambda(2n)\to\infty\) uniformly in \(l\).
This verifies the claimed normal-closure growth for each powered trace while leaving existence of small measures in this family unresolved.
