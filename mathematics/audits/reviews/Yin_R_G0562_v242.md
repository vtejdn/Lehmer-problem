## Squarefree degree, partial periods and maximal orders

At 2026-11-17T13:18 UTC an agent for Yin independently checked the [fixed v242 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#decision-four-term-residual-periods-submitted).
The reviewer previously checked the [v238 local degree argument](Yin_R_G0562_v238.md#both-coefficients) and the [v239 binary period argument](Yin_R_G0562_v239.md#binary-periods).
The reviewer took no part in developing v242 and received its fixed text and scope without development discussion or a previous finding on v242.
The squarefree degree estimate, the partial-period inequality and the three measure consequences hold.

Dividing by the monic \(S\) gives an integral cofactor, so \(\bar S\mid\bar P\) also at \(p\mid A\), where the degree of \(\bar P\) can drop.
The endpoints of \(S\) are units at \(p\).
On nonzero residue classes the reduction is governed by \(X^T+\sigma\) in both orientations, with multiplicity at most \(N\) in \(\bar S\).
The number \(a_p\) counts distinct geometric roots, and hence sums the sizes of the represented Frobenius orbits, allowing several orbits of the same order.
If \(N\le2p^w\), \(d\ge a_p\) suffices.
In the remaining range v238 gives at most two exceptional unit roots in total, counted with multiplicity for the entire parent.
In each orbit containing a nonexceptional root of \(S\), its relative degree over the unramified field supplies at least \(cN/(wp^w)\) roots within its class, and Frobenius supplies the same number in each other class of the orbit.
These contributions from distinct orbits are disjoint.
When \(d>2\) at least one represented orbit contributes in this way.
Their total size \(b\ge1\) satisfies \(a_p\le b+2\le3b\), since each class in an exceptional-only orbit uses an exceptional root.
This proves (1) and also \(a_p\ge\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\), counting at least one orbit per represented order.
The quadratic radial bound gives \(m\ge1/(2\sqrt{10d})\) in degrees one and two.

The reduction factors selected by their orders are relatively prime to the complementary group when both groups retain full multiplicities.
Thus their product lifts to a monic factor \(S_0\) over \(\mathbb Z_p\), of degree exactly \(\delta d\).
Each selected class has multiplicity \(N\) in \(X^{NV}-1\) in characteristic \(p\), since \(p\nmid V\).
Monic division by \(S_0\) leaves an integral remainder divisible coefficientwise by \(p\).
At each root \(\alpha\) of \(S_0\) this gives \(v_p(\alpha^{NV}-1)\ge1\) with \(v_p(p)=1\), including over a ramified extension.
The binomial expansion lifts this inequality to \(v_p(\alpha^{p^i NV}-1)\ge i+1\), including \(i=0\) and \(p=2\).
All other roots are integral and contribute nonnegative valuations, while nontorsion guarantees a nonzero resultant.
Multiplying over the \(d\) roots gives valuation at least \((i+1)\delta d\).
At infinity the bound \(2^d\exp(p^iNVm)\) gives (2), with the single archimedean loss \(d\log2\) for every prime.
At two v166 gives the same bound from a gcd of degree at least \(\delta d\).
The choice \(i=\lceil1/\delta\rceil\) leaves at least \(\delta\log2\) in brackets, while the full-set choices \(i=0\) for \(p\ge3\) and \(i=1\) for \(p=2\) give an absolute positive constant after division by \(p^i\).

For odd \(q\mid s\), writing \(f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\) gives \(v_q(p^{fm}-1)=c_{p,q}+v_q(m)\) by the binomial expansion.
For \(q=2\), an odd power of odd \(p\) gives valuation \(v_2(p-1)\le c_{p,2}\), while an exponent with two-part \(2^a,\ a\ge1\), gives \(c_{p,2}+a\) by successive squaring.
Thus a single \(\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\) is divisible by the product of the claimed prime powers for distinct \(q\).
In the single-tower case \(V\mid2h\), so a represented \(s\) with maximal \(i=v_q(s)\) gives \(V\le2Hq^i\) and \(a_p\ge q^{(i-c_{p,q})_+}\), also for \(i=0\).
In the odd-prime plus case every represented order has the common two-part \(2^{v+1}\).
The chosen \(s\) therefore supplies the two-power and the \(q\)-power in the same lower bound, and \(V\le2^{v+1}Hq^i\).

For the maximal-element criterion, covering by the divisor sets of at most \(r\) maximal orders gives one such set of mass \(\delta\ge1/r\), even if the divisor sets overlap.
The selected maximal order \(s\) occurs, so the period of the covered represented orders is \(s\).
The prime-to-\(\mathcal Q\) part of \(s\) divides \(H\), and its \(\mathcal Q\)-part costs at most \(\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}q^{c_{p,q}}\) in the ratio \(s/\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\).
Using \(a_p\ge\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\) proves (3), and one maximum covers the full set, for which the uniform full-set constant applies.
The bounded-degree estimate covers small degrees throughout.
Nonunit endpoints and nontrivial content cost at least \(\log2\), and additivity extends the exponent-only bounds to integral divisors.
For a dilation, the root raised to the dilation exponent is nontorsion, satisfies the reduced polynomial of the same length, and gives the asserted \(R_L\) loss by v210.
The order hypothesis in (3) is then imposed on the reduced unit factor.
In the example write \(z=5^s\ge5\), so \(D=z^2,\ k=z+1\) are coprime, \(D>2k\), and \(P'(1)=-2z^2+6z+6<0\).
The polynomial has a zero at one and a further positive real zero by its positive leading coefficient.
The bound at \(p=3,q=5\) is uniform since \(w=H=1\) and \(c_{3,5}=1\).
