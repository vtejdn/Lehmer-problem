# Independent review of the coefficient mass omitted by a short interval

<a id="finding"></a>
## Interior and endpoint estimates

On November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the [omitted coefficient estimates](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v541.md#statement).
I received the target and questions about its proof, without participating in its development or consulting a previous finding on these estimates.
Both inequalities, the critical specialization and the comparison with the existing whole-span result follow at their stated scopes.

For a nonzero frequency \(n\), restriction of embeddings has constant fiber size \(d/D\), where \(D=[\mathbb Q(x^n):\mathbb Q]\).
Equal powered images have torsion quotient before powering, so \(D\ge d_0\) even if one class yields several powered images.
With regularization radius \(1/D\), the [discrepancy inequality](../literature/Pri11/Yin_U002.md#pri11-u002) contributes \(O(1/D)+O(\sqrt{|n|h+\log(2D)/D})\) for either cutoff phase component.
The cutoff makes the component globally Lipschitz, including at the origin, and its circle mean vanishes.
The omitted mass is bounded by the fraction with \(|\log |x_j^n||\ge\log(4/3)\), hence by \(2|n|h/\log(4/3)\) using the unit norm and the archimedean height formula.
For \(Vh\le1\) this error is absorbed by the square root, and increasing the absolute constant to at least one covers \(Vh>1\) by the elementary bound on a mean of phases.
These arguments include negative powers.

In the squared modulus expansion the diagonal equals \(L_2^2\), while the ordered off-diagonal coefficient products have total absolute weight \(L_1^2-L_2^2\), also for nonreal coefficients.
The full mean therefore has the claimed lower bound.
Its upper bound is \((1-\eta)\|R\|_1^2+\eta L_1^2\), because exact cancellation with the remainder is available at each circle root.
No uniformity of a phase mean restricted to a subset of embeddings is required.
For \(\eta=1\) the resulting interior inequality is vacuous, as its right side is nonpositive.

At the left endpoint shifting by a monomial gives nonnegative exponents throughout the polynomial.
Each reflected evaluation point is either an original conjugate or \(1/\overline{x_j}\), which is again a conjugate by complex conjugation and reciprocity.
The resulting list may have repetitions, but the cancellation and bound by \(\|R\|_1\) are pointwise, so the changed multiplicities require no equidistribution assertion.
For selected exponents \(a,b\in[0,V]\), the radial error is bounded by \((a+b)|\log r_j|\), whose mean is at most \(4Vh\).
The sum of all absolute coefficient products is \(L_1^2\), which gives exactly the stated radial loss.
For the right endpoint reversal evaluates the original Laurent polynomial at the inverse of a conjugate and preserves its coefficient norms and exponent distances.
A single endpoint term permits \(V=0\) and hence the exact bound.

For the critical sequence, reciprocity pairs outside with inside roots, so the exterior count \(O(a)\) and \(d\asymp as^2\) give \(\eta=O(s^{-2})\).
Torsion separation gives \(d_0=d\to\infty\), and \(bh=O(1)\) gives \(Vh=o(1)\).
Dividing (1) by \(L_2^2\) now makes both error terms vanish under bounded effective support.
With all coefficients of modulus one, the two norms in the conclusion are the number of omitted terms and the square root of the number selected, respectively.
For bounded counts, every strictly smaller integer stays a positive distance below the relevant square root, including when the latter is integral.

The class product is a root of unity times the power of a representative by its class size, so its height is the class size times \(h\) and it is a nontorsion unit.
Distinct classes give distinct products, all conjugate, hence its degree is \(d_0\), its total measure is \(m\), and \(d_0\ge2\) because rational units are torsion.
Voutier's bound \(m>2/\log^3(3d_0)\) absorbs \(\log(2d_0)/d_0\) by boundedness of \(\log(2t)\log^3(3t)/t\) for \(t\ge2\).
A monomial shift of a full annihilator has at least \(d\) distinct zeros and therefore degree \(W\ge d\), giving \(m\le Wh\).
The rational whole-span quadratic estimate and summation over nontorsion factors already occur in the cited earlier theorem.
For a primitive nonunit irreducible factor its leading or constant coefficient has absolute value at least two, giving \(m\ge\log 2\) directly in that comparison.
The two new local estimates assume a unit and vanishing at every rational conjugate.
