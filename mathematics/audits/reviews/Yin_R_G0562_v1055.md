## The two rational tests and the quarter-power estimate

At 2026-11-24T18:54 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [complete statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0903_v7.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0903 v7.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v7.
The two rational tests characterize decomposability over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), and their joint failure gives \([\mathbb Q(\alpha^g):\mathbb Q]\ll W^{1/4}\) with an absolute constant.

The normalized exponents have \(b_0=0\) and gcd one, whether or not \(a_0\) is the smallest exponent.
The inverse double roots give the two value and Euler derivative conditions for each sign on the five torus coordinates.
These equations and the interpolation pencil use neither a two-class graph nor an assumption that \(\alpha\) is an algebraic unit.
Clearing coordinate monomials gives equations of bounded degree, uniformly in the exponents and rational coefficients.
At each point the two interpolation polynomials have degrees at most three.
Their product is \(S_0+\lambda D\), and \(Q_+(b_0)=c_0D'(b_0)\ne0\) recovers the scale of a monic divisor and thereby the whole point.
Finite fibers give dimension at most one, and the sum of the component degrees stays bounded.
All six residues are nonzero, so \(f\) has degree six with six simple poles and its generic fiber is separable and transitive.
For generic \(\lambda\), the six distinct roots of the pencil must split into a triple and its complement.
Conversely each triple gives a unique point, since the prescribed product values give \(Q_-(b_i)=c_iD'(b_i)/z_i\) for the complementary factor and the two cubic evaluations obey the Euler systems.
Thus each curve component determines one orbit of triples under the geometric splitting group.

Near the parameter infinity, each generic root approaches one pole with nonzero first displacement in \(s=1/\lambda\), by simplicity of \(D\) and \(S_0(b_i)\ne0\).
Consequently the order of \(z_i\) is \({\bf1}_{i\in I}-{\bf1}_{0\in I}\), accounting for the scale at \(b_0\).
Moving the place around its Galois orbit gives all triples on the chosen component.
If the group is primitive, it is transitive on pairs because a proper edge orbit or its complement would give a regular graph of degree one or two.
The edge matching, the antipodes of a six-cycle, or the two triangle components would each furnish a block system.
For a triple orbit of size \(h\), double counting gives \(h/2\) incidences per vertex and \(h/5\) per pair.
The incidence Gram matrix therefore acts by \(3h/10>0\) on the sum-zero subspace.
A constant character would give, on adjoining the coefficient at \(0\) making the sum zero, a vector perpendicular to every triple indicator by the order calculation.
It must be the zero vector.

The box count gives a nonzero \(v\) with \(b\cdot v=0\) and \(\|v\|_\infty\le\lceil3W^{1/4}\rceil\), including when the \(b_i\) have mixed signs.
The hypersurface \(z^v=1\) meets the component properly at the contact point and has degree at most \(5\|v\|_\infty\) after clearing monomials.
The field of definition of a geometric component has absolutely bounded degree by the number of components of the rational equations.
Bézout bounds the number of conjugates of the contact point over this field by the product of the geometric component degree and the hypersurface degree.
An isolated component has bounded point degree directly.
The coordinates generate \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) by the exponent gcd, proving the claimed uniform bound in the primitive case.

A nontrivial rational decomposition over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) groups the generic fiber by its inner values, so the group is imprimitive with blocks of size two or three.
For a size-two block the stabilizer of a generic root fixes its mate, which hence belongs to the root field \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)\).
The equivariant pairing gives a nonidentity rational involution, necessarily Möbius, and specializing at infinity identifies its three pairs of rational poles.
Their rational images determine the map over \(\mathbb Q\).
The residue of \(f\circ\tau\) at \(b_i\) is \(\rho_j/\tau'(b_i)\).
Conversely the stated residue equalities cancel all principal parts on the projective line, and the involution negates the resulting constant difference, forcing zero.
The sum or the product of \(T,\tau(T)\) gives a nonconstant invariant of degree two, generating the fixed field and yielding a decomposition.

For a size-three block, \(u=D_A/D_B\) can have a zero or pole at a generic root only over parameter infinity, since its zeros and poles are among the six poles of \(f\) and \(u(\infty)=1\).
At each place there, the roots in a common block specialize to the same pole block, each with order one in the local parameter.
The ratio of their \(u\)-values has divisor zero on the complete splitting curve and is constant.
Its leading coefficient at the chosen place is real by the simple-root expansions.
The block stabilizer acts transitively, multiplying the three values by a common finite-order real scalar because it fixes their constant ratios.
The scalar must be \(1\), since negation cannot preserve an odd multiset of nonzero field elements in characteristic zero.
Thus at least three embeddings of the generic root field agree on \(f,u\), while the degree over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(u)\) is exactly three.
This gives \(f=V(u)\) of outer degree two.
All zeros and poles of \(u\) are simple, and they account for all six simple poles of \(f\), so \(V(s)=as+b+c/s\) with \(ac\ne0\).
Evaluating the cleared numerator at the poles gives the two asserted constants, and conversely these six evaluations leave only a scalar multiple of \(D_AD_B\) in the degree-at-most-six remainder.
This proves the triple test in both directions.

There are \(6!/(2^3 3!)=15\) matchings and \(\binom63/2=10\) unordered partitions into triples.
For a triple decomposition, assigning an entire cubic \(D_A-sD_B\) to \(Q_+\) at the stated parameter would give equal \(z_i^2\) on the three distinct exponents in \(A\), contrary to nontorsion.
Successful decomposition by itself leaves existence of the original contact open.
