# Review of the quantitative lower bound for mixtures of large records

<a id="finding"></a>
## A bounded stabilizer orbit or an independent conjugate over a controlled extension

At 2026-11-25T01:09 UTC an agent outside the development of v103 independently reviewed the [full statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#statement), all three proof sections and the cited input interfaces for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed version without its development discussion.
The measure-product estimate holds with an absolute positive constant and a sufficiently large source degree depending on the stated ceiling.
It applies to arbitrary torsion twists and radical denominators in the indicated families, including one active noncoordinate class and ties in the replacement setting.

**Compression and field degrees.**
Strict first attainment makes the original roots distinct modulo torsion, since otherwise class compression would attain the same measure in smaller degree.
The two specified block representations are absolutely irreducible by the separate coordinate signs or the independent alternating actions on the individual sum-zero spaces.
Unmatched bounded inputs of sufficiently large compressed degree have distinct kernel fields and hence nonisomorphic representations.
On the finite active set this gives a direct sum, and the orbit span of the class of \(x\) contains each entire active summand by multiplicity one.
Its kernel field is the compositum \(L\) of those splitting fields.
The product \(\gamma\) over the class of \(x\) is in this field, preserves total measure and has one actual conjugate per class in its orbit.
Thus its degree \(D\), which can differ from \(\deg x\), is the relevant absolute degree in both height-product arguments.
Equivariance makes the orbit of its nonzero projection to \(V_a\) at most \(D\).
These uses of the field and direct-sum lemmas apply before any bound on the measure of \(x\).

**The case \(D\le d^6\).**
Here \(b=d/(2u)\) and \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\), so \(b^7/d^6\) tends uniformly to infinity at fixed \(M\).
Consequently both \(b^7\) and \(\binom b7\) eventually exceed \(d^6\).
The \(t\) occupied blocks give at least \(b^t\) projected vectors.
In the oriented case one can vary a nonempty proper level set independently in every occupied centered block.
In the signed case the label action gives at least \(b\) placements per partially supported block, and the even flips at full blocks give at least \(2^{b-1}\) distinct patterns independently after the partial supports are placed.
Thus there are at most six occupied blocks.
Within a signed block a support of size \(7\le j\le b-7\) already has at least \(\binom b7\) images, while \(j>b-7\) admits at least \(2^{j-1}\) sign patterns on the supported coordinates.
The latter eventually exceeds \(d^6\) by the same block-size estimate.
Within an oriented block either a level occurs at least \(b-6\) times or a union of level sets has size between \(7\) and \(b-7\).
For the latter assertion, in the absence of a single level in this interval all levels have size at most six, so a partial union first reaching seven has size at most twelve.
For sufficiently large \(b\) the second possibility would produce at least \(\binom b7\) vectors under supported alternating permutations.
The unique majority remains a majority when a block is transported with a sign change.

Hence the class stabilizer preserves a nonempty set of at most 36 labels, with at most 72 actual roots above them.
Choosing any marked root \(a'\) gives \([\mathbb Q(\gamma,a'):\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\le72\).
The excluded class line makes \(\gamma,a'\) multiplicatively independent.
With joint absolute degree at most \(72D\), AD99 Theorem 1.6 yields
\[
       m(x)m(a)=Dd\,h(\gamma)h(a')
          \ \ge\ \frac{c(2)d}{72(\log(216D))^{212}}.
\]
The exponent is \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\).
Since \(D\le d^6\), the last logarithm costs only an absolute factor relative to \(\log(3d)\).

**The case \(D>d^6\).**
Every other active input belongs to the unmatched base through the largest active degree \(d\), including other inputs tied with that degree.
Its surviving label action on \(a\) supplies a lift into \(G\) of a single supported three-cycle that fixes the other active fields pointwise.
The cube of the signed action lies in the elementary abelian label kernel, so the fourth power has order three, induces the same cycle and fixes both roots on each stationary label.
Every conjugate still fixes the other active splitting fields, since these are rationally normal.
One can bound the conjugacy class by \(2\cdot 2^3\binom{d/2}{3}\), counting a cyclic order and signs on a single triple.
Restriction to the active fields determines an automorphism of their compositum, so this bound is independent of the number of inputs.

If every conjugate of this order-three element preserved the rational line of \(\gamma\), it would act on the line by the identity, the only rational cube root of one.
Their normal closure would fix the projected vector and then every translate of that vector.
These translates span \(V_a\), while the chosen three-cycle moves coordinates in either specified representation.
An element \(\sigma\) in the conjugacy class therefore gives multiplicatively independent \(\gamma,\sigma\gamma\).
The stabilizer of \(\gamma\) takes \(\sigma\gamma\) through at most the size of this conjugacy class, giving joint degree at most \(C d^3 D\) for an absolute \(C\).
The two inputs now have equal absolute height \(m(x)/D\).
The AD99 bound implies \(m(x)^2\gg D/(d^3(\log(3D))^{212})\), since \(d^3<D^{1/2}\) and the extra constant costs only an absolute factor in the logarithm.
The function \(U/(\log(3U))^{212}\) is increasing for \(\log(3U)>212\).
Substituting \(d^6\) for \(D\) beyond this threshold gives \(m(x)\gg d^{3/2}/(\log(3d))^{106}\).
Voutier's Corollary 2 gives \(m(a)>2/(\log(3d))^3\), so the product in this regime already dominates the stated \(d/(\log(3d))^{212}\) bound.

On the coordinate lines the primitive unit in the compressed root field realizes the least positive measure there, and its positive powers give every multiple.
First attainment of a strict record forces that primitive measure to equal the record's own measure, since otherwise a smaller positive value would occur at its degree.
For the replacement hypothesis the primitive value is retained explicitly, and the torsion half products in the single nonreal exterior-pair example give precisely the claimed oriented module.
