## Common constituents, saturated generators and equality in the degree bound

At 2026-11-25T04:40 UTC an agent outside the development of [C0901 v156](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v156.md#statement) independently reviewed for Yin its statement and four proof sections.
The reviewer had previously inspected some of the chain and joint-module inputs but received v156 without development discussion.
The single-cutoff independence, the necessary and sufficient partition and degree assertions, and the conditional Lehmer consequences hold as stated.

Choose the absolute cutoff below \(\log2\) and small enough for the compressed chain and the two-field comparison at a fixed individual measure ceiling of one.
A primitive integer minimum of positive measure below this cutoff has leading and constant coefficients of absolute value one, hence represents a unit.
Each compressed chain term has log measure positive and no greater than the original one, so its degree exceeds all required fixed thresholds uniformly by bounded-degree finiteness.
This choice works simultaneously for arbitrarily many inputs: any claimed linear relation involves only finitely many, and the two-field comparison uses their individual bounds.
It does not require the log measure of their product or their summed log measures to be small.

For a single unit \(w\), compression preserves \(M(w)\) and the positive exterior product is a signed monomial in its conjugates.
Its class is nonzero because \(M(w)>1\).
At any stage strictly before its first nonzero leading projection, the projection of the current exterior product also vanishes, since the already accumulated exponent is a positive integer.
Such a stage must be oriented; in the independent signed case the leading summand is the entire space.
Vanishing on the sum-zero spaces makes the exterior coefficients constant within each block.
Then the exterior log mass equals the sum of absolute half-product logs, with each conjugate of the chosen half product repeated its common positive integral multiplicity.
Compression of those products changes no measure.
Iterating therefore gives the exact equality \(M(w)=M(z_i)^E\) with an integer \(E\ge1\), including \(E=1\) if the first stage is already active.
Only the lossless step of v109 is used here; the separate bounded-output matching hypothesis from that material is unnecessary.

In the common rational space modulo torsion a leading summand is faithful for the splitting group of its attached compressed term.
Isomorphic summands from different chains thus have the same kernel field, forcing matching at the uniform cutoff.
Conversely matching identifies the actual orbit spans \(V_z=V_{z'}\).
These spans are multiplicity-free, and their unique simple summand of largest dimension is leading in either chain, so that summand is the same actual subspace.
Distinct surviving summands are pairwise nonisomorphic and hence form a direct sum in each finite comparison.
The choices are consistent under enlarging the comparison family because the subspaces and Galois actions already belong to the ambient rational class space.
The signed roots on corresponding coordinate lines of two matched terms have the same class stabilizers and, by compression, these are their actual point stabilizers.
Their embedded root fields therefore agree after selecting corresponding conjugates, and their attached degrees agree as well.

The intersection of the rational line with the unit lattice modulo the finite group \(\mu(K)\) is a primitive rank-one lattice.
Its two generators and all their torsion representatives have the same absolute height.
For a nonzero multiple represented by any \(\zeta\rho^j\in K\), an automorphism fixing that value fixes the proportional class of the chosen compressed root and therefore the root itself.
Since the multiple already belongs to \(K\), it has full degree \([K:\mathbb Q]\), also after any further nonzero power.
It follows from degree times height that \(M(\zeta\rho^j)=M(\rho)^{|j|}\).
Thus different attached terms can have different total measures, but saturation in their common lattice always produces the same value \(a_W=M(\rho)\); conjugating the embedding preserves it.
This unit itself realizes \(a_W\), which is no larger than the original individual measure.

The class of \(a_W\) has a nonzero \(W\)-projection and its remaining components, if present, arise only at strictly lower attached degrees.
In a nontrivial proposed finite relation, choose one participating \(W\) of maximal attached degree.
The other classes at that degree have different leading spaces, and lower-degree terms cannot contain \(W\).
Projection leaves exactly the chosen coefficient times a nonzero vector.
This rules out all nontrivial rational class relations, hence all integer multiplicative relations among these positive real values, even for products exceeding the cutoff.

For a small realizable \(A\), factoring the input into primitive irreducibles expresses it in the \(a_W\)'s, with each positive factor contributing to just one.
Conversely every such power is realized by a single unit, and independence guarantees uniqueness of the entire exponent vector.
A decomposition of \(a_W\) inside the full Mahler monoid would have both factors strictly smaller, hence already represented below the cutoff, and would split its unique exponent one.
Every near-one product of two or more generators counted with multiplicity is decomposable.
Thus the identified generators are precisely the stated atoms.
A small single-number value must be supported at one atom since its irreducible minimum gives it; every one-atom power is a single-number value.
For each \(a\), any partition \(k_a=\sum j_i\) is realized by the minima of \(\rho_a^{j_i}\), with repetitions permitted.
The possible counts for each atom fill \(1,\ldots,k_a\), and these intervals add to the claimed full interval of factor counts.
Freeness for larger products is asserted inside the generated monoid only.

For any small irreducible input of measure \(a^k\), uniqueness identifies its first active generator with \(a\) and thus the attached degree with \(d_a\).
Torsion-class compression and subsequent chain descent give the lower bound \(d_a\) on the original degree.
Equality forces singleton initial torsion classes and no descent, since every nontrivial change on that route strictly lowers degree.
The original root, in a corresponding embedding, is then in the common root field on its saturated line and has form \(\zeta\rho_a^j\).
The actual equality of measures forces \(|j|=k\).
All listed twists conversely have full degree and the required measure, including inverse powers.
Any realizing polynomial needs at least one factor at each atom, so its degree is at least \(\sum_a d_a\).
Equality forces one factor at each atom with its full exponent and forbids any added positive-degree factor of measure one, including a power of \(X\).
The integer content has absolute value one by smallness.
Taking one indicated minimum per atom attains equality and accounts for every minimizer up to the overall sign.

If the unrestricted gap fails, the least constituent atom of any shrinking sequence is squeezed between one and its given measure, so infinitely many distinct atoms occur arbitrarily near one.
Choosing \(k_j\) distinct ones below the stated logarithmic threshold gives a product of log measure less than \(\delta_j\) and below the same cutoff.
Its unique expression has precisely \(k_j\) atoms, each of exponent one.
In the diagonal two-record construction of v155, after fixing the first record one can choose the second from an arbitrarily late tail with measure below the first record's atom.
Every atom behind a power is no larger than that power, so the second uses a different atom.
The additional tail condition preserves the height and angular estimates of the construction and makes a single-number preimage impossible for these products.
The conclusions need no assumption that the set of sufficiently small positive measures is nonempty.
