# Residual periods and the adjacent-coefficient exclusion

<a id="finding"></a>
## The period bound holds uniformly over the prime divisors of the coefficient sum

At 2026-11-21T16:42 UTC a separate agent acting for Sheng Yin completed an independent review of fixed C0345v872 without participating in its development.
The statement and both argument sections hold as submitted.

Passing to the primitive root parameter by the common power preserves the nontorsion unit condition, and the bounds of C0345v869 apply for every sign and dilation in the statement.
For each odd prime dividing \(2q+1\), the adjacent coefficients are nonzero and opposite up to their chosen signs modulo that prime.
The monic minimal polynomial retains its degree upon reduction.
Removing its greatest common divisor with the short binomial costs at most \(u\) degrees, and subtraction of irreducible-factor multiplicities leaves a divisor of the long binomial even when the two binomials share factors.

For a residue root of degree \(f\le D\), the dyadic part of \(p^f-1\) is the dyadic part of \(p-1\) for odd \(f\).
For even \(f\), its valuation is \(v_2(p-1)+v_2(p+1)+v_2(f)-1\), giving a dyadic part at most \((p+1)f\) since one adjacent even number has valuation one.
For a triadic exponent and \(p\ne3\), the sign-adjusted power has order a power of three.
The triadic part of \(p^f-1\) is \((p-1)_3 f_3\) when \(p\equiv1\pmod3\), and for \(p\equiv-1\pmod3\) it is one for odd \(f\) and \((p+1)_3 f_3\) for even \(f\), where subscripts denote prime parts.
Thus the maximum order over the residual roots is a common multiple bounded by \((p+1)D\) in both cases.
The triadic period is odd and preserves the required sign.
For distinct period and residue primes the long binomial and the shorter annihilator have identical root multiplicity \(p^{v_p(k)}\).
In characteristic three with triadic exponent, choosing the least power of three at least \(D\) supplies multiplicity at least \(D\) at every required root and costs less than \(3D\), including when three divides \(k\).

The nullity of multiplication by the annihilator modulo the reduced minimal polynomial equals their gcd degree even for inseparable reduction.
Smith normal form therefore gives the claimed divisibility of the integer norm, which is nonzero for a nontorsion parameter.
Summing the archimedean bound gives \(D\log2+kL m(\beta)\), with the same period used in the divisibility bound.
For \(k=1\), concavity reduces the exclusion to the endpoints \(3\) and \(2q+1\), whose stated lower bounds exceed \(0.371\) and \(0.773\), respectively, for all \(q\ge1024\).
For each fixed positive \(k\), the first endpoint tends to \(\log(3/2)\) and the second tends to infinity, uniformly over the choice of divisor prime and exponent index.
The resulting threshold may depend on \(k\).
