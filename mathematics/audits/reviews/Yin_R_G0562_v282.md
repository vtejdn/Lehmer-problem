## Mathematical finding

The complete statement and argument are correct for the stated rational, reducible polynomials.
The review covers all prescribed finite torsion tests, both discriminants, the root signature, the coefficient obstruction, the irreducible core and the limiting normalized discriminant.
The checks below are algebraic and analytic proof checks; no numerical computation or formal proof assistant is relied upon.

For fixed \(q\ge2\), the \(q\) roots of \(F_q\) are distinct and real, and \(F_q'(2)=q>0\).
Small positive perturbation by \(1/D\) therefore leaves \(q-1\) roots strictly in \((-2,2)\) and moves the endpoint root to \(T>2\).
The expansion \(T-2\sim1/(Dq)\), together with \(2\cosh a-2\sim a^2\), gives the asserted small positive \(a\).
Restricting to sufficiently large odd \(k\), with \(D=2k\), does not obstruct this choice.

Each target value defining \(h\) has exactly \(N\) simple preimages in \((-2,2)\).
For their lifted roots, \(z^{2kN}=1\), so divisibility of \(R\) by \(2kN\) gives \(C_R=2\) on the roots of \(h\).
The factors \(h\) and \(C_R\) are consequently coprime, and neither shares a root with \(A\), by algebraic integrality of their roots and nonintegrality of \(1/D\).
All \(n\) trace roots are simple, exactly one exceeds \(2\), and none is an endpoint.
Lifting produces exactly two positive real roots and \(n-1\) nonreal conjugate pairs on the unit circle, all simple.
The monic lift is reciprocal.

The decomposition \(S=F_qhC_R-D^{-1}hC_R\) locates the first nonintegral coefficient at depth \(q\).
In the lift, the coefficient at depth \(j\) is the depth-\(j\) coefficient of \(S\) plus an integer linear combination of coefficients at smaller depths.
Thus the first nonintegral depth remains exactly \(q\) in \(P\).

The reversed integral polynomial \(DF_q-1\) satisfies Eisenstein's criterion at \(2\): its leading coefficient is odd, all other coefficients are even, and its constant coefficient \(D\) has 2-adic valuation one.
It follows that \(A\) is irreducible of degree \(q\), and its root field is totally real.
Since \(q\ge2\), at least one real embedding takes \(T^2-4\) to a negative number.
Consequently \(T^2-4\) is not a square in that field, and the lift \(B\) is irreducible of degree \(2q\).
Its field has signature \((2,q-1)\).
The integral polynomial \(DB\) is primitive because its leading coefficient is \(D\) and its central coefficient is coprime to \(D\).
Its only exterior root is \(e^a\), proving \(\log M(DB)=\log D+a\).
Both complementary lifted factors are monic integral and have only roots of unity, so this is the unique noncyclotomic irreducible factor.

For every stipulated torsion trace \(x\), the exact values \(C_N(x)=C_R(x)=2\) and \(h(x)=k\) give \(S(x)=DF_q(x)-1\).
This is a nonzero algebraic integer, and multiplication by the unit \(\zeta^n\) preserves that property.
The product over primitive \(j\)-th roots is the field norm of \(P(\zeta)\), hence a nonzero integer.
It equals \(\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)\), since \(\deg P\) is even.
At the endpoints, \(F_q(-2)=4q(-1)^q\), \(n\equiv q\pmod2\), and therefore
\[
 P(1)=-1,\qquad P(-1)=4Dq-(-1)^q>0.
\]
These identities verify nonvanishing and integrality even when \(J=1\), independently of whether the finite torsion list includes \(-1\).

At the \(R\) cosine nodes, multiplication of the derivative magnitudes gives \(\operatorname{disc}C_R=2^{R-1}R^R\).
The sine product used here follows from \(\prod_{w^R=-1}|1-w|=2\), with \(|1-e^{2i\theta}|=2\sin\theta\).
The product discriminant formula gives precisely the three squared pairwise resultants in the Claim, including \(\operatorname{Res}(h,C_R)^2=2^{2t}\).
The possible denominator exponent is at most
\[
 e=2q-2+2t+2R\le4R-2<4R.
\]
As \(D^4\mid R\), one has \(D^{4R}\mid R^R\), so all these denominators are absorbed.
All trace roots are real and distinct, making the resulting discriminant a positive integer.
The exact signed reciprocal identity is
\[
 \operatorname{disc}P=S(2)S(-2)(\operatorname{disc}S)^2.
\]
It follows directly from the within-pair differences and the resultant \((x_i-x_j)^2\) between the two monic quadratics belonging to trace roots \(x_i,x_j\).
Its sign is \((-1)^{n-1}\), in agreement with the \(n-1\) nonreal conjugate pairs, and its absolute value is a nonzero integer as claimed.

Simultaneous recurrence on the torus applies to the angle vector multiplied by the fixed modulus \(2kND^4\).
It supplies arbitrarily large admissible \(R\) with every \(C_R(y_j)\to2\); the possible bounded-return case instead supplies an exact period and arbitrarily large multiples.
Meanwhile \(C_R(T)=2\cosh(Ra)\), so the logarithmic resultant is \(Ra+O(1)\).
With \(q,k,N\) fixed, division of the discriminant formula by \(n=R+q+t\) gives
\[
 \frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}P|}{2n}-\log(2n)\longrightarrow2a.
\]
The remaining error includes \(O((q+t)\log R/R)\), which tends to zero.
Choosing a sufficiently large admissible \(R\) separately for each successive \(q,J,a\) justifies the claimed diagonal sequence.

The comparisons with [the radial bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v280.md#radial) and [trace endpoints](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#relative) have the stated limited scope.
The examples provide no integral Salem minimal polynomial, and the polynomial discriminants of the reducible model do not become number-field discriminants.
In particular, the primitive integral core has the extra contribution \(\log D\) to its logarithmic Mahler measure.
No conclusion about a sequence of integral Salem polynomials of measure tending to one follows from these rational models.
