## The trace pairing, the rank loss and the Salem application

At 2026-11-16T20:49 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the statement, the three arguments and the declared dependencies of the C345v39 submission dated 2026-11-16T20:44.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and review scope without proof-development dialogue, author explanations, exploration notes or a previous verdict on this version.
The earlier reviews of C345v34 and C345v37 were consulted for their respective input theorems after checking their applications here.

In a totally real multiquadratic field the generating quadratic squareclasses are positive, and their squarefree representatives form a group when multiplication is followed by removal of square factors.
The parity of the valuation at two and the class of the odd part in \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^\times\) are homomorphisms, since removing the square of an odd common factor preserves that residue.
Their common kernel thus has dimension \(h\ge k-2\).
Each of its representatives \(d_0\) contains an even number of primes congruent to three modulo four, so the product of its signed prime factors equals the positive integer \(d_0\).
The square root of that product generates the same field for either choice of sign.
Distinct prime valuations give independence of the signed squareclasses and hence all \(2^{|\mathcal P|}\) sign changes, including when some of the quadratic factors are imaginary.

Each signed prime \(p^*\equiv1\bmod 4\) is squarefree, giving the asserted quadratic integral basis and discriminant.
In the compositum induction, linear disjointness identifies the relative trace pairing on the old field basis with its rational trace pairing, so the coefficients of an integral element in this basis become algebraic integers in the new quadratic field after multiplication by the old discriminant.
Exchanging the two fields shows that both discriminants annihilate the additive quotient by the product order.
Their coprimality forces that quotient to vanish, and the determinant of the product trace matrix has no prime factors beyond the previous primes and the newly adjoined prime.
This proves maximality of the order at every step.

Writing \(s_p=\sqrt{p^*}\), one has \(y_p\omega_p=(2+s_p+s_p^{-1})/4\), so the traces of \(y_p\) and \(y_p\omega_p\) are both one.
Independence of the sign changes then makes the trace pairing of \(Y\) with every basis product one.
For \(a\in\mathcal O_{F_W}\) transitivity gives \(\operatorname{Tr}_{F_W/\mathbb Q}(a y_W)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb Q}(aY)\in\mathbb Z\), with value one when \(a=1\).
In the sign group the subgroup fixing \(F_W\) is the annihilator of \(W\), so a nontrivial monomial in \(Y\) survives the subgroup sum precisely when its exponent vector lies in \(W\).
Its coefficient after tracing is \(2^{-s}\), and at every real embedding its radical factor has absolute value \(d_0^{-1/2}\).
The strict weight bound therefore gives positivity even when the signs of distinct terms are dependent.

The \(h\) independent coordinate projections on \(V\) give each subset of the selected primes exactly once.
All omitted prime factors can only decrease the reciprocal square-root weight, so the sum including the trivial class is at most \(\prod_{j=1}^h(1+p_j^{-1/2})\le\exp(2\sqrt h)\).
For \(0<c<h\) transitivity on nonzero vectors gives probability \((2^{h-c}-1)/(2^h-1)<2^{-c}\) of membership in a uniform subspace of dimension \(h-c\).
Linearity of expectation and \(3\log 2>2\) make the expected nontrivial weight strictly less than one.
When \(c=h\) the zero subspace already has nontrivial weight zero, including when \(h=0\).
The dimension in both cases is \(\max(0,h-\lceil3\sqrt h\rceil)\ge s_k\), since \(k-2\le h\le k\).
Thus \([F_W:\mathbb Q]\ge2^{s_k}\), and the case \(W=0\) gives the trace-one element \(1\) in \(\mathbb Q\).

For each \(a\in\mathcal O_K\), its relative trace lies in \(\mathcal O_{F_W}\), which puts \(y_W\) in the maximal-order trace dual of \(K\) and hence in the trace dual of its suborder \(\mathbb Z[T]\).
Every embedding of \(K\) restricts to a real embedding of \(F_W\), and the trace from \(K\) multiplies the original trace one by \(m/[F_W:\mathbb Q]\).
The same inverses chosen in C345v34 consequently satisfy both upper bounds in C345v39, the exact polynomial identity and the root limit.
In C345v37 the normalized trace bound applies to each positive integer multiplier making the distinguished pole residue integral and permits the multiplier to vary along the sequence.
The residue here is that of the same rational function in \(x\), and \(j>0\).
Dividing \(bj/m\ge97/70-o(1)\) by \(2^{s_k}j/m\le4+o(1)\) gives \(\liminf b\,2^{-s_k}\ge97/280\). [Factor-four construction review](Yin_R_G0562_v34.md#codifferent-branch), [integral residue review](Yin_R_G0562_v37.md#small-trace-branch).

Amoroso and Masser's Theorem 2.1 applies at rank one because \(\sigma>1\) is nontorsion and \(F/\mathbb Q\) is abelian.
The inclusion \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(T)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\sigma)\) gives the relative degree \(D=2m/2^k\), while the Salem conjugates give \(h(\sigma)=\log(\sigma)/(2m)\).
For a fixed \(\delta>0\) and \(2^k\ge m^\delta\), the resulting lower bound is \(c_\varepsilon 2^{-\varepsilon}m^{\delta(1+\varepsilon)-\varepsilon}\).
The exponent is positive for \(0<\varepsilon<\delta\), contradicting \(\log\sigma\to0\) along an infinite subsequence because bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure give only finitely many monic integral polynomials.
The review accepts the external height theorem at statement and application scope. [Amoroso and Masser, Theorem 2.1, printed pp. 1–2](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/c1e00263-b580-4cbd-9216-ab4b78154d72/Lehmer-galois-def.pdf#page=2).

The field assertion and its conditional Salem consequences pass independent review.
Existence of a sequence with \(\sigma\to1\) and \(k\to\infty\) remains open.
