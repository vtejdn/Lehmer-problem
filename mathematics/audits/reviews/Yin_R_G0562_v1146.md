## The two slice estimates hold with the same conditioning, and the tilted tail has the stated scale

At 2026-11-25T00:08 UTC an agent outside the development of [C0901 v86](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v86.md#statement) independently reviewed its whole fixed text for Yin.
The reviewer received the finished claim without its proof-development discussion.
The finite estimate and the asymptotic lower constant \(\pi/\sqrt{12}\) hold for the specified exponent lift.

The short-relation lift transfers the input to an \(r\)-dimensional polynomial of measure at most \(m(P)+\delta\).
The strict plastic cutoff makes all faces signed reciprocal and gives the vertex signs, the zonotope vertex bound, the parallelotope test and exclusion of zero excess at positive measure by v78.
If \(q<2^{r-1}\) the support has fewer than \(3\cdot2^{r-1}\) points, so its polytope is a parallelotope.
The edge-coordinate change after a translation is rational; clearing the common denominator gives a full-rank lattice homomorphism and its dual is surjective on the torus.
It preserves both Haar measure and distinct support points.
The square-face signed symmetry makes the products of opposite corner coefficients equal because those coefficients are unit signs.
Extra terms on the face do not affect this identity, including when \(r=2\).
Independence of the sign ratios on parallel coordinate edges then gives the entire factored corner polynomial.

For the first conditional estimate, order the occupied exponents in the chosen variable.
The second coefficient belongs solely to nonvertex terms since it is the first exponent strictly inside the box interval.
For almost every fixed value of the other variables the coefficient polynomials at all occupied levels are nonzero.
The sparse binomial coefficient bound follows by induction: differentiation eliminates the constant term and retains the coefficient \(n_j a_j\) at index \(j-1\), and reversing before differentiating retains \((b-n_j)a_j\) with the complementary binomial index.
The reversal has the same circle measure, and the derivative inequality is valid at complex coefficients.
Adding the two estimates and dividing by the positive degree \(b\) yields the claimed \(\binom{\ell-1}j\).
At the second coefficient this costs at most \(\log(q+1)\), since there are at most \(q+2\) levels.

Successive selections of a smaller extreme level in the resulting coefficient polynomial leave at most half its previous number of terms.
Used coordinates are constant on the remaining exponents, so another unused separating coordinate exists whenever more than one term remains.
After at most \(\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor\) such steps Jensen bounds the conditional integral below by a single integer coefficient's log modulus minus \(\log(q+1)\).
In the other selection, every surviving nonvertex point agrees at the already chosen endpoints and must therefore have an interior entry at an unused coordinate.
Selecting the cheaper endpoint there leaves at most \(\lfloor(a-1)/2\rfloor\) of \(a\) surviving points.
At most \(1+\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor\) coordinates suffice to clear them all.
Taking the union of the two coordinate sets and extending the endpoint choice arbitrarily preserves this clearing.
Every corner of the full box is occupied, so repeated extreme-coefficient Jensen bounds do recover the selected vertex product in the remaining variables.
Integrating the first lower bound over the coordinates added to its set leaves it valid for precisely the same conditioning as the second.
All exceptional vanishing sets are null; Fubini applies by logarithmic integrability of the nonzero Laurent polynomials.

Thus the conditional lower bound is the larger of \(-h\) and a sum of independent \(\log|1+Z|\) variables with mean zero, where \(h=\log(q+1)\).
For a centered sum \(S\), \(\mathbb E\max(S,-h)=\mathbb E(-S-h)_+\).
Adding independent mean-zero terms increases this last expectation by Jensen.
The size bound on the coordinate union therefore gives the printed \(d\), including \(d=0\).

For \(U=-\log|1+Z|\), the radially regularized logarithms converge in \(L^2\) by square summability of their Fourier coefficients; their pointwise limit away from the one singularity identifies the \(L^2\) limit.
Parseval gives mean zero and variance \(\frac12\sum n^{-2}=\pi^2/12\).
A simple logarithmic singularity permits an exponential moment of \(|U|\) at each parameter strictly below one.
The third-order remainder for the cumulant generating function and its first two derivative bounds are therefore uniform near zero.
Under the exponential tilt at \(t=(h+B\sqrt d)/(\sigma^2d)\), for \(h\le c\sqrt{d\log d}\) the error in the mean is \(O(\log d)\), and the variance is at most \(2\sigma^2d\).
The prescribed interval is at distance at least \((B-2)\sqrt d\) from either side of the mean for large \(d\), giving tilted probability at least one half by Chebyshev.
Undoing the tilt on this interval gives the factor \(\exp(d\psi(t)-t(h+2B\sqrt d))\).
Expanding it yields the leading term \(-h^2/(2\sigma^2 d)\) with error bounded by \(O(1+h/\sqrt d)\), since \(d|t|^3=o(1)\) uniformly.
The available gain \(\sqrt d\) then makes the expectation diverge uniformly whenever \(c<\sigma\).
A putative subsequence violating the liminf has \(q<2^{r-1}\) eventually and \(r-d=O(1+\log(q+1))=o(r)\).
Replacing \(r\log r\) by \(d\log d\) with a slightly larger constant still below \(\sigma\) produces the contradiction with the bounded left side of (2).
This gives the necessary excess for growing lifted dimension, with no optimality or unrestricted gap conclusion.
