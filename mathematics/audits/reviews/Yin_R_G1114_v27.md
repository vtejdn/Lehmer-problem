## Finding

The construction gives the stated complete norm at every divisor of \(d-2\), with both lists of unit norms holding for the same Salem number.
The original measures are bounded above by the golden ratio and can tend to it while the lengths of the lists tend to infinity.

On the circle \(A/z\) is purely imaginary and \(F/z=A/z-1\), giving \(|F|^2-|A|^2=1\).
The polynomial \(z^sF\) has \(s+1\) zeros strictly inside the disk, so Rouché gives that count for \(f_s\) and excludes boundary zeros.
Its signs at 1 and \(\phi\) put the remaining zero \(\theta_s\) in \((1,\phi)\).
Any nonconstant monic integer factor omitting this zero would have nonzero integral constant term of modulus less than one, since \(f_s(0)=-1\).
For any fixed \(1<t<\phi\), \(t^{-s}f_s(t)\to F(t)<0\), proving \(\theta_s\to\phi\).
Expansion gives \(g_s=1-z-z^2+z^s-z^{s+2}\), hence \(f_s+g_s=-z(1+z^s)\).
Applying the circle comparison to \(z^Nf_s+\lambda g_s\) for \(0<\lambda<1\) gives one strictly exterior root counted with multiplicity, and continuity permits at most one at \(\lambda=1\).
For fixed \(s\), the signs on either side of \(\theta_s\) in \((1,\phi]\) then force that exterior root to be real, simple, below \(\phi\) for all large \(N\), and convergent to \(\theta_s\).

For a torsion root of order \(t\), the three choices of conjugating exponent are units modulo \(t\) in their respective cases.
Their identities for \(G=-g_s/f_s\) use \(\xi^N=1\) and the absence of circle zeros of \(f_s\).
Since \(G(z)=1-z+O(z^2)\), \(G(\xi z^2)\) and \(G(z)^2\) have different linear coefficients, as do \(G(-z)\) and \(G(z)\).
Clearing denominators leaves a nonzero integer polynomial of degree at most \(2k(s+2)\), with \(k=1\) or \(2\).
It follows that \(\varphi(t)\le4(s+2)\).
The prime-power formula gives \(t\le2\varphi(t)^2\), so its odd part divides the chosen \(e\).
Its power of two is at most \(8(s+2)\le16s\), and therefore divides \(16s\), which is itself a power of two.
Thus \(t\mid N\), and the torsion roots of the numerator are precisely those satisfying \(\zeta^s=-1\), all primitive of order \(2s\).

At these roots \(f_s(\zeta)=-F(\zeta)+A(\zeta)=\zeta\), and differentiating \(D_N=(z^N-1)f_s-z(1+z^s)\) gives \(D_N'(\zeta)=N+s\).
Dividing by \(1+z^s\) therefore removes every torsion root exactly once and leaves a monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(N+2\) and constant term one.
Its exterior root and inverse belong to the same monic irreducible factor by the modulus of the constant term.
Any further factor would have only unit-circle roots and hence be cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
This proves irreducibility.
If an embedding over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\) sends \(\alpha\) to a conjugate \(\alpha'\), then \(|\alpha'|=\alpha\), and the unique exterior root forces \(\alpha'=\alpha\).
Separability gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) for every \(j\ge1\).

For \(n\mid N\) the absolute field norm is exactly the product of \(|S(\zeta)|\) over \(\zeta^n=1\), by interchanging the two monic resultant products.
At the ordinary nodes \(S(\zeta)=-\zeta\) has modulus one.
At each exceptional node the quotient derivative gives modulus \((N+s)/s\).
There are \(s\) such nodes when \(2s\mid n\) and none otherwise, proving the exact formula.
Since \(j\mid L\), \(je\mid N\), and \(v_2(je)=v_2(j)\le h\).
Also \(16j\mid L\) because its odd part divides \(j\) and its two-part divides \(16s\).
The equality \(v_2(N)=h+4\) uses \(K\le s\) and \(e\) odd, so \(v_2(N/(16j))=h-v_2(j)\le h\).
Both lists consequently use the unit case of the exact formula.
Degree preservation identifies the complete norm of \((\alpha^e)^j-1\) in the field of \(\alpha^e\) with \(C_{je}(\alpha)\).

For each fixed \(K,s\), the eligible \(e\)'s are unbounded odd multiples of a fixed odd integer.
Thus the root approximation and the bound \(s\log(1+N/s)/(N+2)<1/K\) can be enforced together with \(\log e\ge K\log L\).
The latter gives \(\log e/\log d\to1\), while \(e/d=1/(L+2/e)\to0\) for growing \(K\).
With \(s\) the least allowed power of two, \(s\to\infty\), so \(\theta_s\to\phi\) and \(\alpha\to\phi\).
The displayed bound controls the maximum over all divisors simultaneously, since they have only the two computed norm values.

The reviewer read Theorem 2 on p. 2 and the finite-list deduction across pp. 2–3 in [Zaïmi's preprint v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07723v2).
For each prescribed list, infinitely many even degrees each admit infinitely many Salem numbers satisfying the unit conditions, by taking the product of the prescribed exponents.
A bounded absolute height at a fixed degree bounds all coefficients of the monic minimal polynomial and allows only finitely many such numbers.
Thus the statement about arbitrarily large height at an indicated fixed degree follows.
The source comparison uses that preprint statement and its finite-list deduction, without a new review of its construction proof.
The exponents in L462 v28 depend explicitly on the original degree, and the complete original measures tend to \(\phi>1\).
The norms at \(d,2d,\ldots\) and the criterion allowing a freely chosen exponent in a linear range remain outside the divisor calculation.
