## Finding

A separate reviewer for Yin examined both sections of L462v46 at 2026-11-16T14:00 UTC, with the fixed proof and its cited mathematical inputs and without proof-development discussion or a previous verdict on this argument.
The ordering argument establishes the gap for measures with finite support, which suffices for the entire Pisot application.
Its statement for every finite positive measure on torsion traces requires a further justification of the finite-group hypothesis.

**Endpoint values.**
Each Laurent coefficient at infinity is an ordinary moment of \(\lambda\), including its total mass.
Conjugation symmetry of the lift and the integer recurrence for \(v^l+v^{-l}\) give integral doubled character moments, also when the trace is \(-2\).
Writing \(v+v^{-1}=y\) gives \(|1-v|^2=2-y\).
Thus the weighted lift has mass \(2s_n\), and the added atom has exactly mass \(k-2s_n\) since the original measure excludes \(2\).
Subtracting the \(l\)-th Fourier coefficient from \(k\) integrates \((2-v^l-v^{-l})/|1-v|^2\) against the lifted measure.
This ratio is the squared modulus of the geometric sum of length \(l\).
Its integral is an integer by pairing opposite exponents and using the integer mass for the constant term.
Negative indices then follow by symmetry.
The same integer \(k\) works throughout a decreasing sequence of finite endpoint values, and the identity atom increases strictly as those values decrease.
When each support is finite, it is contained in a finite cyclic group by taking a common multiple of its orders, and the claimed contradiction follows.
Successively taking values closer to \(a\) from above proves the stated right-hand gap.

In the general statement a finite positive measure specifies finite mass.
A countable set of torsion traces can carry a finite measure with infinitely many atoms.
At the point where the argument invokes C345v6, the common finite order for the support of each measure has yet to be established from the additional moment integrality.
The integrality computation alone in the displayed proof mentions no such support consequence.
The issue concerns the scope of this invocation, with no counterexample to the stated endpoint order identified.

**Rank-one perturbation.**
The poles \(b_1<\cdots<b_m\) of \(r=V/B\) have positive residues, satisfy \(b_1>-2\), and only \(b_m\) exceeds \(2\).
There is a unique solution of \(r=-1\) on \((-\infty,b_1)\), by the limits \(0^-\) and \(-\infty\).
Each successive gap contains one solution by the limits \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\), and \(r>0\) to the right of the last pole.
The solutions are simple by \(r'<0\).
They exhaust the roots of the monic \(B+V\), with coprimality excluding cancellations at poles.
The inequality \(r(-2)\ge-1\) puts the first solution at or above \(-2\).
If \(q-1>t\), then \(r(2)=-(q-1)/t<-1\) puts the last solution strictly below \(2\).
For \(m=1\), both endpoint comparisons are made on the same interval \((-\infty,b_1)\), so they put the sole solution in \([-2,2)\).

The monic circle lift has nonzero constant and all roots on the circle, including a possible double root at \(-1\).
Kronecker's theorem puts each zero of \(B+V\) on the torsion trace set.
The residue of \(S=1-1/(1+r)\) there is \(-1/r'>0\).
Its strictly proper expansion has integer coefficients by polynomial division at infinity, so this transform belongs to the finite-support class checked above.
Its value at \(2\) is \(1+t/(q-1-t)\).
The gap above one gives \(q-1\le (1+1/\epsilon_1)t\), including the trivial range \(q-1\le t\).
The exact branch identity with bounded derivative implies \(q\to\infty\) and \(j/(q-1)\to0\) along \(\theta-q\to0\), even for varying \(j\).
Since \(t\le j\), inequality (1) then eventually forces \(-C(-2)/B(-2)>t\).

**Simplex bound.**
Here \(F(-2)=-4-j/(q-1)+4E<0\).
Multiplication by \(-F(-2)>0\) converts \(-j/F(-2)>t\) into \(F(-2)>-j/t=-c\).
Substitution gives exactly \(E>1-c/4+j/[4(q-1)]\).
For \(j=c=2\) the right side is \(1/2+1/[2(q-1)]\), exceeding \(E_*/2=1/2+1/[2(q^2-1)]\).
The difference between \(E_*\) itself and the lower bound is \((c-j/(q+1))/4>0\) when \(q-1>Kt\), so the odd-degree equality case satisfies the same strict bound.
