The reviewer received the fixed argument without its development discussion or a previous verdict and took no part in developing it.

Uniformly in the prescribed short range, \(s=(\ell-u)/u\to\infty\).
The winding and annulus arguments of [v760](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v760.md#winding) apply to the polynomial itself for either sign and any integer \(B\ge2\), independently of the separate residue generation hypothesis there.
There are exactly \(u\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity, and each has \(u\log|z|=\log B+O_B(e^{-c_Bs})\).
Thus for a monic factor \(S\), \(u m(S)\) equals \(j\log B\) with error \(O_B(je^{-c_Bs})\), including \(j=0\).
The two coefficient blocks have no common root since \(B^2\ne1\); the first is monic, the second has unit constant coefficient, and both have length \(B+1\).
On the circle the two moduli agree, with logarithmic mean \(\log B\), so [v194, equation (1)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#statement) yields the claimed bound on \(v m(S)-d\log B\).
Combining the two estimates costs the extra \(j(u-1)\) when replacing \(v\) by \(\ell-1\).
Since \(d\le\ell\) and \(j\le u\), the full integer discrepancy is at most \(\sqrt{\ell}\exp(O_{B,\gamma}((\log\ell)^\gamma))\) uniformly in every allowed \(u\) and both signs.

The prime generator chosen from \(B\) is a nonsquare integer different from \(\pm1\).
The unconditional rank-one statement of [Pappalardi](../literature/Pap96/Yin_U001.md#rank-one-orders), with its admissible slowly increasing function, gives a logarithmic gain over the square-root scale of \(\exp(((1-\log2)/2)L-L^{3/4})\), where \(L=\log\log\ell\).
For \(\gamma<\delta<(1-\log2)/2\) this exceeds \((\log\ell)^\delta\) eventually and hence dominates the logarithm of the discrepancy factor, uniformly in the short range.
Deleting the density-zero exceptions and the prime divisors of \(2B\) works simultaneously for all directions.
For odd \(\ell\), the opposite parities of \(u,v\) give \(F(\epsilon)=0\).
At every \(q\mid B\) the reduction after division by this linear factor is a separable primitive root grid, of order \(\ell\) for the plus sign or characteristic two and of order \(2\ell\) otherwise.
For odd \(q\) its orders modulo \(2\ell\) and \(\ell\) agree.
Each monic factor therefore has degree a multiple of all these orders, and their least common multiple is the size \(M_\ell\) of the generated subgroup in the cyclic multiplicative group.
The discrepancy is a multiple of \(M_\ell\) of strictly smaller absolute value and so vanishes.
Writing \(g=\gcd(u,I_\ell)\), integrality of \(d/M_\ell=jI_\ell/u\) gives \(u/g\mid j\).
Positivity of \(d\) gives \(j>0\), so no factor of \(P\) is cyclotomic and all multiplicities contribute to the sum \(\sum t=g\).
This yields the exact degree allocation and measure estimates, including irreducibility when \(g=1\).
Positive powers of a Salem number are Salem by uniqueness of its exterior conjugate and preservation of its degree, so any Salem factor in any dilation would force \(j=1\) downstairs and hence \(u\mid I_\ell\).

Every noncyclotomic factor after dilation lies above a root of \(P\), since the dilation of the linear factor contributes only roots of unity.
The length of the section \(F\) is at most \(2(B+1)\), so the [complex radical theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) gives the stated denominator \(\gcd(k,R_B)\) for every branch and irreducibility at coprime dilations whenever \(P\) is irreducible.
The exponential measure error is eventually less than \((\log B)/2\) uniformly in the selected directions.
On the separate [three-generator prime set](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v760.md#statement), every positive direction and both signs have an irreducible \(P\) of measure at least \(\log(B/4)\).
The same length argument therefore applies to all its dilations without a slope or signature requirement; the presence of three distinct numerator primes makes this lower bound positive.

For the one-form sieve, divisibility classes for an lcm coprime to \(n\) have counting error \(O(1)\) in the \(m\)-interval.
Squarefree weights on the divisor-closed truncated set give the displayed diagonal form since \(\sum_{d\mid(a,b)}\varphi(d)=(a,b)\).
Möbius inversion yields \(\lambda_1=1\) and \(|\lambda_a|\le a\) because the relevant sub-sum of \(1/\varphi(d)\) is at most \(G\).
The quadratic counting error is \(O(z^4)\), negligible relative to \(x/(\varphi(n)\log x)\) throughout the specified range.
Grouping the harmonic sum by radicals contributes at most \(1/\varphi(a)\) per possible radical \(a\), and removing prime factors of \(n\) costs at most \(n/\varphi(n)\).
These steps give (1) uniformly, with all sieved prime values larger than \(z\).
Relative density one among primes gives a density-one dyadic proportion by the prime number theorem.
Every \(\ell\) has the same number of short partners in either sample, so its exceptional contribution stays \(o(1)\).
The mean bound for \(u/\varphi(u)\) gives \(\sum_{U<u\le2U}1/\varphi(u)=O(1)\).
Summing the progression bound for integer short exponents thus leaves \(O(1/U)\) of all pairs in addition to the exceptional totals.
For prime short exponents \(u\), failure of the sufficient criterion requires \(u\mid I_\ell\), and each \(1/\varphi(u)=1/(u-1)\ll1/U\).
Dividing by the actual number of short primes gives the same proportion bound for both signs and all coprime dilations together.
