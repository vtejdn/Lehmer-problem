### Fixed points in the original action

The Galois group acts transitively on the \(p\) input pairs, so its permutation image is primitive and has order divisible by \(p\).
Cauchy's theorem supplies an element of order \(p\), necessarily a full cycle.
These are precisely the hypotheses needed for the recorded classification of groups missing \(A_p\), both for \(H\) and for any transitive subgroup \(L\) on the same labels.
The exceptional degrees are \(11\) and \(23\).
An affine involution in odd characteristic has slope \(-1\) and one fixed point.
For a nonidentity linear projectivity the distinct rational eigenspaces have total dimension at most \(h\), with each dimension at most \(h-1\).
The increment in the number of projective points upon increasing a dimension \(d\) to \(d+1\) is \(q^d\).
Concentrating the dimensions therefore bounds the count by the sum for dimensions \(h-1\) and \(1\), namely \(1+(p-1)/q\le(p+1)/2\).
This includes elements with only one rational eigenvalue and nontrivial Jordan blocks.

An involution with a nontrivial field component has \(q=t_0^2\), with fixed field of size \(t_0\).
If \(r\) independent fixed lines span all fixed lines, choose their representatives as a basis of that span.
After the first nonzero coordinate is normalized to one, each subsequent coordinate satisfies \(\sigma(x)=a x\) for some nonzero coefficient.
Two nonzero solutions have ratio in the fixed field, so there are at most \(t_0\) solutions including zero.
Summation over the position of the first coordinate gives at most \(\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}t_0^j\) lines.
Twice this sum is at most \(2+\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}q^j=p+1\), since \(2t_0^j\le q^j\) for each \(j\ge1\).
Thus \(p-2c>(p+1)/2\) excludes every alternative to \(A_p\le H\).
Applying the same argument inside any transitive \(L\) containing a conjugate of the original involution gives \(A_p\le L\).

### The action on the conjugates of each field power

For any \(w=y^j\in F\), its conjugates are closed under inversion by reciprocity, and nontorsion makes every pair distinct internally.
A pair fixed by conjugation is real or lies on the unit circle, according as conjugation fixes or exchanges its members.
Every moved pair contains exactly one nonreal exterior number.
Any embedding of \(\mathbb Q(w)\) extends to \(\mathbb Q(y)\), and a root lying over a nonreal exterior \(w'\) is itself nonreal and exterior.
Distinct \(w'\) yield distinct lifts even when several conjugates of \(y\) have the same power.

The abelian-power exclusion in v310 uses the strict cutoff \(M(y)<(3+\sqrt5)/2\) or the existence of a neutral conjugate of \(y\).
For either choice, the normalizer argument there shows that each order-\(p\) subgroup acts nontrivially on the rational span of the classes of the conjugates of this \(w\).
It acts nontrivially on the conjugates themselves and on their inverse pairs, since the group fixing all pairs setwise on those conjugates is a 2-group.
The kernel \(K\) on the pairs therefore has order prime to \(p\).
Its normal image in \(H\) has order prime to \(p\) as well, whereas every nontrivial normal subgroup of the faithful primitive image is transitive.
Hence \(\pi(K)=1\), and \(c>0\) ensures that complex conjugation acts nontrivially on the output pairs.

For a stabilizer \(J\) of one output pair, the map \(G/J\to H/\pi(J)\) has as fibers exactly the orbits of \(D=\ker\pi\).
Transitivity makes their sizes equal, and the abelian group \(D\) acts on each through its regular elementary abelian quotient.
The fiber stabilizer consequently acts by affine maps on that quotient.
There are three cases for \(L=\pi(J)\).

1. If \(L\) fixes an input label, the equivariant map onto the \(p\) labels either identifies the actions or has at least two points above every label, giving at least \(4c\) moved output pairs.
   If \(L\) is intransitive without a fixed point, a smallest orbit has size \(2\le k\le p/2\), and \(A_p\) is transitive on the \(k\)-subsets.
   The \(2\binom{p-2}{k-1}\ge2(p-2)\) subsets containing exactly one member of a chosen transposition are all moved.
2. A transitive \(L\) missing \(A_p\) contains no conjugate of \(t=\pi(\tau)\), so \(t\) moves every coset.
   The coset action is faithful because a normal subgroup of \(H\) missing \(A_p\) intersects the simple \(A_p\) trivially and then centralizes it, and the centralizer in \(S_p\) is trivial.
   A faithful image containing an element of order \(p\) requires at least \(p\) cosets.
3. For \(L\supseteq A_p\) there are at most two fibers of size \(s=2^a\).
   An order-\(p\) element preserves each fiber and acts nontrivially on one, so \(p\) divides \(|\operatorname{AGL}_a(2)|\).
   Counting ordered bases yields \(p\mid 2^i-1\) for some \(1\le i\le a\), hence \(s\ge p+1\).
   Complex conjugation either exchanges the two fibers or acts nontrivially within at least one fiber.
   In a preserved fiber a nonidentity affine map fixes at most \(s/2\) points, since its fixed set is empty or a proper affine subspace.

These counts give at least \(B=\min(4c,(p+1)/2)\) moved output pairs in every case except the original action.
There the count is exactly \(2c<B\), since \(0<c<(p-1)/4\).
If the total exterior count of \(y\) is below \(B\), the lifting argument forces the original action for every positive power in \(F\).
The count of \(p\) two-member inverse pairs gives degree \(2p\) for each such power.

### Mixed signs, products and the prime families

The fixed input labels carry both signs when \(R,N>0\).
There are more than \((p+1)/2\) fixed labels, so a three-cycle can change the distribution of these signs while leaving a fourth label fixed.
Its signed lift commutes with conjugation on unsigned labels.
On fixed labels the signed commutator compares the original sign to its permuted value, since the lift's own two sign factors cancel.
The commutator thus belongs to \(D\) and has a nonconstant sign vector.
Adding its translate by a three-cycle on two equal entries and one unequal entry produces a weight-two vector.
Transitivity of \(A_p\) on unordered pairs gives every weight-two vector, and these span the even sign subgroup.

After raising a root-generated radical to a positive power that clears the torsion factor, its monomial has a nonzero integral exponent vector \(a\).
The independence in v306 identifies equality of monomials even modulo torsion with equality of exponent vectors.
The coordinate characters on the even sign subgroup are distinct because a sign change in coordinates \(i,k\), with \(k\) distinct from \(i,j\), distinguishes \(i\) from \(j\).
Their rational character projections isolate the occupied coordinates of \(a\), and transitivity brings every coordinate line into the orbit span.
An order-\(p\) element cycles these lines, giving the required nontrivial action on the orbit and hence on its inverse pairs for reciprocal outputs, without a bound on measure.
For an output below \(B\), the original action forces \(D\) to fix each pair, so the line through \(a\) affords a single character of the even sign subgroup.
Distinctness of the coordinate characters leaves exactly one occupied coordinate.
Independence and nontorsion ensure that the \(2p\) input roots retain distinct \(k\)-th powers for every nonzero integer \(k\).
These powers have \(e\) exterior conjugates, including negative powers by reciprocity.
Lifting all exterior conjugates gives the lower bound \(e\) below the threshold, and the other outputs have at least \(B>e\).
An input root attains equality.

The five-term family has \(R=1\), \(2c=e_p-1=p/3+O(1)\) and \(N=p-e_p>0\) eventually.
For the seven-term family the corresponding ratios along irreducible primes are \(2c/p\to\delta\) and \(N/p\to1-\delta\), with \(0<\delta<1/2\).
In both cases \(c\) tends to infinity, \(c<(p-1)/4\) and \(2c+1\) is eventually smaller than both \(4c\) and \((p+1)/2\).
Both measures are eventually below the plastic number, so Smyth's theorem applies to every nontorsion output of no greater measure and supplies reciprocity.
Radicals of units are units, since each and its inverse satisfy monic equations over the algebraic integers.
The integers between the ambient lower bound \(2c\) and the attainable upper bound \(e_p=2c+1\) are precisely the two values stated.
The existence of an infinite irreducible seven-term subsequence and the choice between the two possible ambient minima remain open in this argument.
The review used the recorded theorem statements of Jones and Smyth, and the existing family results at the indicated interfaces.
It did not reprove the published classification or re-audit the original literature proofs.
