## Both finite tests count the stated reciprocal elements of the divisible group

At 2026-11-24T22:42 UTC an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed all four sections of the fixed claim for Yin.

The degree estimate, the two norm searches and the rational-subspace alternative hold at their stated scopes.

Take a common normal closure and let \(T\) map the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^d\) to the multiplicative classes of the \(d\) conjugates of \(x\), retaining individual embeddings even when classes repeat.
The kernel is invariant under the permutation action.
Transporting the inner product from its orthogonal complement gives \(TT^*=I\) on the quotient, and the induced action is orthogonal.
Transitivity and \(\operatorname{tr}(TT^*)=\dim V_x\) thus give squared norm \(\dim V_x/d\) at every one of the \(d\) vectors.
For \(P=(1+c)/2\) the fixed-space identity from v62 gives rank \(r(x)\), since products with their ordinary complex conjugates are positive real and their multiplicative relations are exactly the rational relations of their logs.
The projected vector of a root vanishes precisely on the circle, as that positive product is torsion precisely when it is one.
Reciprocity gives exactly \(2l(x)\) nonneutral roots.
Taking \(\operatorname{tr}(PTT^*)\) now gives the first inequality, and powering an element of \(D(w)\) puts its conjugate classes in \(V_w\).
Nontorsion gives \(r(x)\ge1\) by the unit hypothesis.
The product of all conjugates of \(w\) is a sign, and in the reciprocal case the inverse labels further bound the dimension by half its degree.

For each retained root \(a\), its inversion \(\tau\) is a field automorphism, and \(v=N_{K(w)/K}(w)\) is a unit.
The quotient \(v/\tau v\) is norm one and lies in the multiplicative group of conjugates of \(w\).
At every neutral inverse pair of \(a\), the two embeddings on \(K\) are complex conjugate, so the quotient has modulus one there.
At most one embedding per remaining inverse pair takes it outside the circle.
If nontorsion it consequently supplies a witness irrespective of whether \(a\) itself belonged to \(D(w)\).
In the opposite direction the bounded-measure transfer from v59 gives a witness \(x\) of measure at most \(2k\,m(w)\).
The degree bound places \(x\) in the enumeration, and pairing its monomial relation with its own log function produces a nonzero correlation with a conjugate of \(w\).
The conditional norm formula then makes one of the quotients for a conjugate of this \(x\) nontorsion.
Thus restricting to the stated finite box loses no positive answer, while a slightly larger computable coefficient box is harmless when the signature conditions and norm test are retained.

For a complex Salem input the two exterior roots and their inverses lie above the one complex place of its trace field.
A nontorsion relative norm-one unit in its root field therefore has exterior values forming either a nonreal conjugate pair or a real value counted twice among the root-field embeddings.
In the latter case its minimum has one distinct real exterior root and a neutral root, and adjusting the sign gives a genuine real Salem value \(\beta>1\).
The two square roots of \(-\beta\) form the nonreal exterior pair of the quadratic lift.
This doubles the degree since the positive real field \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) has no such square root, and the lift has the same total measure, with the neutral conjugates preserved.
Both operations keep the output in \(D(w)\).
The unrestricted norm count in v59 for \(v/\tau v\) supplies \(4m(w)\).
At the v59 threshold for \(k=2\), the norm itself is relative norm one and the single-sum count supplies \(2m(w)\).
The resulting complex Salem witness has degree at most \(4b\) by the first part, including when it is obtained by the quadratic lift.
The same nonzero-correlation selection on a bounded complex Salem witness gives a retained input with a passing quotient.
Irreducibility, the specified root counts and the root-of-unity test can all be checked with algebraic numbers, and the usual monic coefficient bounds give a finite set at the indicated degree and measure.

For the arrangement test, any rational vector \(q\in V_w\) can be cleared to a multiplicative class of an actual monomial in the splitting field.
All torsion differences there lie in its finite group of roots of unity.
Raising to the prescribed common multiple makes class equality into actual equality of conjugates, opposite classes into actual inverse conjugates, and \(c\)-fixed classes into real values.
The class condition \((1+c)gq=0\) gives a positive real torsion product of the root and its conjugate, hence modulus one.
Conversely each actual equality, inversion, real value or neutral value gives its indicated class condition.
There are \(|G|/|H(q)|\) distinct values after powering, and \(Z(q),R(q)\) are unions of the corresponding stabilizer cosets.
With an inverse and a neutral conjugate present, exactly half the nonneutral values are exterior and half the real values are exterior.
A real neutral conjugate would be \(1\) or \(-1\), impossible for a nontorsion element.
These observations verify both counts in (2).

Every witness from a rational pattern belongs to \(D(w)\), while any reciprocal neutral witness in \(D(w)\) can be powered into the monomial group and then powered further without increasing its count of distinct exterior roots.
Reciprocity and a neutral conjugate persist, giving equality of the two minima, also for an empty set.
For the complex Salem test the only possible collapse of the two nonreal exterior values on powering is to the same real value, since they are complex conjugates.
Conversely two exterior values and zero real exterior values give a complex pair, while one exterior value gives a signed real Salem number and the indicated lift.
For each nonzero rational point, intersecting all defining subspaces containing it identifies its incidence pattern among the four families.
That intersection has the same pattern on its complement of finitely many proper subspaces and zero, which contains a rational point.
This gives the finite arrangement procedure conditional on the rational action and complex conjugation being supplied, separately from the norm search that needs no unit-group basis.
