## Sharp cost with all prescribed contacts

On 2026-11-20 a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the open decision `power-schedules-submission` and its arguments independently of their development.
I find the fixed-seed assertion and the unrestricted limit proved, including the full interval of squares.
The earlier separated-block theorem imposes restrictions on exterior labels, and the ordinary short-interval theorem assumes vanishing relative interval length for its spectral conclusion.
Here both the arbitrary ties and the restoration of all contacts across a fixed relative interval are justified for powers of fixed integer degree at least two.

For any pair of labels the mean value bound gives a possible integer partner within a constant times \(N^{1-d}\log N\) of \(\alpha n\).
For rational \(\alpha\) a nonzero discrepancy is bounded below by its denominator's reciprocal.
For irrational \(\alpha\), the number of indices in the full interval is bounded above by the count up to \(CN\) in any fixed neighborhood of the integers, so the upper density tends to zero with the neighborhood length.
At most one partner occurs per index eventually.
Finitely many label pairs therefore cost \(o(N)\) indices even after marking both ends, uniformly over the chosen subsets.

Each exact tie pattern determines its primitive index ratios, and every subset of every possible tie is among finitely many such patterns.
An equality between two phase coordinates at the same depth would equate powers of conjugates, whose common positive height first forces equal exponents and whose torsion-free pairwise quotients then force equal labels.
Consequently the leading coefficient of the cleared determinant is nonzero at each sampled point, also on a zero-dimensional sampled component.
For the cyclic closure with component order \(K\), the closed subgroup generated by \(u^K\) is contained in the identity component and its \(K\) translates cover the closure, forcing equality with the identity component.
On each progression a nontrivial character therefore has irrational angle multiplied in its leading coefficient by the nonzero integer \(K^{d-1}\).
Weyl's criterion and fixed-lag differencing give the required component averages in every fixed degree.
The first nonzero determinant coefficient on the component and the squared Vandermonde have null zero sets there.
The upper density estimate for fixed closed sublevel sets implies the moving inverse-logarithm threshold with \(o(N)\) exceptions, since the relevant parameter ranges lie inside \([1,O(N)]\).
Bounded higher coefficients leave a determinant lower bound of order \(L^{-j}/\log(2L)\) for \(j\le s\), and fixed-size cofactors grow at most polylogarithmically.
This proves the claimed inverse bound simultaneously for all surviving subblocks.

With \(r=O(L)\), the product of the diagonal-block inverse norm and the off-block row norm is \(O(L^{s_0+1}rL^{-s_0-6})=o(1)\).
Reversing the weight costs \(\exp(O(L))\), and the nonzero integral endpoint gives the lower bound after losing at most \(O(D_N L)\) exponent mass, also with one designated slot deleted.
Since \(r\ge\delta N\), the total loss parameter satisfies \(D_N=o(r)\).
In particular \((1+D_N)/t_*\le\sqrt{(1+D_N)/r}=o(1)\), while \(t_*=o(r)\).
The expense of the higher jets remains negligible since \(t_*^2\log(2Lw)/(wt_*L)=o(1)\) by \(t_*=O(r)\), \(w\ge r\), and polynomial growth of \(Lw\).
Conversion of the endpoint bound from one-norm to coefficient sup norm in packing adds at most \(\log(w+1)=o(t_*L)\).
These estimates preserve both the packing surplus and the designated-slot division contradiction.
Compression ensures that each powered minimum has full degree and hence measure \(M(P)^p\), so the integral combination and gcd removal preserve the double contact at every original slot.
