### Normalized local depths

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(A)\), and choose a finite Galois extension \(E/\mathbb Q\) containing \(A,B\) and their conjugates.
An automorphism fixing \(A^aB\) attains the maximal conjugate modulus on each factor, since \(a\ge1\) in late rows.
Strict dominance then forces it to fix \(A\) and \(B\), giving \(F=K(B)\).
The second exponent below \(2\) gives \(H\to\log 2\) by the accepted [norm asymptotic](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#statement).
The accepted [relative orbit bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits) gives \(n\to\infty\) even over the preceding splitting fields and hence over \(K\).
The reviewer used the respective [norm review](Yin_R_G0562_v1225.md#finding) and [orbit review](Yin_R_G0562_v1219.md#finding) at these interfaces.

The reviewer checked the two-image estimate directly because C0901 v200 contains no selected assessment.
For distinct \(K\)-images \(B_j,B_l\), an absolute automorphism taking \(B_j\) to \(B\) takes \(B_l\) to a conjugate of strictly smaller modulus.
Thus \(r=B_j/B_l\) is a nontorsion unit, and \(h(r)\le2h(B)\le2b\) since every conjugate of \(B\) has modulus at most \(B\).
At every finite place the minimum of the two translate valuations is bounded by the valuation of their difference \(A^aB_l(1-r)\), whose unit factor has valuation zero.
The normalized gcd mass is consequently at most \(L_1(r)=[E:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\log |N_{E/\mathbb Q}(1-r)|\).
The \(t=1\) bound of [C0345 v730](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) applies with height at most \(2b\), giving \(O(\sqrt{b\log(1/b)})\) uniformly in the pair and \(a\).
Here \(h\log(1/h)\) is increasing for all sufficiently small positive \(h\).
The [cyclic norm review](Yin_R_G0562_v870.md#finding) covers this bound.
No comparison between different rows or estimates for prime ideals with large relative local degree from v200 enter the argument.

For a fixed \(p\), a uniformly chosen embedding \(\tau:E\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) induces a place \(P\mid p\) with probability \(e_P f_P/[E:\mathbb Q]\).
As \(v_p(\tau x)=v_P(x)/e_P\), averaging \(\log p\) times the smaller of two transported depths gives the summand at \(p\) of their normalized ideal gcd.
For a single image the same formula gives its normalized norm after summation over \(p\), proving the first moment identity.
For fixed \(\tau\), the transports \(\tau(2-A^aB_j)\) exhaust all images over \(\tau|_K\) once each.
Two independent uniform indices therefore realize the squared conditional threshold fraction, including diagonal choices with probability \(1/n\).
Averaging \(\tau\) after choosing the indices and integrating the common threshold gives \(H/n\) on the diagonal and at most \((1-1/n)O(\sqrt{b\log(1/b)})\) off it after summation over \(p\).
Jensen bounds the squared marginal fraction by this conditional second moment before integration.
The nonzero integral translates have finite prime support, and \(b\to0\), \(n\to\infty\) and \(H\to\log2\) give \(u\to0\).

Retaining \(m\) deepest embeddings at \(p\) leaves normalized mass \(\log p\int (F_p-m/D)_+\,dt\), including at equal depths.
For late rows take \(m=\lceil D\sqrt u\rceil\) and use \((x-\rho)_+\le x^2/(4\rho)\) with \(\rho=\sqrt u>0\).
The total loss is at most \(\sqrt u/4\).
On the disjoint intervals \((0,c_p]\), Cauchy–Schwarz for the measure \(\log p\,dt\) bounds the capped mass by \((u\sum c_p\log p)^{1/2}\).
Taking \(c_p\) to be the largest depth at \(p\) counts all the mass on \(S\) and yields the claimed lower bound.

### The one-prime construction

The largest odd \(k\) in the construction satisfies \(0\le(n-\sqrt n)\log2-k\log3<2\log3\).
Thus \(k/n\to\log2/\log3\) and \(3^k/2^n\to0\).
Since \(3^k\equiv3\bmod4\), the digit integer is \(2^{n-2}-T\) with \(T=(1+3^k)/4\) a positive integer less than \(2^{n-17}\) for large \(n\).
Writing it as \((2^{n-2}-1)-(T-1)\) shows that every stated high bit is one.
The coefficient convention \(b_j2^{j-1}\) gives \(f(2)=2^n-1-4\sum b_j2^{j-1}=3^k\).
All the proposed moves have disjoint supports below \(n-3\) and consume available digits.
Their change \(2X^{j-1}(X-2)\) gives \(-2\) at \(1\), zero at \(2\) and \(2^j\equiv1\bmod3\) in the derivative at \(2\).
Among the integers from zero through five the necessary parity leaves three counts, which realize all three residues modulo three.
Consequently the constant term \(f(1)\) of the shift is twice an odd integer and \(f'(2)\) is a 3-adic unit.
Since \(n\) is a power of two, \(f(X+1)\equiv X^n\bmod2\), verifying every hypothesis of the [monic Eisenstein criterion](../literature/Con26d/Yin_U001.md#con26d-u001).
Irreducibility gives degree \(n\), and the constant coefficient \(-1\) makes the roots units.

The decreasing positive function \(U\) has \(U(1)>1\), \(U(2)<1\), so its unique positive solution \(U(\beta_n)=1\) satisfies \(1<\beta_n<2\).
For any root \(w\), the triangle inequality gives \(U(|w|)\ge1\).
Equality at \(|w|=\beta_n\) forces the phases of \(w^{n-2}\) and \(w^{n-3}\) to equal that of \(w^n\), because both coefficients stay positive.
The phase of \(w\) then has square and cube one, proving strict dominance.
The lower bound \(U(\beta_n)\ge(2/\beta_n)U(2)\) gives \(\beta_n\ge2(1-3^k/2^n)\) and the required convergence.

At \(2\in\mathbb Z_3\) the polynomial has valuation \(k>0\) and its derivative valuation zero.
The established [Hensel estimate](../literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001) applies in \(\mathbb Q_3\) and gives a root with distance of valuation \(k\) from \(2\).
Reduction of the product of the \(n\) monic integral linear factors in a local splitting field shows that a second such residue would give multiplicity at least two in \(\bar f\), contradicting \(\bar f'(2)\ne0\).
The unique corresponding completion is \(\mathbb Q_3\), since the embedded primitive element lies there.
The norm \(f(2)=3^k\) and integrality exclude positive depths at any other rational prime.
Applying this simple-residue argument at each prime of the normal closure above three gives coprimality of every pair of distinct conjugate translates there.
The construction imposes no adjacent-record factorization and thus leaves the gap question open.
