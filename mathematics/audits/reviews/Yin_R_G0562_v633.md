# Independent review of the computable width for the unrestricted prime-pair test

<a id="finding"></a>
## Relative estimates, row coverage and the full-width comparison

On November 20, 2026, an agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the complete [growing-width claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#statement) without participating in its development.
The conclusions follow from the stated fixed-seed estimates and prime-pair criterion.
This review checks their application here and the new uniformization argument.

For fixed seeds, a loss of degree requires an internal quotient of distinct conjugates whose order divides the prime exponent.
The finitely many internal quotients exclude only finitely many primes.
Equality of powers across lists forces a fixed ratio of positive absolute heights, which prescribes at most one ordered pair of distinct primes.
In the mixed-seed case [v497](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#statement) gives the two-sided estimate for the integer quotient since the distinct prime exponents have gcd one.
The estimate is independent of the existence of a row, as its ingredients concern the powered resultant and the two indices alone.
In the repeated-seed case the logarithm of the numerator equals \(4\log|BD|-2\log|D|\).
The [clipped correction estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#rate) bounds its logarithmic gcd loss by \(o_P(p+q)\), while the complete and diagonal logarithms equal \(F+O_P(\log(2pq))\) and \((q-p)\log M(P)+O_P(\log(2+q-p))\), respectively.
These estimates apply also when \(q-p\) stays bounded.
For the same seed of degree \(e\) and log measure \(h\), one has \(\Gamma\ge [4eq-2(q-p)]h\ge 2(p+q)h\).
For mixed seeds the lower bound \(F\ge (\deg P_1)q\log M(P_2)\) likewise gives a positive lower bound on \(\Gamma/(p+q)\).
Thus both relative errors tend uniformly to zero over partners of the fixed seeds.

The coefficient bound enumerates every possible minimum because each product in an elementary symmetric coefficient is bounded in modulus by the full Mahler measure.
Each root modulus is the positive algebraic square root of the product of the root and its complex conjugate.
Real algebraic comparison therefore decides each measure bound and each maximum in \(\exp F\), including equality.
For equal seeds \(\exp\Gamma=(\exp F)^4/M(P)^{2(q-p)}\), and the diagonal product can be computed as the field norm of \(a^p-a^q\).
After verifying degrees and disjointness, all divisors, indices and denominators in the comparison are defined and nonzero.
Taking logarithms of the two inclusive algebraic comparisons gives exactly the requested relative inequalities at that tested integer \(j\).
Each fixed \(j\) eventually satisfies \(j\le p\) and passes simultaneously for its finite seed list, including a vacuous list.
The maximum passing integer therefore tends to infinity without an effective bound on the eventual onset.

For an actual row of width \(b(p)\), [the tangency inequalities](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) count distinct prime-factor incidences, so selecting one factor in each slot costs at most \(b(p)\) in total degree even when the selected minima coincide.
The strict row threshold implies \(\log M(P_1)<(1+q/p)\log Q\le 3\log Q\) and \(\log M(P_2)<(1+p/q)\log Q<2\log Q\).
Every qualifying choice thus enters the selected enumeration, and repeated-factor contact and rational coprimality supply the hypotheses for its integer divisor.
Each Sylvester row has Euclidean norm at most \(H\), giving the asserted degree-cost inequality at the actual degrees.
The [bounded-partner criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#threshold) applies since \(1<Q<6/5\) and the supplied width tends to infinity.
Deleting the coincident partner changes the rejection count by at most one.

For a fixed hypothetical subcritical seed, the eventual width conditions \(b(p)\ge2e\) and \((1+1/b(p))h<\log Q\) hold simultaneously.
The upper estimate \(F\le e(p+q)h\) follows by summing positive parts separately, so the certified divisor lies strictly below the full permitted Hadamard budget for every partner eventually.
This establishes the stated limitation for the scalar comparison alone and leaves the existence of individual rows open.
