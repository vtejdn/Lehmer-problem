## Dyadic precision and the integral kernel

At 2026-11-19T01:57 UTC I checked the [congruence constructions](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v274.md#precision), the four contact equations, the rank and the lifting to integral kernel classes for both graph signs.
For every fixed monic \(F\) with unit constant term, \(X\) is a unit modulo \(2\) in the quotient by \(F\).
Squaring \(X^M\in1+2R\) exactly \(k\) times gives \(X^{Mn}-1\in 2^{k+1}R\).
The power \(s=\lceil q/(k+1)\rceil\) therefore supplies \(q\) digits for the row and its formal reversal, even when \(\bar F\) has repeated factors.
Its width \(Ms\) and its log norm are \(o(n)\) whenever \(q=o(n\log n)\).
The factorization of its graph into a power of a root-of-unity polynomial and a signed binomial excludes all nontorsion roots.
For the perturbed monic row, the added constant \(2^{q+1}\) exceeds \(2^s\), an upper bound for the original power on the closed disk.
Every zero of the reversed row is then inside the disk, and the quotient of the reversed by the original row has modulus at most one there.
The graph has no zeros in the open disk, and signed reversal excludes all zeros outside the circle.
For each of the two prescribed minima, a conjugate inside the disk gives nonvanishing of the numerator and a conjugate outside excludes graph divisibility.
The eventual strict budget follows from \(\log_2\|A\|_1\le q+2\) and \(\log b=o(n)\) when \(q\le(1/8-\eta)n\) for fixed positive \(\eta\) and \(b\ge Ms\).

The remaining assertions use squarefreeness of \(\bar P\) and the [identity \(\bar P=\bar Q\)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests).
In the unramified splitting field take the distinct lifts \(x,y\) on the same disk, \(r=y/x\), \(a=v(r-1)\), \(S=r^n\) and \(h=(S-1)/n\).
The value and logarithmic derivative equations at \(x\) make \(U(x^n)/n,V(x^n)/n\) integral.
Expanding the rows at \(x^n\) and using both equations at \(y\) gives the two displayed congruences in the [determinant calculation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v274.md#determinant).
I obtain determinant \(S(y-x)^2-xyh^2\).
After division by \(Sxy\) its negative is \(T_k/4^k-T_0\).
The initial summand \(T_0^2/4\) in this difference has valuation \(4a-2\), and every later summand has valuation at least \(4a+2j-2\) at index \(j\ge1\).
The adjugate thus gives valuations at least \(2k-D\) on both row values and \(k-D\) on their derivatives.
The exchange of lifts also preserves \(a\).
Moving a distance of valuation at least \(k+1\) to the powered Teichmüller lift preserves both bounds by Taylor expansion.
Frobenius permutes the roots of \(\bar P\), so for \(k>D\) every such root is a double root of \(\bar A\).

Saturation identifies the integral rank with the dimension of the image of the kernel modulo two.
Divisibility by \(\bar P^2\) then forces rank zero at \(b<2d\).
On the characteristic-zero remainder space the reversal exchanges the length-two local factors for each of \(d\) inverse pairs, so each eigenspace has dimension \(2d\).
The opposite rank inequality gives rank \(b+1-2d\) when \(b\ge2d\), with the stated full reduction.
Each of the \(d\) distinct powered roots contributes at least \(2k-D\) to the resultant valuation, with a logarithmic forced-divisor cost for fixed \(P\).

For a graph remainder in \(2^{2k}R_2\), where \(R_2=R\otimes\mathbb Z_2\), evaluation and differentiation at the double roots give errors divisible by \(n^2\).
Dividing by \(n\) leaves the same four congruences modulo \(n\), hence still forces \(\bar P^2\mid\bar A\).
This excludes primitive vectors on the first \(2d\) columns at precision \(2k\), for every actual width \(b\ge2d-1\).
All their Smith exponents are therefore finite and at most \(2k-1\).
These columns span the signed eigenspace over \(\mathbb Q_2\), so a graph remainder divisible by \(2^{q+2k}\) is \(2^qL_b(C)\) for some \(C\in\mathbb Z_2[Y]\) of degree below \(2d\).
Subtracting \(2^qC\) places the row in the \(2\)-adic kernel, whose residue classes have integral representatives by Smith form for the full map.
At \(b=\lceil n/\sqrt{\log n}\rceil\), the additional \(2k\) digits preserve the strict budget below slope \(1/8\) and \(b\ge M\lceil(q+2k)/(k+1)\rceil\) holds eventually for fixed \(F\).
For rows allowing a common factor the stated \(q=o(n\log n)\) permits a sublinear width depending on \(F,q,n\).
I find both the remainder examples and the conclusions about exact kernel classes valid on these domains.
The argument gives no norm bound on the exact integral representative of a passing congruence class.

## Endpoint weighting, consecutive powers and the odd triple

At 2026-11-19T01:57 UTC I checked the [weighted endpoint formula](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v275.md#rate), the construction of simultaneous nonzero witnesses and the conditional count bounds.
Distinct powers in the fixed list have distinct measures and full degree.
Equal positive absolute heights show that a relation between two conjugates of the base unit can only make their quotient or product torsion.
The all-power condition rules out a torsion quotient of distinct conjugates and hence leaves independent representatives after taking one from each inverse pair.
Away from the stated bad primes the squarefree product of the powered minima is separable modulo the prime, the \(n\)-th roots of \(\sigma\) have distinct residues, and the distinct \(\Phi_d\) with \(d\mid M\) have disjoint reductions.
For a base conjugate \(x\) only one \(d\) can contribute locally.
Its valuation is realized at a unique node above each matching \(x^a\), with multiplicity \(t_a\), so the local maximum uses precisely \(m_d=\max_{a:\Phi_d\mid Y^a-\sigma}t_a\).
Inversion preserves each ideal valuation.
Summing over inverse-pair representatives accounts for half the complete resultant logarithm.
The normalized common valuations for two independent representatives are bounded by those of \(x^{Mn}-1,y^{Mn}-1\).
The cited fixed-field unit gcd estimate makes this \(o(n)\), since the distinct pairs have height \(O(n)\) and the coefficient in that estimate can be arbitrarily small.
The fixed bad primes and primes dividing the exponent cost \(o(n)\) by applying the stated prime estimate at \(Mn\).
Möbius inversion in the divisors of the fixed \(d\) gives the resultant main term \(n h\varphi(d)\) and an \(O(\log(2n))\) error.
These facts give \(\log B_n=nhK_\sigma(t)/2+o(n)\) for all integer exponents tending to infinity.

For the consecutive powers at the negative endpoint, the orders present are exactly \(2j\), \(1\le j\le r\), giving \(D=T-K\) with the stated \(K\).
Fix \(a\) and let \(k=\lfloor2D/a\rfloor\).
The jet bound at sufficiently large fixed width gives doubled slope at most \((2T+ka)h+\eta\).
Under the assumed \(k+1\) common copies, division leaves a contact at level \(a\) and two at every other level, since the powered minima are distinct and separable under substitution.
Only the cyclotomic factors of \(Y^a+1\), of total degree \(a\), can each lose one unit of endpoint weight.
At a maximizing node and its inverse, the two quotient graph equations have unit determinant after removing the maximal common row power of \(Y+1\).
The resulting lower bound on the larger quotient row costs at most \(w\log2\) in its logarithmic norm, including the case of fixed \(w\).
Using the coefficient bound to recover its Mahler measure and restoring \(k+1\) copies gives a doubled-slope excess of at least \(((k+1)a-2D)h>0\) over the interpolation main term.
One fixed positive \(\eta\) fits all these gaps and the strict passing budget, and one sufficiently large width then works for all the finitely many target indices.

Removing the actual common copies leaves double contact at every level and a nonzero value in each row at the chosen powered node.
The two signed projections preserve all reciprocal divisibilities and at least one value there, at bounded cost.
For the final linear combination each of the \(r\) parameter polynomials has a nonzero coefficient from its own witness and degree at most \(r-1\).
Their union of roots excludes at most \(r(r-1)\) of the \(r(r-1)+1\) available integers.
This proves simultaneous nonvanishing on one passing row for either prescribed graph sign.
Padding costs at most two in norm and preserves the budget whenever \(b(n)\to\infty\) and \(\log b(n)=o(n)\).
The all-power norm representative and the small-measure reduction apply to a positive irreducible measure below \(B/(4T-2K)\).
Thus an infinite count exclusion implies the stated conditional bound, with denominators \(2,6,14,22,34\) for \(r=1,\dots,5\).

For the unmodified triple the negative endpoint has weight \(10\) and the positive endpoint weight \(8\) at multiplicity two.
Dropping one copy at level one leaves weight \(10\), while dropping one at level two leaves weight \(8\).
Restoring the powered minima costs \(h\) and \(2h\), respectively, so both comparisons reach exactly \(6h\).
At odd exponents the modified triple \(\delta,\delta^2,-\delta^3\) instead has disjoint negative-endpoint root sets.
The matched pair of levels \(1,3\) has phase \(-1\) and even exponent sum, while the other two pairs have phase \(+1\) and odd sum.
The sharp signed-row theorem gives the exact \(6h\) limit at widths tending to infinity with \(b=o(n)\), and every fixed positive excess can be attained at a sufficiently large fixed width.
Its strict-budget consequence thus holds also on the indicated larger padded widths, giving the conditional \(B/12\) at odd exponents.
I find the argument valid with those distinct width quantifiers.
The optimal coefficient rate for the original three minima and the existence of the assumed count exclusions remain open in this argument.
I used the cited unit-gcd and resultant estimates at their stated scopes and did not reprove their external inputs.

## The formal theorem on cyclotomic overlap

At 2026-11-19T02:12 UTC I independently checked the [formal statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v431.md#statement) and all three sections of its proof for Sheng Yin.
I received the fixed Claim and used my preceding [review of the ledger argument](#rates).
I had no part in developing or writing the Claim.

For the [contraction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v431.md#contraction), the excluded primes ensure separability of the powered minima, the node equation and the factors of \(Y^M-1\) in reduction.
At a retained prime each positive valuation therefore corresponds to one node and one powered root, while more than one level can use the same \(\Phi_d(x^n)\) at different nodes.
The maximum counts its largest multiplicity exactly once.
Unramifiedness makes the normalized local exponents integers, and the nonzero resultant bounds their support.
The fixed-field gcd bound allows any positive coefficient on the height of the independent pairs, and the discarded-prime estimate applies with the integer exponent \(Mn\).
Together they permit replacing the local maximum by a sum with error \(o(n)\) after summation over primes.
Inverse pairing contributes the factor \(1/2\), and Möbius inversion contributes \(\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)e=\varphi(d)\) for each fixed \(d\).
This gives the asserted limit along all exponents, including those divisible by the fixed orders in \(\mathcal D\).

For the [witness construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v431.md#witnesses), the maximal common endpoint factor is removed from the quotient rows only after the hypothetical \(k+1\) powered factors have been divided out.
The inverse nodes have distinct residues since \(F(1)F(-1)\) is invertible at retained primes.
Their equations force divisibility of a nonzero integral divided derivative, giving the required bound on the norm of at least one quotient row.
Both losses of \(w\log2\), for endpoint evaluation and for passage from norm to Mahler measure, are \(o(n)\) at the fixed interpolation width.
The inequality \((k+1)a>2D\) gives strict slack for every target, so one width works after choosing an excess smaller than all the finitely many gaps and \(B-C_rh\).
Field-degree preservation and distinct heights ensure that removal of the actual common copies keeps every required double contact.
The signed linear combination preserves each prescribed nonzero value and the width multiplier has one-norm at most two.
Thus the same fixed input and graph sign work throughout the tail of any specified growing widths with \(\log b=o(n)\), including widths larger than \(n\).
The choice of a positive irreducible measure below \(B/C_r\) is valid by factorization, and the all-power representative realizes the distinct measures \(h,\ldots,rh\).

The [triple comparison](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v431.md#triple) gives only the endpoint slopes \(5h,4h\) for the original minima.
The sign change at the third level yields the required phases at each odd exponent and total weight \(12h\) in the sharp signed-row theorem.
This proves the least-norm limit \(6h\) along \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(n)\), as well as attainment of every positive excess at a sufficiently large fixed width.
For existence alone at a particular odd \(n\ge3\), distinct powered conjugates \(z=\alpha^n\) also permit rational Hermite interpolation of \(U(z)=-\alpha\) and \(U'(z)=-1/(n\alpha^{n-1})\).
These data are Galois equivariant, so clearing denominators in the rows \(U,1\) supplies double contact and nonzero values before the two signed projections.
For the passing conclusion only the eventual bounds enter, and \(12h<B\) gives the fixed positive excess required for padding.
I find the full formal argument valid, with the three-value bound \(B/12\) conditional on an infinite odd-exponent exclusion.
