## Independent review of the frame estimate and its critical-scale consequences

For a row isometry \(A\), the unrestricted difference digits have joint characteristic function \(\prod_j\cos^2(\theta_j/2)\).
Inserting the standard Gaussian characteristic function at \(\sqrt{2}H^{1/2}(AB-z)\) gives the nonnegative cosine product times a unit complex phase.
The triangle inequality discards that phase for every fixed center.
Writing the Gaussian density with the additional form \(I+H\) and compensating by \(\exp(G^{\mathsf T}HG/2)\) gives the stated inverse square-root determinant.
After the coordinate change, the compensator is exactly \(\exp(\sum_j\langle b_j,g\rangle^2/2)\), since \(AA^{\mathsf T}=I\).
The squared norm of the linear map sending \(a_j\) to \(b_j\) is at most \(\rho=t/(1+t)\).
Consequently \(c_j=\|b_j\|^2/\rho\le\|a_j\|^2\), and the sum of its weighted projections is bounded by the identity.
The residual eigenvalues and their unit eigenvectors complete a geometric frame.
Taking each added function to be one leaves the integrand unchanged.
All zero \(b_j\) may be dropped, including when \(H=0\).

The exponents \(c_j\) in the frame inequality cancel the denominator \(c_j\) in the cosine powers.
On \(|x|\le\pi/\sqrt{2\rho c_j}\) the central cosine bound cancels the entire positive quadratic exponential.
Outside that interval the Gaussian integral with density proportional to \(\exp(-(1-\rho)x^2/2)\) is at most \(2(1-\rho)^{-1/2}\exp(-\pi^2(1-\rho)/(4\rho c_j))\).
Using \((1-\rho)/\rho=1/t\) produces the claimed \(E(t,\delta)\); the sum of the original weights is at most the row dimension by taking the trace.
The bounded measurable Gaussian Brascamp–Lieb theorem in the cited source applies to truncations and monotone convergence gives the stated extension, since every one-dimensional upper bound is finite.
This verifies (4) uniformly in the center and the full positive semidefinite range \(H\preceq tI\), including a singular tilt.

The exact return probability lower bound gives \(-L^{-1}\log p_T\le d\log(4T)/L+(kd-1/L)m\le sh\).
The entropy chain rule allocates this bound to conditional block laws given their entire past.
At each such past whitening the next carry changes the new block vector only by a known shift.
Centering at the conditional mean of the whitened new block and testing with its negative quadratic form gives \(-\operatorname{tr}(H\Sigma_i)\) under the bridge.
Eigenvectors and the choice of \(H\) can depend on this past since the unrestricted estimate is uniform.
Writing \(x=\lambda+\varepsilon\), the optimizer of \(\tfrac12\log(1+v)-vx\) over \(v\ge0\) is \((1/(2x)-1)_+\), bounded by \(t=1/(2\varepsilon)\).
Using \(x\) instead of \(\lambda\) subtracts the nonnegative \(\varepsilon v\) and the optimized value is precisely \(\phi(x)\).
Thus averaging costs at most \(h+E(t,\delta)\) per dimension.

Below \(y=\log(1/2)\), \(\phi(e^y)\) has first derivative \(e^y-1/2\) and positive second derivative \(e^y\).
At the join the derivative agrees with the constant branch.
Applying Jensen to the equal-weight choice of cut and eigenvalue and the bridge law on pasts gives the bound on the exponential of the averaged log determinant, since \(\phi\) decreases to zero up to \(1/2\).
All logarithms are finite for fixed positive regularization and the terminal covariance can be zero.
At the critical scale \(d\to\infty\), \(d/s\to1\) and \(h\to1/2\).
For \(\varepsilon=\sqrt\delta\) the exponential in \(E\) decays as \(\exp(-\pi^2/(2\sqrt\delta))\), faster than its prefactor grows.
The smaller positive solution for the limiting geometric mean is \(x_{1/2}=0.07929716978\ldots\).
The count bound follows by charging each eigenvalue at most \(\eta\) at least \(\phi(\eta+\varepsilon)\), which tends to infinity as both arguments vanish.
These conclusions concern the regularized determinants and the averaged count of small eigenvalues, including possible exceptional pasts.

In checking (2), the scaled neutral embedding gives columns of squared length \(s\), and shifting a window rotates its row coordinates by a common orthogonal map.
Degree windows have full row rank by the power-basis determinant.
Every leverage is a diagonal entry of an orthogonal projection and lies in \([0,1]\).
At lengths \(l>d\) the first and last leave-one-out determinants coincide with \(\det W_{l-1}\); both endpoint leverages are therefore \(1-\det W_{l-1}/\det W_l\).
The first endpoint leverage decreases as the window grows because \(W_l\) increases in positive semidefinite order.
For \(M=\lceil N/2\rceil\) every full-window column is an endpoint of an \(M\)-window, which bounds its leverage above by \(g_M\).
As \(M\ge2b\), flooring \(M/(2b)\) places \(j_0\) between \(M/4\) and \(M/2\), with \(j_0\ge b\ge d\).
The logarithmic ratios telescope from \(j_0+1\) to \(M\), and each dominates \(g_M\).
The trace bound gives \(\det(W_M)^{1/s}\le M\), while concavity of log determinant on the average of \(j_0/b\) rotated \(W_b\)'s gives \(\det(W_{j_0})^{1/s}\ge(j_0/b)\det(W_b)^{1/s}\).
Their logarithmic ratio is at most \(s(\log4+\Delta_b)\), and \(M-j_0\ge N/4\) gives the asserted constant and range.
The arithmetic degree-window bound uses the two real row norms with product at most \(d e^{m(d-1)}\), and the complete scaled Vandermonde determinant of absolute value at least one.
It gives exactly the stated upper bound on \(\Delta_d\).
Since \(N=kd\) and \(s/d\le1\), any permitted \(\Delta_b=o(k)\) implies \(\delta\to0\); \(m\log d\to0\) suffices at \(k\sim1/(2m)\).
The degree window lies in range eventually because \(k\to\infty\).

Finally, project the whitened outputs onto eigenvectors of \(W_N\) with eigenvalues at least \(Rs\).
The sum of squared components of each original column is \(s\), so after projection and whitening its squared norm is at most \(1/R\).
The remaining rows form an isometry and the conditional covariance is the corresponding compression.
The entropy budget stays \(sh\), while the Gaussian defect costs only \(uE(t,1/R)\), where \(u\le s\) is the retained dimension.
Dividing the small-eigenvalue count by \(s\) gives a bound tending to zero uniformly even when \(u/s\to0\), by choosing \(\varepsilon=R^{-1/2}\).
The empty compression has count zero.
In increasing eigenvalue order, interlacing gives \(\lambda_j\le\lambda_j^{\rm comp}\le\lambda_{j+s-u}\).
Thus at most \(s-u\) further eigenvalues can fall at or below the cutoff in the full space, including ties.
This proves (3), with strict inequality in the count of discarded phase eigenvalues.
Taking \(1\ll R\ll k\) puts their cutoff \(Rs\) at \(o(N)\).
The comparison with the earlier \(1/(2\log2)\) upper bound retains that bound's unrestricted Gram scope.
