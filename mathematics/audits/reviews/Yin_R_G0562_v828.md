## Independent review of the torsion degree allocation and the two quadratic tests

At 2026-11-20T23:53 (UTC), an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the entire submitted C345 v691 and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no part in developing v691 and inspected its fixed proof and required mathematical inputs without development dialogue, author explanations or prior findings on v691.

For either sign, the torsion classification deletes precisely the linear factors of \(X^L-\epsilon\) at \(1,-1\), independently of \(a\).
The surviving roots are simple and nonreal and number \(D=d-2\).
If \(\beta=r^{1/q}\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(r)\), it generates that field because its \(q\)-th power is \(r\).
Powering therefore gives a bijection of conjugates.
The radical is a unit with precisely one exterior conjugate, and an inverse pair of nonreal neutral conjugates forces its minimal polynomial to be reciprocal.
This identifies the two real factors even when \(q\) is even.
The angular pairing for the original circle roots holds for every seed and uses no primality assumption.
Its error divided by \(q\) is strictly less than half the spacing \(2\pi/q\) of the lifts of the assigned root.
The chosen labels over distinct conjugates of \(r\) are distinct.
They occur in conjugate pairs since none of their \(q\)-th powers is real.
The candidate labels are the roots of \(A(X^q)\), simple since the original roots are nonzero and simple.

For a factor of degree \(k=\varphi(j)\) with \(b\) assigned labels, the neutral product of absolute values is at most
\[
                2^{kD}\bigl(\pi/(2aqL)\bigr)^b.
\]
The exterior and contracting factors together contribute at most \(4^k\beta^k\).
Since \(\beta^q<a+1\le4a/3\), \(k\le qL\) and \(L\ge6\), the constant \(2^{(D+4)qL}\) suffices.
The norm is a nonzero integer because \(\beta>1\) cannot be a root of unity.
If \(qb>k\), integrality gives \(b-k/q\ge1/q\), so the stated finite seed cutoff follows.
The effective bound on \(q\) applies to composite and prime indices at both signs and \(d\le n+1\).
Indeed its estimate at a nonreal Salem conjugate has modified heights bounded by \(\max(\log\beta,\pi)/d\) and \(\pi/d\).
The cited explicit height bound then gives \(q/\log(4qn)\ll d^2\log^4(3d)\) and its effective inversion gives the required range.
Thus the maxima of the cutoffs may be taken over a set depending only on \(n\).
Beyond that threshold the individual upper bounds \(b\le k/q\) sum to equality because \(A(X^q)\) has degree \(qD\) and the total number of chosen labels is \(D\).
Their even counts give precisely the asserted necessary degree divisibilities.

For a prime \(p\), a primitive root of order \(j\) maps to order \(j/\gcd(j,p)\).
The preimage orders and degrees in the submitted prime test follow also when \(p=2\).
The factors with \(p\mid s\) impose only the evenness of \(\varphi(s)\), automatic for \(s>2\).
When \(p\nmid s\), the degree of the factor of order \(s\) imposes \(2p\mid\varphi(s)\), which suffices also for the factor of order \(ps\).
On the positive-sign progression both primitive third and fourth roots survive, giving a coprime order of degree two for every prime.

In that progression the original resultant is one in absolute value.
The monic integral cofactor of \(\Phi_s\) in \(A(X^p)\), evaluated at the unit \(\beta\), consequently shows that \(|N\Phi_s(\beta)|=1\).
With a label of order \(s\), conjugation gives \(b\ge2\).
Inserting \(k=2\) into the preceding product bound gives \(1\le2^{2D+6}a^{2/p-2}\).
Since \(2-2/p\ge1\), this yields \(\log a\ll d\).
Without such a label, both target roots lie on the same grid of \(2pL\)-th roots as the labels.
After the angular error is allowed, each neutral conjugate remains at angular distance at least \(2\pi/(3pL)\) from either target.
The chord at angular distance \(\theta\in[0,\pi]\) is at least \(2\theta/\pi\), which gives the required lower bound \(1/(pL)\).
For \(\beta\ge2\), each exterior factor has absolute value at least \(\beta/2\) and each contracting factor at least \(1/2\).
Multiplying with the neutral factors in the unit norm gives \(\beta^2/16\le(pL)^{2D}\).
The resulting bound on \(\beta\) holds also below two.
Now \(a-1<r=\beta^p\), \(D\le n-1\) and \(p\ll n^2\log^5(3n)\) give \(\log a\ll n^3\log^6(3n)\) with an effective absolute constant.
Excluding all prime field radicals excludes all indices exceeding one by taking a power of the positive radical.
The positive-unit substitution criterion applies for every \(h\), and the \(h\)-th roots of each conjugate contribute its original total multiplicative Mahler factor.
This proof places no squarefreeness requirement on \(a(a-1)(a+1)\).
