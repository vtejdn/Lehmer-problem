## Finding on the final statement and argument

The incremental gcd bound (1), cofactor bound (2), dense-input construction and all parts of (3) follow in the stated ranges.

Writing \(F=G F_0\) adds exactly \(m(G)\) to the two-variable mean and to its specialized one-variable mean.
The original support therefore gives a valid comparison error for \(F_0\), even if division increases the number of nonzero coefficients.
Using the divided support separately gives a second valid bound.
The width dependence increases on \(0<w<n\), and when the actual width is zero the circle powering map preserves the vertical mean exactly.
Removing the integer content along with the common polynomial factor makes \(F_0\) primitive with no base-only nonunit factor.
Since its specialized monomials stay distinct, integer content cannot reappear by collision.
If all remaining components are horizontal torsion translates, their rational factors specialize to cyclotomic products.
Thus a noncyclotomic irreducible in \(H/G\) requires a nontorsion horizontal component, forcing at least \(c_R\) before the subtraction of the comparison error.
If there are several rational factors with such components, additivity charges their individual partial-degree bounds with their true multiplicities, and only one error is paid for specializing the product.

The strict inequality in the claim excludes even one surviving copy of any noncyclotomic irreducible of \(H/G\).
Because \(G\) is integral, \(m(G)\ge0\), and each \(P\) of width exceeding \(w\) is too wide to divide \(G\).
An occurrence of that \(P\) in \(H\) must therefore survive division, giving (2) by additivity.
When \(m(P)\to0\) and the error has limit superior below \(c_R\), (2) eventually gives \(m(Q)>0\).
At width at most \(w\), the two endpoint coefficients of the lift of \((z^n-1)P\) have gcd \(P\), which verifies the exception.

The coefficient of \(z^a\) in \(PQ\) is precisely the finite convolution in the recurrence.
Its vanishing outside the union gives support containment, and requiring a nonzero coefficient in both end intervals gives the prescribed block-index span.
The latter condition matters even at width zero, as the exponents of \(2z^2-1\) lie in \(\{0,2,4\}\) but do not occupy the endpoint \(4\).
The final wording imposes both conditions.
For a chain of gcds the nonnegative stage differences are exactly the measures of their integral quotients.
Taking the positive part of each applicable stage lower bound and summing proves the telescoping claim with all multiplicities preserved below the specified thresholds.

For the integral family, \(A_n\equiv(z-1)^{n-1}\pmod p\) follows from \(n=p^\ell\).
The perturbation misses both ends, giving a monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(2n-2\), and the nonnegative perturbation preserves the strictly positive coefficients of \(A_n^2\).
The translated polynomial is Eisenstein since its constant term \(n^2+pT(1)\) has valuation one at \(p\).
That value exceeds \(p\) and is divisible by \(p\), excluding zero, one and every prime value allowed at one for a cyclotomic polynomial.
Multiplication by \((z-1)^2\) gives the stated three layers with nonzero outer middle coefficients at \(\pm(s+1)\).
After translation their width is at most \(2(s+1)\), the gcd is a Laurent unit, and the centered comparison also applies since \(s+1<n\).

For \(T=A_j(z)A_j(z^{-1})\), the conditions \(p\nmid j\) and \(1\le j<n\) give admissible span \(j-1\), and \(T(1)=j^2\).
The two squared moduli \(|A_n|^2\) and \(|A_j|^2\) have disjoint zero sets on the circle because \(\gcd(n,j)=1\).
Reciprocity and absence of circle roots then give exactly \(n-1\) exterior conjugates.
The second difference has exponents \(0,n-j,n,n+j,2n\), all distinct.
Applying weighted arithmetic-geometric mean to the centered circle modulus gives the lower mean \(\log(1+p)\), since both \(\log|A_n|\) and \(\log|A_j|\) have zero mean.
The two squared differences after multiplication have combined ordinary mean \(2(1+p)\), giving the upper bound by concavity.

For a fixed nontrivial two-torus character \((a,b)\) its specialized mean is zero unless \(an+bj=0\).
For \(b\ne0\) that equation requires \(n\mid b\), impossible eventually independently of \(j\).
For \(b=0\) it is impossible for every \(n\).
The mean convergence is therefore uniform on trigonometric polynomials, hence on continuous functions.
Truncating the logarithm of the sum below at \(-K\) leaves an error bounded in integral by \(\int(-\log|1-v|^2-K)_+\,dv\) for Haar probability on the circle.
This tends to zero and applies both before and after taking the full torus mean.
At fixed second coordinate \(e^{i\theta}\), Jensen gives the first-coordinate logarithmic mean as \(2\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt p\,|\sin(\theta/2)|)\).
Integration gives exactly \(4/\pi\) times the integral in (3) for each fixed prime \(p\).
