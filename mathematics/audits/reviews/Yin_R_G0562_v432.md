## Independent review of fiber weights and the single-grid obstruction

At 2026-11-18T16:15 UTC a reviewer outside proof development examined all five sections of [the fixed ledger argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v194.md#fibers) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed text and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings about this argument.
The finite mass quantization and its equality case hold for \(m>2s\).
The endpoint criterion, the separate bounds for disjoint midpoint grids and the single-grid estimates (3)–(4) also hold with their stated hypotheses.

### Recovering the parity constraints

At a preimage \(x\) of \(b\), the residue of the branch rational function is \((2-x)R(x)/(Q_0(x)P'(b)C_m'(x))\).
The roots \(b\) are simple interior points, so each has \(m\) simple real preimages and \(Q_0\) is nonzero there by disjointness.
Equating the residue to \(w_b/m\) gives (1) at every root of the monic divisor \(P(C_m)\).
The polynomials \(P(y)/(y-b)\) in the interpolation formula for \(D\) are monic of degree \(N-1\), so the coefficient of that degree is \(S\).

In the integral quotient, adjoining \(x\) with \(C_m(x)=y\) gives a free module over \(\mathbb Z[y]/(P)\) with basis \(V_0,\ldots,V_{m-1}\).
The left side of (1) therefore has integral classes for each basis coefficient.
The trigonometric product identity gives \(C_jV_{m-1}=yV_{j-1}+2V_{m-j-1}\) for \(1\le j\le s\).
The low indices run from \(0\) to \(s-1\), and the high indices start at \(m-s-1\ge s\).
Consequently the two coefficients contributed by \(a_s=1\) recover \(2D\) and \(yD\) separately.
The degree-\(<N\) representative of the first is \(2D\) itself, giving \(2S\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\).
If \(2S=1\), then \(G=2D\) is monic, and the representative of \(yD\) is \((yG-P)/2\), which proves the full coefficient congruence.

### Rigidity of the interlacing pair

The positive partial-fraction residues force exactly one zero of \(G\) between consecutive roots of \(P\), since \(G/P\) decreases from \(+\infty\) to \(-\infty\) on each such interval.
On dividing \(P_{j-1}\) by \(P_j\), the negative remainder alternates in sign on the roots of \(P_j\) and is positive on the largest.
It has exactly the next required degree, has positive leading coefficient \(e_j\), and after normalization strictly interlaces \(P_j\).
This constructs the claimed Jacobi matrix, with \(P\) and \(G\) as the characteristic polynomials of the full matrix and its first trailing principal submatrix.

Both \(2I\pm J\) are positive definite because the eigenvalues are in the open interval \((-2,2)\).
Parity first makes \(d_1\) even, and the bounds on its diagonal entry give \(d_1=0\).
For \(N\ge2\), \(e_1\) is then the positive even leading coefficient of \(yG-P\), and \(e_1=(J^2)_{11}<4\), so \(e_1=2\).
The whole remainder is even, hence \(P_2\) is integral.
The next monic quotient, or the terminal linear polynomial when \(N=2\), makes \(d_2\) integral.
The positive second minors force \(|d_2|<1\), so \(d_2=0\) and both second minors equal two.
At each later step \(e_{j-1}\) is a positive integer by monic division of two already integral polynomials.
The two leading-minor recurrences give \(e_{j-1}<2-|d_j|\), so \(e_{j-1}=1\).
Its monic remainder gives integral \(P_j\), hence integral \(d_j\), including the terminal case, and \(|d_j|<1\) forces zero.
The new minors again equal two.
For \(N=1\) the original parity condition already gives \(P=y\).
For larger \(N\) the full characteristic recurrence starts with \(y,y^2-2\) and the trailing one with \(1,y\), giving \(P=C_N\) and \(G=V_{N-1}\).
The identity \(C_N'=NV_{N-1}\) gives equal branch weights, and \(C_N\circ C_m=C_{mN}\) gives exactly the midpoint grid.

### The endpoint and disjoint grids

At the zeros of \(C_k\), the interpolating condition reduces to \((2-x)R\equiv Q_0V_{k-1}/2\).
Using \(xV_{k-1}=C_k+2V_{k-2}\) and even \(A\) gives the integral representative \(S_k\) in the ledger.
The image of \(S_k\) modulo \((2-x,C_k)\) is precisely \(S_k(2)\bmod2\), since \(C_k(2)=2\).
When this vanishes, subtracting \(S_k(2)C_k/2\) and dividing by \(2-x\) gives an integral solution.
Thus the criterion is sufficient for interpolation at the new nodes, and necessary for any actual return with those weights.
Under the excluded parity \(A/2\) and \(J_0(2)\equiv Q_0'(0)\bmod2\) are both odd, so the parity expression has residue \(2k-1\equiv1\bmod2\) for every \(k\).
This eliminates the sole half-mass possibility and leaves a minimum permitted mass of one on the complete fibers.

For a midpoint grid of size \(k\), reflection of the \(2k\) points of arguments \((2a+1)\pi/(2k)\) on the circle pairs equal cosines.
The geometric sum for that circle grid gives mean \(0\) for \(C_n\) unless \(2k\mid n\), and mean \(2(-1)^{n/(2k)}\) in the remaining case.
The integral coefficients in \(Q_0 C_{2k_i-s}\) have positive frequencies between \(2k_i-2s\) and \(2k_i\), with top coefficient one because \(k_i>s\).
Every larger group contributes zero and group \(i\) contributes \(-2u_i\).
Once \(2u_j\) is integral, every earlier group's contribution is integral by the same frequency formula.
The exact integral moment of the entire measure thus proves \(2u_i\in\mathbb Z\) successively, without an arithmetic relation among grid sizes.

### Nonuniform weights on a single new grid

For even \(A\) the constant coefficient of \(Q_0W\) in the integral cosine basis is even, as follows by evaluation at zero, and the means of its positive frequencies are even integers.
Thus the half-uniform measure passes every test against \(Q_0W\).
The actual measure restricted to the new grid also passes because all other neutral nodes are old traces killed by \(Q_0\).
If the sum of absolute differences of the weights were below \(1/(2M)\), the differences in all tests \(Q_0 C_j\), and in the constant-basis test \(Q_0\), would be integers of absolute value below one.
These tests span all real polynomial tests after multiplication by \(Q_0\).
Interpolation at each node where \(Q_0\ne0\) now forces equality of individual weights, excluded by the endpoint criterion.
This proves (3) for the sum of absolute differences, including unequal total masses.

At square constants, full mass converging to \(1/2\) gives the fixed-input limit \(\rho/2\).
Its mass on the finite closed set of old traces is zero, hence the new-grid mass \(S\) tends to \(1/2\).
Multiplying the branch formula over that grid gives the displayed product for \(G_k^k\), since \(C_k(2)=2\) and \(\prod_y|C_k'(y)|=2^{k-1}k^k\).
Positivity and integrality imply \(|\operatorname{Res}(C_k,R)|\ge1\).
The old exterior trace contributes \(\sigma^k+\sigma^{-k}\) to the absolute denominator and each of the remaining \(s-1\) old traces contributes at most two.
It follows that \(\liminf G_k\ge1/(2\sigma)\) and \(\limsup\log(S/G_k)\le\log\sigma\).
The relative entropy in the direction from the uniform probability to \((c_y/S)_y\) is exactly \(\log(S/G_k)\).
The finite entropy inequality and the triangle inequality therefore give \(1/(2M)\le\tfrac12\sqrt{2\log\sigma}\) in the limit, which is (4).
This is a necessary condition for half-mass convergence on the specified single new grid.
Existence for arbitrary nonuniform weights in this parity remains open.
