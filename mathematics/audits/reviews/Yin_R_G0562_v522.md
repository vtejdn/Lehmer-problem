### Limiting rank and the two types of collision

I fixed all nodes except \(y=Y_i\) as in the proof.
At \(N=0\), the fixed equations require all \(e-2\) distinct zeros, giving the stated three-dimensional kernel.
An invertible fixed-column minor provides an analytic basis near \(N=0\), with the prescribed basis at zero obtained by a constant change of coordinates.
The constant coefficients of the two moving rows at \(N=0\) impose independent functionals \(a_0+ca_e,ca_e\) on this kernel.
Their top coefficients likewise impose \(a_e+ca_0,ca_0\).
Thus there is a nonvanishing maximal minor at each end of the homogenized \(y\)-line at the limit, possibly a different minor at each end.
At every other point where \(H_0(y)\ne0\) and \(y\ne y^{-1}\), evaluating a quadratic at the two distinct points has rank two.
Compactness of the complex projective line therefore reduces the problem to the two inversion fixed points and the fixed nodes and their inverses.

At an inversion fixed point the first three logarithmic jets of \((y^{-1},1,y)\) have determinant \(2\).
Multiplying by the local scalar \(y^{-(s-1)}H_0(y)\ne0\) preserves invertibility.
In the moving jet basis, the normalized value row has coordinates
\[
 (1+c,\ (1-c)\theta,\ (1+c)\theta^2/2)+O(|\theta|^3).
\]
The derivative row has coordinates
\[
 (c,\ (1-c)N-c\theta,\ (1+c)N\theta+c\theta^2/2)
       +O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3).
\]
The first coordinate determines the proportionality coefficient up to \(O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3)\).
The second gives \(\theta=O(N)\) and hence \(\theta/N\to (1-c^2)/(2c)\).
In the third the terms of degree two in \(\theta\) cancel, leaving \((1+c)N\theta+o(N^2)\), which excludes proportionality.
The bounds hold along complex sequences because the chosen basis and its inverse are analytic and bounded near the limit.

At a fixed node \(w\) of parameter \(d\), the two exact fixed equations imply \(u_0=N(u_1+d v_1)\) and \(v_0=-u_0/d\).
At its inverse the equations take the same form with \(d\) replaced by \(1/d\), by swapping \(u,v\) and negating the logarithmic derivative.
Projecting along the moving \(u_1,v_1\) kills those vectors and both constant vectors exactly.
At \(N=0\) the covector \((1,-w-w^{-1},1)\) kills both value triples, and its value on each of their first logarithmic derivatives is \(w-w^{-1}\).
The simple zero of \(B\) gives a common nonzero limiting normal coordinate \(\alpha\) for \(u_2,v_2\).

Writing \(a=1-c/d\), the first two coordinates of the restrictions have determinant \(c\theta^2-da^2N^2+O((|N|+|\theta|)^3)\).
If \(|\theta/N|\) tends to zero or infinity, the leading quadratic term on the corresponding scale is nonzero.
Every possible sequence of common zeros therefore has a subsequence with \(\theta/N\to t=q-1/q\), where \(q^2=d/c\) and \(q\ne0,\pm1\).
The first coordinate fixes the limiting proportionality coefficient at \(1/(1+q)\).
In the normal coordinate, subtracting this multiple of the value row from the derivative row and dividing by \(\alpha N^2t\) yields the limit \((1+q)(c+1/q)/2\).
Vanishing would require \(cd=1\), which the assumptions exclude at both choices of \(w\).

### Polynomial gcd and coefficient growth

For every permissible fixed choice of the other nodes there is consequently a punctured disk in \(N\) on which no \(y\) is a common zero.
If a common factor had positive \(y\)-degree, its leading coefficient would stay a nonzero polynomial in \(N\) for some permissible choice of the other nodes, since these choices form a nonempty Zariski open set.
Small nonzero \(N\) outside the finitely many roots of that leading coefficient would preserve its positive degree and give a common complex root.
Applying this contradiction to each node puts the gcd in \(L[N]\), which leaves the highest and lowest exponents in every node unchanged.
This algebraic width conclusion uses \(c_i\in\mathbb C^\times\setminus\{1,-1\}\), \(c_i\ne c_j\) and \(c_ic_j\ne1\) for \(i\ne j\).
The self-inverse exclusions matter already at \(y=1\), where \(c_i=-1\) gives a zero value row and \(c_i=1\) gives dependent rows for every \(N\).
The signed Salem representatives satisfy all these conditions.

For both signs the outside-place gcd estimate and the fixed-place recurrence estimate apply with the same hypotheses checked in R562 v521.
I verified that the finite excess is \(\log p\), the height is at least \(ph/e\), the specialization avoids each fixed proper exceptional set eventually, and the finite common valuation is \(o_P(p)\).
Xiao's Theorem 3.3 and Lemma 4.1 enter at statement and applicability scope.
Their original proofs lie outside this review.
There are \(e\) equally frequent embedding classes, one for each of the exterior and interior choices at a single node.
The signed generator's projective height is therefore \(ph\,s(2e)/e+o_P(p)=eph+o_P(p)\).
Its primitive integral coefficient maximum realizes that height logarithmically, and passage to its one-norm costs at most \(\log(e+1)\).
Finally, at \(N=0\) the two signed cofactors each have equal nonzero extreme coordinates, so their mixed edge expression is generically nonzero and stays nonzero for every sufficiently large integer \(p\).
The equal-width two-line formula gives existence, coprimality and the same exponent for the least cost, since its parity factor and the comparisons of the one-norms are bounded independently of \(p\).
