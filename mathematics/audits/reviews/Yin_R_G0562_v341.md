### Residue normalization and the norm

In (3), \(N\bmod d\) belongs to \(\{0,r\}\) since the leading coefficient is occupied.
Reversal sends a residue \(j\) to \(N-j\), and both residues must remain present when the polynomial is reciprocal up to sign.
If \(N\equiv0\bmod d\) then \(-r\equiv r\bmod d\), impossible for \(d\ge3\) with \(\gcd(r,d)=1\).
Writing \(N=nd+r\) thus makes reversal exchange the two rows.
Coefficient comparison gives \(V(y)=\sigma y^{n-a}U^*(y)\) with \(a=\deg U\le n\), so the effective exponent is \(s=d(n-a)+r\).
It satisfies \(\gcd(s,d)=1\), and \(U(0)=\pm1\) under the subplastic hypothesis from v337.
In particular \(U\) is coprime to the monomial shift and \(\gcd(U,V)=1\) gives \(\gcd(U,U^*)=1\).
The case of a constant unit \(U\) gives a measure-one binomial.

For general \(d,s\), substitution by \(x^{\gcd(d,s)}\) preserves the circle mean.
The numbers \(\zeta^S\) with \(\zeta^D=1\) run through every \(D\)-th root of unity.
The product identity \(\prod_{\xi^D=1}(u+v\xi)=u^D-(-v)^D\) yields exactly (5), including the sign for even \(D\).
The product has nonzero constant term and its measure is the sum of \(D\) rotation-invariant measures, giving (6).

A unit-circle root of \(f\) would have its inverse among the roots of \(f\) by complex conjugation and would thus be a common root with \(g\).
The interior roots of \(g\) are precisely the inverses of the exterior roots of \(f\) with their multiplicities.
If \(A\) is the leading coefficient, Jensen gives \(\prod|a_\nu|=|A|/M(f)\), also for the empty product.
The factors defining \(C\) have boundary modulus one and cancel every interior pole of \(f/g\).
Thus \(C,B\) are bounded by one on the disk, with \(C(0)=|A|/M(f)\) and \(|B(0)|=1/M(f)\).
Each is bounded away from zero at the origin uniformly in the family.
On the circle the numerator of \(B^D-\varepsilon_D z^S C^D\) contributes the norm polynomial and its denominator contributes \(D\,m(g)=D\,m(f)\).
The nonzero rational difference is analytic across the circle with integrable boundary logarithm, so (9) gives the upper error \((\log2)/D\).

### Nondegeneracy of subsequential limits

After passing to a subsequence the number of disk zeros of \(g_i\) is fixed and at most \(K\).
Their moduli are bounded below by their product, hence by \(\Theta^{-1}\).
The chosen normalized Blaschke factor tends to one locally if its zero tends to a point on the boundary, because \((a-z)/(1-\bar a z)=a\) when \(|a|=1\).
All other limiting factors are genuine Blaschke factors.
This proves the asserted local convergence to a finite product \(C\) with \(C(0)>0\).
Normality of \(B_i\) and its lower bound at zero give a nonzero limit \(B\), and \(B_i/C_i\) converges holomorphically near zero.

The finitely many possible signed \(A_i\) and endpoint units can be fixed along the subsequence.
When \(|A_i|>1\), the modulus of \(B(0)/C(0)\) is \(1/|A_i|<1\).
In the monic-up-to-sign case write \(c=f_i(0)/A_i=\pm1\).
Coefficient division by \(g_i(0)=\pm1\) gives integral Taylor coefficients at every order.
Rouché's theorem counts \(k\) interior zeros of \(g_i-\lambda c f_i\) for \(0\le\lambda<1\), using \(|f_i|=|g_i|>0\) on the circle.
The limiting polynomial \(g_i-c f_i\) is nonzero by coprimality and positive degree.
Its zero at the origin has order at most \(k\), since all zeros in a sufficiently small surrounding circle persist with multiplicity under the perturbation.
As \(g_i(0)\ne0\), this is exactly the order of the first nonconstant term of \(f_i/g_i\).
If the limit were \(c\), local convergence would make each of these first \(K\) Taylor coefficients tend to zero and thus eventually vanish by integrality, a contradiction.
This proves precisely the exclusion of a unimodular constant needed for the boundary argument, including nonmonic seeds.

### The limiting deficit measure

Take a further subsequence with \(S_i/D_i\to t\in[0,T]\).
On the punctured disk the squared moduli of \(B(z)\) and \(|z|^t C(z)\) are real analytic.
Agreement on an open set would extend throughout that disk.
At zero the lower bounds on \(B(0),C(0)\) require \(t=0\), and the resulting equality of moduli forces \(B/C\) to be a unimodular constant on a neighborhood of zero.
Hence the set of strict inequality is dense.
At every point in it the smaller term divided by the larger has modulus tending to zero exponentially in \(D_i\), since their \(D_i\)-th roots have distinct limiting moduli.
This justifies (14) even if the smaller limiting term is zero.

For a hypothetical fixed negative error, the nonnegative measures in (12) have mass at least \(\epsilon\) and at most \(\log\Theta+(\log2)/D_i\).
The upper bound uses the nonzero integral polynomial \(P_i\), whose logarithmic measure is nonnegative.
Compactness of the circle gives a weak subsequential limit \(\nu\), and testing by the constant one preserves the lower bound on its mass.
The logarithm of the analytic difference has its Poisson boundary integral as an upper bound, including boundary zeros by regularization.
The boundary measure occurs in this upper bound with the negative sign in (15).
At fixed \(z\) its Poisson kernel is continuous on the circle, so weak convergence and (14) give (16) on the dense set.
Both sides of (16) are continuous throughout the punctured disk, which also covers zeros without taking their logarithms.

On every circle of radius \(\rho\) sufficiently near one, \(C\) is zero-free and its boundary modulus tends uniformly to one as \(\rho\uparrow1\).
Taking logarithms and averaging (16) yields \(t\log\rho+\int\log|C(\rho e^{iv})|\,d\nu_{\rm Haar}(v)\le-\nu(\mathbb T)\).
Here the Poisson kernel has angular mean one, and the left side tends to zero.
This contradicts the positive deficit mass.
The subsequence argument applies to every hypothetical violation with fixed \(K,\Theta,T,\epsilon\), giving the claimed uniformity without an effective lower error rate.
Each nonconstant coprime seed is nonreciprocal up to sign, so v337 gives the plastic lower limit for the whole specialized polynomial.
Along an input sequence (3) with \(d\to\infty\), the effective denominator is \(d\), and positive measures tending to zero therefore require at least one of the three budgets to be unbounded.

### The scope examples

For the plastic seed, \(f+zg=-(z^4+1)\), so \(s=d\) gives arbitrarily large unreduced \(d\) with \(D=1\) and measure one.
The same seed has \(g\equiv-z(z+1)\bmod f\).
Monicity and \(f(0)=f(-1)=-1\) give \(\operatorname{Res}(f,z)=\operatorname{Res}(f,z+1)=1\), and the scalar \(-1\) contributes sign \((-1)^3\).
Thus its seed resultant is exactly \(-1\).

For \(S_i/D_i\to\infty\), the second \(D_i\)-th-root modulus vanishes at every punctured interior point.
The Poisson majorization can then retain only \(B\).
For the multinacci Pisot units the unique interior pole tends to \(1/2\).
Their elementary Laurent identities give \(f_i/g_i\to-1/(1-2z)\) away from \(1/2\), and the positive normalization yields \(C=(1-2z)/(2-z)\) and \(B=-1/(2-z)\) on the entire disk.
The density \(\log|2-e^{iv}|\) is nonnegative, has mean \(\log2\) and has harmonic extension \(\log|2-z|\).
It therefore achieves equality in (18) with positive mass.
This demonstrates a possible loss in that inequality alone, while actual specialized measures in the unbounded-offset regime remain undecided by this argument.

Finally, \(U(y)=1-2y\) and \(V(y)=y-2\) have distinct roots and gcd one.
Their displayed polynomial with residue one equals \(x^d(x-2)+(1-2x)\).
The summands without \(x^d\) have equal modulus on the circle, and Rouché's theorem followed by a limit gives at most one exterior root counting multiplicity.
The polynomial is negative at two and positive for sufficiently large positive \(x\), so this root is real and simple.
Its equation implies \(0<\beta_d-2=(2\beta_d-1)/\beta_d^d\le2^{2-d}\), verifying convergence to two.
