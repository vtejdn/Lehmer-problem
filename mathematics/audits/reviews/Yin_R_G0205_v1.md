### Exact input, trace and state obligations

For the original nonmonomial integer P with actual span d>=1 and nonzero endpoints, b_d=a_0 a_d is nonzero.
Symmetric Laurent expansion gives B=b_0+sum_{l=1}^d b_l(z^l+z^-l).
The monic triangular integer polynomials C_l(x) satisfy C_l(z+z^-1)=z^l+z^-l, so R=H^2-b_0-sum b_l C_l has exact degree d and signed leading coefficient -a_0 a_d.
No leading coefficient is normalized away.
Triangle inequality on the original circle gives 0<=R<=H^2.

Under x=2 cos(theta), Haar probability pushes forward to dx/(pi sqrt(4-x^2)).
This accounts for both halves of the circle and the normalization integral one.
For any integer polynomial Q(x), the arcsine integral equals CT Q(z+z^-1); it is an integer.
In particular I[0,2h]=binomial(2h,h), I[0,2h+1]=0 and every I[n,k] is integral.
The bounds |I[n,k]|<=2^k H^(2n) and 0<=I[n+1,0]<=H^2 I[n,0] follow from the actual nonnegative R, and do not require positivity of its coefficients or of odd-k states.

The zero polynomial is excluded.
For a nonzero monomial P=a z^j, R=0, I[n,k]=0 for n>=1 and all nu_n vanish; at n=0 the usual initial arcsine moments remain.
This is the direct mathematical exceptional branch. fast.py deliberately rejects monomials and endpoint-zero representations because its declared domain is the stripped nonmonomial actual span.
No native monomial test or implementation coverage beyond that domain is claimed.

### Boundary, signs, triangularity and exact division

For fixed n,j>=0 consider F(x)=x^j sqrt(4-x^2) R(x)^(n+1).
Both endpoint limits are zero, including endpoint zeros of R.
Its derivative is a polynomial divided by sqrt(4-x^2), an integrable singularity.
Integrating on [-2+epsilon,2-epsilon] and passing to the endpoints therefore gives the exact zero total derivative.
Writing it over the arcsine measure yields

    (4-x^2)[j x^(j-1) R+(n+1)x^j R']-x^(j+1)R.

The r_l contributions are exactly 4[j+(n+1)l]r_l x^(j+l-1) and -[j+1+(n+1)l]r_l x^(j+l+1).
When j=l=0, the coefficient of the formal negative-power term is zero and that term is omitted.
The derivation never divides by R, so interior zeros, endpoint zeros and repeated zeros require no additional exclusion.

For k>d and j=k-d-1, the only degree-k term has coefficient -[j+1+(n+1)d]r_d.
Since d>=1 and n,j>=0, this signed coefficient is nonzero.
Every other exponent is smaller than k.
Moving that term to the other side gives exactly D25 (5), including its minus sign on the second sum and its signed denominator.
This successively computes I[n,d+1],...,I[n,2d] from the d+1 retained moments.
Multiplication by R gives the full next state through D25 (6), whose maximum required index is 2d.
Induction from the exact initial integral identifies every recovered state with its actual integral.
Integrality then proves every individual division exact.
It is not an assumption of monic R or a consequence of positive numerators.

### Operation, bit and output costs

Constructing the autocorrelations and all C_l coefficients costs O(d^2) integer operations.
One update performs d reductions with O(d) terms each and d+1 sums of length d+1.
For N>=1 the resulting O(Nd^2) arithmetic count is valid, with O(d) live state integers when output history is not retained.
The direct growing Laurent product has support O(nd) and a fixed O(d)-width multiplier, giving O(N^2d^2) arithmetic operations.
These are upper bounds, not claims that sparse inputs attain them or that bigint operations are constant time.

The basis recurrence gives ||C_l||_1<=2^(l+1); together with the original autocorrelation l1 bound this yields the displayed conservative W<=2^(d+2)H^2.
For j<d, each first-sum coefficient multiplier is bounded by 4(n+2)d and each second-sum multiplier by (n+2)d.
Summing absolute values against |I[n,k]|<=2^(2d)H^(2n) proves the numerator bound 5(n+2)d W 2^(2d)H^(2n), including every partial sum.
The divisor is at most (n+2)d W; state-update sums omit the extra nd factor.
Consequently B_N=O((N+1)log(2H)+d+log(N+2)+log(d+1)) bounds all relevant bigint sizes.
Standard quadratic multiplication and long division give the stated conservative O(Nd^2 B_N^2) bit upper bound; live state needs O(d B_N) bits.

Retaining every scalar numerator through N can cost O(N^2 log(2H)) bits.
All retained state vectors additionally cost a factor d and the degree contribution to each value's size.
Thus checkpoint-only retention is a narrower data scope and does not imply constant total-output cost for all moments.
The source polynomial and metadata remain separate input costs.
No floating complexity estimate is substituted for these bit bounds.
