## Signed Herglotz measure and coefficient comparison

From \(C/B=zK_q/(1-z^2)\) one obtains \(J_q=j/K_q=zF/(1-z^2)\) with \(F=jB/C\).
Writing \(E=\int d\kappa\), the part of \(F\) linear in \(x\) is \((1-E)x-a-\int y\,d\kappa\), and the remainder is \((x^2-4)\int (x-y)^{-1}\,d\kappa\).
After multiplication by \(z/(1-z^2)\), the integrand in the remainder becomes \((1-z^2)/(1-yz+z^2)\), the transform of the probability giving half its mass to each point over \(y\).
For the linear part the numerator after multiplication by \(z\) is
\[
(1-E)(1+z^2)-(a+\textstyle\int y\,d\kappa)z
   =\alpha(1+z)^2+\beta(1-z)^2 .
\]
Putting \(z=1,-1\) gives \(\alpha=F(2)/4=-j/[4(q-1)]\) and \(\beta=-F(-2)/4\ge j/[4(q+1)]\).
All interior trace weights are positive, and evaluating \(J_q(0)=1\) gives \(\alpha+\beta+E=1\), including \(E=0\).
For the genuine \(q=4,\ C=2x-1,\ B=x^2-2x-1\) example of L462v43, I checked the exact transform with \(\alpha=-1/6,\ \beta=7/10\) and \(7/15\) divided equally over the two points of trace \(1/2\).

On the specified path \(C_h(2)=h+2\) and \(q-1\mid h+2\), hence \(h\to\infty\).
Multiplying the exact numerator from L462v44 by \(J_q/2\) verifies the coefficient identity through degree \(2h\).
Fixed coefficients of \(B^\sharp\) converge to those of \(V/U_0\) because \(K_q\to K\), \(K(0)=2\), and the exact numerator stabilizes at each degree.
The integral coefficients therefore agree eventually at each fixed index.
If agreement persisted through \(h+1\) along a subsequence, the coefficients of \(U_0B^\sharp\) in this first range would equal those of the fixed polynomial \(V\).
Signed reciprocity of \(U_0B^\sharp\) in degree \(2h+2+\deg U_0\) controls the reflected range, leaving at most \(\deg U_0+2\) further positions.
This gives bounded support and contradicts the fixed-support Mahler gap because \(B^\sharp\) has exactly one exterior root tending to 1 and \(M(U_0)=1\).
Here \(j=2,\ q\to\infty\) gives \(\theta-q\to0\) by the branch derivative identity, as required for the delay-one return.
Thus the first discrepancy \(n\) exists, tends to infinity, and obeys \(n\le h+1\) eventually.
In particular every fixed \(n+l\) lies between 1 and \(2h\) eventually.

## The analytic discrepancy and the six exclusions

Put \(\tau_q=2(\omega_q-\alpha\delta_1)\).
For each \(k>0\), \(\int v^k\,d\tau_q=[z^k]J_q-2\alpha\), and the total mass tends to two.
These measures converge weakly to \(\tau\) with transform \(2J\), so \([z^k]J=\int v^k\,d\tau\) for \(k>0\).
Convergence follows from convergence of both signs of the fixed moments and density of trigonometric polynomials.
Rationality of \(J=2/K\) places its circle poles among the roots of \(U_0\).
For a positive Herglotz measure, the radial kernel bound gives at most a simple pole and the radial residue determines the atom there.
Removing these kernels leaves a rational function regular across the circle and its boundary real part gives the continuous density \(\sigma=2\Re J\) of \(\tau\) away from the pole locations.
In particular \(\sigma\) is essentially bounded and invariant under reflection.

In the weak limit of the measures on \((v,v^n)\), integration against \(v^l u\) gives the limit of the \((n+l)\)-th moments of \(\tau_q\) for all fixed signed \(l\).
The first marginal is \(\tau\), and the complex measure \(\chi\) obtained by weighting by \(u\) satisfies \(|\chi|\le\tau\).
After fixing \(n\) modulo all orders of the finitely many atoms, the moving moments of \(\tau\) tend to those of \(\chi_0\) at index \(l\), because the continuous part has Fourier coefficients tending to zero.
Since \(\alpha\to0\), the moment of \(\chi-\chi_0\) at \(l\) is exactly the limit \(d_l\) of the Taylor coefficient differences of \(J_q,J\) at \(n+l\).
For \(l<0\) the prefix agreement of the integral series transfers through the invertible factor \(1-z/2\), giving \(d_l=0\).
For any fixed \(v_0\), the Cesàro averages of \((v/v_0)^{-k}\), \(1\le k\le N\), tend boundedly to the indicator of \(v_0\).
Their integrals against \(\chi-\chi_0\) vanish, so \(\chi\) and \(\chi_0\) have exactly the same atoms even if mass had approached an atom from nearby points.
Off the finite atomic support \(|\chi|\le\sigma\,dm\) for normalized arclength \(m\).
It follows that \(\chi-\chi_0=g\,dm\) with \(|g|\le\sigma\).

In the convention \(\widehat f(l)=\int v^{-l}f(v)\,dm(v)\), \(f(v)=g(v^{-1})\) has \(\widehat f(l)=d_l\).
Its negative Fourier coefficients vanish, so its Poisson extension \(F_1\) is bounded and analytic, with \(|F_1(v)|\le\sigma(v)\) almost everywhere by reflection symmetry.
The coefficients of \((1-z/2)F_1\) are \(d_l-d_{l-1}/2\) for \(l\ge0\), using \(d_{-1}=0\).
These are limits of integers by the moving-window identity, and the first is the limit of nonzero integers, hence a nonzero integer.
The bounded analytic function has square summable coefficients by Parseval on radii tending to one.
Its integer coefficients consequently have finite support, which gives the asserted polynomial \(H\).

Jensen's formula applies to \(F_1=H/(1-z/2)\), with \(|F_1(0)|\ge1\).
The nonzero rational densities have integrable logarithms, including at their isolated zeros.
Since \(J=2/K\) and \(\rho=2\Re K\), one has \(\sigma=2\rho/|K|^2\) almost everywhere.
The cyclotomic factors have measure one and \(M(2-z)=2\), giving \(M(\sigma)=M(\rho)/2\).
I recomputed \(K(z)+K(1/z)\) and \(J(z)+J(1/z)\) for all nine rows of L462v44.
The four rows with \(M(\rho)=1\) contradict \(1\le M(F_1)\le M(\sigma)\).

For \(V=1-z^2\) or \(1-z+z^2\) with \(U_0=1\) I confirmed \(\rho=2\) and \(\sigma=|V/(1-z/2)|^2\).
The rational quotient \(F_1/(V/(1-z/2))^2\) is holomorphic in the disk.
A genuine pole at a circle zero of \(V\) would violate the almost-everywhere bound along an adjacent arc.
After canceling any removable factors the maximum principle and its value at zero force this quotient to be a real constant of modulus one.
The linear coefficient of \(V^2/(1-z/2)\) is \(1/2\) for \(1-z^2\) and \(-3/2\) for \(1-z+z^2\), which contradicts integrality for either global sign.
The three remaining necessary profiles are \(2-z,\ (2-z)/(1-z)\) and \((2-z)(1+z)\).
The argument establishes the six exclusions conditional on the successful inverse constructions on this path and does not decide existence for the three remaining profiles.
