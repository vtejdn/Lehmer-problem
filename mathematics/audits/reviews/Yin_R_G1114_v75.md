## Finding on the odd norm, local transport and gap criterion

At 2026-11-16T16:55 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin inspected the complete [dyadic-order claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#statement).
The reviewer had worked on Pisot trace prefixes and had seen the earlier aggregate first-return statement, but took no part in developing this argument.
The assignment supplied the fixed claim without its development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict on it.
All three argument sections and the stated equivalence pass, including the uniformity for varying bases and the restriction by an arbitrary \(g(d)\to\infty\).

Since \(p\) is odd, exponentiation by \(p\) permutes the primitive \(J\)-th roots.
Thus the absolute resultant of \(Q(z^p)\) at those roots is the same as that of \(Q\).
The exact cyclotomic polynomial is \(z^{J/2}+1\), and evaluation at the conjugates of \(\alpha\) gives the absolute norm.
This norm is nonzero since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
For the [multiplicity formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#local) the input polynomial is \(Q\), the tame order is one, and its sole residue root of that order has multiplicity \(h\le D<J/2\) because \(p\ge5\).
In particular the strict ramification threshold holds even if the reduction is inseparable or \(h=0\).
Equivalently, evaluation at a primitive \(J\)-th root gives the unique minimal \(2\)-adic valuation \(h/(J/2)\) in the Taylor expansion around one.
All \(J/2\) evaluations therefore contribute \(h\) in total, proving the claimed odd part.

Equation (2) of the [signed resultant estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) applies with degree \(D\), exponent \(J/2\) and sign \(-1\).
No conjugate of the irreducible \(Q\) can be torsion.
The product formula for the \(p\)-th roots of each conjugate gives \(m(P)=m(Q)\).
Using \(m(Q)\le1\), the [positive-measure degree bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#transfer) and \(J\in[d,2d)\), the relative error after removal of \(2^h\) is bounded by a constant times
\[
 \frac{D^3\log^4(2D)\log(2d)+D\log^3(2D)}{d}.
\]
This tends uniformly to zero already for \(D=O(\log d)\), with bounded \(D\) included.
The stronger degree restriction is available for the discriminant comparison.
The [signed-estimate review](Yin_R_G1114_v40.md#cyclic-substitution-review) covers the two-logarithm and positive-measure inputs at these conventions.

For an odd prime divisor of the norm, a prime ideal dividing \(\alpha^{J/2}+1\) gives a residue element annihilated by the \(J\)-th power and taking value \(-1\ne1\) at \(J/2\).
Its order is exactly \(J\), a power of two, hence \(J\mid\ell^f-1\) for its residue degree \(1\le f\le D\).
For \(f\) odd the odd geometric sum leaves \(v_2(\ell-1)\).
For \(v_2(f)=s>0\) the two neighboring even integers \(\ell-1,\ell+1\) have smaller valuation one, and the other \(s-1\) difference-of-squares factors have valuation one apiece.
Thus \(v_2(\ell^f-1)=s+\max(v_2(\ell-1),v_2(\ell+1))\) in the latter case.
These two possibilities yield the two classes modulo \(B\), with \(B\ge J/D\ge p\).
For \(B\ge4\) the sole possible prime \(\le B\) in them is \(B-1\), of class three modulo four, excluding the chosen \(p\).

The base [discriminant bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#discriminant) gives \(D\log D+2(D-1)m(Q)\) as an upper bound on \(\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|\).
Here \(D\log D\le(\log d)/4\) eventually and \(2D=o(\log d)\), whereas \(\ell\ge B-1\ge d/D-1>d^{1/3}\) eventually.
No contributing prime can divide \(\operatorname{disc}Q\).
In the product of derivatives \(p\gamma^{p-1}Q'(\gamma^p)\) over the \(d\) roots, the \(\gamma^{p-1}\) contribute absolute product one because \(Q(0)\) is a unit.
Each \(Q'\) value occurs \(p\) times, giving \(|\operatorname{disc}P|=p^d|\operatorname{disc}Q|^p\) and hence separability also for \(P\).

Fix \(\gamma^p=\alpha\).
An odd prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of the base with residue order \(J\) divides \(\alpha^{J/2}+1\), so the preceding exclusion of \(\ell=p\) applies to every such ideal.
Exponentiation by \(p\) on \(\langle\bar\alpha\rangle\) is bijective.
Its order-\(J\) solution lies in the base residue field, and multiplying by a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity gives order \(pJ\) since \((p,J)=1\).
The polynomial \(X^p-\alpha\) is the minimal polynomial over the base field by the relative degree \(p\).
It has simple reduction at \(\mathfrak q\).
Its local factors correspond to the field factors of \(L\otimes_K K_{\mathfrak q}\), one for each prime above \(\mathfrak q\).
The unique simple root with order \(J\) lifts to one linear factor by Hensel's lemma, all other factors having reductions of order \(pJ\).
The completion, residue field, ramification index and normalized valuation therefore agree at the unique matching prime.
Conversely taking the \(p\)-th power preserves order \(J\) at every order-\(J\) prime upstairs.
The geometric quotient of \(\gamma^{pJ}-1\) by \(\gamma^J-1\) reduces to the unit \(p\), and the factor \(\alpha^{J/2}-1\) is a unit there as well.
Thus both integral valuations equal that of \(\alpha^{J/2}+1\) in the base field.
Summing each valuation times \(f_{\mathfrak q}\log\ell\) over every matching prime ideal gives exactly \(\log W\).

A uniform positive lower bound for \(m(Q)\) gives \(\log W\gg d\) directly by the uniform relative estimate.
For the converse one first fixes any nontorsion unit of logarithmic measure at most one.
The [power-substitution lemma](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) supplies all sufficiently large prime exponents for that fixed base.
Indeed a proper factor would give a \(p\)-th root of \(\alpha\) in its base field by Bézout's identity, whereas the possible roots there have bounded degree and height and each occurs for at most one prime.
Infinitely many of the admissible primes are one modulo four by the square-plus-one argument in the claim.
With \(D\) fixed, both \(\log d/(4\log\log(3d))\) and any prescribed \(g(d)\to\infty\) eventually exceed \(D\).
Along these degrees \(cd\le\log W=(1+o(1))(J/2)m(Q)\le(1+o(1))d\,m(Q)\), so \(m(Q)\ge c\) independently of the base.
Factorization of a general integer polynomial reduces its positive-measure gap to the one for units.
A nonunit leading coefficient costs at least \(\log2\), and a monic irreducible nonunit factor with nonzero constant has the same lower bound from its norm.
Monomial factors contribute zero and factors of measure greater than one in logarithmic units already exceed the tested range.
The resulting criterion leaves its degree-proportional lower bound as the open condition.

The [earlier primitive-valuation review](Yin_R_G1114_v69.md#exact-orders) and [power-substitution review](Yin_R_G1114_v57.md#primitive-window-review) cover their respective fixed inputs.
The signed root-distance and degree estimates are used at their recorded application scope, without a new audit of the underlying transcendence or Dobrowolski bounds.
