## Finding on the unit coefficients and the degree-nineteen cases

At 2026-11-16T15:23 UTC a reviewer for Yin independently checked both sections of [L462v62](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v62.md#simple).
The reviewer received the fixed ledger and assignment, had no part in developing its argument, and used no proof-development dialogue, earlier review of this ledger or requested verdict.
The circle-root criterion, its coprime corollary, and both endpoint candidates pass.

For \(|z|<1\), the strict bound \(|az^d+bz^e|<2\) excludes zeros of \(A\).
On the boundary equality in the triangle inequality and \(A=0\) require \(az^d=bz^e=-1\), at which \(zA'=-(d+e)\).
These simple roots are closed under inversion and Galois conjugacy, so their constant-normalized product \(G\) is a signed cyclotomic product.
The factor \(E=A/G\) has only exterior zeros.
In the cancelled quotient \(W=\epsilon z^{N-e}E^*/E\), the zeros at zero and the reflected zeros of \(E\) give total degree \(N-e+(e-\deg G)=N-\deg G>0\).
The angular derivative of a finite Blaschke product's argument is a sum of positive Poisson kernels, counting the kernel at zero for each power of \(z\).
Thus its degree counts exactly the distinct simple preimages of \(1\) on the circle.
Any overlap with \(G\) raises the multiplicity to two, and these roots exhaust the degree \(N\) of \(\Pi\).

At a common root \(\zeta\), the derivative quotient for \(A(1/z)/A(z)\) is \(-\zeta^{-2}A'(\zeta^{-1})/A'(\zeta)=-1\).
Hence overlap is equivalent to \(\zeta^N=-1\), which is impossible at either endpoint because \(N\) is even.
Both endpoints are always roots of \(\Pi\).
The Chebyshev identity removes these two simple roots, and the remaining reciprocal pairs map with nonzero derivative to traces in \((-2,2)\).
This proves both directions of the asserted test.
For coprime \(d,e\), any common root has order dividing \(\gcd(2d,2e)=2\).
As a check on the multiplicity condition, \(h=4,d=2,e=4,a=1,b=-1\) gives the common root \(i\) with \(i^{10}=-1\), and the trace \(C(x)=2x^4-5x^2\) has a double zero.
The [six-term restriction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) motivates this sign class, while the [inverse construction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) still requires an integral denominator satisfying the residue and endpoint conditions.

At \(x=\pm2\), evaluation of \(U_k(\pm1)=(\pm1)^k(k+1)\) gives both endpoint formulas.
Since \(m-e,m-d>0\), the endpoint satisfies \(C(2)\ge d+e>0\), and its maximum occurs with two positive signs and least possible sum \(d+e=3\), giving \(4m-3=4h+1\).
For \(h\le18\) this is at most 73, so \(q-1\mid C(2)\) and \(q\ge77\) are incompatible.
At \(h=19\), one negative sign already gives \(C(2)<60\), while two positive signs give \(C(2)=80-d-e\).
Consequently the only eligible pairs have \(d+e=3,4\).
Their positive endpoints are 77 and 76 respectively, and in each case the inverse divisor is one since \(q-1\ge76\).
The complete tuples \((d,e,q,C(-2))\) are \((1,2,78,-39)\) and \((1,3,77,-4)\).
Both pairs pass the coprime test.

For either candidate, a successful denominator would give \(F(T)=0\), \(F(2)=-2/(q-1)\) and \(F'\ge1\) on \([2,T]\) by the [positive-residue expansion](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#endpoints).
Integration gives \(0<T-2\le2/(q-1)\le1/38\).
For the monic [Lehmer trace polynomial](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v37.md#pruning) \(Q=x^5+x^4-5x^3-5x^2+4x+3\), direct evaluation gives \(Q(2+1/38)=-321593/79235168<0\).
Its unique root above two therefore lies strictly above \(2+1/38\).
This verifies the strict comparison conditional on a successful inverse construction.
The ledger supplies two valid numerators and leaves existence of their integer denominators open.
