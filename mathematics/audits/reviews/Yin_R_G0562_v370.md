## Finding on the local lower bound and the sharp trinomial example

At 2026-11-18T11:22 UTC a separately assigned reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the complete [scaled-disk ledger argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v154.md#disk).
The reviewer did not develop that argument and received its fixed text without its proof-development dialogue or earlier findings on it.
The prior general doubling identity was familiar from the reviewer's work on C364; its independent assessment remains at [the earlier whole-claim finding](Yin_R_G0562_v367.md#distinct-two-row-cost-review).
The specialization, its resultant, and the lower bound for every allowed competitor were checked directly here.
The one-disk and additive lower bounds hold in the stated residue characteristics and for all \(e\ge2\).
For the trinomial in the specified prime range the valuation \(p^2s\) is indeed the minimum.

### Initial reductions and the characteristic-dependent multiplicity count

With \(v(\ell)=1\), each Gauss valuation on the given disk belongs to the value group of \(K\), being the minimum among finitely many coefficient valuations.
Common scaling by the smaller valuation therefore leaves two integral polynomials and at least one nonzero reduction over \(k\).
After removing the initial monomial of each row, the coefficient of \(Y^j\) in \(W(b+\tau Y)\) is divisible by \(\tau^j\) in \(\mathcal O\).
This gives \(n=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\le g/\sigma\), even if one reduction vanishes identically.

At a unit \(\alpha_i\), \(p\alpha_i^{p-1}\) is invertible for \(\ell\ne p\).
The inverse branch thus exists with integral coefficients and has nonzero unit first derivative.
Consequently a root of multiplicity at least \(e\) gives a power series divisible by \(T^e\).
The factors removed from the Laurent rows only contribute powers of units to their ratio.
After \(T=\tau Z\), every nonconstant term of its branch factor vanishes on reduction.
This preserves the first \(e\) Taylor congruences without dividing by any factorial.
The common reduction of \(\beta_i\) is nonzero, and the distinct reductions of \(\alpha_i\) have ratios of exact order \(p\).
Hence taking the exponent \(r_1-r_0\) preserves distinctness, and all \((Y-\bar y_i)^e\) divide their respective \(A+\lambda_i B\).
These statements take place in the actual finite residue field of the splitting extension; rational residue nodes are not required.

If \(n<e\), two of these combinations vanish as polynomials and force both rows to vanish, contradicting the normalization.
If removing their gcd leaves a constant pair, at most one of the distinct \(\lambda_i\) can give the zero combination, including the case of a zero row.
At all other nodes the gcd therefore contributes \(e\) zeros counted with multiplicity.
The inequality \(e(t-1)\ge M_e(t)\) follows from \(2e(t-1)-((e-1)t+2)=(e+1)(t-2)\ge0\).

For a nonconstant reduced ratio, put \(h=\deg J\), \(d_0=n-h\), and \(h_i=\operatorname{ord}_{\bar y_i}J\).
When its derivative numerator is nonzero, it has degree at most \(2d_0-2\), since either the two leading terms cancel or at least one original degree was smaller.
At a node with \(h_i<e\), the reduced combination vanishes to order at least \(e-h_i\) and the reduced denominator is a unit there.
Ordinary differentiation lowers the order by at most one in any characteristic.
Thus \(2d_0-2\ge\sum_i(e-1-h_i)_+\ge(e-1)t-h\), and \(2n\ge(e-1)t+2\).
If the derivative vanishes identically, the nonconstant rational ratio belongs to \(k(Y^\ell)\) by differentiating on the basis of powers \(Y^j,\ 0\le j<\ell\).
Substitution of \(Y^\ell\) multiplies its rational degree by \(\ell\), giving \(d_0\ge\ell\).
Together with the unconditional \(n\ge e\), this proves \(g\ge\sigma\max(e,\min(\ell,M_e(t)))\), including \(e\ge\ell\) and the threshold \(\ell=M_e(t)\).
The normalization of \(\sigma\) is unchanged by any later finite splitting extension.

### Resultants, row contents and disjoint reductions

For a normalized linear factor \(uX+vZ\) the precise Gauss valuation after scaling is \(\min(v(ub_j+v),v(u)+\sigma_j)\).
If \(v(ub_j+v)>0\), then \(u\) has to be a unit and this minimum is the stated weight \(w_j\).
Otherwise the minimum is zero.
Gauss multiplicativity adds these weights and the scalar content valuation.
A linear factor with positive weight fixes its sole reduced root \(-v/u\), so it contributes on at most one of the chosen original residues.
The determinant of two factors has valuation at least the smaller of the two weights in any disk.
Since both are at most \(\sigma_j\), this dominates their product divided by \(\sigma_j\).
All positive products for that pair occur in at most one selected disk, which justifies adding them on distinct residues.

After common \(\ell\)-content removal at least one row is primitive at \(\ell\).
Call this row \(V_0\) for the determinant estimate and let \(c\) be the content valuation of the other row.
Then \(\sum_j g_{V_0,j}/\sigma_j\le\deg V_0\), since each normalized factor contributes at most one to the sum.
The scalar contribution \(c\deg V_0\) therefore pays for the \(c\sum_j g_{V_0,j}/\sigma_j\) missing from the linear-factor products.
This proves \(v_\ell(H)\ge\sum_j g_{U_0,j}g_{V_0,j}/\sigma_j\) and hence (1) and (3).
Restoring a common scalar of valuation \(c_*\) adds \(c_*(\deg U_0+\deg V_0)\), also when one row is constant.
The same-residue nested-disk discussion correctly identifies the overlap in the linear-factor charges and asserts no additive inequality in that setting.

### The exact lower bound for the adjacent trinomial rows

The trinomial reduces modulo \(p\) to \(x^p-x-1\), and successive Frobenius powers of any root add \(1\) until the first return at \(p\).
This gives irreducibility in the stated family.
At \(\ell\ne p\), the reduction of the trinomial is \(Y^p-1\), with \(p\) distinct roots in a finite residue extension.
The equality \(\beta_i=1+a\alpha_i\) gives \(y_i=\alpha_i,\ \sigma=s\).
In classes \(0,1\), \(\lambda_i=\bar y_i\) remains valid after either individual base-monomial removal because \(\bar b=1\).
Assume both initial degrees are \(<p\).
The polynomial \(A+YB\), of degree at most \(p\), vanishes on all \(p\) nodes and so equals \(c(Y^p-1)\).
At each node \(z\), differentiating \(A+YB=(A+zB)+(Y-z)B\) there gives \(B(z)=cpz^{p-1}\) by the double-jet condition.
Both sides have degree at most \(p-1\) and agree on the full grid, hence \(B=cpY^{p-1}\) and \(A=c((1-p)Y^p-1)\).
Since \(\ell\nmid p-1\), the degree hypothesis forces \(c=0\), contradicting the common scaling.
Thus \(g\ge p s\) and the resultant bound is at least \(p^2s\).

For the witness take \(u=-ax,\ w=1-x^p\), so that \(u-w=P(x)\).
Expansion of \(u^p+(p-1)w^p-puw^{p-1}\) in \(u-w\) has first nonzero term \(\binom p2 w^{p-2}(u-w)^2\) at every root of \(P\) in characteristic zero.
There \(w=-ax\ne0\), giving exactly the stated double multiplicities.
The two displayed rows have nonzero constant terms, and the possible common root \(X=1\) is excluded by \(U_0(1)=(-a)^p\).
As \(\deg U_0=p\), the resultant has magnitude
\[
               (pa)^p |U_0(1)|^{p-1}=p^p a^{p^2}.
\]
Its valuation is \(p^2s\).
Fermat's congruence permits \(s=p-1\) at every \(\ell>p\).
The extra condition \(p\mid\ell-1\) concerns realizing the entire grid in \(\mathbb F_\ell\) instead of its finite extension.
