# Classical subspace actions on conjugate labels

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T16:07 UTC a reviewer outside proof development inspected the complete [C345 v269 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v269.md#decision-classical-subspaces-submitted) for Yin using the fixed proof and its sources.
The exclusion holds for all three cases of Liebeck and Shalev's definition, uniformly in field size, dimensions and almost simple extension at fixed upper bound \(M\) on the positive measure.

### Degrees, field conventions and configurations

In the journal facsimile, Liebeck and Shalev specify on pp. 497–498 the single-subspace stabilizers, the nested or complementary linear pairs of dimensions adding to the ambient dimension, and the orthogonal subgroups in the even-characteristic symplectic group.
On p. 501 they use \(P(G)>q^{n/2}\) apart from finitely many groups, where \(P\) is the smallest faithful permutation degree.
It is the bound for this group invariant that applies to \(T\), including its action on a nontrivial quotient set.
With \(Q\) the natural-module field size, \(q=\sqrt Q\) in the usual unitary notation, so \(Q^{n/4}\) suffices also there.
For an odd-dimensional orthogonal model in characteristic two the symplectic model has dimension \(n-1\), and \((n-1)/2\ge n/4\).
The three factors in Byott's outer automorphism formula are bounded by \(\max(n,4)\), \(2\log_2 Q\) and six respectively, including the doubled field factor for minus orthogonal groups and the triality factor.
Thus \(e=[H:T]\ll n\log(2Q)\).
Together with \(|T|\le Q^{n^2}\), the lower degree bound prevents bounded \(n\) on any subsequence on which \(\log |H|/\log b\) diverges.
Finitely many exceptional \(T\) have bounded \(|H|\).
The known block-size bound gives \(\log d\asymp\log b\), while \(b\le|H|\) gives \(\log d=O(n^2\log(2Q))\).
In particular \(eQ^2=b^{o(1)}\), and \(Q^{n/4}\) dominates \(e(\log(3d))^{212}\) uniformly as \(n\to\infty\).

Primitivity makes the nontrivial normal subgroup \(T\) transitive.
For \(j\) selected labels, their \(H\)-orbit comprises at most \(e\) translates of their \(T\)-orbit.
The factors \(u\) and \(w^j\) bound the additional block and root choices.
Thus a \(T\)-orbit bounded by \(b^{j-1}c\) gives absolute joint degree \(O_j(d^{j-1}ec)\), also at most \(d^j\).
The same upper bound applies over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), so Delsinne's theorem at independent roots gives (1).
In a nonsingleton fiber over a nontrivial \(T\)-set of size \(b_0\), two different labels are independent and their ordered orbit has at most \(b^2/b_0\) images under \(T\).
The resulting **absolute** joint degree is at most \(ed^2/b_0\).
The two-height bound with logarithmic exponent 212 contradicts bounded measure since the nontrivial action of the simple \(T\) on the target is faithful.

### Patches on individual subspaces and pairs

A transitive translation action on a patch of size \(|W|\) lifts to a finite group acting on the lattice of its actual roots modulo torsion.
The distinct label lines remain distinct there, so the finite lattice image has order at least \(|W|\), forcing its rank to grow by reduction modulo three.
For \(Q>2\) choose an ordinary pair in the real projective configuration, then a third parameter on the natural-field affine line of that pair.
The three labels are multiplicatively independent.
For \(Q=2\), projection at the origin, removal of repeated projected points, and translation of each triple to any other in the chosen parallelogram force independence of the quartet.
The third subspace in the first construction lies between the common hyperplane and the sum of the first two subspaces, giving at most \(Q+1\) possibilities.
The fourth in the binary construction lies in a quotient of dimension three over the common hyperplane, with at most \(Q^2+Q+1\) possibilities.
These are bounds on orbit images under the linear simple group, allowing the separate \(e\) factor even for graph automorphisms.

In the linear case the shear fixing the hyperplane and ambient complement has determinant one.
For complementary pairs the fixed companion itself supplies the parameter space.
For nested pairs it supplies a complement to the smaller space inside the larger one.
When \(k<n/4\) the different dimensions distinguish both components, and each parameter space has dimension at least \(n/2\).
Otherwise transitivity on \(k\)-spaces and at most two components of dimension \(k\) give \(b\ge Q^{k(n-k)}/2\), bounding the order-to-degree ratio.
This includes unordered equal-dimensional complementary pairs and the coincidence possible in an equal-dimensional nested pair.
Dual spaces give the same counts and equivariant configurations.

With nondegenerate ambient polar or Hermitian form and restricted radical of dimension at most one, \(\operatorname{rad}(U^\perp)=U\cap U^\perp\).
A complement to this radical inside \(U^\perp\) is nondegenerate and disjoint from \(U\).
After passing to \(U^\perp\) if needed one has \(k\le n/2\), and the indicated complement contains a totally singular space \(W\) of dimension at least \((n-k-1)/2-1\).
On \(U+W\) the translations and linear changes of coordinates on \(W\) preserve the complete form, since \(W\) is totally singular and perpendicular to \(U\).
Taylor's Theorem 7.4 applies to this possibly degenerate subspace because the ambient radical is zero.
In the induced affine group, commutating the shear \(t_i\mapsto t_i+t_j\) on translation coordinates with translation in the \(j\)-direction produces translation in the \(i\)-direction with arbitrary coefficient.
Lifting those commutators puts the translations in the projective simple group, by determinant in the unitary group and the definition of \(\Omega\) in the orthogonal group.

For a totally singular \(U\) the same construction works inside a maximal singular space when its codimension over \(U\) is at least \(n/8\).
Otherwise the finite-field Witt index gives \(k\ge n/4\) for large \(n\).
The graphs in the paired hyperbolic space are distinct for distinct matrices.
Graph shears preserve the alternating form for symmetric matrices, the quadratic form for alternating matrices with zero diagonal, and the Hermitian form for skew-Hermitian matrices, including fixed-field alternating matrices.
Even the latter count is \(Q^{k(k-1)/4}\).
The full linear isometry orbit loses a factor of at most \(2(Q+1)\) on restriction to the simple group.
For orthogonal groups with zero polar radical, Taylor's reflection generation excludes only \(O^+(4,2)\) in its scope.
Witt extension conjugates reflections whose defining lines have the same square class of nonzero quadratic values, so the abelianization has at most two involutory generators.
The resulting orbit lower bound applies also when the maximal singular spaces split into two simple-group orbits.
The quadratic lower exponent in \(n\) bounds \(\log|H|/\log b\), again a contradiction.

### Even-characteristic refinements and odd orthogonal spaces

Two refinements of a fixed symplectic form differ by the square of a unique linear functional, and nondegeneracy identifies that functional with a unique \(t\).
For \(t\) in a totally singular \(W\), the transvection in the proof satisfies \(F(x+B(x,t)t)=F(x)+B(x,t)^2\) and fixes \(W\) pointwise.
It therefore sends \(F_s\) to \(F_{s+t}\) for \(s\in W\).
Both orthogonal types have such a \(W\) of dimension at least \(a-1\).
The binary parallelogram argument uses the additive group of \(W\) over its prime field, even when \(Q>2\).
The fourth quadratic function is the sum of the first three, an identity preserved under every pullback by \(T\), giving at most \(b^3\) ordered images.

In odd dimension with degenerate polar form but regular quadratic form, normalize \(F(r)=1\) on the radical line \(R\).
An isometry fixes \(r\) because squaring is injective.
For a symplectic map of \(V/R\), the discrepancy in quadratic values on a chosen complement is the square of a linear functional.
Adding its value times \(r\) gives the unique isometric lift, in agreement with Taylor's Theorem 11.9.
For a proper nonzero projected space \(Y\), irreducibility of the natural symplectic action makes its orbit nontrivial.
Multiple fibers are excluded by the two-height argument.
With singleton fibers the \(T\)-sets are isomorphic, and the restricted polar radical on \(Y\) has dimension \(\dim\operatorname{rad}(U)-\dim(U\cap R)\).
Thus the projected label is totally isotropic when \(U\) is totally singular, or has polar radical at most one in the remaining cases.
The previous constructions use just these conditions.
Zero image gives only the fixed radical line for a nonzero label, whereas full image makes the proper subspace a complement and hence gives the refinement action.
These alternatives exhaust the degenerate-polar case.
Finally bounded \(d\) and \(m\) bound every coefficient of the primitive minimal polynomial of the class product, giving finitely many measure values.
