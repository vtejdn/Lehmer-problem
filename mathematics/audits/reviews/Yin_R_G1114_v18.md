## Finding

The complete v5 argument establishes both assertions and both families of positive examples.
The constant in both uses of the standard theorem is the same [Lehmer root](../../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v2.md#statement).

Moving a positive factor \(Q\) across a transvection replaces its parameter \(v\) by \(Qv\), because \(Q\) fixes both cusp vectors and preserves the pairing.
Consequently the positive product \(R\) retains its original order of multiplication and preserves \(3H\).
Expanding \((BR)^m\) gives the ordered factors \(R^iBR^{-i}\), \(0\le i<m\), followed by \(R^m\).
Only this last positive factor needs a signed-permutation extension for the first assertion.

The dual unit lines are the four coordinate lines and the eight half-vector lines.
Two different half-vector lines of the same parity differ in exactly two signs for any chosen representatives, so their inner product vanishes.
The absolute inner product across distinct frames is \(1/2\), which makes the three orthogonal frames intrinsic.
On an original cycle of length \(\ell\), stepping \(m\) places at a time returns after \(\ell/g\) steps for \(g=\gcd(\ell,m)\).
The return is therefore conjugate to \(h_C^{m/g}\) at every starting block, including along each separate cycle of \(R^m\).
An element of \(S_3\) outside the three-cycles already fixes a frame.
A three-cycle fixes a frame after this power precisely when \(3\mid m/g\), or \(v_3(m)>v_3(\ell)\).
Transport then supplies a consistent frame on every block exactly for the multiples of \(k\), including every positive \(m\) when \(\mathcal B\) is empty.

The chosen orthonormal dual frame gives integer coordinates preserving the norm in each block, and arbitrary orientations produce a signed permutation for \(R^k\).
The even lattice norm supplies the parity hypothesis of v3 for every transformed parameter.
Each eigenvalue of \(A\) contributes its \(k\)-th power with its original algebraic multiplicity to the spectrum of \(A^k\), so \(M(P_{A^k})=M(P_A)^k\) even when powered eigenvalues coincide.
The gap and the strict interval of v3 apply to precisely this powered measure.
The earlier \(d\)-bound applies to the same word, so taking the larger of the two lower bounds gives exponent \(1/\min(d,k)\).
For a sequence with \(M(P_A)>1\) tending to one, \(k\log M(P_A)\ge\log\lambda_{\rm L}>0\) forces \(k\to\infty\).

For the simultaneous construction, each of the three frames contributes one copy of the original bilinear form.
Thus \(J\) scales it by three, and orienting all lines gives an intertwining signed permutation for each positive factor separately.
For \(F(e)=3e'\), \(F(f)=f'\), the linear pairings with \(e',f',Jv\) are \((e,x),3(f,x),3(v,x)\) respectively.
In the \(e'\)-transvection the squared norm of \(Jv\) is \(3(v,v)\), agreeing with the factor three in \(F(e)\).
In the \(f'\)-transvection the squared norm of \(Jw/3\) is \((w,w)/3\), which cancels the factor three in \((f',Fx)\).
The other linear pairing is \((Jw/3,Fx)=(w,x)\), proving intertwining for both cusp generators.
When \(w=3u\), the ambient parameters \(Jv\) and \(Ju\) are integral and have even norms \(3(v,v)\) and \(3(u,u)\).
The image contains the ambient hyperbolic plane over the reals, so its orthogonal complement is positive definite and invariant under the ambient isometry.
All complementary eigenvalues have unit modulus, and the direct sum gives exact equality of the two complete Mahler measures.
The standard theorem applies with the identity embedding of the ambient lattice, and interchanging \(e,f\) proves the other cusp case.

For the examples, the order-three leaf cycle moves the coordinate frame.

The two remaining diagonal entries sum to \(2-cn+c^2n^2/4\).
