### Residual orders, multiplicities and the divisor lemma

Because \(S\) is monic, its reduction has the same degree \(d\), so residual divisibility implies \(d\le B\).
If an order \(t>1\) occurs, it is coprime to \(p\), divides \(B\) and contributes an orbit of size \(f_t=\operatorname{ord}_t(p)\).
For an odd prime \(q\mid t\), setting \(u=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\) gives \(v_q(p^{u v}-1)=v_q(p^u-1)+v_q(v)\).
For \(q=2\) at odd \(p\), the corresponding loss is bounded by \(v_2(p^2-1)-1\) for both parities of the exponent.
The resulting prime-power divisors of \(f_t\) multiply since they are coprime.
The prime support of \(B\) is fixed, hence \(t/f_t\) is bounded, also at \(t=1,\ f_1=1\).

Writing \(J=p^eJ'\) with \(p\nmid J'\) shows \(X^J-1=(X^{J'}-1)^{p^e}\) in the residual ring, where the polynomial inside is separable.
An orbit with multiplicity \(\mu\) is therefore captured precisely at multiples of \(b=t p^{\lceil\log_p\mu\rceil}\), including \(\mu=1\).
The bound \(b\le p\mu t\ll\mu f_t\le d\) gives the active-divisor size hypothesis up to a fixed constant.
For a fixed \(b\), all contributing orbits have the same order \(b/p^{v_p(b)}\), are disjoint on the \(\varphi(t)\) primitive roots, and have multiplicities bounded by \(p^{v_p(b)}\).
Their aggregated weight is at most \(b\).
An order may occur at several \(b\)'s when orbit multiplicities differ, but each orbit contributes its full degree with multiplicity just once.

I checked the [numerical box lemma](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v256.md#box) at these weights.
Absorbing overlapping prime powers of \(H\) into the variable exponents preserves \(r\) and gives the required coprime fixed cofactor.
The complementary-divisor shell count has \(O((1+u)^{r-1})\) choices in a logarithmic interval of length one, leaving a fixed fraction of the weight above \(\eta d/(1+\log(B/d))^{r-1}\).
Binning the first \(r-1\) complementary coordinates in intervals of length \(W=(1+\log(B/d))^{1-1/r}\) gives \(O(W)\) bins, since \(1-(1-1/r)=1/r\).
Within a bin the last coordinate varies by \(O(W)\) because the total coordinate sum ranges over an interval of width \(O(1+(r-1)\log(1+\log(B/d)))\).
For \(r=1\) there are no binned coordinates and that width is bounded.
Taking coordinatewise minima of the complements thus bounds the lcm of one heavy bin by \(C d\exp(CW)\).
The proof allows the prime \(p\) among the variable primes and also permits \(B=d\).

### Resultants and the estimate at surviving cuts

Every selected \(b\mid J\) contributes its entire orbit with multiplicity to \(\gcd(\bar S,X^J-1)\).
The selected weight \(\delta d\) is an integer and satisfies \(\delta\gg 1/W\).
For \(j\ge0\) the quotient in the proof reduces to \((X^{p^jJ}-1)^{p-1}\), which is divisible by \(X^J-1\), also when \(p\mid J\).
Multiplication by each of the \(i+1\) factors on the rank-\(d\) integral quotient has cokernel modulo \(p\) of dimension \(\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar G)\), for the particular factor \(G\).
Smith normal form gives the asserted \(p\)-adic contribution for each factor, and nontorsion excludes a zero determinant in characteristic zero.
The product of all determinants is the resultant with \(X^{p^iJ}-1\), up to the immaterial sign convention.
The archimedean bound is \(2^d\exp(p^iJm)\).
At \(i=\lceil1/\delta\rceil\), one has \((i+1)\delta\log p-\log2\ge\delta\log2\) and \(p^i\le\exp(O_p(W))\).
Combining this with the lcm bound gives (1), since \(W\ge1\) allows the remaining \(W^{-1}\) factor to be absorbed in the exponential.

At every surviving cut with gap \(G\), the two descriptions of the partial sum and the integer norm give \(Gm\le d\log L\).
Summing these inequalities gives \(Dm\le(s-1)d\log L\).
Inserting \(B\le AD\) yields the stated upper estimate proportional to \(\log L\,e^{-X}\) with \(X=\log(B/d)\).
The coefficient of \(X^\gamma\) is fixed with \(\gamma<1\), so after comparison with (1) all sufficiently large \(X\) satisfy \(X\ll1+\log(1+\log L)\).
This also covers \(r=1\) and the bounded values of \(X\), since \(L\ge2\).
The coefficient dependence in (2) follows.
At fixed \(L\), the cut condition therefore gives both the gap and \(d\gg D\).
For \(s\le5\) a vanishing cut would require a monomial or binomial to vanish on a nontorsion unit.
For \(s=6,7\) the smaller side has at most three terms, and the individual trinomial bound gives \(m\ge\log\theta\) whenever there is a vanishing cut.
The unconditional assertion through seven terms in v257 is the gap.

### Applications and coefficient length

For the five terms at \(0,n-l,n,n+l,2n\), the length is \(2|a|+2|b|+|c|\) and the support degree is \(2n\).
Under the stated parities the residual polynomial times \(X^n-1\) equals \(X^{3n}-1\) in characteristic two, with multiplicity for every \(n\).
Thus \(B=3n\) satisfies the size condition with \(A=3/2\).
Fixed prime support of \(n\) supplies the required period family even when \(2\) or \(3\) divides \(n\).
The extension beyond the earlier one-additional-prime bound concerns the allowed period primes, with the dependence on coefficient length stated in v257.

For the four terms at \(0,k,D-k,D\), the length is \(2|a|+2|b|\).
At \(p\mid b\) the reduction is a nonzero multiple of \(X^D+\sigma\).
At \(p\mid a\) it is a nonzero multiple of \(X^k(X^{D-2k}+\sigma)\).
The monic integral division descends modulo \(p\), and the unit constant term permits cancellation of \(X^k\).
For either resulting \(T\) and either sign, \(X^T+\sigma\) divides \(X^{2T}-1\) as a polynomial, including in characteristic two.
These facts check the whole residual divisor, so repeated factors require no extra restriction.
The prime is fixed in the stated application, and allowing a choice among coefficient primes at bounded length only requires taking the minimum over finitely many primes.

The respective common exponent gcds are \(\gcd(n,l)\) and \(\gcd(D,k)\).
Compressing by that gcd preserves signs, term counts and lengths and divides the relevant period parameter by precisely the same gcd.
The [radical comparison](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) applies to every branch of the root and transfers the gap at cost \(R_L\).
The original cut bound at four or five terms gives proportional degree at the original width.
Finally, a primitive irreducible nonunit contributes at least \(\log2\) from its leading or constant coefficient, and nontrivial content contributes at least \(\log2\) as well.
Additivity and nonnegativity of logarithmic measure complete the integral-divisor claim.
