## Independently reconstructed arbitrary-row obstruction

If prime reciprocals had finite sum, -log(1-1/p)<=1/p+O(1/p^2) outside finitely many primes would make the reciprocal Euler products bounded.
Their finite expansions include any fixed harmonic partial sum after enough primes have been included, contradicting harmonic divergence.
Removing the finitely many primes<=R preserves divergence.
Therefore a finite product of DISTINCT primes>R has product(1-1/p)<=1/2, so a squarefree a as in D49 exists.
No rate, effective bound for a or distribution theorem is needed.

For each r<=R and proper g|a, gcd(r,a)=1 and a/g has a prime>R, hence g<=a/(R+1).
Thus b=rg<a, gcd(a,b)=g, lcm(a,b)=ar<=Ra and phi(b)=phi(r)phi(g).
Prime factorization separates the <=R primes in r from the >R primes in g, proving the constructed b are all distinct, and none is a.
The sum of phi(g) over all divisors is a by grouping residues by gcd; removing g=a gives a-phi(a).
Including b=a therefore gives the exact selected mass phi(a)+(a-phi(a))F_R, at least aF_R/2.
This remains a LOWER bound on the full row, with no omitted-term equality claimed.

For each odd prime power p^e, p^(e-1)(p-1)>=p^(e/2); for powers of2 the only loss relative to square root is the factor1/sqrt2 at exponent1.
