# Finite small-value bounds at algebraic conjugates for sparse polynomials

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T03:37 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the whole [C345 v738](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v738.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing this argument and received its fixed text and requested scope without its development discussion or an earlier verdict on v738.
The inspection covered the full-orbit and neutral-phase estimates, the original radial values and the sharpness example.
The term count in the statement is an integer, as required for its sharpness example.

For the primitive polynomial \(a\prod_i(X-z_i)\), integrality and nonvanishing of its discriminant give \(\sum_{i\ne j}\log|z_i-z_j|\ge-(2D-2)\log a\).
The pair comparison in the [reflected energy proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) uses only root locations.
Summed over ordered pairs, the radial factors there cost \(2(D-1)\sum_i\log^+|z_i|\).
The discriminant and radial terms together equal \(2D(D-1)v\) as an upper charge, with the leading coefficient counted once in \(Dv\).
Damping with \(\rho=e^{-1/D}\) costs \(D(D-1)\log(1/\rho)\) off diagonal, and each diagonal term is bounded by \(\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\).
The expansion into nonnegative squares bounds its first harmonic by the full energy.
After division by \(D^2\), the two damping charges are \(O(\log(2D)/D)\), and \(\rho^{-1}\) is bounded.
The mean change from reflected roots to phases is bounded by \(D^{-1}\sum_i|\log|z_i||\le2v\), using the positive archimedean terms for the number and its inverse.
These estimates hold with arbitrary leading and constant coefficients for the stated nonzero inputs.

For \(D\ge1/v\) and \(v\) small, monotonicity gives \(\log(2D)/D\le v\log(2/v)\), within (2).
If \(D\le v^{-1/2}\), then \(Dv\le\sqrt v\) while \(2/\log^3(3D)\gg 1/(\log(1/v))^3\), contradicting the corollary for small \(v\).
Degree one is excluded independently by its height bound.
In the remaining interval \(v^{-1/2}<D<1/v\), both \(\log D\asymp L_v\) and \(\log\log D\asymp\log L_v\) hold.
The full [Voutier bound](../literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) implies \(1/D\ll v L_v^3/(\log L_v)^3\); multiplication by \(\log(2D)\) gives (2).
The quoted theorem and corollary apply to nonintegral algebraic numbers as well as integers and use the degree of the actual power whose phase is being tested.

In the torus comparison, the circle law is supported on the parametrization with exponents \(a_j\), while the product law gives independent uniform angles in all \(s\) coordinates.
If no nonconstant coefficient reaches \(1/(2r)\), the constant dominates for \(x<1/4\).
Otherwise one independent coordinate alone traverses a circle of radius at least \(1/(2r)\).
If two points of this circle lie in a disk of radius \(x\), their chord has length at most \(2x\).
Fixing one point consequently puts all such angles within angular distance \(O_r(x)\) of it for small \(x\).
This gives the linear full-torus sublevel bound, uniformly in the other coordinates.
On every translate of the one-dimensional parametrization, the sparse frequencies remain distinct and the Euclidean coefficient norm stays one, so its sublevel probability is \(O_r(x^{1/l})\) by the [ordinary circle estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#transfer).

The product Fejér kernel is nonnegative and places at least a constant depending only on \(r\) inside the box of angular radii \(1/J\).
On that box the change in \(F\) is bounded by \(J^{-1}\sum_{j>0}|c_j|\le\sqrt r/J\).
The convolution therefore majorizes a positive multiple of the indicator of \(B_t\) pointwise, even when \(t=0\).
Fubini's theorem and the translated circle estimate bound its mean for the circle law.
For the full-torus \(L^2\) norm, convolution by this unit-mass kernel is a contraction, giving at most \(\lambda(B_y)^{1/2}\ll_r y^{1/2}\).
The product Fourier support lies in a box with at most \((2J+1)^s\) lattice points.
Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz give the claimed absolute Fourier coefficient sum and hence (3).
No full-dimensional assumption is imposed on the circle law by this use of the product norm.

When \(q=v'\cdot a=0\) the Fourier discrepancy vanishes exactly.
For each \(q\ne0\) the signed harmonic on the original phases is, up to complex conjugation, the first harmonic on the distinct conjugates of \(\alpha^{|q|}\), because the extension degree gives every such conjugate the same multiplicity.
This power stays nontorsion.
Its height is at most \(sJu\), and the estimates of (2) together with monotonicity of \(V\) and eventual decrease of \(V(x)/x\) give \(\Delta_J\ll_r\sqrt{JE}\) in the small-parameter range.
For the neutral Salem phases the [power-sum estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#salem) instead bounds each nonzero mode by \(O_r(\sqrt{JE_0})\), retaining the normalization by \(d-2\).
It applies even when the mode grows with the support.

Put \(a=1/l,\ z_0=E^{1/(l+2a)}\), and \(z=\max(t,z_0)\) in the range where both are small.
For \(J=\lceil1/z\rceil\), \(J\asymp1/z\), the convolution threshold is \(O_r(z)\), and \(Ju\ll u/z_0\to0\).
The error \(J^{s/2}O_r(\sqrt z)\sqrt{JE}\) is at most \(O_r(z^{-l/2}\sqrt E)\), which is \(O_r(z^a)\) by \(E\le z^{l+2a}\).
The same optimization works with \(E_0\) for the Salem neutral orbit without any height comparison between different powers.
Since \(z_0^a=E^{1/(l^2+2)}\), the phase bound has the stated exponent.
Parameters bounded away from the small-error regime and large thresholds are covered by the trivial fraction bound.
This also checks the two-term endpoint \(l=1\).

For the radial return, Markov's inequality with the original average absolute logarithmic radius bounds the exceptional fraction by \(2u/z_0\).
As \(u\le E\) in the small range and \(l+2a-1\ge a\), this is \(O_r(z_0^a)\).
Every other conjugate changes \(Q\) by at most \(\sqrt r(e^{z_0}-1)\) upon replacement by its phase, since the exponents after normalization all lie between zero and \(W\).
The phase estimate at the enlarged threshold gives the claimed bound for the original complex values, including exact zero; its extension to several polynomials is the union inequality.
A normalized monomial has constant modulus one.

Finally \(X^j-2\) is Eisenstein at two, the conjugates have equally spaced arguments, and their common radius tends to one.
At any fixed sufficiently small positive \(t\), the limiting circle sublevel has boundary consisting of finitely many angles.
Uniform convergence of the radial polynomial values and convergence of the grid averages therefore give its normalized arc length as the limit also for the actual conjugates.
The arc length is asymptotic to a positive constant times \(t^{1/l}\) as \(t\downarrow0\), ruling out any larger uniform limiting threshold exponent.
Here \(h=(\log2)/j\to0\) and the total logarithmic Mahler measure stays \(\log2\), as specified.
