## All-length exclusion

I checked the program's degree bound using the prime-power formula for the totient: in the product for \(\varphi(q)^2/q\), only \(2^1\) has a factor below one, and that factor is \(1/2\).
To check the enumeration by a different route, I generated orders by increasing prime factors, with totient contribution \((\ell-1)\ell^{e-1}\) from each prime power \(\ell^e\).
This gives 117 orders \(q\ge3\) with \(\varphi(q)\le60\), the largest being 210.
In the factorization of \(\Phi_q\) into integer powers of \(1-z^k\), the exponent for \(k\mid q\) is \(\mu(q/k)\).
Thus the required prefix \(1+3z^2+O(z^4)\) is equivalent to total exponents \((0,-3,0)\) for \(k=1,2,3\).
I enumerated subsets of even and odd orders separately and joined complementary exponent triples subject to the total degree bound.
For \(q\ge3\), \(\Phi_q(-1)\) is \(\ell\) when \(q=2\ell^e\) for a prime \(\ell\) and \(e\ge1\), and one otherwise.
This calculation gives 41,268 distinct parents, every one with \(A(-1)>1\).

For the length bound, a phase zero common to the parent and the sine changes neither neighboring sign.
At the zero the weak phase inequality still forces a strict residue sign, since the residue formula subtracts one at an odd index and adds one at an even index.
I sorted the rational angles from both factors with multiplicity, using a common integer scale divisible by the relevant denominators.
After dropping locations of even multiplicity, I scanned the intervals from right to left, tracking the longest later positive interval for each negative one.
If the smaller width of one such pair is \(w>0\) in angle units \(u\), then \(p>2/w\) forces an odd index in the first open interval and a later even index in the second.
This gives a negative residue before a positive one, which rules out every cut.
