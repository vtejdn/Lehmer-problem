## Whole proof

Removing a common monomial preserves length and both roots \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\), so the normalized support spans \([0,D]\).
Its \(s-1\) gaps sum to \(D\), where \(2\le s\le6\).
For the Salem unit, the squared norm in C855 v2 Part A is \(d(t^2+(-t)^2)\), giving \(d\ge1/(20t^2)\).
I reused the [accepted whole proof of C855 v2](Yin_R_G1086_v1.md#raw-p2-whole) for that theorem.
I checked the statement of Lemma 1 against [Dobrowolski's 2006 published edition, p. 203](../literature/Dob06/readme.md#edition).
The Salem minimal polynomial is monic, integral, noncyclotomic and irreducible, has nonzero constant term and divides the normalized \(s\)-term multiple.
Its multiplicative Mahler measure is \(e^t\).
Thus a largest gap satisfies \(gt\ge Dt/5\ge0.31d/(10\cdot6!)\) when \(e^t-1\le2t\).
The inspection of Dobrowolski's paper covered the statement and this application.

Writing \(P=X^NB+H\), evaluation yields \(|B(\lambda)|\le\|H\|_1e^{-(N-c)t}\), where \(c=\deg H\).
The reciprocal decomposition gives \(|H^*(\lambda)|\le\|B\|_1e^{-(D-c-a)t}\), where \(a=\deg B\), and \(D-c-a=N-c=g\).
Either outward-facing block of length at most two would be a nonzero constant or have the form \(\pm X^v\pm1\), whose magnitude is at least \(\min(1,e^t-1)\).
The uniform lower bound for \(gt\) makes \(6e^{-gt}=o(t)\), excluding both possibilities.
The two disjoint blocks therefore have length three each, improving the evaluation bound on each side to \(3e^{-gt}<1/2\).

Normalize each outward-facing block to have positive leading coefficient.
Length three permits a constant \(3\), a binomial with absolute coefficients \(1,2\), or a trinomial with unit coefficients.
In the binomial case only \(X^a-2\) can have magnitude below \(1/2\).
In the trinomial case \(X^a-X^b+1\), \(X^a+X^b-1\) and \(X^a+X^b+1\) all exceed one when evaluated at \(\lambda\).
For the surviving \(Q_{a,b}=X^a-X^b-1\), including \(b=0\), the difference \(e^{at}-e^{bt}\) lies between \(1/2\) and \(3/2\).
Thus \(e^{at}>3/2\), and \(e^{bt}<3/(2t)\) follows from \(e^{(a-b)t}-1\ge t\).
In turn \(e^{at}<3/(2t)+3/2\), which bounds the width of each block by \(O(\log(2/t)/t)\).
Twice the maximum width \(W\) is therefore less than \(d\) throughout an absolute neighborhood of zero.

Multiplying the two root equations at reciprocal points gives \(T_B(\lambda)=T_H(\lambda)\), including when either side vanishes.
The difference multiplied by \(X^W\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z[X]\) and has degree at most \(2W\), hence vanishes identically by minimality of \(d\).
Reversal preserves autocorrelation.
In \(3-C_a-C_{a-b}+C_b\) the extreme positive exponent is \(a\), with coefficient \(-2\) for \(b=0\) and \(-1\) otherwise.
When \(b>0\), the two intermediate contributions have opposite signs and coincide exactly for \(2b=a\).
In that case \(b=a/2\), and in every other interior case the positive coefficient determines \(b\).
Thus the normalized outward-facing blocks coincide and reversal recovers the asserted form with the relative sign \(\sigma\).

Since \(g=N-a>0\), the two blocks in that form are disjoint for either sign, even for \(b=0\).
Their total length is six, establishing the converse inclusion for the sequence equivalence, while the forward inclusion applies to the tail of every sequence tending to one.
The improved evaluation bound gives (2), and \(g\gg d/t\gg t^{-3}\) with \(D=g+2a\) gives \(1-g/D=O(t^2\log(2/t))\).
The whole reduction and its estimates follow from the two stated inputs.
Existence or exclusion of a sequence of Salem factors within the resulting family remains undecided by this argument.
