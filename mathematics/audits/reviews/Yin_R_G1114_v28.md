## The integral numerator, its measure and the multinacci example

A reviewer outside the development of L462 v29 checked its four fixed sections for Yin on 2026-11-16T12:27 UTC.
The assignment supplied the target and dependency scope without development dialogue, separate author explanations or earlier findings on this argument.
I used L462 v31 at the statement of its sparse phase estimate to inspect the final corollary.

The powers in \(V_i\) cannot cancel identically, since \(f_i/g_i\) has a pole at \(a_i=1/\theta_i\), while \(\sigma_i z^{b_i}\) is a polynomial.
The nonzero integral polynomial has \(m(V_i)\ge0\), and the boundary identity in v26 gives \(m(V_i)\le j_i\log\theta_i+\log2\).
This bounds the liminf and permits a subsequence attaining it.
I checked the statement of Salem's closedness theorem on p. 7 of the retained Smyth survey.
Since \(\theta>1\) is Pisot, the reversal \(Q\) of its minimal polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), has constant coefficient one, and its only root in the closed unit disk is the simple root \(a=1/\theta\).
These facts also hold for a nonunit limit.

After taking the locally uniform subsequential limit of the bounded analytic \(B_i\), division by \(b_{a_i}\) on a fixed disk about zero gives convergence there to \(G=B/b_a\).
The condition \(g_i(0)=1\) makes all coefficients of the series for \(f_i/g_i\) integers.
Cauchy's formula gives convergence coefficient by coefficient, hence eventual constancy at each index.
In particular \(G(0)=c_0=\pm1\), and \(G'(0)\) is a nonzero integer by the Schwarz lemma argument of v26 for the linear coefficient.
Writing \(Q=(z-a)Q_1\) gives \(Q/b_a=(1-az)Q_1\), so \(H=(Q/b_a)B\) is bounded analytic in the open disk.
Near zero it equals \(QG\), giving integral Taylor coefficients and constant coefficient \(c_0\).
For coefficients \(h_k\), Parseval gives \(\sum_k|h_k|^2r^{2k}\le\|H\|_\infty^2\) for \(0<r<1\).
Increasing \(r\) to one makes the squares summable, so only finitely many of the integral coefficients are nonzero.
The rational formula \(B=b_a H/Q\) extends over the boundary, giving \(|H|\le |Q|\) there because \(|b_a|=1\).
A common polynomial factor would imply \(Q\mid H\), making \(G\) a polynomial bounded by one on the disk.
Its value at zero would force it to be constant, contradicting its nonzero derivative.
The content of \(Q\) is one, so this also establishes coprimality over the integers.

The functions \(F_i\) are nonzero rational functions analytic across the circle, of modulus at most two there.
Their boundary zeros give integrable logarithms.
The displayed \(\nu_i\) are thus positive measures of total mass \(\log\theta_i+(\log2-m(V_i))/j_i\), tending to \(\log\theta-\ell\).
For each \(z\) with \(B(z)\ne0\), the ratio \(C_i(z)/B_i(z)\) tends to zero, and so does its \(j_i\)-th power.
Factoring out \(B_i(z)^{j_i}\) proves the pointwise logarithmic limit even as \(j_i\) varies.
Weak convergence tests the continuous Poisson kernel at each fixed \(z\), giving the claimed upper bound for \(\log|B|\), including its zeros.
The angular average of this kernel on \(|z|=r\) is one, so integrating subtracts exactly the mass \(\log\theta-\ell\).
In fact \(Q/b_a\) is zero-free on the closed disk and its modulus at zero is \(1/a=\theta\), hence its logarithmic angular mean is \(\log\theta\) at every such radius.
Jensen's polynomial formula gives the boundary limit \(m(H)\) even with roots on the circle.
Thus \(m(H)\le\ell\).
If \(\ell=0\), integrality gives \(M(H)=1\), so the leading coefficient has modulus one and Kronecker's theorem gives cyclotomic factors up to sign, including a possible constant.

For the multinacci family, the cited root argument gives irreducibility, exactly one exterior root and \(\theta_m\uparrow2\).
The constant and leading coefficients force the sign to be minus if \(g_m\) were proportional to \(f_m\), whereas the linear coefficients of the two polynomials are both minus one for every \(m\ge2\).
Summing the geometric series gives exactly the two displayed products with \(1-z\).
On compact subsets of the unit disk avoiding \(1/2\), the denominator \(1-2z+z^{m+1}\) stays bounded away from zero eventually.
The ratio therefore tends locally uniformly to \(-1/(1-2z)\), giving \(H=-1\).

For the phase corollary take the cyclotomic multiple \(D(z)=1-z\), which has measure one, nonzero constant and reversal \(-D\).
The three exponents \(0,m,m+1\) in \(Df_m\) are distinct.
The sparse phase theorem therefore applies with \(s=3,\ \Theta=2\), uniformly in \(m,b,\sigma\).
Its error for \(m(V_j)/j\) is at most \(C(3,2)j^{-1/11}\), while \(\log\theta_m\ge\log\theta_2>0\).
One can choose \(j_*\) so that the error is at most \((\log\theta_2)/2\) and \(j_*(\log\theta_2)/2\) exceeds the prescribed cost.
The sparse estimate used here precedes the later application of v31 to original cyclic norms and has no dependency on the multinacci example.
I accept the numerator criterion, measure inequality, example and application at this stated scope.
