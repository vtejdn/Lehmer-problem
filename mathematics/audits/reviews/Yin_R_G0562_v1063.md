## The relative discriminant norm and the conditional index bound

At 2026-11-24T19:24 an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the complete [relative discriminant claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0902_v3.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed claim and assignment without development dialogue or a prior verdict on this version.
The [review of the original trace construction](Yin_R_G0562_v1040.md#finding) and the [review of the radical minimum](Yin_R_G0562_v1062.md#finding) apply to the indicated earlier inputs.
The divisibility, the exact dyadic contribution and both root-discriminant estimates hold for every stated parameter.
The final index estimate holds in the parameter range of the radical minimum theorem.

The Eisenstein trace polynomial gives a unique dyadic completion of degree and ramification index \(n\), with uniformizer \(w\).
The polynomial \(X^2-wX-w\) is Eisenstein there and defines \(x=T-1\).
In the extension's integer-valued valuation, \(a\) and \(bx\) for \(a,b\in B_{\mathfrak p}^\times\) have valuations \(2v_{\mathfrak p}(a)\) and \(2v_{\mathfrak p}(b)+1\).
Thus no cancellation enlarges the order with basis \(1,x\), and its discriminant \(w(w+4)\) has valuation two in \(B_{\mathfrak p}\), since \(v_{\mathfrak p}(4)=2n\).
Residue degree one gives norm factor \(2^2\).

At an odd place, removing a uniformizer square from the radicand gives either an étale unit quadratic or a tame ramified quadratic with discriminant exponent one.
The endpoints \(w,w+4\) are locally coprime there.
For the odd \(d_0=n-3\), the special values \(U_j(1)=j+1\) and \(U_j(-1)=(-1)^j(j+1)\) give \(C(0)=2n-5\) and \(C(-4)=-1\).
Since \(n\) is even, \(H(0)=-2(2n-5)\) and \(H(-4)=2L^2\) are the signed norms.
Odd places contributed by \(w\) therefore give a norm dividing \(2n-5\).

For \(\ell\mid L\), the valuations of all conjugates of \(w+4\), counted with multiplicities and normalized by \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\), sum to \(2a\).
At a local degree \(e_{\mathfrak l}f_{\mathfrak l}\) their total is \(f_{\mathfrak l}v_{\mathfrak l}(w+4)\).
This bounds the sum of residue degrees on the places with odd valuation by \(2a\).
The derivative calculation gives \(H'(-4)=(1-2^{2n-1})/2-L^2(1/2+2C'(-4))\), whose valuation is \(t\) if \(t<a\).
The linear coefficient is a signed sum of all products omitting one root.
The minimum product valuation is \(2a\) minus the largest root valuation, so the largest valuation is at least \(2a-t>a\).
All valuations are nonnegative, making this largest value unique.
All the images of a local irreducible factor have equal valuation, so the corresponding place has degree one.
The remaining root valuations sum to at most \(t\) and hence contribute at most \(t\) to the residue-degree sum for odd valuations.
When \(t=0\) the exceptional place has even valuation \(2a\), and when \(1\le t<a\) its possible extra contribution gives at most \(t+1\le2t\).
Together with the trivial \(2a\) bound for \(t\ge a\), this gives exactly the claimed prime-by-prime divisibility.

For \(L\) a power of three, \(2n-1\) is odd, so the gcd factor is one.
In general it is smaller than \(2^{2n-1}\), and the remaining polynomial factor \(4(2n-5)\) has \(2n\)-th root tending to one.
The tower formula gives the relative contribution to the root discriminant in the statement.

For the separate assertion on \(I\), each nontorsion relative unit generates \(K\) or an index-three subfield by the reviewed exterior-place argument.
Every such field has degree greater than one.
Apply Dimitrov's bound to the reciprocal of the monic minimal polynomial of the generator, which is a noncyclotomic integer polynomial with constant coefficient one.
An inverse root of modulus at most \(2^{-1/(4D_F)}\) gives the first lower bound on \(m(u)\).
This use of the house theorem was checked at statement scope. [Dimitrov's primary statement](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/22975)
The order index gives \(|\Delta_F|\le|\operatorname{disc}P|\), and applying Hadamard to the \(D_F\) Vandermonde rows gives the second term in \(c_F\).
Taking the minimum over the possible fields and combining \(\mu\ge c\) with \(\mu\le2\sqrt{D_H/I}\) yields \(I\le4D_H/c^2\).
The proved upper bound on the relative discriminant alone supplies no positive uniform lower bound for the absolute-field quantity \(c\).
