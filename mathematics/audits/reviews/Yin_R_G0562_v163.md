## Whole-proof finding

The argument proves that every nonzero integral Laurent polynomial of positive logarithmic Mahler measure with \(k\) monomials in any finite number of variables satisfies \(m(P)\ge c/(k^4\log(2k))\) for a single absolute \(c>0\).

### Height and torsion fibers

In Amoroso and Delsinne's unconditional Theorem 1.3 use the fixed base \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), whose field factor is \(5^{-c'}>0\).
If \(K/\mathbb Q\) is finite abelian, restriction embeds the Galois group of \(K K_0/K_0\) in the abelian group of \(K/\mathbb Q\).
The tower degree formula puts the relative degree over \(K K_0\) between \(\delta/2\) and \(\delta=[K(\beta):K]\).
Thus \(\delta h(\beta)\gg b(\delta)\), since the ratio of the log-log factors at \(\max(1,\delta/2)\) and \(\delta\) is bounded away from zero.
I checked the statement and its discrepancy with display (5.26) in the ESI edition.
The unramified-case inequality quoted from (4.16) on manuscript page 22 has \(D h(\alpha)\) as its height term, which gives precisely the factor \(1/D\) in the theorem.

A geometric \(Y\)-dependent component of a two-variable polynomial is a curve.
On such a curve other than a torsion coset, Bilu's Theorem 5.1(a) makes its torsion points finite, since each maximal torsion subvariety has dimension zero.
One can exclude their base coordinates and the zeros of the end coefficients of both the curve's equation and the entire fiber by excluding a finite set.
All primitive grids of sufficiently large prime order avoid that set.
At a surviving base point \(\zeta\), a root of the component is nonzero and nontorsion, and its \(\delta\) conjugates over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\) are distinct roots of the same degree-\(R\) polynomial.
There is no requirement that the component itself be defined over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\).

Averaging the Jensen formula over a common Galois splitting field weights each conjugate base point equally.
At each finite place the Gauss norm of the specialized polynomial is at most one because its coefficients are algebraic integers, including evaluations of negative powers of \(\zeta\).
Multiplicativity gives \(-\log|A_R|_v\ge\sum_i\log^+|\beta_i|_v\), so the product formula for the leading coefficient pays for every finite-place height contribution, also with repeated roots.
The grid mean therefore dominates \(\delta h(\beta)\).
For each cutoff the clipped logarithm is continuous, so its product-grid integral with full circle measure in \(Y\) tends to its two-torus integral.
Sending the cutoff down to \(-\infty\) gives the required upper bound on the limsup of the original grid means.
Finally \(b(t)>0\), and the logarithmic derivative with respect to \(\log t\) of \(b(t)\) has the sign of \(3/[\log(5t)\log\log(5t)]-4/\log(2t)\), which is eventually negative.
It follows that \(\min_{1\le t\le p}b(t)\gg b(p)\), uniformly for \(p\ge1\).
These arguments use the grid limit for each fixed curve, allowing its prime threshold to depend on the curve.
The order of this grid prime has no bearing on the compression prime.

### Compression and the reverse specialization estimate

After subtracting the minimum exponent and dividing by the gcd, the exponent differences have gcd one and the circle power map preserves the measure.
For \(B^{k-1}<p\le2B^{k-1}\), two distinct parameters lie in a common one of the \(B^{k-1}\) residue boxes.
Their difference is invertible, and the representatives \(y_i\) of its multiples of the exponents satisfy \(|y_i|<p/B\).
Some \(y_i\ne0\) since the differences have gcd one, while \(y_1=0\).
With \(N\) the inverse parameter and \(x_i=(e_i-Ny_i)/p\), the map \(p x_i+Ny_i=e_i\) keeps all \(k\) terms distinct.
The common row shift changes the specialization only by a Laurent monomial and leaves \(1\le R<2p/B<p\).

I rederived the lower comparison in C345v161 at \(H=p\).
Jensen's reflected-root identity is valid off finitely many exceptional fibers and has integrable fiber measure even with common base content.
On each \(H\)-point orbit the product of the regularized disk factors for one root is \(1-r^H\bar v^H X^N\) at phase zero.
Its average logarithm costs at most \(H^{-1}\log(1/(1-r^H))\) in the lower direction.
For \(D=|1-\bar vY|<1/(4R)\) the true root is within \(2D\) of \(Y\), including exterior roots since \(|v|\ge1-D\).
With \(S\) the coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm, the fiber derivative on the intervening segment is at most \(RS(1+2D)^{R-1}\ll RS\).
Parseval supplies \(\|P\|_\infty\ge S/\sqrt k\) because the frequencies remain distinct.
Thus the measurable-set bound gives \(\Pr(D<s)\le C(C_0R\sqrt{k}\,s)^{1/(k-1)}\), including larger \(s\) by the bound one.
For \(k\ge3\), integrating against \(\epsilon/[s(s+\epsilon)]\) with \(\epsilon=(C_0R\sqrt{k})^{-1}H^{-(k-1)}\) costs \(O(k/H)\) per root, using \(0<1/(k-1)\le1/2\).
Taking \(r=1-\epsilon\) gives \(\log(1/(1-r^H))\le\log(1/\epsilon)\ll k\log(2H)\).
For \(k=2\) the ratio character of the two monomials is nonconstant on both tori, giving exact equality of measures.
This verifies the error \(O(Rk\log(2p)/p)\), including the dependence on a growing \(R\), without using a bound on the base exponents.

### Absolute constants, torsion factors and number of variables

For \(B=\lceil(D_0 k)^8\rceil\) one has \(\log(2p)\asymp k\log(D_0k)\) with uniform comparison constants for \(k,D_0\ge2\).
The error divided by the preliminary lower bound \([k\log(D_0k)]^{-4}\) is at most \(C D_0^{-8}k^{-2}(\log(D_0k))^5\).
Its supremum in \(k\ge2\) is \(O(D_0^{-8}(1+\log^5D_0))\), so \(D_0\) can be fixed before any polynomial is chosen.
The preliminary lower bound is at most a fixed multiple of \(b(p)\), so taking \(D_0\) sufficiently large leaves at least half the full lower bound proportional to \(b(p)\).
Since \(\log\log(5p)\gg\log(2k)\) and \(\log(D_0k)\ll_{D_0}\log(2k)\), its order is at least \(1/[k^4\log(2k)]\).

If all horizontal components are torsion cosets, a primitive defining character \((a,j)\) can be oriented with \(j>0\).
Rational conjugacy gives factors \(\Phi_e(X^aY^j)\), with multiplicity, and Gauss's lemma leaves an integral base factor \(G\) carrying the content.
Each factor is monic in its character, so its top row is a single unit monomial and the top coefficient of \(F\) equals \(G\) up to such a unit.
Width additivity gives \(j\le j\varphi(e)\le R<p\), excluding \(pa+Nj=0\) by reduction modulo \(p\).
Every horizontal factor therefore specializes to zero logarithmic measure, even when the specialized exponent is negative.
The remaining base factor specializes to \(G(z^p)\) of exactly the original measure of \(G\).
Another occupied row makes the support of the top row strictly smaller than \(k\).
This gives strong induction with the same constant, since the target bound decreases in \(k\), and a monomial of positive measure has \(m\ge\log 2\).

For any fixed finite-dimensional support the differences of distinct exponents specialize to nonzero polynomials in \(N\), so they collide for only finitely many integers \(N\).
Each nonconstant torus character likewise has zero mean on the specialized circles for all sufficiently large \(N\), proving convergence for continuous clipped logarithms by trigonometric approximation.
The supremum of each resulting integral polynomial is at least one by Parseval.
The measurable-set inequality bounds its negative logarithmic tail below \(-T\) by \(C(k-1)e^{-T/(k-1)}\) in integral, uniformly in \(N\).
Clipping and then removing the cutoff proves the claimed convergence of measures.
These measures are nonnegative by univariate Jensen, and when the limit is positive the specialized measures eventually satisfy the positive lower bound.

The historical comparison uses Dobrowolski's Theorem 1 with the support of the entire input and Proposition 1 with the support of the monic irreducible polynomial itself.
Both statements were checked on printed pages 202–203.
I found no unresolved implication in the fixed proof.
The source theorems were checked for their statements and the indicated applications, with the additional final-step check for Amoroso and Delsinne.
This review provides neither a full reproof of the external theorems nor a priority finding or a completion review for the unrestricted Lehmer problem.

## Second independent whole-proof finding

I completed an independent inspection of the [submitted v163 proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#decision-polynomial-support-lower-bound-submitted) at 2026-11-17T04:52 UTC for human record author Yin.
I had no role in developing this proof and received no development discussion, author explanation or prior proof-review conclusion before returning my assessment.
I first saw the other finding in this file when recording my completed review.
The argument gives \(m(P)\ge c/(k^4\log(2k))\) with a single absolute \(c>0\) for all integral Laurent polynomials \(P\) with positive logarithmic Mahler measure and \(k\) terms, in every finite number of variables.

Two distinct prime choices account for the uniformity of the height step.
The compression prime \(p\) fixes the lift \(F\), after which a separate sequence of primitive base grids tends to Haar measure.
For a fixed horizontal curve other than a torsion coset, Bilu's finiteness theorem excludes only finitely many torsion projections, so the threshold for the grid prime can depend on \(F\).
The relative minimal polynomial of a surviving nontorsion root divides the entire fiber over its cyclotomic base field.
Each of its \(\delta\le R\) distinct roots contributes the same absolute height, and the finite-place Gauss norm and the product formula pay for the nonarchimedean terms even when the fiber is nonmonic.
Clipping the logarithm from below gives the correct upper bound on the grid limsup.
The fixed base \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) in Amoroso and Delsinne's unconditional Theorem 1.3 has discriminant \(5\), and adjoining it changes the relative degree by a factor of at most two.
These facts give the absolute lower bound proportional to \(b(R)\).

I rederived the cyclic comparison from the product over the \(H\) preimages and checked the reflected-root estimate through its dependence on \(R\).
When the reflected distance \(s\) is below \(1/(4R)\), the true root is within \(2s\), and on that segment the derivative is bounded by \(O(R\sum|a_i|)\).
Parseval uses the distinct specialized frequencies, so the measurable-set inequality bounds the logarithmic regularization excess by \(O(k/H)\) per root at the submitted scale.
The regularized orbit product then gives total reverse error \(O(Rk\log(2H)/H)\), irrespective of the horizontal exponents or root multiplicities.
For two terms the quotient character is nonconstant before and after substitution, giving equality directly.

The pigeonhole difference is nonzero modulo \(p\), and the primitive exponent differences guarantee that its representatives occupy at least two rows.
With \(B=\lceil(D_0k)^8\rceil\), the error divided by the preliminary height bound is \(O(D_0^{-8}k^{-2}\log^5(D_0k))\).
Its supremum over \(k\ge2\) is \(O(D_0^{-8}(1+\log^5D_0))\), which allows a single choice of \(D_0\).
The factor \((\log\log(5p))^3\) then supplies three powers of \(\log(2k)\), leaving the denominator \(k^4\log(2k)\).
In the other branch, the oriented primitive character of every horizontal torsion factor has vertical exponent \(0<j\le R<p\).
Reduction modulo \(p\) excludes \(pa+Nj=0\), so every such factor has measure zero on specialization, with any multiplicity.
The top row retains the full base factor up to a signed monomial and has fewer terms, which gives exact descent without accumulating error.

For each finite multivariate support, frequency collisions disappear eventually along \((1,N,\ldots,N^{h-1})\).
The one-variable negative logarithmic tails are bounded in integral by \(C(k-1)e^{-T/(k-1)}\) below \(-T\), uniformly in \(N\).
Together with character convergence for continuous clipped logarithms this proves convergence of measures and transfers the one-variable bound to every positive limit.
I found no unresolved implication or additional uniformity hypothesis in the submitted proof.
I checked the statements and applications of the external theorems, including Dobrowolski's two distinct support counts.
Full proofs of the published theorems and historical priority were outside this inspection.
