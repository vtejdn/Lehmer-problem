# Independent review of height transfer to subfields of the divisible conjugate group

<a id="finding"></a>
## Orbit products, the sharp golden-ratio minimum and the least available ranks

At 2026-11-24T23:01 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v73.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v73.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, and checked the final nontorsion qualification in the argument-rank assertion.
The rank identity, both height bounds (1), all three attained minima, and divergence of the two minima over the entire divisible group are valid.

For an algebraic subfield \(F\), the restriction of \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/F)\) to the original splitting field acts on the actual finite orbit \(\Omega\).
Its orbits give algebraic-integer units \(z_O\) whose products are fixed by that absolute subgroup, hence belong to \(F\) even when \(F\) is infinite or nonnormal.
Uniform averaging over the finite image \(H\) sends \([a]\) to \([z_O]/|O|\), since images are counted on actual conjugates before passing modulo torsion.
This average is a projection onto \(V^H\), so the orbit products span it over \(\mathbb Q\).
Conversely a positive power of each \(x\in D(w)\cap F\) is a product of conjugates up to torsion, giving \([x]\in V\), and the subgroup fixing \(F\) fixes \([x]\).
These inclusions give equality of rational ranks without needing to realize an arbitrary fixed rational vector by a prescribed representative in \(F\).

The product of all \(z_O\) is the rational norm of \(w\), equal to a sign, so the sum of their classes vanishes.
Deleting the zero classes retains this relation with coefficient one on each remaining orbit.
If \(r>0\), the nonzero list spans dimension \(r\) and is dependent, hence contains at least \(r+1\) entries counting orbits, including any repetitions.
The sum of their absolute heights is at most \(\sum_O |O|h(w)=d h(w)\).
Each nonzero entry is an actual nontorsion unit in \(D(w)\cap F\), so replacing each height by either of the stated infima proves (1) with the indicated factor.

For \(T=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{tr}}\), a nontorsion unit has real fraction one, and Garza's bound is \(\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)=\tfrac12\log\phi\).
The field \(T(i)\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\) with central complex conjugation, and contains every finite abelian extension by the real and imaginary parts argument in v55.
For a unit \(z\) in it, all conjugates of \(z/c(z)\) have modulus one, so this quotient is torsion.
The element \(zc(z)\) is totally positive since each image is the squared modulus of a conjugate of \(z\).
Both square roots are totally real algebraic units, and the positive choice has the same absolute height as \(z\) because its square has height \(2h(z)\) by the torsion quotient.
It is nontorsion whenever \(z\) is.
This gives the same \(\tfrac12\log\phi\) height floor on \(T(i)\) and on its abelian subfield.
In each of the three cases a nontorsion element in the intersection gives \(r\ge1\) and \(m(w)\ge\log\phi\).
The real quadratic unit \(\phi\), of conjugate \(-1/\phi\), belongs to all three fields, lies in its own group and has total measure \(\log\phi\).
This verifies sharpness for the original input measure in each assertion.

For any nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) with relative degree \(e\) over \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\), use \(F=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}(x)\).
Every element of \(F\) has relative degree at most \(e\).
The rank-one specialization of Delsinne applies to each nontorsion orbit product, and its logarithmic exponent is \(3\cdot(2(1+1)^2(1+1)!)=48\).
The allowed denominator increases with the relative degree, so it can be bounded above by \(c_2(1)e(\log(3e))^{48}\).
Summing the strict height bounds on at least two actual nontorsion products gives the claimed strict lower bound for \(m(w)\), even if the height infimum is unattained.
In particular the relative degree of every choice of nontorsion group member tends to infinity along a shrinking sequence.

For nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), its conjugate classes form a nonzero rational submodule \(V_x\subseteq V\), with the Galois action on classes factoring through the original splitting field.
The negative eigenspace of the same ordinary complex conjugation has dimension \(k(x)\), since \((1-c)[a]\) is the class of the square of the phase of \(a\).
On a rational irreducible summand \(W\subseteq V_x\) one has \(j=\dim W^{-c}\le k(x)\).
When \(j=0\) or \(t=\dim W\ge8j^2\), v68 supplies a vector in this very \(W\) fixed by at least half the conjugates of \(c\).
The selected product of the original conjugates of \(w\) then gives the lower bound on \(m(w)\), with no use of a bound on \(m(x)\).

Otherwise \(j\ge1\) and \(t<8K^2\) under \(k(x)\le K\).
The intersection of rational \(W\) with the original conjugate-class lattice has full rank \(t\) in \(W\) and is invariant under \(G\).
Thus the field fixed by the kernel of the action on \(W\) has degree at most \(3^{t^2}\) by reduction modulo three.
Its associated fixed space in \(V\) contains \(W\), so (1) provides at least twice the least absolute height of nontorsion units of degree at most \(3^{64K^4}\).
In this remaining case \(K\ge1\), and the list of nontorsion units of such bounded degree and height at most one is finite and nonempty because it contains \(\phi\).
It admits effective enumeration by integral coefficient bounds, and heights can be ordered by comparing positive algebraic multiplicative measures after taking powers to a common degree.
An integer rank cutoff suffices since \(k(x)\) is integral.
This gives one positive floor for the original \(m(w)\) whenever any nontorsion \(x\) has \(k(x)\le K\), which proves the assertion about the least argument rank over the full group.
