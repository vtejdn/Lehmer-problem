## Whole-proof finding

At 2026-11-21T09:30 UTC an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin recommends acceptance of the [09:28 UTC submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#statement) in full.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its review scope and took no part in its development.

The two orientations above the exceptional block give the two real embeddings of \(K\), exchanged by precomposition with \(\tau\).
Above every other block, complex conjugation exchanges the two orientations, so for either embedding \(\sigma\) above that block one has \(\overline{\sigma}=\sigma\circ\tau\).
Changing all roots by the rational sign of \(r\) preserves the inverse pairing, gives exceptional root \(R^{-1}\) in the majority orientation, and preserves the absolute product one.
Consequently the other \(b-1\) roots have strictly positive logarithmic moduli summing to \(t=\log R=m/2\).
Both monic real polynomials have positive value at every \(q>R\), since each real factor is positive and each nonreal pair gives a positive squared modulus.

In the logarithm of the evaluation quotient the \(k\)-th coefficient is the real part of the inverse power sum minus the forward power sum, divided by \(k\).
The exceptional root contributes \(2\sinh(kt)/k\), and each remaining root contributes \(-2\cos(k\theta_i)\sinh(kt_i)/k\).
Strict superadditivity of \(\sinh\) on positive arguments applies at every \(k\), including when all cosine factors equal one, because \(b-1\ge2\).
The same comparison with absolute cosine bounded by one gives the coefficient upper bound \(4\sinh(kt)/k\le4tR^k\).
Summing the geometric majorant at \(Q\ge10R\) gives \(L(Q)\le4t/9\).
For each dyadic tail the linear term ties its initial term while every higher term is strictly smaller by the factor \(1/(2^k-1)\).
Absolute convergence and positivity thus justify both strict tail domination and the total bound \(\sum_j L(q_j)<2L(Q)<m\).

In a quotient of two distinct subset products, the coefficient of the first unequal logarithm is \(\pm1\), and the absolute contribution of all later indices is smaller.
Equality of the principal fractional ideals would therefore give a unit \(w\) with \(0<|\log|w||<m\) at the chosen real place.
The relation \(\tau w=w^{-1}\) gives the opposite logarithm at the other real embedding and zero logarithm at each complex embedding.
Summing positive logarithms counts each conjugate of the unit \([K:\mathbb Q(w)]\) times, so its own measure lies in \((0,m)\) and its degree is at most \(2u<2ub\), contradicting minimality.
At each finite prime fixed by \(\tau\), the valuation of an evaluation quotient equals its own negative.
This excludes all primes over inert or ramified primes from every subset product.
The distinctness concerns ideals themselves and supplies neither a bound on the number of split primes used nor an unrestricted lower bound for positive Mahler measure.
