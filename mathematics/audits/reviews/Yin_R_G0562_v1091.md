## Embedding count, conjugate bound and Salem transfer

The integrality, positivity, bound (1), rational case and implication for Salem sequences hold under the stated hypotheses.

Since \(i\notin F\), an embedding \(s:F\to\mathbb C\) has two extensions to \(F(i)\), distinguished by the sign of \(i\).
The values of \(\beta=(t+iy)/2\) under them are mutually inverse, since \(s(t)^2+s(y)^2=4\).
As \(\beta\) generates \(L\), the resulting list over all \(s\) is exactly the conjugate list for its minimal polynomial.
The relation \(u_{\bar s}=1/\overline{u_s}\) makes the summand in \(D\) independent of the representative of each pair and gives unit modulus at real \(s\).
Each \(s\) contributes \(|a_s|\) to total logarithmic measure, and each nonreal pair contributes \(2|a_s|\).
For \(u_s=e^{a_s+i\theta}\), the imaginary parts of \(u_s+u_s^{-1}\) and \((u_s-u_s^{-1})/i\) are \(2\sinh(a_s)\sin\theta\) and \(-2\sinh(a_s)\cos\theta\).
This proves the alternative formula for \(m\) with the indicated factor of one half inside the inverse hyperbolic sine.
Kronecker's theorem gives \(m>0\) for a nontorsion unit, so \(r\ge1\) and \(D>0\).
The hyperbolic addition formula on nonnegative arguments gives \(\sum_{s\in J}\sinh^2|a_s|\le\sinh^2(\sum_{s\in J}|a_s|)\), proving \(D\le4\sinh^2(m/2)\).

The identity \(|u_s|^2=u_s/u_{\bar s}\) expresses every summand of \(D\) as an algebraic unit plus its inverse minus two, proving algebraic integrality.
Let \(g\) extend an embedding of \(\mathbb Q(D)\) to the normal closure of \(L\).
Writing \(g(i)=\epsilon i\) with \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) gives \(g(u_s)=u_{g\circ s}^{\epsilon}\) for every \(s\), using the same sign throughout the sum.
Thus the image of each summand is \(v+v^{-1}-2\) for \(v=u_{g\circ s}/u_{g\circ\bar s}\).
This calculation remains valid even when \(g\circ\bar s\) differs from \(\overline{g\circ s}\).
Composition by \(g\) permutes the embeddings of \(F\), so the two indices used by each ratio are disjoint from those used by every other ratio.
The triangle inequality then gives \(\sum_{s\in J}|\log|v||\le\sum_s|a_s|=m\).
For \(m\le1\), the modulus of each term is at most \(|v|+|v|^{-1}+2\le2+2\cosh(1)<6\).
Every conjugate of \(D\) is therefore bounded by \(6r\) in modulus.
Its norm from \(\mathbb Q(D)\) is a nonzero rational integer, so \(1\le|N_{\mathbb Q(D)/\mathbb Q}(D)|\le D(6r)^{k-1}\).
Together with the upper bound on \(D\), taking the positive square root and applying \(\operatorname{arsinh}\) gives (1).
For rational \(D\), positivity and integrality already give \(D\ge1\), hence \(m\ge2\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)=2\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\) for every \(m\).

In the cited construction a Salem input \(\tau\) yields \(r=[\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12}):\mathbb Q(\tau)\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})]/2\in\{1,2\}\) and \(m(\beta)\le4\log\tau\).
If \(\tau\to1\), then \(m(\beta)\to0\) and the hypothesis \(m\le1\) in (1) holds eventually.
Any subsequence with \(k\le K_0\) would have \(m\ge2\operatorname{arsinh}(12^{-(K_0-1)/2}/2)>0\), proving \(k\to\infty\) independently of the original Salem degrees.
