### The initial bounds and the phase marginal

The small positive measure reduction supplies a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit \(S\), so its exterior radial logarithms sum to \(m\), their maximum \(t\) is at most \(m\), and \(dtm\ge1/20\).
Consequently \(d\ge 1/(20m^2)\).
Since the leading term is separated by \(k\), comparison with all the lower terms at a largest root gives \(kt=O_{a,b,c}(1)\).
Thus \(kh=(kt)m/(dt)=O(m^2)\).
The nonzero relation of width \(2n\) has five terms, so the sparse width bound gives \(nh\gg1\).
Dividing gives \(k/n=O(m^2)\), hence \(l\sim n,\ l/k\to\infty,\ d/l=O(m)\).
When \(|a|\ge|b|\), \(aX^k+b\) has every root in the closed disk, including the equality case.
The actual lower block has degree \(n\), and the upper block starts at \(n+l\).
Their separating gap \(l\) satisfies \(l-2k=n-3k\ge (2n)/4\) eventually, verifying the v170 hypothesis with the full degree and a fixed positive parameter.

When \(R=|b/a|>1\), any fixed positive Fourier index \(v\) gives \(r=vk\le T=\max(d,vk)\) and \(Th=\max(m,vkh)\to0\).
The degree-\(d\) relation is \(S\) itself, so the v186 bound applies even if raising to the \(vk\)-th power lowers degree.
It gives vanishing nonconstant Fourier coefficients of the phase measures, with their original multiplicities.
Trigonometric approximation then gives the uniform circle marginal.
The proportion outside \(k|\log|x||\le\delta\) is at most \(2kh/\delta\to0\) by reciprocity.

### Radial limits and exactly circular roots

Putting \(T=x^k,\ Y=x^l\) in the equation gives \((aT+b)Y^2+cY+(a/T+b)=0\).
Throughout the band \(e^{-\delta}\le|T|\le e^\delta\), we have \(|a|e^\delta=\sqrt{|a||b|}<|b|\).
This bounds both the leading and constant coefficients away from zero and bounds all three above, so the two roots lie in a fixed compact annulus.
Thus \(l|\log|x||\) is bounded there and \(|T-\xi_x|=O(k/l)\).
At \(T=\xi\) on the unit circle the substitution \(Z=Y(a\xi+b)/|a\xi+b|\) gives \(Z+Z^{-1}=-c/|a\xi+b|\).
The absolute radial logarithms of both solutions equal \(F(\xi)\), including at a double root.
Compactness of the annulus and continuity of \(F\) give uniform convergence to the graph in the band.
The contribution outside the band to a bounded continuous test function vanishes, and on that compact graph the phase equidistribution gives the asserted joint limit.

On a closed arc with \(2|a\xi+b|>|c|\) both solutions at the unit circle are distinct and have modulus one.
In finitely many smaller neighborhoods of the arc their continuations are nonvanishing, with uniform bounds on \(|TY'(T)/Y(T)|\).
For sufficiently large indices the radial segment from \(\xi_x\) to \(x^k\) lies in such a neighborhood and the continued branch ending at \(Y_x\) starts on the unit circle.
Integration gives \(l|\log|x||\le Ck|\log|x||\), forcing \(|x|=1\) since \(l/k\to\infty\).
On a closed arc with the opposite strict inequality, \(F\) has a positive minimum, excluding circular roots in the band by the uniform convergence.
The transition solves \(a^2+b^2+2ab\cos\theta=c^2/4\) and consists of at most two points.
Arcs of arbitrarily small measure around these points and the vanishing mass outside the band exhaust the uncertainty in the count.
Inversion pairs all off-circle conjugates, proving the limit for \(2e/d\).

### Outlying roots, integrality and the Salem consequence

For a large exterior root put \(u=\log|x|>\delta/k\).
Then \(|(a x^{-k}+b)x^{-l}|\ll e^{-\delta l/k}\).
In the identity \((a x^k+b)x^l=-c-(a x^{-k}+b)x^{-l}\) the right side is uniformly bounded above and away from zero.
Taking moduli and logarithms gives \(\log|a x^k+b|=-lu+\log|c|+O(e^{-\delta l/k})\).
Dividing the identity by \(x^l\) also yields \(x^k=-b/a+O(e^{-\delta l/k})\), hence \(ku=\log R+O(e^{-\delta l/k})\).
The \(j\) interior partners have equal absolute radial logarithms.
Summing the band limit with denominator \(d\) gives \(\int F\,d\mu+o(1)\) there, since the omitted proportion tends to zero and \(F\) is bounded.
Adding the large pairs contributes \(2(lj/(kd))\log R+o(1)\) to \(2lm/d\), since even \((l/k)e^{-\delta l/k}\to0\).

Monicity makes the absolute resultant the product of \(|a x^k+b|\) over all original roots.
It is a nonzero integer because \(-b/a\) has modulus greater than one and cannot be a rational algebraic unit.
The continuous band approximation \(\log|a\xi_x+b|\) has circle mean \(\log|b|\) since \(|a/b|<1\).
The large interior partners have \(|b|-|a|\le |a x^k+b|\le|a|+|b|\), so their total normalized logarithm is \(o(1)\).
For the exterior outliers the preceding root estimates give \(-(lj/(kd))\log R+o(1)\) after summation and normalization, including the term \(j\log|c|/d=o(1)\).
This verifies the second identity and its upper bound on \(lj/(kd)\).

In the original-degree quadratic sum the band contributes \(O(d^2/l^2)=O(m^2)\), and all \(2j\) outliers contribute \(O(dj/k^2)\).
The positive floor \(1/10\) forces \(dj\gg k^2\), and then \(j\ge1\) and \(m>\delta/k\) give \(k\to\infty\).
When \(|c|>2(|b|-|a|)\), the open arc set in (1) has positive measure, ruling out both bounded \(e\) and \(e=o(d)\) along a shrinking sequence.
Finally a Salem factor of degree at least four has a positive exterior root and a root on the unit circle.
For \(a,b\) of the same sign, normalize them positive and use \(x^s+x^{-s}>2\) at the positive exterior root to get \(c<-2(a+b)\).
The circle equation has no solution in that range by the triangle inequality.
This exclusion is independent of the size of \(m\).
The fixed-coefficient conclusions leave the indicated opposite-sign Salem regime open.
