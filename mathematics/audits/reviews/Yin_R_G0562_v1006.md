## The cofinite degree fraction and the measure bound hold

At 2026-11-21T14:50 UTC a separate agent acting for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the fixed statement and three argument sections of C0345v859.
The reviewer did not participate in developing this proof.

Over the algebraic closure the Laurent factors of a rational irreducible polynomial form a single orbit without repetitions in characteristic zero.
One can translate the supports to have minimum zero in each coordinate and normalize a fixed extremal coefficient to one, so conjugation preserves the support.
The Minkowski sum formula now gives precisely one \(c\)-th of the full width on each component, and rules out a one-dimensional component polygon when the full area is positive.
A nonconstant relation between the two characters modulo constants would place the curve in one component of a character fiber, a translate of a one-dimensional subtorus.
This would give a segment Newton polygon, proving independence.
The principal divisor map on the smooth projective model has constant kernel, and the intersection of the rational character-divisor plane with the integral divisors supported at the finite set of ends is a rank-two lattice.
Thus the saturation has finite index over the character lattice, invariant under conjugacy even when no individual component is defined over the rationals.

The generic fiber of the scalar power map over a component consists of \(d^2\) radical choices, permuted by a subgroup of translations of \(\mu_d^2\).
Its orbits correspond to components because the power map is finite étale; there are no extra components supported over special points.
The character group annihilating the Galois subgroup has order the number of these orbits.
For an annihilating character the radical monomial lies in the original function field, its \(d\)-th power lies in the character lattice, and changing the representatives of the exponents changes its class by a lattice element.
If it represents the trivial saturation quotient class, independence after taking its \(d\)-th power makes both original exponents divisible by \(d\).
This proves the required injection and the bound \(I\), without assuming that the geometric pullbacks split over a prescribed number field.

The unimodular coordinate change works with either sign and also when one primitive coordinate is zero, since the other is then a unit in \(\mathbb Z\).
For a single component the extreme coefficients in the new \(X\)-coordinate are nonzero over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)\), so substitution of \(X=y^g,T=s^m\) gives total degree \(W/c\) over \(s\).
Distinct generic roots remain distinct after extending the base in characteristic zero, and taking the \(g\)-th roots introduces no multiplicities since all roots are nonzero.
The deck group permutes the components transitively by translation on generic fibers over the original curve, hence their \(s\)-degrees agree.
The scalar pullback for \(d=\operatorname{lcm}(g,m)\) maps onto the diagonal pullback by a finite surjection, giving \(q_m\le I\).
After choosing a maximizing \(m_0\), every component's pullback by every \(h\)-th power is irreducible: a split at its generic point would force more than \(q_{m_0}\) components at \(hm_0\), since all other components still have generic preimages.
Étaleness over the torus excludes any vertical components in that test.

I checked Zannier's Theorem 2.1 in the author's preprint against the local passage and the resulting covers.
A common number field of definition suffices because only finitely many components at the chosen levels are used.
Their projections satisfy (PB) and have degree \(W/(cq_{m_0})\).
Over the cyclotomic closure of that number field the theorem gives full residue degree at every preimage outside a finite torsion set on the one-dimensional base.
After exclusion of finitely many further values, the normalization and the affine model agree in the fiber, so its point field is generated by \(y\) with \(s\) already in the base.
Taking the \(m_0\)-th powers of all exceptional values excludes every bad lift, even when the levels differ between geometric components.
The twist changes \(y\) to a torsion multiple of \(z\), and decreasing the coefficient field to \(H\) can only increase its degree.
The exceptional set is attached to the full fixed pair and supplies no uniform exclusion along varying pairs.

For a nontorsion root, adjoining both twists to \(\mathbb Q(z)\) costs at most \(22A\) by C0345v775, while their joint field has degree \(\varphi(w)\) and lies in \(H\).
The two towers give the asserted absolute degree fraction, including when their fields intersect nontrivially.
Multiplication by the absolute height and the uniform width-times-height estimate of C0345v769 give the stated measure bound for increasing primitive norm.
The same multiplication remains valid if the displayed lower approximation to the width-times-height is negative, by nonnegativity of the height.
On a finite initial range of primitive norms the positive lower comparison in C0345v775 is uniform over twists and dilations.
Together these give a positive \(b_F\) for the stated cofinite torsion fibers, with no claim of a Lehmer gap for exceptional fibers.
