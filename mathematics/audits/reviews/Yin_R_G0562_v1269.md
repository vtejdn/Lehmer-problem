## Uniform bound, sharpness for the graph equation and ramification

At 2026-11-25T15:59 UTC a separately assigned agent reviewed the [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v190.md#statement) and all four parts of its argument for Yin.
The reviewer received the target and scope without development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings on this claim and took no part in developing its proof.
The count holds for integer heights \(q\ge2\), uniformly in the height and the two coprime exponents.

On a disk centered at a solution \(z_0\), substituting \(z=z_0(1+w)\) gives
\[
  V(x,z)/V(x,z_0)=(1+Aw)/((1+w)(1-Dw)).
\]
Here \(A\) is integral and \(D\) has positive valuation.
All three logarithms therefore converge on every closed disk of positive depth, and division by the equation at \(z_0\) gives the required implication even when the logarithm has torsion kernel.
The three logarithmic derivatives give a numerator of degree at most two over an integral denominator with constant one.
The derivative is nonzero because the original rational function \(z^N/V(x,z)^m\) has a zero or pole at \(z=x\), where \(x\ne0\) and \(x^2\ne1\).
The recurrence from degree three onward gives the minimum coefficient valuation \(t_0\) among \(h_0,h_1,h_2\).
Scaling to the disk of depth \(1/E\) gives coefficient valuations \(\nu(h_{j-1})+j/E-\nu(j)\) for \(j\ge1\).
Thus at least one of the first three is at most \(t_0+3/E\), and the tail tends to infinity.
The last index of minimum valuation satisfies \(J-3\le E\nu(J)\).
For \(j\ge16E^2\), \(j-3\ge3j/4\) and \(3j/(4E)\ge3\sqrt j>\log_2j\).
At \(E=1\) the indices \(5,6,7\) already fail the inequality, and \(j-3>\log_2j\) for \(j\ge8\).
Strassmann's division argument in a finite extension containing the roots under comparison and a scale of valuation \(1/E\) proves both disk bounds, including torsion solutions.

The order of \(\bar z\) divides \(2^{r_z}-1\), so simple-root lifting gives its odd-order representative in \(\mathbb Q_2(z)\).
Uniqueness in any common extension makes the representative depend only on the residue.
The local value group then gives \(\nu(z-\xi)\ge1/E_z\), also when \(z=\xi\).
Since \(r_z\mid f_z\), a root of degree at most \(B\) with \(r_z>B/2\) must have \(E_z=1\).
Each of the fewer than \(2^{B+1}\) possible residues contributes at most four solutions.
For \(r_z\le\lfloor B/2\rfloor\), one may instead use \(E_z\le B\) and the disk bound \(16B^2\), with fewer than \(2^{\lfloor B/2\rfloor+1}\) residues.
This proves the explicit estimate, including \(B=1\) where the second group is empty.
Its implied \(O(2^B)\) constant is absolute because \(\sup_{B\ge1} B^2 2^{-B/2}<\infty\).
In a genuine integral realization the graph sign is positive as \(1<T<p/q\), each conjugate is a dyadic unit, and the separable degree-\(d\) minimum contributes \(d\) distinct solutions.
The bound on the fraction is \(C2^B/d\), and taking \(B\) equal to the largest local factor degree proves the claimed lower bound on it.
If degrees stayed bounded along a subsequence with \(q\to\infty\), the uniform archimedean radius \(3/2\) would bound the integer coefficients and give finitely many possible exterior roots, incompatible with \(1<T<1+1/q\).

Away from the two open sign disks, each of \(\nu(z-1),\nu(z+1)\) is at most one, whence \(l=\nu(z-z^{-1})\le2\).
For positive \(s=\nu(N)\), coprimality makes \(m\) odd.
Odd powering, including its inverse for negative \(m\), preserves the valuation of the difference from one on principal units.
Hence \(\nu(z^N-1)=e+l\) by the exact formula for \(V-1\).
The residue group has odd order, so \(z^{N/2^s}\) has residue one.
Starting at valuation at least \(1/E_z\), successive squarings take the valuation to at least one within \(k=\lceil\log_2 E_z\rceil\) steps and add at least one at each later step.
This follows by induction from \(t\mapsto\min(2t,t+1)\), including the possible extra cancellation when \(t=1\).
Thus \(s\ge k\) gives \(1+s-k\le e+2\), and the remaining values of \(s\) satisfy the stated inequality directly.
The two sign disks contain only the signs and the possible exceptional reciprocal pair by the cited sign-disk theorem.

At \(q=4,u=1,v=2^B\), clearing unit denominators leaves precisely the simple residues of \(z^{2^B-1}-1\) among the nonzero roots in characteristic two.
Each lifts uniquely in the degree-\(B\) unramified extension and \(z=-1\) fails the equation.
This gives \(2^B-2\) nontrivial unit roots of local degree at most \(B\).
The lower bound establishes sharp order for the graph equations alone.
