## Compressed degrees, the unique primitive Salem line and individual bounded orbits

At 2026-11-25T01:41 UTC an agent outside the development of v113 independently reviewed the [full statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v113.md#statement), its three proof sections and the cited fixed inputs for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed version without its development discussion.
The rank bound holds on the entire divisible group, and the stated classification, gap equivalence and strict-record alternative follow without a common bound on the orbits.

**The rank bound and every Pisot degree.**
The orbit of \([x]\) has size \(d_x\) because multiplication by the torsion-fiber size gives exactly the distinct classes of the actual class products.
This accommodates arbitrary radical lifts and twists outside the original splitting field.
If \(i\) is the first active constituent, multiplicity one gives \(W_i\subseteq V_x\subseteq V_{z_i}\), so \(\dim V_x\le ub\) on the formal inverse-pair model for the whole remaining stage.
The projection to \(W_i\) is equivariant and nonzero, giving a lower bound for \(d_x\) from its orbit.
In the signed space the coordinate multiples have \(2ub\) different images.
In the oriented sum-zero space the images of the coordinate axes are the centered basis vectors and their negatives, distinct for block size at least three.
The common sign action records the transitive orbit of the actual roots on these vectors, again giving \(2ub\) images.
For a noncoordinate vector in \(t\ge2\) blocks, transitivity covers all blocks by support sets and the independent within-block actions give at least \((u/t)b^t\) choices.
For a noncoordinate vector in one block, the supported sign/permutation orbit or the level-set orbit on a sum-zero block gives at least \(u\binom b2\) choices.
These estimates use the action on \(W_i\) itself, regardless of any additional summand at that stage.
Thus \(d_x\ge2ub=d_i\) for \(b\ge9\), and \(\dim V_x\le d_x/2\).
Every stage has positive measure at most \(m(w)\), so the least stage degree tends to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness, uniformly in the choice of \(x\).

For a Pisot unit \(p\) of degree \(e\), an integer relation among its conjugate classes gives zero upon taking the logarithm of the modulus after any embedding.
Transport a conjugate having maximal coefficient to \(p\), and subtract that maximal coefficient times the unit norm relation.
The remaining summands are products of nonpositive coefficient differences with strictly negative logarithms.
Their sum vanishes only if all coefficients agreed.
The all-ones vector is indeed a relation modulo torsion, proving rank exactly \(e-1\).
Any Pisot number in \(D(w)\) would be a unit because every finite valuation vanishes on that group.
It would therefore satisfy \(e-1\le d_p/2\le e/2\), hence \(e\le2\), contrary to the stage-degree lower bound for sufficiently small \(w\).
The argument uses no upper bound on \(p\).

**Primitive Salem values and terminal constituents.**
In the embedded real field of any Salem member \(p\), the unit line modulo signs has a primitive positive generator \(\sigma>1\).
Then \(p=\sigma^h\) and \(\mathbb Q(p)=\mathbb Q(\sigma)\), by the two inclusions from the construction.
The conjugates of \(\sigma\) map bijectively to the conjugates of \(p\) under powering, giving the Salem signature; the rank-one relative norm kernel supplies the same primitive unit.
Since \(D(w)\) is saturated and contains \(p\), it contains \(\sigma\).
The nonzero log-modulus function \(f_\sigma\) is a rational linear combination of the translates \(f_{w'}\) for conjugates \(w'\) of \(w\).
Its square norm is positive, so at least one scalar product \(\langle f_{w'},f_\sigma\rangle\) is nonzero.
Translation on the Galois group transfers this to \(\langle f_w,f_{\sigma'}\rangle\ne0\) for a conjugate \(\sigma'\).
Below \(\tfrac12\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\) the strengthened primitive Salem projection bound gives \(\log\sigma\le m(w)\), including when the original \(p\) has arbitrarily large measure.
This allows a fixed bounded-output threshold, for example \(T=1\) after shrinking \(m(w)\), when applying the chain comparison to \(\sigma\).

The unique exterior conjugate of \(\sigma\) excludes torsion quotients between distinct conjugates, by transporting either member of such a pair to the exterior one.
Since its degree is large, the actual-root dichotomy applies.
In the oriented case ordinary complex conjugation would preserve the half containing the real exterior root, but carry a neutral root in that same half into the opposite half.
There is a neutral label there because this is a Salem number and the block has at least two labels.
Thus the independent sign model applies, and distinct sign characters together with transitivity give absolute irreducibility of \(V_\sigma\).
The chain comparison yields \(V_\sigma=V_{z_i}\), which is irreducible precisely at \(i=s\).
For matching classes, point stabilizers determine the same compressed root field at corresponding embeddings.
In the positive real embedding torsion in that field is just the two signs, so \(z_s\) is a signed conjugate of a nonzero integral power of \(\sigma\).
The reverse implication follows by Galois invariance and saturation of \(D(w)\).
Two primitive positive Salem numbers matching this orbit of lines satisfy, after transport to the first exterior embedding, a power relation involving a conjugate of the second with modulus different from one.
It must be the second number or its real inverse.
Their fields coincide, and primitivity, positivity and orientation above one identify the two numbers.
This proves uniqueness and the degree identity with \(z_s\), without bounding the powers of this primitive terminal value.

**Iteration and the two directions of the gap equivalence.**
After one iteration the value is a real unit greater than one in \(D(w)\), being a signed product of exterior conjugates.
Each further value is at least its predecessor.
For the individual fixed group the set of measure values below any fixed ceiling is finite, so a bounded orbit reaches a fixed value after finitely many steps.
For a real fixed unit \(y>1\) all conjugates other than \(y\) have modulus at most one.
If any is neutral, its inverse is its complex conjugate, so irreducibility makes the minimum reciprocal and \(y^{-1}\) is the only interior root.
This yields a Salem number, while the case of all other roots strictly interior is Pisot and was excluded.
Every positive power of the unique primitive Salem unit is itself fixed, and these powers exhaust all such fixed units.
If the group contains no such line, its nondecreasing nontorsion orbits must all tend to infinity.

A sequence of input measures tending to zero with individually bounded orbits therefore supplies a sequence of primitive Salem values with logarithms at most those original measures.
The bound on the primitive value comes from detection, independently of the size of the terminal power or the number of steps taken.
Conversely any shrinking Salem sequence already has fixed orbits.
This proves precisely the equivalence of positive uniform gaps in the stated two classes.
For a strict record the chain stops at its initial compressed representative by first attainment.
An available Salem line then identifies the record itself with a signed primitive Salem conjugate up to inversion, since a proper power would exceed a positive measure available at the same degree.
In this case the entire group is the rank-one radial group of \(\sigma\), whose primitive measure generator is \(\sigma\) and whose first measure values are all fixed powers.
This proves both strict-record alternatives.
