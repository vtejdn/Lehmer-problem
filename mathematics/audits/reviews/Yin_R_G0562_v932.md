## Independent review of the entropy recurrence and the lifting bounds

At 2026-11-21T07:28 UTC a separate reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the full statement and proof submitted at 2026-11-21T07:22 in [C345 v791](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v791.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received the fixed claim and the review scope without development discussion.
The earlier results were checked at the interfaces in the bindings below.

For any law \(P\) supported on one specified line sum, the product law \(Q\) of its marginals is positive on the support of \(P\), even in the presence of deterministic bits.
Writing \(Q_y\) for its conditioning on that sum gives \(D(P\Vert Q)=D(P\Vert Q_y)-\log Q(y)\).
Fourier inversion bounds \(Q(y)\) by the product-modulus integral on the torus of the integral power basis.
In each contiguous block of at most \(d\) positions the retained columns are rationally independent, since dividing by the initial power leaves exponents less than the minimal-polynomial degree.
The induced torus map sends Haar probability to product Haar probability, also for an empty or shortened block.
Hölder over \(k\) blocks therefore gives one \(k\)-th root of a one-dimensional \(k\)-th moment per retained site.
At a retained site \(2p(1-p)\ge k^{-a}\), so that moment is bounded by the integral of \(\exp(-k^{1-a}\sin^2\pi x)\).
Reflection about \(1/2\) and \(\sin\pi x\ge2x\) on \([0,1/2]\) bound this integral by \((\sqrt\pi/2)k^{-(1-a)/2}\).
The remaining sites each have entropy at most \(u=H_2(k^{-a})\).
Thus \(t\ge (H(P)-Lu)/\log2\) retained sites supply the claimed line dependence bound, also when the lower bound on \(t\) is nonpositive.

For a uniform point of a maximal \(j\)-dimensional fiber the \(L\) slices have prescribed \((j-1)\)-dimensional records and marginal entropies at most \(\log M_{j-1}\).
For \(j=1\) the corresponding total upper bound is \(L\log2\).
Conditioning the row at a site on every earlier row retains its prescribed last-direction sum.
Each slice chain rule uses fewer conditioning bits, so its conditional bit entropy bounds from above the corresponding conditional bit entropy given all earlier rows.
Subtracting the joint chain rule in this direction gives a lower bound on \(\sum_i H(X_i)-\log M_j\) by the sum of the expected conditional row dependences.
The conditional row entropies themselves sum to \(\log M_j\), and their \(Lu\) allowances sum to \(L^j u\).
This proves \(h_{j-1}-u\ge(1+b)(h_j-u)\) for every dimension up to \(J\), with the same \(k,a,u,b\), and gives the stated finite bound on the actual maximum fiber.

When \(k/J\to c\in(0,\infty)\), one has \(Jb^2=o(1)\) and \(Jb=((1-a)/(2c\log2)+o(1))\log k\).
Balancing the resulting power with \(u=O_a(k^{-a}\log k)\) gives exponent \(1/(1+2c\log2)\).
The bounds are uniform in the choice of algebraic unit of a given degree.
For arbitrary parameters, positive rate and the rational kernel dimension first force \(k\gg_h J\).
Then \(u\to0\) at \(a=1/2\), so positive rate in the recurrence forces \(J\log(1+b)=O_h(1)\), whence \(k\gg_h J\log k\gg_h J\log(2J)\).
Taking a mask of density at least \(\delta\) with rate at least \(h\) gives full-grid rate at least \(\delta h\).
The matching construction cited in the statement requires both its entropy slack and its bound on the polynomial root discriminant.

Deleting any one full block of \(d\) coordinates gives an information set for the one-dimensional binary kernel, since the block columns differ from the power basis by an invertible multiplication modulo two.
The tensor of \(J\) such coordinate projections is an isomorphism from the full-grid code onto the coordinates of the product of their complements.
For independently selected blocks, each site has inclusion probability at least \(\theta=(1-1/\lfloor L/d\rfloor)^J\), with leftover positions in no deleted block.
Independent thinning with the ratio of \(\theta\) to that inclusion probability makes every inclusion probability exactly \(\theta\).
Conditional on any product and thinning outcome, the retained bits of a uniform codeword are independent fair bits.
For a fixed word the expected centered retained weight is \(\theta(w-n/2)\), so Jensen first in the random set gives the moment bound \(\exp(nv^2/8)\) in the required direction.
Optimizing at \(v=4\theta x/n\) gives \(\Pr(w>n/2+x)\le\exp(-2\theta^2 x^2/n)\).

If \(L/(Jd)\to c>1/\log2\) with \(c\) finite, both \(\theta\) and the dimension fraction \((1-d/L)^J\) tend to \(e^{-1/c}>1/2\).
Choose a fixed \(\epsilon>0\) with dimension fraction eventually greater than \(1/2+2\epsilon\).
The signed collision inequality at weight \((1/2+\epsilon)n\) bounds the fraction of light liftable words by \(M_J 2^{-\epsilon n}\).
Since \(\log M_J=o(n)\), the light liftable fraction is exponentially small, and the centered-weight bound gives exponential decay for the omitted tail as well.
For a mask of size \(s\), \(D_S\ge s-q\) and \(q/n\le\delta/3-\epsilon\) give \(3D_S/2-s\ge3\epsilon n/2\), in particular \(D_S>0\).
If the fraction \(p\) of liftable words satisfies \(\log(1/p)\le\eta n\), the two error terms of the shortened-code inequality together cost at most \(n(\sqrt{\eta/2}+\eta/\log2)+o(n)\) since \(D_S\le n\).
Taking \(\eta\) small in terms of \(\epsilon\) would force \(\log M_S\) to grow at a positive linear rate in \(n\).
The finite entropy bound at \(a=1/2\) gives \(\log M_S=o(n)\) uniformly for the specified upper and lower bounds on \(L/(Jd)\), excluding that range of \(p\).
These exclusions concern positive exponential fiber rate and do allow subexponential fiber sizes at the critical scale.
