## Height normalization, neutral extensions and the primitive count

At 2026-11-20T17:02 UTC a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the statement and all three argument sections of C0345v594 and found them valid.

Each signed cut sum is integral and nonzero, with absolute height at most \(Y=\log j+Nm/d=O(\log(2d))\), by averaging its archimedean bounds and using integrality at finite places.
The independent embedding fixing the coefficient gives a nontorsion ratio \(\eta\) in a compositum of degree at most \(d^2\).
Multiplying its minimal polynomial by the constant term gives an integer polynomial with constant term one, still having no torsion roots.
The disc bound supplies a conjugate of the inverse of modulus at least \(2^{1/(4D)}\), whose contribution to the absolute height is divided by \(D\).
Thus \(|n|\log(2)/(4D^2)\le h(z/\sigma z)\le2Y\), and taking differences of edge exponents gives the window \(2U\).
The original support reconstruction applies with these windows since every legal splitting of the chosen supports obeys them, and full terminality still tests every permitted split.
The finite integer window ensures an attained maximum, while the uniform fixed-arity bound and \(Bm\to0\) force its maximizing weight to diverge.

A neutral conjugate and its inverse are conjugate roots of the same irreducible polynomial, so substitution of the inverse defines an involution on the original field.
For the stable compositum \(E\), each restriction fiber has exactly \(d/[E:\mathbb Q]\) members, hence has a neutral member under the strict inequality.
At that member inversion agrees with complex conjugation on the generator and therefore on the whole field.
Restriction shows that each embedding of \(E\) intertwines its involution with conjugation.
Naturality takes the norm to its inverse, so all conjugates of that unit have modulus one, and Kronecker and norm transitivity give the stated torsion norm over \(F\).
For \(F=\mathbb Q(c)\), the norm height divided by \([K:F]\) equals \(h(c)\) and is at most \(h(z)\), also for negative edge exponents.

A nonzero primitive coordinate bounds the positive dilation by \(2U\), and the zero vector contributes at most one list.
The resulting logarithmic loss is \(O(\log(2d))\), sufficient under the separate degree hypothesis and, since eventually \(\log B\le L\), in the first signature regime.
In the intermediate regime a single equality and sign pattern costs \(O(r)\) in logarithmic count.
Nonzero group sums follow from minimality, and boundedly many groups would give only \(O(B)\) lists even with unrestricted degrees for the grouped coefficients, by the fixed-coefficient incidence bound.
Thus their number diverges and the least nonzero group size is \(o(r)\).
The extremal embedding of its sign gives a base with log modulus at least \(m/k\), and the degree and height of that group's coefficient give the loss \(g\log B+O(L+\log(2k)+\log(2r)+\log(2+Y))\).
The upper degree bound \(2kB^{2r}\) and the signature hypothesis make every term here \(o(rL)\).

In the final regime each output is allowed its own stable compositum of degree at most \(B^{2r}\).
A neutral member over the restriction of the fixed expanding embedding bounds the output minus its constant group by twice the larger side's coefficient mass.
Inversion followed by that fixed embedding conjugates every coefficient and output on the stable compositum, so their moduli stay fixed and the opposite side contributes at most one further mass.
The choice of side and of evaluating embedding is independent of the dilation, including when the output varies.
The complex matrix estimate at threshold four applies to threshold three and gives the asserted uniform hit count, with logarithm \(o(rL)\).
The weak endpoint of each degree comparison is covered by its lower regime, including \(B=1\) when the intermediate regime is empty.
Subtracting these losses from the retained logarithmic count proves the conclusion on arbitrary interlaced subsequences.
