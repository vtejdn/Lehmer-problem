## Local valuations and exceptional directions

The proof establishes both rectangle estimates and their stated consequences.
At a simple octic root the identity for the critical value of the quadratic in \(V\) makes \(F_U=-D'/(4A)\) a unit, and \(2A\) is a unit as well.
Thus the implicit branch has a unit second derivative, and the derivative of \(Vf'/f\) there is a unit.
Both integral implicit expansions converge on the open disks, also after finite ramified extension.
The residual factor in the implicit equation is a unit throughout the disk, so the section and \(J\) have corresponding zeros with the same multiplicities.
For a compatible pair, the binomial change and its inverse preserve valuations of differences since their linear terms are units and the higher terms in a difference quotient have positive valuation.
At the critical point the logarithmic derivative vanishes and \(J''\) is a unit modulo \(p\).
The logarithm is used for the critical value in the unramified field, where its linear term gives its valuation on \(1+p\mathcal O\).
The integral square-root change then identifies the two section roots over an extension and gives their distance valuation \(v_p(H)/2\), since \(2\) is a unit.
For a squarefree section the discriminant counts this pair twice, and a complementary resultant counts it once precisely when the factors separate the two roots.

Differentiating the critical value cancels the coefficient of \(V'\), giving \(H'=-\log(V/[v])\) and \(H''=-V'/V\).
Consequently \(h_0,h_1\in p\mathcal O\), \(h_2\) is a unit and \(v_p(h_n)\ge-v_p(n(n-1))\) for \(n\ge2\).
For \(n=3,4\) the denominator is a unit outside the stated set, and for \(n\ge5\), \(n-2-\log_5 n\ge1\) and grows without bound.
After substitution \(r=pT\) all nonconstant terms are divisible by \(p^2\).
If \(p^2\mid h_0\), division by \(p^2\) leaves a restricted series with a unit quadratic coefficient and all higher coefficients in \(p\mathcal O\).
At each lifting step the scalar reduction of the unit factor is invertible, so division by the reduced quadratic supplies both corrections, of degrees unrestricted and less than two respectively.
Completeness gives a factor which is a unit at every integral argument and two integral roots with multiplicity for the monic quadratic.
Rescaling their distances cancels the factor \(p^2\) exactly, yielding (3), also for coincident centers.
The coefficient estimate only enters for primes outside \(S\), which explicitly contains \(3\).

For two distinct primitive positive directions the determinant is a nonzero integer of absolute value at most \(KL\).
Two slopes closer than the stated threshold to the same center would force this determinant to have valuation exceeding \(\log_p(KL)\), since both denominators are units.
This also works in a ramified field and allows equality at the threshold for the retained slopes.
Eight geometric pairs account for at most sixteen exceptions per prime and at most \(16\log(2KL)\) of discriminant weight on each retained direction per prime divisor of \(k\) outside \(S\).
At the five fixed primes, the bound in v819 gives the additional cubic logarithm after its finite exceptional set of directions is removed.
In an integral monic factorization the square of the complementary resultant divides the full discriminant, and the certificate divides the absolute trace discriminant, whose square divides the full discriminant.
These comparisons require no splitting assumption at a residue collision.
The graph contains at most one point per index, so the rectangle exceptions restrict to \(O(M/\log M)\) indices by the prime-counting upper bound.
The logarithms of \(2KL\) and \(3+K+L\) are \(O_A(\log M)\), and \(\omega(k)\le\log(2M)/\log 2\).
For \(l=k^2+1\), primitivity holds identically and \(l/k\to\infty\), so the simple circle and exterior roots in v793 and their reciprocal interior roots give eventual squarefreeness.
