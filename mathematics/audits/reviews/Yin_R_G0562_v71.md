## Even flips, parity cosets and growth with the degree

The torsion-class product construction of C345v61 gives an irreducible reciprocal polynomial of the same measure with distinct root classes modulo torsion.
The present hypotheses refer to the roots and the signature after this operation.
Under that signature ordinary conjugation induces just one transposition \(\pi=(j\,k)\) on the labels, since every real and every neutral label is fixed.
Conjugating gives every edge transposition in the permutation image, and the transpositions generate each component symmetric group independently of the others.
Each component has the same size by transitivity, and all its edges occur in the original orbit.
In the component of a real label, \(b\ge a+3\) guarantees a neutral label because at most \(a+2\) labels are nonneutral globally.

Take a real label \(i\) and a neutral label \(\ell\) in the same component.
A conjugate \(c'\) of ordinary conjugation \(c\) can transpose \(i,\ell\) on labels.
Its permutation \(\tau\) is disjoint from \(\pi\).
In the signed semidirect product, \(cc'\) and \(c'c\) have equal permutations, so their quotient has the sum of their sign vectors.
The terms involving the signs of \(c\) give \(e_i+e_\ell\) because conjugation fixes the real roots and inverts the neutral roots.
The other terms give either zero or \(e_j+e_k\).
The resulting diagonal has a supported vertex and, since \(b\ge5\), an unsupported vertex in the component of \(i\).
The label transposition of those two vertices has a Galois lift.
Conjugating the diagonal by that lift simply permutes its coordinates, and taking the product isolates a two-sign flip.
Transport within and between components gives all separate even flip groups.
For two different labels the positive equal heights and torsion separation rule out a two-term dependence, since equality of the powered heights forces equal absolute exponents.
Dividing any putative torsion monomial by its transform under any within-component pair flip now forces the two exponents to vanish.
All labels are covered, proving full independence and exact counting of the Galois orbit of a monomial by signed exponent vectors.

For any algebraic unit the sum of logs over its distinct conjugates is zero, so half the sum of absolute logs gives its logarithmic measure even when its minimal polynomial is nonreciprocal.
The sum of the absolute exponents in a fixed coordinate over the distinct absolute orbit is a positive integer \(D\), constant by transitivity.
For \(2\le s<b\), parity can be corrected at an omitted vertex of each occupied component, giving every one of the \(2^s\) sign patterns over each absolute vector.
Averaging over one sign gives at least the corresponding individual term's absolute value and hence at least the average of these \(s\) individual bounds.
On summing, this yields \(2^{s-1}DL/s\).
For a supported pair \(i,j\), the label stabilizer of \(i\) realizes at least \(b-1\) images of \(j\), including when they are in different components.
One support through \(i\) can account for at most \(s-1\) of these images, and each such support contributes at least one to \(D\).
Thus the claimed factor follows from \(D\ge(b-1)/(s-1)\) and the integer minimum \(2/3\) of \(2^{s-1}/(s(s-1))\), attained at \(s=3,4\).

For \(s\ge b\) the even flip subgroup acts on a fixed signed support with orbit size \(2^{s-f}\), where each of the \(f\) fully occupied components imposes one independent parity condition.
Partial components allow arbitrary supported signs, and \(f\le\lfloor s/b\rfloor\).
For a uniform pattern on one such coset, fixing any four signs leaves at least one unprescribed coordinate in every full component, since \(b\ge5\).
All assignments of the prescribed signs therefore have equal counts.
Expansion gives second moment \(Q=\sum x_i^2\) and fourth moment \(3Q^2-2\sum x_i^4\).
For \(Q>0\) interpolation gives \(\mathbb E|X|\ge\sqrt{Q/3}\), which also holds for \(Q=0\).
Cauchy–Schwarz then gives the sum of the individual absolute terms divided by \(\sqrt{3s}\).
One coset per absolute vector suffices for the lower bound, and the same \(D\) counts the absolute exponents upon summation.
At \(s=5\), \(2^{4s/5-1}/\sqrt{3s}=8/\sqrt{15}>5/3\).
The logarithmic derivative of \(2^{4s/5}/s^{3/2}\) is \((4/5)\log2-3/(2s)>0\) thereafter, proving the result for all \(s\ge b\).

Two labels in one component have joint root-field degree at most \(2d(b-1)\le d^2/t\), since the second root has at most two choices on each of the other \(b-1\) labels once the first image is prescribed.
The height estimate in C345v68 applies to any invariant partition of a compressed reciprocal unit polynomial.
It gives \(L\gg\sqrt t/(\log(3d))^{106}\) here, and \(b\sqrt t=\sqrt{bd/2}\).
The resulting bound diverges with \(d\), uniformly over the permitted supports and exponents.
For one nonzero exponent, torsion separation preserves all \(d\) distinct roots under powering and reciprocity gives the same measure formula for a negative exponent.
For \(M(P)\to1\), finite bounded-degree coefficient sets force \(d\to\infty\).
The height inequality for the partition alone then gives \(t=o((\log(3d))^{212})\), hence \(b\to\infty\) and both component conditions eventually hold for fixed \(a\).
With one real exterior root and irrational ratio of the two exterior log moduli, C345v57 makes the original representatives independent, so a collision within or between labels is impossible before compression as well.
This is a restriction on monomials and leaves the existence of small-measure input sequences open.
