# A lower bound for the input Mahler measure from finite-place disks

<a id="input-bound-review"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T12:23 UTC an independent agent reviewer for Yin checked both sections of the [disk-sum implication](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v160.md#input-bound).
The reviewer had previously inspected the row congruences and the Salem disks independently, had no role in developing the height implication and received no discussion of its development or previous verdict on it.
The inequality, its uniform consequences and the Salem specialization follow with the stated constants.

Fixing any admissible \(p\), the finite comparison supplies one primitive witness \(F\) on classes \(0,1\) for which the residual factor product \(R\) satisfies \(m(R)\le2ph\).
The degree and row coprimality assertions apply to \(F\).
Its row degrees are at most \(16d+1\) and \(16d\), respectively, by taking the integer parts of the degree cap after removing the row offsets.
Stripping their monomials preserves these upper bounds, and a zero degree sum would make \(F\) a binomial having a repeated nonzero root in characteristic zero.
Thus \(1\le N\le32d+1\), while the exponent span is at most \(p(16d+1)\).

In the row-difference-one congruences for an integral presentation, the multiplier \(p\) is a unit at \(\ell\ne p\) and \(X\) has valuation zero on every selected disk.
The integral modulus \(Q\) therefore gives \(\min((p-1)a_D,v_D(Q))\) as a lower bound on both rows after multiplication by \(T\).
Subtracting \(c_D\) and retaining the nonnegative part gives \(u_D\) for those same two rows of \(F\), for every presentation chosen at an individual disk.
Primitivity guarantees at least one primitive row at each prime, so the factor determinant estimate bounds the valuation of their single nonzero integer resultant below by \(\sum_D u_D^2/s_D\) across distinct center residues.
The sum over primes with weights \(\log\ell\) thus gives a nonnegative lower bound \(B\) for the logarithm of this resultant.
With multiplicity two, the arithmetic row inequality gives \(m(R)+2h+(2/p)\log(CD)\ge(1-4/p)B/N\) for the actual span \(D\).
Substitution of the upper bounds on \(m(R),D,N\) gives (1), since \(p>4\).

The eliminant has valuation at least \(pa_D\), so \(c_D\le a_D\) guarantees \(u_D\ge(p-2)a_D\).
After division by \(2p+2\), the coefficient of \(pW(P,p)/d\) is
\[
 \frac{(1-4/p)(p-2)^2}{p(2p+2)}\,\frac{d}{32d+1}
 \longrightarrow \frac1{64}.
\]
The additive error is \(2\log(Cp(16d+1))/(p(2p+2))=o(1)\) uniformly in the stated prime window.
This proves the lower-limit implication and also forces \(pW(P,p)/d\to0\) along every sequence with \(h\to0\), even when the finite disk data vary with the input.
Here \(W(P,p)\) is the sum for the particular choices under consideration.

In the Salem specialization \(6p\mid d\), the \(m/q\) distinct residues at three have \(s_D=1/q\) and \(a_D\ge1\).
The cofactor \(1-X\) has valuation \(1/q\) at the disk centered at one and zero on the others, so all disks qualify.
Their total contribution is at least \(m\log3\), giving \((\log3)/64\) as a lower bound for the limiting logarithmic Mahler measure along admissible pairs.
The independently known Salem limit is \(\log2\).
For arbitrary units the required supply of disks with \(pW(P,p)/d\) bounded below remains open.
