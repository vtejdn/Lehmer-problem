# Independent review of the residual cyclotomic moments under finitely many forbidden orders

<a id="finding"></a>
## Protected conductors and localization of a possible excess

At 2026-11-24T20:51 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v28.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v28.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v28.
The residual moment vanishes at every order \(s\) dividing no forbidden member, even without integrability assumptions at forbidden roots.
Finite logarithmic integrals of the residual at all forbidden orders force it to be its mass times Haar measure.

If a root of order \(b\) maps under its \(k\)-th power to order \(s\), then \(s=b/\gcd(b,k)\) divides \(b\).
Thus all factors of \(\Phi_s(X^k)\) are allowed under the stated hypothesis on \(s\).
Its log integral against \(\mu\) is nonnegative and its positive part is bounded by \(\varphi(s)\log2\), giving the same uniform bound on the negative-part integral.
Taking the chord to a nearest primitive point and bounding the remaining chord factors by two gives \(E_s(kx)\le C_s+(\log|\Phi_s(e^{2\pi i kx})|)_-\).
This proves (2) on the submeasure \(\nu\) and on every fixed power image of \(\nu\).

Within each short interval, grouping denominators \(q\) by the exact valuations at all \(p\mid s\) ensures that its class lcm \(D\) has those same valuations.
Consequently \(D/q\) permutes the primitive fractions at denominator \(s\).
The Lipschitz bound gives \(E_s(qx)\le\log(D/q)+E_s(Dx)\), with the usual extended bound at a singularity.
Since \(D\) divides the containing interval's lcm, its logarithm has the claimed \(C\delta\log(2/\delta)N\) bound.
The number of valuation lists grows at most as \((1+\log N)^{\omega(s)}\) up to a fixed \(s\)-dependent factor.
Applying (2) once per nonempty class bounds the integral above the common threshold by \(O_{s,\nu}((1+\log N)^{\omega(s)}/(\delta N))\).
These errors tend to zero after summing on the dyadic tail at fixed \(\delta\).
Sending \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves (3), and adjoining finitely many terms gives an integrable supremum over all \(q\).
For \(s=1\) the valuation list is empty and the target is the single point zero.

For irrational \(x\), a smallest circular gap among \(0,sx,\ldots,2Nsx\) gives \(\|qsx\|\le1/(2N+1)\).
Dividing a nontrivial common divisor of its nearest numerator \(p\) and \(q\) would give a closer return, so \((p,q)=1\).
The minimizing denominators tend to infinity because every fixed positive denominator has positive error.
For a nonmultiple \(n\le N\), comparison with \(np/q\) gives \(\|nsx\|\ge(1-n/(2N+1))/q>1/(2q)\).
Two points in a block of length \(q\) have a nonzero difference of indices less than \(q\), giving separation at least \(1/(2q)\) by the cited spacing argument.
Hence the number of nonmultiples within distance \(y\) of zero per block is \(O(qy)\), with the count zero for \(y\le1/(2q)\).
Integrating this bound at \(y=se^{-u}\) for \(u\ge J\ge\log(2s)\) costs \(O_s(e^{-J})\) after division by \(N\), since \(q\le2N\).

For a multiple \(n=lq\le N\), the error from \(lp\) is less than \(1/2\).
If \(lp\) is nonprimitive modulo \(s\), every primitive numerator is at least \(1/2\) away in the scaled coordinate, so the truncated tail is zero.
If \(lp\) is primitive, so is \(p\), and the nearest primitive distance at \(nx\) is exactly \(l\) times that at \(qx\).
There are at most \(N/q\) such indices, giving the bound \(E_s(q_N(x)x)/q_N(x)\) for their normalized tail.
The suprema in (3) decrease to zero almost everywhere and the full supremum is integrable, so dominated convergence applies to the measurable choice \(q_N(x)\).
The residual and its fixed power images are atomless.
The bound on \(g_s+E_s\) controls the continuous truncation error, and the continuous-test second moment converges since the pairs with \(k(x-y)\in\mathbb Z\) have zero product measure at every nonzero integer frequency \(k\).
Taking the Cesàro limit and then removing the truncation gives (4), including each fixed multiplier \(k\).

For each tolerance, (4) excludes only a density-zero set of indices for each of finitely many previous subproducts.
The progression congruent to one modulo the prescribed orders and previous choices has positive density, so all seven required nonempty subproducts can be selected with small integrals.
The two- and three-difference products are allowed and equal one in modulus on the removed atoms.
Their nonnegative integrals therefore give the bounds \(-3\epsilon\le\int g_s\,d\nu\le7\epsilon\), proving (1).

If all forbidden residual integrals are finite, the allowed ones are finite by domination by \(\mu\) for their negative parts.
By (1), only the finite list of divisors of forbidden orders can contribute to the divisor sum for \(\int\log|1-z^k|\,d\nu\).
It is periodic with period \(\operatorname{lcm}(B)\), using period one if \(B\) is empty.
Its lower bound gives the ordinary uniform return estimate by \(|1-e^{2\pi ix}|\le2\pi\|x\|\).
For positive residual mass the ordinary rotation lemma gives Cesàro mean absolute value zero for these periodic integrals, so every term vanishes.
Finite Möbius inversion and the normalized cyclotomic moment theorem then identify the even residual as Haar times its mass.
For zero residual the identity is immediate.
The unconditional uniformity of the removed atomic masses makes the resulting measure a convex combination of Haar and the forbidden primitive-orbit probabilities.
Thus strict excess above all their objective values forces each maximizing probability to have a residual moment equal to \(-\infty\) at some forbidden orbit.
The result does not establish existence of such an excess.
