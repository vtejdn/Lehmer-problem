### Recovering and certifying the rational factors

The [production program](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0175/census.py) factors each parent over the integers, reconstructs it and checks the cyclotomic polynomial at each reported order.
Its monic Chebyshev trace and homogeneous Horner recurrence recover the full reciprocal factor, and normalization of the rational Sturm remainders uses only positive multipliers.
The serial loop and the ordered parallel map enumerate the same increasing list of positive \(b\) coprime to three.

For the independent check, I formed \(H_b\) by adding its six signed coefficients at \(0,3,12,b,b+9,b+12\).
I constructed the three proposed cyclotomic factors explicitly as \(X+1,\ X^2+1,\ X^4-X^3+X^2-X+1\) and divided by exactly those prescribed by the congruences.
Every division had zero remainder, and the remaining monic reciprocal polynomial \(P\) agreed coefficient by coefficient with the stored polynomial.
For \(\deg P=2n\), I recovered \(Q\) by successively subtracting multiples of \(X^n(X+X^{-1})^j\) for \(j=n,n-1,\ldots,0\), expanding each term with integer binomial coefficients.
The remainder was zero in all cases.

I certified irreducibility of each monic trace \(Q\) independently of the production factorization of \(H_b\).
For successive primes \(p=2,3,5,\ldots\), I skipped reductions sharing a factor with their derivative and factored the other reductions over \(\mathbb F_p\) with python-flint 0.9.0.
Any proper rational factorization of \(Q\) would have a monic integral factor of degree in \([1,\lfloor n/2\rfloor]\).
At each prime this degree would be a subset sum of the modular irreducible factor degrees, since monicity preserves degree after reduction.
The intersection of the possible degree sets was empty for all 480 traces, in each case at or before \(p=23\).
Every trace also had a real root in \((-2,2)\).
At that embedding \(t^2-4<0\), so it cannot be a square in the field of an irreducible trace root \(t\).
Consequently a root of \(X^2-tX+1\) has degree \(2n\) over \(\mathbb Q\), proving irreducibility of the full \(P\).
Its exterior roots distinguish it from every cyclotomic factor, and the exact reconstruction establishes multiplicity one for each listed factor.

### Exact signatures and small directions

For all 480 traces I computed the signed remainder sequence using integer pseudo-division with positive leading-coefficient multipliers and positive content removal.
The chain ended in a nonzero constant and neither endpoint \(-2,2\) was a root.
Reading signs at \(-\infty,-2,2,+\infty\) and omitting zeros gave exactly the four stored variations for every direction.
Each interior real trace lifts to two circle roots, each real exterior trace to one exterior root of its sign, and each conjugate pair of nonreal traces to one conjugate pair of exterior roots.
As an additional check for all 17 admissible \(b\le25\), pure-Python SymPy 1.14.0 reproduced the direct factorization of the parents, and the J170 routines for integer remainders and rational isolating intervals reproduced the four variations and signatures.

The factor degree ranges are \(12\)–\(370\) through \(360\) and \(12\)–\(730\) through \(720\).
The occurrence counts for orders \(2,4,10\) through \(720\) are respectively \(240,120,48\).

The two degree-fourteen factors match the displayed polynomials.
After sign change, exact rational evaluation has signs \((-,+)\) at \(27/20,7/5\) for \(b=2\), and at \(5/4,13/10\) for \(b=4\).
Irreducibility, reciprocity and the signature therefore certify both Salem numbers.

The other 472 directions have one negative exterior root and one nonreal exterior pair, including 232 through \(360\).
