## The wall terms cancel on rational classes and the same field contains the actual radical measure values

The field and group assertions, the growth exponent for distinct values, the treatment of chamber walls and the cubic example hold at their stated scopes.

Automorphisms trivial on the rational class space take each original root to a torsion multiple inside its finite splitting field.
Raising every original root to the order of that field's roots of unity therefore supplies ordinary monomials in \(L\) whose classes span \(V\).
All elements of \(D(w)\) are algebraic units by their power relations and finite-place valuations.
The log-modulus functional annihilates \(V^{-c}\) and is injective on the *rational* space \(V^c\): a fixed class with zero log modulus has twice its class represented by \(a\bar a=1\).
Real-linear injectivity of the functional on a space of dimension greater than one is not used.
For \(P=(1+c)/2\) this proves that the rational matrix condition \(Pg=P\) is equivalent to \(\ell\circ g=\ell\).
Taking adjoints for the invariant inner product identifies \(H\) with the pointwise stabilizer of \(V^c\), and the negative-functional coset, if present, acts there as minus the identity.
In particular \(V^H=V^c\) and \(L^H\) is real at the chosen embedding.

For any radical or torsion-twisted input \(x\), every absolute lift of \(H\) preserves the modulus of each actual conjugate upon applying that lift, since this depends only on its class.
It permutes the exterior set.
The actual measure is the product of that full set times a rational sign, hence is fixed even when conjugates of \(x\) are outside \(L\) or distinct conjugates have equal class.
Positivity and closure of \(D(w)\) put it in \(U\).
Averaging classes realized by ordinary monomials in \(L\) over \(H\) can be carried out by actual products in \(F\); squaring gives positive representatives and preserves the rational span.
Thus \(U\) spans \(V^c\); Dirichlet's unit lattice and the absence of positive torsion other than one give its rank \(r\).

To check the degree factor in (3), use a normal field containing \(L\) and all conjugates of \(x\).
Over its group every actual conjugate occurs with the same multiplicity, irrespective of torsion collisions.
Every element of \(G\) also occurs with the same restriction multiplicity.
The ratio of these multiplicities gives \(\deg x/|G|\) multiplying the strict-positive sum over \(G\), including on walls.
The chosen subset of group elements is stable under left multiplication by \(H\), so each chamber image belongs to \(V^H\).
At a generic point of the wall \(\ker\ell\) the changing functionals are precisely \(\pm\ell\), since all translates have the same dual norm.
In dimension one its origin is on only that one distinct hyperplane, so the same crossing argument works.
On \(V^c\) the difference map acts by \(\nu|H|\), since the terms in the negative coset, when present, have the opposite scalar and the same cardinality.

The lattice from \(L\) has regular class points off the fixed spaces of every nonidentity group element inside either adjacent open chamber.
For each \(a\in U\), large positive multiples of these points and their translates by \([a]\) still have that property.
Their actual representatives in \(L\) thus all generate \(L\).
The four measures in the ratio have degree factor one in (3); equality of classes becomes equality of positive real values.
This proves \(U^{\nu|H|}\subseteq\mathcal M\).
It bounds the exponent of a finite quotient because \(U\) is finitely generated, and also makes the rational span of its measure subgroup all of \(V^c\).
A group element fixing all actual measure values must accordingly lie in \(H\).
Galois correspondence yields the asserted field identity, and the rank of the difference of the adjacent chamber maps gives \(s\ge\lceil r/2\rceil\).

For a rational class \(v\) in any wall face, choose a neighboring chamber by an infinitesimal generic perturbation.
For each rational point \(p\) in the relative interior of the same face, the vanishing indices are the same, and \(\ell(gp)=0\) gives \(cgp=-gp\) by projection and rational injectivity.
The newly positive indices among the wall terms are stable under \(g\mapsto cg\) because the two real functionals coincide identically.
If \(c\ne1\) these terms cancel in pairs on \(p\).
If \(c=1\), vanishing requires \(gp=0\), so each term makes zero contribution.
This includes the class zero of the torsion inputs.
By rational linearity the neighboring chamber agrees with the strict face map on the space spanned by the rational relative-interior points.
Thus all output classes lie in the finitely many rational chamber images of dimension at most \(s\), also after the possibly varying scalar degree factor.
The positive value group embeds in its logarithmic unit lattice, where height extends to a norm.
The triangle inequality for the exterior product gives \(h(M(x))\le\log M(x)\), hence lattice counting gives the upper growth bound on distinct numbers.
For the matching lower bound the full lattice of ordinary monomials maps to a rank-\(s\) lattice under a maximizing chamber map.
Translating a bounded parallelepiped far along a regular lattice vector in that chamber avoids the finite union of stabilizer spaces, and positive dilations preserve these conditions.
Its integer grid has distinct images, every grid input generates \(L\), and the input logarithmic measures are \(O_w(k)\) at dilation \(k\).
This gives the required lower order for all large \(T\) by taking an integer dilation of size comparable to \(T\).

For \(X^3-4X+1\), irreducibility and discriminant \(229>0\) nonsquare give the totally real \(S_3\) splitting field.
The class span is the nonzero standard plane because the root product is \(-1\).
In a generic chamber the positive selections among the three rotations and among the three reflections each have size one or two by their separate zero sums.
Each resulting complex coefficient of \(z\) or \(\bar z\) has modulus one.
The real-linear map is nonzero with determinant given by the difference of their squared moduli, hence has rank one.
Therefore the count grows linearly despite \(r=2\); the faithfulness of the standard plane with \(c=1\) gives the full splitting field.
