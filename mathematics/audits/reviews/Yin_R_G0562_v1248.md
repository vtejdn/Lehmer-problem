## Counting the real split factors and computing the full displacement

An agent outside the development of [C0901 v170](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v170.md#statement) reviewed the fixed statement and the three proofs for Yin.
The fixed pre-assessment text had SHA-256 `5C734331D6CBD9CDB759C9924611537C0BCB46F8D1C8F8E6A220A7952F12C5FA`.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its dependency locators without development discussion of this claim.
The count, its realization by powers in an order, the length and degree estimates, and the separate conjugate calculation hold for the indicated totally real number fields.

For each powered Salem root, all conjugate traces are strictly inside \((-2,2)\) except for the distinguished value, which is \(>2\).
A conjugate on the unit circle could attain an endpoint after taking a power only if the original conjugate were torsion.
All embeddings of \(F_z\) extending the distinguished embedding of the field generated by the powered trace would share the exceptional value.
There is exactly one such embedding, giving equality of the two fields.
Thus the specified reduced traces force inclusion of both embedded trace fields in \(k\).
Ramification at a real place bounds all norm-one reduced traces by two in absolute value, since the local algebra is Hamilton.
The two required sets of real embeddings have sizes \([k:F_x]\) and \([k:F_y]\), and coincidence of both restrictions is exactly the identity restriction to \(F_xF_y\).
Their union gives (1) without requiring any disjointness of these fields.

In the algebra \((t_x^2-4,t_y^2-4)_k\), a real local algebra is split precisely when at least one parameter is positive.
The trace discriminants change signs on exactly the required fibers; neither vanishes at any real place.
The elements \((t_x+I)/2,(t_y+J)/2\) each satisfy the designated quadratic and have reduced norm one.
The standard four-generator span is a full order since both Hilbert parameters are integral.
Each quadratic unit generates a finite integral algebra and has an inverse there; a positive power is one modulo twice that algebra by finiteness of its unit group in the finite quotient.
Twice the quadratic span lies in the corresponding integral coordinate span \(1,I\) or \(1,J\).
Hence both powers and both inverses belong to the same order, proving attainment with the stated freedom in the exponents.
For one root, the separate order containing the unpowered element is closed because \(J\) acts on that quadratic algebra by its nontrivial conjugation and \(J^2=-1\).

In a split plane with real characteristic roots, the diagonal action of the powered matrix is a dilation of the upper half-plane, with achieved minimum displacement \(2a\log x\) on the imaginary axis.
A norm-one matrix with a nonreal characteristic pair at a split place is elliptic and attains zero displacement there.
The action is semisimple in every factor, so the product minimum of the sum of squared displacements is attained at a tuple of minimizing points; omission of any hyperbolic factor would change the metric being estimated.
This proves (2).
Distinct embedded root fields and preservation of these fields under powers give the hypotheses of the two-unit height product in v47.
The joint degree \(D\) is at most four times the trace compositum degree \(f\), which itself is at most \(n_xn_y\).
Using the total log measures \(\log x,\log y\) in v47 bounds their product below by a constant times \(n_xn_y/[f(\log(12n_xn_y))^{212}]\).
The product of the two scaling factors in (2) is \(4ab[k:\mathbb Q]/\sqrt{n_xn_y}\), giving (3) exactly up to the absolute constant.
When \(x,y\le B\), the same lower bound forces \(f\gg n_xn_y/[(\log B)^2(\log(12n_xn_y))^{212}]\), and (1) gives \(r+1\ge f(1/n_x+1/n_y)\).
Thus bounded \(r,B\) imply bounded degrees and hence finitely many polynomials by bounded houses.
In the example the two quadratic trace discriminants are \(13\) and \(8\), giving distinct real quadratic fields; their compositum has four embeddings and the union of the two marked sets has size three.
Substitution \(t=Z+Z^{-1}\) in the two trace minima reproduces the displayed reciprocal quartics.

For two different trace conjugates only one of them can be carried to the exceptional trace by any single embedding, by injectivity.
The equal individual degrees give two disjoint fibers of size \(h\) in their compositum, and the same Hilbert-symbol construction attains the resulting minimum.
At the distinguished embedding \(|x|>1\) and \(|y|=1\), so a multiplicative relation between the roots first forces the exponent of \(x\) to vanish and then the exponent of \(y\) to vanish as well.
Both root degrees are \(2n\) and adjoining the roots to the trace compositum costs at most a factor of four.
Substitution of \(J\le4nh,\ d=2n\) into the two-point curve bound of v150 gives \(m/n\ge[N(29920\log(4N))^{18}]^{-1}\) with \(N=\lceil\sqrt{2J}\rceil\le4\sqrt{nh}\).
Since \(h\le n\), multiplication gives (4).
The source comparison in the final paragraph was checked against the statement of [Frączyk–Pham, Theorem C, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02332#page=4): the ambient group is fixed, the lattice irreducible and arithmetic, and the short *squared* lengths belong to a cyclic subgroup.
It is a comparison, not a further premise giving a bound on the one-element length in one split plane.
