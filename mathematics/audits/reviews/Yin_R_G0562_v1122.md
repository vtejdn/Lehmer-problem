## Positive images, individual conjugation probabilities and the trace-field examples

At 2026-11-24T22:42 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v65.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v65.
The strict bound (1), the finite inequality (3), both bounds in (2) when \(\delta>0\), the shrinking-sequence consequences and the examples on the orthogonal branch hold.
The height argument applies to nonintegral algebraic numbers as stated.

The exponents of \(A_N\) satisfy \(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)=B_N\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}j(N-j)=B_N(N+1)/3\).
On the unit circle its modulus is that of the Vandermonde on \(1,v,\ldots,v^{N-1}\), since the extra monomial factors have modulus one.
Hadamard and the maximum principle applied also to the reversed polynomial give the uniform archimedean bound after dividing by \(\max(1,|v|)^{B_NJ}\).
At the finite places the integral coefficients give the corresponding bound one, even at a denominator of \(z\) after this same normalization.
At a positive real image \(v\), the normalized \(j\)-th factor is \(1-e^{-j|\log v|}\) and \(v=1\) is excluded by nontorsion.
Concavity of \(y\mapsto\log\min(1,y)\) first averages \(j\) to \(J\), then averages \(|\log v|\) over the indicated embeddings.
The average on that subset is at most \(2H/R\), since the absolute logarithm sum at all archimedean places, counted with embedding multiplicities, is bounded by \(h(z)+h(z^{-1})\).
The extracted factors at all places contribute \(B_NJH\), and the product formula for the nonzero \(A_N(z)\) gives (3) with complementary contribution \((1-R)\log N/(N-1)\).

For \(0<p\le1\), \(L_0=\log(8/p)\) gives \(L_0/p>1\).
The stated ceiling yields \(N-1\ge15L_0/p\) and \((N+1)/3\le6L_0/p\).
Also \(L_0-\log L_0\ge\log8-\log\log8>\log(17/8)\), giving \(\log N\le2L_0\).
If \(R\ge p\), \(H\le2h\) and \(h\le p^2/(100L_0)\), then \(JH\le0.12p\).
The increasing function \(R\log^+(R/(2JH))\) is then at least \(p\log(25/6)>p\), while the right side of (3) is at most \((2/15+0.12)p<p\).
The contradiction excludes equality in the claimed height floor.
With \(z=w^2\), images above each real conjugate are positive and the same fractions can be measured in a common finite field by constant extension counts.
The height of \(w^2\) is \(2h\), including a possible degree drop.
If \(p\ge\sqrt h\) then \(\log(8/p)\le\log(8/\sqrt h)\), and the complementary case gives the stated asymptotic directly.
The comparison with Garza uses the positive-argument inverse hyperbolic sine and its published height normalization.

For \(g^{-1}cg(a)=b\) the image of \(ab\) under \(g\) is \(|g(a)|^2>0\).
A torsion \(ab\) with such an image must equal one, so \(b\ne a^{-1}\) ensures \(z=ab\) is nontorsion and \(h(z)\le2h\).
The fraction of positive images of \(z\) is at least \(p_b\), giving the individual probability bound on the entire indicated range.
Choosing an off-circle \(a\) makes \(c(a)\ne a^{-1}\), and every element commuting with \(c\) realizes \(b=c(a)\).
This gives \(p_b\ge1/C\).
For any fixed \(a\), exactly the automorphisms sending \(a\) to a circle point realize the inverse image, so the other images carry total probability \(\delta\).
This remains true if \(a^{-1}\) is absent from the root list.
The normal subgroup generated by the conjugates of \(c\) acts on \(a\) with orbit size \([T(a):T]=D\), by restriction from the compositum over the normal field \(T\).
The possible images are contained in this orbit, so one noninverse probability is at least \(\delta/D\) whenever \(\delta>0\).
Monotonicity of \(p^2/\log(8/p)\) gives (2) after multiplication by \(d\).

At \(p\ge\epsilon\sqrt{\log d/d}\) and growing \(d\), the denominator \(\log(8/p)\) is \(O_\epsilon(\log d)\), so the total measure has a uniform positive floor.
Taking all noninverse pairs gives the uniform little-oh on a shrinking unit sequence, since the estimates use only \(d,m\) and the tested fraction.
The two displayed choices of \(b\) then force the stated divergence of \(C\sqrt{\log d/d}\), and of \(D\sqrt{\log d/d}\) when \(\delta\) stays positive uniformly.
Kronecker gives \(\delta>0\) for nontorsion units.
For \(r\) real roots one can use \(p=r/d\) directly, so \(r\ge\epsilon\sqrt{d\log(2d)}\) also yields a positive gap, with bounded degrees covered by coefficient finiteness.
For totally real reciprocal traces all off-circle roots are real, since a nonreal reciprocal pair over a real trace has modulus one.

For the examples, the natural \(S_n\)-action gives a primitive totally real field in arbitrarily large even degrees by v45.
The prescribed signs of \(b\) give signature \((2a,n-a)\) for the quadratic extension and the norm image of units contains the base-unit squares, so its norm-one kernel has rank \(a\).
A nontorsion element of that kernel has a real reciprocal pair at every split place, and a nonreal circle pair at every other place, with no image equal to either sign.
Its trace has both an interior and an exterior conjugate, hence generates a nontrivial subfield of \(F\).
Primitivity forces that field to be \(F\), and inversion nontrivial on \(w\) gives the full quadratic field.
The primitive mixed-trace criterion applies with \(a\ge2\), so the units are orthogonal as asserted and the number of real roots is exactly \(2a\).
