## Conclusion

The norm comparison gives an integer grading at every precision strictly above the stated cap.
The directed-path conclusion holds for the components meeting the degree threshold, and the mass estimate holds for any fixed finite set of primes on the stated shrinking Salem rows.

**Grading.**
For adjacent roots \(y,b\), the compositum degree \(w\le uv\le Dd\) divides \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,Dd)\), as do the two individual degrees.
All conjugates of \(yb/2\) lie in \(1+\{x:v_p(x)\ge T\}\).
This is a multiplicative group for real \(T>0\), so taking the compositum norm and the \(L/w\)-th power preserves the precision.
Transitivity of norms gives the quotient of the two vertex labels asserted in the Claim.
Inversion takes each label to its reciprocal, and reversing the image arrow keeps the head-to-tail quotient the same.
On any simple circuit, multiplication along the traversal therefore gives \(2^{Lh}\equiv1\) at precision \(T\), where \(|h|\le D+d\).
For \(h\ne0\), the odd-prime lifting formula applied to \(2^{(p-1)L|h|}-1\) bounds \(v_p(2^{L|h|}-1)\) by \(s_p+\lfloor\log_p(Dd)\rfloor+\lfloor\log_p(D+d)\rfloor\).
This also rules out the two-edge circuit formed by opposite arrows.
Signed length thus vanishes on every closed walk by decomposition into simple circuits and backtracks, giving the grading on each component.
A simple path between opposite sides has odd length and nonzero signed length \(h\).
If both endpoint factors are self-reciprocal their labels are signs, so squaring yields the same contradiction with \(2L|h|\) in the exponent since \(p\) is odd.

**Large local degrees.**
For an original edge at depth at least \(T\), the conjugates over either coordinate field give the derivative inequalities of the cited local estimate with \(t=T\).
They hold for the stated separable integral polynomials because products over all other integral root differences have nonnegative valuation, and the discriminant counts the contribution of each local conjugate.
If the fields differ, the conclusion \(wT\le2C\) rules out an endpoint of degree \(u>2C/T\).
Consequently any neighbor of such a vertex has degree \(u\), also along reflected edges since inversion preserves the embedded fields.
For a fixed root at one end, transporting a matching pair by a local embedding supplies a match at the other end.
Two matches at that root would give a difference of depth at least \(T\) between two roots of the opposite polynomial.
The derivative there has valuation at least \(T\) at every conjugate of either matching root, forcing the opposite discriminant valuation to be at least \(uT>C\).
This proves uniqueness in both directions and bounds the degrees in the original and reflected matchings.
Each vertex then has at most one incoming and one outgoing arrow.
Finiteness and the grading imply that a connected component with an edge of this kind is a directed path.

**Nonsplit places.**
For a Salem coordinate \(x\), the two roots \(x,x^{-1}\) of the trace quadratic are distinct.
Its local irreducible factor is preserved by inversion exactly when an embedding taking \(x\) to \(x^{-1}\) fixes the trace and induces the nontrivial automorphism of the local quadratic extension.
The uniqueness of the conjugate of maximal modulus gives \(\mathbb Q(A^a)=\mathbb Q(A)\) for positive \(a\).
Inversion acts on both generators in this field, and their traces therefore generate the same fixed field.
These embedded equalities also give equality of the corresponding completions.
At any odd prime where both individual quadratics are nonsplit, depth greater than \(T_0\) would itself give an edge between two self-reciprocal factors on opposite sides.
At two the difference of \(2\) and the unit \(YB\) is a unit.
For fixed \(S\), the remaining depths on the simultaneously nonsplit subset are bounded by the caps \(T_0(p)=O_S(\log(2d))\), since \(D<d\).
On this subset the capped and uncapped depths coincide, so its norm mass is bounded by the sum of the capped masses over all places above \(S\).
With \(U=O(\log(2n)/n)\) this gives the displayed square-root bound.
Here \(n\le d\) and \(n\gg d/(\log(3d))^{O(1)}\), which make the bound tend to zero.
The result leaves open the mass of places where at least one individual trace quadratic splits, including places at which the two coordinate completions coincide.
