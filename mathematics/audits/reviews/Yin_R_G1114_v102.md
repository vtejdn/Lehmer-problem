# Review of the second coefficient discrepancy at delay two

<a id="delay-two-return"></a>
## Both first-scale models fail at their next discrepancy

At 2026-11-16T19:55 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked all sections of L462v124, v125, v127 and v128.
The reviewer had no role in developing these proofs and received the four fixed arguments without development dialogue, author explanations or an earlier verdict on them.
The [signed discrepancy review](Yin_R_G0562_v29.md#centered-limit) and the [first marginal review](Yin_R_G1114_v101.md#moving-period) supply the previously checked inputs at the first scale.
Their hypotheses hold for every sequence of monic Pisot minimal polynomials with \(M(z^2f\pm f^*)>1\) tending to one.
Such a sequence eventually has the matched sign, unit constant, a first discrepancy \(n\to\infty\) and a sign \(a=\pm1\) that can be fixed on a subsequence.
The attached conjugation-invariant measure has mass \(1+t/2\to1\).
Hence every joint weak limit of its powers is a probability.

**Recurrence and interpolation.**
If \(d=\deg D=O(s)\), enlarging any fixed window by \(d\) controls the error in \(\sum d_m h_{j-m}\) by \(\|D\|_1\) times the uniform moment error, where \(\|D\|_1=\sum_m|d_m|\) is bounded.
The corresponding integral is bounded by \(2\mu(\mathbb T)^{1/2}\|D(\bar v)\|_{L^2(\mu)}\), independently of \(j\).
Integer rounding therefore gives a simultaneous exact recurrence through the full interval needed for interpolation, including negative indices.
The two unit end coefficients give a unique bilateral extension from \(d\) consecutive terms.
Reflection preserves the recurrence because \(D^*=\pm D\), including for repeated circle roots, and the initial data agree with their reflections by the local recurrence.
For \(S=1+\sum_{j>0}b_j z^j\), \(D(z)S(z)\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(d\) and \(D(z)S(1/z)\) is a Laurent polynomial by reciprocity.
The coefficient at \(z^j\) in their sum is the bilateral recurrence at \(j\), including the doubled constant \(b_0=2\).
This proves \(S(1/z)=-S(z)\) as a rational identity, even with repeated roots of \(D\).

Since \(h_1=F(0)-1=0\), the numerator and denominator of the inverse quotient \(F'\) both start with \(-z\).
Their reciprocal relation gives circle modulus one at regular points and excludes any remaining circle pole.
After clearing \(D\) its rational degree is at most \(d+2\).
The comparison difference is \(z(F-F')\) times a unit at zero, so the local agreement through \(4d+12\) gives a common quotient jet through \(4d+11\).
The common Blaschke multiplier has degree at most \(d+3\), counting the single seed pole and all disk poles of \(F'\) with multiplicities.
Its two products are Schur, and the product for \(F'\) has Blaschke degree at most \(2d+5\).
The finite Schur uniqueness theorem thus gives \(K^0=S\), impossible since \(S\) is analytic in the disk and the numerator of \(K^0\) at the matched root \(\tau\in(0,1)\) is \((1-\tau)(1-1/\tau)\ne0\).
The argument works also for one binomial and for coincident exponents in a binomial product.

**First-scale agreement.**
At a fixed multiple of \(p=n+1\), the two possible marginal laws give exactly the corresponding model coefficient by integrality.
For \(p\nmid j=O(n)\), the integers at \(j+kp\) give every mixed moment \(2\int Xy^k\), with \(X=v^j,\ y=v^p\) in the limit.
For the arclength density \(W/2\), \(W=2+a(y+y^{-1})\), the projected measure weighted by \(2X\) has density \(Q\) bounded by \(W\).
Fourier decay and integrality make \(Q\) an integer Laurent polynomial.
The pointwise quadratic zero of \(W\) gives a double root of \(Q\) at \(-a\), so \(Q/W\) is integral Laurent and has modulus at most one.
Parseval leaves zero or a signed monomial.
In the latter case \(X=b y^l\) almost surely, giving doubled moment two at \(2(j-lp)\), a nonzero index in a fixed linear window where the integer bound is one.

For the half-atom law, the continuous contribution \(B_k\) tends to zero, and the even mixed moments show the atomic mean \(A\) is an integer.
Each \(B_k\) is then an integer of modulus at most one.
An extreme value \(B_k=b=\pm1\) would confine the continuous component to \(Xy^k=b\), and the atomic component already has \(y=a\).
The resulting binomial product falls under the recurrence exclusion at \(s=n\), with positive second exponent \(|j+kp|\) since \(p\nmid j\).
Both signs of this exponent give the same modulus.
Once \(B_k=0\) for all \(k\), the initial zero at \(0<|j-lp|<p\) forces \(A=0\), taking \(l\) fixed after extraction.
These checks exclude any sequence of mismatching nonmultiples in a fixed linear window.
They also apply to each fixed character in a joint limit by adding its signed exponents and using the doubled mass at index zero.
If \(\deg f/n\) stayed bounded, cross multiplication with the selected model would give a polynomial of degree at most \(\deg f+2+p\) vanishing to strictly higher order, again contradicting the interior pole.

**The longer window.**
For a full model prefix through \(q-1\), coefficient extraction in \((1-z)^2J=1-z^2-2zK^0\) gives \(J_j=2(1-A(j))\) for \(1\le j<q\).
Fejér positivity gives the stated inequality with constant term \(q(1+t/2)\).
In the polynomial models \(A(s)\) equals \(s+a(s-p)_+\).
In the positive geometric model \(A(s)\) has order \(s^2/p\) for \(s\ge2p\), while pairing consecutive terms for the alternating model gives \(lp+\min(r,p)\) at \(s=2lp+r\), \(0\le r<2p\).
Thus \(A(j)-1\) is comparable from below to \(A(q)\) on \([q/2,3q/4]\) when \(q/p\to\infty\), including the negative polynomial case where \(A(j)=p\to\infty\).
Every other term is nonnegative, so the weighted sum, even after subtracting \(q/2\), bounds a positive constant times \(q^2A(q)\).
This gives \(t=O(q^{-2})\).
The first-scale Gram bound holds on every translated block of \(p-1\) consecutive positive indices, giving an absolute moment sum \(O_C(q/\sqrt p)\) through \(Cq\).
Since \(q\log\alpha=O(1)\), the reciprocal quadratic kernel has coefficients \(O_C(q)\) there.
Convolution followed by multiplication by \(t/2\) gives the claimed \(O_C(p^{-1/2})\) error, sufficient also for all fixed multiples of \(q\) in the bilateral convention.

**The second discrepancy.**
The interior pole gives a finite first mismatch \(q\) with either disk-analytic model, and the first-scale agreement gives \(q/p\to\infty\).
At this scale the actual mismatch and the model coefficient are bounded integers, so they can be fixed along a subsequence.
For the continuous marginal, the same double-zero argument applied to the limiting pair \((x,y)=(v^p,v^q)\) now gives a nonzero signed monomial quotient because the model coefficient at \(q\) is zero.
This forces \(y=c x^b\) almost surely and hence binomial concentration at the positive exponent \(q-bp=O(q)\), excluded at \(s=q\).
For the half-atom marginal let \(e\) denote the model coefficient at \(q\).
For each fixed \(r<0\), \(0<q+rp<q\) eventually, and its known doubled moment is \(e a^r\), both when \(p\mid q\) and when \(p\nmid q\).
Fourier decay on the continuous component as \(r\to-\infty\) forces the atomic mean to be \(e\).
The continuous conditional mean has modulus at most one and integer constant Fourier coefficient \(h_q-e\ne0\), so it is a constant sign \(c\) almost everywhere.
Equality in the conditional mean then forces \(y=c\) on this component, while \(x=a\) on the other.
The product of the two corresponding binomials has coefficient absolute sum at most four and degree \(p+q=O(q)\), contradicting the same recurrence exclusion.
These arguments exhaust the extracted weak limits and rule out a delay-two sequence with measures tending to one from above.
The conclusion is qualitative and supplies no numerical gap.
