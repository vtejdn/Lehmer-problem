## Independent review of the degree formula, periodicity and quadratic example

At 2026-11-21T07:10 UTC a separate agent working for Sheng Yin reviewed the full proof of [C345 v789](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v789.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing the proof and received the fixed text and the requested scope without development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings on this claim.
The earlier theorems listed below were checked at their stated interfaces.

For \((U,V)=(z,z^n)\) the section has width \(Dn+O_F(1)\), and its normalized polynomial has leading coefficient one and constant coefficient \(\pm1\).
Thus \(z\) and \(z^n\) are units, and the individual height theorem gives \(n h(z)\to m(F)/D\) for every sequence of nontorsion roots.
A fixed pair with nontorsion first coordinate occurs at most once as \(n\) varies, so the sequence avoids the finite singular locus and lifts over \(\mathbb Q(z)\).
The \(V\)-degree of the irreducible Laurent equation is the degree of the projection \(U\), since both its extreme \(V\)-coefficients are nonzero.

Fix any root \(\lambda\) of the monic positive-measure factor \(q\).
It is nonzero and has multiplicity \(e\) in the top coefficient.
For generic \(w\to\infty\), the equation \(F(U,w)=0\) has exactly \(e\) solutions near \(\lambda\) by uniform convergence of \(w^{-D}F(U,w)\) on the boundary circle.
Any limit of their lifts on the compact normalization has \(U=\lambda\) and \(V=\infty\).
Conversely a pole \(Q\) above \(\lambda\) of order \(s_Q\) contributes exactly \(s_Q\) such lifts, by the local degree of \(1/V\).
Generic fibers avoid singular and critical values, so counting the small-disk solutions once on the curve gives \(\sum_{Q:U(Q)=\lambda,\ V(Q)=\infty}s_Q=e\).

All poles above all conjugates of \(\lambda\) form a Galois-invariant finite set.
Choosing one disjoint geometric neighborhood per pole and permuting them at each embedding gives the same union of off-circle neighborhoods.
A neighborhood over an interior value may require both \(|U|<1\) and \(|V|>1\), which excludes \(V=U^n\).
The boundary theorem assigns an empty neighborhood at each circle value, and the combined contribution over the remaining places is \(e(\deg q)h(\lambda)/D=e\,m(q)/D\).
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(z)\) has equally many extensions to the compositum with the branch field.
The surviving average therefore counts the embeddings of one irreducible factor, with denominator \(d_n\).

Rouché's theorem applied to \(X^{-Dn}F(X,X^n)\) gives exactly \(e\) roots with multiplicity in each fixed exterior disk.
They tend to its center, and their lifts eventually lie in the chosen neighborhoods since \(|X|^n\to\infty\) uniformly there.
The positive limiting average forces at least one visit by each factor, and one visit already contributes a fixed positive multiple of \(n/d_n\).
Thus \(n/d_n\) is bounded, permitting replacement of the boundedly many logarithms by their center values with error \(o(1)\).
Finiteness of the count vectors, conjugation symmetry and \(m(S_n)=d_n h(z)\) give both limits and the stated intersection.
The argument allows every sequence of factors.
For \(D=1\) the allocation agrees with the two-block formula.

When \(e=1\) each qualifying disk supplies a simple nontorsion root.
In the real case all factors visiting the disk contain that same root.
In the nonreal case complex conjugation forces every visiting rational factor to contain the unique root in each disk of the pair.
This proves existence, uniqueness and multiplicity one.
Absolute irreducibility and the two-dimensional polygon exclude a torsion binomial component, so the torsion pairs form a finite set by Beukers and Smyth.
At an occurring pair the first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term in log coordinates gives the coefficient \(H_r(1,n)\) of \(t^r\) along the section.
Homogeneity makes \(H_r(1,n)\) a nonzero polynomial, and \(X=\zeta e^t\) is a local coordinate change with nonzero derivative.
Apart from finitely many indices the multiplicity at that pair is therefore \(r\) on the residue class \(\zeta^n=\eta\).
For a given \(\zeta,n\) only one \(\eta\) can occur, so summing these contributions counts the cyclotomic degree with its actual multiplicities.

In the example the discriminant changes sign between \(0\) and \(\beta_3\), hence has an odd-order zero and is nonsquare in \(\mathbb C(U)\).
The middle coefficient \(U^2\) and \(A(0)=1\) give primitivity in \(\mathbb C[U][V]\), proving absolute irreducibility.
On \(|U|=1\) one has \(|A|\ge 12\), and the rotated polynomial \(w^2+w/|A|+1\) has two distinct unit-circle roots.
The fiber theorem for \(k=1,\ l=n\) now places every exterior root near an exterior zero of \(A\).
The only such zeros are the simple roots \(\beta_3,\beta_4\) of the two distinct irreducible quadratics, so the two Rouché disks exhaust the exterior roots.
Uniqueness in each disk fixed by conjugation makes its root real and eventually positive.
Reciprocity of the full polynomial and uniqueness of its noncyclotomic factor give the two reciprocal interior roots and the asserted measure limit.
The bounded degree loss from \(2n+4\) forces infinitely many distinct factors.

For an infinite set of Salem numbers the opposite exposed edges must both have positive full measure.
The two vertical edges have polynomial \(1+V^2\), leaving only the normals to the horizontal edges.
The support differences include a horizontal unit step and a vector of vertical component one, so the translation stabilizer is trivial.
The single-orbit theorem then requires the top roots on one of those rays to form one rational orbit, allowing inversion of the short coordinate.
Both \(A\) and \(A^*\) have two distinct nonzero rational root orbits, and inversion preserves the number of such orbits.
This excludes both rays for arbitrary torsion translations and common dilations.
