### Minimality and the two field-degree formulas

If \(S_z(X)=U(X^t)\) with \(t\) the gcd of its positive exponents, irreducibility of \(S_z\) gives irreducibility of \(U\), and division of all exponents by \(t\) leaves gcd one.
Every rational Laurent relation therefore vanishes at every \(\xi z\) with \(\xi^t=1\).
Character orthogonality extracts its vanishing residue-class subsums, including when the original exponents are negative.
A minimal relation occupies one residue class and its difference gcd is divisible by \(t\).
The minimal polynomial attains \(t\) because any proper vanishing subset or its complement would omit the leading term and contradict minimal degree.
Shifting and dividing the exponents of the chosen subset by \(g\) preserves all the evaluations up to a common nonzero factor on passing to \(\beta=\alpha^g\).
Consequently \(t(\beta)=1\).

For the finite cyclotomic argument, \(E=K\cap A_jC_j\) contains \(A_j\), so each \(E\)-embedding sends \(\beta\) to a \(j\)-th-root-of-unity multiple of itself.
The ratio of the norm to \(\beta^{[K:E]}\) belongs both to \(K\) and to \(C_j\), hence to \(E\).
The polynomial obtained by taking the norm from \(E\) of \(X^{[K:E]}-\beta^{[K:E]}\) is monic of the full minimal degree.
Its equality with \(S_\beta\) forces \([K:E]=1\), establishing (1).
The subgroup fixing \(K\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}(A_jC_j/A_j)\) has fixed field \(K\cap C_j\) on \(C_j\).
It is also the subgroup fixing \(A_j(K\cap C_j)\), by the Galois correspondence inside \(C_j/\mathbb Q\).
Thus the two fields agree, and restriction gives the stated degree ratio since \(K\cap C_j\) is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\).
It divides \(\varphi(j)\), including the trivial losses for \(j=1,2\).

Over \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), the equality of all power fields of \(\alpha^t\) places \(F(\alpha^t)\) inside every \(F(\alpha^j)\).
The automorphisms of \(F(\alpha)/F(\alpha^t)\) act faithfully by a cyclic subgroup of \(\mu_t\), since the base contains \(\mu_t\) and the field contains all the roots of \(X^t-\alpha^t\).
In its subgroup of order \(b\) exactly \(\gcd(b,j)\) elements have \(j\)-th power one, and they also form the stabilizer at exponent \(\gcd(b,j)\).
This yields both parts of (4).
The intersection is attained at \(j=t\) and \(F(\alpha^g)=F(\alpha^t)\) because \(t\mid g\).
For the finite computation, the commutator fixes the maximal abelian subfield of the rational splitting field \(N\), so its orbit on \(\alpha\) is the full \(F\)-orbit.
The fiber above \(\alpha^t\) in that orbit counts exactly the embeddings fixing \(F(\alpha^t)\), giving the specified \(b\).

### Height and all powers of the factor

I checked Amoroso–David's Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.3 on manuscript pp. 6–8, and the source unit's relative-rank argument for Proposition 2.4. [Source edition](../literature/AD22/readme.md#edition)
For primitive integral coefficients the projective coefficient height is \(\log\max|c_i|\).
Minimality ensures each ordered proper prefix survives, while a binomial with \(H=0\) has only torsion roots.
The equality of power fields holds in particular at the torsion exponent of the rational-conjugate group.
These are precisely the hypotheses for the bound on \(D\) in Proposition 3.3, whose gap argument also gives \(Wh(\beta)\le H\) without a threshold.
Powering the relative conjugate group by the same exponent gives a free group of the same rank \(r\) whose elements generate the relative normal closure as a field.
Its Galois group acts faithfully, giving \(D\le3^{r^2}\) and the claimed square-root lower bound on rank.
I used the published product-of-heights and abelian relative-height theorems as inputs at their established scopes.

For \(B=[F(\beta):F]<T\), \(h(\beta)\le 1/T\) and the abelian relative-height inequality first give \(B\gg T/(\log(2T))^4\).
Hence \(\log\log(5B)\gg\log\log T\) for large \(T\), which gives (2) on reapplying the same inequality.
When \(B\ge T\) the claimed bound is weaker than \(B\ge T\) for large \(T\).
Every power of \(\beta\) has absolute degree at least \(B\) and at most \(\deg\beta\le W\).
Taking an exponent divisible by every torsion-quotient order collapses precisely those classes, giving the same degree bound for their number.
Every \(F(\alpha^j)\) contains \(F(\beta^j)=F(\beta)\), yielding the asserted containment of their normal closures.

A primitive subset taken from a relation of primitive length \(L\) has coefficient length at most \(L\), so \(H\ll_L1\).
The bounded-support lower bound gives \(Wh(\beta)\gg_L1\) for units.
For nonunits \(m(\beta)\ge\log2\) and \(\deg\beta\le W\) suffice.
The bounded radical-denominator theorem applies to each chosen lift \(\alpha\) of this seed and compares their measures by constants depending only on \(L\).
This gives (3) with the same uniformity for every choice of minimal subset, so convergence for one choice gives convergence for all, and degree at least one forces \(W\to\infty\).

### The term thresholds and examples

A split into two nonempty vanishing supports among five terms uses a monomial or a binomial on one side.
Neither can vanish at a nontorsion unit, since a binomial would force a positive integral power of it to be a rational unit, hence \(1\) or \(-1\).
For seven terms the smaller side has at most three, to which the sharp trinomial bound applies after clearing denominators and shifting exponents.
In the six-term example, \(1<u\), \(1<\alpha<u\), and \(u^2=3u-1\) verify all five nonzero prefixes, while each alternating triple is a shifted copy of the vanishing quadratic evaluated at \(u\).
Both triples have normalized width two.
Since \(u\in F\) and \(h(\alpha)=h(u)/p\), the positive height floor on \(F\) excludes \(\alpha\in F\) for large primes \(p\).
Splitting \(X^p-u\) over \(F\) therefore gives a nontrivial subgroup of \(\mu_p\), necessarily of order \(p\), contradicting the superpolynomial bound one would get from the full width \(2p+1\).
Finally \(2^{p-1}\Phi_p(X/2)\) is irreducible with full support, gives \(t(2\zeta_p)=1\), and has \(p\)-th power field \(\mathbb Q\) at that root.
The full field is \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\), realizing the degree loss \(p-1\) for every prime.
