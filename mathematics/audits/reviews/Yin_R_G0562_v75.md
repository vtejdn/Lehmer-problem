## Finding on arbitrary field elements and integral units

At 2026-11-16T23:08 UTC an independent reviewer for Yin checked [C345v75](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v75.md#statement), submitted at 23:06 UTC, including its two argument sections and the cited comparison.
The reviewer did not participate in developing the proof and received the fixed text and scope without proof-development dialogue, an author explanation or an earlier verdict.
The explicit relative-norm criterion, the signature-dependent rank bound and the cyclic conclusion for complex Salem fields pass.

For \(u\ne1\) in \(B^\times\), all factors in the product formula for \(1-u\) are nonzero.
At a positive real image \(x\), \(|1-x|/\max(1,x)=1-e^{-|\log x|}\le|\log x|\).
Removing the \(\max(1,|u|_v)\) factors over all places costs precisely \(e^{mh(u)}\), including finite-place denominators with the usual local-degree weights.
The other \(m-g\) archimedean embeddings cost at most \(2^{m-g}\).
The sum of \(|\log x|\) over the \(g\) positive images is at most \(m(h(u)+h(u^{-1}))=2mh(u)\), so the product of their residual factors is at most \((2mh(u)/g)^g\).
If \(h(u)\le(\delta/2)4^{-1/\delta}\) and \(g/m\ge\delta\), this is at most \((4^{-1/\delta})^{g}\le4^{-m}\).
Also \(h(u)\le1/8\) throughout \(0<\delta\le1\), so the product-formula upper bound is at most \((e^{1/8}/2)^m<1\), a contradiction.
This calculation permits arbitrary valuations of \(u\) and includes both embeddings at a complex place.

The \(m\) distinct inverse pairs give distinct trace values, and their quadratic preimages are distinct, hence \(K/B\) is quadratic with involution \(\iota(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}\).
Above each neutral trace embedding its two extensions are conjugate on the generator \(\lambda\), hence on all of \(K\).
Thus \(\gamma\iota(\gamma)\) has image \(|\sigma(\gamma)|^2>0\) at each of the \(g\) embeddings.
Its height is at most \(2h(\gamma)\) by Galois invariance and subadditivity, which verifies the threshold for arbitrary nonzero \(\gamma\).
When \(\gamma\) is integral the norm identity supplies the integral inverse \(\iota(\gamma)\).

For a relative norm-one integral unit the absolute-value logarithms vanish over neutral traces.
Over each real split trace place they form one opposite pair.
Over a complex trace place they form one opposite pair above either chosen embedding, with the same absolute values above its conjugate.
These are exactly \(a+s\) free real coordinates for purposes of the upper bound.
Within a bounded box of such coordinates all archimedean conjugates are bounded, and bounded degree and integrality leave only finitely many possible elements.
Consequently the image is discrete and the finite kernel consists of roots of unity.
Every root of unity in \(K\) lies in this kernel, since at a neutral trace embedding conjugation and inversion agree on its value, and the embedding is injective.
A maximal real-independent subset of the image generates a lattice of rank at most \(a+s\), and reduction modulo that lattice leaves finitely many image representatives in a bounded parallelotope.
This bounds the rank of the image and the number of multiplicatively independent small algebraic integers, since any relation modulo the finite kernel can be raised to a power to remove its torsion factor.

In the complex Salem case there are two exterior inverse labels, one conjugate pair of nonreal trace embeddings, and \(g=m-2\ge m/3\).
The admissible uniform threshold at \(\delta=1/3\) is \(h(\gamma)\le1/768\).
The relative unit \(\lambda\) has nonzero image at the exterior place, so the discrete subgroup in this single coordinate is infinite cyclic.
Lifting a generator gives the entire relative unit group modulo the roots of unity in this particular field.
The [earlier one-block argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#fields) gives the same logarithmic description in its restricted setting, where the only torsion units are \(\pm1\).
Here no restriction on the block action or on the size of the finite torsion kernel is needed.
