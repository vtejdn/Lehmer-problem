## Affine fibers, collision estimates and individual directions in a relative field

At 2026-11-25T04:29 UTC an agent outside development of [C0901 v153](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v153.md#statement) independently reviewed for Yin its statement and all three proof sections.
The reviewer had audited predecessor v148 and received v153 without its development discussion.
The bounds for arbitrary translated anti-invariant projections, separate coefficient fields and the remaining relative angle fibers yield the claimed uniform line-mass estimate.

In one coordinate, a zero target requires the destination signed coordinate to be real, with at most \(a=b^{1/2+o(1)}\) possible labels in its destination block.
For a nonzero target the difference of the two signed formal coordinates is supported on one label or on a \(c\)-exchanged pair.
Even with oriented mean subtraction, two candidate sparse lifts of the same vector differ by block constants but their difference is supported on at most four labels per block.
For \(b>4\) those constants must vanish, so the target still specifies at most two labels.
Alternating transport and the available sign corrections keep the destination labels uniform upon conditioning on a first signed position, leaving at least \(b-1\) choices.
For two exceptions the remaining affine target is obtained by subtracting the known first contribution and dividing by the other nonzero coefficient.
Thus the one-position probability bound applies independently of that conditioning and of the target.

For a uniform \(g\) on a left coset \(g_0H\) and an independent uniform \(h\in H\), the pair \(g,gh\) consists of two independent uniform coset draws.
The sum of squares of the target probabilities is their collision probability and bounds the square of every individual atom.
After fixing \(h\), a collision is equivalent to \(cg(v-hv)=g(v-hv)\); \(g\) remains uniform on its coset.
Cosets may be refined to the available supported actions because a bound uniform on every smaller coset bounds their union by averaging.

For sparse \(v\), the number \(e\) of marks is \(b^{o(1)}\), and their images under \(h\) within each transport are random distinct positions in their destination blocks.
Choosing any \(j\) to meet the original marks and exposing positions without replacement bounds the probability of at least \(j\) overlaps by \(\binom ej(e/(b-e))^j\).
For exactly \(j\) overlaps, at least \(2(e-j)\) positions in the difference are marked just once and cannot cancel; there are at most \(2e\) marks.
In the oriented case the background difference is constant in each block outside this sparse union, hence is its unique majority there.
The involution exposure of v144 for a difference with \(k\) exceptions uses at most \(k^k\) patterns. Each fixed draw needs a real label, and each transposition forces one partner.
It gives \(b^{-(1/2-o(1))k}\) uniformly for \(k\le2e\), including the trivial bound for \(k=0\).
Multiplying the overlap bound with the conditional probability at \(k\ge2(e-j)\) yields \(b^{-e+o(e)}\) after the \(e+1\) terms are summed.
For \(e\ge3\) the collision probability is therefore \(O(b^{-3+o(1)})\).

For a dense block of the source with at least \(L=\lceil(\log b)^2\rceil\) exceptions, independent even flips give at least \(2^{L-1}\) images on its destination in the signed case.
In the oriented case a union of colors of size in \([L/2,b/2]\) gives at least \(\binom b{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\) arrangements.
These bounds exceed every fixed power of \(b\) and are uniform over initial block transports.
To leave at most five exceptions in the difference, specify their positions and transported coefficients, costing \(O(b^{10})\) choices.
In the oriented case the background difference can be fixed by one ordinary position and one transported coefficient, at an additional \(O(b^2)\) cost.
The other entries are then determined, so this event has probability \(O(b^{-4})\).
For a difference with at least six exceptions the sparse involution bound is \(b^{-3+o(1)}\) if all its blocks are sparse; each individual dense-block estimate of v144 also gives \(O(b^{-3+o(1)})\) if one is dense.
This proves the asserted maximum atom \(b^{-3/2+o(1)}\) by taking the square root, with no assumption that zero is a most likely target.

For three or more exceptions the orbit dimension is at most \(ub=b^{1+o(1)}\).
The maximum absolute log functional is at most this dimension times its absolute first moment, giving the \(b^{-1/2+o(1)}\) weighted fraction on the whole group.
For two exceptions, condition on each individual signed position to bound its own weighted contribution using the uniform affine estimate for the other one.
The first-moment comparison of v144 then controls the sum of those two contributions in terms of the absolute first moment of their actual combination, without a loss for cancelling or unbalanced coefficients.

For any line containing orbit values of the actual Mahler value \(A\), their ratios are real, so their classes all have the same \((1-c)\)-image.
This necessary condition holds on every active projection and permits arbitrary line directions and torsion.
On cosets fixing the other active fields, \(|L'|\) is constant and the largest-term affine probability bound applies.
For a noncoordinate leading projection the whole-group weighted estimate deals with \(|L_1|\).
If a second active label field is not contained in the largest splitting field, fixing that field makes \(L_1\) constant and still supplies the local alternating actions on the second term through normality.
The conditional absolute first-moment bounds of v148 consequently apply in each case.
The block inversion at each involved term gives exponent \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) for its block size; taking half that constant with strict slack absorbs the \(o(1)\) losses and the fixed factor two relating the mean absolute log to \(\mu/D\).

In the nested coordinate case, the bound on the conditional \(|L'|\) mass and \(\mathbb E|L_1|\le8m/d\) give \(\delta\le b^{-1/2+o(1)}+4mq/\mu\).
If \(\delta\ge d^{-1/5}\), \(b=d^{1-o(1)}\) makes the first term negligible compared with \(\delta\).
The two estimates for \(\mu\) then give \(q\ge d^{4/5-o(1)}\) and \(\mu/D\ll m/(\delta d)\); therefore \(h(C)=O(d^{-4/5})\).
The integral remainder and full relative-degree statements invoked from v148 hold before any assumption on a particular line.
At each fixed image of \(r\) the \(q\) distinct relative values of \(C\) yield the phases of \(A\) by one rotation.
For \(1\le k\le q\) the reflected energy estimate with \(\rho=e^{-1/q}\) incurs \(\rho^{-k}\le e\), and conversion to phases costs at most \(2kt_0h(C)\) in any fiber, using the sum over all embeddings of the unit.
With \(t_0=d^{o(1)}\), the two small terms \(t_0h(C)\) and \(t_0\log(3q)/q\) are both \(d^{-4/5+o(1)}\) or smaller.
For frequencies up to \(H=\lfloor d^{1/4}\rfloor\), the square-root error is \(O(d^{-11/40+o(1)})\) and the linear error is \(O(d^{-11/20+o(1)})\).
An order-\(H\) nonnegative Fejér kernel centered at each prescribed phase has value \(H\) there; its Fourier sum divided by \(H\) costs at most a constant times the largest nonconstant normalized coefficient.
The two point masses thus total at most \(d^{-1/4+o(1)}\) uniformly in the relative fiber and its rotated direction.
Weighting by the fiberwise constant \(|L_1|\) and using (2) of v148 yields \(\delta\le b^{-1/2+o(1)}+2d^{-1/4+o(1)}\), contradicting the assumed threshold.

With only one active term the orbit of the projected signed coordinate has full size \(d=D\); hence \(q=1\) and \(4m/\mu\) itself is \(d^{-1+o(1)}\) or smaller by the unmatched estimate.
If another nested field is present, \(b_j\le u\ll(\log(3d))^{212}\) while its own block inversion gives \(\log b_j\gg\mathcal H(m)\).
Thus \(\log d/\mathcal H(m)\to\infty\), making the established \(d^{-1/5}\) bound sufficient for every required fixed exponent in this case.
Each line on the fast branch therefore carries at most the stated fraction of \(\mu\), and summing over lines proves the necessary count for half the exterior or interior mass.
In the low interval the value is reciprocal and \(\mu\le2m\), giving the respective lower contributions \(m\) from \(A\) and \(A^{-1}\).
These are estimates for exact directions modulo \(\pi\).
