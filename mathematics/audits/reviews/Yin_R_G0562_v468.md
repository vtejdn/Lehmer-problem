The reviewer had no part in developing L216 and received the fixed material without proof-development dialogue, author explanations or prior conclusions.

The equality of orders also holds at every positive primitive slope, as stated.

### Local branches and power projections

The coefficient of \(u\) in \(B(-\beta e^u)\) is \(-\beta B'(-\beta)\ne0\).
On \(|u|=K\beta^{-r}\) the modulus of the other summand is at most \(C\beta^{-r}\exp(rK\beta^{-r})\) with a constant independent of the lift.
Choose \(K\) first and then \(r\) large to get one simple zero by Rouché, counted with multiplicity.
The \(a\) angular neighborhoods are disjoint, the positive term \((\log\beta)/a\) dominates the radial error \(O(\beta^{-r}/a)\), and the exact circle count exhausts the exterior roots.
Raising to \(a/q\) yields (2) with the same constant for every divisor \(q\).

For an independent check of the unit resultant, at a zero \(t\) of \(t^4+t^3-1\) one has \(g(t)=-t(1+t^2)\).
The four \(t\)'s have product \(-1\), and the product of \(1+t^2\) is \(1\) by evaluation of the monic quartic at \(i,-i\).
Thus the absolute section resultant is \(1^a=1\), with unit leading coefficients.
For every section root, \(B(t)\), \(t\) and \(\alpha^b\) are units, and an integral unit has inverse given by an integral polynomial in itself.
It follows in order that \(B(t)^{-1},\alpha^b,t^{-1},\alpha^{-b}\in\mathbb Z[t]\).
Bézout now recovers \(\alpha\), proving the equality of orders and of their signed discriminants by the two integral power bases.
In particular the embeddings remain distinct on \(\alpha^{a/q}\).

The section has unit endpoints and the two exponent differences \((1,0),(0,1)\) in the two-variable support, satisfying the hypotheses of the fixed height estimate.
Its limiting integral is \(\log\beta>0\) by reversal on the circle and the single exterior pole.
The same estimate gives \(d\gg a\sqrt r\), hence \(\epsilon\to0\) uniformly.
Comparing \(b m(P)/d=\log\beta+O(\epsilon)\) with \(m(P)=(k/a)(\log\beta+O(\beta^{-r}))\) proves \(d=kr(1+O(\epsilon))\).
Here \(k\ge1\) by Kronecker for a nontorsion monic unit.
Preservation of degree yields \(m(P_{a/q})=(a/q)m(P)\) exactly.
Eventual reciprocity of \(P\) passes to its power projection, which has the same distinct embeddings.

### Counting the discriminant loss

For the folded roots \(w_i\), the common product \(A=\prod_{i<j}|1-w_i\bar w_j|^2\) is positive since distinct roots on the circle cannot coincide.
With the other variables fixed, its factors involving \(w_i\) are \(\left|\prod_{j\ne i}(1-w_i\bar w_j)\right|^2\).
Successive boundary maxima and the circle Vandermonde bound give \(A\le d^d\), including when some folded interior points coincide.
The normalized distance of a pair on opposite sides or with a boundary root is exactly \(|1-w_i\bar w_j|\).
For a pair strictly on the same side the correction is \(\delta_{ij}\le1\), by the disk identity \(|1-w_i\bar w_j|^2-|w_i-w_j|^2=(1-|w_i|^2)(1-|w_j|^2)\).
In the discriminant product of squared distances each root's radial normalization has exponent \(2(d-1)\), so
\[
 \log|\operatorname{disc}P|
 = 2(d-1)m(P_{a/q})+\log A
       +\sum_{\substack{i\ne j\\\text{strictly on the same side}}}\log\delta_{ij}.
\]

Two exterior roots in one class have distance \(O(\beta^{-r}/q)\) by (2) and denominator \(|z_i\bar z_j-1|\ge |z_i||z_j|-1\gg1/q\).
The quotient of these quantities equals their folded \(\delta_{ij}\).
For each of the \(k_c(k_c-1)\) ordered exterior pairs in class \(c\), conjugate inversion gives one distinct ordered interior pair with exactly the same \(\delta_{ij}\).
The two sets are disjoint even when the original exterior pair consists of conjugates.
These pairs therefore contribute at least \(2(r\log\beta-C)\sum_c k_c(k_c-1)\) to the loss in (4), and all remaining losses are nonnegative.

The discriminant part on the right of (5) is at most \(d\log^+(d/|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d})\), which after division by \(2(r\log\beta-C)\) costs \(O(kD_P)\).
For the measure part the resulting upper bound is
\[
 \frac{d\,m(P_{a/q})}{r\log\beta-C}
   =\frac{k^2}{q}\bigl(1+O(\epsilon)\bigr),
\]
since both \(1/r\) and \(\beta^{-r}\) are \(O(\epsilon)\).
The leading coefficient here is one, which permits subtraction of the full \(k^2/q\) in the variance identity.
The remaining \(k\) is absorbed by \(kD_P\), and the integral nonzero discriminant gives \(1\le D_P\le1+\log d\).

### Sparse allocations

Multiplication of (1) by \(q/k^2\) gives an upper bound \(O(qD_P/k+\epsilon)\) for the normalized mean square.
Every empty class contributes \(1/q\) to that mean square.
For fixed \(q\), convergence of the mean square forces convergence in each class.
With at least \(\eta q\) empty classes the \(O(\epsilon)\) term is eventually at most \(\eta/2\), giving \(D_P\gg_\eta k/q\).
If \(k/q\to\infty\), the positive part in \(D_P-1\) is eventually a positive logarithm and exponentiation gives the claimed root discriminant restriction.
For \(q=2\) the stated parity bound follows immediately.
When \(a\) is prime the only divisors are \(1,a\), for which the unnormalized variances are \(0\) and \(k-k^2/a\), respectively.
The estimates thus impose the stated restrictions on some shrinking allocations while leaving the existence of a shrinking factor unresolved.
