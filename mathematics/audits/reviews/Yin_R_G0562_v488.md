### The coefficient space over the seed field

Write \(\beta=\gamma^p\), so \(\beta^n=z=\alpha^p\).
The representative theorem gives \([\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]=e\) and \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]=d\) at every window prime, and gives \(p<n\) for large \(d\).
For the inverse \(r\) of \(p\bmod n\) in \([1,n-1]\), \(pr=1+vn\) gives \(1\le v<p\) and \(\beta^r=\gamma\alpha^v\).
The Euler derivative of \(a(X^n)+X^rb(X^n)\) is \(n(\mathcal Ea)(X^n)+X^r(r b(X^n)+n(\mathcal Eb)(X^n))\).
Substituting the four equations (2) therefore gives both full centered equations, with signs \(1-\sigma\gamma\) and \(1+\sigma\gamma\) respectively.
Under the inverse involution the first equation cancels the two values in the diagonal at \(\gamma\), and the centered derivative equation cancels the two first derivatives there.
The resulting rational polynomial is divisible by \(P^2\) by separability.
Also \(q_0=C_n(q)\) and \(C_j(C_n(Y))=C_{jn}(Y)\) for \(j\ge1\).
Thus the two indicated reduced polynomials lie below degree \(d/2\) and have exactly the seed Chebyshev coefficients in their occupied indices.

There are \(4e+1\) variables for \(a\), \(4e\) for \(b\), and \(e\) altogether for \(U,V\).
All four differences evaluated on an integer coefficient cube belong to \(\mathcal O_K\).
At an embedding with \(s'=|\log|\alpha'||\), the centered exponents of \(z\) have absolute value at most \(2e\), and the factor \(\alpha^v\) increases the absolute exponent of \(\alpha\) by at most \(p\).
The trace powers have smaller exponents.
The Euler multipliers and constant shifts are \(O(J)\), giving the submitted individual coordinate bound.
Real embeddings and the two real coordinates for each conjugate complex pair contribute \(e\) coordinates per algebraic integer.
A shared box of side \(1/3\) bounds each conjugate difference by at most \(\sqrt2/3<1\), so the norm forces equality of the two integers.
Using \(\sum_{\alpha'}|\log|\alpha'||=2h\), the number of quadruples is at most \((T+1)^{4e}\exp(O(eL))\).
Here both \(\log(e+1)\) and \(\log(J+1)\) are \(O(\log(2dp))\).
With \(l=\lfloor e/4\rfloor\) the ratio \(e/l\) is absolutely bounded for all eligible even \(e\ge4\).
The indicated choice of \(T\) therefore leaves a fiber larger than \((T+1)^{5e+1-l}\), with \(\log(T+1)=O(L)\).
Applying an injective coordinate projection to the original fiber in its affine span shows that its differences have dimension \(>5e+1-l\), and those differences have coefficient sizes at most \(T\).

When \(u=w=0\), the reduced polynomials \(U,V\) vanish identically by their degree bounds, and both value and derivative of \(a,b\) vanish at the nonzero \(z\).
Thus \(Q_0^2\) divides each of \(a,b\).
The remaining coefficient dimensions are at most \(2e+1\) and \(2e\), so the trace image has rational dimension \(>e-l>e/2\).
A single \(K_0\)-line has rational dimension \(e/2\), so two trace vectors have nonzero determinant over \(K_0\).
The proportional-coordinate locus cannot contain this image either, by the rational trace-pair argument of L462 v206.
On the product of two copies of the rational coefficient space, the required nonzero value components, rational coordinate minors and a determinant component are nonzero polynomials of bounded total degree.
Their product can be avoided on a bounded integer grid.
There are \(O(e)\) basis vectors, so the resulting two rows still have logarithmic coefficient size \(O(L)\).
The determinant identity follows by eliminating the common \(J A_i(\beta)\) term in the centered derivative equations, giving the factor \(1-\gamma^2\ne0\).

### Removing common zeros and counting the four classes

For each row, \(E(\beta)=(A-A^*)(\beta)\ne0\) follows from its nonzero centered first trace.
The powered minimum \(Q_0(X^n)\) is therefore coprime to \(E\).
The added polynomial in (3) has double zeros at all conjugates of \(z\), so it preserves all four seed equations.
After substitution it is invariant under reversal about degree \(4d\), hence preserves \(E\).
At each nonzero zero \(\xi\) of \(E\), the coefficient of \(\nu\) in \(A(\xi)\) is \(Q_0(\xi^n)^2\xi^{ne}\ne0\).
Also \(b(z)\ne0\) implies that \(b\) has no zero among the conjugates of \(z\).
Inversion permutes those conjugates since \(Q_0\) is reciprocal, so \(Q_0\) is disjoint from the nonzero root set of \(b^\flat\).
Consequently the coefficient of \(\nu\) in \(a\) evaluated at each such root is likewise nonzero.
The coefficients of \(1,Y^{4e}\) in \(a\) each have slope one in \(\lambda\).
The product of all the avoidance forms has degree at most \(4d+4e+2\), and a grid of that extent in both parameters leaves a choice with both gcds equal to one and the two endpoints nonzero.
The nonzero constant term of the new \(a\) also excludes zero as a common root with \(b^\flat\) when \(\deg b<4e-1\).

The perturbed row is still supported in classes \(0,r\bmod n\).
Its norm is controlled by the grid size, the original coefficient bound, and \(\|Q_0\|_1^2\), giving logarithmic size \(O(L+e)=O(ph)\) throughout the window.
The content of \(A\) is the gcd of all coefficients of \(a,b\), because the two classes are disjoint.
Primitive scaling therefore divides both seed rows by the same integer.
It scales the full trace representatives, the values, the remainders and the joint determinant in the prescribed way.
The integral odd trace chart and uniqueness of the degree-\(<d/2\) representatives keep the scaled coefficients integral, and their indices divisible by \(n\) give integral seed representatives.
For each diagonal its two halves are disjoint modulo \(p\).
The two classes in the first half give \((0,0),(0,1)\bmod(p,n)\) and those in the reversed half give \((1,1),(1,0)\).
All are occupied since \(a\ne0\), \(b\ne0\), and \((p,n)=1\).
With only the zero class of \(A\bmod n\), the two halves of \(F\) would lie separately in classes zero and one modulo \(n\).
Division by \(P_0(x^n)^2\) then forces \(P_0(t)^2\mid a(t^p)\), ruling out a nonzero evaluation at \(\beta\).

The reduced coefficient logs are \(O(L)\) and the squarefree first lift has measure bounded by the logarithm of at most \(d\) times the largest reduced coefficient.
L462 v199 bounds the normalized remainder norm using those same coefficients.
For a nonzero evaluation of a polynomial \(C\) of degree \(k\) at \(\beta\), the absolute log norm divided by \(d\) is at most \(\log\|C\|_1+(k/d)ph\).
Taking \(k\le4d\) for each value and \(k\le8d\) for the joint determinant, with \(\|\mathcal EA\|_1\le4d\|A\|_1\), gives all the stated \(O(ph)\) bounds on the same pair.

### The seed diagonal and both actual cofactor measures

Writing \(t=x^n\), the two terms with exponent zero modulo \(n\) yield exactly \(G(t)=a(t^p)+\sigma t^{p-v}b^\flat(t^p)\).
Reversing in the complementary residue gives \(F(x)=G(t)+\sigma x t^{4ep}G(1/t)\).
The first seed row has nonzero endpoints and degree \(4ep\), and the other term has powers at least \(p-v>0\) and at most \(4ep-v<4ep\).
Their two distinct residue classes have coprime rows by the construction, and the coefficient lists are exactly those of \(a,b\), up to reversal and sign.
It follows that \(G\) is primitive and \(\|G\|_1=\|A\|_1\).
The polynomial ring in \(x\) is free over the polynomial ring in \(t=x^n\) with one basis monomial per residue.
Thus \(P^2\mid F\) forces \(P_0^2\mid G\) already in the seed polynomial ring.
The coefficients of \(F\) too have content one by their disjoint classes modulo \(p\).

At \(|t|=1\), the product over \(x^n=t\) of the two-term expression is \(G(t)^n-(-\sigma)^n t(t^{4ep}G(1/t))^n\).
The two terms have equal modulus, yielding the bound \(2|G(t)|^n\).
Haar integration over these fibers gives \(m(F)\le m(G)+\log(2)/n\).
In both polynomial rings removing the double input factor subtracts exactly \(2h\), and stripping the cyclotomic factors costs zero.
The degree-\(4dp+1\) primitive \(F\) satisfies the two-class determinant bound \(ph\le2m(F)+O(\log(2+4dp+1))\).
As \(\log(2+4dp+1)=o(ph)\) uniformly, the lower bound \((1/2-o(1))ph\) holds for its actual cofactor and then, by the transfer, for the actual seed cofactor as well.
Both have the asserted \(O(ph)\) upper bound from their coefficient sums.
This comparison uses the same window prime for both inputs, with no demand that it lie in the small seed's own fourth-logarithmic window.

Along any hypothetical shrinking sequence of reciprocal seeds, the degrees \(e\) tend to infinity by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness.
There are thus arbitrarily large representatives with \(e\ge4\), and the present construction produces four-class witnesses of either prescribed sign at **each** window prime, with both normalized cofactor measures tending to zero.
In the converse direction one prime and a pair with the required vanishing suffice by the positive cofactor lower bound at large representative degree.
Under an unrestricted positive gap the lower bound continues to apply to every such eligible positive-sign pair.
These facts preserve the quantifiers of the representative equivalence when witnesses are restricted to the stated shape.

### The derivative remainder and its exact relative norm

Differentiating \(C_n(\gamma+\gamma^{-1})=\gamma^n+\gamma^{-n}\) gives \(C_n'(q)=n(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})/(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})\).
For each sign, \((2\sigma-q)/(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})=-(\gamma-\sigma)/(\gamma+\sigma)\), and the denominators are nonzero since \(\gamma\) is nontorsion.
This gives (4).
As \(\gamma\notin K\), vanishing of \((C+B)\gamma+\sigma(C-B)\) forces \(C=B=0\).
Then \(w/u=-1\), contradicting the selected trace ratio.
This verifies nonvanishing of each individual remainder independently of the joint determinant.

The inverse involutions have fixed fields \(\mathbb Q(q)\) and \(K_0\) in \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) and \(K\), respectively.
The element \(\alpha\) is moved by inversion, so \(K\mathbb Q(q)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
Both \([\mathbb Q(q):K_0]\) and \([\mathbb Q(\gamma):K]\) equal \(n\).
The \(n\) embeddings over \(K\) restrict distinctly to \(\mathbb Q(q)\) because the latter generates the compositum with \(K\), and thus give every embedding over \(K_0\).
Their product computes the desired relative norm of \(D\).
For the \(n\) roots \(\eta^n=\alpha\) and any scalars \(a,b\in K\), oddness of \(n\) gives \(\prod_\eta(a\eta+b)=a^n\alpha+b^n\).
Applying this identity to the numerator and the denominator of (4) yields exactly \(\{\alpha(C+B)^n+\sigma(C-B)^n\}/(\alpha+\sigma)\), including when \(C+B=0\).
