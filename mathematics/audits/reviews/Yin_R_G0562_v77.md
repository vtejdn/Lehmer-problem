## Height products, an abelian relative degree and interpolation

At 2026-11-16T23:11 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the whole C345v77 submission dated 2026-11-16T23:10.
The reviewer received the fixed submission and its stated inputs without development dialogue or a previous verdict on this version and took no part in its development.

For a reduced presentation \(R=A/B\) of degree \(k\), the defined nonzero value \(y\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
The polynomial \(A(T)-yB(T)\) vanishes at \(x\) and is nonzero since \(R\) is nonconstant.
Consequently its degree bounds \(e=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q(y)]\) by \(k\), including when leading terms cancel.
The joint field is \(\mathbb Q(x)\), while the individual degree of \(y\) is \(d/e\).
The absolute height formula for primitive minimal polynomials gives \(h(x)=L_x/d\) and \(h(y)=eL_y/d\) with all finite-place contributions included.
Thus the rank-two product theorem of Amoroso and David has joint degree \(d\) and product exponent \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\).
Multiplying by \(d^2\) gives the first alternative, and rearranging gives \(d/e\le c_1^{-1}L_xL_y(\log(3d))^{212}\).

If there is a multiplicative dependence, the group generated by \(x,y\) has free rank exactly one since \(x\) is nontorsion.
A lift of a free generator belongs to this very group and is itself nontorsion.
Orient it so \(x=\zeta\rho^u\) with \(u>0\), and write \(y=\eta\rho^v\).
Both torsion factors lie in \(\mathbb Q(x)\), and their joint field \(F\) is cyclotomic and abelian.
Both \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(x)\) and \(\rho\in\mathbb Q(x)\), while \(x=\zeta\rho^u\in F(\rho)\), so \(F(\rho)=\mathbb Q(x)\).
The case \(v=0\) allows every torsion output covered by the hypotheses.

Over the algebraic closure the map \(T\mapsto\zeta T^u\) is surjective on the multiplicative group.
Any zero or pole of \(R\) there would therefore give a zero or pole of the substituted function there.
If this function were identically \(\eta T^v\), the divisor of \(R\) would be supported at zero and infinity, giving \(R=cX^j\) with \(c\) rational.
Matching the exponents gives \(v=uj\), and matching coefficients gives \(c=\eta\zeta^{-j}\in\{1,-1\}\), precisely the excluded functions.
For \(v\ge0\) subtract \(\eta T^v B(\zeta T^u)\) from \(A(\zeta T^u)\).
For \(v<0\) subtract \(\eta B(\zeta T^u)\) from \(T^{-v}A(\zeta T^u)\).
In both cases the polynomial is nonzero, lies in \(F[T]\), vanishes at \(\rho\) and has degree at most \(ku+|v|\).
Thus \(D=[F(\rho):F]\le ku+|v|\).

The minimal polynomial over \(F\) bounds the degree of \(\rho\) over the larger base \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) by \(D\), and \(D\le d\).
Delsinne's rank-one theorem applies because \(\rho\) is nontorsion, and its denominator exponent is \(3\cdot 2\cdot 2^2\cdot 2!=48\).
Writing \(D_{\rm ab}\) for that relative degree, \(D_{\rm ab} h(\rho)\gg(\log(3D_{\rm ab}))^{-48}\), hence \(D h(\rho)\gg(\log(3d))^{-48}\).
Torsion factors have no effect on height, so \((ku+|v|)h(\rho)=k h(x)+h(y)\), also for negative \(v\) or \(v=0\).
After multiplication by \(d\) this proves the second alternative without an integrality assumption.

At fixed \(H>0\) the independent and dependent left sides are at most \(kH^2\) and \(2kH\), respectively.
Since \(\log(3d)>1\), both yield the asserted common bound of exponent 212 with a positive constant depending only on \(H\).
A subsequence with \(L_y\) bounded would therefore contradict the specified little-oh condition whenever \(L_x\) is bounded and \(d\to\infty\).
The earlier C852 statement is a comparison for a fixed rational function.
The present argument makes the constants independent of the varying coefficients and uses the output's own total logarithmic measure.
For \(d\ge2\), \(P_x(0)\ne0\) since \(x\ne0\).
Thus \(X+P_x(X)\) has nonzero constant and degree exactly \(d\), cannot be any signed power, and takes the value \(x\) at \(x\).
The same direct check works with \(2P_x\).
This verifies the degree-\(d\) example for nonmonic primitive polynomials as well.
The logarithmic losses in the lower bounds still leave room below the degree of that example.
