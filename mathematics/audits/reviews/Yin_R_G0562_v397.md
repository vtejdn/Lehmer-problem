## Factor selection, interpolation and resultant separation

At 2026-11-18T13:50 UTC an agent outside proof development independently checked the whole [reciprocal-family argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v171.md#family) from the fixed mathematics and its cited inputs.
The reviewer received no development dialogue.

The strict measure bound excludes nonreciprocal factors.
For a fixed Fourier character of weight \((a,b,c)\), the condition \(a+n(b+uc)=0\) eventually forces \(a=b=c=0\).
Uniform approximation therefore gives convergence of the integrals for every continuous logarithmic truncation, and decreasing the truncation gives the required upper bound for the limsup of the untruncated measures.
With \(z=e^{it},X=e^{is}\) one choice of square root gives \(q=\cos((t+s)/2)/\cos(t/2)\).
The reciprocal quadratic in \(W\) contributes zero beyond its leading coefficient when \(|q|\le2\) and otherwise adds \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|q|/2)\).
Changing variables about \(t=\pi\) gives precisely the factor \(2/\pi\) and upper endpoint \(\pi/6\).
Integration of \(\log(\pi/(3t))-\sin^2 t\) gives the displayed upper bound, less than \(0.54<2\log(1.32)\).
A nonreciprocal factor of this monic unit polynomial would require a distinct reciprocal partner with the same measure.
I checked the application of [Smyth's primary-source bound](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) to this pair.
The block separation has \(g=un-1\), \(b=1\), and \(4(un-3)-D=(2u-1)n-13>0\) under the stated hypotheses.
The degree and individual measure conclusions of the [separated-block theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement) thus apply with fixed constants \(L=4\), \(\delta=1/4\).

The difference \(V-U=Z^u(Z-1)\) has absolute resultant three with \(U\), so the rational expression for each root in its \(p\)-th power is defined for every \(p\mid n\).
At a nonidentity \(a\in\mu_n\) in characteristic three the derivative is \(na^{n-1}(1-a)\), a unit.
Each simple lift is nontorsion since the order of a torsion lift would have a factor \(p\mid n\) and its \(p\)-th power would have strictly smaller cyclotomic degree.
There are at most \(K\) nontorsion factors by their uniform degree bound, and selecting a factor with at least \((n-1)/K\) such lifts occurs before the choice of \(p\).
For that prime the largest powered residue class contains at least \(p(n-1)/(nK)\) selected marks.

In the unramified valuation ring put \(\beta_i=c+3cZ_i/\nu\).
Since \(c^\nu=1\) and \(\nu\) is a unit there, \((\beta_i^\nu-1)/3\equiv Z_i\bmod 3\).
The derivatives of \(U,V\) at one have residues \(1,-1\).
Dividing the original equation by three now yields \(\bar\alpha_i=(\bar Z_i+1)/(\bar Z_i-1)\).
At \(\bar Z_i=1\) its defining equation would give \(2=0\), and \(\bar Z_i=-1\) would give \(\bar\alpha_i=0\).
Distinct original residues thus give distinct interpolation nodes away from both poles of the two possible fractional linear functions.
For \(r\ge2\), a polynomial of degree at most \(r-2\) agreeing with either function, times any fixed nonzero scalar, would give a polynomial identity on multiplying by the linear denominator and counting the \(r\) distinct zeros.
Evaluating at the pole contradicts the nonzero numerator.
The degree-\((r-1)\) interpolation coefficient is consequently a unit, both for \(\alpha_i\) and for the adjacent-row values \(\alpha_i^{\pm1}\beta_i^t\), including negative \(t\).
On the other hand, reducing \(W(c+3cZ/\nu)\) modulo the monic node polynomial gives a coefficient of degree \(r-1\) divisible by \(3^{r-1}\), since only terms of degree at least \(r-1\) can contribute.
This proves the lower bound for every integer multiplier, including the least \(b_p\), and for the magnitude of the constant row in either orientation.
The case \(r=1\) asks only for integral valuation.
For positive stripped degree \(N\) the constant-row resultant satisfies \(\log H/N=\log|b|\), as needed.

The monic resultant identity for \(E,B(x^p)\) has absolute value \(3^n\), since composition multiplies the exponent of \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\) first by \(\nu\) and then by \(p\).
The rotation calculation retains all root labels, including repeats, because the divided difference is the symmetric geometric sum at coincidence.
The complementary pair product is rational and integral when the roots are partitioned into those of \(P\) and \(E/P\).
This proves the stated index divisibility and gives \(\log I_p/(pd)\le n\log3/(2d)=O(1/u)\).

Rounding \(D=(2u+1)n+1\) gives an error \(O(n)\) while \(u\to\infty\) uniformly in the permitted prime sets.
The bounds \(D/K\le d\le D\) shift \(\log(3d)\) by at most \(O(1)\) from \(Y^{1/4}\), putting all the primes inside the window for the selected factor.
The interpolation bound gives \(\liminf\log H/(Np)\ge(\log3)/K\) for a constant row, while the [short-denominator estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v378.md#window) applies uniformly to the small index and bounded measure with \(e=2\).
Its integral witnesses have two nonconstant rows, exact multiplicity and the asserted degree cap.
For the final lower bound on the cofactor, the two-row determinant from the [rotated determinant estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#rotation) is nonzero by the distinct leading exponents.
At a double root one can replace a column by the sum, making both its entries vanish there.
The powered minimal polynomial therefore divides the stripped determinant, giving \(p\,m(P)\le2m(F)+O(\log(2+\deg F))\).
Removing scalar content first and then the two copies of \(P\) gives \(m(R)\ge p\,m(P)/2-2m(P)-O(\log(2+\deg F))\).
Here \(m(P)\) is uniformly bounded below and \(\log(2+\deg F)/p\to0\), proving the asserted \(m(R)/p\ge m(P)/3\) for sufficiently large degree.
The constant-row lower bound and the nonconstant-row upper bound establish the separation claimed for this reciprocal family.
