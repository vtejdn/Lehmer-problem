## The two intervals and the degree obstruction

The order-dependent bound, its consequences for integral perturbations and its degree-only corollary all hold at the scope stated in L457 v6.
For \(\theta_j=2\pi j/p\), differentiating the sine quotient with respect to \(2\cos\theta\) gives \(B_n'(b_j)=(-1)^{j+1}p/(4\sin\theta_j\sin(\theta_j/2))\).
Substituting \(r=p-2(d-1)\) in the numerator of \(B_m\) then gives the displayed sign identity with \(+(-1)^{j+1}H(b_j)\).
The two powers of \((-1)^{j+1}\) cancel on the parent term.

For \(d=2\) reciprocity fixes the entire quartic, whose quadratic in \(z^2\) has distinct negative reciprocal roots of unequal absolute values.
For \(d\ge3\), covering all interior sine zeros would force divisibility by \((z^{2d-2}-1)/(z^2-1)\), since their conjugates give all the remaining roots of this squarefree divisor.
Its coefficient of \(z^2\) is one even for \(d=3\).
The reciprocal quartic quotient would have linear coefficient zero and quadratic coefficient two, so would equal \((1+z^2)^2\).
Thus there is an unmatched simple sine zero, with a neighboring negative interval of length at least \(1/(SD)\) by the rational separation bound at that zero.
Both factors have sign \((-1)^d\) near the final endpoint.
The final positive interval lies after the negative one and has length at least \(1/(2D)\), using the endpoint denominator two.

Nonnegative residues throughout a consecutive block in the negative interval force strict alternating signs for \(H\), and nonpositive residues in the final positive interval force the reverse strict alternation.
For \(H\ne0\), a block of \(h+2\) nodes requires \(h+1\) distinct zeros, while for \(H=0\) one node already gives the phase sign.
If both intervals had at least \(h+2\) nodes, a negative residue would precede a positive one.
This proves the count bound, including vacuous cases with few nodes.
Each open interval of length greater than \((h+2)/p\) contains the requisite consecutive nodes, including at the endpoint \(1/2\) since \(p\) is odd.
This yields \(p\le(h+2)SD\), and \(h=0\) gives \(p\le 2SD\le2D^2\).

The prime-power factors in \(\varphi(q)^2/q\) are all at least one except the factor \(1/2\) for \(2^1\).
Thus \(\varphi(q)\le2d\) gives \(q\le8d^2\).
Ordering the distinct primes also gives \(q/\varphi(q)\le k+1\le1+\log_2 q\), which verifies the stated bound on \(D\) and its constant.
The reciprocal trace has integer coefficients, and the leading coefficients of \(Q_{-1}\) and \(tB_n\) from the parent lift give their difference degree \(n-1\) with leading coefficient two.
Adding \(H+1\) of degree at most \(n-2\) preserves both properties.
In the lift the lowest possible power from \(z^NH(z+z^{-1})\) has exponent \(N-h\), while the geometric tail starts at \(r>N\).
This gives precisely the guaranteed prefix through \(N-h-1\), and a fixed parent makes \(S,D\) constant, excluding \(p\to\infty\) with \(h=o(p)\).
The earlier fixed-parent example and the fixed-number-of-terms measure obstruction concern different scopes and are used only for comparison.

## Angular matching and the uniform conductor bound

The complete proof in C890 v1 establishes the stated maximum, including the numerical constant \(48\pi\).
Adding the cut angle and the endpoint \(\pi\) to the \(d-2\) sine points balances the \(d\) parent points.
At a removed common root, the phase vanishes but keeps its sign on both sides.
The other interior events reverse the relative sign individually, including the cut angle.
Starting with agreement and ending with disagreement therefore gives the consecutive pairs as stated, including the last pair with \(\pi\).
The desired sign is constant within each such interval, and at a removed double zero strictness still forces the prescribed nonzero sign of \((-1)^{j+1}H(b_j)\).

Consecutive interior grid points force a separate zero in each gap for nonzero \(H\).
Gaps from different intervals have disjoint interiors, so all intervals use the same budget \(h\).
For \(H=0\), any grid point interior to one of these intervals would violate strictness.
An interval with \(m_i\) nodes has length at most \((m_i+1)\Delta\), since successive multiples of \(\Delta\) cover the angular range with boundary gaps at most \(\Delta\).
The last grid point is only \(\Delta/2\) from \(\pi\).
Summing \(m_i+1\le \max(m_i-1,0)+2\) over at most \(d\) intervals gives the total \(\delta=(h+2d)\Delta\), and individually each length is at most \((h+2)\Delta\).
Exceeding the two proposed conductor bounds makes the individual length less than \(\pi/(d-1)\) and the total less than \(1/24\).
Consequently a same-list pair on the sine side must contain \(\tau\), and equal list sizes force exactly as many same-list pairs on the parent side.

Conjugation and the first two Newton identities give upper-angle cosine sums \(0,-3\) for the parent.
Removing \(1,-1\) from the \(2s\)-th roots gives the sine-angle sums \(0,-1\), also for \(s=2\).
Thus \(D_1=0\) and \(D_2=-2\).
If all pairs crossed lists, \(D_2\) would be within \(2\delta\) of \(1+\cos(2\tau)\ge0\), which is impossible.
There is therefore one parent pair \(\alpha,\beta\) and one same-list pair on the opposite side.

If \(\tau\) is matched to \(\pi\), each sine angle has a parent match.
Adding the error \(|\alpha-\beta|\) to the sine-pair errors bounds both \(|2\cos\alpha|\) and \(|2\cos\beta|\) by \(\delta\), by \(D_1=0\).
For \(0\le x\le\pi\), the chord to \(i\) has length \(\sqrt{2}|\cos x|/\sqrt{1+\sin x}\).
Matching the two parent angles to \(i\) gives the claimed upper chord sum \((1+\sqrt2)\delta\).

If \(\tau\) is matched to \(b\in S\), \(\pi\) has a parent match.
Replacing that parent by \(\pi\) and \(\beta\) by \(\alpha\), and cancelling the other sine matches, uses disjoint pair lengths totaling at most \(\delta\).
For \(u=\cos b\) and \(t=\cos\alpha\), the first moment gives \(|2t-1-u|\le\delta\).
The second gives \(|4t^2-2u^2+2|\le2\delta\), whose expression inside the absolute value is nonnegative.
In particular \(|t|\le\sqrt{\delta/2}\), \(1-u^2\le\delta\), and \(u\le-1+\sqrt{2\delta}+\delta<0\).
Hence \(1+u\le1-u^2\le\delta\) and \(|t|\le\delta\).
The ordinary pairs, the original chord to \(-1\), and the chord from \(\beta\) to \(\alpha\) cost at most \(\delta\) together.
The extra chord from \(-1\) to the sine root has length \(\sqrt{2(1+u)}\le\sqrt{2\delta}\), and twice the chord from \(\alpha\) to \(i\) costs at most \(2\sqrt2\,\delta\).
These estimates account for every parent and sine root, including coincident points matched at zero cost.

In either case the full upper and lower matching costs at most \(2((1+2\sqrt2)\delta+\sqrt{2\delta})<1\).
Indeed at \(\delta=1/24\) the first contribution is less than \(1/3\) and the second is \(1/\sqrt3<2/3\).
The target multiset is exactly that of \((z^{2s}-1)(1+z^2)^2/(z^2-1)\), retaining its extra multiplicities even if \(i\) is a sine root.
Telescoping bounds the \(k\)-th power-sum difference by \(k\) times the total chord cost.
At the first differing coefficient, Newton's identities cancel every earlier term and force this difference to have magnitude at least \(k\).
The resulting equality of the two polynomials contradicts squarefreeness.
At \(h=0\), the second term in the maximum is \(96\pi d\) and dominates \(4(d-1)\).
