## Finite-time bounds and their scope

The modular hypothesis gives both stated positive lower bounds with a constant depending only on \(A,L,\eta\).
The Salem subfield, norm-one, and generator conclusions also follow, independently of the modular hypothesis.
The upper bound on the primes witnessing the modular condition for Salem inputs of bounded measure applies uniformly once \(d>\lceil 2L/\eta\rceil\).
The return estimate applies to every irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial, and its interpretation in the cyclic surface-bundle realization applies under the realization's hypotheses, which hold for sufficiently late strict records.
The proof leaves the existence of witnesses on an arbitrary shrinking-measure sequence open.

### Transfer from a determinant to the two observables

The columns of \(K_u\) are the power-basis coordinates of the products \(u\alpha^j\) for \(0\le j<d\), so the determinant is the nonzero integral norm of \(u\).
With \(q=\deg F\), the latest input to the first \(d\) outputs of \(F^b\) has index \(d-1+bq\le t-1\).
Extending the orbit by zero thus preserves these outputs.
The circle multiplier has squared modulus at most \(\|F\|_\infty^{2b}\), and Plancherel gives this multiple of \(E_t(u)\) as an upper bound for their total squared norm.
Hadamard's inequality and the arithmetic-geometric mean give the matching lower bound \(d|\det F(B)|^{2b/d}|\det K_u|^{2/d}\).
At each root \(\beta\), applying the maximum modulus principle to \(F\) or \(z^qF(1/z)\) gives \(|F(\beta)|\le\|F\|_\infty\max(1,|\beta|)^q\), which proves (3) after multiplication.
The circle mean of \(\log|1+z\beta^{t-d}|\) is \((t-d)\log^+|\beta|\), including the integrable circle zero.
For a maximizing phase, the two blocks therefore give determinant modulus at least \(e^{(t-d)m}|\det K_u|\), while disjointness gives total squared norm at most \(2E_t(u)\).
This proves (4) also at \(t=2d\).

For the median, \(Y/d\to\infty\) permits the uniform density-one estimate for \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,X^j-1)|/(jm)\).
The power-basis index and field-norm bounds put the difference from \(d\log r_j/(jm)\) between zero and \((d\log d+(d-1)m)/(Ym)\), which tends uniformly to zero at the specified \(Y\).
Fewer than half the window indices are then exceptional for any fixed relative tolerance. [Full resultant estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#linear)

### Construction in the two prime ranges

Writing \(g=\gcd(\overline P,G)\), the kernel of multiplication by \(G\) consists of the multiples of \(\overline P/g\) modulo \(\overline P\), a space of dimension \(\deg g\).
This works with all multiplicities and needs no separability modulo \(p\).
When the integral determinant is nonzero, each dimension of the mod-\(p\) kernel contributes a factor of \(p\) in Smith normal form.
For a large prime, \((\lfloor p^\theta\rfloor+1)^{R+1}>p^L\) exceeds the number of residue classes modulo \(H\).
The difference polynomial has a nonzero residue polynomial because each coefficient has modulus less than \(p\) and at least one is nonzero.
As \(H\) has positive degree and divides that residue, the difference polynomial is nonconstant.
For \(D=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^s):\mathbb Q]>R\) the difference stays nonzero at \(\alpha^s\), and its determinant ratio is at least \(p^{\eta-\theta}/(R+1)\ge2\).
The upper determinant estimate gives \((\eta-\theta)\log p\le RA m+\log(R+1)\).
For a Salem number every embedding above the distinguished embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^s)\) must send \(\alpha\) to a root of modulus \(\alpha\), forcing it to be the identity and giving \(D=d\).
Hence the prime bound holds on the large-degree tail of every bounded-measure Salem family.
The degree qualification matters if one includes small degrees in the proposed bound on all witnessing primes.
For example, with \(A=2,L=4,\eta=1\), the degree-four Salem example admits \(s=1,H=\overline P\) for every prime.

For bounded primes, deleting the factor \(Z^v\) preserves the gcd because \(X\) is invertible modulo \(\overline P\).
The residual nonzero roots have degrees dividing \(\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,L)\), and \(Z^{p^\ell-1}-1\) has simple roots there.
Its \(p^a\)-th power therefore includes every required multiplicity.
The \(b_0+1\) factors of \(Z^{hp^{b_0}}-1\) each yield at least \(p^{\eta d}\) in the determinant after substitution \(Z=\alpha^s\).
Their product is nonzero by nontorsion, and \(p^{(b_0+1)\eta}/2\ge2\) even at \(p=2\).
Both constructions give \(q\le Kd\), with \(K\) independent of the input.
The bound \(b\ge(T-d)/(Kd)-1\) implies \(db/(T-1)\ge1/(2K)\) eventually, and (3) gives \(m\ge(\log 2)/K\).
When \(p\ge p_0\) and \(D\le R\), the conjugates of \(\alpha^s\) each occur \(d/D\) times in the full powered list, so \(m=d\,m(\alpha^s)/(sD)\ge \mu_R/(AR)\).
Here \(\mu_R>0\) follows by bounding integral coefficients at bounded degree and bounded measure and applying Kronecker's theorem to exclude measure one.
Now (4) gives a lower bound of \((T-d)m/(T-1)\) for the normalized heat observable, and the median estimate applies in every branch.
For the example with \(d=r-1\), the single reciprocal off-circle pair and the absence of cyclotomic factors give irreducibility, since a cyclotomic factor would have order \(2^j r\) and degree at least \(d\).
The reduction modulo two is \(\Phi_r\), which divides \(X^{d+1}-1\), and \(s=d+1\le2d\) witnesses the condition. [Circle argument for the Salem factor](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v296.md#barrier)

### Returns across all primes and orders

Write \(g=\gcd(\bar P,X^j-1)\) and \(\beta=\deg g/d\).
Each of \(X^j-1\) and the \(a\) successive factors \(\Phi_{p^i}(X^j)\) is divisible by \(g\) modulo \(p\) and has nonzero integral determinant on \(B\).
The determinant of their product has absolute value at least \(p^{(a+1)d\beta}\) and at most \(2^d\exp(jp^a m)\).
With \(x=jm/d\in(0,1)\), the specified floor gives \(xp^a\le1\) and \((a+1)\log p>\log(1/x)\), including when \(a=0\).
This proves the rank estimate simultaneously in \(p,j\), its stated contrapositive, and its uniform convergence to zero on the windows \(J_dm/d\to0\).
For the general linear-algebra bound no restriction on reciprocity or primitivity is needed.

The cited surface-bundle construction applies when \(P\) is monic, irreducible, reciprocal, noncyclotomic, of degree \(2g\ge4\), and outside \(\mathbb Z[X^k]\) for every \(k>1\).
Its integral homology action is multiplication by \(X\) on the single module \(\mathbb Z[X]/P\).
After reduction the fiber coinvariants are \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(\bar P,X^j-1)\), to which the base circle adds one dimension for the same fibration at every \(p,j\).
Sufficiently late strict records satisfy the realization hypotheses.
In particular a substitution \(P(X)=Q(X^k)\) with \(k>1\) would give the same measure at smaller degree.
More generally, products of equal-size torsion-quotient classes of conjugates would preserve the total measure and reduce the degree if any class had more than one member.
Strictness excludes that case, giving degree \(d\) at every power. [Class-product compression](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels)
For \(d>R\) the large-prime bound thus leaves a fixed finite prime set under the measure ceiling.
The exponents \(h=p^a(p^\ell-1)\) needed to clear nonzero residual roots and their multiplicities have bounded maximum on that set.
Taking \(j=hs\) now yields \(\deg\gcd(\bar P,X^j-1)\ge\eta d\) at \(j=O(d)\).
Conversely a rank bound of this size at \(j\le Cd\) gives (1) directly with \(s=j,H=Z-1,A=C\).

### Finite-time energy at a prescribed relative norm

At the distinguished place of \(k\), evaluate the first coefficient vector at \(\lambda^{-1}\) and the last at \(\lambda\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|y_0|\le d\lambda^{d-t}E_t(u)\), using the factor \(\lambda^{-(t-1)}\) from the last iterate.
At every circle embedding, evaluation costs at most \(\sqrt d\) on each of the \(t\) iterates, whose values have the same modulus.
Thus \(\sigma(y)=|u(\omega)|^2\le dE_t(u)/t\).
The \(s=[k:\mathbb Q(y)]\) embeddings inducing the distinguished value all satisfy the first bound, including any that also satisfy the circle bound.
Multiplying these \(s\) bounds and the \(e-s\) others yields (8) by integrality of the nonzero norm.
For \(y\in L\), \(s/e\ge1/f\) and \(t\ge d\), so the claimed lower bound follows.

Multiplication by \(I=[\mathcal O_K:\mathbb Z[\lambda]]\) sends \(\mathcal O_L\) into the power order.
The image of \(I\mathcal O_L\) under the \(f\) real embeddings has covolume \(I^f\sqrt{|D_L|}\).
The half-sized box with coordinate bounds \(R\lambda^{-\ell}/2,R/2,\ldots,R/2\) has volume \(R^f\lambda^{-\ell}=2^fI^f\sqrt{|D_L|}\), larger than the covolume even at \(f=1\).
Two points in it differ by a nonzero lattice point, yielding \(a\) in the box of the proof.
The exponents of the centered iterates lie between \(-\ell\) and \(\ell\), and the two off-circle embeddings agree on \(L\).
Every coordinate of every embedded iterate is therefore bounded by \(R\).
The centered element is integral in the power order and has relative norm \(a^2\).
The cofactor row bound for \(V\) uses each \(\max(1,|\lambda_i|)^{d-1}\) at most once, giving \(\|V^{-1}\|_2^2\le d^{d+1}\lambda^{2(d-1)}/(I^2|D_K|)\).
The tower formula gives \(|D_K|\ge |D_L|^{[K:L]}\), so the discriminant quotient after substituting \(R^2\) is at most one.
This gives the upper bound in (6) with the displayed power \(d^{d+2}\).

At \(T\) the two logarithmic bounds have error \(O(d\log d+dm+\log T)\) about \(Tm/f\).
Using \(f\le d/2\), the relative contribution of \(d\log d\) is \(O\bigl(1/(m(\log d)^3)\bigr)\), hence \(O((\log\log d)^{-3})\) by the degree-dependent lower bound.
The \(dm\) term costs \(O((\log d)^{-4})\) and the \(\log T\) term is smaller than the \(d\log d\) term.
All three estimates hold uniformly, so division by the estimate for \(L=k\) gives the index \(e/f\) with uniform relative error.

The centered monomial belongs to each restricted class in the norm-one assertion.
The cofactor estimate with \(|\det V|^2\ge1\) gives its upper bound with \(\log E_T\le Tm+O(d\log d+dm+\log T)\), while (6) for \(f=1\) gives the lower bound shared by those classes.
For \(\mathbb Q(y)\subsetneq k\) the trace-field index is at least two, so uniformity in (7) excludes these vectors at the fixed \(2-\delta\) threshold.
At a minimizer, set \(H=Tm/e\).
The distinguished-place bound gives \(-\log|y_0|\ge (T-d)m-\log d-\log\kappa_T=(1-o(1))Tm\).
Every other logarithm is at most \(U=\log(d\kappa_T/T)=(1+o(1))H\), and integrality gives \(-\log|y_0|\le\sum_{\sigma\ne0}\log\sigma(y)\le(e-1)U\).
Consequently the nonnegative deficits \(U-\log\sigma(y)\) sum to \(o(eH)\), proving the asserted exceptional count for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\).
