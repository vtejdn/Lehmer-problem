# Critical-span trinomials on reciprocal conjugates and four-term tests on the unit circle

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T03:55 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the submitted [C345 v739](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v739.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the claim and received its fixed text without the development discussion or a prior verdict.
The review covered the finite bounds, exact zeros, the small-measure limit and the four-term estimate.

For \(g=\gcd(a,b)\) the pair of coordinates \(z^a,z^b\) recovers \(z^g\) by a signed Bézout combination, since \(z\ne0\).
Hence its orbit has exactly \(D\) points with constant multiplicity \(d/D\), and reciprocity sends that orbit onto itself by coordinatewise inversion.
Two distinct points on a line determine its coefficients up to scale over the splitting field.
For the line \(A+Bx+Cy=0\) with \(ABC\ne0\), inversion gives \(Axy+By+Cx=0\).
After multiplication by \(A\) and translation this conic has equation \(uv=BC\), which is irreducible over \(\mathbb C\).
Thus its intersection with every Galois translate of the line has at most two orbit points.
Inversion gives exactly \(M\) orbit points on the fixed conic when the original line has \(M\).
Each of these points belongs to a uniform translate of that line with probability \(M/D\), giving \(M^2/D\le2\).
A missing coefficient fixes either coordinate or their ratio and gives occupancy at most \(1/e_*\).
These arguments prove the exact-zero estimate even when the coefficient line is initially specified over \(\mathbb C\).

Permutation of ordered triples fixes the product of their nonzero determinants over the full orbit, with every sign included in the original factors.
The product is an integer of absolute value at least one by integrality of all entries.
Normalizing the row at \(z\) costs at most \(\log\sqrt3+W\log^+|z|\), and each position of each row occurs at most \(d^2\) times in the nonzero triples.
The total logarithmic cost is at most \(3d^3(\log\sqrt3+Wh)\).
Taking the normalized coefficient functional as one coordinate is legitimate for complex coefficients by completing its representing unit vector to a unitary basis.
Expansion in this coordinate bounds the determinant of each selected triple by \(3t\), as every two-row minor of the other coordinates has modulus at most one.
With \(\delta=|S_t|/d\) and \(L=\sqrt{2/e_*}\), removing the first projective point at the second draw costs \(1/D\le L\), and removing the span of the first two points at the third costs at most \(L\).
Thus for \(\delta>2L\) the proportion of nonzero selected triples is at least \(\delta^3/4\), giving the factor \(12\) in (1).
For \(\delta\le2L\) the additive term suffices.
Padding a binomial of width at least two keeps the same normalized evaluation.
At width one the fraction of nonzero selected pairs is \(\delta(\delta-1/d)\), their determinant is at most \(2t\), and the analogous total cost is at most \(2d^2(\log\sqrt2+h)\).
For \(\delta>2L\) these bounds imply (1), since \(\log(1/(2t))>\log(1/(3t))>0\) and \(\delta^2\ge\delta^3\).
Width zero reduces to the normalized constant.

For any \(j\), the relative norm \(\gamma\) of \(\alpha\) into \(E=\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\) is a unit, and \(\gamma^j=(\alpha^j)^{[\mathbb Q(\alpha):E]}\) proves both nontorsion and the height identity.
Its degree is at most \([E:\mathbb Q]\), so its total logarithmic measure is positive and at most \(dh\).
Choosing powers of bounded degree along a subsequence with \(dh\to0^+\) would produce units of bounded degree and bounded measure with infinitely many positive measures tending to zero, contrary to the elementary coefficient bounds for monic polynomials.
The accepted [Smyth estimate](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) supplies the eventual reciprocal unit property with this total-measure normalization.
Together these steps give the stated uniform threshold consequence, including the condition on \(Wh\) beyond bounded span.

For \(\beta=\alpha^j\) the roots of the monic minimal polynomial are distinct, and the product of their ordered nonzero pair distances is at least one by its discriminant.
The sum of the logarithms of the chordal denominators over ordered pairs is \((D_j-1)\sum_i\log(1+|w_i|^2)\), at most \(D_j(D_j-1)(\log2+2jh)\).
For a set of chordal diameter \(\epsilon<1\), its \(N(N-1)\) ordered pairs give (3) by \((N-1)^2\le N(N-1)\) when \(N\ge1\).
Every powered conjugate has \(d/D_j\) original preimages.

For the four-term claim the larger pair length satisfies \(A\ge1/2\) because \(2A^2+2B^2=1\).
At a neutral root \(z\), the phase product \(\xi\) in the first pair is its two coefficient phases multiplied by \(z\) to the sum of its two exponents, with the same description for \(\eta\) in the other pair.
Subtracting \(\eta\overline{(U+V)}\) from \(U+V\) leaves \((\xi-\eta)\overline U\), including when either pair has zero sum.
If \(|U|\le\sqrt t\), division by the first coefficient modulus bounds the distance between its exponent-difference power and a fixed unit point by \(\sqrt t/A\le2\sqrt t\).
Otherwise division by \(|U|\) gives the same disk radius for the power indexed by the difference of exponent sums.
The first exponent is nonzero because the exponents are distinct, and the second because the two pair sums of exponents differ by hypothesis.
Both have absolute value at most \(2W\), and changing the sign conjugates a unit point and the disk center together.
The two chordal diameters at most \(4\sqrt t\) give (2) by (3) for sufficiently small \(t\).
At zero, each powered target has at most \(d/e_*\) original preimages.
When all four coefficient lengths agree, pairing the smallest two exponents and the largest two fulfills the unequal-sum hypothesis.
For a Salem number in the stated neutral-root use, \(d\ge4\) and \(d/(d-2)\le2\).
