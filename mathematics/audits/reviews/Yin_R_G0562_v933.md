On the blowup the height of the pulled-back bundle is the sum of the coordinate heights even along the exceptional divisor.
The bundle is nef with square two, and torsion pairs outside the center are dense and have height zero.
This verifies geometric bigness and equality of the two minima on the smooth projective surface.
At a finite place the unit section has ideal \((x-a,y-b)\) on the integral torus.
On a valuation-ring point away from the center the generator with larger absolute value divides the other, giving the model Green function in (4).
The horizontal center is regular, and the two affine blowup charts over the section are regular.
The effective exceptional divisor gives a nonnegative local contribution.
At infinity the cutoff is constant one near the center, so subtracting the logarithmic singularity leaves the continuous chart function \(-\log\max(1,|t|)\).
Outside that region the cutoff is continuous, nonnegative and supported where the differences have modulus less than one.
Thus the exceptional divisor is an admissible arbitrary last entry in the limit-height theorem.

For the calibration, the prime divisors of \(\zeta_p-a\) in \(K(\zeta_p)\) are finite in number.
Choosing \(q\) outside their residue characteristics and the orders of \(b\) at those primes ensures that the reduction of \(\zeta_q\) has exact order \(q\) at every extension of each prime.
At all remaining finite primes the first-coordinate difference is a unit.
This proves zero height for the exceptional divisor on the calibration points, including every infinite embedding by the choice of cutoff.
Distinct growing primes \(p,q\) eventually violate the pair of divisibilities \(p\mid u,\ q\mid v\) for each fixed nonzero character \((u,v)\), proving strictness.
Bilu's theorem applies with its projective height bounded by the sum of the coordinate heights.
A proper algebraic closed subset containing a subsequence would force its full absolute orbits into a finite union of proper conjugate closed subsets.
The torus is irreducible, so this union is proper and has Haar measure zero on the compact torus by the one-variable polynomial zero bound applied successively to the two variables.
This contradicts the weak limit on the subsequence.
Genericity passes to the lifts because any proper closed subset of the blowup has proper closed image of dimension at most one.
The sequence-independent exceptional height limit is therefore zero.

For a section root \(r\) the projected width \(2l+4k\) gives \(h(r^k)=O(1/s)\), uniformly, and the same holds for \(t\).
The unit-circle fibers and simple roots of the top coefficient give exponential approach at both exterior disks.
In particular \(1<|r|<|t|\) eventually.
Since \(r,t\) are conjugate and have equal positive height, a character with torsion value would force equal absolute exponents and then equal or reciprocal moduli.
Taking \(k\)-th powers preserves this independence, so *every* sequence of chosen pairs is strict and generic.

Take a normal field defining all centers in the absolute orbit of \((\beta_3,\beta_4)\).
Their conjugates are off the compact torus and the vanishing limit applies to each of their finitely many exceptional heights.
At each infinite embedding sending the chosen pair into \(B_3\times B_4\), one of the image centers is the distinguished pair of positive poles.
The local function there is at least \(cs-\log C\) and all other summands are nonnegative.
The archimedean weight counts every complex embedding once after division by the absolute compositum degree.
Each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(r,t)\) extends equally often, and two different embeddings of this field give different ordered pairs.
Thus the lower bound uses precisely the fraction of the *joint* orbit in \(B_3\times B_4\), including the multiplicities implicit in both coordinate projections.
The original pair gives one visit, so the joint degree divided by \(s\) diverges.
The sequential argument allows the factor and the ordered pair to vary and proves the asserted uniformity.

For bounded \(H\), v790 gives \(d/s=H+o(1)\) from the equal allocation over the two poles.
Consequently the joint degree divided by \(d=[\mathbb Q(r):\mathbb Q]\) diverges.
For \(H=2\), each disk contains the \(k\)-th power of exactly one exterior conjugate and is invariant under complex conjugation, so these two exterior conjugates are real.
The measure is the signed product \(\delta=\pm rt>1\).
Among unordered pairs of distinct conjugates of \(S\), only the full exterior pair attains product modulus \(\delta\).
Every embedding of the joint field fixing \(\delta\) therefore has one of at most two ordered images, proving \([\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]\ge [\mathbb Q(r,t):\mathbb Q]/2\).
An embedding fixing \(\delta^n\) also attains this maximum and hence fixes \(\delta\), since both orderings give the same signed product.
Separability gives the stated equality of the fields of its positive powers.
