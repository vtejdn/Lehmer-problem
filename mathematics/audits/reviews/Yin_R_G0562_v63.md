## The positive unit, its exact lifts and its conjugates

I checked the class-product reduction in C345v61.
The torsion classes have equal size \(f\), and raising the product of a class to a common \(N\)-th power gives the \(fN\)-th power of any representative.
Thus distinct class products cannot have torsion quotient, they form a single inverse-closed orbit, and each class contributes the same exterior log sum before and after compression.
Multiplication of the representative logarithms by \(f\) preserves rational rank one.
For the resulting polynomial the finitely generated group of logarithms is therefore \(r\mathbb Z\) with \(r>0\), and the integral weights \(w(x)\) have gcd one.

Bézout applied to \(x c(x)=\exp(2r w(x))\) gives \(\kappa=\exp(2r)\) as an actual monomial in the roots, hence an algebraic unit.
For a positive weight \(h(\kappa)w(x)\le h(x)+h(c(x))=2h(x)\), and transitivity makes \(h(x)\) common to all roots.
Summing the exterior log moduli gives \(M(P)=\kappa^{W/2}\).
The height inequality uses the conjugates of the compressed polynomial.

Inversion commutes with the Galois action on the multiplicative group and permutes the actual roots by reciprocity, so the group generated by these actions is finite.
The formula for inverse complex conjugation extends from roots to every monomial, including the torsion subgroup, and induces \(v\mapsto v-w(v)a\).
Its fixed hyperplane and the image of \(a\) identify it with the orthogonal reflection at \(a\), with \((a,a)=2\) and \(w(v)=(a,v)\).
The averaged form is rational, including its final scaling, so a nonzero orthogonal complement of the signed orbit span would contain a nonzero lattice vector.
The represented unit would have zero log modulus after every embedding and thus be torsion by Kronecker, contrary to this nonzero vector.
The signed orbit spans, is closed under its own reflections, and has integral pairings with the entire multiplicative lattice.
All squared root lengths are two, so it is reduced and its simple roots have only single bonds.
The normalized finite classification gives \(A,D,E\) components, all isomorphic by transitivity of the signed action.

For \(b=h a\), conjugating the actual multiplicative reflection sends \(x\) to \(x(h\kappa)^{-(b,[x])}\).
If two choices of \(h\) give \(b\), these reflections agree on the vector of every original root and take it to actual roots.
Compression forces those images to be equal as algebraic numbers.
The ratio of the two possible lifts of \(b\) raised to each \((b,[x])\) is therefore one.
Because \(h^{-1}\) permutes the original roots, the pairings have gcd one, forcing equality of the two lifts themselves.
In particular equality modulo torsion introduces no extra signed conjugates at a given root vector.
Their log moduli are \(r(a,b)\), and equality to \(2r\) occurs only at \(b=a\).
This yields all five allowed levels and the strict dominance of \(\kappa\).

If an orthogonal root occurs, an actual conjugate lies on the unit circle, also when its inverse was the signed lift.
Complex conjugation then puts its inverse in the same orbit, giving reciprocity.
Without an orthogonal root the system is connected and transitivity precludes any orthogonal pair.
Three independent simple roots would have all mutual pairings \(-1\), making their sum of squared norm zero.
Ranks one and two leave at most six root vectors and hence at most six actual conjugates.
This is a bound on the exceptional degree of \(\kappa\).
