## Independent review of the fixed-seed delay progressions

At 2026-11-21T02:03 an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete [C345 v718](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v718.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had previously inspected v717 but took no part in developing v718 and received no development discussion or prior verdict on its proof.
The review checked both signs for each auxiliary characteristic, the new square obstruction, the density formulas and the quantifiers in the fixed-seed conclusion.

Suppose \(p\mid R\).
The positive \(p\)-th radical generates \(K\) because its \(p\)-th power generates \(K\), so reduction of the resultant identity supplies the target root multiset of \(P\) once, with all its reduction multiplicities.
If a specified target root has order \(m\) divisible by \(p\) in characteristic \(q\ne p\), every source above it has order \(mp\).
The orbit-length ratio for these orders divides the target multiplicity, by Frobenius transitivity on each source orbit.
This is the needed interface with v717.

For \(q=2,3\) I checked the three specializations \(0,1,-1\) of the seed polynomial directly.
At a root other than \(1,-1\), neither the extra linear factor nor the deleted cofactor changes the multiplicity of the binomial \(X^{N_q}-\eta\).
Its separable part gives exact multiplicity \(q^{v_q(N_q)}\).
For odd \(p\ne q\) and \(p^{w_{p,q}}\mid N_q\), the order \(p^{w_{p,q}}\) supplies a target at positive binomial sign or in characteristic two.
In characteristic three with negative sign, a primitive root of order \(2^{1+v_2(N_q)}p^{w_{p,q}}\) has \(N_q\)-th power of exact order two, hence equal to \(-1\).
The order of \(q\) modulo \(p^w\) is \(t=\operatorname{ord}_p(q)\), while modulo \(p^{w+1}\) it is \(pt\) for \(w=v_p(q^t-1)\).
The extra power of two in the target order contributes a power of two to the least common multiple defining its orbit length, and \(p\nmid t\).
The ratio thus remains \(p\), contradicting the target multiplicity even when \(q\mid N_q\).
The same argument at \(p=2,q=3\) with \(8\mid N_3\) uses order eight for positive sign and order \(2^{1+v_2(N_3)}\) for negative sign.
For both, passing from \(2^b\) to \(2^{b+1}\) doubles the orbit length since \(b\ge3\) and \(v_2(3^2-1)=3\).

For odd \(a\), the necessary congruence \(a^2-a\equiv2b\bmod4\), where \(b\) is the multiplicity of \(-1\) in \(U\), gives \(b=0\) at \(a\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(b=1\) at \(a\equiv3\bmod4\).
For a positive square field radical \(\beta\), reciprocity gives \(\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}\beta=1\) and \([K:\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1})]=2\).
Thus \(P(-1)=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(1+r)\) must be the square of the integral norm of \(\beta+\beta^{-1}\) from that subfield.
These norm identities do not require even \(a\).
I obtained the following four values using \(P=G_a/U\), taking \(G_a'(-1)/U'(-1)\) when \(-1\) is deleted.

| Delay parity | Seed modulo four | Allowed sign | \(U\) | \(P(-1)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| even | 1 | \(+\) | \(X-1\) | \(a+1\) |
| even | 3 | \(-\) | \(X+1\) | \((a+1)n+1-a\) |
| odd | 1 | \(-\) | \(1\) | \(2(a+1)\) |
| odd | 3 | \(+\) | \(X^2-1\) | \(((a+1)n+1-a)/2\) |

The even-delay values are both two modulo four and cannot be squares.
For odd delays at \(a\equiv1\bmod4\) the fixed value gives the stated additional nonsquare exclusion.
At \(a\equiv3\bmod4\) the odd-delay value is positive, injective in \(n\), and \(O_a(X)\) for \(n\le X\), yielding at most \(O_a(\sqrt X)\) potential failures across both signs.
For even \(a\) the v716 input excludes every odd delay and, when \(a+1\) is nonsquare, every delay.

On the single class modulo \(2D\) the checked parity obstruction excludes 2 and every odd prime divisor of \(H(a)\) is excluded in characteristic two.
At either stated delay cutoff \(R\mid H(a)\), so \(R=1\) at both signs, irrespective of the valuations of \(H(a)\).
The positive-unit factor theorem then gives irreducibility for every \(k\), and the root product of \(P(X^k)\) gives logarithmic measure \(\log r\).
The single class has density \(1/(2D)\), also for \(D=1\).

For the larger union, \(w_{3,2}=1\).
For \(p>3\), equal offsets give nested congruence classes and distinct offsets give disjoint classes already modulo \(p\), proving both formulas for \(c_p\).
At two, the class modulo eight is contained in the parity class for equal offsets and is disjoint otherwise, giving exactly \(1/2\) or \(5/8\).
The two-condition can be dropped in the two all-delay cases.
Taking products uses coprime prime-power moduli, and removal of finitely many small delays preserves the density.
At \(a\equiv3\bmod4\) discarding the at most \(O_a(\sqrt X)\) remaining square exceptions from the odd-prime classes gives the stated lower density.
These computations give densities of the specified sufficient progressions and the asserted lower bound for the full set of successful delays.
