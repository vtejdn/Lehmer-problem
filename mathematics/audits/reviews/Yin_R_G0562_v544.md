## Outcome for the uniform count and the ordinary polynomial

All five sections hold with the stated constants and choices of sign.
The uniformity includes \(n=2\), while the ordinary-polynomial estimate holds from \(a=2\).
The computable onset at a fixed degree bound does not require deciding the unrestricted infimum.

**Uniform initial estimate.**
Choosing the C421 norm representatives once preserves each measure and decreases or preserves its degree.
Distinct powered conjugacy classes and equality of powered and original degrees are sufficient throughout, which verifies the alternative for an original list already injective at the chosen exponent.
With \(D\le a\), \(t\le2a\) gives \(u=Dt/N\le1/a\).
At \(a=2^{300}\), one has \(200<\ell<301\), \(301^5+1<2^{300}\) and \(80\cdot301^4<2^{300}\).
The ratios \(\ell^5/a\) and \(\ell^4/a\) decrease thereafter, giving both \(t\le2a\) and \(a/(4\ell^3)>20\ell\) throughout.
The two exponent classes remain disjoint even at \(n=2\).
Using \(L/N=n/2\) and \((1-u)^{-1}\le2\), the excess from the leading term \(tS/(1-u)\) is at most \(4S\).
The next two terms in twice the logarithmic bound divided by \(n\) are at most \(10\ell/a\) and \(8\ell\), and the box rounding contributes at most \(4\ell\).
These estimates use \(\log(1+L)/n\le 1+\log(a^3+2)/2\le2\ell\) for all \(n\ge2\).
They imply (3) with room at the lower endpoint.
All seed degrees are at least two, and \(\log(3d_i)\le2\ell\) makes Voutier's lower bound strictly greater than \(1/(4\ell^3)\).
Consequently \(tS>20\ell\), ruling out a single nonzero row by the powered minima and the one-norm lower bound \(\exp(ntS)\).

**Contact preservation and (2).**
At each selected powered node the smaller vanishing order is removed from the two rows, leaving at least one nonzero value there.
If \(c_i<t\), the remaining graph still vanishes and both values are nonzero.
The differential polynomial contains the local common square together with at least \(\max(0,t-c_i-1)\) further orders, which proves the lower power \(t+c_i-1\) also for \(c_i\ge t\).
Its nonzero least term and (3) give \(\sum_i c_i h_i\le24\ell\).
Thus each \(c_i<96\ell^4\), the total removed degree is at most \(96D\ell^4\), and every designated graph root keeps at least \(a\) copies.
The other common factors never enter this division.
For a degree-\(k\) numerator each of the \(k\) quotient coefficients in a single synthetic division is at most \(\|E\|_1\) from the leading end if \(|r|\le1\), or \(\|E\|_1/|r|\) from the constant end otherwise.
Iteration over a monic divisor yields the claimed one-norm cost even when intermediate quotients have complex coefficients.
The sign projections retain the reciprocal powers, and every designated node forbids at most one slope because the two unmultiplied projected values sum to a nonzero value.
Since \(2/n\le1\), the logarithm charged for division is at most \(288D\ell^5\), and the sign and final cutoff terms each cost at most \(3\ell\) after normalization by \(n\).
After subtracting \((a-1)S\) the additional term \((\lceil\ell^5\rceil+1)S\) is at most \(\ell^5+2\).
Using \(a/(a-1)\le2\) verifies the coefficient \(1000(D+1)\) in (2).

**Effective comparisons and uniform convergence.**
The derivative estimate bounds the selected degree by \(2b/(a-1)\) and the sum of its distinct measures strictly by \(B\).
At fixed \(D\) the elementary symmetric bounds apply to every root configuration of measure less than \(2^{1/4}<2\).
Enumeration, exact algebraic comparisons and choosing the least available degree at each numerical value consequently compute the finite optimum without losing compatible subsets.
After taking logarithms in (4) its added error coefficient is \(2000\log2\), greater than 1000, and \(\ell\le L_a\).
For a maximizing subset with positive slack the dyadic search terminates because \((k+2)^5/2^k\to0\).
This quantity decreases for \(k\ge300\), and \(L_a\le j+2\) throughout \([2^j,2^{j+1})\), so one computed onset works at all future \(a\) and every exponent.
If the optimum is zero the pointwise inequality already gives equality.

These facts give the stated uniform limit.
Two positive values with sum below \(B\) have smaller value strictly below \(B/2\), proving the one-instance consequence.

**Ordinary integer polynomials.**

Its leading term is at most \(t'S+2S\), and its two non-leading fraction terms are at most \(2\log(6N')/a\) and \(\log(1+E)\).
