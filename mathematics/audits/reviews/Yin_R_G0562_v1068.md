## Arc estimates, Fourier coefficients and arithmetic inversion

At 2026-11-24T19:49 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [characterization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v13.
The reviewer received the fixed proof without development discussion, author explanations or previous review conclusions about it.
Nonnegativity of all cyclotomic logarithmic integrals is equivalent to the conjugation symmetrization being normalized arc length.
The full Mahler integral for every nonzero real polynomial and the example in the statement both follow.

Each cyclotomic logarithm has finite upper bound, so the nonnegativity hypothesis ensures absolute integrability, including absence of mass at its zeros.
Factorization therefore gives the finite integrals \(A(k)\ge0\) and \(A(kj)-A(k)\ge0\) by summing over divisors of \(kj\) that do not divide \(k\).
The Fejér weights sum to \(N/2\), and the nonnegative kernel gives \(\sum_jw_j\cos(j\ell t)\ge-1/2\) for each \(\ell\).
The ratio \(|1-u|/|1-ru|\le2/(1+r)\) holds also at \(u=1\), allowing the pointwise logarithmic upper bound at singularities.
For \(r<1\) the logarithmic power series is absolutely convergent, so it may be summed against the finite Fejér weights.
The bound at \(r=1-1/N\) is \(O(\log(2N)/N)\) independent of the angle.
Integrating at \(z^k\) and using monotonicity under divisibility forces \(A(k)=0\) for each \(k\).

For \(N=\lfloor1/(2\delta)\rfloor\), the telescoping bound \(|1-w^j|\le j|1-w|\) makes every summand at most \(-\log2\) on \(|1-z^k|<\delta\).
The weighted average has mean zero and has the preceding upper bound on the complement, yielding (2) with a constant independent of \(k\).
To control the image under \(z\mapsto z^j\), approximate the center \(t\) by choosing \(1\le k\le Q=\lceil x^{-1/2}\rceil\) with \(\|kt\|\le1/Q\).
The full radius-\(x\) arc maps under this further \(k\)-th power into \(|1-w|<C\sqrt x\) for an absolute \(C\).
Its original preimage is bounded using (2) at exponent \(jk\), uniformly in \(j,t\).
For the small radii allowed in (2) this yields \(O(\sqrt x\log(2/x))=O(x^{1/3})\).
The total mass bound covers the remaining radii.

The Fejér kernel of order \(2N\) is \(O(N)\) on the inner arc of radius \(1/N\), whose mass is \(O(N^{-1/3})\).
On the annulus at distance between \(2^l/N\) and \(2^{l+1}/N\), its bound \(O(N2^{-2l})\) multiplies an arc mass \(O((2^{l+1}/N)^{1/3})\).
The resulting geometric sum gives \(O(N^{2/3})\) uniformly in the center and the image measure.
The second integration is a finite Fourier expansion with nonnegative weights and terms \(|\widehat\nu(jn)|^2\).
The weights exceed \(1/2\) for \(1\le n\le N\), proving (4).
For every prime power the inequality \((b+1)^2\le\binom{b+3}{3}\) bounds the squared divisor count by the ordered four-factor count.
Summation over the first three factors bounds its partial sum by \(X(1+\log X)^3\).
Cauchy–Schwarz on a dyadic interval now gives \(O(N^{-1/6}(1+\log(2N))^{3/2})\), summable over dyadic \(N\) with an absolute constant independent of \(j\).

For each fixed \(j\), radial logarithms with \(r\ge1/2\) are bounded above by \(\log2\) and below by \(f_j-(\log2)/2\), an integrable lower bound.
Dominated convergence identifies their integral limit with \(A(j)\).
At \(r<1\) absolute convergence permits integration of the series term by term, and the dyadic estimate at \(r=1\) justifies taking the limit in the integrated series.
The double series in arithmetic inversion is absolutely bounded by \(\sum_m\tau(m)|\widehat\nu(jm)|/m\), so grouping by \(m=n\ell\) is valid.
The inner divisor sum of the Möbius function makes only \(m=1\) contribute, giving \(\operatorname{Re}\widehat\nu(j)=0\) for every \(j\ge1\).
The negative coefficients are conjugates, and the constant coefficient is one, so Fourier uniqueness gives the asserted symmetrized measure.

Conversely, positivity gives \(\nu\le2\lambda\) when the symmetrization is \(\lambda\).
For a nonzero real polynomial the absolute value of its logarithmic modulus is integrable against arc length even at its finitely many circle roots, and hence is integrable against \(\nu\).
Conjugation invariance of the integrand gives the same integral against \(\nu\) and \(\lambda\), which is \(m(P)\).
For monic cyclotomic polynomials this value is zero by Jensen's formula.
The density \(1+\sin\theta\) is nonnegative of mean one and its odd part cancels on symmetrization.
Thus the conclusion singles out arc length itself when conjugation invariance is assumed.
