The monic polynomial and its unit constant term give nonnegative contact valuations and nonzero residues at every prime.
Primitivity recovers a residue root from its two powers, so its order equals the order generated by the two coordinates.
The proper intersection has at most sixteen geometric points also in characteristics two and five.
Inversion takes a Frobenius orbit of more than eight points into itself, and an involution commuting with a transitive cycle acts by a shift.
A fixed inverse point has both coordinates in the prime field, excluding shift zero on this orbit.
The half-period then gives order dividing \(p^{f/2}+1\), while an orbit of length at most eight gives order dividing \(p^f-1\).
Only the multiple residue classes require this bound.

For \(p\le17\) the earlier estimate absorbs the finitely many factors \(p^{16}\) in the root bound since every residue order is positive.
For larger primes the projection estimate either bounds the derivative directly or supplies critical units with the required reductions, height and degree.
Squarefreeness excludes an exact coincidence of the two powers.
Pages 2–4 of the [author edition](../../literature/pdf/BL96.pdf) give the normalized valuation, actual residual exponent and Theorem 3 as transcribed in the cited inspection.
For independent units both exponents are positive, the height majorants dominate \(h(\alpha_i)\) and \((\log p)/D\), and \(1\le D\le16\).
Putting \(L=C_F(\log N+\log p)\) gives \(b'=(k+l)/(DL)\) and bounds the maximum in the theorem by \(O_F(L)\), even when \(b'<1\).
The resulting bound is uniform in the local field and linear in the actual residue order.

In the dependent case all torsion orders are bounded in degree sixteen, so the local height inequality bounds the valuation of every nonzero torsion difference from one.
A lift of a rank-one generator belongs to the original subgroup, so it is a local unit with reduction order dividing \(g\).
Height homogeneity and Northcott's theorem give the stated bounds for its height and both exponents.
For nonzero \(n\), raising to \(gm\) eliminates the torsion factor and preserves the valuation lower bound.
Since \(1/e\ge1/16>1/(p-1)\), lifting gives the claimed equality for the resulting power of \(\gamma^g\), including negative \(n\).
The local height inequality and \(v_p(mn)\le\log|mn|/\log p\) suffice for the asserted root estimate.
The discriminant sums these valuations over at most 32 contributing roots, giving the exponent eight and the stated small-prime sum.

For fixed \(g\), a primitive residue root forces \(p\mid A_g\) because \(p\nmid g\).
Torsion exclusion makes \(A_g\) nonzero, and evaluation on the complex unit circle bounds its logarithm by \(O_F(\varphi(g))\).
For each such prime the contacts from each root of \(S\) are bounded by its derivative valuation, so the weighted prime sum uses only \(\sum_{p\mid A_g}\log p\le\log A_g\).
This also covers \(A_g=1\).
The two cutoffs discard at most \(l^{1-9\epsilon+o(1)}+l^{1-3\epsilon+o(1)}=o(d)\) in total since \(N\asymp l\) and \(d=l^{1+o(1)}\).
Nonnegativity permits this union bound, and \(\log(l/d)\to\infty\) absorbs the \(O_F(d)\) term in the full resultant estimate.
