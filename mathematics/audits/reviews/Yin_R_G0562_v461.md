### Eliminating the remaining orders

The identity \(g+B=-X-X^3\) and evaluation of \(g\) at \(0,\pm i\) show that the graph has no common numerator and denominator zero.
Reversal then gives nonvanishing of both polynomials on the unit circle.
For a torsion pair generating a cyclic group of odd order, squaring is an automorphism.
At order \(M=2v\) with \(v\) odd, the exponent \(2+v\) is odd and coprime to \(v\), yielding the two coordinate signs in the squared graph relation.
At \(4\mid M\) the exponent \(1+M/2\) is a unit and negates a generator.
At least one coordinate must change since the coordinates generate the group, and any automorphism fixing \(U\) fixes \(f(U)\).
These cases account for every torsion pair and justify using the four \(H_{e,\delta}\) and two \(S_\delta\) as necessary tests.

I expanded the four squared-coordinate numerators and the two sign numerators over the integers.
After division by \(-2\), the two residual products were \(X^8R(X+X^{-1})\) and \(X^8T(X+X^{-1})\).
The two other square products were \(\Phi_1^3\Phi_2\Phi_4\Phi_3^2\Phi_7\) and \(\Phi_1\Phi_2^3\Phi_4\Phi_{18}\Phi_{10}\), and the sign products were \(X\Phi_1\Phi_2\Phi_4^2\) and \(\Phi_{24}\), in the listed order.
I also recomputed the successive Frobenius powers by polynomial remainders.
At the sixth and seventh iterations the remainders were \(z^4+z^2,z\) for \(R\bmod2\) and \(z^6-z^4-z,z\) for \(T\bmod3\).
Both reductions take value one throughout their respective prime fields.
Each monic septic therefore divides the squarefree \(z^{p^7}-z\) and lacks a factor of degree one.
The only other possible factor degree is seven, proving irreducibility over the prime field and hence over \(\mathbb Q\).
The signs \(R(2)=-1\), \(T(-2)=1\) give a real root outside \([-2,2]\) for each monic septic, excluding roots of unity from both residual products.

For each surviving order I reduced the graph equation with the tabulated \(Y\) modulo \(\Phi_j\) and obtained zero.
There is only one second coordinate for each first coordinate since \(B\) is nonzero there.
The eight disjoint primitive first-coordinate orbits have sizes \(1,1,2,2,6,4,6,8\), giving exactly thirty ordered pairs.

### Section orders, multiplicities and the degree bound

For coprime \(a,b\), Bézout gives \(\langle \xi^a,\xi^b\rangle=\langle\xi\rangle\), so the section order is the lcm of the two coordinate orders.
In first-coordinate order \(1,2,3,4,7,10,18,24\) this gives section order \(2,2,6,4,14,10,18,24\), respectively.
The first two columns combine to \(b\) odd, with either parity of \(a\).
For each remaining column, \(\operatorname{ord}(\xi^a)=M/\gcd(M,a)\) and the exponent of the signed monomial \(f(\xi^a)\) give the claimed conditions on \(a,b\).
As a separate arithmetic check, at each possible section order \(M\) I reduced the six monomials of \(F_{a,b}\) modulo \(\Phi_M\) for every \(a,b\bmod M\) with \(\gcd(a,b,M)=1\).
The vanishing pairs agreed with the primitive section table in every case.

At a multiple root, differentiation of \(X^b=f(X^a)\) at the root would give \(Uf'(U)/f(U)=b/a\).
This rational value is unchanged by passing to any conjugate of \(U\).
At order \(j\ge3\) the conjugate with argument \(2\pi/j\) gives \(t=2\cos(2\pi/j)\ge-1\), so the phase formula is strictly negative.
It is also negative at \(U=1\).
At \(U=-1\) the only possible slope is six, for which \(Y=U^6=1\) contradicts the graph value \(-1\).
Thus all torsion roots are simple.
For \(c=\gcd(a,b)>1\), taking the \(c\)-th power sends an order \(r\) to precisely \(r/\gcd(r,c)\).
This assigns each lifted order to just one primitive table factor, and the nonzero derivative of the power map preserves multiplicity one.

Even \(a\) allows only \(2,6,14\), of total degree at most nine.
Odd \(a\) and even \(b\) allow only order four.
In the odd-odd case, orders \(18\) and \(24\) require incompatible residues of \(b\bmod3\), giving the upper bound \(1+4+8=13\).
At \((a,b)=(1,101)\) exactly orders \(2,10,24\) occur.
For \(a=3,\ 3\nmid b\), only orders \(2,4,10\) survive, with the stated congruences.
Order ten can occur with order two, whereas order four requires even \(b\), so at most five degrees are lost from the total \(b+12\).
This proves the exact formula and the lower bound \(b+7\) for the nontorsion degree at every such \(b\).
The result extends the three cyclotomic congruences beyond the finite census and leaves possible splitting of the nontorsion part beyond it open.
