### Rational divisors and the negative exterior root

For primitive directions at sufficiently large slope, C399 gives simple roots, reciprocal individual rational factors and independent natural alternating actions on their inverse-pair labels.
A trace root \(y=x+x^{-1}\) determines its pair, and a single rational trace orbit lifts to the original irreducible reciprocal factor over \(\mathbb Q\).
The eigenvalues \(2+y\) are distinct and nonzero since \(-1\) is torsion.
An alternating three-cycle on a partially selected orbit can exchange one contributing entry with one omitted entry while fixing the choice elsewhere up to order.
Consequently a subset product fixed by the rational splitting group must contain all or none of each orbit.
Conversely a union gives the integer \((-1)^j f(-2)\) for its monic integral divisor \(f\), and the complementary integer proves \(|f(-2)|\mid |H(-2)|\).
This is a criterion over the original rational field even though the multiplication matrix is diagonalized over the splitting field.

For odd \(a\), there is one negative exterior root and no positive one at these slopes.
The trace of that root is less than \(-2\), all real circle traces are strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), and each pair of conjugate nonreal traces gives a positive product of the two translated entries.
Thus exactly the rational subsets containing the marked orbit have negative product.
The least degree with a negative integral exterior-power eigenvalue is the degree \(j\) of this orbit.
C399's uniform degree comparison gives \(j/r=(s/2)(1+o(1))\) for the exterior count \(s\) of its lifted rational factor, for both parities of \(b\).
Here \(s\) is odd, so \(j<r\) is equivalent to \(s=1\) for all sufficiently large slopes.
The remaining conjugates beyond the exterior root and its inverse then lie on the circle and the degree exceeds two, proving the Salem equivalence after sign change.
From \(1/4<|x^a|<4\) at every conjugate one obtains \(|x|,|x^{-1}|<4\).
For each positive unitary divisor \(h\) and each \(j\), the \(\binom nj\) determinant factors are bounded by \(h+10^j\le T+10^j\).
The strict modulus in the text therefore detects integer vanishing exactly, with no separability requirement modulo three.

### Endpoint orders in residue characteristic

Write \(P=-g(X^a)-X^b B(X^a)\).
For odd \(a,b\), \(P(1)=2\), \(P'(-1)=6a-b\), and the cyclotomic factors other than \(X+1\) may only have orders \(10,18,24\).
They have values \(1,1,1\) at \(1\) and \(5,3,1\) at \(-1\).
Dividing out the simple \(X+1\) gives \(H(2)=1\) and \(T=(b-6a)/(5^{\delta_{10}}3^{\delta_{18}})\).
The factor \((-1)^n\) between \(H(-2)\) and the value of the reciprocal core at \(-1\) disappears in the absolute value.
If \(p^2\mid T\) then \(p\) is odd, \(p^2\mid m=b-6a\) and \(p\nmid a\) since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
In characteristic \(p\), the substitution \(X=-Z,\ y=Z^a\) is unramified at \(Z=1\), and replacing \(Z^m\) by one changes the polynomial only in order at least \(p^2\).
I factored the comparison polynomial directly as
\[
 L(y)=(y-1)(y^4-1)(y^5-1).
\]
For odd \(p\ne5\) this has order three at one with leading coefficient \(20\) modulo \(p\).
For \(p=5\) the last factor is \((y-1)^5\), giving order seven with leading coefficient \(4=-1\bmod5\).
In both cases these orders are strictly below \(p^2\).
At \(p=3\) the presence of \(\Phi_{18}\equiv(X+1)^6\) would contradict order three, even before accounting for \(X+1\).
At \(p=5\), oddness and \(5\mid b-6a\) give \(b\equiv a\bmod10\), and primitivity gives \(\gcd(a,10)=1\).
Thus \(\Phi_{10}\) is present and contributes order four modulo five.
The other listed cyclotomics are units at \(-1\) at these respective primes, and all three are units there for \(p>5\).
After removing the cyclotomics, the orders are \(3-1=2\) or \(7-1-4=2\).
The identity \(X+X^{-1}+2=(X+1)^2/X\) converts this into a simple trace root at \(-2\).
Two different rational factors receiving part of \(v_p(T)>1\) would both vanish there and contradict simplicity.
For \(v_p(T)=1\) allocation to one factor follows from integrality, proving unitary divisibility for every divisor and its complement.

### Both parities and the scope of modular evidence

All monic rational trace factors satisfy \(f(2)>0\) by their root signatures.
For odd \(a,b\), the product is \(H(2)=1\), so \(f(2)=1\) and \(f(-2)\equiv1\bmod4\), including for composite divisors.
It follows that \(\operatorname{sgn}(f(-2))=\chi_4(|f(-2)|)\).
Each prime dividing \(|f(-2)|\) occurs to its full \(T\)-valuation, so this character equals \((-1)^e\), where \(e\) counts exactly the primes specified in the indicator formula.
The sign of the translated product is \((-1)^{\deg f+e}\), which proves the indicator for every such rational divisor.
For \(m=c^2\), primitivity follows from \(\gcd(a,c)=1\).
Only removal of \(\Phi_{18}\) could change the parity of a valuation at a prime \(3\bmod4\).
When \(3\nmid c\) its order criterion fails, and when \(3\mid c\) we have \(9\mid m,\ 3\nmid a\), so the calculation of order three applies before any division, excluding \(\Phi_{18}\) without presupposing \(9\mid T\).
All unitary divisors are then \(1\bmod4\), so the indicator is just \(\deg f\bmod2\).

For odd \(a\) and even \(b\), the only possible cyclotomic factor is \(\Phi_4\) when \(4\mid b\), with value 2 at either endpoint.
The monic parent takes values \(2,-2\) at \(1,-1\), so \(H(2)=T=1\) or \(2\), respectively for \(4\mid b\) or \(b\equiv2\bmod4\).
In both cases \(n=2a+b/2-\delta_4\) is odd and \(H(-2)=T\).
Since \(f(-2)\equiv f(2)\bmod4\), each factor with \(f(2)=1\) has \(f(-2)=1\) among the candidates \(\pm1,\pm2\).
If \(T=2\), the sole remaining factor has \(f(-2)=2\) by the full product.
This proves \(f(-2)>0\) for all factors in both even cases, so again only the marked factor has odd degree.
For \(4\mid b\) each monic reciprocal lift \(S=X^jf(X+X^{-1})\) has \(|S(\pm1)|=1\), so [Gross and McMullen's Proposition 3.3](../literature/GM02/Yin_U001.md#unramified-reciprocal), checked on printed page 9 of the author copy, gives \(j\equiv s\bmod2\) for its \(s\) exterior roots, of which only the negative real root is unpaired by complex conjugation.
Every odd divisor must contain it and that one factor is itself an odd divisor, proving the third equivalence with the strict threshold.

As an arithmetic cross-check I divided the parents at \(a=1\) by their cyclotomic parts and reconstructed \(H\) from the reciprocal coefficients and the recurrence for \(x^k+x^{-k}\).
For \(b=12,14,13\) I obtained \((n,H(2),H(-2))=(7,1,1),(9,2,2),(8,1,-7)\), respectively.
For \(b=15,131,55\), the values \(H(-2)=9,25,49\) have \(H'(-2)\equiv2,4,6\) modulo \(3,5,7\), respectively.
These are finite checks of the endpoint computations.
For a specified input, monicity preserves the degree of each rational divisor under reduction, so failure of a degree to occur among all available modular factor products, with multiplicity, excludes it over \(\mathbb Q\).
Occurrence of a modular degree alone supplies no rational divisor.
The Salem interpretation uses the large-slope hypothesis, whose onset is not made effective for a finite sample by this argument.
Along sequences actually satisfying the criterion with \(r\to\infty,\ a\to\infty\), L205 gives \(a\log\sigma\to\log\beta_4\) and hence \(\sigma\to1\).
Existence of such a sequence remains open.
