## Review of the coefficient windows and the two conditional laws

At 2026-11-16T19:36 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Yin checked all three sections of the [conditional moment argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#window).
The reviewer took no part in its development and received no proof-development dialogue, author explanation or earlier finding about this argument.
The [dyadic concentration review](Yin_R_G0562_v28.md#dyadic-limit) supplies the eventual unit norm, sign, convergence of the seed quotient and bound on the length of its dyadic prefix for every positive-excess sequence tending to one at delay two.
The [positive-real function review](Yin_R_G1114_v97.md#positive-return) covers the representing measure and its normalization.

Write \(n\) for the first discrepancy and \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2\), where \(\alpha\) is the measure of the delay polynomial.
The prefix coefficients \(K_j=-t(j-1)\) through \(n\) and \(t=O(n^{-2})\) bound the absolute row sums of the Gram matrix of \(1,v,\ldots,v^{n-1}\) uniformly.
For a block starting at \(b\ge1\), the inner products of \(v^{b+n-1}\) with these \(n\) monomials give the block moments in reverse order, up to conjugation.
This monomial has squared norm \(1+t/2\), so the operator bound gives \(\sum_{j=b}^{b+n-1}|K_j|^2=O(1)\) independently of \(b\), including the factor two between coefficients and moments.
On any window through \(Cn\) the coefficient sum in absolute value is consequently \(O_C(\sqrt n)\).
Since \(t=4\sinh^2((\log\alpha)/2)\), the quantity \(n\log\alpha\) stays bounded.
The coefficient of degree \(j\le Cn\) in \(((1-\alpha z)(1-z/\alpha))^{-1}\) is bounded by \((j+1)\alpha^j=O_C(n)\).
Multiplying by the numerator \(1-z^2-2zK\) and by \(t\) gives \(tJ_j=O_C(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly on the window, including at zero.
Each nonzero integer coefficient of \(K^0=K-tJ/2\) then costs at least \(1/4\) in the sum of squares of coefficients of \(K\), which bounds their number on the same window.

The joint weak limit has mass one since \(K(0)=1+t/2\to1\), and its first marginal is Haar because all nonconstant fixed moments of the original circle measure tend to zero.
For fixed \(l\ge1\) and integer \(r\), the character \(x^{-r}y^l\) on \((x,y)=(\bar v,v^{n+1})\) gives the coefficient with positive index \(l(n+1)+r\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
It lies in a fixed multiple of the discrepancy window, so its doubled limiting moment is integral.
The conditional moment \(P_l=2\mathbb E[y^l\mid x]\) has norm at most two and these integers as its Haar Fourier coefficients.
Parseval makes its Fourier support finite, and the integer Laurent polynomial bound \(\sqrt5\) for three or more terms applies to the resulting continuous representative.
Two nonzero coefficients can be aligned on the circle, so in that case both are units.
Substituting the defining centered quotient \(V\) into \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2F)\) cancels the factor \((1-z)^2\) and gives \((1-z^{n+1}V)/(1+z^{n+1}V)\).
The first row therefore vanishes at fixed negative offsets, and at fixed nonnegative offsets it converges coefficientwise to \(-2\lim V=-R\).
The prescribed compact convergence of \(V\) suffices for this use.

For \(P_1=a+bx^k\), normalize \(Y=y/a\) and \(w=(b/a)x^k\).
Put \(m=(1+w)/2\) and \(L=\mathbb E[Y^2\mid x]=P_2/2\).
The complex variance inequality \(|L-m^2|\le1-|m|^2\) extends everywhere by continuity and full Haar support.
At any \(w=1\), its right-hand side is \(O(h^2)\) in angular displacement \(h\), fixing the value and angular derivative of \(L\) as \(1\) and \(ik\).
A single term is therefore \(L=w\).
For two terms \(2L=cx^p+dx^q\), \(p<q\), both signed characters equal one at all the \(k\) roots of \(w=1\).
Thus \(p,q\) are multiples of \(k\), including when \(w=-x^k\), and differentiation gives \(p+q=2k\).
For conditionally independent copies \(Y,Y'\), averaging \(1-\cos(2h)\le4(1-\cos h)\) at \(e^{ih}=Y\overline{Y'}\) gives \(1-|L|^2\le4(1-|m|^2)\).
Near a root of \(w=1\) this compares \(\sin^2((q-p)h/2)\) with \(4\sin^2(kh/2)\), whence \(q-p\le2k\).
The only unequal multiples allowed are \(0,2k\), and evaluation at a root fixes both signs to give \(L=(1+w^2)/2\).
The alternatives concern the whole Laurent polynomial \(L\), so they hold globally for the joint limit.

When \(L=w\), the conditional variance of \(Y^2\) is zero.
For either square root \(u\) of \(w\), the conditional mean of \(Y/u\) equals \(\operatorname{Re}u\), giving the stated weights on \(u,-u\).
In the other case \(Z=Y/u\) has conditional mean \(\operatorname{Re}u\) and \(\mathbb E[Z^2\mid x]=\operatorname{Re}w\).
Then \(\mathbb E[(\operatorname{Re}Z)^2\mid x]=(1+\operatorname{Re}w)/2=(\operatorname{Re}u)^2\), so \(Z\) lies on the two points \(u,\bar u\) almost surely.
Consequently \(Y\) lies on \(1,w\), with equal weights whenever they are distinct.
The support collapses to one point when \(w=1\).
All three sections pass at the stated scope of necessary conditions on subsequential limits.
Realization of either law by a sequence of actual positive-excess inputs remains open.
