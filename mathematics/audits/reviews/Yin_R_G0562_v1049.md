## Cluster divisibility, simultaneous equality and the Salem example

At 2026-11-24T18:27 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v6.md#statement) and all five proof sections of C0901 v6, including the amended wording on accumulation across primes.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous review conclusions about v6.
The two valuations in (1) have the stated lower bound and can both equal it for one integral coprime pair with nonzero reflected determinant.
Their consequences for the archimedean norms and the stated Salem family also follow.

For a class of size \(m\), the inertia-orbit bound gives \(v_p(x_i/\omega-1)\ge1/m\).
The linear term of the \(p\)-th power then dominates, including in the comparison of two distinct roots of that class.
This gives the indicated node valuations, distinctness, the analytic branch and the derivative for either sign of \(t\).
The class polynomial \(K_C\) is defined over the unramified field of the residue because automorphisms fixing the residue permute the nodes of the class.
In the free module modulo \(K_C^2\), reducing \(Z^n\) past degree \(2m-1\) preserves the valuation bound \((n-j)/m\) at coefficient \(j\) by the monic recurrence.
After multiplying by \(b_n\), the lower bound for \(n\ge p\) is positive at \(p\) and increases to infinity.
For \(2m\le n<p\) all the coefficients also reduce to zero.
These estimates justify convergence in the doubled algebra and its reduction to the truncated exponential for all \(t\) prime to \(p\).
The value and first derivative at each distinct \(z_i\) determine the remainder in characteristic zero.
After removing unit factors, the columns of the reduced upper coefficient block of \(Z^j,\omega^tf_tZ^j\) are the falling factorials of degrees \(0,\ldots,m-1\) evaluated on \(m,\ldots,2m-1\).
These residues are distinct modulo \(p\), giving an invertible block.
Thus the contributions of degrees \(j\ge m\) belong to \(p^m\mathcal S_C\), forcing each lower Taylor coefficient of each row to be divisible by \(p^{m-j}\).
This proves (4) and the first valuation bound by multiplying the evaluations over all \(m\) nodes of each class.

Removing common scalar \(p\)-content preserves contact, so (4) still applies to both reduced rows.
For a \(p\)-primitive first row the Gauss norm of its factorization cancels its leading coefficient valuation against the negative root valuations.
Applying the same calculation at \(c+pZ\) gives the sum of \(\min(1,v_p(\gamma-c))\) over roots reducing to \(\bar c\), at least \(m\) by (4).
At each of these roots every Taylor term of \(V\) has valuation at least \(m\min(1,v_p(\gamma-c))\).
The clusters are disjoint, other integral roots contribute nonnegative valuations, and at nonintegral roots the possible loss is at most the contribution of \((\operatorname{lc}U)^v\) in the product formula.
This proves the second valuation bound, including after reversing the roles of the rows or restoring common content.
The Sylvester matrix has \(v\) shifted rows from \(U\) and \(u\) from \(V\), giving (2) by Hadamard.
In the amended statement the prime powers accumulate when they divide the same nonzero integer resultant.
Separate choices of rows give no general divisibility assertion for a single resultant.

For the local order, the reduction of \(x-\omega\) has \(m\)-th power zero, so \(Z=(x^p/c-1)/p\) belongs to \(R_C\).
Equality of the valuations of \(z_i-z_j\) and \(x_i-x_j\) gives unit determinant for the change of power bases and \(R_C=\mathcal O[Z]\).
With \(\lceil m/2\rceil\) ordinary and \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) exponential columns the same falling-factorial determinant gives a basis modulo \(K_C\).
The omitted generators have weights at least the largest selected weight, so expansion in this basis puts them in the span of the selected weighted columns.
Their diagonal weights give exactly the stated Smith exponents, including the odd-\(m\) endpoint.

For equality in (1), both leading coefficients are free in the doubled contact kernel.
After imposing zero constants, division by \(Z\) leaves rank \(2m-1\) modulo \(Z^{2m-1}\) by the same factorial determinant using \(m\) ordinary and \(m-1\) exponential columns.
The three excluded subspaces therefore miss at least one residue line even at \(p=3\).
The reduced rows obtained have no common zero at the origin, and a common factor invertible at the origin would give a nonzero low-degree relation modulo \(Z^{2m}\), contradicting the basis.
Their lifts can be chosen over the unramified field generated by the class residue and transported around its Frobenius orbit.
Remainders modulo the pairwise coprime \(Q_C^2\) consequently descend to \(\mathbb Z_p\).
Adding \(Q^2\) makes the first row monic, with exactly \(m\) roots in each designated residue class by multiplicity of its reduction.
For \(0<v_p(\gamma-c)<1\) the degree-\(m\) Taylor term would have unique minimal valuation, so every such root has \(v_p(\gamma-c)\ge1\).
In the scaled coordinate, a multiple of \(Q_C^2\) remains divisible by \(p^m\) after dividing by \(p^m\).
Reduced coprimality then gives valuation exactly \(m\) for the second row at every first-row root in this class, and its nonzero scaled constant gives the same valuation at every powered node.
The adjustment at the other residue factors gives valuation zero there.
The norm and resultant valuations are therefore exact for the same local pair.

Rational approximation within the solution space is available because the double-contact conditions at fixed row degrees are homogeneous rational linear equations, also after clearing a negative monomial.
The norm and resultant are continuous and nonzero at the constructed pair.
Perturbing the second row by a small rational multiple of \(Q^2\) preserves both contacts and avoids the at most two scalar roots of a nonzero Laurent coefficient of the reflected determinant.
If the second degree increases, padding it to the new degree multiplies the old resultant by a power of the first leading coefficient, a \(p\)-adic unit.
Prime-to-\(p\) denominator clearing therefore yields integral rows with both prescribed valuations and a nonzero resultant.
The sharpness conclusion imposes no height or width cutoff.

For the cubic example, the two interior zeros of \(Q_0\) and the one interior zero of \(Q_0^*\) give winding number \(N+1\).
The \(N+1\) distinct unit-circle preimages, an exterior real zero between \(\theta\) and \(\theta+\varepsilon\) for large \(N\), and its reciprocal exhaust the degree \(N+3\).
The distinct exterior roots approach \(\theta\), and their irreducible factors contain their reciprocals by the unit norm.
Bounded-degree finiteness gives \(e_N\to\infty\), while the unique exterior conjugate ensures a singleton fiber, hence no degree loss, for every positive power.
For \(q\ne23\), evaluation at a root of the squarefree \(\bar Q_0\) gives a nonzero value of the auxiliary polynomial of degree at most six because that root is nonzero and \(\bar Q_0^*\) is coprime to \(\bar Q_0\).
It follows that \(\sum_C(m_C-1)\le6\), even if the residue characteristic divides a multiplicity.
Writing \(a_C=m_C-1\), the excess \(\sum_Cm_C^2-e_N\) is \(\sum_C(a_C^2+a_C)\le42\).
The example excludes compulsory concentration for all bounded-measure inputs, with measures tending to the strictly positive \(\log\theta\).
It does not decide whether concentration can be forced on all sufficiently small positive-measure inputs.
