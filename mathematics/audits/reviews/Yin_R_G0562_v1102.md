## The obstruction includes the dyadic case and is realized over infinitely many bases

The \(2n\) Salem conjugates give exactly \(n\) traces, since equality of two traces identifies the two roots of the same reciprocal quadratic.
One trace exceeds two, and the others lie strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\) since a conjugate equal to \(\pm1\) is excluded.
With even \(n\), exactly \(n-1\) embeddings give \(t-2<0\) while all give \(t+2>0\).
Thus \(A=-N(t-2)\) and \(H=N(t+2)\) are positive integers.
In a common integral basis the two multiplication matrices differ by \(4I\), so their determinants give \(H\equiv-A\bmod4\).
The norms are therefore both odd or both even.
If odd, then \(AH\equiv3\bmod4\), requiring an odd exponent of some rational \(p\equiv3\bmod4\).
In the decomposition of that exponent as \(\sum_{v\mid p}f_v v(t^2-4)\), at least one summand is odd.
This gives an odd valuation at an odd residue degree, so \(\mathrm Nv\equiv3\bmod4\) divides \(AH\).
Adjoining the square root there forces ramification index two because its valuation in the normalization of \(B_v\) is half-integral.

If even, a prime \(v\mid2\) divides \(t-2\) and also \(t+2\) since their difference is four.
It contributes at least \((\mathrm Nv)^2=4^{f_v}\) to the norm product, independently of its ramification over \(\mathbb Q\).
This dyadic estimate requires no odd valuation and implies no extra ramification assertion.
The trace-polynomial representation gives \(P(1)=-A,\ P(-1)=H\) for even \(n\), yielding both the endpoint product and the bound \(C(B)\).
The relative norm of \(\alpha^2-1\) is \((\alpha^2-1)(\alpha^{-2}-1)=-(t^2-4)\).
The cyclic norm bound applies to \(\alpha^2\) of absolute height \(m/n\) in the containing field of degree \(2n\), with coefficient one on its first cyclotomic term.
It gives exactly the upper estimate in (1) for every \(T\ge2\).
Along a shrinking sequence the trace degrees tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
Taking \(T\) of order \(\sqrt{(n/m)\log(n/m)}\) for large \(n\) gives \(\log C(B)\ll\sqrt{nm\log(n/m)}\).
The quotient \(m\log(n/m)/\log n=m+m\log(1/m)/\log n\) tends to zero, which proves (2).

For every fixed even \(n\ge4\), all the prescriptions modulo rational primes at most \(4^n\) can be chosen irreducible and separable, including the one at two.
At the two additional primes the indicated squarefree factor patterns exist because there are enough distinct linear factors and irreducibles of the required degrees.
One further prime \(s\) may be taken outside all the prescribed primes and the finite set ramifying in the previously produced fields.
The class \(X^n+s\bmod s^2\) makes the resulting monic polynomial Eisenstein there.
CRT is applied coefficient by coefficient without disturbing monicity.
Starting from \(\prod_{j=1}^n(X-j R_0)\) requires perturbations bounded by the fixed modulus \(J\).
In the rescaled polynomial, the alteration of the coefficient of \(Y^j\) is \(O(J/R_0^{n-j})\) for \(j<n\).
The signs at endpoints of disjoint intervals around the \(n\) distinct limiting roots therefore persist for large \(R_0\), giving all roots real.
Irreducibility modulo two supplies degree \(n\).

A squarefree reduction gives unit polynomial discriminant locally, hence no local index and the specified unramified splitting pattern.
It also gives the indicated Frobenius cycle structure in the normal closure since the reduced roots are distinct.
The transitive group containing an \((n-1)\)-cycle is doubly transitive by the action of its point stabilizer.
The additional transposition then has every transposition as a conjugate, giving \(S_n\).
The Eisenstein prime is totally ramified and distinguishes the new field up to rational isomorphism from the previous finite list.
This construction gives infinitely many bases separately for each even degree.

The sole prime above \(2\) has residue degree \(n\), and an odd rational prime at most \(4^n\) has residue norm \(p^n\equiv1\bmod4\).
Any prime above a larger rational prime has norm larger than \(4^n\).
Consequently \(C(B)=4^n\), even if the second minimum in its definition were empty.
Using \(T=5\) and dividing (1) by \(2n\) gives \(\log2\le(\log5)/4+2m/n\).
Thus \(m/n\ge(\log2-(\log5)/4)/2=\log(16/5)/8>0.145\).

For the extension assertion fix any totally real \(B\) of degree \(n\ge2\) and any one real embedding \(\sigma\).
Choosing \(\mathfrak p\) unramified in the previous finite list and a coset representative of valuation one modulo \(\mathfrak p^2\) ensures the same valuation for every member of the coset.
The image of that coset is a translate of a full lattice in \(\mathbb R^n\).
A ray interior to the required sign orthant eventually has distance to its boundary larger than the lattice covering radius, producing \(b'\) with all the required strict signs.
The odd valuation gives irreducibility of the quadratic and ramification at \(\mathfrak p\), while the signs give exactly one split real place.
The resulting signatures are \((2,n-1)\) and \((n,0)\).
Dirichlet's theorem gives unit ranks \(n\) and \(n-1\), and the relative unit norm contains the squares of the base units, so its kernel has rank one.
At every complexified base place an element of that kernel has modulus one.
At the split place a nontorsion kernel unit has real reciprocal values, so sign and inversion select a value \(\alpha>1\) at a prescribed lift.
This value occurs at only one embedding of the quadratic extension, forcing \(\alpha\) to generate that extension over \(\mathbb Q\).
The trace generates \(B\), since it belongs to \(B\) and the extension of its trace field obtained by adjoining \(\alpha\) has degree at most two and absolute degree \(2n\).
The other \(2(n-1)\) conjugates have modulus one, giving a Salem number in the usual degree-at-least-four convention.
The fresh ramified prime separates the quadratic extensions over \(B\), and the construction can be repeated for each choice of split place.
