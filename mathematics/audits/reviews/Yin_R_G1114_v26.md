## Finding

For every offset, \(A,B\) have no common root, including at zero, and \(A^2+B^2\) and \(A^4+B^4\) have nonzero constants.
If the fourth phase product has measure one, its integer factor \(A^2+B^2\) has measure one and consists of cyclotomic factors up to sign.
At each of its roots the quotient \(A/B\) equals \(i\) or \(-i\).
For \(u\) odd, \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_u)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2u})\) and adjoining \(i\) gives \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{4u})\) of twice the degree.
Thus the orders are divisible by four and negation preserves each set of primitive roots, making the polynomial even.
Splitting \(f\) into even and odd coefficients gives a nonzero product \(H=EO\), since \(f(0)\ne0\) and \(-\theta\) cannot be a conjugate.
The reciprocal-polynomial product of these components is \(X^{m-1}H(1/X)\) for either parity of \(m\).
The odd-coefficient identity forces \(k+\operatorname{ord}_0 H=m-1-\deg H\), and its value at one forces \(f(1)^2=f(-1)^2\).
This singles out at most one offset.

Measure one for the eighth phase product would also make \(A^4+B^4\) even, since the order-eight quotient at its roots generates a field containing \(i\).
The sum and product of \(A^2,B^2\) would then be even, so substitution of \(-z\) fixes or exchanges their unordered pair over \(\mathbb Q(z)\).
In the first case \(f\) would have just one parity.
In the second the valuations at zero force \(a=b=0\), where \(A^2+B^2\) already has leading coefficient \(1+f(0)^2>1\).
This proves strict positivity of the eighth logarithmic measure at every offset.

In the fourth-phase real tests, \(f(1)<0\) follows from the factor for \(\theta\), the positive factors for the other real roots and the conjugate pairs.
For \(k>0\), or \(k<0\) with \(f(0)>0\), \(A+B\) tends to \(+\infty\) on the positive real axis and is negative at one.
For \(k<0\) with \(|f(0)|>1\), the leading coefficient of \(A^4-B^4\) already gives measure greater than one.
Any other real conjugate supplies an uncanceled exterior pole, and beyond the outermost pole on that half-line the absolute ratio \(|A/B|\) passes through one as it tends to zero.
The residual case therefore requires \(f(0)=-1\), negative \(k\), and no other real conjugate.
Its degree is odd, and pairing the nonreal conjugates gives \(f(-1)<0\), hence \(f(-1)=f(1)\).
The existence of an exceptional seed with fourth rate zero remains open in this argument.

The delay polynomial is monic with constant \(-\sigma\) and is reciprocal up to sign.
The strict circle comparison and the real sign test on either side of \(\theta\) give a single exterior root with multiplicity one.
Its factor has unit constant and must contain the single interior reciprocal root, so every other irreducible factor is cyclotomic.
For \(G=g/f\), the conjugate torsion equations with exponents two and one yield finitely many rational-function comparisons as the signs vary.
Their right sides have a pole at \(\theta\) whereas their left sides are finite at \(\theta\), using that neither \(-\theta\) nor \(\pm\theta^2\) is a root of \(f\).
The torsion roots consequently lie in a finite set independent of \(N,\sigma\).
Logarithmic differentiation at such a root gives the stated fixed value for \(N\) if the root is multiple.
For all large \(N\) the cofactors are therefore simple, range over a finite set and give \(k=\deg H_{\rm cyc}-m\).

On \(y^d=w,\ w^j=1\), the phase polynomial \(wA-\sigma B\) agrees with \(y^b(z^Nf-\sigma g)|_{z=y}\).
It is nonzero because \(A,B\) have distinct zeros, and each of these phase polynomials belongs to a finite list for fixed \(f,j\).
At a common torsion root the omitted cofactor evaluation is replaced by the derivative quotient for \(S\), which has size comparable to \(d\).
For the other factors the phase-product proof accounts for leading coefficients, exterior roots and torsion separations.
At fixed nontorsion algebraic circle roots the recorded power estimate gives logarithmic error \(O_{f,j}(\log d)\) before division by \(d\).
Multiplying over \(w\) gives the stated transfer since \(j\) is even, and the minimum over the finitely many actual offsets is positive at \(j=8\).
The source estimate was applied at its recorded scope without a new audit of its original proof.

For the quartic, direct substitution gives \(f(4/3)=-17/81,\ f(-4/5)=-49/625,\ f(-1)=1\).
These signs and the derivative put the two real roots in the claimed ranges, with the conjugate pair strictly inside by their product.
An integral proper factor omitting the exterior root would have nonzero constant of absolute value less than one, proving irreducibility, and \(g\) is not an associate of \(f\).
Exact polynomial multiplication verifies the three factorizations displayed in the ledger.
On the stated progression the torsion roots are precisely \(-1\) and the primitive sixth roots, each simple for large \(N\), giving \(k=-1\).
The only factor with positive logarithmic measure is the reciprocal quartic with traces \(-1\pm\sqrt2\).
The exterior root has modulus \(\lambda\) determined by \(\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=1+\sqrt2\), strictly between \(2\) and \(5/2\) on the trace scale.
Thus the fourth rate on this progression is exactly \(\log\lambda\in(0,\log2)\).
