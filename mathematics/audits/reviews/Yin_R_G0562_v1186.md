# Review of the minimum strict exterior count in a divisible conjugate group

## Terminal minima, log symmetry and the square endpoint

At 2026-11-25T02:45 UTC an agent outside the development of v126 independently reviewed its [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v126.md#statement), the three proof sections and their fixed inputs for Yin.
The reviewer had separately constructed examples of the v109 square pattern, and received v126 without its development discussion.
The conditional terminal formula and the unconditional minimum \(b\) in the specified large-degree square pattern hold for strict exterior counts, including for members of arbitrary degree and measure.

**Descent for a finite or infinite family.**
The nonempty set of possible strict exterior counts has a minimum by well ordering, and any element attaining it uses finitely many inputs.
Compression preserves the sign of the logarithm within each torsion class and replaces its roots by one product.
An orbit product of modulus greater than one requires at least one original root of modulus greater than one.
Normality partitions the original conjugate set into disjoint orbits, so the number of distinct exterior products cannot exceed the number of original strict exterior roots even if some product values coincide.
The constituent selection in v119 guarantees that the product remains nontorsion at each removal, retains all the other active components up to a nonzero scalar, and after finitely many removals leaves one actual leading constituent \(W\).
This proves the strict-count version used here, since the original argument for selecting the subgroup did not require that the circles themselves be counted.

For a noncoordinate line the height-versus-maximum-log estimate in v115 uses the signed orbit size at least \(ub(b-1)/2\).
As total measure is the positive sum and also the negative absolute sum for a unit, its own proof gives at least \((b-1)/4\) entries of each strict sign.
This contradicts \(l(y)\le L<(b_0-1)/4\).
On a coordinate line the \(2ub\) projected signed coordinates have equal embedding multiplicity, implying opposite pairs of logarithms.
The rational rank of these logarithms is therefore at most the number \(l(y)\) of positive entries, and equals \(\dim W^c\) by the fixed-space formula.
In an oriented sum-zero module, an exchanged block pair contributes \(b-1\) such dimensions and a fixed positively oriented block contributes at least \(b/2-1\), from the cycles of an involution after removal of the constant.
Both exceed the hypothesis on \(L\).
All blocks are therefore fixed with negative sign, and their actual products and all their conjugates have modulus one.
Kronecker gives torsion, removing the full space of block constants and making the selected term terminal.
The independent signed case was already terminal.

The actual terminal root classes are now the coordinate orbit.
Multiplication by a nonzero rational and the equal class fiber size make \(l(y)\) an integer multiple of \(l(z_j)\), also for negative scaling since the terminal orbit is reciprocal.
Every terminal unit belongs to the full family group, by its construction from the conjugates of its own input.
This gives \(L\le l(z_j)\le l(y)\le L\) and yields the stated minimum over all terminals.
Its size bound makes \(2l(z_j)<\deg z_j\), so it has a neutral conjugate.
Bounded-degree finiteness at every stage and the uniform block-size bound imply \(b_0\to\infty\) as the common measure bound tends to zero.
For integer-polynomial inputs of small positive measure, after removing zero roots the noncyclotomic factors are monic units up to signs and their individual log measures are bounded by the sum.
The strict cutoff on \(L\), or an a priori fixed bound on \(L\) at sufficient smallness, remains the condition in this application.

**One group element reversing all the logarithms.**
In the given square pattern the active half contributes one exterior root, and its inverse contributes \(b-1\).
The log-modulus functional vanishes on every summand outside that block.
Because \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\), one has \(b=d/(2u)\gg_M d/(\log(3d))^{212}\).
The real-place estimate bounds the total number of real conjugates by \(O_M(\sqrt{d\log(3d)})=o(b)\).
Thus the active half contains a nonreal root and its complex conjugate, since conjugation preserves that half by fixing \(r\).
They are on two distinct labels and have identical moduli.

A Galois element sending \(r\) to \(r^{-1}\) has negative sign on the active block and permutes that block's labels by some \(\pi\).
If \(\pi\) is even, postcompose by the supported lift of \(\pi^{-1}\).
If \(\pi\) is odd, postcompose by the supported even permutation given by the equal-modulus transposition times \(\pi^{-1}\).
In either case the active log list is negated, and every other summand is carried into the space where the functional vanishes.
The same group element consequently negates the functional on the whole space.
Extending it to a normal closure of any finite tuple preserves the class action for arbitrary radicals and torsion multipliers.
Multiplication by the lift permutes the embeddings while negating every log in the tuple simultaneously.

**Every line at a square endpoint.**
All half products are torsion, so their constants give the full kernel in the oriented module, leaving the irreducible \(u(b-1)\)-dimensional sum-zero representation.
Every nonzero class spans this same representation under conjugation.
On the active half complex conjugation acts by an involution with positive sign, giving fixed dimension \(R\ge b/2-1\).
The projection-count proof in v64 counts actual embeddings with multiplicity and uses the number of nonneutral embeddings.
The joint symmetry just proved gives this number as \(2l(x)\) without requiring that \(x\) itself be reciprocal.
Thus \((\deg x)R\le 2l(x)u(b-1)\).
For a noncoordinate vector the orbit lower bound gives \(\deg x\ge ub(b-1)/2\), proving \(l(x)\ge b(b-2)/8\).
This exceeds \(b\) once \(b>10\), eventually uniformly at the given measure ceiling.
On the individual coordinate lines each signed class is counted equally often and exactly \(b\) source roots are exterior.
Hence \(l(x)=b\,\deg(x)/d\) with integral positive degree ratio, and the source \(r\) attains \(b\).
