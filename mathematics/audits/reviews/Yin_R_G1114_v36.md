## The coefficient, positivity and recurrence windows

The estimates apply to a single positive-constant input once \(q\) is large on the fixed branch.
The stated logarithmic positivity window and the square-root prefix under a maximal moment both follow, including for singular moment matrices and for either sign of the maximal moment.

### Coefficients on the square-root window

The positive-real-part function \(H_q\) is normalized by \(H_q(0)=1\), so its Herglotz measure \(\lambda_q\) has mass one.
Real coefficients give conjugation symmetry and \(h_i=2\int v^i\,d\lambda_q\) for \(i\ge1\).
The derivative identity of L462v36 gives \(j/\delta=q+1/\theta-H_q'(0)\), with \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\).
Hence \(\delta=O_j(q^{-1})\), \(q\delta=j+O_j(q^{-1})\) and \(\epsilon=\delta/\theta=O_j(q^{-2})\).

Dividing the denominator \(W\) of L462v36 by \(1-z^2\) gives \(1-Z\) with the asserted sign.
Expanding its numerator as
\[
 \delta(z^{-1}-z)+\epsilon(\theta-z)(q-z^{-1})H_q
\]
gives precisely \(B\) since \(\epsilon\theta=\delta\).
The possible negative power cancels at \(H_q(0)=1\).
Each coefficient of \(B-jH_q\) is \(O_j(1/q)\) by the three displayed branch bounds and \(|h_i|\le2\).
In particular the individual coefficients of \(B\) are uniformly bounded in their index.

The sum of the absolute coefficients through degree \(n\) is a submultiplicative norm on polynomials modulo \(z^{n+1}\).
The coefficient of degree \(i\ge1\) in \(Z\) has size \(O_j(i/q)\), since the convolution with \((1-z^2)^{-1}\) adds at most \(i\) bounded \(H_q\) coefficients, with prefactors \(\delta\) and \(\epsilon\).
Consequently \(\|Z\|_1=O_j(n^2/q)\) in the truncated ring.
This is at most \(1/2\) for \(n\le c'_j\sqrt q\) with \(c'_j\) sufficiently small.
The zero constant makes the truncated geometric inverse exact there and gives \(\|Z/(1-Z)\|_1\le2\|Z\|_1\).
Bounding an individual coefficient of \(BZ/(1-Z)\) uses the maximum coefficient of \(B\), so there is no extra factor of \(n\).
Combining with \(B-jH_q\) proves (1) at all positive indices, with negative indices following by symmetry.
At zero, \(A(0)=2j\) equals the moment mass exactly, while \(K_q(0)=j\) has the Herglotz constant-term normalization.

### Integer principal minors on the logarithmic window

The moment matrix is positive semidefinite since its quadratic form is an integral of the squared modulus of a trigonometric polynomial.
The row-sum bound from (1) puts each principal block of \(G_n\) within operator norm \(e=O_j(n(1+n^2)/q)\) of the corresponding positive block.
Thus all block eigenvalues are at least \(-e\).
Their trace at size \(\ell\) is exactly \(2j\ell\), whence the sum of their absolute values is at most \(2j\ell+2\ell e\).
If the determinant is negative, select one negative eigenvalue, of modulus at most \(e\).
The diagonal excludes \(\ell=1\), and arithmetic-geometric mean on the other \(\ell-1\) absolute eigenvalues gives the claimed determinant bound using \((\ell/(\ell-1))^{\ell-1}\le\exp(1)\).

The permitted \(n=O_j(\log q)\) lies inside the coefficient window for large \(q\).
Uniformly there \(ne\to0\), so replacing \((2j+2e)^{n-1}\) by \(O_j((2j)^n)\) costs a bounded factor.
Since \((2j)^n\le q/(\log q)^5\), the bound on any such negative determinant is \(O_j((\log q)^{-2})<1\) in absolute value eventually.
This contradicts integrality, with the same bound for every choice of a principal block.
The criterion by all principal minors therefore gives positive semidefiniteness and permits zero determinants.

### Saturation and the periodic prefix

For fixed \(L\), (1) and \(A(L)=2j\tau\) give
\[
 \int |v^L-\tau|^2\,d\lambda_q
       =2-2\tau\Re\int v^L\,d\lambda_q=O_{j,L}(1/q).
\]
Multiplying by \(v^k\) does not change modulus, so Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the difference between the \(k+L\) and signed \(k\) moments of \(2j\lambda_q\) by \(O_{j,L}(q^{-1/2})\), uniformly in \(k\).
Choose \(b_j\le c'_j\) so that the two errors from (1) at indices up to \(\lfloor b_j\sqrt q\rfloor\) sum to less than \(1/2\) for large \(q\).
That choice depends only on \(j\).
Increasing the threshold on \(q\) for the fixed \(L\) makes the moment-shift error less than \(1/2\).
The integer \(A(k+L)-\tau A(k)\) therefore vanishes on the whole asserted range, including \(k=0\).

For a purely atomic limiting measure, the L462v36 description writes its transform as a periodic sequence plus the transform of a trigonometric-polynomial density.
Uniqueness of the atomic and absolutely continuous parts makes the latter part zero here.
Finite Fourier inversion represents the periodic sequence by a measure supported on roots of unity of a common order \(L\), giving \(L\)-th moment \(2j\).
Along the subsequence, integrality makes both that index and the fixed initial period agree exactly with their limits eventually.
Iteration of (3) then covers every integer index through \(\lfloor b_j\sqrt q\rfloor\).
If the agreement continued forever, the Taylor series of \(K_q\), after its constant-term adjustment, would be rational with denominator dividing \(1-z^L\).
The actual \(K_q\) has a pole at \(1/\sigma\in(0,1)\), since \(D\) vanishes there and the numerator contribution from \(f/D\) cannot cancel with the regular term by coprimality of \(f,f^*\).
Thus each such finite input must eventually depart from the periodic sequence.
