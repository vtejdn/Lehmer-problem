## The weighted lower bound and the exhibited permutations

At 2026-11-20T17:25 UTC a separate reviewer for Sheng Yin inspected the full statement and proof of C0345v600 and found both conclusions valid.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received the fixed Claim without development dialogue or author explanations.
The review also consulted the existing independent finding on the permutation construction as background to that input.

At each internal vertex the incoming edge and a largest child by leaf count are two distinct entries, including at the vertex adjoining the distinguished root leaf.
Deleting these two leaves a product at least as large as the minimum product obtained by deleting any two entries.
An edge carrying a subtree of size \(s\) has coefficient magnitude at most \(s\exp((N+U)\delta)\) by expanding its cut and dividing by the conjugate power on that edge.
The near-neutral scale bounds this exponential uniformly in the input for fixed \(K\), and the exactly neutral factor is one.
Each light edge has a sibling with at least as many descendant leaves, so successive light subtree sizes along a leaf path at least double.
Summing the charges along such a path gives a convergent series bounded by a constant times \(\sum_{a\ge0}(a+1)2^{-a}\), and summing over leaves accounts for every factor exactly once in the upper estimate.

Writing \(C\ge1\) for the exponential tree and label count base multiplied by the leaf budget base to power \(\lambda\), reconstruction gives \(A_j\le C^j M^{j-2}\).
For \(j\ge3\), \(j/(j-2)\le3\), so for sufficiently small \(m\) the witness lower bound forces \(M\ge\log(2)/(32C^3m)\).
The maximum is taken over individual full tuples and the prescribed local window, so raising its defining ratio to the power \(r-2\) retains the coefficient factor for that very tuple.
For bounded \(r\) the free-output estimate with rational carriers bounds even the numerator uniformly once the minimum power degree exceeds four, whereas the maximum tends to infinity.
Dividing by the largest dilation multiplicity in the fixed-tuple estimate costs \(O(\log r+L)\) in the logarithm, which is negligible relative to \((r-2)L\).
For a neutral sequence the degree-preserving norm representative has the same measure and a neutral conjugate, so applying the count afresh there suffices for the stated exclusion criterion.

In the permutation family, reciprocity provides an embedding of modulus \(e^{-H}\), and the nonzero geometric sum has modulus at most \(n\) there.
After common denominator clearance, the triangle inequality forces some entry of block \(i\) to have magnitude at least \(e^{HL_i}/(pn)\), using the positive integer output.
Deletion of two entries spoils at most two of these block estimates, even if one deleted entry is the output.
The smallest possible sum of the surviving \(n-2\) starts is at least \(p(n-2)(n-3)/2\); every additional retained factor has logarithm at least zero.
The cited house estimate applies to the noncyclotomic monic minimal polynomial and gives \(pH\ge\log(2)/4\).
For \(p<\log n\) the resulting quadratic lower estimate dominates \(\lambda^{-1}n\log n\) uniformly for large \(n\), and for \(p\ge\log n\) the factorial already has logarithm at most \(pn\).
The remaining bounded values of \(n\) require only a constant base depending on \(\lambda\).
This bounds the exhibited permutations only. The external house theorem was used at its recorded statement scope.
