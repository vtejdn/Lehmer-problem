# Inverse pairs and factor measures on polynomial progressions

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:38 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the full [C345 v757](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v757.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing this result and received its fixed text without the development discussion or a previous verdict on it.

Inversion preserves geometric irreducibility in the Laurent ring.
For any common irreducible factor \(g\) of \(G\) and \(G^*\), both \(g,g^*\) divide \(G\).
If associated, their Newton polytope is centrally symmetric up to translation; otherwise their product divides \(G\), and the difference body of the polytope of \(g\) is a summand with its original dimension.
Product polytopes add because exposed initial forms multiply without vanishing.
For a putative summand of dimension at least two, the real linear projections whose restriction to its direction space has rank less than two are contained in the zero set of a nonzero minor polynomial in the entries of the projection.
Thus an arbitrarily small perturbation of the given projection attains rank two there while preserving all the strict inequalities of the projected chain.
Every subset of that chain is still a strictly convex lower chain, because a skipped chord slope is a positively weighted average of a consecutive range of ordered slopes.
Its only other boundary edge is the chord of its endpoints, so there is at most one direction with two parallel edges.
The projected symmetric summand would force two independent such directions by opposite exposed faces, contradicting the actual projected hull.
The same argument covers supports too small to span a projected polygon, which cannot have a two-dimensional summand.

A segment factor is univariate in a primitive Laurent character and hence a binomial over the algebraic closure with nonzero root \(\lambda\).
The saturated direction can be completed to an integral basis, so vanishing of \(G\) on the entire character hypersurface forces each coefficient in the complementary Laurent variables to vanish at \(\lambda\).
The inverse factor forces the same at \(\lambda^{-1}\).
Three points of \(S\) cannot be collinear since their projections have distinct first coordinates and their skipped slopes strictly increase.
Thus every nonempty coset part has at most two terms, and at least two since a single term cannot vanish at a nonzero value.
Removing its monomial yields \(c_0+c_1Z^l\), \(l\ge1\), from which \(\lambda^{2l}=1\) follows for coincident as well as distinct reciprocal roots.
This proves the assertion about all common hypersurfaces without any requirement that inversion fix each support point.

For a nontorsion inverse pair in a nonzero specialization the base \(z\) is algebraic and its coordinate powers lie on \(G=G^*=0\).
A common hypersurface gives \(w\cdot u=0\) immediately since \(z^{w\cdot u}\) is torsion there.
Every remaining component has codimension at least two and is defined over the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q\).
The [bounded-character theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#source) applies to these untwisted powers of a single nontorsion element and provides an integer character of bounded norm taking value one.
Finitely many components of each of finitely many polynomials yield a finite list of annihilating vectors.
The argument detects inverse roots even when they belong to different factors.

For homogeneous powers the binomial expansion gives exactly the direction stated, and division of \(B\cdot u\) by the positive \(h b^D\) gives a nonzero polynomial in \(n/b\), since the different vector components index distinct powers.
Its nonnegative rational zeros over the finite list suffice, with no dependence on the scale of \(n,b\) or on \(h\).
For a general fixed polynomial, the integral Taylor coefficients \(f^{(k)}(n)/k!\) have degree precisely \(D-k\).
For large \(n\), successive absolute direction ratios are \(O_f(b/n)\), making the first nonzero coefficient of any candidate relation dominate the remaining geometric tail uniformly once \(n>A(1+b)\).
The polynomial-translate conclusion is restricted to that range.
At all allowed nonexceptional parameters the real coefficients put the inverse of every unit-circle root in the same polynomial, so all such roots are torsion.
The primitive irreducible nonmonic and content cases each cost at least \(\log2\); a remaining noncyclotomic monic factor is nonreciprocal and obeys [Smyth's bound](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
Additivity of logarithmic measure on products of integer Laurent polynomials therefore gives the lower bound for each positive factor.

At a fixed exceptional homogeneous ratio \(x/y\), the variables have the form \(n=mx,\ b=my\) with integer \(m\ge1\) even at \(0/1\).
The specialized polynomial is a monomial times a dilation of one of finitely many base polynomials, with exponent \(hm^D\).
For a nontorsion unit root \(z\) with \(z^l=\beta\), put \(s_0=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q(\beta)]\).
The norm \(\gamma\) is a unit and \(\gamma^l=\beta^{s_0}\), so \(h(\gamma)=s_0h(z)>0\) and \(m(\gamma)\le m(z)\).
Its degree is bounded by the degree of a base polynomial, independently of the dilation.
The nonzero measures of units of that bounded degree have a positive lower bound by bounded-coefficient finiteness, completing the all-slope assertion at \(X^D\).
For the cubic example multiplication successively modulo \(X^2-3X+1\) gives coefficient pairs \((3,-1),(8,-3),(21,-8),(55,-21),(144,-55),(377,-144),(987,-377)\) through \(X^8\), verifying the asserted factor and actual inverse pair.
Any rational quotient of distinct powers of its exterior root would be fixed by quadratic conjugation while also being sent to its reciprocal, impossible for a positive nontrivial power of that root.
Thus the rational dependence among three distinct powers has all coefficients nonzero and gives the indicated unrestricted-coefficient obstruction to excluding inverse pairs.
