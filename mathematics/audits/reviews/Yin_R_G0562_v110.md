## Norm estimates, product doubling and Chang's source argument

At 2026-11-17T01:18 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined C345v110 as submitted at 2026-11-17T01:10, its dependency interfaces and the current Cha03-U001.
The reviewer had no role in developing C345v110 or Cha03-U001 and received their fixed texts without proof-development dialogue or a previous verdict.
The reviewer contributed to the prior C345v106 and checked its two uses here.
The source inspection covered printed pp. 945–949 of [Chang's retained published edition](../../literature/pdf/Cha03--published.pdf), including the typeset displays in Propositions 6, 8, 9 and 11 and the intervening statement of Freiman's theorem.

**Valuation comparison.**
Proposition 6 (1.7) gives \(c_h=2h^2-h\) for the squared \(L^{2h}\) norm at one prime, and Proposition 8 (1.15) gives \(c_h^R\) at \(R\) primes.
A nonzero term in the constant Fourier coefficient of \(B^h\bar B^h\) must have at least two positions at its minimum valuation by reduction of its signed sum modulo \(p\).
Taking absolute values of the input coefficients increases the even moment by the triangle inequality on that coefficient sum.
For the resulting nonnegative weights, summing over all pairs and forgetting the minimum condition gives an upper bound, regardless of ties.
At fixed shared valuation \(j\), each of the \(\binom{2h}{2}\) pair types contributes at most \(\int |B_j|^2 |B|^{2h-2}\), even when the constrained slots are on the same side.
Hölder and division when \(\|B\|_{2h}>0\) prove the non-strict bound claimed by the source unit.
In particular \(c_3=15\) is iterated on the squared sixth norm before taking its square root.

There are several reasons to use this count when reading the published displays.
The first equality on p. 946 overcounts when more than two slots tie, as identified in Cha03-U001.
A single nonzero valuation component makes its right-hand side \(c_h\) times its left-hand side.
In addition, the middle condition in (1.11) on p. 945 need not make the integral vanish as printed.
For \(p=2,h=2\), \(2+2=1+3\) has sorted valuations \((1,1;0,0)\), satisfying \(j_1\ne j_{h+1}\le\min(j_2,j_{h+2})\) with a tie on the right.
The unique-minimum criterion used above and in the submitted proof is sufficient.
Proposition 9 (1.16) prints a strict inequality, whereas (1.15) and the coordinate projection establish a non-strict one, with equality on a singleton of dimension zero.
Cha03-U001 and C345v110 use only the non-strict bounds.

**Sixth moments and arithmetic decay.**
On each terminal fiber of joint valuations, a nonzero weighted sextuple has distinct slots for its largest entry \(u\) and another entry \(v\ge u/5\).
The positive integer in the largest slot equals a signed sum of the five others.
There are at most 30 ordered slot pairs, and for fixed entries the absolute value of the requisite Fourier coefficient of the four remaining factors is bounded by \(\int|B_E|^4\).
The interval \([u/5,5u]\) meets at most seven standard dyadic intervals, so symmetry and \(2|b_u b_v|\le |b_u|^2+|b_v|^2\) bound the pair sum by \(7W\sum_{e\in E}|b_e|^2\).
Dividing by the fourth power of the sixth norm if it is positive verifies the terminal squared estimate and hence the factor \(15^{R/2}\sqrt W\) in (2).

For \(Z=de/(d,e)^2\), its unselected prime factors are positive integer powers or one.
Thus \(Z\) dominates both \(2^{|j(d)-j(e)|-1}\) and \(2^{\sum_p|v_p(d)-v_p(e)|}\).
Multiplying their inverse fourth powers yields the stated joint decay of \(Z^{-1/2}\).
Each joint cell costs at most \(W\), and each coordinate costs at most \(\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^{-|k|/4}\), independently of the chosen primes.
The Möbius inverse applied to positive Fourier coefficients has entries \(-2\mu(d/u)u/d\), giving the absolute Gram bound \(4\zeta(2)/Z\).
The symmetric Schur estimate and Parseval therefore control the inverse norm by \(C^{R+1}\sqrt W\).

In a harmonic band \([H,2H)\), each coincidence \(kd=le\) has multipliers \(k=ut,l=vt\) for the reduced ratio \(e/d=u/v\).
There are at most \(2H/\max(u,v)\) choices and each has weight at most \(H^{-2}\), including the diagonal.
The same row bound now gives a band \(L^2\) norm \(O(C^{R+1}\sqrt{W/H}\,\|b\|_2)\).
The sum of the band weights is at most one, so the sixth norm is bounded by the estimate for the undilated exponential sum.
Hölder interpolation gives the power \(H^{-1/8}\) in the fourth norm, which sums over \(H=2^s\).
The real part with a minus sign has the circle logarithm's coefficient \(-1/(2k)\) at each individual positive harmonic and has zero mean.
Uniqueness of the Fourier coefficients identifies the \(L^4\) limit with \(L\).
Finally \(\|L\|_1\ge\|L\|_2^3/\|L\|_4^2\) supplies \(W^{-3/2-1}=W^{-5/2}\), with all dimension-dependent factors absorbed in an absolute base raised to \(R+1\).
The all-zero coefficient case satisfies the same comparison directly.

**Dimension and product doubling.**
On pp. 947–948 Chang defines the affine dimension of the finite prime-exponent vectors and selects an injective projection onto that many prime coordinates.
The rational rank of the differences equals their real dimension by matrix rank over \(\mathbb Q\).
Positive rational dilation translates the vectors, so the asserted multiplicative coset hypothesis suffices regardless of the ambient primes.
Page 948 states Freiman's result with \(|2A_1|<\alpha|A_1|\), \(\alpha<\sqrt{|A_1|}\), an ambient additive subgroup of the reals, and at most \(\alpha\) progression generators in the subgroup.
For \(A_1=\log D\) and subgroup \(\log\mathbb Q_{>0}\), the sumset has size \(|DD|\), and exponentiating the progression gives the required affine-dimension bound.
The typography \(|A|^2\) in Proposition 11 on p. 949 conflicts with this application, while Proposition 12 prints \(|A^2|\).
The claim uses the valid non-strict dimension conclusion under both strict hypotheses by taking \(\alpha=K+1\) and treating \(n=|D|\le\alpha^2\) separately.

An independent geometric count also verifies this inference.
For \(n\) exponent vectors of affine dimension \(t\ge1\), triangulate their convex hull with all \(n\) points as vertices.
One can start with a spanning simplex and insert each remaining point.
For an exterior point, coning the visible boundary facets joins it to at least \(t\) old vertices and keeps the previous edges.
For a point in the relative interior of an existing face of positive dimension, subdividing the simplices containing that face joins it to all vertices of a containing \(t\)-simplex.
Only subdivision of an edge can remove an old edge, in which case exactly that edge is removed.
Thus each insertion adds at least \(t\) net edges.
There are at least \(tn-t(t+1)/2\) edges at the end.
Their midpoints are all distinct and none is an existing vertex, since faces in a geometric triangulation meet only in common faces.
Twice the vertices and twice the midpoints are elements of the vector sumset, giving
\[
        |DD|\ \ge\ (t+1)n-t(t+1)/2.
\]
In particular \(t\le2(K-1)\) when \(|DD|\le Kn\), using \(n\ge t+1\).
It also gives \(t\le\alpha\) under the source's strict hypotheses, since the lower-bound ratio \(t+1-t(t+1)/(2n)\) is increasing for \(0\le t\le n-1\) and exceeds \(\alpha\) at \(t=\alpha\) when \(1<\alpha<\sqrt n\).
Dimension zero is immediate.
This check requires no estimate on the size of a containing progression.

**Shifts and scope.**
The integer signed binomial product, when polynomial, has unit leading coefficient in absolute value and only circle roots.
Its logarithmic integral vanishes, and \(\sum |b_d|\le\sum b_d^2\).
C345v90 thus supplies the quantitative lower bound stated in the proof, uniformly over complex \(|a|\ge1\).
At fixed \(R,W\), measure at most one bounds the integer mass \(S\).
Clearing the product of all negative binomial powers preserves the logarithmic measure and gives a nonzero integer polynomial with at most \(2^{S+1}\) terms when \(a\) is integral.
Its nonvanishing follows by evaluating the original shift at a root of the nonconstant parent.
DS17-U001 applies at its retained univariate statement scope and gives the asserted positive gap.
The norm lower bound applies uniformly to every choice of the primes, so a bounded-cost subsequence would contradict \(S\to\infty\), both for minimum occupancy and for product doubling.
The reviewer found no defect in C345v110 or in the inequalities used from Cha03-U001.
The two additional qualifications above concern the published source displays.
The review checked the source unit's actual statements and arguments and the other fixed inputs at their consumer interfaces.
