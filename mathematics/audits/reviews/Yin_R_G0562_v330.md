## Independent review of both constructions

At 2026-11-18T01:32 UTC an agent outside proof development independently reviewed the [v330 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v330.md#decision-bounded-energy-band-dips-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and its scope, without development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or an earlier finding on v330.
The complete cyclotomic part satisfies (1) with its full multiplicities, and its removal from an integer parent gives (2).
For the second family, both inequalities in (4) hold with absolute constants for every choice and partition of the distinct primes.

### Torsion sets and their multiplicities

A minimal vanishing subsum of \(u\) nonzero rational multiples of roots of unity has all root ratios of order dividing the product of the primes at most \(u\).
I checked the argument in the [fixed rational-relation bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0262_v2.md#minimal-vanishing-order).
After division by a base root, write \(M\) for the order generated by the ratios.
If \(p^a\Vert M\) with \(a\ge2\), the degree \(p\) extension over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{M/p})\) forces each residue-group sum to vanish separately.
Minimality then puts every normalized root in the zero group, contradicting the occurrence of \(p^a\).
For squarefree \(M\), the degree \(p-1\) extension over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{M/p})\) forces all \(p\) group coefficients to agree.
A zero common value would again put all terms in one group, so every group is occupied and \(p\le u\).
Rational coefficient denominators are scalars in these fields and can also be cleared by one common nonzero integer.
Repeated root values and negative rational coefficients are allowed.

For a polynomial with \(u\le s\) distinct exponents, any torsion zero partitions its terms into minimal vanishing subsums.
Choose the first index in each block as its base.
The number of partitions is at most \(\mathcal B_s\), and there are at most \(p_s^s\) assignments of power ratios to all indices.
Requiring both the coefficient sums to vanish and the power equations to be consistent ensures that every point of a retained pattern actually is a zero.
At least one exponent difference is nonzero since each block has two or more distinct indices.
For one solution \(z_0\), division of any other solution by \(z_0\) reduces the system to the common kernel of the powers, exactly \(\mu_g\) by Bézout for the signed differences.
The nonzero power of \(z_0\) prescribed by a root of unity makes \(z_0\) torsion.
Thus the entire coset \(z_0\mu_g\) belongs to the torsion zero set, and every torsion zero belongs to a retained coset.
Intersecting any finite list of these cosets gives either the empty set or another full coset by the same kernel calculation.

The monic squarefree polynomial of one coset is \(X^g-\xi\), and the \(L^1\) norm of its real logarithmic modulus is \(c_h\) by rotation and invariance of circle measure under the \(g\)-th power.
Inclusion–exclusion on root indicators counts every root of a union once, even for nested or repeated cosets.
Applying that identity to the sum of the root logarithms gives (7) almost everywhere, with at most \(2^T-1\) nonempty subfamilies in the triangle bound.
The finitely many zeros of the binomials can be omitted in this identity, including zeros of any terms that occur with negative sign.
All the individual root logarithms are integrable.

At \(\alpha\ne0\), the change from ordinary derivatives through order \(r-1\) to Euler derivatives is triangular with diagonal \(\alpha^j\) at order \(j\).
For a parent with at least two terms each Euler power is nonzero, since some exponent \(n_i\ne0\) retains the nonzero coefficient \(n_i^j a_i\) at every positive order.
It has at most \(s\) terms even if a constant term disappears.
Intersecting its torsion zero sets for \(0\le j<r\) therefore expresses the multiplicity-\(\ge r\) layer as a union of at most \(T_s^r\) cosets.
Vanishing for \(j=0,\ldots,u-1\) would annihilate the nonzero vector \((a_i\alpha^{n_i})_i\) by the Vandermonde matrix on the distinct integer exponents.
Thus all layers above \(s-1\) are empty.
Each torsion root occurs in the layer product exactly as often as its multiplicity in \(P\), and rationality includes its complete cyclotomic conjugacy class with the same multiplicity.
The monic product is therefore \(C_P\), with monomial factors omitted.
Summing (7) over the layers gives (8), including \(K_1=0\).

### The sparse integer quotient

Let \(H\) denote the largest absolute coefficient and \(u\) the actual number of terms.
In the one-variable identity-map case of the [regularization estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#regularity), the normalized polynomial \(P/H\) has coefficient maximum one and the identity map has no nonzero lattice relation.
For \(u\ge2\), the source parameters \(\alpha=1/2\), \(\epsilon=1\) yield \(24u^2\) for the logarithmic smoothing cost.
Restoring \(H\) gives \(m(P)\ge\log H-24u^2\), since \(\tfrac12\log(|P|^2+H^2)\ge\log H\).
Here only Proposition 4.10 enters at its [recorded applicability scope](../literature/BGMP24/Yin_U001.md#bgmp24-u001).
Integrality gives \(H\ge1\), so \(\int\log^+|P|\le\log(sH)\) and (9) follows.
For a single integer monomial the negative part vanishes.
Factoring out the monic \(C_P\) gives an integer Laurent quotient after a monomial shift and monic polynomial division.
The mean of \(\log|C_P|\) is zero, so its positive part has integral \(\|\log|C_P|\|_1/2\).
Applying \((-\log|P|+\log|C_P|)_+\le\log^-|P|+\log^+|C_P|\) away from the finitely many circle zeros gives (2).
Taking the coefficient maximum can only decrease this negative part.

### Energy for the prime-partition family

The monic gcd removes the smaller multiplicity at each cyclotomic index, leaving coprime monic cyclotomic products \(A,B\), each of logarithmic mean zero.
Jensen's formula in \(Y\) gives \(\int\max(\log|A|,\log|B|)=\tfrac12\|\log|A|-\log|B|\|_1\).
In the difference the common gcd cancels almost everywhere.
The \(L^2\) cosine series of \(\log|1-e^{iat}|\) then gives (11) by summing over the common multiples of \(a\) and \(b\).
For \(a=N/p\), \(b=N/q\), with \(p\ne q\), its gcd factor is \(1/(pq)\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \((\sum_{p\mid N}1/p)^2\le 2k\sum_{j\ge2}j^{-2}\), so the squared norm is \(O(k)\) for either choice of signs.
These checks recover the upper bound and identities used from the [original construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v328.md#example).

Uniform \(t\) corresponds to independent uniform choices of \(J\bmod N\) and \(x\in[0,1]\).
Conditional on the same \(x\), the CRT makes \(J\bmod p\) mutually independent and uniform.
For \(1/4\le x\le3/4\) the distance of \((r+x)/p\) from an endpoint is at least \((\min(r,p-1-r)+1/4)/p\).
The two endpoint residues contribute \(O((\log p)^4/p)\) to the fourth moment of the negative part.
For all remaining residues the decreasing function \(\log^4(1/y)\) bounds the corresponding normalized sum by an absolute multiple of its integral \(24\).
Together with the bounded positive part this gives a uniform fourth moment.
For \(p\ge3\) both first and second nonconstant Fourier modes have zero grid mean at every \(x\), giving mean zero and variance \(1/2\) for \(C_p\).
For \(h(c)=\tfrac12\log(2-2c)\), the derivative has constant sign and \(|h'|\ge1/4\) on \([-1,1)\).
Applying this to two independent copies of \(C_p\) gives \(\operatorname{Var}(h(C_p))\ge1/32\).
The prime \(2\) can occur just once.

For a conditionally independent copy \(Z'\), write \(S=Z-Z'=\sum_p W_p\) with \(W_p=\epsilon_p(H_p-H'_p)\).
These independent centered variables have uniformly bounded fourth moments and total variance at least \(2(2k-1)/32\) for \(k\ge1\).
In \(\mathbb E S^4\) only \(\sum_p\mathbb E W_p^4\) and \(6\sum_{p<q}\mathbb E W_p^2\,\mathbb E W_q^2\) survive, giving \(O(k^2)\).
Logarithmic convexity of \(L^p\) norms yields \(\mathbb E|S|\ge(\mathbb ES^2)^{3/2}/(\mathbb ES^4)^{1/2}\).
Consequently \(\mathbb E|Z|\ge\tfrac12\mathbb E|S|\gg\sqrt{k}\) uniformly on the central half of the \(x\) interval, regardless of the conditional mean of \(Z\).
Integrating on that interval and applying (10) proves the lower bound in (4).

Finally every divisor of squarefree \(N\) has at most \(2^{2k}\) divisors, and Möbius inversion gives at most that many binomial logarithms in \(\log|\Phi_t|\), each of norm \(c_h\).
There are at most \(2^{2k}\) possible \(t\), each with multiplicity at most \(k\) in \(A\), giving the stated bound \(k2^{4k}c_h\).
Bounded energy forces bounded \(k\) by (4), which now bounds the negative logarithmic mean throughout this family.
In the reversal example \(|A^*|=|A|\) on the circle and Jensen gives the asserted exact energy.
The sparse bound applies to removal of the complete cyclotomic part and leaves the question for general coprime rows at bounded independent measure open.
