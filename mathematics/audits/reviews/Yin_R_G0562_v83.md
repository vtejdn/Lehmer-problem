## Field coordinates, fiber averages and binomial clearing

For \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)\) and \(s=n/q\), the masking factors in \(D_q(X^s)\) are exactly \(1-X^{n/p}\) for \(p\mid n\).
If an \(n\)-th root has proper order then that order divides some \(n/p\), so the mask kills its value.
On primitive roots its modulus is at most \(2^r\), giving a full-grid mean at most \(2^r B\varphi(n)/n\le2^r B\).
For the cyclic coefficient function of the masked polynomial, the normalized Fourier coefficient is \(1/n\) times the polynomial evaluated at the inverse grid character.
Summing the moduli therefore gives exactly the full-grid average.
The integer coefficients permit Sanders's signed coset expansion with a bound at \(\max(1,2^rB)\), also for a vanishing mean.
At \(\zeta_n\) a coset of size greater than one sums to zero since the character is faithful and nontrivial on its subgroup.
The remaining singleton cosets supply at most \(K(B,r)\) signed powers.

The primitive element \(\zeta_n\) generates its field over \(\mathbb Q(y)\) for \(y=\zeta_n^s\), and the ratio of cyclotomic degrees is \(s\), including \(s=1\).
Consequently \(1,\zeta_n,\ldots,\zeta_n^{s-1}\) is a basis over that field.
Writing each singleton exponent as \(j+sa\) shows that the signed sum has at most \(K(B,r)\) nonzero basis coordinates.
These coordinates are \(D_q(y)P_j(y)\), and \(D_q(y)\ne0\) since \(y\) has order \(q\).
This count holds at arbitrary input degree.
If an integral block vanishes at \(y\) but is nonzero, it has degree at least \(\varphi(q)\).
Different blocks have disjoint exponent classes, so \(\deg P\ge j+s\deg P_j\), whence \(j\le H\) for each such additional block.
The same degree comparison gives \(\deg P_j\le\varphi(q)+H\) for every nonzero block.

For \(x^s=v\) with \(v\) primitive of order \(q\), \(x^n=1\).
If \(p^a\Vert n\), the \(p\)-part of \(s\) is \(p^{a-1}\), so the requirement \(p\mid\operatorname{ord}(v)\) forces \(p^a\mid\operatorname{ord}(x)\).
Thus all \(s\) roots in every fiber are primitive, and the fibers partition the primitive \(n\)-th roots.
The fiber is a coset of the \(s\)-th roots of unity, and averaging \(x^{i-j}\) on it gives zero for \(0\le i,j<s\) with \(i\ne j\) and one for \(i=j\).
This yields the asserted recovery formula and \(U_q(P_j)\le U_n(P)\) after the triangle inequality and averaging.

At \(q=p\), \(D_q(Y)=1-Y\) has degree \(q-\varphi(q)\).
At \(q=pt\), \(D_q(Y)=(1-Y^p)(1-Y^t)\) has degree \(q-\varphi(q)+1\).
Thus \(\deg G_j\le q+H+1\) and its full-grid mean is at most \(4B\).
The cyclic representative has a bounded signed coset expansion by the same Fourier convention.
A coset of residues modulo \(l\mid q\) in the interval \(0,\ldots,q-1\) gives \(Y^u(1-Y^q)/(1-Y^l)\), where \(0\le u<l\).
Clearing one \(1-Y^l\) per progression therefore bounds the term count uniformly, as in C345v81.
At most \(H+2\) original exponent positions occur from \(q\) through \(q+H+1\).
The discrepancy from the ordinary cyclic representative is a sum of terms \(a_h(Y^h-Y^{h\bmod q})\) over these positions, including when \(H\ge q\).
This bounds the term count even for arbitrarily large coefficients at those positions.
Multiplication by the same product of binomials preserves a bound on this count.

There are at most \(K(B,r)+H+1\) nonzero blocks, so the common product in the proof has boundedly many binomial factors.
Each block receives its own clearing factors once and the other factors can multiply its term count by at most two per factor.
The substitution \(Y=X^s\) and shift \(X^j\) preserve a block's term count.
All added binomials have positive exponent and measure one, so the resulting nonzero integer multiple has exactly \(M(P)\).
The Dobrowolski–Smyth theorem now gives the uniform positive logarithmic gap, including monomials and polynomials with nonunit content.
For general fixed \(r\) the same common-product argument uses the hypothesized squarefree result on the bounded set of blocks.
The unconditional conclusion for arbitrary \(r\) is the reduction and block bound.
Finally \(\Phi_nQ\) has degree \(\varphi(n)+\deg Q\), zero primitive values, and measure \(M(Q)\) for every nonzero integer polynomial \(Q\).
Thus an unbounded degree allowance beyond \(\varphi(n)\) at prime orders includes the whole unrestricted measure spectrum, and a gap for this enlarged class would imply the unrestricted gap.
