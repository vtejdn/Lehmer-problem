## Review of the multiplier theorem

The determinant coordinates, the uniform norm estimate, the prime-exponent assertion, the invariant factor slopes and the two-sign gcd bound hold under the hypotheses in the reviewed statement.
I checked that the revised opening explicitly requires a nontorsion quotient for **every** pair of distinct roots.
This quantifier is necessary for the asserted separability at all powers.
For example, \((X^2-3X+1)(X^2+3X+1)\) has a pair \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) of nontorsion quotient, but \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\) have the same even powers.
The pairwise hypothesis also makes the generic weight argument below apply to different inverse pairs.

### Coordinates and angle

For two graph pairs, multiplying each graph by the other pair's second entry and subtracting produces their determinant at \(X^n\).
The derivative of \(X^n\) is nonzero at every root of \(G\), so the order of that determinant at the corresponding powered root is at least two.
Monic division then gives integral coefficients for each quotient when the row is integral.
Reversal of the two numerators of the coordinate formulas at width \(2m+k\) gives their signs \(\sigma,-\sigma\), since \(Q_n^2\) is reciprocal at width \(2m\).
For a combination \(A=A_+U+A_-V\), expansion gives \(B=a_nU\) and \(C=a_nV\).
These identities and the product rule for signed graphs prove injectivity, surjectivity over \(\mathbb Q\), and the coefficientwise congruence for the integral image once \(a_n\ne0\).
At a sufficiently large prime \(p\), the two primitive reductions are nonzero multiples of the reciprocal \(\bar G=\bar Q_p\) by the good-prime rank formula.
The coefficient \(2t_+t_-\) in the reduced determinant is then a unit modulo \(p\).

In a cofactor the contribution \(m v/2\) from a column at a site of weight \(v\) is independent of that column.
Sorting the remaining contributions \(|v||l-m/2|\) forces deletion of the central column, with complementary columns \(l,m-l\) at the unique assigned site for each distance.
In this pair the maximizing powers of the site are the same, including their torsion phases.
After removal of the nonzero value multipliers, the derivative coefficients are \(Nq,1+Nq\) in some order for the same selected exponent \(q\).
The determinant of the leading pair is nonzero for each site and each sign.
Both cofactor polynomials thus have a single leading power \(Y^{m/2}\) at a generic weight.
In the exterior product of multiplication columns the leading coefficient is nonzero because shifting by \(Y^{m/2}\) preserves independence of the two complementary reversal bases together.
The two leading terms in the determinant polynomial have identical scalar factors and add in characteristic zero.
Continuity of the finite support maxima gives the same weight identities at the archimedean vector, even when several absolute values tie or equal one.

After fixing the torsion class, different collected exponent vectors give algebraic bases with nontorsion ratios, and every surviving coefficient is polynomial in \(N\).
Xiao's placewise estimate therefore applies coordinate by coordinate, including to the exterior product and the determinant polynomial.
The common scalars relating specialized cofactors to primitive rows cancel in each normalized ratio.
The product of the singular values of the matrix of normalized columns is \(\exp(o(n))\), and every singular value is bounded above by a constant depending only on the fixed dimensions.
This gives the uniform lower norm comparison, including multipliers chosen after \(n\).
The coefficient one-norm upper bound follows from convolution and the triangle inequality.
Finally \(M(Q_n^2)=\exp(2nS)\) and \(\|Q_n^2\|_1/M(Q_n^2)\) lies between \(1\) and \(2^{2m}\).
The determinant norm calculation proves both eventual nonvanishing and the asserted logarithm of \(|a_n|\).

### Quotient and shortest row

The covolumes of the image and its saturation have slopes \((k+1)C_0\) and \(C_0+kS\), respectively, so the index has slope \(kL\).
The exponent of the quotient divides \(|a_n|\), of slope \(2L\).
The content lemma makes each individual multiplication block saturated in the coefficient lattice, hence its columns remain independent over every prime field.
This gives at least the dimension of the larger block in initial unit Smith factors, by applying the divisibility order at each prime.
For \(k=2j\) the sum of the remaining \(j\) normalized logarithms tends to \(2jL\), with upper limit \(2L\) separately, so each tends to \(2L\).
Multiplication by \(1+Y\) injects this quotient into the one at \(2j+1\) because monic division detects integrality in both unique coordinates.
At a prime \(\ell\), counting the \(\ell\)-torsion in \(\ell^{e-1}D\) for every \(e\ge1\) gives the descending invariant-factor divisibilities for a subgroup \(D\).
Thus the largest \(j\) odd-width factors also have slope \(2L\) and subtraction from the total gives \(L\) for the remaining possible factor.
This includes the trivial quotient at \(k=0\) and the cyclic quotient at \(k=1\).

In the plane, completing the primitive first column to a lattice basis makes the index the gcd of the second column's other coordinates.
Its first coordinate is a unit modulo that gcd because the second column is itself primitive.
This gives exactly the stated congruence lattice for the numerator pair \((s,t)\).
The two generator heights have slope \(C_0\), so the logarithm of the shortest norm is \(nC_0+\log\mu_\sigma-\log J_\sigma+o(n)\).
Here \(C_0-L=3h\), and the Salem substitution \(C_0=(6d-4)h\) gives \(L=(6d-7)h\).
The numerator-rank input applies for \(k+1\le d/2\), in particular to both plane columns and all their nonzero rational combinations when \(d\ge4\).
The resulting approximation formula leaves the asymptotic behavior of \(\mu_\sigma\) open.
For \(D=\gcd(J_+,J_-)>1\), eliminating \(A_-\) from the two unit proportionalities gives \(A_+[v(1+Y)^2-w(1-Y)^2]=0\) modulo \(D\).
The content lemma permits cancellation of primitive \(A_+\) even at composite \(D\), giving \(v=w\) and \(4v=0\) modulo \(D\).
Since \(v\) is a unit, \(D\mid4\), also valid at \(D=1\).
