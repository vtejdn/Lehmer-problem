## The embedded quadratic extension, lifting and the parity exception

At 2026-11-24T21:55 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v48.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v48.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v48.
The extension and relative-unit-line classification, lifting of base automorphisms, unrestricted zero-infimum equivalence and totally real trace count hold in their stated ranges.

For each member \(x\), \(X^2-(x+x^{-1})X+1\) is the quadratic minimum over the embedded base \(B\), so its nonidentity relative automorphism inverts \(x\).
For independent \(x,y\), their compositum over this same \(B\) has absolute degree at most \(4n\), and each element has absolute degree \(2n\).
The rank-two absolute height product gives \(m(x)m(y)\gg n/(\log(12n))^{212}\) after multiplication by \(4n^2\).
For fixed \(M\) the lower bound eventually exceeds \(M^2\), independently of \(B\)'s signature or normality.
Two different quadratic extensions would meet precisely in \(B\).
Any dependence would force a nonzero power of \(x\) to equal a nonzero signed power of \(y\), hence to belong to \(B\), after removing a possible torsion factor by powering.
The inverter would fix and invert that common power, contradicting nontorsion.
Thus all members generate the same embedded \(K\), and bounded measures put them on one rational line modulo torsion.

The group of norm-one units over \(B\) is a subgroup of the finitely generated unit group.
In its free quotient the rational line meets the lattice in the multiples of a primitive vector, giving the exact twists \(\xi\epsilon^k\) within the relative group.
For a twist \(v\) generating \(K\), \(v+v^{-1}\in B\) and \([K:\mathbb Q(v+v^{-1})]\le2\), so the trace generates \(B\).
The degree filter in (2) and the height formula \(m(v)=2n|k|h(\epsilon)\) therefore give precisely the required set.
If some member \(x=\omega\epsilon^s\) has full degree in all positive powers, raising \((\xi\epsilon^k)^j\) to \(|s|\,|\mu_1|\) identifies it, up to inversion, with a nonzero power of \(x\).
Hence every such twist and all its positive powers generate \(K\).
The later unrestricted selection uses primitivity in the full unit group, as supplied by its separate saturation input.

Extend any rational automorphism of \(B\) to the algebraic closure.
The image of \(x\) has unchanged measure and its trace generates \(B\) as the same embedded field, so uniqueness forces the image of \(K\) to equal \(K\).
Restriction from the subgroup preserving \(B\) is therefore onto \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(B)\).
For an all-power reciprocal generator, distinct conjugates cannot have torsion quotient.
The cited reciprocal-field theorem gives exactly the identity and inverter on \(K\) at sufficiently large \(n\) with \(M\) fixed.
Both fix \(B\) pointwise, proving triviality of its rational automorphism group.
If the unrestricted infimum vanishes, full-unit-group saturation and norm descent give primitive reciprocal all-power generators of positive measures tending to zero and degrees tending to infinity.
The preceding restriction therefore applies to their original trace fields for all sufficiently late indices.
The reverse implication follows from these units' integral minima, and this equivalence leaves existence of an unrestricted shrinking sequence open.

For the totally real calculation, \(t\) is assumed to generate \(B\).
No embedding sends \(t\) to \(2\) or \(-2\) at \(n>1\), and \(H(t)>0\) supplies an exterior place.
If all places were exterior, a quadratic root \(x\) would be totally real.
The product formula gives \(h(x)=H(t)/(2n)\) even if \(x\in B\), since the absolute radial logarithm at each embedding is the indicated \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) and the sum of the signed log moduli of a unit is zero.
The same equality follows by counting both inverse roots when the extension is quadratic.
The fixed positive absolute height bound for a totally real nontorsion element contradicts \(H(t)\le M\) for large \(n\).
Thus there is an interior place as well, at which \(t^2-4\) is negative.
This gives the quadratic extension, and counting both roots at each place gives \(m(x)=H(t)\).
The exterior place supplies a real embedding of \(K\), forcing its roots of unity, and the relative norm-one torsion, to be precisely \(\{\pm1\}\).
Since \(x=\pm\epsilon^s\) generates \(K\), \(\epsilon\) generates \(K\) and its trace \(t_0\) generates \(B\).
Its cost is positive and at most \(M\).

Outside the unit circle every conjugate of \(\epsilon\) is real, by its real trace and the quadratic equation.
If a quotient of two distinct conjugates is torsion, move the numerator to an exterior conjugate of maximal modulus by a single Galois automorphism.
The denominator then is real of the same modulus and different from it, so their quotient is \(-1\), which is itself Galois fixed.
It follows that powers lose degree precisely at even exponents when \(-\epsilon\) is a conjugate.
The corresponding condition for \(t_0\) is equivalent, since an embedding sending \(t_0\) to \(-t_0\) has the two possible lifts with values \(-\epsilon,-\epsilon^{-1}\).
Inversion restricts nontrivially to every nonzero power field, which has degree two over its own trace subfield.
That trace subfield is contained in \(B\), so it equals \(B\) exactly when the powered generator still generates \(K\).

The exceptional places agree for every \(\pm T_k(t_0)\), since raising a nonzero root to a positive power preserves whether its modulus differs from one.
Their costs are \(kH(t_0)\), giving strict separation for different \(k\), and the two signs at fixed \(k\) are distinct at any exterior embedding.
Writing \(J=\lfloor M/H(t_0)\rfloor\), the displayed set therefore has \(2J\) elements if \(-t_0\) is not conjugate and \(2\lceil J/2\rceil\) otherwise.
An automorphism of \(B\) preserves \(H(t_0)\), hence can send \(t_0\) only to \(t_0\) or \(-t_0\).
If the latter is a conjugate it generates the same embedded field, giving the sign automorphism.
With one exceptional place its unique absolute value cannot be attained by a distinct negative conjugate, and the parity restriction disappears.
