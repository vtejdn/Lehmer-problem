At 2026-11-21T05:29 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed [C345 v769](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer received the fixed argument without participating in its development and requested a definition-level clarification about the fixed underlying divisor during review.
The revised metric paragraph supplies that approximation, and the reviewer checked it directly.

The kernel of \((i,j)\mapsto x^i y^j\) modulo roots of unity is generated by the primitive vector \((b/g,-a/g)\), since a nonzero integral power of \(z\) cannot be torsion.
This excludes any fixed finite set along the sequence, including singularities of the torus curve.
The same relation proves genericity after conjugation into a fixed absolute component, since all images of a fixed algebraic point are finite in number.
Rational irreducibility makes the absolute components conjugate and rules out a monomial constant on any one of them by the common exponent difference of conjugate binomials.
If \(F\) divided a nonempty proper subsum, the containment of Newton polygons would force the complementary factor to be a monomial with zero exponent, by widths and boundedness.
A shared absolute component would give a rational common factor by conjugation, so the union of intersections with all proper subsums is finite.
The gap bound applies to either end of a consecutive cut over a field containing both translations and \(z\), using \(|\xi|_v=|\eta|_v=1\) even at the finite places.
The product formula yields the factor \(h(z)\) with its absolute normalization.
Together with rank two, which bounds \(W/N\) above and away from zero, this gives the required uniform upper bound.

The orders of the coordinate functions are integral vectors of degree zero without a rational linear dependence, and hence also without a real linear dependence.
Every nonzero direction therefore gives a real divisor of positive degree after taking the negative part.
For a rational direction the local functions are positive scaled pullbacks of the canonical metric on \(\mathbb P^1\) along a nonconstant morphism of projective curves.
For an irrational direction the local orders at each boundary point have nonzero scalar product with the limiting vector, and the sign is constant on a single neighborhood for the finite support.
On the normalized line, two rational directions in this neighborhood bracketing the limiting one give positive barycentric weights.
Because the underlying divisor is linear throughout this neighborhood, their weighted metrized divisors have the required geometric divisor exactly at each step.
At a finite place a common dominating graph model carries the two pullbacks of \(\mathcal O(1)\), whose degrees on vertical curves are nonnegative.
Positive real combinations remain vertically nef.
At the infinite places positive combinations and uniform limits preserve continuous plurisubharmonic local metrics.
Near a boundary point the sign of the singular logarithm fixes the branch uniformly and the remaining logarithms of units converge uniformly.
Off small neighborhoods of the boundary the logs are bounded on compact analytic sets, including at the non-Archimedean places.
Outside one finite set of places the common smooth model has disjoint boundary sections and coordinate divisors with no vertical component.
Hence the interpolated metrics already agree there with the fixed model of the limiting divisor.
This gives exactly the common model and the local uniform approximation on a fixed divisor required by [Definition 4.1 and the subsequent semipositivity convention](../literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#adelic-divisors).
At every fixed algebraic point, including on the boundary, the heights converge from nonnegative convex combinations, since the metrics agree at the good places.

On the torus the directional height is \(|sa+tb|h(z)\), using the positive and negative parts of the product formula when the real scalar has either sign.
It tends to zero because \((a/N,b/N)\) is asymptotically orthogonal to \((s,t)\).
Nonnegativity at every point and genericity of the moving points give both minima zero.
In the Ballaÿ–Sombra text Theorem 4.22 gives equality in the fundamental inequality, Corollary 6.12 gives differentiability, and Proposition 6.4 gives the common global height limit for every fixed second adelic real divisor.
The integral sup-metric pullback by the coordinate attaining \(N\) has height \(Nh(z)\), unchanged by any coefficient conjugation or root of unity.
For a rational limiting direction the integral monomial pullback is ample because its degree is positive on the projective curve, and the second bundle is semipositive and admissible.
The normalized variety height for the first is zero by Theorem 4.22 or by pulling back the canonically metrized projective line along the finite morphism.
These are the hypotheses of [Chambert-Loir–Thuillier's Lemme 6.1](../literature/CLT09/Yin_U001.md#height-convergence).

The common limit is uniform on roots along any fixed convergent directional subsequence, since a violating selection would itself be a generic small sequence on the same component after conjugation.
The width divided by \(N\) also converges on that subsequence as a continuous function of normalized direction.
The torsion-point theorem gives a finite set independent of the translations, and a fiber in each translated section has at most \(g\) elements by Bézout's identity on the exponents.
The Euler derivatives through order \(s-1\) for \(s\) distinct exponents give an invertible Vandermonde matrix at a nonzero root, bounding its multiplicity by \(s-1\).
The normalized untwisted section has degree \(W\) and content one by eventual injectivity on the support.
Thus it supplies \(W-O_F(g)\) nontorsion roots counted with multiplicity, and its polynomial measure tends to \(m(F)\) by the Boyd–Lawton theorem with shortest relation of sup norm \(N/g\).
Summing their heights determines the common limit of \(Wh(z)\), and the untwisted lower bound in v253 gives positivity.
Compactness removes the subsequence restriction.

For every cyclotomic coefficient field the translated coefficient moduli preserve the finite Gauss norms after projection becomes injective.
The multiplicative Gauss norm is therefore one for the integral rational norm polynomial at every prime, which proves primitivity.
Its degree is \(kW\), with \(O_F(kg)\) torsion roots counted with multiplicity across all embeddings.
Every other root satisfies the uniform height estimate, even if one factor occurs in several conjugate sections.
The identity \(m(S)=\deg(S)h(z)\) then proves both the relative factor share and the equivalence with \(\deg S=o(W)\) even for increasing \(k\).
