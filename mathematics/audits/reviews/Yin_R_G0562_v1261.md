# Independent review of the all-height trace-pole census through trace degree forty

<a id="finding"></a>
## All grids at every height and two further fixed-height ranges

At 2026-11-25T15:06 UTC, a reviewer outside the development of the census independently checked the [reported exclusions](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/dense_trace_census.md#census) for Yin.
The reviewer received the specified files without development dialogue or an earlier finding on this census and consulted the prior reviews of the [shared moment bound](Yin_R_G0562_v1242.md#finding) and the [scalar moment windows](Yin_R_G0562_v1238.md#finding) for the two search routines.

Put \(M=q(q+1)\) and let \(D\) be the trace degree.
A realization on any separating grid gives an irreducible trace polynomial with one root in \((0,1/M)\) and \(D-1\) distinct roots in \((-4,0)\).
The quadratic equation \(z+z^{-1}=x+2\) over the totally real trace field has nonreal roots at a negative trace embedding, so adjoining the exterior root \(T\) has relative degree two.
Thus \(T\) has degree \(2D\), Mahler measure \(T\), and \(1<T<1+1/q\).
Voutier's Corollary 2, checked at statement and normalization scope, gives \(2/(\log(6D))^3<\log T<1/q\). [Author edition, pp. 1, 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.3110#page=4)
For \(D\le40\) one has \(6D\le240<1709/5=\sum_{j=0}^{8}6^j/j!<e^6\), hence \(q<108\).
The inclusive loops over \(2\le q\le107\) and \(2\le D\le40\) therefore cover every possible height in this degree range.

For \(H(x)=\sum_{i=0}^D c_i x^{D-i}\), the pole fixes \(\sum_i c_i M^i=4M+1\).
Before digit \(c_j\) the carry \((4M+1-\sum_{i<j}c_iM^i)/M^j\) determines \(c_j\bmod M\).
The triangular substitution \(z^DH(z+z^{-1}-2)=\sum_i c_i z^i(1-z)^{2(D-i)}\) and Newton's identity then determine the moment modulo \(jM\).
At search depth \(k\) the reflected search rounds each distance downward, bounds \((1-1/(2D))^j\) below by \((2D-k)/(2D)\) for \(j\le k\), and rounds \(\log(2D)\) upward by \((7/10)\lceil\log_2(2D)\rceil\).
Scaling the resulting squared charges by the least common multiple through \(k\) makes their sum integral, so taking the floor of the enlarged allowance preserves every realizing prefix.
The driver retries each open case at a larger depth, including \(D\) itself when necessary.
Zero final carry is equivalent to the exact pole.

I reran all three reported commands with Python 3.14.3 and SymPy 1.14.0.
Of the 4,134 height-degree pairs, 3,687 exhausted their moment prefixes, with the latest exclusion at moment 38.
All 447 remaining pairs reached the terminal search and none yielded a realization.
I also replaced the signature test in a separate run with direct pole evaluation, endpoint tests and a rational Sturm chain computed by signed polynomial remainders.
Among the 451 full coefficient vectors that passed the carry test, 434 vanished at an excluded endpoint and nine had an incompatible sign at \(-4\) or \(0\).
The other eight were squarefree, and in each case the Sturm counts on \((-4,0)\) and \((0,1/M)\) differed from the required pair \((D-1,1)\).

The separate scalar search combines the two coprime digit residues modulo \(q\) and \(q+1\) and enumerates the corresponding progression of Newton sums in the open windows for pole value \(4M+1\).
Its extra integer lower bound follows from the exterior reciprocal pair contributing strictly more than two and every interior pair contributing at least minus two.
All 48 degrees \(23\le D\le70\) at \(q=4\) and all 198 degrees \(23\le D\le220\) at \(q=7\) exhausted their prefixes by moment 18.
These exclusions overlap the all-height result for \(23\le D\le40\).
