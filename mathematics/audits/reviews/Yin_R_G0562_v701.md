## The degree estimate and the uniform gap

At 13:27 UTC on November 20, 2026, a separate agent reviewing for Sheng Yin independently checked the complete statement and proof of claim version 564 and found both conclusions proved.
The reviewer received the fixed claim without the proof-development discussion and took no part in developing it.

For \(c=1\), the two possible trinomials are \(\Phi_3\) and \(\Phi_6\), and \(F=A(X^k)(1+\epsilon X^n)\) has only torsion roots.
For \(c=-1\), the assumption \(n>2k\) gives six distinct terms of unit magnitude with both endpoint gaps equal to \(k\).
Along a shrinking sequence, the six-term threshold in version 250 eventually applies and gives \(d/D\to0\) and bounded \(Dh(x)\).
If \(k/D\) stayed bounded below on a subsequence, the top monomial and the remaining five terms would satisfy version 169 with an empty binomial product on that subsequence.
Consequently \(k/D\to0\), \(D/n\to1\) and \(0<h(x^k)=(k/D)Dh(x)\to0\), regardless of any common exponent divisor.

The sum \(A+A^*=2aY\) excludes a common zero, and the discriminant five gives a simple zero of the numerator for either sign of \(a\).
For \(b>1\) this zero gives Eisenstein irreducibility even over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(Y)\), and for \(b=1\) the projection is an isomorphism of smooth projective curves.
The point \((x^k,x^{n/b})\) lies in the smooth affine locus because the denominator and the partial derivative \(bU^{b-1}\) are nonzero there.
A fixed point of positive base height can occur only finitely often, so the sequence on each fixed curve is generic.
The finite pullback of the standard metric gives an ample semipositive bundle of curve height zero and point height \(h(x^k)\), as required by Yuan's Theorem 3.1.
The review used the established equidistribution theorem at its statement and applicability level.

The two numerators of \(G-1\) are \(-2aY\) and \(2(1-Y^2)\), respectively, and each has Gauss norm \(1/2\) against the denominator of norm one.
All \(b\) distinct residue roots of \(U^b-1\) lift over the completed geometric Gauss field, so the branch selected by a primitive root has local degree one and mass \(1/b\).
The open conditions on both functions include that branch, hence simultaneously hold for a conjugate at every sufficiently late index.
Extending the embedding to \(\mathbb Q(x)\) preserves both conditions.

For every \(q\mid b\), the order formula \(s/\gcd(s,n/b)=b\) gives \(v_q(s)=v_q(n)\).
Putting \(c_q=v_q(2^{\operatorname{ord}_q(2)}-1)\), order lifting in the single residue field gives \(q^{\max(0,v_q(n)-c_q)}\mid f\).
These coprime divisors multiply, with total loss at most \(\prod_{q\in Q}q^{c_q}\), also for empty \(Q\).
In the same completion the residue of \(x^t\) is one because the multiplicative residue group has odd order.
Any \(w_i\ge1\) before the last squaring would give all subsequent valuations at least two, contrary to \(w_j<3/2\).
Thus \(2^j/e\le2^jw_0=w_j<3/2\), including \(j=0\), and \(d\ge ef\) proves (1) with a constant depending only on \(Q\).
Only finitely many choices of signs and \(b\) occur.
When the complementary divisor is at most \(H\), (1) forces a positive lower bound for \(d/n\), contradicting \(d/D\to0\) and \(D/n\to1\).
Additivity and nonnegativity of integral Mahler measure extend the gap to every positive-measure divisor.
This establishes existence of \(\eta_{Q,H}\) without giving an effective value.
