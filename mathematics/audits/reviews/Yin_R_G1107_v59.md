## Finding

The strict cut forces full real cyclotomic degree at every prime conductor in the statement.
For the specified zero-profile sequence, the joint-law bounds, logarithmic norm tests and discriminant test hold, and the resultant ceiling is \(2e^{-1/8}\).

**Field generation.**
Complex conjugation pairs the \(p-1\) cyclotomic embeddings, giving \(n\) real-field embeddings indexed by the nonzero residues modulo sign.
If a multiplier fixes \(u\), multiplication by it preserves the negative exponents, since the corresponding action fixes the value at each conjugate after relabeling.
Doubling the last \(\kappa\) indices modulo sign gives the first \(\kappa\) odd residues modulo sign.
The sum of squares over both signs equals \(2\kappa(2\kappa-1)(2\kappa+1)/3\).
Each factor in the numerator is nonzero modulo \(p\) and so is 3.
Invariance of this sum forces \(a^2=1\bmod p\), which leaves only the identity on the real subfield and gives an orbit of size \(n\).
The factors \(4-b_j^2\) and \(1/b_j^2\) are strictly positive on the grid.
Also \(1+\zeta^{2j}\) has cyclotomic norm \(\Phi_p(-1)=1\), so \(b_j\) is an algebraic unit.
Thus \(h(b_j)\), \(p\rho_j\) and \(U_j\) are integral conjugate families with precisely the same sign cut, separately generating the field.

**Limit and tail control.**
Matching the first two coefficients after the constant forces \(x=0\) and \(a_0=2\) by the [prefix identity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-coordinates).
In particular \(C\) has degree \(n-1\) and leading coefficient two as required by the [fixed-prefix estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v10.md#statement).
The fixed rational profile has numerator and denominator of total degree seven and all their zeros on the unit circle.
The estimate therefore gives \(1<M(P)\to1\).
The [endpoint conclusion](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#only-left-endpoint) and [completed-grid comparison](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v23.md#completed-grid) give \(a\to-2\), \(\kappa/n\to0\), and weak convergence of the positive measures weighted by \(X_j\) to \(2(t+2)t^2\,d\omega(t)\).
Away from fixed neighborhoods of \(-2,0\), the reciprocal of \((b_j-a)b_j^2\) is positive and bounded uniformly for large \(p\).
There the bounded mean of \(X_j\) controls the tails of \(U_j\), while the grid mass in the two neighborhoods tends to their arbitrarily small arcsine mass.
This gives tightness, and the vanishing fraction of negative nodes gives nonnegative support in the second coordinate of every limit.
For a nonnegative test supported off the two points, division by \((t-a)t^2\) converges uniformly and gives limit \(2\int V\,d\omega\) for the empirical first moment.
Weak convergence with a bounded truncation of \(u^+\) gives the asserted upper bound for the limiting moment.
Removing the spatial cutoff uses that the arcsine marginal has no atom at either point.

The logarithm of \(X_j\) has uniformly integrable positive part since \(\sup_{x>K}\log(x)/x\to0\) and the \(X_j\) have bounded means.
For angular variables in \([0,\pi]\), the cosine difference formula gives \(|2\cos\theta-2\cos\phi|\ge4\sin^2(|\theta-\phi|/2)\).
The distance to the midpoint is at least \(\pi/p\), while the distance to \(\pi/2\) is at least \(\pi/(2p)\).
Thus the grid-spacing error of order \(1/p\) in counting an angular interval is absorbed by its length whenever the relevant centered interval contains a node.
The two exceptional sets \(|b_j|<\epsilon\) and \(|b_j-a|<\epsilon\) have fractions \(O(\epsilon)\) and \(O(\sqrt{\epsilon})\), respectively, uniformly for \(0<\epsilon<1\).
Integrating the resulting exponential tails gives uniform integrability of \(\log^+|U_j|\), including on the negative nodes.

**Arithmetic tests.**
For a fixed bivariate polynomial satisfying the nonvanishing condition, a single negative node ensures that the algebraic integer \(R(b_1,U_1)\) has nonzero norm.
For any fixed nonzero univariate polynomial, eventual nonvanishing follows directly from \(n>\deg R\) and the full degree of \(U_1\).
The grid average of the logarithm is therefore nonnegative in either case.
Polynomial growth on \([-2,2]\) in the first variable bounds its positive logarithmic part by a constant plus a fixed multiple of \(\log^+|U_j|\).
After truncation below at \(-T\), upper-tail control passes this inequality to each weak limit, and then \(T\to\infty\) gives the logarithmic test including its possible zero set.

The squared Vandermonde product is the absolute discriminant of the integral monic minimal polynomial, since \(U_j\) lists all its distinct roots.
Thus the sum of the off-diagonal logarithms is nonnegative.
The lower-truncated diagonal changes the product-law average by \(-T/n\), and the upper tail of the difference logarithm is controlled by those of the two coordinates.
The product empirical laws converge to \(\eta_U\otimes\eta_U\), giving nonnegative logarithmic energy.
Positive mass on the diagonal would give energy \(-\infty\), as the positive part is integrable, so the marginal is atomless.

For a fixed multiplier \(a_0\ge2\), large primes divide none of \(a_0,a_0-1,a_0+1\).
The induced map is a permutation without fixed nodes on the half-grid.
The identity \(B_n(0)=\pm1\) puts \(1/b_1\) and hence \(U_1\) in \(\mathbb Z[b_1]\).
The divided difference at any two grid nodes is therefore integral, and field generation makes it nonzero for the indicated pairs.
These divided differences are the \(n\) conjugates, with multiplicity, of the one at index 1.
The absolute difference of the corresponding trace coordinates equals \(|1-\zeta^{(a_0-1)j}|\,|1-\zeta^{(a_0+1)j}|\).
Each binomial has product \(\sqrt p\) on the half-grid by pairing opposite indices in the full product \(p\).
This gives \(\prod_j|U_{j'}-U_j|\ge p\), and permutation preserves both marginals' uniform logarithmic tail control.
Passing to a limiting correlated law uses its own joint distribution and gives the claimed nonnegative integral of \(\log|u-v|\).

**Resultant.**
From \(\prod_j|B'(b_j)|=p^{n-1}\), \(\prod_j|b_j|=1\) and \(\prod_j|Q(b_j)|=\Delta\), the exact product of \(|U_j|\) is \(p\Delta\).
On a subsequence attaining the logarithmic upper limit \(D\), tightness and the two logarithmic truncations give \(0\le D\le\int\log u\,d\eta_U\), since \((\log p)/n\to0\).
The first-moment bound ensures finite positive logarithmic part.
Applying the univariate test at \(y-2\) and Jensen gives \(\int |u-2|\,d\eta_U\ge1\).
With \(Z=u/2\) the finite logarithmic integral ensures \(Z>0\) almost everywhere, and the mean \(m\) satisfies \(m\le1\).
For the auxiliary probability \(\nu=Z\eta_U+(1-m)\delta_*\), one has \(\eta_U\ll\nu\).
The relative entropy of \(\eta_U\) with respect to \(\nu\) is \(-\int\log Z\,d\eta_U\).
The partition into positive and negative differences gives a binary divergence bounded below by \(2(r-s)^2\), using second derivative \(1/[r(1-r)]\ge4\).
Here \(2(r-s)=\int|Z-1|\,d\eta_U+1-m\ge1/2\).
Consequently \(\log2-D\ge1/8\), yielding the stated ceiling.
All limiting conclusions are necessary conditions on the specified prime sequence with growing matching length.
The tests allow \(\Delta=1\) and leave existence of such a sequence open.
