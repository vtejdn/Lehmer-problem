## The original seed satisfies the short-row estimate at each prescribed linear prime

At 2026-11-24T18:08 an agent outside the development of this result independently checked the entire fixed claim for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed input and its scope without proof-development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on this claim.
The earlier determinant finding covers its original range \(p\ge3e\).
For the extension below that range the reviewer checked the series, divided differences and the uses of the determinant against the fixed argument.

In the local splitting field \(L\), the inertia subgroup for \(L/\mathbb Q_p(x)\) is \(I\cap H\), where \(H\) fixes \(x\).
Thus the index \([I:I\cap H]\) is the ramification index of \(\mathbb Q_p(x)/\mathbb Q_p\), and its orbit fits inside the residue class of size \(m\).
Hensel lifting in \(\mathbb Q_p(x)\) gives the Teichmüller representative \(\omega\) of its nonzero residue.
Consequently \(v_p(x/\omega-1)\ge1/m\), including the case \(x=\omega\).
For \(p\ge3M_p\), two distinct roots in one class cannot have equal \(p\)-th powers, since their ratio differs from one with valuation at least \(1/M_p>1/(p-1)\).
On this ball the term \(pu\) has uniquely smallest valuation in \((1+u)^p-1\) for \(u\ne0\).
Frobenius is injective on distinct residue classes, so all powered nodes are distinct and their minimal polynomial has degree \(e\).

The local series uses nodes of valuation at least \(1/M_p\).
In the same-class block the largest divided-difference index is \(d=2M_p-1\).
At \(n=p\) the tail bound \((n-d)/M_p-(n-1)/(p-1)\) is at least \((p-3M_p+1)/M_p>0\), and its slope is positive.
Below \(p\) the factorial denominators are units.
In the distinct-class blocks \(d\le2M_p-2\), and the prefactor \(p\) makes the term of order \(d\) vanish on reduction as well.
The reduced diagonal matrices use factorials of indices at most \(2M_p-1<p\), and their Vandermonde factors are units.
The determinant valuation and the row-span and norm conclusions therefore follow with precisely the local hypothesis stated.
The proof of the row height bound only uses the resulting span obstruction, full powered degree, the two-jet count and the common-divisor estimate.
These inputs apply even when \(p\le e+1\).

For \(s=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)]\) the binomial implies \(s\le p\).
Equality is equivalent to composition, because \(P\) divides the monic \(Q(X^p)\) for the minimum \(Q\) of \(\alpha^p\), and equality of degrees gives equality of polynomials.
Conversely a composition forces its inner-variable polynomial to be irreducible, giving \(s=p\).
The \(p\) lifts of each conjugate preserve the total logarithmic measure in this case, so the house theorem in degree \(D=e/p\) gives \(h\ge c_0p/e\).
For \(s<p\), Bézout applied to \(s,p\) and the relative norm produces a \(p\)-th root \(\beta\in\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)\) of \(\alpha^p\).
Its quotient by \(\alpha\) is torsion.
Writing \(d_\beta=\deg\beta\le D\), one has \(c_0/d_\beta\le m(\beta)=d_\beta h/e\).
This gives (4) throughout the noncomposition case, including proper loss of degree under powering.

For a residue of degree \(f\), Frobenius permutes \(f\) distinct classes of the same size \(m\).
The coefficient box has \((K+1)^{n+1}>p^f\) distinct integral lists, giving a nonzero integral difference \(E\) with the stated norm and degree.
Its reduction vanishes at the \(p\)-th powers of all \(f\) residues.
The values \(E(\omega_i^p)\) are divisible by \(p\) in unramified fields.
The binomial expansion gives \(v_p(x_i^p-\omega_i^p)\ge\min(1,p/m)\), because every intermediate coefficient is divisible by \(p\) and the last term has valuation at least \(p/m\).
Polynomial evaluation preserves this congruence.
Hence the \(fm\) roots contribute at least \(f\min(m,p)\) to the valuation of the norm, with nonnegative contributions at every other root.
The condition \(n<D\) excludes zero norm even if powered roots coincide.
The archimedean product uses all \(e\) embeddings with repetition after powering and gives the upper bound \((K(n+1))^e\exp(pnh)\).

If \(m\ge e^{3/4}\), then \(f\le e^{1/4}\) and \(n\le1+e^{1/4}\log(3e)=o(\sqrt e)\), uniformly for \(p\le3e\).
Equation (4) guarantees \(n<D\) because \(h\le1\).
Putting \(q=ph/e\le3\), division of the logarithmic norm bounds gives contributions bounded by constants times \((A+\log\log(3p))/\log p\) and \(q/A+q/(f\log p)\).
Here \(\log(2+f\log p/A)/f\ll\log\log(3p)\) uniformly in \(f\ge1\).
The choice \(A=\max(1,\sqrt{q\log p})\) gives (1), and \(m<e^{3/4}\) is covered by its first term.
For \(\sigma=p/e\) in (2), the logarithmic term is at most a constant times \(\sigma/C\).
The square-root term divided by \(\sigma\) is \(O((\sigma\log p)^{-1/2})=o(1)\), and the degree term divided by \(\sigma\) also tends to zero.
Thus \(\min(M_p,p)<p/3\) for sufficiently large \(C,e\).
Since \(p\) itself exceeds this bound, it follows that \(M_p<p/3\).
The same estimates in a fixed linear interval permit an upper endpoint \(be\) with a constant depending on \(b\).

For the asserted schedule, \(h\log(2/h)=o(\theta)\) excludes composition by (3) uniformly for \(p\ge e\theta\).
Below \(3e\), condition (2) then supplies \(p\ge3M_p\).
Above \(3e\) the previous determinant applies directly.
With \(x^2=h\theta/\log(2/h)\), one has \(x=o(1)\), \(x\ge h\) eventually and \(B=e+\lceil ex\rceil=e+o(e)\) as the common row degree budget.
Dividing the explicit row-height estimate by \(p\), bounding \(\log(2+pB)/p\) at \(e\theta\), and allowing for the ceiling gives (6).
The first term and the part \(x\log(4\mathrm e/x)/\theta\) are \(O(\sqrt{h\log(2/h)/\theta})\).
Since \(h\ge c_0/e\) and \(\theta\gg\log\log(3e)/\log(3e)\), one has \(x\gg e^{-1/2}/\log(3e)\) and \(\log(4\mathrm e/x)=O(\log(2e))\).
In particular \(\log(2e)/(e\theta x)=O((\log(3e))^3/\sqrt e)=o(1)\), and the rounding terms vanish as well.
All estimates are uniform over the full prime tail, so eventually \(H\le2^p\) and \(B\le2e\) there.
Taking \(\theta=\min(a,1)\) allows any prescribed \(p(e)\ge ae\) on the original shrinking seeds.
The lower estimate on each ordinary score and its \(\log2\) fallback gives the converse for the extended finite test.
The sublinear conclusion requires both the absolute lower cutoff for \(\theta\) and the stated rate condition on each seed sequence.

For \(Q(Y)=Y^2-3Y+1\), its larger root is a nontorsion quadratic unit with logarithm strictly between zero and one.
Any \(p\)-th root in its quadratic field has bounded degree and absolute height, giving finitely many possibilities, each accounting for at most one exponent because the unit is nontorsion.
The prime binomial criterion therefore gives irreducibility of \(Q(X^p)\) for every sufficiently large odd prime.
For \(p>5\) the quadratic has distinct roots modulo \(p\), so the largest multiplicity is exactly \(p\) at degree \(2p\).
The ordinary value equation with coprime rows would place a degree-\(2p\) root in its powered quadratic field.
This verifies the claimed obstruction at fixed positive measure.
The house bound is used at statement scope, and the existence of any shrinking sequence remains open.
