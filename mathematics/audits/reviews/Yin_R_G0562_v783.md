## Integral multiplicity, sample differences and the two-binomial comparison

The finite inequality (1), the cover bound (2), the example and the joint-order bound hold with their stated hypotheses and constants.
The hypotheses permitting a bounded-cost cover or sufficiently large orders for the completion count remain separate existence questions.

Put \(F=X^q-1\) and \(g=\gcd(\bar P,\bar F)\).
In \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(\bar P)\), multiplication by \(\bar F\) kills a class \(h\) exactly when \(\bar P/g\) divides \(h\), by cancellation and coprimality after dividing by \(g\).
The kernel therefore has dimension \(\deg g\), including when \(p\mid q\) or \(\bar P\) has repeated factors.
The integral multiplication determinant is the nonzero resultant, since no root of \(P\) is torsion.
In Smith form precisely \(\deg g\) diagonal entries are zero modulo \(p\), so \(p^{\deg g}\) divides the resultant and only its finitely many prime divisors contribute.

On the circle, replacing \(z^{a_j}-z^{a_i}\) by \(z^{a_j-a_i}-1\) preserves absolute value, even for negative \(a_i\).
Hadamard bounds the resulting product by \(n^{n/2}\), and consecutive exponents at a primitive \(n\)-th root give all Fourier rows and attain equality.
The product over positive differences is a polynomial of degree \(D\), so the maximum principle on this polynomial and its reversal gives the exterior factor \(|z|^D\).
Taking products over the roots of \(P\) counts every occurrence of a repeated sample difference and every root multiplicity, with total exterior factor \(M(P)^D\).
Combining the resultant bounds and dividing the logarithms by \(d\) gives (1).

An arithmetic progression with step \(q\) gives \(\binom n2\) differences divisible by \(q\) and \(D=q n(n-1)(n+1)/6\).
Divisibility of \(X^q-1\) into every corresponding binomial preserves the gcd rank lower bound, giving exactly the term \(3(\lambda(q)-\log n/(n-1))/(n+1)\) in \(qm/d\).
Its positive part is valid since \(M(P)\ge1\).
The supremum of the positive parts is convex and nondecreasing, and \(\log n/(n-1)\to0\) gives strict positivity at positive arguments.
Jensen's inequality yields (2), including repeated periods.
For \(1\le k\le k_0\), its right side is at least \(B(\delta/k_0)\) under the stated total-weight hypothesis.
If a product of binomials covers the reduction, each irreducible multiplicity \(t\) is covered by the sum of the individual multiplicities truncated at \(t\).
Multiplication by the irreducible degrees and summation give the rank sum at least \(d\).

For coprime odd \(a,b>1\), the binary roots of each \(G_h\) are the distinct nonidentity \(h\)-th roots, including one of exact order \(h\).
The two root sets are disjoint, so the least common period is \(ab\), while \(a+b=d+2\) and the two binary ranks are \(a-1,b-1\).
The perturbation \(KX^{d/2}\) preserves the monic reciprocal shape and binary reduction for even \(K\), and its circle modulus strictly exceeds that of \(G_aG_b\) when \(K>ab\).
This supplies lifts without unit-circle roots.

The larger constant in the [specialized two-binomial estimate](../literature/HB26/Yin_U001.md#hb26-u001) applies to the information supplied by the binary reduction.
Indeed, put \(L=\log 2\) and let \(x,y\) be the two normalized binary rank weights, so \(x+y=L\).
Convexity and \(B(0)=0\) give \(B(x)+B(y)\le B(L)\).
In the supremum for \(B(L)\), the \(n=2\) term is zero.
The terms for \(3\le n\le4\), \(5\le n\le8\) and \(n\ge9\) are bounded respectively by \(L/4,\ 2L/7\) and \(3L/10\), using the decrease of \(\log n/(n-1)\).
Thus even the sum before Jensen gives a coefficient at most \(3L/10<\log(5/2)/4\), since \(2^{11}<5^5\).
Both coefficients multiply \(d/(a+b)\), and further primes can supply extra information for individual lifts.
I checked equation (14) of Corollary 2.7 in Hakimoglu-Brown's author-posted manuscript, including the divisibility hypothesis and the two-binomial coefficient.

When \(P(0)=\pm1\), every residue root is nonzero and lies in a finite extension of \(\mathbb F_p\).
At a multiple of its order each distinct selected root contributes at least once to the gcd, with no separability assumption on \(P\).
The single-period case of (2) now gives the joint-order lower bound, and its positive right side for a nonempty prime set also implies \(m>0\).
The [completion count](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v645.md#statement) calls for sufficiently large orders at eligible primes in relation to a specified span.
This lower bound does not establish that comparison.
In Silverman's [journal theorem](../literature/Sil12/Yin_U001.md#fixed-period-valuation), pp. 757–759, the finite-place sum starts at one period and the estimate uses its multiples with triangular weights.
I checked this use and the stated binary comparison at their source passages, without auditing either paper's complete proof.
