# Review of the branch-two bound for all six-term trace lifts

## The one- and two-dimensional limiting measures and return to the denominator

On 2026-11-16T17:40 a separately assigned agent for Yin independently checked the [complete six-term claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#statement), the full argument in [L0462 v87](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#statement) and the hypotheses on its mathematical inputs.
The reviewer had reviewed the earlier cubic-offset claim, took no part in developing either new proof and received no development dialogue, author explanation or prior verdict on these fixed arguments.
The review found both absolute bounds valid for the stated positive-constant branch, for all offset ratios and both degree parities.

The branch identity makes a vanishing excess equivalent along a sequence to \(q\to\infty\), and the denominator is an integer polynomial of measure strictly above one tending to one there.
The lower and upper terms of the anti-reciprocal lift pair about an empty midpoint.
The four-term bound removes every degenerate case.
For a six-term escaping sequence the two interior lower indices are \(d<e=o(h)\).
The six-term reduction gives exactly the allowed coefficient bounds on the subsequences with fixed primitive pair or with unbounded reduced upper index.
In the fixed case the constant-two irreducible factor has unit leading coefficient and cannot be reciprocal up to sign.
One circle root would force that reciprocity by conjugation, so this factor is strictly exterior and the remaining circle factors are cyclotomic with all their multiplicities.

In the two-dimensional case each nonzero fixed lattice vector has eventually nonzero specialization because every exact relation is a multiple of the primitive one.
Simultaneous extraction of the signs gives an additive order with both coordinate vectors positive, even when some specializations stay bounded.
The value one at index zero and the doubled moments on its positive side give \(J+J^*=2\widehat\mu\).
The integer prefix yields integral \(B=(A/2)J\) in this ordering and excludes a Haar marginal by the fractional coefficient of \(A/2\).
At every fixed mixed index all Taylor indices are strictly positive below the end of the prefix.
The resulting integer mixed transforms of \(A u^k\Omega\) give those of \(|A|^2\Omega\) on nonzero layers.
On the zero layer \(A^*B+AB^*\) has precisely the required normalization.

I checked the definition (12.2) and Corollary 12.4 in the [published Sanders article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00041-020-09732-y).
It applies to finite complex measures on the duals of all the discrete lattices in this argument.
Its finite integer sum of coset indicators gives character densities on compact annihilators.
Splitting each annihilator into connected torsion translates preserves finiteness, and the only full-dimensional terms come from singleton cosets, with their integer coefficients.
Combining identical translates before using mutual singularity gives nonnegative densities for the positive measure \(|A|^2\Omega\).

A proper connected component projecting onto the whole base has codimension one and a defining character with nonzero last coefficient.
On a common multiple of its covering degrees and torsion translation orders, the corresponding power of \(u\) is constant on each fiber and is the indicated base character.
Division by \(|A|^2\) preserves integrability of the projected mass and loses only singular projection over its torus zeros.
Distinct slopes separate by unbounded integer frequencies, so Riemann–Lebesgue isolates each Fourier coefficient of \(A f_s\) as a limit of integers.
The coefficients of that integrable function tend to zero, hence give an integer Laurent polynomial.
In two variables the primitive linear \(A\) and its reversal are relatively prime, since their triangular supports cannot be translates.
Reality of \(f_s\) therefore gives divisibility by \(A\) in the integer Laurent ring.
In one variable the poles of the rational quotient occur in reciprocal-conjugate pairs, and a nonzero finite pole must lie on the circle by the root restriction.
Such a pole contradicts integrability, and division by the unit leading coefficient gives an integral Laurent quotient here as well.
Any nonzero projected graph density would then have integer mean one.
It would exhaust the marginal, and strict triangle inequality on the nontrivial characters of an absolutely continuous probability would make its other integer Fourier coefficients zero.
This contradicts the earlier exclusion of Haar.

After removal of those components only the full-torus density contributes continuous rows, with finite support in the last frequency even after division by \(|A|^2\).
Where the marginal density is positive, the conditional measure at a last nonzero row pushes forward to a probability with just its constant and first pair of harmonics, giving the bound \(1/2\) on that moment.
The marginal density has measure less than one by strict Jensen or by loss of total mass to the singular part, and \(M(A)=2\) in both dimensions.
Thus a surviving integer Laurent row would have measure below one.
The singular part of the fixed marginal is supported on finitely many proper torsion translates and torus zeros of \(A\), all killed by a fixed product of character differences.
Its specialization has positive exponents \(O(D)\) on the extracted sequence.
The same product kills every remaining mixed row over that marginal, regardless of the chosen intermediate indices.
Integer coefficients turn limiting zero into eventual zero, and a diagonal choice of violating indices would contradict this conclusion on \(D\ll t\ll h\).
An extra binomial shift of order \(D\) preserves that intermediate-scale conclusion.

I checked the initial bounds separately for comparable offsets, coalescing offsets and \(e/d\to\infty\).
In the coalescing case the first-index alternatives bound \(p=2(e-d)\) by \(4u\) whenever the first nonzero index \(u=o(d)\) occurs, and the exact prefix model then has at most five nonzeros through \(p\).
For separated offsets the rotated first marginal is one of the nine profiles, whose atoms have order dividing six and whose continuous mass is positive.
At \(e+ld\), the term \(b[w^l]J/2\) occurs only on the nonnegative side, with Taylor constant one at zero.
The opposite tails on multiples of six give integers whose difference is \(b\) times the atomic mass, strictly between minus one and one, so all atoms disappear.
In the additional classification in v87, the double zeros of the bounded mixed density force \((2-\zeta)(H/V)(\zeta)=b\) at every root of \(V\).
The norms of \(2-\zeta\) exclude \(-1\) and the primitive sixth roots, leaving \(V=1,1-w\).
The two explicit model transforms multiply by the prefix to give \(1,XY\) and have positive real part in the disk as claimed.
For the denominator proof the atom-free conclusion already suffices.
The one-offset mixed-row argument applies before \(e\), giving a zero window, while the short Bessel bound covers the preceding indices.
The resulting absolute moment sum through a fixed fraction of \(e\) bounds row sums of the signed character Gram matrix.
On any fixed number \(K\) of indices the positive-part correction has norm \(o(1)K\), so testing large fixed \(K\) bounds the number of nonzero integer coefficients wherever the active multiplier is fixed.
In the fixed-pair case the first fractional coefficient gives the Haar congruences even for an earlier integral multiplier or a coefficient of magnitude \(3/2\), and bounded \(g\) requires only the moment bound.

For moving pairs the expansion of \(1/S_i\) costs at most two in total absolute coefficients for each power of \(z^e\) that enters the specified initial interval.
The signed two-sided Fourier sequence for \(WJ_i\) needs a bounded initial correction including the contributions of the Taylor constant at indices zero and \(p\).
Its coefficients at \(-r<0\) are exactly \(-[z^{r+p}]WJ_i\), so the bound for the filtered denominator and the initial sum of \(1/S_i\) control every further shift from the character multiplier.
This gives bounded-variation representations for both series on the right of the comparison identity.
For the fixed primitive pair, all cyclotomic factors remain in \(E_i\), the exterior inverse is absolutely summable, and the local moment sum controls shifts of order \(g\).
Both identities therefore have an \(O(h)\)-degree left side with a zero prefix of length proportional to \(h\) and bounded circle multipliers on the right.
The two cutoffs apply to the actual denominator.
In the moving case \(|1-z^{2(e-d)}|\le2|a z^d-b z^e|\) and \(4-|a z^d+b z^e|^2\le4|2+a z^d+b z^e|\), giving the needed extra squared binomial even where \(S_i\) vanishes.
In the fixed case the exterior factor has a uniform positive minimum on the circle for that fixed pair.
The final integer multiple has bounded circle \(L^1\) norm, hence bounded support, and its cyclotomic factors preserve the measure of the original denominator.
The fixed-support gap excludes both subsequences, which gives absolute constants without an estimate uniform in the fixed pair at each intermediate step.
The proof gives no numerical gap or upper bound on \(q\), and the external Fourier and Mahler theorems were used at statement and applicability scope.
