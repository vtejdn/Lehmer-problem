### Rational degrees and separately prescribed progressions

At a good prime, monicity and the nonzero residue discriminant give distinct separable residue roots.
They lift in a finite unramified local extension, and Frobenius orbit lengths on the roots agree with the degrees of the monic irreducible factors of the reduction.
Thus the possible divisor degrees are the subset sums of the cycle lengths.
The splitting field \(L\) and any cyclotomic field \(K\) are finite Galois, and their compositum group identifies with the pairs of automorphisms agreeing on \(L\cap K\).
Projection of the fiber over the designated cyclotomic automorphism to \(L\) is therefore a nonempty coset of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/(L\cap K))\).
Because each \(d_i\ge5\) under the submitted size hypothesis, the perfect groups \(A_{d_i}\) lie in \([G,G]\), which fixes the abelian intersection \(L\cap K\).
Within any feasible sign vector, multiplying any representative by \(D\) gives exactly the entire product of the prescribed sign classes on the blocks.
This uses no independence of the block signs themselves.
Chebotarev in \(LK\) realizes each such cycle pattern at arbitrarily large distinct primes since the cycle lengths and the cyclotomic restriction are invariant by conjugacy.
Conversely a good prime in the progression is prime to its modulus and is unramified in both fields, so its Frobenius belongs to that fiber.

For a block of degree \(d\), a full cycle has sign \((-1)^{d-1}\) and realizes least cycle length \(d\).
If this sign is forbidden the permutation must have at least two cycles, giving least cycle length at most \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\).
Two cycles with the balanced lengths \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor,\lceil d/2\rceil\) change the sign and attain that bound, including when their lengths coincide.
The full product within a fixed sign vector permits all the blockwise maxima at once.
The smallest over its blocks and then the largest over attainable sign vectors is therefore the exact single-prime optimum (3).
Every blockwise optimum is at least \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), and the block of size \(m\) supplies the universal upper bound \(m\).

### Separation of the proper small divisor degrees

A rational factor of least degree has a monic integral representative and retains degree \(m\) in every reduction.
Thus every intersection in (1) contains \(m\).
At the first prime take a full cycle on a block with agreeing sign and \((1,d_i-1)\) on a block with opposite sign.
A positive subset sum below \(m\) can use only ones, of which there are at most \(t\), or a single long part \(m-1\).
Any additional positive summand with that long part reaches \(m\).

At the second prime the open interval \(m/3<j<(m-1)/2\) has length \((m-3)/6>t+1\) under \(m\ge12(t+2)\).
It therefore supplies an integer even after removing all \(t\) possible values \(d_i-m+1\).
On each block use a full cycle or \((j,d_i-j)\) according to its sign in any feasible vector in this second progression.
Every part exceeds \(t\), and two long parts already exceed \(m\).
One long part together with any \(j\) reaches at least \(m\), while three short parts exceed \(m\).
Consequently the only possible positive sums below \(m\) are \(j,2j,d_i-j\).
The strict interval gives \(2j<m-1\), and the forbidden choices rule out \(d_i-j=m-1\).
The two sets of smaller degrees are disjoint even when the sign vectors allowed in the two progressions differ.
The two prime choices are separate Chebotarev applications, each with infinitely many primes, so they can be made distinct and arbitrarily large.
This proves (1), and the threshold assertion follows since \(m\) is the smallest positive degree of any rational divisor.
As a finite arithmetic cross-check, subset-sum enumeration for 5,052 choices of block sizes and sign pairs near the stated threshold for \(t=1,2,3\) also gave empty intersection below \(m\).

### Verifying the joint trace action for each fixed seed

For every \(s\ge3\), common zeros of \(g,B\) in characteristic zero would satisfy \(U^{s-2}=-1\) and \(U^2+U+1=0\), which are incompatible.
All roots of \(g\) are nonzero and simple since \(g=g'=0\) would give \(U=-s/(s-1)\) and \(s|U|^{s-1}=1\).
Thus \(g(cU^k)\) has a simple zero at which \(B(cU^k)\ne0\) for each nonzero \(c\) and \(k\ge1\).
At its linear prime the polynomial \(g(cU^k)+eV^kB(cU^k)\) is Eisenstein in \(V\).
Its coefficient content over \(\mathbb C[U]\) is a unit by coprimality, and its total degree is exactly \((s+1)k\).
The three exponent vectors of \(1,U,V\) in \(F\) give the whole support-difference lattice.
For primitive \((a,b)\) the torsion-quotient theorem therefore yields \(g_v=1\), excluding all distinct nontorsion roots with torsion ratio and preserving every power field even after adjoining \(E=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
These characteristic-zero steps also cover \(s\equiv2\bmod3\).

Suppose unequal, noninverse nontorsion roots become multiplicatively dependent.
Inverting one root if necessary preserves the reciprocal section, and yields a relation \(x^v/z^u\) torsion with positive coprime \(u,v\).
Writing \(iu+jv=1\), the element \(w=x^iz^j\) satisfies \(x=\xi w^u,\ z=\eta w^v\) with \(\xi,\eta\in E\) torsion.
Power-field preservation identifies \(E(x)=E(z)=E(w)\), of degree \(D_E\le(s+1)N\).
With \(h(x),h(z)\ll_s N^{-1}\), the AD07 relative bound applied to \(x\) gives \(D_E\gg_s N/(\log(2N))^4\).
Applied to \(w\), it gives \(h(w)\gg_s 1/(N(\log(2N))^4)\), since the numerator \((\log\log(5D_E))^3\) is bounded below by a positive absolute constant.
The identities \(h(x)=u h(w), h(z)=v h(w)\) give \(u,v\ll_s(\log(2N))^4\).
For \(u\ne v\) the two scaled plane curves have different degrees and are irreducible, hence share no component.
All \(E\)-conjugates of \((w^a,w^b)\) belong to their intersection because their coefficients are in \(E\).
The two coordinates generate \(E(w)\) by coprimality of \(a,b\), so Bézout gives \(D_E\le(s+1)^2uv\ll_s(\log(2N))^8\), contradicting the lower bound.
For \(u=v\), coprimality gives \(u=v=1\) and the torsion-quotient exclusion supplies the contradiction.

For the resulting independent pair the AD99 product-height lower bound has exponent \(2\kappa(2)=212\).
Each root has rational degree at most \((s+1)N\), so the joint degree \(D_{\mathbb Q}\) has \(\log(3D_{\mathbb Q})\ll_s\log(2N)\).
The upper bound \(h(x)h(z)\ll_s N^{-2}\) therefore implies \(D_{\mathbb Q}\gg_s N^2/(\log(2N))^{212}\).
The single-block input gives the natural alternating or symmetric action in every high primitive slope at fixed \(s\not\equiv2\bmod3\).
At the other fixed seeds it gives this action for odd \(a\), and the necessary upper bound \(r\ll_s(\log(3(a+b)))^{424}\) for multiple blocks excludes them above the stated sufficiently large constant.
Reciprocity and simplicity identify inverse pairs with distinct trace roots.
Each rational orbit of traces corresponds to the root orbit of one reciprocal irreducible factor and has half its degree.

The commutator of the composite trace group projects onto each individual \(A_{d_i}\) and lies in their product.
The subdirect product lemma for nonabelian simple groups would force a proper such subgroup to identify two coordinates by an isomorphism.
Equal alternating orders at these unbounded degrees give equal degrees, and every automorphism of \(A_d\) for \(d\ge9\) is induced by relabeling.
After that relabeling, an element normalizing the diagonal must have equal two permutations because the centralizer of \(A_d\) in \(S_d\) is trivial.
The entire trace group normalizes its commutator, so corresponding traces on these different orbits would have the same stabilizer and generate the same degree-\(d\) rational field.
The two quadratic lifts would have joint degree at most \(4d=O_s(N)\), contradicting the AD99 joint bound.
This proves \(D\subseteq G\) without requiring the full symmetric groups, their sign characters, or the two trace splitting fields to be independent.

### Heights, the minimum and common dilations

There are \(a\) exterior roots per exterior pole and a fixed positive number of such poles depending only on \(s\).
Each nontorsion monic integral unit factor has an exterior root by Kronecker, yielding \(1\le t\ll_s a\).
The individual degree lower bound \(2d_i\gg_s a\sqrt r\) gives \(m\gg_s a\sqrt r\) uniformly, so \(m\ge12(t+2)\) eventually.
On the unit circle \(B\) is the reciprocal of \(g\), whence the limiting height integral is the positive number \(I_s=m(B)\).
The height theorem applies over characteristic zero even when \(g,B\) have a common reduction at two.
At degree \(2d_i\ge2m\), the error is bounded uniformly by a fixed multiple of \(\delta=r^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\log(4m)/(2m)}\), since \(\log(2d)/d\) decreases for \(d\ge2\).
For large \(r\), the lower bound \(I_s-O_s(\delta)>0\) allows one to bound the measure of every factor below by \(2m(I_s-O_s(\delta))/b\).
The block of degree \(m\) gives the upper bound \(2m(I_s+O_s(\delta))/b\).
This justifies (5) without assuming that the smallest-degree block attains the minimum measure.

In any chosen class, writing \(U=\max_p u(p)\), one has \(m/3\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\le U\le m\) in this range.
Consequently \(\mu_{a,b}\asymp_s m/b\asymp_s U/b\), uniformly in the direction and in its prescribed class.
The maximum is attained, so positive lower bounds on measure are equivalent to the existence of a good prime with every irreducible degree at least \(cb\), with a single \(c>0\) across the chosen set.
The same comparisons give the equivalence of convergence to zero on each specified high-slope sequence.
Finally, for each fixed nonexceptional \(s\), C345 v391 excludes a sequence of measures tending to zero at bounded slopes and identifies all factors after common dilation of sufficiently large primitive directions as irreducible substitutions preserving the measures.
Thus the primitive criterion covers a uniform gap for the entire family in exactly the stated nonexceptional case.
All the arguments distinguish the equivalence from deciding whether the uniform positive constant exists.
