## Rotation, punctures and uniform volume

The argument proves the uniform volume bound with two fixed boundary components for every polynomial satisfying the stated hypotheses.
To check the homology calculation, take the classes of the \(N\) lifted edges in their cyclic order.
The intersection matrix is, up to sign, the difference of the forward and backward cyclic shifts.
Its rank is \(N-1=2g\), so the classes span rational homology and the kernel of the map sending coordinate vectors to the edge classes is exactly the constant line.
The rotation by a unit \(u\) therefore has the regular cyclic characteristic polynomial divided by \(x-1\).
In its twist correction only the powers \(j\) satisfying \(uj\equiv\pm1\bmod N\) contribute.
They give opposite unit coefficients at the two required exponents, so one integral twist exponent produces the entire \(P\).
Irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) implies the symplectic irreducibility required by Margalit and Spallone, and the genus, cyclotomic and power-substitution hypotheses match their Proposition 2.

For a nonneighbor step, a reduction on the sphere with just its branch points would lift to a reduction on the closed cover, since the two branched disk covers have no disk components.
After marking the origin, a new reducing circle must cut off precisely that mark and one old mark.
Two different members of an invariant multicurve cannot both do so, hence the old mark is fixed.
The correction to the vertex permutation is either trivial or a transposition of neighbors, and the rotation moves every vertex by a nonneighbor step.
This leaves only infinity as the other enclosed mark.
The corresponding vertex disk would support the whole mapping class because a pure mapping class on its complementary twice-marked disk is a boundary twist.
A disk-supported braid has total winding zero about the origin, as the argument of the coordinate has a continuous branch on that disk.
The proposed braid has winding \(u\bmod N\), which is nonzero and is independent of the chosen end-fixing isotopy.
Thus the sphere class is pseudo-Anosov, and every filled branch point upstairs has at least two prongs.
The two lifts of the origin are individually fixed by the odd-order rotation and lie outside the edge twist.

For a neighbor step, the inverse image of the three-point disk has one boundary and Euler characteristic \(-1\).
Banding this torus to one mark leaves positive genus on each side after filling, including when \(g=2\).
On the closed surface a finite segment of the orbit of its boundary fills, by pseudo-Anosov growth of curve-graph distance.
Every essential test curve that becomes trivial on closing bounds a disk containing both marks, so must intersect that boundary already at the initial iterate.
These two cases give the finite filling segment needed by Hirose, Kalfagianni and Kin, Theorem 5(a), with a single curve and \(3g-3+2>0\).
The separating twist preserves capped homology, so the closed criterion still gives pseudo-Anosov type even if the capped mapping class changes.
The two fixed boundary generators have one invariant sum relation, supplying exactly \(x-1\) as the characteristic polynomial of the capping kernel.

In the nonneighbor construction the triangulation carries the rotation simplicially and each polygon edge has a bounded neighborhood away from the poles.
In the neighbor construction the fixed disk requires only the one-sector fan shear, and the torus and band reach the mark through a bounded number of seam triangles.
The two twist curves occupy separate heights, so each drilling uses its own bounded neighborhood whatever the surgery coefficients.
For fixed \(N\) the triangulated parent for \(a\) periods consequently costs \(O(N)+O(a)\) with absolute constants.
Long fillings on the retained cusps approach the open hyperbolic volume at each fixed \(a\).
The straightening bound on those closed fillings, followed by division by \(a\) and passage to the limit, removes the entire initial \(O(N)\) contribution.

For the explicit family, \(\lvert z-m\rvert=\lvert1-mz\rvert\) on the unit circle gives the claimed Rouché count for every multiplier strictly between zero and one.
Passage to the limit leaves at most one exterior root with multiplicity, and \(F(2)=-(m-2)2^{N-1}+2m-1<0\) throughout the stated range.
For a nontrivial root of unity \(\zeta\) the norm identity would require \(m^d\le2^d\) in degree \(d\), since \(\zeta^{N-1}=\zeta\) would force its order to divide both \(N\) and \(N-2\).
The root \(1\) is absent from \(P\).
Kronecker's theorem and reciprocity now give a single irreducible Salem factor, and all the interior coefficients \(1-m\) are nonzero.
In the general record application the two geometric sums leave a denominator \((x-1)^2\).
One multiplication by \(x-1\) makes the cofactor exactly the product of the period binomials divided by \(x-1\) and at most doubles the collected support.
Dobrowolski's bound applies to this whole multiple, whose Mahler measure equals that of the record.
Only the four-term construction yields the asserted converse volume estimate.

The external theorems were used at their statements and hypotheses, including Proposition 2 on p. 856 of Margalit and Spallone, Theorem 5(a) and Remark 7 on pp. 3 and 8 of Hirose, Kalfagianni and Kin, and Theorem 1 on p. 202 of Dobrowolski.
