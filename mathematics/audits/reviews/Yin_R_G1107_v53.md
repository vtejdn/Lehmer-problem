## The rational construction and its sharpness for a nonzero integer derivative

For \(s=2\), the prefactor in the residue formula times \(\sqrt{2+b_j}\,b_j^4\) is \(\mu_j=(4-b_j^2)b_j^4/p\), since \(2\sin(\theta_j/2)\cos(\theta_j/2)=\sin\theta_j\).
These masses are positive on the odd half grid.
For \(\deg f\le d\), the full grid integrates \((4-t^2)t^4f(t)^2\) exactly when \(2d+6<p\).
The zero-angle term vanishes and the other grid points pair under reflection, giving the factor \(1/(2\pi)\) in the integral over \([0,\pi]\).

The angular derivative estimate gives a one-sided interval of length \(\delta=1/(4ed)\) on which \(|f|\ge3\|f\|_\infty/4\), including at an endpoint maximum.
Removing the three neighborhoods in the ledger leaves at least \(5\delta/8\).
On that remainder, both \(|\sin\theta|\) and \(|\cos\theta|\) are bounded below by a constant times \(\delta\), and one is at least \(1/\sqrt2\).
Thus the weight \(64\sin^2\theta\cos^4\theta\) is at least a constant times \(\delta^4\).
Integration gives the \(Ad^5\) squared-norm inequality.
Taking \(f=K(x,\cdot)\) bounds \(K(x,x)^2\) by \(Ad^5K(x,x)\), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the same uniform bound \(Ad^5\) off the diagonal.
The reproducing identity then cancels every moment through degree \(d\) in (5).

For both inverse moments, \(\sum\mu_j b_j^{-2k}\) is bounded uniformly because the exponent \(4-2k\) is nonnegative.
Consequently the kernel correction in \(A_{ki}\) has size \(O(d^{5/2})\), including at the final node.
The nearest angular offsets from \(\pi/2\) have magnitudes \(\pi/(2p)\) and \(3\pi/(2p)\), giving absolute traces asymptotic to \(\pi/p\) and \(3\pi/p\) in either order.
Choosing \(c\) small preserves the opposite signs of \(A_{1+},A_{1-}\) and both positive signs in row two.
After setting \(w_\pm=u_\pm/p^2\) and multiplying row one by \(p\), all four matrix entries are bounded above and away from zero in absolute value, uniformly for sufficiently small \(c\).
The choice \(w_*=-2\mu_n=O(p^{-3})\) gives right-hand side \((O(p^{-1}),1+O(p^{-2}))\).
The determinant \(a\ell+bg\) stays positive and bounded away from zero, and the two Cramer numerators are \(b+O(p^{-1})\) and \(a+O(p^{-1})\) with uniform errors.
Thus both central weights are positive and \(O(p^{-2})\), and the inverse moment sums are exactly \(0,1\).

The simple grid poles cancel in \(B_n\sum h_j/(t-b_j)\).
The vanishing moments give \(\deg H\le n-d-2\), hence \(n-\deg H\ge\lfloor cp^{2/5}\rfloor+2\).
The inverse moments give \(H/B_n=0\) and \((H/B_n)'=-1\) at zero, so the monomial coefficients are exactly \(0\) and \(-B_n(0)=\pm1\).
The kernel correction \(\sum_iw_iK(x_i,b_j)\) has magnitude \(O(c^5)\) uniformly because \(d^5\le c^5p^2\).
When this magnitude is below \(1/2\), subtracting \(2\mu_n\) reverses just the final sign, with both central atoms positive.
At \(-2\), \(m=n-2\) gives the neutral ratio \(-8\).
The density contribution is \(O(c^5)\) since \(\sum\mu_j/(2+b_j)=p^{-1}\sum(2-b_j)b_j^4=O(1)\).
The two central atom contributions are \(O(p^{-2})\) and the same cancellation of \(2+b_n\) makes the last one \(O(p^{-1})\).
These bounds preserve the strict endpoint for small \(c\) and large \(p\).
Rational approximation inside the affine space with the two prescribed coefficients and the degree cap preserves the finitely many strict signs and the endpoint for each fixed \(p\).

The matching upper exponent applies to the prescribed nonzero derivative.
In C891 v2, \(R=(t-a)H/B_n\) has \(|R'(0)|=|a|>1\) for the jet in (1), while the interpolation estimate gives \(|R'(0)|\le(p^2/4)A_D\) and \(A_D=O(M^{-5})\) at \(s=2\).
Here \(M=1+\lfloor(L-3)/6\rfloor\), yielding \(L=O(p^{2/5})\) for these rational polynomials too.
If the value and derivative are merely required to be integers, both can vanish.
The rational quartics \(H=(-1)^n(4/p)t^4\) in L457 v7 then give \(L=n-4\) with the same strict cut and endpoint.
The opening states the sharp exponent for \(H(0)=0,\ |H'(0)|=1\) and identifies the quartic example when both coefficients vanish.
I found no gap in the construction or in this comparison.
