Let \(\mathcal D_P\) and \(I_P\) be the decomposition and inertia groups at a prime \(P\) of the normal closure \(L\).
Choosing a uniform conjugator of an initial \(P_0\) gives a uniform \(P\), because all fibers have size \(|\mathcal D_{P_0}|\).
Conjugation bijects \(\Phi_{P_0}\) with \(\Phi_P\).
Conditional on the original lift, its conjugate is uniform on its conjugacy class, giving exactly (1) and its independence of \(P_0\).
The distribution sums to one over the \(s\) right cosets, and at an unramified prime there is a single lift at each chosen \(P\).

For \(v=P\cap F\), the relative local decomposition and inertia groups are \(\mathcal D_P\cap H\) and \(I_P\cap H\), since \(L_P/F_v\) is Galois even for a nonnormal \(F/\mathbb Q\).
The elements of \(I_P x_0=\Phi_P\) in any one right coset of \(H\) number \(|I_P\cap H|\) if that intersection is nonempty.
Thus the number of cosets hit is \(e_v\) by the ramification index tower formula.
The residue quotient of \(\mathcal D_P\cap H\) consists of the powers of \(p^{f_v}\)-Frobenius on \(\kappa(P)\), so it contains the \(p\)-Frobenius precisely for \(f_v=1\).
Among all primes of \(L\) over \(p\), the proportion over \(v\) is
\[
 \frac{|H|/|\mathcal D_P\cap H|}{|G|/|\mathcal D_P|}
                 =\frac{[F_v:\mathbb Q_p]}{[F:\mathbb Q]}
                 =\frac{e_v f_v}{s}.
\]
Their conditional contribution to \(a_p(H)\) is \(1/e_v\) when \(f_v=1\) and zero otherwise.
This yields one contribution \(1/s\) per degree-one prime of \(F\), including each ramified such prime.
Conjugation by an element of \(H\) preserves the lift distribution and permutes cosets by the right \(H\)-action.
Double transitivity makes the complement of \(H\) a single orbit, giving the other weight.
A transposition in \(S_s\) has \(s-2\) fixed points, yielding the claimed maximum for \(s\ge4\).

For the relative torsion-quotient classes, transitivity gives one common size \(l\), and a class product has the form \(\xi a^l\) for a torsion \(\xi\) and any member \(a\).
Different classes therefore have nontorsion product quotients.
Consequently \(D'=D/l\), \(h(\beta)=l h(\alpha)>0\), and within each packet all powered conjugates are distinct.
The original packet and the packets indexed by different primes have distinct positive heights \(h(\beta)\) and \(p h(\beta)\), even if the automorphisms used at the different primes are unrelated.
Extending a chosen \(g\) off \(L\) only supplies the full root set of the conjugated polynomial there.

Fix the same maximizing coset \(Hg\) for all \(P\mid p\), and suppose \(x\in\Phi_P\cap Hg\).
The identity \(x=hg\) for some \(h\in H\) gives \(x^{-1}|_F=g^{-1}|_F\), which is the required direction of the embedding.

For any normalized coefficient \(a\in F\) integral at \(v\), the roots of \(X^{p^{f_v}}-X\) furnish its residue representative \(z\in F_v\), also unique for that residue in \(L_P\), by simple-root lifting.

The difference of the \(p\)-th powers of \(x^{-1}a\) and \(x^{-1}z\) has valuation at least \(1/e_v\) by the factorization of a difference of powers of integral elements.
Since \((x^{-1}z)^p=z\), the diagonal coefficient differences have the claimed valuation.
All other terms of the polynomial \(p\)-th power have multinomial coefficients divisible by \(p\) and integral remaining factors.
This proves the full comparison at the \(1/e_v\) scale, including when the compositum of the coefficient fields is more ramified locally.

At size \(S=D'(T+t)\), a divided derivative of order \(j\) costs at most \(S^{j+1/2}\max(1,|z|)^{S-1}\) in its archimedean row norm.
