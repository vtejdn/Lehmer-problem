## Prefix restoration, the ordered Fourier bound and its consequences

Multiplying by a monomial puts the numerator in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) with nonzero constant term.
Since \(P(0)=\pm1\), the germ \(H\) has integral Taylor coefficients, and termination would give Laurent divisibility.
A common root of the shifted numerator and \(P\) would likewise give divisibility, by irreducibility and monic division.
All interior roots are therefore poles, and their moduli have product \(e^{-m}\) since the product of all root moduli is one.
Clearing them gives \(F=IH\) holomorphic on a disk of radius greater than one with circle mean modulus \(C\).

On the first \(k\) occupied monomials, compression of multiplication by \(I\) is a contraction with triangular matrix and diagonal \(e^{-m}\).
Its least singular value is at least \(e^{-km}\) by the determinant formula.
The matrix applied to the selected coefficient vector gives every coefficient of \(F\) at those positions since all earlier occupied positions are present.
Each output coefficient has modulus at most \(C\) by the Fourier integral, so \(\|T\|_2\le e^{km}\sqrt{k}\,C\).
The constant term of \(I\) is its circle mean, giving \(\|I-I(0)\|_2^2=1-e^{-2m}\) by orthogonality.
Cauchy--Schwarz thus gives exactly the error factor in (4).
The identity \(G=I(0)T+I(H-T)\) holds locally at zero, where the second term has order greater than \(s_k\).
Every intervening Taylor coefficient of \(G\) is consequently zero and its first \(k\) occupied coefficients each have modulus at least \(e^{-m}\).

The [JKS24 source unit](../literature/JKS24/Yin_U001.md#jks24-u001) records Theorem 1.1(i) of arXiv:2303.10919v2 on page 3 for increasing frequencies and arbitrary complex amplitudes.
For integer frequencies the integrand is continuous and one-periodic, so the long average equals Haar probability integration on the circle by splitting the integration interval into full periods and a bounded remainder.
In each Taylor truncation at degree \(M\ge s_k\), listing only nonzero coefficients assigns the first \(k\) the weights \(1/(i+1)\), independent of the gaps in their degrees.
Any later coefficients contribute nonnegative terms to the weighted sum even when their moduli are small.
The holomorphic extension of \(G\) gives uniform convergence of the truncations on the circle and hence convergence of their mean moduli.
These facts establish (1) with the stated factor \(1/26\).
This review used the recorded primary statement and its circle specialization at statement and applicability depth.
The original proof of Theorem 1.1(i) was outside its scope.

For the choice \(k=\lfloor 1/(m\log(1/m))\rfloor\) one has \(k\to\infty\), \(km\to0\), and \(\log k=\log(1/m)-\log\log(1/m)+o(1)\).
Since \(k(1-e^{-2m})\le 2km\), the denominator of (1) tends to 26 and its harmonic sum is \(\log k+O(1)\), giving (2) along every admissible sequence.
When \(0<m\le1/2\), \(k=\lfloor1/m\rfloor\) gives \(k+2\ge 1/m\), \(e^{-m}\ge e^{-1/2}\) and \(e^{km}\sqrt{k(1-e^{-2m})}\le e\sqrt 2\).
Integrating \(1/x\) over \([2,k+2]\) bounds the harmonic sum from below by \(\log((k+2)/2)\).
Rearranging yields (3), with the complementary range immediate from \(C\ge0\).
Finally \(P\) generates a prime ideal in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) by monicity and irreducibility, and that ideal is disjoint from the powers of \(X\).
It remains prime after localization, so every admissible \(B\) gives an admissible \(A=B^2\) with the claimed circle modulus, independently of width and reciprocity.
The implication from a uniform upper bound on the square integral to a Mahler gap follows from (3).
Existence of such numerators with uniformly bounded integral remains open in the stated application.
