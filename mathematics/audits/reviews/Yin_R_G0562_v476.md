### Exterior lifts for the full seed family

The support contains exponents \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\), and the endpoints used in the individual height estimate are units.
A common zero of \(g,B\) would have \(T^{s-2}=-1\) and \(T^2+T+1=0\), which are incompatible.
The exterior list of \(B\) is nonempty since \(g\) has a real root greater than one and the absolute product of its roots is one.
The simplicity and absence of circle zeros in C391 apply to the full fixed seed family.
Consequently \(I=\sum_j\log|\tau_j|>0\) and \(\omega_*>0\) depend only on \(s\).
Every real or nonreal exterior zero is included separately, including both members of every conjugate pair.

In the variable \(u\), the first term of the section equation has linear coefficient \(\tau_jB'(\tau_j)\ne0\), and the other term on the contour is \(O_s(|\tau_j|^{-r}\exp(rK|\tau_j|^{-r}))\).
One \(K\) and then one sufficiently large lower bound on \(r\) work for the finite list of poles and all \(a,b\).
Distinct poles can be separated in the \(X^a\) variable, and the \(a\) local inverses over a single pole are disjoint.
The resulting \(at\) exterior roots exhaust the exact count.
The parent has \(2at\) off-circle roots in total, while \(d_P\gg_s a\sqrt r\) for each nontorsion factor.
Thus each such factor has a circle root for large \(r\), giving reciprocity by conjugation and irreducibility, including when there are no real exterior roots.

Summing log radii on a factor gives \(m(P)=(Ih_P/a)(1+O_s(e^{-cR}))\), since the number of its occupied lifts is at most \(h_P/\omega_*\).
The height theorem gives \(bm(P)/d_P=I+o_s(1)\) uniformly at first, using the degree lower bound and \(|g|=|B|\) on the circle.
The two comparisons yield \(d_P\asymp_s r h_P\gg_s R\).
Now \(\log(2d_P)/d_P=O_s(\log(2R)/R)\) even for unrestricted \(a\), by monotonicity at large degree.
Substitution gives exactly the relative error of (3).

### Leading coefficient in the product discriminant

Different \(b\) cannot share a nontorsion root since \(B(x^a)\ne0\) and subtraction gives \(x^{b-b'}=1\).
Thus the monic product \(Q\) is squarefree with \(|\operatorname{disc}Q|\ge1\).
For any fixed lift of \(\tau_j\), the difference of two exterior points has modulus \(O_s(|\tau_j|^{-\rho}/a)\) and \(|x\bar z-1|\ge |x||z|-1\gg_s1/a\).
The latter uses the positive minimum of the log moduli in the full pole list.
The loss for each ordered pair at this lift is at least \(\rho\log|\tau_j|-O_s(1)\).
Its reflected interior pair gives the same loss and is disjoint from all exterior pairs.
Conjugate inversion is injective on ordered pairs, even when the two exterior points are complex conjugates.

The disk product bound \(D^D\) follows by successively maximizing a polynomial modulus in each variable and applying Hadamard on the circle.
In the discriminant product of squared distances the radial normalization contributes \(2(D-1)m(Q)\).
There are \(N_\lambda(N_\lambda-1)\) ordered exterior pairs at \(\lambda\), yielding the factor two on the left of (5) after including the interiors.
Dividing by \(2\rho I(1-O_s(1/R))\) is valid uniformly since \(\omega_\lambda\ge\omega_*\).
The term \(D\log D\) then costs \(O_s(W\log(2D))\).
The measure term has upper bound
\[
 \frac{D\,m(Q)}{\rho I}(1+O_s(1/R))
 \le \frac{W^2}{a}\bigl(1+O_s(\eta+\varepsilon_R)\bigr),
\]
using \(D\le(1+\eta)\rho W(1+O_s(\varepsilon_R))\) and \(m(Q)=IW(1+O_s(e^{-cR}))/a\).
Thus the coefficient of \(W^2/a\) tends to one when the errors do.
Adding the diagonal contribution \(W\) gives (4), which can be compared with the identical leading term in (7).

### Slope intervals, incidences and primitive density

Select one factor per qualifying exponent once, so each subsequent family still comes from distinct directions.
On a dyadic weight interval \(H\le h_P<2H\), each slope subinterval of relative width at most \(\kappa_s\min(1,a/H^2)\) fits (4).
Dividing \([R,2R]\) into intervals of length at most \(R\kappa_s\min(1,a/H^2)\) realizes the claimed count with disjoint endpoint assignment.
Among the \(m\) selected factors incident to a fixed lift of weight \(\omega\), the weighted sum of squared incidences at all other lifts is at least \((W-\omega m)^2/(a-\omega)\).
Adding the fixed lift gives the excess \(a\omega(m-W/a)^2/(a-\omega)\) above \(W^2/a\).
This is at least \(\omega_*m^2/4\) since \(h_P\le a/2\).
For a single lift no selected factor qualifies, and an empty incident family contributes zero.
The error in (4) is absorbable here because \(\eta H^2/a\le\kappa_s\) and \(H^2\varepsilon_R/a\le K^2\varepsilon_R/a\), with both upper constants chosen sufficiently small.

For \(m>0\), the remaining inequality is \(m^2\ll_s W\log(2D)\), with \(W\le2Hm\) and \(D\ll_s RHm\).
Writing \(v=m/H\) yields \(v\ll_s\log(2+RH^2)+\log^+v\), so \(m\ll_s H\log(2+RH)\) since \(R\ge1\).
Summing over \(at\) lifts counts each chosen factor at least \(H\) times because each occupied weight is at most one.
Summing the resulting per-window bounds uses \(O_s(\log(2+K))\) dyadic ranges and \(\sum H^2\ll K^2\), proving (6).

For a reducible core the smallest factor weight is at most half the total \(a\).
The condition at \(K=a/2\) becomes \(a\varepsilon_R/4\le c_s\), as permitted after choosing the constant in the stated first-exponent range.
The term \(a\log(2+a/2)+a^2/4\) is \(O(a^2)\) for all integers \(a\ge1\).
Counting complete periods gives at least \((\lfloor R\rfloor-1)\varphi(a)\) primitive exponents, and the prime-power totient formula gives \(\varphi(a)\ge\sqrt{a/2}\).
The resulting exceptional fraction is \(O_s(R^{-1/4}(\log(2R))^{1/4})\) uniformly throughout the claimed range.
An irreducible core contains all the exterior roots, hence its log measure is \(I+O_s(e^{-cR})\).
These bounds leave open the existence of individual exceptional sequences carrying factors of vanishing positive measure.
