# Ramification loss in a nonnormal subfield and height growth for Salem numbers

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T03:19 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the whole [C345 v734](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v734.md#statement), including its final comparison between the trace and Salem splitting fields, and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing v734 and consulted the fixed argument and its direct inputs without its development discussion or any earlier verdict on it.
The previously reviewed prime-power determinant was reused at its stated scope for general number fields, including ramified primes.

For each prime, the local different exponent is at least the ramification index minus one, also in wild characteristic, as follows from the unramified subextension and its Eisenstein uniformizer calculation.
The discriminant exponent sums these local exponents weighted by the residue degrees.
Together with \(\sum_{v\mid p}e_v f_v=s\), this proves the first inequality of (3) with precisely \(\delta_p\) in the nonnormal field.
Discriminant transitivity up to \(K\) and the monic power-basis bound in the original generator give the second inequality.
Neither step charges the discriminant of the normal closure.

Given the optimizing local lift \(x\) and power \(k\), a uniform conjugator gives both a uniformly distributed conjugate of \(x^k\) and a uniformly distributed prime \(P\) over \(p\).
The conjugate being in the chosen \(H\)-coset implies the full set of lifts at \(P\) meets that coset.
No conditional uniformity of the chosen conjugate among those lifts is required.
Indeed, writing \(U\) for the indicator that the conjugate is in the chosen coset and \(V\) for the indicator that the full lift set meets it, one has \(U\le V\).
The distribution for a freshly sampled uniform lift gives the probability \(\mathbb E[V/e_v]\) for the chosen coset.
This is at least \(\mathbb E U-\mathbb E(1-1/e_v)\), including when \(U\) and \(v\) are dependent.
The restriction probability \(e_vf_v/s\) evaluates the latter expectation to \(\delta_p\), giving (4).
Conjugation transports all the data between base primes and a central \(b\)-th power has singleton class and coset mass one.

On the small-measure interval the total logarithmic prime weight is comparable to \(T^2\log(2T)\), while the total ramification loss is at most \(\log d+2\).
Thus the lower bound from (5) survives with a fixed proportion by enlargement of \(A_\eta\).
In the relative determinant \(T\log(2T)\gg A_\eta\log(2d)\) absorbs the archimedean term even after inserting the factor \(\eta\).
There are \(O(T^2)\) powered nodes per relative conjugate, each exponent at most \((2T^2\log(2T))^b\).
Division of the saving \(T^3\log(2T)\) by the height coefficient gives \(T^{-(2b+1)}(\log(2T))^{1-b}\).
In terms of \(d\) the iterated logarithm therefore has exponent \(2b+1+1-b=b+2\).
The identity \(Dh(\alpha)=m/s\) uses both integrality and the original degrees.
The resulting polylogarithmic bound on \(s\) makes \(\log(2D)\) comparable to \(\log(2d)\) and gives the same comparison for \(g_b\), enlarging constants for bounded \(d\).

For the unrestricted-height interval a large \(A_\eta\) makes the entire loss \(\log d+2m\) at most \(\eta/2\) times the interval's logarithmic weight, by the prime number theorem on \([X,2X]\).
Some prime has \(w_{p,k_p}\ge\eta/2\), and \(p\ge (2D)^{4/\eta}\) then leaves at least \((\eta/4)\log p\) after the single-prime expense \(\log(2D)\).
The bounded power cost gives (6).
If \(D\le D_0\) and \(m\le\log(2d)\), rearrangement would give \(d=O_{b,\eta,D_0}((\log(2d))^{b+1})\), impossible at growing degree.
In the remaining large-degree case \(X\) is comparable to \(m\), giving \(m^{b+1}\gg d\log(2m)\), hence first \(\log(2m)\gg_b\log(2d)\) and then (2).
For bounded \(d\) the stated single-prime bound supplies a positive constant when \(m\le1\); the other heights already have a positive constant lower bound.
All these conclusions using (5) require it only on the interval for the relevant assertion.

For an actual Salem number, its two inverse roots over each trace are distinct and the trace field has index two.
All traces are real while the normal closure of the trace field is totally real, so the Salem number, having nonreal conjugates, lies outside that closure.
In the full splitting field the distinct conjugates of ordinary complex conjugation each fix one inverse pair and flip all others.
Conjugation by a central element fixes each of these involutions and hence fixes each pair as a set, including in degree four.
It fixes each trace, giving inclusion of the trace closure in the field fixed by the full center and the stated quotient surjection.
The ADZ14 comparison concerns application at a fixed prime via a uniform bound on local degree in that center-fixed field.
Such a bound would pass down to the trace closure; a bound only on the quotient by the trace group's own center concerns a smaller field.
The paragraph does not assert that (2) is optimal or exclude other applications of existing height results.
If the entire trace group has exponent dividing \(b\), applying v731 to its normal closure bounds \(h(\alpha)\) below by a constant since the relative degree there is at most \(D_0\), giving the advertised linear bound after multiplication by \(d\).
