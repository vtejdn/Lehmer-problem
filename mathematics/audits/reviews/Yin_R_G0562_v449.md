# Independent review of power and trace-field certificates on the 72 Salem inputs

## The field exclusions and their finite scope

On 2026-11-18T17:36 UTC a separately assigned agent for Sheng Yin checked the complete [J174 argument](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0174/readme.md#powers), its final program and all three certificate tables.
The reviewer had no role in developing J174 and received its fixed mathematics without development discussion, author explanations of the proof or a prior finding on J174.
The computation author's cross-check is described in the fixed text.
The reviewer had previously contributed L200, used upstream in the definition of half-mass admissibility, and saw the separate J173 finding while inspecting the requested review format.
All power exclusions, trace-field and intersection conclusions, the commensurable minima and the stated range of the rational-transformation result hold.

### Source coefficients and local power tests

I read the header and all 72 data lines of the [registered native catalogue](../../literature/pdf/Mos26b/SalemList.txt).
Its positive-root orientation and the author's completeness statement below \(1.3\) through degree 44 are precisely the source facts used here.
The earlier [J173 review](Yin_R_G0562_v445.md#finding) records its comparison of the native table with the identified public revision.
My numerical checks start from the local native coefficients.
For each degree \(2s\) I reflected the coefficients about the central term and built \(Q(t)\) independently from the center coefficient and the polynomials \(C_0=2,\ C_1=t,\ C_{j+1}=tC_j-C_{j-1}\).
I checked ranks 1 through 72, both strict signs at \(7/6,49/37\) by rational evaluation, and the maximum degree 46.

If \(\beta^p=\sigma\) with \(\beta\in\mathbb Q(\sigma)\), the generated fields coincide.
Both \(\beta\) and \(\beta^{-1}\) are integral by the monic equations over the algebraic integers.
Under the common field embeddings one conjugate has modulus \(\sigma^{1/p}>1\) and all others have modulus at most one.
Thus Voutier's bound applies at the original degree \(d\) and gives \(p<(\log\sigma)(\log(3d))^3/2\).
I checked with rational arithmetic that the two indicated partial exponential sums exceed \(49/37\) and 138, respectively, and that their resulting upper bound on \(p\) is below 17.
The resulting six primes are exactly the ones in the table.

For each of the 432 distinct rank-prime pairs I checked by trial division that \(\ell\) is prime, that \(p\mid\ell-1\), and that the proposed root is nonzero.
By summing the coefficients times the individual modular powers I verified \(P(a)=0\), the exact displayed nonzero value of \(P'(a)\), and the displayed value of \(a^{(\ell-1)/p}\ne1\).
At rank 1 for \(p=2\) this gives \(a=17\), derivative 3 and character value 22 in \(\mathbb F_{23}\).
The maximum witness prime is 727.
Hensel lifts the indicated simple root to \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), giving an embedding of the number field defined by the irreducible \(P\).
An image of \(\beta\) would be a local unit and would reduce to a \(p\)-th root of \(a\), incompatible with that character value.

Taking positive powers preserves a Salem field because the exterior conjugate is unique and no two conjugates can have the same power after transporting one to that exterior conjugate.
Consequently a positive relation \(\eta^v=\sigma^u\) in coprime exponents places the Salem number \(\eta\) in \(\mathbb Q(\sigma)\).
Bézout then gives a \(v\)-th root of \(\sigma\) in that field, and any prime divisor of \(v>1\) would give a forbidden prime root.
Hence all commensurable Salem outputs are the positive powers of the listed input.
To check the claimed minima, I factored each full polynomial modulo two, found the orders of the roots of each irreducible factor by successively stripping prime factors from \(2^{\deg g}-1\), and combined their degrees with their multiplicities.
All 72 resulting weights and least exponents, using the reconstructed \(Q(0),Q'(0)\) and the fixed admissibility criterion, agree with J173.
Minimizing the logarithmic measure \(u\log\sigma\) over those powers therefore gives the stated \(e(\sigma)\log\sigma\).

### Frobenius and intermediate fields

At each indicated squarefree prime the roots of the trace polynomials lie in a common unramified splitting extension locally, with the residue Frobenius cycles lifting uniquely.
An embedding of the proposed subfield into the source field gives an equivariant map from the embeddings of the source onto those of the proposed subfield.
All fibers have the same size \(h\), the ratio of the trace degrees.
A source cycle of length \(a\) mapping to a cycle of length \(b\) covers each of its points \(a/b\) times, so \(b\mid a\) and the lengths assigned to that target must sum to \(hb\).
This applies to distinct target cycles of the same length as well.

I recovered the factor-degree multisets independently using polynomial arithmetic modulo each prime, a derivative gcd for squarefreeness and the numbers \(\deg\gcd(Q,X^{\ell^j}-X)\).
Subtracting contributions of the proper divisors of \(j\) gives the number of degree-\(j\) factors multiplied by \(j\).
For the compatibility test I filled one *target* cycle at a time, trying every submultiset of available lengths divisible by \(b\) summing to \(hb\), and recursed on the unused lengths.
This independently tests the source-cycle assignment used in the certificate program.
Equal-degree pairs require identical sorted cycle lists.

Of the 5112 ordered pairs of distinct ranks, exactly 4627 fail trace-degree divisibility.
For the other 485 I verified each stored prime, both displayed degree lists, the failed equivariant assignment and the absence of an exclusion at any smaller prime by this test.
The first-exclusion counts at \(2,3,5,7,11\) are \(298,142,28,16,1\).
For all 39 composite trace degrees I independently checked the squarefree factor list and that the indicated prime factor degree \(r\) is strictly greater than half the trace degree.
The largest prime of reduction in this table is 31.
Each such \(r\) divides the order of a permutation in the rational Galois group.
A proper system of \(b\) blocks of size \(c\) induced by an intermediate field would have \(b,c\le s/2<r\) and its permutation stabilizer has order \((c!)^b b!\), excluding \(r\).
The remaining 33 fields have prime degree.
For arbitrary embedded copies of two distinct catalogue trace fields, any intersection larger than \(\mathbb Q\) would be the whole of each by primitivity and contradict the ordered-pair exclusions.

### Transformation range and reproduction

A rational trace value with nonzero denominator lies in the source trace field.
If it is a Salem trace, twice its field degree is the degree of the output Salem number, which is therefore at most the degree of the source.
For a listed source of degree at most 44 and an output below \(1.3\), the catalogue author's stated completeness supplies an output rank.
The field exclusions force the ranks to agree.
For a source already below \(1.3\) this gives precisely the remainder test \(Q_i\mid U-XV\), since the positive exterior value is uniquely determined by its trace.
The larger-value strip is covered for listed sources and the degree-46 comparisons apply to listed pairs.

I ran the final program with SymPy 1.14.0 and its output directory redirected to a disposable directory.
It reproduced the bytes of each of the three fixed TSVs.
I checked that the final writer closes a unique same-directory temporary file before replacing each table.
For a simulated replacement failure the existing table retained its bytes and the temporary file was removed.
