## Saturation in the original field, degrees, trace generators and relative norms

At 2026-11-24T21:41 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v43.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v43, including the clarified strict degree threshold and nontorsion trace-generator hypothesis.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v43.
The saturation identity, exact high-degree spectrum, trace-field and relative-norm estimates, and the unrestricted zero-infimum criterion follow at their stated scopes.

If \(y\in K^\times\) is nontorsion and dependent with \(x\), a relation \(y^b=\xi x^a\) with \(b>0,\ a\ne0\) and \(\xi\in\mu(K)\) implies that every finite valuation of \(y\) is zero.
Thus the saturation may be computed in \(\mathcal O_K^\times/\mu(K)\), a free abelian group by Dirichlet's unit theorem.
If the coordinates of \(x\) in a lattice basis have gcd \(s\), dividing by \(s\) gives a primitive vector.
Bézout shows its integer multiples exhaust the lattice points on this rational line.
Lifting to a unit \(\epsilon\) gives \(x=\omega\epsilon^s\), and the nontorsion elements on that line are precisely the \(\xi\epsilon^k\) with \(k\ne0\).
Conversely any such element is dependent with \(x\), since a power kills the two torsion factors.
This saturation takes place in the original field \(K\).

For \(j\ge1\), \((\xi\epsilon^k)^{js|\mu(K)|}=x^{kj|\mu(K)|}\) generates \(K\), since all positive powers of \(x\) do and inversion preserves a field.
The element on the left already belongs to \(\mathbb Q((\xi\epsilon^k)^j)\subseteq K\), proving equality of fields for every positive power of every indicated twist, including \(k<0\).
In particular \(\epsilon\) has degree \(d\).
Absolute height is invariant under torsion twists and scales by the absolute value of the exponent, so \(m(\epsilon)=m(x)/s\) and \(m(\xi\epsilon^k)=|k|m(\epsilon)\).

For a nontorsion \(y\in K^\times\) outside this line, \(\epsilon,y\) are multiplicatively independent and their joint field is \(K\).
The rank-two height product at joint degree \(d\), multiplied by \(d\,d_y\) for \(d_y=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\), gives
\[
 m(\epsilon)m(y)\ \ge\ c\,d_y(\log(3d))^{-212}
\]
with an absolute positive \(c\).
This computation uses the actual individual degree of \(y\) and applies also to nonintegral \(y\) via its absolute Weil height.
Thus if \(m(y)\le M\), an independent \(y\) has \(d_y\le c^{-1}M m(\epsilon)(\log(3d))^{212}\).
When \(d\) is strictly above that threshold, every positive measure at most \(M\) realized above it comes from the line and occurs at degree \(d\).
Conversely \(\epsilon^k\) realizes each integer multiple \(k\,m(\epsilon)\) with \(1\le k\le\lfloor M/m(\epsilon)\rfloor\) at that degree.
Degrees at or below the threshold remain outside this classification, and if \(d\) itself is at or below it there are no field elements of degree above it.

For reciprocal \(x\), inversion is a nontrivial automorphism of \(K\) with fixed field \(\mathbb Q(x+x^{-1})\), by the quadratic satisfied over the trace field.
All nontorsion elements of that proper field are outside the line, whose elements of infinite order generate \(K\).
A nontorsion trace-field generator has \(d_y=d/2\) and hence satisfies (3) by the independent product bound.
Integrality of \(y\) and the existence of a real embedding are unnecessary.
Torsion generators fall outside (3), as is necessary already for \(x=2+\sqrt3\) with trace field \(\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q(1)\).
On the indicated shrinking sequence, \(m(\epsilon)\le m(x)\to0\) and \(d/(\log(3d))^{212}\to\infty\), proving the asserted divergence.

For every proper subfield \(H\) the norm \(u=N_{K/H}\epsilon\) is a unit with \(h(u)\le[K:H]h(\epsilon)\) by the product over relative embeddings and subadditivity of height.
Multiplication by \([\mathbb Q(u):\mathbb Q]\le[H:\mathbb Q]\) gives \(m(u)\le d\,h(\epsilon)=m(\epsilon)\).
If \(u\) is nontorsion, its membership in \(H\) excludes it from the line, so the independent bound gives \([\mathbb Q(u):\mathbb Q]\ll m(\epsilon)^2(\log(3d))^{212}\).
The alternative permits torsion, and the degree bound concerns the field of \(u\) itself.

Under the unrestricted zero-infimum hypothesis one may choose nontorsion unit minima of positive measures tending to zero and replace each by the measure-preserving all-power representative.
Saturation in each resulting field leaves its degree unchanged and can reduce its measure by the factor \(s\).
The primitive generators have positive measures tending to zero, so their degrees tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
Smyth's bound makes \(x,\epsilon\) reciprocal eventually, and the two inverters agree on \(x^{|\mu(K)|}=\epsilon^{s|\mu(K)|}\), a generator of \(K\).
Their trace fields therefore coincide and the stated internal-spectrum and trace restrictions apply on that sequence.
The converse uses the integer minimal polynomials of the primitive generators, and establishes the zero-infimum equivalence without proving that such a shrinking sequence exists.
