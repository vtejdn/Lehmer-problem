## The positive radical attains the minimum

At 2026-11-17T09:59 UTC an agent outside development of v205 reviewed the [fixed submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#decision-positive-radical-minimum-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed statements and arguments without development discussion, author explanations, exploration notes or earlier findings on v205.
The reviewer had independently examined v202, which supplies the irreducibility tests cited in the prime-block application.
The proof establishes (1), both orbit alternatives, the count (2) under its stated hypotheses, and the equivalence for sequences of normalized blocks.

A positive \(k\)-th root in \(K\) generates \(K\), since its \(k\)-th power generates that field.
Both it and its inverse are integral, and the degree-height formula gives it logarithmic measure \(B/k\).
The coefficient bound \(2^d\exp B\) leaves finitely many degree-\(d\) integral minimal polynomials and finitely many roots.
Distinct positive exponents give distinct positive roots for \(0<\beta\ne1\), also when \(\beta<1\).
For admissible \(k,l\), a Bézout combination of \(\operatorname{lcm}(k,l)/k\) and \(\operatorname{lcm}(k,l)/l\) gives the positive \(\operatorname{lcm}(k,l)\)-th root in \(K\).
The admissible exponents therefore have a largest member \(r\) and consist of its divisors.

Each absolute conjugacy class above \(S\) meets the fiber \(\alpha^h=\beta\), since an embedding of the field of the \(h\)-th power extends to the field of \(\alpha\).
For a root there, put \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):K]\).
The tower formula and the height power rule give \(m(\alpha)=eB/h\).
The relative norm \(w\) satisfies \(w^h=\beta^e\) even for a nonreal root.
In the chosen real embedding, its absolute value is one of \(w,-w\) and equals \(\beta^{e/h}\).
If \(e/h=a/b\) is reduced, Bézout for \(a,b\) expresses \(\beta^{1/b}\) as a product of integral powers of \(|w|\) and \(\beta\).
Consequently \(b\mid\gcd(h,r)=t\), which gives \(h/t\mid e\).
For the positive radical, \(X^{h/t}-\beta^{1/t}\) gives the opposite degree bound and hence degree \(d h/t\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Its measure \(B/t\) is the minimum, so full degree is equivalent to \(t=1\), and \(h=r\) attains \(B/r\) over all dilations.
The nonzero simple roots of \(S\) each have \(h\) distinct preimages, giving the stated multiplicities.

In the fiber put \(u=\beta^{1/t}\) and \(s=h/t\).
Two roots there are absolutely conjugate exactly when they are \(K\)-conjugate, since every embedding taking one to the other fixes their common \(h\)-th power.
The quotient \(\alpha^s/u=\xi\) runs over \(\mu_t\).
Above each orbit, fixing one \(\xi\) reduces conjugacy to the stabilizer over \(L=K(\xi)\).
For an irreducible factor of \(X^s-u\xi\) of relative degree \(j\), \(L\subseteq K(\alpha)\), so its absolute degree is \(d f_\xi j\).
The \(s\)-th power of its root's norm to \(L\) equals \(u^j\xi^j\).
Dividing this norm by the positive \(u^{j/s}\) gives a root of unity, so the real number \(u^{j/s}\) lies in \(K(\mu_\infty)\).
Writing \(a/b=j/s\) in lowest terms and combining it with \(u\) gives \(y=u^{1/b}\) in a finite \(K(\zeta_M)\).
This is Galois over \(K\) with abelian group by restriction to the cyclotomic field.
Complex conjugation fixes \(K\) pointwise and commutes with that group, so every \(K\)-conjugate of \(y\) is real.
For odd \(b\) the only real solution to \(Y^b=u\) is \(y\), while for even \(b\) all solutions in \(\mathbb R\) have square \(y^2\).
Thus \(b\mid r/t\) for odd \(b\), and \(b/2\mid r/t\) for even \(b\).
Since \(b\mid s\) and \(\gcd(s,r/t)=1\), the only denominators are one and two.
With \(1\le j\le s\) the degrees are \(s\) and \(s/2\), and a half-degree factor requires exactly one other of that degree by separability and the sum of degrees.
Multiplication by \(d f_\xi\) and the radical measure identity give the two pairs of absolute degrees and measures claimed.

An odd \(s\) precludes the denominator two.
If \(\beta\) has a negative real conjugate, its corresponding real embedding rules out an even \(r\), and \(u\) has negative image because \(t\) is odd.
Any extension of this embedding to \(K(\zeta_M)\) commutes with conjugation on the two generators \(K\) and \(\zeta_M\), using the real image of \(K\) and inversion on roots of unity.
It would therefore send a real square root of \(u\) to a real number whose square is negative.
With no split, the number of orbits of primitive \(j\)-th roots equals \(\varphi(j)/[K(\zeta_j):K]\).
The restriction subgroup on \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_j)\) has fixed field \(K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_j)\) because \(K(\zeta_j)/K\) is Galois.
The displayed quotient therefore equals the intersection degree, proving (2) for all divisors, including \(j=1\).

In the example \(w^{-1}=11-2\sqrt{30}>0\), and \(y=\sqrt5+\sqrt6\) has inverse \(\sqrt6-\sqrt5\).
If \(y\in K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{30})\), then \((y-y^{-1})/2=\sqrt5\in K\), contradicting the distinction of the quadratic fields.
The positive fifth root \(w\) lies in \(K\) whereas the positive square root \(w^2y\) of \(\beta\) does not, giving \(t=5\) at \(h=10\) regardless of other divisors of \(r\).
For primitive \(\xi\) of order five, \(\xi+\xi^{-1}\) satisfies \(Z^2+Z-1=0\), so \(K(\xi)\) contains \(\sqrt5,\sqrt6\) and \(y\).
Taking a square root of \(\xi\) inside \(\mu_5\) gives both distinct linear factors of \(X^2-w\xi\).
As an exact check, the trace of \(w^5\) is \(5100502\), and rational factorization of \(X^{20}-5100502X^{10}+1\) gave simple factors of degrees \(4,8,8\), including \(X^4-22X^2+1\) over the trivial orbit.

For normalized blocks in the v198 range, the single root in a disk with positive real center is real and exterior and hence belongs to \(S\).
An even block gives a conjugate of opposite sign by the same argument, so (2) then counts every noncyclotomic factor after dilation.
The v202 certificates force the corresponding primes to avoid \(r\) by (1).
For fixed block primes there are uniform constants \(0<B_0\le B\le B_1\) from v198.
Along any sequence of noncyclotomic factors with measures tending to zero the bound \(B_0/r\) forces \(r\to\infty\).
Conversely, along a subsequence with \(r\to\infty\), taking \(h=r\) realizes \(B/r\le B_1/r\to0\).
Whether these exponents are unbounded for the normalized family remains open.
