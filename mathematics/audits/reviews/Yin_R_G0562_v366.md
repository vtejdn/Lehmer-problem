## Independent finding on the repeated-zero argument

At 2026-11-18T10:56 UTC a separately assigned reviewer for Sheng Yin checked all five sections of [the fixed ledger argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v152.md#separation).
The reviewer had no part in its development and received its mathematical text and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior finding on this argument.
The row congruences, ideal-power containment and both resultant divisors follow in their stated ranges, including the endpoint \(e=p\) of the square-exponent bound.
The degree and measure estimates, universal projector degree and multiplicity-one example also follow.

### Separating the rows and their jets

Over \(\mathbb Z_p\), the ideals generated by \((T-r_j)^e\) are pairwise comaximal because each difference \(r_i-r_j\) is a unit.
Their product is monic of degree \(ke\), so reducing any Chinese remainder representative gives an integral operator of order at most \(ke-1\).
On a monomial in row \(j\), \(E=x\,d/dx\) has eigenvalue \(r_j+pm\), and the error in the prescribed residue is divisible by \(p^e\).
For \(b>0\), \(E(P^bG)\) is divisible by \(P^{b-1}\).
Acting on \(F\) therefore leaves at least \(a-ke+1\) powers of \(P\), proving (1).
Putting the falling factorial of \(T-r_i\) with spacing \(p\) in the target row multiplies its coefficient at \(r_i+pm\) by \(p^t t!\binom{m}{t}\).
Evaluation kills the remaining \(P\)-multiple, and the removed factor \(\alpha^{r_i}\) is a unit.
This gives (2), also when \(m<t\).
Since \(A=\mathbb Z_p[x]/(P)\) is free as a \(\mathbb Z_p\)-module and \(\beta\) is a unit, division by \(p^t t!\beta^t\) gives the claimed exponent \(e-t\) whenever \(t<e\le p\).
In particular \(t=p\) never occurs at \(e=p\).

At a good prime the reduction of \(A\) is finite and reduced, so Frobenius carries its power basis to a basis.
The determinant is consequently a unit before reduction, giving a basis for \(A\) in powers of \(\beta\), hence \(\deg Q=d\).
The characteristic polynomial of multiplication by \(\beta\) is \(Q\), with reductions of its roots obtained by raising the distinct roots of \(\overline P\) to the \(p\)-th power.
They remain distinct, making \(Q'(\beta)\) invertible in every local factor.
For any \(e\) satisfying \(ke\le a\), (1) therefore gives coefficientwise \(p^e\)-divisibility of the remainder modulo \(Q\).
For \(e\le p\), in the Taylor coefficient of order \(j<e\) of \(\sum_i C_i Q^i\) at \(\beta\), terms with \(i>j\) vanish and the \(i=j\) term is \(C_j(\beta)Q'(\beta)^j\).
Induction makes each term with \(i<j\) divisible by \(p^{e-j}\), because its entire polynomial already has this divisibility.
The derivative bound and the power basis now give \(p^{e-j}\mid C_j\) coefficientwise, proving (3).

### Resultant valuations

Dividing out common integer content preserves \(P^a\)-divisibility by Gauss's lemma and leaves some row nonzero modulo \(p\).
In (3) this row reduces to a nonzero multiple of \(\overline Q^e\), so its degree is at least \(de\).
Removing initial powers of \(X\) preserves (3) since \(Q(0)\) is a unit and \((p,Q)/(p,Q)^e\) is nilpotent.
For coprime polynomial rows, the Sylvester cokernel has finite \(\mathbb Z_p\)-length \(v_p(\operatorname{Res}(U,V))\).
The domain maps to zero modulo \(I^e\) and the target contains representatives of every class, since division by monic \(Q^e\) leaves degree below \(de\).
In the basis \(X^iQ^j\), \(0\le i<d\), \(0\le j<e\), the modulus for a coefficient in layer \(j\) is precisely \(p^{e-j}\), by expanding the generators \(p^{e-h}Q^h\).
The quotient length is therefore \(de(e+1)/2\).
Using instead \((p^e,Q)\) gives length \(de\) for arbitrary \(e\), and the degree needed for the same surjection follows from the nonzero reduction divisible by \(\overline Q\).

For the stronger bound, consider a normalized linear factor \(L=uX+v_0Y\) of a primitive homogenized row.
Its coefficients are integral and one is a unit, and Gauss multiplicativity shows that the leftover scalar is a unit regardless of the leading coefficient of the row.
If \(L(\beta_j,1)\) reduces to zero, then \(u\) is a unit, the coefficient of \(Z\) in \(L(\beta_j+pZ,1)\) has valuation one, and the disk valuation is \(t_j=\min(1,v(L(\beta_j,1)))\).
If the evaluation reduces to a nonzero element the disk valuation is zero.
Distinct reductions of the nodes imply that a given factor has positive \(t_j\) for at most one \(j\).
Since \(Q(\beta_j+pZ)\) is coefficientwise divisible by \(p\), a row in \(I^e\) has disk valuation at least \(e\) at every node.
For a row of content valuation \(c\), its primitive part thus has factor weights summing to at least \(\max(e-c,0)\) at every node.

The determinant of two primitive factors is a difference of their values at a node weighted by integral coefficients.
Its valuation is at least the smaller of their two truncated weights, hence at least their product, since the weights are in \([0,1]\).
For each pair at most one node gives a positive product, allowing summation over all nodes and then all pairs.
For two primitive rows this contributes at least \(de^2\) to the homogeneous resultant valuation, which is also the ordinary resultant valuation.
If \(U\) has content valuation \(c>0\), the primitive part contributes at least \(de\max(e-c,0)\) and its scalar contributes \(c\deg V\ge cde\).
This argument covers constant rows and nonmonic rows with nonunit leading coefficient.
In the example \(Q^e,Q^e+p^e\), each of the \(de\) roots counted with multiplicity of the monic first row gives second-row value \(p^e\), proving equality for the ideal implication.

### Degree, measure and necessity of the projector order

The rows of the Sylvester matrix have Euclidean norms \(\|U\|_2,\|V\|_2\) with multiplicities \(\deg V,\deg U\).
The sparse coefficient bound for \(T\ge2\) applies to the primitive \(F\), whose coefficients include those of each row, including after the monomial removals.
Combining it with Hadamard's inequality and multiplicativity of Mahler measure gives (5).
The degree sum is at least \(de\), so its positive term is at most \(e\log p\).
For the nontorsion units in the stated uniform comparison, \(d\ge2\), \(e/a\le1/2\) and \(1/m(P)<\log^3(3d)/2\) give the stated \(O(\log^4(3p)/p)\) relative upper bound.
These applications use DS17 and Voutier at their recorded statement scope.

For a universal indicator, the values at \(r+pX\) for \(0\le X<e\) determine the coefficients \(p^j H^{[j]}(r)\) modulo \(p^e\) through a Vandermonde matrix invertible for \(e\le p\).
Terms of order \(j\ge e\) vanish in that modulus.
Reduction modulo \(p\) therefore forces the full constant jet at each class.
For \(k=2\) the given polynomial for the class \(1\) has these jets and its leading coefficient is a signed \(\binom{2e-2}{e-1}\), a unit for \(p>2e-2\), including \(e=1\).
Uniqueness of the representative in degrees below \(2e\) proves the lower bound.
For general fixed \(k,e\), in the Taylor coefficient of order \(e-1\) of \(\prod_{u=1}^{k-1}(u+Z)^{-e}\) all summands have the same sign and positive magnitude.
The resulting rational leading coefficient survives for all sufficiently large \(p\), so the same jet argument proves necessity of degree \(ke-1\) on the indicated classes.

Finally the power basis for \(\beta\) at a good prime implies \(p\nmid [\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\beta]]=c\).
Multiplication by \(c\) sends \(\alpha^r\) into the smaller order, giving the required integral \(W\), which is nonzero because \(\alpha\) is a unit.
The exponents in the two rows are distinct modulo \(p\), the resulting polynomial vanishes at \(\alpha\), and a constant row \(c\) makes its resultant a \(p\)-adic unit.
Any removed common integer content is also prime to \(p\).
This verifies the asserted multiplicity-one obstruction independently of the universal operator lower bound.
