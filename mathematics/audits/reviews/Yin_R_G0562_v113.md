## The real-place constant, radial smoothing and the linear-degree example

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(x)\) and normalize its local degrees to sum to one over each rational place.
The exclusions on \(y\) allow the product formula for both \(y\) and \(y^2-1\).
For a finite place with \(|y|_v\le1\) both numerator factors have modulus at most one, while at \(|y|_v>1\) the local quotient is \(|y|_v^{-a}\).
At an archimedean place inversion preserves \(G_v\), and taking \(r=\min(|y|,|y|^{-1})\) gives its stated squared expression.
The maximizing value of \(r^a(1-r^2)\) occurs at \(r^2=a/(a+2)=\varphi^{-2}\), with maximum \(\varphi^{-a}(1-\varphi^{-2})=c\).
The second square has square root \(2r^{a+1}|\sin\psi|\le2\chi(y)\).
After dropping finite factors, the geometric mean over archimedean embeddings bounds the height exponential from above and is at most their arithmetic mean of \(G_v\).
The resulting bound is \(c+2d^{-1}\sum_j\chi(R(x_j+x_j^{-1}))\), including the correct repetition when \(y\) belongs to a proper subfield.

The rational map \(W=R(z+z^{-1})\) has degree \(2k\), and reciprocal conjugation acts as complex conjugation on its values.
On the circle \(W\) takes values in the extended real line.
Thus its Cayley transform \(V\) has unit modulus there, including at poles of \(W\), and has no zero or pole on that boundary.
Removing its disk zeros and poles using their Blaschke factors leaves a function and its reciprocal holomorphic on the closed disk, both bounded by one there by the maximum principle.
There are at most \(4k\) factors counted with multiplicity.
The equality \(\chi(W)=|\tanh(\log |V|)|\) follows directly by subtracting and adding the squared moduli of \(W-i\) and \(W+i\).
The absolute logarithm of the product is bounded by the sum of the nonnegative \(-\log|B_b|\).
Subadditivity of \(\tanh\) and \(\tanh u\le1-e^{-2u}\) give the sum of the disk defects, with zeros and poles covered by limits.

For a disk factor of logarithmic distance \(v\ge\delta\), the triangle inequality compares its Poisson denominator at distance \(t+v\) with the denominator at \(v\), with constant at most two before squaring.
The numerator contributes \(1-e^{-2t}\le2\delta\).
For \(b=0\) the defect is simply \(1-e^{-2t}\) and the chosen Poisson kernel is one.
If \(v<\delta\), angles within \(\delta\) of the pole direction have \(\delta P_{e^{-\delta}}\gg1\).
At larger circular distance \(\omega\), the exact defect is \(O(\delta^2/\omega^2)\) by \(t+v\le2\), and the proposed majorant has that order uniformly through \(\pi\).
The sum of the majorants clipped at one therefore covers the strip and has first and second integral moments \(O(k\delta)\).
At distances at most \(\delta\), each denominator modulus changes by at most \(\delta\) and is at least \(1-e^{-\delta}\gg\delta\) at either argument, giving the required two-sided local comparison even after clipping.

Hadamard on the Vandermonde rows and the leading-coefficient discriminant factor give \(\log|\operatorname{disc}P|\le d\log d+2(d-1)m\), so \(\Delta\ge1\).
The total positive radial mass is \(m-\log|a_d|\), while the total negative radial mass in absolute value is \(m-\log|P(0)|\).
Both subtracted logarithms are nonnegative for the given primitive polynomial, so the half absolute radial mass restricted to nonreal inputs is at most \(m\).
In the smoothed pair computation, the loss \(2(d-1)\sum_j t_j^+\) combines with the discriminant correction \(2(d-1)\log|a_d|\) to yield exactly \(2(d-1)m\).
Ordered unequal pairs contribute \(d-1\) from smoothing, and the diagonal costs at most \(d\log(1/(1-e^{-2/d}))\le d\log d\) for \(d\ge2\).
This proves (4) with the stated \(\Delta\) for both nonmonic and unit-leading inputs.

The Fourier coefficients of \(H\) are the reflected power sums times \(e^{-\delta\ell}\) in absolute value.
For \(\delta\ge2/d\), comparison of their squares with the energy uses \(\ell e^{-2(\delta-1/d)\ell}\ll1/\delta\).
A subset with \(|t_j|\le\delta\) contributes Poisson kernels of logarithmic distance between \(\delta\) and \(2\delta\), each having mass bounded below on the arc of radius \(\delta\) about its own angle.
The local comparison therefore pays an absolute factor when replacing the sum of the angular majorant over this subset with \(\int wH\).
Cauchy–Schwarz on \(H-d\) gives the error \(\sqrt{k\Delta/d}\).
Real inputs have zero chordal defect, and there are at most \(2T/\delta\) nonreal inputs outside the strip.
In the nontrivial range \(k(\Delta+T)/d<1/16\), \(2/d+\sqrt{T/(kd)}\le1\) and gives both \(T/(d\delta)\le\sqrt{kT/d}\) and \(k\delta=2k/d+\sqrt{kT/d}\).
The first summand is absorbed using \(\Delta\ge1\), including \(T=0\).
The complementary range follows by \(\chi\le1\).

Since \(-\log c=(a+1)\log\varphi\), (1) gives the claimed threshold and the fixed positive height loss forces the stated degree bound.
A bounded full input measure and the nonzero integer discriminant give \(\Delta+T=O(\log(2d))\).
For a reduced nonconstant rational function, its equation over the output field has degree at most \(k\), and adjoining \(x\) after its trace costs at most two.
This proves the output degree and the total-measure consequence, while rational exceptional outputs and a pole give an equation over \(\mathbb Q\) and require \(2k\ge d\).
The comparison to C345v77 correctly distinguishes its bounded total output measure hypothesis.

In the example \(\lambda=i\varphi\) gives \(\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=i\), so \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) contains \(i,\sqrt5\) and has degree four.
Squaring gives \(\lambda^4+3\lambda^2+1=0\) and the four conjugates have the stated moduli.
Prime radicals in this fixed field would be units of degree at most four and positive height \(h(\lambda)/p\), contrary to finiteness at bounded degree and height for arbitrarily large \(p\).
If the radical extension had degree \(e<p\), its norm would yield a \(p\)-th power equal to \(\lambda^e\) in the base field, and Bézout would make \(\lambda\) itself a \(p\)-th power there.
Thus the composite polynomial is irreducible of degree \(4p\).
The product of its root derivative moduli gives \(p^{4p}|\operatorname{disc}P_0|^p\), since the product of all root moduli is one and every base root occurs \(p\) times after powering.
This cancels the \(\log p\) in the discriminant deficit, with \(m=2\log\varphi\) and \(T\le m\).
The degree-\(p\) trace polynomial maps the original trace to \(i\), a height-zero output in a field containing \(i\).
The example realizes the stated degree order with a trace field admitting no real embedding.
