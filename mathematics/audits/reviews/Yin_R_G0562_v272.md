# Signed Galois groups for the five-term Salem family

The large-prime trace-group alternatives, the full sign kernel for every prime \(q\equiv1\bmod5\), and the infinite subfamily with full signed symmetric group follow with the stated quantifiers.

### Prime-degree traces and Frobenius at two

At offset one the Salem theorem applies for every prime \(q\ge5\).
The unique exterior root \(\tau\) is real and lies in \((3/2,5/2)\), so \(m(\tau)=\log\tau\) stays bounded while its degree \(2q\) grows.
The torsion-quotient class of \(\tau\) is a singleton by its modulus, and conjugacy gives the same for all roots.
Every root has its inverse present, and any torsion-product mate must equal that inverse by the singleton property.
Distinct pairs have distinct traces because the trace determines \(X^2-tX+1\).
The faithful label action is precisely the splitting group of the traces.
Its transitivity and prime degree \(q\) make the entire set a minimal nontrivial block, so the natural-action result of C345 v270 yields \(A_q\le H_q\le S_q\) for sufficiently large \(q\).

In characteristic two \(P_q\) is \((X^{3q}-1)/(X^q-1)\), with simple roots of orders exactly \(3\) and \(3q\).
There is one trace from the order-three pair.
On the primitive \(3q\)-th roots, representatives of inversion classes can be chosen with exponent one modulo three.
Doubling the exponent and restoring this convention acts on the nonzero coordinate modulo \(q\) by multiplication by \(-2\).
No two distinct inverse pairs have the same trace, also in characteristic two, so the monic degree-\(q\) reduction \(W_q\) is squarefree.
Unramified Frobenius at two therefore realizes this permutation of trace roots.
The \(q-1\) upper traces split into \(h=(q-1)/\operatorname{ord}_q(-2)\) cycles, whose combined sign is \((-1)^{q-1-h}=(-1)^h\).
Writing \(-2=g^a\) for a generator of \(\mathbb F_q^*\) gives \(h=\gcd(a,q-1)\), odd precisely when \(-2\) is a nonsquare.
The supplementary laws of quadratic reciprocity give this sign for \(q\equiv5,7\bmod8\), selecting \(S_q\) among the large-prime alternatives.

### Squarefreeness of the trace at five

The endpoint values of \(C_j\) are \(C_j(2)=2\) and \(C_j(-2)=2(-1)^j\).
Differentiating the recurrence at \(2\) gives \(C_j'(2)=j^2\), and parity gives the displayed derivative at \(-2\).
Thus \(W_q(2)=-1,\ W_q(-2)=-5\), with \(W_q'(-2)=q^2+2(q-1)^2\equiv1\bmod5\) in the selected class.

Since \(5\mid q-1\), differentiation of \(X^{q-1}\) vanishes identically in characteristic five.
At any repeated root \(x\), put \(y=x^{q-1}\ne0\).
The specialized quadratic and its ordinary derivative both vanish at \(x\).
Its discriminant reduces to \(4(y^4-y^3-y+1)=4(y-1)^2(y^2+y+1)\), and its product of roots is \(y^{-2}=x^2\).
In the second branch \(y\) has exact order three, and \(x=\pm y^{-1}\) has order divisible by three.
On the other hand \(x^q=xy=\pm1\) forces that order to divide \(2q\), a contradiction.
In the first branch \(y=1\) gives \(x=\pm1\), and \(P_q(1)=W_q(2)=-1\) eliminates \(+1\).
A repeated trace necessarily pulls back to a repeated root even at a ramification point of \(X\mapsto X+X^{-1}\).
The only surviving candidate is \(-2\), whose trace derivative is a unit.
Thus the collision of \(x\) with its inverse at \(-1\) gives a simple trace.
The entire trace splitting field at five embeds locally in the unramified extension lifting the finite splitting field of this squarefree reduction.
As a separate computational check, recurrence and polynomial Euclidean division gave \(\gcd(W_q,W'_q)=1\) modulo both two and five for \(q=11,31,41,61,71,101\).

### The full kernel and the infinite subfamily

Complex conjugation fixes the real roots and inverts the \(q-1\) remaining pairs, hence acts trivially on every trace.
The conjugates of this sign vector by the transitive group include \(\mathbf 1+e_i\) for every \(i\).
Their pairwise sums generate the whole even subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^q\).
For \(D=\prod_i(x_i-x_i^{-1})\), multiplication of the two endpoint products gives \(D^2=\prod_i(t_i^2-4)=5\), independently of the chosen roots.
Every prime of \(T_q\) above five has ramification index one when \(q\equiv1\bmod5\), so five cannot be a square in \(T_q\).
It follows that \(D\) is moved by some element of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_q/T_q)\), and that element changes an odd number of signs.
This gives the full kernel even for those \(q\equiv1\bmod5\) below the threshold for the trace-group theorem.
The signed permutation image lies in the full inverse image of \(H_q\) in the wreath product and has its order \(2^q|H_q|\), proving equality without a further splitting assumption.

For a finite set of old odd primes, impose divisibility of \(N\) by their product and by five, and \(N\equiv2\bmod8\).
The coprime odd and dyadic moduli admit arbitrarily large such \(N\).
Then \(\Phi_5(N)>1\) is \(7\bmod8\).
Its Jacobi symbol for \(-2\) is negative, forcing a prime factor \(q\) with negative character, even if the integer is not squarefree.
The factor divides neither \(N\) nor five, and \(N\equiv1\bmod q\) would force \(q=5\).
Hence \(N\) has order exactly five modulo \(q\), giving \(q\equiv1\bmod5\).
Together with the negative character this gives \(q\equiv21\) or \(31\bmod40\), outside the original list.
The argument proves infinitude of their union as stated.
