## Sampling, localization and the integral obstruction

The choice of the two nodes works for every cut.
If the enclosing pair straddles the cut, its left node is within one mesh step of the middle arc, far enough from the first node that its predecessor exists.
The replacement pair lies before the cut, stays within \(2\Delta\) of the target, and lies in the phase arc because \(2\Delta<\pi/12\).
In either block the opposite parities give \(H\le A\) at one node and \(H\ge-A\) at the other, where \(A=K\max_{\rm pair}|b_j|^2\).
Continuity gives the intermediate bound even when the phase vanishes or changes sign.
The trace varies by at most \(2\Delta\) across the chosen pair.

The inverse images of all interpolation targets lie in the middle arc; the node traces are within \(4\Delta\) of their targets, hence have absolute value below one.
Since \(8\Delta<1/(2M)\), two intermediate traces whose indices differ by \(k\ge1\) are separated by at least \(k/(2M)\).
The absolute Lagrange denominators are therefore at least \(i!(M-i)!/(2M)^M\), and each numerator is at most \(3^M\) on \([-2,2]\).
Summing yields the stated \(K36^M\) bound.

The squared sine quotient is an even polynomial in \(\sin\theta\) of trigonometric degree at most \(2M-2\); the full product is even and has degree at most \(67M\).
For its Laurent polynomial \(L(z)\), the functions \(z^D L(z)\) on the disk and \(z^{-D}L(z)\) on the exterior are regular at zero and infinity, respectively.
This gives the claimed complex bound on each horizontal line and Cauchy's estimate \(\|T'\|_\infty\le eDS\).
On \(|t|\ge1\), the power of sine absorbs \(36^M\) since \(36(3/4)^{32}<1/2\), and \(2^{-M}\le M^{-2}\) from \(M=4\) onward.
On \(|t|<1\) a maximizing angle may be taken in the middle arc.
At the intermediate angle the kernel is at most one and its product with \(t^2\) is at most \(4/M^2\), including at zero by continuity.
Using \((|t|+2\Delta)^2\le2t^2+2(2\Delta)^2\) gives \(10K/M^2\) there.
The loss in passing back to the maximizer is at most \(2\Delta eDS<S/2\) when \(S>0\), since \(4\pi e\cdot67/100000<1/2\).
At the center both weights are one and stationary, so the global bound controls \(H(0)\), while division of the derivative bound by two gives \(|H'(0)|\le2010K/M\).

When \(M>2010K\), both bounds are strictly below one; for an integral polynomial this forces \(t^2\mid H\), including the zero polynomial.
For the exceptional parent the trace is \(t^2E_{s-1}\) and the grid sign expression has the stated phase, whose denominator exceeds \(1/8\) throughout the prescribed arc.
Thus \(K=8\) suffices independently of \(s\), and \(M\ge20000\) suffices for the two integral coefficients to vanish.
The coefficients \(1,1,4\) of the lifted neutral polynomial give zero quotient shift and remainder leading coefficient \(4-(n+1)+(n-1)=2\); \(\deg H\le M\le n-2\) preserves both.
The strict residues and endpoint then have exactly the signs required in C881 v10.
One real root in each of its \(n-1\) disjoint intervals and the two residual nonreal roots exhaust degree \(n+1\) with multiplicity, excluding the forced double root of \(Q\).
Since degree is integral, the exclusion through \(M\) gives the strict bound \(\deg H>p/100000\).
For the stated general sequence, \(K=o(p)\) implies \(M>2010K\) eventually; the same contradiction uses both the neutral double factor and the full integer root-count hypotheses.
I found no gap in these conclusions; this review does not establish optimal constants or existence of perturbations above the bound.
