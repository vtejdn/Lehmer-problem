# Separability at five and the Salem sign kernel

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T17:03 UTC a reviewer outside proof development inspected the complete [C345 v273 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v273.md#decision-universal-salem-inversions-submitted) for Yin from its fixed argument and cited interfaces.
The trace polynomial is squarefree modulo five for every prime \(q\ge5\), including \(q=5\).
The full sign kernel therefore holds throughout this range, with the signed symmetric group for the stated sufficiently large primes.

### Resultants and candidate roots

Substitution gives \(F(x,x^q)=xP_q(x)\), and on this parametrization \(D=xF_x+ayF_y\) equals \(x\) times the derivative of \(xP_q(x)\) in characteristic five.
The constant term of \(P_q\) rules out zero.
For \(F\) the coefficients in descending powers of \(y\) are \((x-2,x,x-2x^2)\), and for \(D\) they are \((x+2a(x-2),(1+a)x,x-4x^2)\).
I independently expanded the four-by-four Sylvester determinant of these two coefficient triples in each residue class, then factored over \(\mathbb F_5\).
The resulting scalars multiplying the submitted monic factorizations are respectively \(-1,-1,1,2,1\) in the order \(a=0,1,2,3,4\).
Common finite roots force that determinant to vanish even at a specialized \(x\) where the leading coefficient in \(y\) drops.

The candidate \(x=1\) fails since \(P_q(1)=-1\).
For \(x^2+x+1=0\), the order is three and \(x+x^{-1}=-1\).
Here \(C_q(-1)=-1\) since \(3\nmid q\), and \(C_{q-1}(-1)\) is either \(2\) or \(-1\), giving nonzero \(W_q(-1)\) in both cases.
The two extra candidates \(x=\pm2\) occur only for \(q=5\) and both have trace zero.
The recurrence gives \(C_5(0)=0\), \(C_4(0)=2\), so \(W_5(0)=-3\).
This leaves only \(x=-1\) as a possible repeated parent root.

Every root \(t\) of the trace polynomial has a preimage \(x\ne0\), by solving \(x^2-tx+1=0\).
If \(t\) has multiplicity at least two then so does every preimage in \(P_q\), so only \(t=-2\) remains to check.
The endpoint derivative formula \(C'_j(-2)=(-1)^{j-1}j^2\) gives \(W'_q(-2)=3q^2-4q+2\).
Its discriminant is \(-8\equiv2\bmod5\), a nonsquare.
Thus \(-2\) is simple for all five residues, even though \(x=-1\) is a repeated root of the parent.

### Consequences over the trace field

The squarefree monic reduction of \(W_q\) splits in a finite residue field.
Its roots lift by Hensel's lemma over the corresponding unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_5\), so every valuation of five in the trace splitting field is one.
For \(\Delta=\prod_i(x_i-x_i^{-1})\), the endpoint values give \(\Delta^2=W_q(2)W_q(-2)=5\).
Thus \(\Delta\) lies outside the trace splitting field and is negated by some element of the sign kernel, providing an odd inversion.
Complex conjugation fixes the unique real pair and flips every other pair.
Transitivity gives the conjugate vectors \(\mathbf 1+e_i\), whose pairwise sums generate the whole even subspace.
Together these elements give all \(2^q\) inversions without a lower threshold beyond \(q\ge5\).
The trace action contains \(A_q\) for sufficiently large \(q\) by the fixed trace result, and its Frobenius at two is odd for \(q\equiv5,7\bmod8\).
In these cases it is \(S_q\), and the signed image has the full wreath-product order.
