## Rotation identity and preservation under squaring

At 2026-11-21T09:46 UTC an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin recommends acceptance of the [v817 statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v817.md#statement) and its full [argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v817.md#proof).
The reviewer had no role in developing the argument.

The section is monic with constant term one, so the product of the moduli of the roots of each monic integral factor is one.
For every positive divisor \(h\mid k\), coprimality gives \(\gcd(h,l)=1\), and rotating \(z\) by \(\zeta\in\mu_h\) fixes \(U\) and multiplies \(V\) by \(\eta=\zeta^l\).
Subtracting the section equation at \(z\) gives exactly the displayed expression for \(P(\zeta z)\).
The two values \(y=aV\) and \(y'=-1-y\) have product \(B\), including when \(a=0\).
The product of \(\eta y-y'\) over nontrivial rotations is \((-1)^{h-1}D_h(B)\), since adjoining \(\eta=1\) gives \((-1)^{h-1}(y^h-y'^h)\) and the quotient is a polynomial identity.
This also gives the empty product for \(h=1\) and handles a zero quadratic discriminant.

For \(z\ne w\) the rotation product of absolute differences is \(|(z^h-w^h)/(z-w)|\), while the diagonal gives \(h|z|^{h-1}\).
Over all roots of \(S\) the diagonal therefore gives exactly \(h^{\deg S}\).
The distinct powers hypothesis makes the change between the two power bases nonsingular, and the absolute ratio of their Vandermonde determinants is the additive index.
Each pair internal to \(S\) occurs twice and each complementary pair once.
The oriented cross product is the quotient of the two monic resultants, and its factors are integral divided differences permuted by Galois, proving integrality even at primes dividing the original resultant.
The steep-direction application uses v793 for simplicity at both poles, on the circle and by inversion, and v796 for exclusion of nontrivial torsion quotients.

At \(h=2\), \(D_2=-1\) makes the index and the absolute cross quotient individually one, so every prime valuation of the complementary resultant is preserved.
Each cross sum divides the resulting unit product in the ring of algebraic integers, and replacing \(w\) by \(w^{-1}\) stays in its reciprocal factor.
The direct order argument also applies to the full section: when \(k\) is even the sole odd term is \(X^{2k+l}\) with coefficient one, and \(x^2\) is an integral unit with inverse in its generated order.
Consequently the order equality passes to every factor, and the v805 lower bound applies on its stated small-degree steep sequences.
