## The three intersections and the degree of the surviving factor

On 2026-11-18 at 06:25 UTC, a reviewer outside proof development checked the [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v349.md#statement), the two applications below, and their indicated dependencies for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proofs and the requested scope without proof-development discussion.
The earlier modulus-53 review was consulted as background for the use of a degree bound on a surviving factor.

Let \(H=B(\varepsilon^pX^a,\varepsilon^bY^a)\) for one of the three indicated transforms.
For a common geometric component with nonconstant \(Y\)-equation, clearing Laurent monomials would give a common factor over \(\mathbb C(X)[Y]\).
A Bézout identity over \(\mathbb Q(X)[Y]\) would persist after extension of scalars, so the rational gcd is nonconstant in \(Y\), and Gauss's lemma gives a common rational Laurent factor.
A common vertical component \(X=c\) instead gives a common zero of all the nonzero \(Y\)-coefficients of both polynomials.
These are rational Laurent polynomials in \(X\), so \(c\ne0\) is algebraic and its minimal polynomial divides both.
In either case rational irreducibility gives \(B\mid H\).
This argument covers curves whose rational irreducible equation factors over \(\mathbb C\).

On substituting \(X=x^p,\ Y=x^b\) the divisibility would give \(J(x)\mid J(\varepsilon x^a)\) up to Laurent units.
For \(a=2\), the image of a root of greatest modulus \(R>1\) would then have modulus \(R^2>R\).
For \(a=1,\ \varepsilon=-1\), the two normalized polynomials have equal degrees and nonzero constants, hence are proportional.
Sign change preserves roots of unity and permutes rational irreducible factors up to units.
The unique noncyclotomic factor would satisfy \(P(-x)=\pm P(x)\), forcing all support differences to be even.
Thus none of the three pairs has a common component.

For a root \(\xi\) of order \(n\), the exponents \(1+n/2\) for \(4\mid n\), \(2\) for odd \(n\), and \(n/2+2\) for \(n\equiv2\bmod4\) are units in their respective residue rings.
In the last case \(n=2u\) with \(u\) odd, so \(u+2\) is odd and coprime to \(u\).
The corresponding images \(-\xi,\xi^2,-\xi^2\) have the same vanishing order \(e\) in \(J\) as \(\xi\) by rational conjugacy.
The derivative of \(x\mapsto\varepsilon x^a\) is nonzero at \(\xi\), giving order \(e\) for both restricted equations.
Since \(p,b\) generate \(\mathbb Z\), the diagonal parameter gives a surjection of Laurent coordinate rings and a closed immersion of tori.
At its image point the finite local intersection algebra therefore surjects onto the local ring of the parameter curve modulo the two restricted equations.
In the parameter's discrete valuation ring at \(\xi\), the restricted equations generate the ideal \(((x-\xi)^e)\), so the quotient has length \(e\).
This proves the claimed lower bound on intersection length even at singular points and for repeated cyclotomics.
Different \(\xi\) have different image points.

The reviewer read Lemma 2 and its proof on pp. 7–8 of the [Beukers–Smyth author edition](../../literature/pdf/BS02--author.pdf).
The proof explicitly bounds the sum of local intersection numbers in the torus by \(V(N(B)+N(H))-V(N(B))-V(N(H))\), using ordinary Euclidean area and the cited intersection theorem of Fulton.
The intersection theorem is accepted at the scope stated there.
Here \(N(H)=aN(B)\), hence each sum is bounded by \(2aV\), including for a segment of area zero.
Assigning each torsion root to the single transform for its order gives \(\deg C_0\le (2+4+4)V\).

The lattice map \((i,j)\mapsto(pi+bj,j)\) has determinant \(p\).
When both projected extremes survive, its image polygon lies in a rectangle of widths \(d,s\), whence \(D=d-\deg C_0\ge d(1-10s/p)\).
For the centered prime lift the row gcd removes only cyclotomic factors, and \(h<p\) separates the projected exponents in the support of each Laurent factor.
Each nonunit factor has positive fast width, and exactly one carries the simple factor \(P\) on the diagonal.
Inversion fixes that carrier up to a Laurent unit by the reciprocity of the reduced lift and of \(P\).
Its integral diagonal has unit endpoints since its extreme coefficients divide those of the reduced multiple.
These steps in the earlier carrier argument require only \(h<p\).
With two or three classes and \(p>3\), centering gives \(h=2\) and \(s\le2\).
If \(d<p\), each occupied row contributes at most one exponent, giving either a unit-endpoint binomial of measure one or a reciprocal trinomial \(x^{2e}+w x^e+1\) of measure one or at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
Both contradict the required measure interval.
Thus \(d\ge p\), and for \(p>20\) the positive factor \(1-20/p\) gives \(D\ge p-20\).
Smaller odd primes satisfy the bound immediately.

For two classes the reduced graph \(f(X)+\sigma Y f^*(X)\) is primitive and linear over \(\mathbb Q(X)\), and both rows have nonzero coefficients at exponents \(0\) and \(n\).
Its Newton polygon has area \(n\).
The nonconstant seed condition follows from the positive measure, and the positive offset \(S\) gives diagonal degree \(pn+S\).
Consequently \(D\ge(p-10)n+S\), implying \(p\le D+9\) for \(p>10\) and the same bound directly for the smaller odd primes.
The review confirms all assertions of the fixed claim for the stated primitive core and arbitrary cyclotomic padding.

## Two classes for the degree-ten polynomial

For the [degree-ten corollary](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v148.md#lehmer), the specified polynomial \(L\) is the irreducible Salem polynomial identified in the [fixed benchmark](../../claims/Yin_C_G0226_v1.md#lehmer-joint-prime-control).
The constant and linear terms give gcd-one support.
Direct rational substitution gives \(L(117/100)=-14912584160608812851/10^{20}\) and \(L(118/100)=9247445562263351/97656250000000000\).
Thus the unique exterior root satisfies \(1.17<\lambda<1.18<\theta\).
The cutoff \(p\le19\) leaves \(17,19\) among primes \(p\ge17\).
At \(p=17\), \(7n+S\le10\) forces \(n=1\) and \(S\le3\), while at \(p=19\), \(9n+S\le10\) gives \(n=S=1\).
Writing \(f=1+AX\) after changing the overall sign, set \(t=\lambda^p,\ u=\lambda^S\).
The equation \(1+At+\sigma u(A+t)=0\) gives \((A+\sigma u)(t+\sigma u)=u^2-1\).
Here \(t>1.17^{17}>14\) and \(u<1.18^3<1.65\), hence \(|A+\sigma u|<(1.65^2-1)/(14-1.65)=53/380<0.14\).
For \(S=1,2,3\), the intervals \((1.17,1.18)\), \((1.36,1.40)\), \((1.60,1.65)\) respectively place \(u\) at distance at least \(0.17\) from every integer.
This verifies the exclusion of exactly two classes at every prime \(p\ge17\) for this polynomial.
The review of the ledger covers this corollary and its use of the sharper graph inequality.

## Three classes at 29

At \(p=29\), a two- or three-class cyclotomic multiple of \(L\) would have a monomial-normalized carrier diagonal divisible by \(L\), with \(d\le\lfloor 10\cdot29/9\rfloor=32\).
Its support uses at most three residues modulo 29, including zero.
The reviewer independently divided each \(x^e\), \(0\le e\le32\), by the displayed monic polynomial over \(\mathbb Z\), and reduced the resulting coefficient columns by Gaussian elimination modulo the prime 1009.
For every \(1\le a<b\le28\) this tested exactly the columns with \(e\bmod29\in\{0,a,b\}\).
All 300 matrices with four columns, all 75 with five and all three with six had full column rank.
In each case a maximal minor is nonzero modulo 1009 and hence over \(\mathbb Q\).
The [submitted verifier](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0167/verify_p29.py) independently obtained the same counts and no deficient matrices using integer maximal minors.
This verifies the [finite exclusion](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0167/readme.md#p29) at 29.
For every prime \(p\ge31\), the bound \(p\le D+20=30\) already excludes two and three classes.
A one-class multiple would have only exponents divisible by \(p\), since its constant term is nonzero.
The \(p\) rotations of \(\lambda\) by \(p\)-th roots of unity would all be expanding roots of that multiple, although \(L\) supplies its only expanding root.
It follows that every cyclotomic multiple of \(L\) occupies at least four classes at every prime \(p\ge29\).
This finite computation concerns the specified degree-ten input and degree cap at 29.
