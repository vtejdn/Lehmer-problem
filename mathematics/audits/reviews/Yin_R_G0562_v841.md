## Independent whole-proof finding

At 2026-11-21T01:03 (UTC), an agent working for Sheng Yin independently checked the submitted C345 v704 statement and its complete proof and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing v704 and received the fixed proof, input references and requested audit scope, without its development discussion or a previous mathematical verdict.
The reviewer developed the earlier Salem arguments v685 and v703.
The present inspection rederived the signed partition estimate and checked the broader v704 proof directly, including the changes in hypotheses since v108 and v703.

For roots inside the closed unit disk, the squared gap between the two sides of the disk inequality is \((1-|z|^2)(1-|w|^2)\).
If both roots are outside, division by \(|z||w|\) expresses their distance as that of their reflections in the disk.
For a mixed pair, the denominator \(\max(1,|z|)\max(1,|w|)\) converts the distance into the modulus of the indicated linear factor exactly.
Zero causes no division in either applicable case.
A squarefree monic integer polynomial has nonzero integral discriminant, so the log of the absolute product of the ordered distinct-pair distances is nonnegative, including the empty product at \(d=1\).
Inserting a factor \(\rho^2\) in the reflected product costs at most \(\log(1/\rho)\) per ordered pair, since
\[
 |1-\rho^2 z|^2-\rho^2|1-z|^2=(1-\rho^2)(1-\rho^2|z|^2)\ge0.
\]
The two modulus factors for every ordered pair contribute in total \(2(d-1)\log M(P)\), where \(M(P)\le B\) is the multiplicative Mahler measure.
Adding the \(d\) diagonal terms contributes at most \(d\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\), and the absolutely convergent expansion sums to \(\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^{2j}|t_j|^2/j\).
With \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\), the three contributions sum to at most \(3d\log(2dB)\), using \(1/(1-e^{-2/d})\le1+d/2\).
On \(j\le d\), removal of the damping costs at most \(e^2\).
The reflected exterior terms have total modulus at most \(r\).
Consequently \(U\le6e^2d\log(2dB)+2r^2H_d\le45A\), since \(6e^2<45\) and \(H_d\le2\log(2d)\).
These estimates require neither irreducibility nor a restriction on the constant coefficient beyond integrality.

For each permitted length, conjugation invariance of a tuple is a finite union of closed permutation conditions.
The modulus and product constraints confine each tuple to a closed subset of a compact annulus product, with the empty tuple an additional compact choice.
Continuity at negative indices follows from the lower modulus bound one.
Both the minimum past error and the smallest real next power sum among minimizers therefore exist.
All fits attaining both extrema yield the same predictor and require only the previously reconstructed sums, with \(s_i=0\) at the artificial nonpositive indices.
The true exterior tuple is admissible.
Its errors at nonpositive indices have modulus at most \(r\), including the count of exterior entries at index zero.
Comparing it with a minimizer bounds the maximum past discrepancy between their exterior sums by twice the largest true residual in the window.
The product of the two annihilating polynomials has degree at most \(2r\) and absolute coefficient sum at most \(4^r B^2=K\).
Its recurrence holds at every integral shift for each of the nonzero exterior entries, including repeated or shared entries, so it bounds the next discrepancy by \(2K\) times that window residual.
At \(r=0\) the sole list is empty, \(y_j=0\), and the discrepancy of the two lists vanishes identically.
The convention \(w=\max(1,2r)\) thus covers the full zero-exterior case, including products of distinct cyclotomic factors and a possible factor \(X\).

Each positive residual enters at most \(w\) future windows with weights bounded by its own \(1/i\).
The initial windows can be charged \(r^2H_{\min(w,d)}\) in total, even when \(w>d\).
Squaring the displayed prediction bound and summing therefore costs at most \(2U+8K^2(wU+r^2H_{\min(w,d)})\).
Using \(w,K\ge1\), \(r^2H_{\min(w,d)}\le2A\), and \(U\le45A\) gives \(90+360+16=466\) in units of \(wK^2A\).
Since \(w\le1+2r\) and \(K^2=16^rB^4\), this is at most \(W/4\).

At each index the fixed coefficient residue and all previous exact coefficients and traces specify one residue class for \(s_j\) modulo \(jM\), including at composite \(M\).
The nearest member with the prescribed tie rule is determined by this same past.
Its distance from \(s_j\) is at most twice the prediction error, since \(s_j\) is itself a member of the class.
The signed offsets thus have \(\sum jz_j^2\le W/M^2\), and integrality implies \(\sum j|z_j|\le W/M^2\).
Newton's identities recover each exact coefficient in succession, so the code is injective.
A nonzero offset at \(j>\lfloor W/M^2\rfloor\) would exceed the total budget, proving the suffix determination and the stated strict separation threshold.
The signed partition product has logarithm after removing its exponential prefactor bounded by \(2v^{-1}\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell^{-2}=\pi^2/(3v)\).
Minimizing \(vW/M^2+\pi^2/(3v)\) gives precisely the factor \(2\pi/\sqrt3\) in (1).
For fixed \(B,r\), summing the degree bound through \(D\) costs a factor at most \(D\), absorbed in the stated order.
The two measure-one polynomials in the last example are distinct and coefficientwise congruent modulo their prime degree by the binomial theorem.
Here \(W=2000p\log(2p)<p^2\) for all sufficiently large primes, so allowing the repeated factor really can destroy the separation conclusion.
