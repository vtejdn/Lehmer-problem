## Coefficient prefixes and rational interlacing

The movable-grid theorem holds for every integer \(q\ge2\), \(k\ge1\) and \(N\ge2q(q+1)k^2\).
The polynomial on each constructed grid has rational coefficients, the specified root distribution and exact pole value, and integral coefficients through \(c_k\).
The congruences permit an integral completion without the root requirements.
Whether a single integral completion satisfies all the signs remains open.

This was an initial independent inspection for Yin at 2026-11-25T09:24 UTC.
The reviewer worked outside the development of the proof and programs, using the fixed claim, the reconstruction argument and the trial files, without development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or earlier review conclusions on these results.

With \(M=q(q+1)\), division by \(M^j\) after subtracting the previously chosen pole digits gives the class of \(c_j\) modulo \(M\).
Newton's identities give slope \(-j\) for the corresponding shifted Chebyshev sum, so every target is within \(jM/2\) of an allowed ordinate, including a tie.
The same identities recover the prefix from these ordinates.
The remaining divisible pole error can occupy \(c_{k+1}\) since \(k+1\le n=N+1\).

At the midpoint angles, cosine frequencies \(1,\ldots,2N-1\) sum to zero, giving the sine Gram matrix \(NI/2\) in the required range \(k<N\).
After cancellation of the linear term, its orthogonality bound and \(\|a\|_2\le\sqrt{k}M\) give a derivative bound of \(jkM/N\) in the maximum norm for component \(j\).
Integrating on the radius to \(a\) supplies the extra factor \(1/2\), so the nonlinear error is at most \(M/4\) even at \(N=2Mk^2\).
The displacement derivative of the angular map has absolute value at most \(Mk(k+1)/(2N)\le1/2\).
For \(0\le x\le1/M\le1/6\), the larger root of \(z+z^{-1}=2+x\) lies in \([1,2]\), giving the stated Chebyshev derivative bound also at \(x=0\) by continuity.
The variation of the normalized target for \(w\) in the prescribed interval is at most \(1/M\).
Thus the fixed-point map takes the closed cube into itself with Lipschitz constant at most \(1/2\), and its fixed point depends continuously on \(w\).

At the lower endpoint, at least \(\lfloor N/4\rfloor\ge3k^2\ge k+2\) of the midpoint angles exceed or equal \(3\pi/4\) before deformation.
They remain above \(\pi/2\) after displacement by at most \(1/(2k)\).
Their factors alone give \(4^{-k}(1+2M)^{k+2}>4M+1\), whereas the endpoint \(w=1/M\) gives zero.
Continuity therefore realizes the pole value in the interior.
Strictly increasing angles separate all the negative roots, so each interior trace node can be chosen rational in its separating interval with angular error less than \(1/N\).
The endpoints are already rational.
Fixing the first \(k\) coefficients and solving the pole equation for the constant term preserves density of rational vectors in the remaining real affine space.
The strict alternating signs persist at the finitely many rational nodes, and the positive pole value gives the remaining sign change above zero.
The \(n\) disjoint sign-change intervals exhaust the degree and imply simplicity.

At such a grid the positive pole weights have total mass \(D=1-K/(M^nQ(1/M))\) strictly between zero and one.
The rational witness satisfies the residue and both moment intervals at each of its first \(k\) steps.
The width \(4D<M\) makes each surviving digit unique on the chosen grid.
This argument allows \(k=\lfloor\sqrt{N/(2M)}\rfloor\) for every \(N\ge2M\), with the grid chosen for that degree and prefix.

The five finite trials also reproduce as stated.
The reviewer ran the retained program, inspected its rational target rounding and checked its proposed rational grids by a separate exact series calculation.
For this calculation write \(P^*(z)=z^nP(1/z)\), form \(Q^*\) directly by multiplying the node factors, and expand \(A(z)=H^*(z)/Q^*(z)\) through the coefficient being tested.
The coefficient of \(z^{j+1}\) in \(1-A(z)\) gives the unweighted moment of order \(j\), and the coefficient of \(z^j\) in \((A(z)-1+D)/(1-z/M)\) gives the pole-weighted moment of order \(j\).
Intersecting the two Chebyshev bounds with the required residue reproduced the first failures \(5,10,17,14,5\) in the order of the trial table, with the selected prefix recovered in each case.
These are exclusions of five specified rational grids.
