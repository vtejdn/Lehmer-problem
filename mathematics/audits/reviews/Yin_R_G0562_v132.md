## The common cofactor, uniform target degrees and exponential coefficient cost

At 2026-11-17T02:54 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the full C345v132 submission fixed by its contributor at 2026-11-17T02:53 UTC.
The reviewer had no role in developing this version and independently reconstructed the two limiting arguments, the progression, the integral norm obstruction and the converse.

The reciprocal-normalized quadratic satisfies \(f(z)/z=z-z^{-1}-1\), giving \(|f|^2=1+|z-z^{-1}|^2\) on the circle.
For \(u<1\), Rouché counts \(k+1\) roots of \(z^kf+u\) strictly inside the disk, so continuation to \(u=1\) permits at most one exterior root, with multiplicity.
The endpoint values \(H_k(-1)=2,\ H_k'(1)=1-k<0\) and \(H_k(\varphi)=1\) leave all roots of \(F_k\) except one strictly inside.
Its nonzero unit constant term forbids a nontrivial complementary interior-only factor, and reciprocity would pair more than one interior root with an exterior one.
The negative sign of \(H_k(s)\) eventually for each \(1<s<\varphi\) puts \(\theta_k\to\varphi\) from below.
For this fixed Pisot seed the accepted sufficiently-large-delay construction applies.
Since \(F_k(1/\theta_k)<0\), the positive completion is negative at \(\theta_k\) and eventually positive at any fixed \(s>\theta_k\).
Its unique exterior root approaches \(\theta_k\) from above, also along any fixed progression of unbounded delays.

The reduction \(G_{k,N}=(z-1)(z^NF_k+F_k^*)\) uses \((z-1)^*=-(z-1)\).
The normalized rational function \(U=-F_k^*/F_k\) has no circle pole and has \((U(0),U'(0))=(1,-1)\).
On a torsion root of even delay \(N\), the comparison exponent is \(2,\ 1+n/2,\ 2+n/2\) for orders \(n\) odd, divisible by four and twice odd, respectively.
Each is prime to its corresponding \(n\); raising \(-\zeta\) or \(-\zeta^2\) to \(N\) introduces no sign.
In the square comparisons the composed side has derivative zero at zero while \((U^2)'(0)=-2\); in the negation comparison the derivatives have opposite signs.
Each rational difference has a nonzero numerator, so only finitely many torsion points occur at a fixed seed over even delays.
Taking the common multiple of all their orders preserves their presence exactly as at \(N=k+2\).
The simple derivative comparison \(N>|U'(\zeta)|\) removes possible multiplicities at all large delays, and \(2F_k(1)\ne0\).

Independently expanding the three eliminants for \(J=f^*/f\) gave, with denominator products in the order of the submitted differences, the numerators
\[
 -2(z-1)^3(z+1)\Phi_3^2,\qquad 4z(z-1)(z+1),\qquad
 -2(z-1)(z+1)^3\Phi_6^2 .
\]
At the base exponent \(G_{k,k+2}=z^{2k+2}f-f^*\).
The congruence \(6\mid 2k+2\) excludes orders three and six since \(f-f^*=2(z^2-1)\), and retains both endpoints.
Thus the precise eventual cofactor of \(G_{k,N}\) on the selected progression is \(z^2-1\).
The remaining polynomial is the whole Salem minimal polynomial and has degree \(N+k=2m\).

For fixed \(R\) the measure bound follows by multiplying the monic shifted lift by \(z^2-1\), whose Mahler measure is one, and using the circle supremum of the resulting polynomial.
It is uniform in both \(k,N\).
If the limiting circle inequality held everywhere, \(s=|z^2-1|\) would give \(|R(z+z^{-1})|\le 2s/(\sqrt{1+s^2}+1)\), forcing both endpoint values to vanish.
Then \(z^{\deg R}R(z+z^{-1})/(z^2-1)\) is a nonzero integer polynomial whose modulus is strictly below one except possibly at the two endpoints.
Its squared mean would be less than one, contradicting the positive integer sum of squares of its coefficients.
The violation is therefore strict at some interior upper semicircle point.
Placing \(z_k^k f(z_0)\) on the negative real axis within angle \(\pi/k\) retains a fixed strict margin since \(f(z_k)-f(z_0)=O(1/k)\).
The angular Lipschitz bound \(O_R(k)\) extends it over a centered arc of half-width \(a_R/k\) independent of the delay.
For all large \(k\) this arc lies strictly in the open upper semicircle.

The short-arc estimate C345v108 applies at every center to each monic irreducible Salem unit.
As its only off-circle conjugates are positive real, the missing arc forces \(D/\log(2D)\ll_R k^2\), equivalently \(D\ll_R k^2\log(2k)\).
For fixed \(k,R\) the target set is consequently finite by bounded degree and bounded measure.
Multiplying the vanishing equation by \(\tau^m(\tau^2-1)\) in its centered form gives the stated quadratic in \(u=\tau^m\) with constant \(-H_k^*(\tau)\ne0\), since both \(1/\tau-1\) and \(F_k(1/\tau)\) are negative.
At most two distinct delays at this seed can yield that fixed target and fixed perturbation.
The order of choices is essential: first take \(k\) sufficiently large for the finite list to have its missing arcs, then take a common sufficiently large \(N\) on that seed's progression.

At cap \(B\) and trace degree \(t\ge d+2\), a Salem number has at least \(d+1\) distinct interior neutral traces.
At growing degree C345v108 locates these in predefined separated arcs away from both endpoints.
In the complementary bounded-degree range, bounded measure leaves only finitely many targets and hence gives common separation and endpoint avoidance there too.
The bound on \(|R|\) at these nodes has the denominator \(|z^2-1|\), now bounded below uniformly.
Interpolation bounds all integer coefficients independently of both construction parameters; for \(d=0\) one point suffices.
Once isolated from zero this is a finite list.
The converse follows from monic division by the prescribed target's trace polynomial: the negative remainder has degree \(<t\), is nonzero once \(m>t\) by irreducibility, and makes the entire lift divisible by the Salem minimal polynomial.
Staging all \(d\le j\) at cap \(j\) and the first \(j\) members of a fixed enumeration simultaneously covers each fixed \(R\), and eventually each \(d,B\) with all changing coefficients.
The freedom of the delays also permits strictly increasing degrees and \(\theta_k<\sigma_{k,N}<\varphi\); the two endpoints squeeze the sequence to \(\varphi\).

Finally, at the fixed exterior trace \(y=\tau+\tau^{-1}\), dividing \(G_{k,N}(\tau)\) by \(\tau^{N+k}(\tau^2-1)\) independently gives the normalized numerator \(f(\tau)+\tau^{-k}-\tau^{-N-k}f^*(\tau)-\tau^{2-N}\).
It tends to \(f(\tau)\ne0\), since \(\varphi\) is a quadratic Pisot number.
The other trace conjugates lift to fixed unit points different from the endpoints and have bounded evaluations of \(S_j\).
Interpolation of the negative remainder at these \(t\) conjugates leaves, after division by \(\tau^{m_j}\), only the exterior cardinal polynomial \(D_\tau(X)/((X-y)D_\tau'(y))\) times \(-f(\tau)/(\tau^2-1)\).
Its leading coefficient is nonzero, so the unique minimal-degree solution has eventual degree \(t-1\) and coefficient norm comparable to \(\tau^{m_j}\).
For any prescribed degree cap the same exterior evaluation gives the exponential lower bound directly.
Under simultaneous degree and measure caps only finitely many targets survive the classification, which permits the fixed-target pigeonhole step for subexponential coefficients.
Lehmer's Salem polynomial has degree ten and trace degree five, giving the stated degree-four consequence.
