Having inspected that proof and its use here, the reviewer recommends whole-claim adoption conditional on the decision binding C345 v686 at its diagonal argument and specifying the strict range of the v684 input.

For both signs, the original classifications give \(z>3/2\), \(b<z+1\), and \(r<a+1\), including at seed two.
The absolute error \(|z-b|=(bz-1)z^{-n}\) is at most \(3(3/2)^{2-n}\), and the same bound holds for \(|r-a|\).
Once the bound is below \(1/10\), \(k\ge2\) gives \(a>(b-1/10)^2-1/10>b+1\), even at \(b=2\).
If \(r=z^k\), a conjugate above the distinguished exterior embedding of \(\mathbb Q(r)\) must have modulus \(r^{1/k}>1\).
The uniqueness of this conjugate forces \([\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q(r)]=1\) by separability, and irreducibility at seed two then excludes \(a=2\).
For \(a\ge3\) the effective index estimate applies to this entire \(k\), giving an absolute bound \(k\ll n^2\log^5(3n)\) independent of the two signs and both seeds.

The circle equation reads \(Lt-2h_b(t)\equiv\arg\epsilon\bmod 2\pi\) with the stated choice of branch.
The derivative bound on \(h_b\) makes the lifted map \(t-2h_b(t)/L\) increasing and onto, with inverse slope at most \(3/2\).
For the positive sign the phases \(Lx,Lsx\) are \(2\pi,4\pi\), and for the negative sign they are \(\pi,3\pi\).
The resulting angles are positive and strictly below \(\pi/3\) for all sufficiently large \(L\), excluding the possible cyclotomic factors even at \(b=2\).
The two resulting conjugates of \(z\) power to roots at \(a\) because \(r=z^k\).
Subtracting their two seed equations at each angle yields \(2E_j\equiv\arg\eta-k\arg\epsilon\bmod 2\pi\), which puts both \(E_j\) in \((\pi/2)\mathbb Z\) for independent signs.

For the first angle, \(\sin h_b(t)=\sin t/|b-e^{it}|\) and \(0<t<\pi/3\) give \(h_b(t)>\sin t/(b+1)\).
The positive real part in the arctangent gives \(|h_a(kt)|\le|\sin(kt)|/(a-1)\le k\sin t/(a-1)\).
Thus \(a\ge b+2\) makes \(E_1\) strictly positive, including the boundary \(a=b+2\).
It follows that \(kF(x)\ge\pi/2-\arcsin(1/a)\), whereas \(F(x)\le t_b(x)/(b-1)\le 6\pi/(bL)\).
These inequalities give an effective \(k\ge c bL\), and \(a>z^k-1>(3/2)^k-1\) makes \(\arcsin(1/a)=o(k/(bL^3))\) uniformly with an effective threshold.
Indeed the numerator is exponentially small in \(L\), while the denominator is at least \(c/L^2\).

At zero, implicit differentiation gives \(t_b'=1/v\), \(F''=t_b''=0\), and \(v F'''=h_b'''/v^3\).
The differentiated sine series has \(h_b'''(0)=-\sum_{j\ge1}j^2/b^j=-b(b+1)/(b-1)^3\), giving the required negative cubic coefficient of magnitude comparable to \(1/b\).
For derivative orders one through five, the series bounds are effective geometric moments.
Each differentiated implicit equation has highest derivative coefficient \(1-2h_b'/L\ge2/3\), and the remaining terms contain a derivative of \(h_b\) times products of bounded lower inverse derivatives.
This gives a computable \(O(1/b)\) bound at order five uniformly over all \(b,L\).
Oddness removes the even Taylor coefficients at zero, so the linear terms cancel in \(sF(x)-F(sx)\), leaving a positive term of order \(1/(bL^3)\) and error \(O(1/(bL^5))\) for either fixed choice of \(s\).
After multiplication by \(k\), the error from the two target-seed phases is at most \((s+1)\arcsin(1/a)\), strictly smaller than that positive term for all sufficiently large effective \(L\).
The same phase difference \(sE_1-E_s\) is \(O(\log^5(3n)/n)+o(1)\) by the exponent cap, contradicting its positive membership in \((\pi/2)\mathbb Z\).
All tail comparisons reduce to bounds on exponential or logarithmic powers with computable constants and eventually monotone comparisons.
For the remaining finitely many delays the fixed-delay cutoff bounds \(a\) uniformly in the target sign, and \(k<\log(a+1)/\log(3/2)\) and \(b<z+1\) leave a finite search.
Comparison of the two isolated real algebraic numbers decides each candidate, so the argument supplies an algorithm without a running-time estimate.

For \(p\ge11\) the exact case in the prime-power dichotomy gives a member of this effective finite list with \(k=p^e\).
The ratio \(\log(2p)/\log\beta\) is effectively bounded there since \(\beta>3/2\).
The other case has \(d/(n+1)\ge3/4\), \(4j_p\le8\sqrt p\), and gives a positive constant multiple of \(\log(2p)\) for \(\log\beta\) above an effective absolute prime cutoff.
The uniform prime-power gap covers every prime below that cutoff and every \(e\).
Multiplication by \(p^e\) gives (2), and at \(a=2\) there are no prime divisors of \(R\).
For \(p>n\), (2) implies \(n\log(2n)<C\log(a+1)\).
For \(A=\log(a+1)\) sufficiently large, \(\log(2C_1A/\log\log(a+3))\ge\tfrac12\log\log(a+3)\), so \(C_1>2C\) excludes that inequality under the displayed threshold.
Enlarging \(C_1\) on the effective bounded range deals with all remaining seeds.

At \(a\ge3\), the delayed trace and dyadic argument in v684 give \(v_p(R)\le v_p(H(a))\) at all \(p<n\).
Its odd-prime input requires the strict inequality.
For \(p=n\ge7\) prime, v686 proves that \(p^e\mid R\) forces \(p^{e+1}\mid a\).
In the branch \(p\mid a\), this follows from the valuation of \(a(a^{p-1}-1-\epsilon p)\).
For \(p\nmid a\) the three coefficient congruences at degrees \(2,3,4\) would give \(a^2\equiv-2\epsilon\) and then \(p\mid20\), which is impossible.
Thus \(p^e\mid a/\operatorname{rad}(a)\mid H(a)\) on the diagonal whenever such a radical occurs.
Combining the two ranges with exclusion of \(p>n\) proves (3) at every asserted seed and threshold.
