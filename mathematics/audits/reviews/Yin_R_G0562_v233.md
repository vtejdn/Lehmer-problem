## Degree of the individual factor with a partially shared end

At 2026-11-17T12:43 UTC an agent for Yin independently checked the [submitted C345v233 proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v233.md#decision-binomial-end-resultant-submitted) and its cited interfaces.
The reviewer had no part in the development of this claim and received its fixed text and review scope after working on a separate reciprocal five-term problem.
The degree and measure bounds hold with constants depending only on the coefficient length \(L\), including when the end blocks have common roots.

Write \(u=\log|x|>0\) and \(t=\max u\) over the exterior conjugates.
The largest term of the parent and its remaining terms imply \(bt\le\log L\), and the factor's logarithmic measure is bounded by \(\log L\) because its integral cofactor has nonnegative logarithmic measure.
The quadratic inequality for the full original degree then gives
\[
                1/20\le d\sum_{u>0}u^2\le dtm,
\]
which implies \(d\gg_L b\).
This suffices whenever \(\Delta/b\) is bounded, including (1) since \(1+\kappa/b\ge1\).
For \(|\mu|\ge1\) the actual upper block has roots of modulus \(|\mu|^{-1/b}\le1\).
When \(\Delta/b\) is sufficiently large, the gap \(G=D-b-e_k\) dominates \(b\) and is at least \(\Delta-b\).
In the v170 capacity argument, \(j_0=\lfloor(G-5b)/2\rfloor\) is then nonnegative, the containing-polynomial degree \(j_0+3b\) is comparable to \(G\), and \((j_0+3b/2)/(j_0+3b)\ge1/2\).
The length of the whole lower block controls the endpoint values, independent of its number of separated groups.
These facts give \(d\gg_L G\) at the cited interface.

For \(|\mu|<1\), divide \(P(x)=0\) by \(x^D\) and use \(P(1/x)=0\).
Distinct exponent groups in the hypotheses show in the first case that all omitted terms have exponent at most \(e_k-D\) and in the second case that all omitted terms have exponent at most \(-e_1\).
Each absolute sum is at most \(L e^{-\Delta u}\).
Once \(\Delta u>C_L\), the closeness of \(x^{-b}\) to \(\mu\) puts its modulus in the interval \([|\mu|/2,(1+|\mu|)/2]\).
The possible nonzero pairs of binomial coefficients come from a finite set at fixed \(L\), so the resulting upper and lower bounds for \(bu\) are uniform within this case.
Taking the local logarithm of \(\mu x^b\) identifies an exterior center at distance \(O_L(e^{-c_L\Delta/b}/b)\).
The circles around different centers may be taken disjoint and exterior with a margin of order \(1/b\), since their common radius from the origin is \(\exp(-\log|\mu|/b)\) and their angular separation is \(2\pi/b\).
The binomial stays bounded away from zero on each circle of radius \(\epsilon_L/b\) about a center, by scaling the power coordinate there or directly for bounded \(b\).
On such a boundary the remainder divided by \(|x|^D\) has magnitude at most \(L e^{-c_L(D-e_k)/b}\).
Thus Rouché counts exactly one root of \(P\) near each center, counting multiplicity.
The identified roots of \(S\) above the cutoff inject into the corresponding centers.

Each interior inverse center \(\eta\) is a root of \(B\), and every point on the segment between \(1/x\) and \(\eta\) has modulus \(\le e^{-c'_L/b}\).
Termwise differentiation and \(\sum_j |[X^j]A|\le L\) bound the derivative by \(L\sup_{j\ge1}j e^{-c'_L(j-1)/b}=O_L(b)\).
No bound on \(\deg A\) is required in this step.
Together with the estimate at \(1/x\), this bounds \(|A(\eta)|\) by \(C_L e^{-c''_L\Delta/b}\) at each reached center.
On all the other interior centers \(|A(\eta)|\le L\).
If \(N\) of the reached centers are nonshared, multiplication of the nonzero values gives
\[
             -\kappa\le\log V
             \le b\log L+N\{\log C_L-c''_L\Delta/b\}.
\]
Hence \(N\ll_L b(b+\kappa)/\Delta\) in the large-gap regime.
Since \(B\) is squarefree and the high roots belong to different centers, at most \(r=\deg\gcd(A,B)\) other high roots can occur, irrespective of multiplicity in \(A\).
The low roots give \(\sum u^2\le C_Lm/\Delta\), and the high roots contribute at most \(O_L((N+r)/b^2)\).
Multiplication by \(d\) and the original quadratic lower bound prove (1).

In primitive integral normalization the product of the two factors selecting the shared and nonshared centers equals the primitive part of \(B\) up to sign.
In particular the positive leading coefficient of the nonshared factor divides \(|B_1|\).
The absolute resultant with \(A\) is its leading coefficient to the power \(\deg A\) times \(V\), also when the nonshared factor is the constant one or \(A\) is constant.
Integrality and coprimality give the lower bound one for this absolute resultant, proving \(\kappa\le a\log |B_1|\) without a sign choice on any individual evaluation.

For reciprocal monic unit factors the v186 estimate applies to the full parent with width \(D\) and at most \(L\) terms, giving \(Dm/d\gg_L1\).
The asserted measure bound follows by the degree estimate; if \(\Delta h\ge\delta\), the alternative bound follows directly by \(m=dh\).
For other positive-measure irreducible factors the endpoint and Smyth bounds give an absolute positive constant, and \(\Delta/(DW)\le1\).
Mahler measure is multiplicative for all factors, including content, so the conclusion extends to the stated integral divisors.
In the example the three low coefficients contribute \(5\) to the length, the middle two contribute \(12\), and the final two contribute \(4\).
The binomial roots give \(A=1\), so \(r=\kappa=0\) for every allowed \(\ell,b\).
The example has value \(-1\) at one and positive leading coefficient.
The displayed exponents allow fixed \(b\) and unbounded \(\ell\) with \(\Delta/D\ge1/3\).
