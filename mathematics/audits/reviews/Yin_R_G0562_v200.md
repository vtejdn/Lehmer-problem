## A common generator and the sharp count of exponents

At 2026-11-17T09:37 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [whole v200 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v200.md#decision-common-rational-generator-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its dependencies without development discussion or author explanations, and derived the bounds and the group argument before consulting the prior findings on the dependencies.
The proof establishes the classification and both bounds on the number of attainable exponents for every stated \(W,L\).

Write \(t=\log L/m\) and \(x=d/W\).
When the larger absolute delay is positive, swap the functions and invert the first as needed to apply v199 with \(n\ge1\) and a signed second delay of absolute value at most \(n\).
Inversion interchanges the numerator and denominator and preserves their bounds.
Outside the identity alternative the height estimate gives \(n\le W+dt\) since \(h(\alpha)=m/d\).
For \(x>2C_1\), compression gives \(x^2\le4C_1^2(1+xt)\).
If also \(x>1\), division by \(x\) gives \(x\le4C_1^2(1+t)\), so a single absolute constant in (1) excludes every exceptional pair.
The same degree hypothesis makes any zero-delay function \(P/Q\) identically one, since \(P-Q\) vanishes at \(\alpha\) and has degree at most \(W\).
Thus every pair satisfies an exact signed relation modulo powers of \(X\), including the zero-delay cases.
The bound \(m\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\) used in v199 gives the claimed sufficient condition after an absolute change of constant.

The allowed integral pairs form a finite set and include a representation of \(X\).
Evaluation is defined throughout their generated group \(\Gamma\), since all generating numerators and denominators are nonzero at \(\alpha\).
A rational constant in \(\Gamma\) evaluates to a rational algebraic unit and is therefore \(1\) or \(-1\).
Nontorsion excludes \(\alpha^k=-1\) for every integer \(k\).
Every finite-order rational function is a rational constant, so \(\Gamma\) and its normalized image are torsion-free.
Multiplication by \(X^{-\operatorname{ord}_0 f}\) defines a homomorphism from \(\Gamma\) into itself because \(X\) belongs to \(\Gamma\).
Taking orders at zero in each pair relation cancels the monomial exactly under this map.
Fixing any nontrivial normalized generator gives a nonzero vector in the rationalization of the image.
Its relation with each other generator places the latter in its rational span, since a relation involving only the nonzero vector would have zero coefficient.
The image is finitely generated and hence either trivial or infinite cyclic.
In the cyclic case an integral generator \(H\) of the image belongs to \(\Gamma\), has order zero at the origin, is nonconstant, and evaluates to an integral power \(\alpha^N\).
Every allowed \(f\) is then \(X^aH^b\) with \(a=\operatorname{ord}_0 f\) and \(n=a+bN\) by nontorsion.

The reduced pair has at most one member divisible by \(X\), so removing its monomial factor gives nonzero constant terms, \(|a|\le W\), and the same coefficient lengths before dividing out any common integer content.
Write \(H=U/V\) in jointly primitive coprime integral form with both constant terms nonzero.
For \(k=|b|>0\), the contents of \(U^k,V^k\) are the \(k\)-th powers of the original coprime contents by Gauss's lemma, and the polynomial factors on the two sides remain disjoint.
The normalized pair for \(f\) is therefore precisely these powers up to simultaneous sign and interchange for \(b<0\).
One of \(U,V\) has positive degree, giving \(k\le W\).
That polynomial has at least two nonzero integer coefficients, so its supremum on the circle is at least its normalized \(L^2\) norm, which is at least \(\sqrt2\) by Parseval.
The coefficient length bound on its \(k\)-th power gives \(2^{k/2}\le L\).
The case \(b=0\) respects the same bounds.
Counting the integer coordinates, with \(|a|\le W\) and \(|b|\le\min(W,2\log_2 L)\), gives the asserted upper bound even if exponent values coincide.
In the trivial image case the \(2W+1\) possible monomial exponents also satisfy the bound for \(L\ge2\).

For each fixed \(W,L\), v191 supplies degree-\(D\) nontorsion units with \(\alpha^D=1+\alpha\) and \(m(\alpha)\) bounded below by a positive constant for all sufficiently large \(D\).
Hence arbitrarily large \(D\) satisfy both (1) and \(D>2W\).
All the displayed polynomials have degree \(a+b\le W\), length \(2^b\le L\), and denominator one.
Equality of two values of \(a+Db\) forces equality of \(a\) modulo \(D\), then equality of \(a\) and \(b\) separately.
There are at least \(W/2\) choices of \(a\) and at least \(\min(W/2,\log_2 L)\) choices of \(b\).
Since \(\log(2L)\le2\log L\) for \(L\ge2\), their product is at least \(W\min(W,\log(2L))/4\), including \(W=1\).
