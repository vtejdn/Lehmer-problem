### The two elimination identities

I recomputed the determinants directly from the coefficient rows in ascending powers of \(Y\).
For the adjacent specialization the row is \((1+X,1+X+X^2,X+X^2)\).
For the balanced specialization it is \((1,-1-X,X,-X-X^2,X^2)\).
In each case a coefficient \(f_j\) contributes \(sXf'_j+jt f_j\) to the derivative row, with \((s,t)=(1,n)\) or \((k,b)\).
Shifting the reversed rows gives Sylvester matrices of orders four and eight.
Exact determinants in integer polynomial rings give the adjacent sextic in v319 multiplied by \(-X\) and the balanced reciprocal sextic multiplied by \(-X^5\).
In the latter determinant the two highest sextic coefficients are exactly \(U,V\) in v319.
All the other coefficients outside the indicated ranges vanish.

In the adjacent sextic the degree-five coefficient is \(-1\).
In the balanced sextic \(\gcd(k,b)=\gcd(a,b)=1\).
At a prime dividing \(b\), \(V\equiv k^4\ne0\).
At the other primes where \(U\) vanishes, the evaluations at \(k=-b\) and \(k=-2b\) give \(b^4\) and \(-b^4\), respectively, both nonzero even in characteristic two.
The integral Sylvester identity expresses the determinant as a polynomial combination of the input and its derivative row before specialization.
It therefore continues to give divisibility after reduction if a leading coefficient disappears.
The substitution \(X=z^k\) preserves nonvanishing and multiplies Laurent width by \(k\), including when \(\ell\mid k\).
The derivative row specializes to \(zH'\) by the chain rule, also for \(Y=-z^n\).

For \(Q=TS\) the product rule gives \(g=\gcd(\bar T,\bar T')\mid\bar Q,\bar Q'\), with arbitrary multiplicities in \(\bar S\).
Differentiating \(H=z^hQ(z+z^{-1})\) shows that the same pullback of \(g\) divides both \(H\) and \(zH'\) in the residue Laurent ring.
The coefficients at exponents \(\pm\deg g\) of that pullback are the leading coefficient of \(g\), with the constant case having width zero.
Widths add under multiplication over a field, so \(2\deg g\le6k\).

### Unramified local components and the height of a primitive unit

Its lift gives an unramified field of its residue degree in \(K\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell=\mathbb Q_\ell[X]/(T)\), whether or not the order generated by the trace root is maximal at \(\ell\).
These components account for at least \(r=\max(d-6k,0)\) local degree in the field generated by the chosen trace root.

Over each such component a ramified quadratic \(D_\ell\) remains a ramified quadratic field, since its compositum has even absolute ramification index and relative degree at most two.
The inertia involution acts trivially on residues and takes \(u\) to \(u^{-1}\).
The generator assumption gives \(2d\) distinct conjugates and identifies \(\mathbb Q(u+u^{-1})\) with \(K\), since \(u\) is quadratic over that subfield of \(K\).
For each of the at least \(2r\) conjugates \(\alpha\) at these components, \(v_\ell(\alpha^2-1)\ge1/2\), normalized by \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
For one \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), at least \(r\) of these conjugates satisfy \(v_\ell(\alpha-\varepsilon)\ge1/4\), also at the dyadic prime.
Their squared pairwise differences contribute at least \(r(r-1)/4\) to the discriminant valuation, and the remaining differences are integral.
The Vandermonde row bound \(\sqrt{2d}\max(1,|\alpha|)^{2d-1}\) gives exactly the right side of (2) upon squaring and taking logarithms.
