## The source estimate and its use for arbitrary denominators

A reviewer outside the development of L464 v29 and Wus02-U001 inspected both for Yin on 2026-11-16T10:07 UTC.
The reviewer received the fixed materials, publisher address and scope, with no development discussion, author explanation or previous finding on these inputs.

I checked the ordinary complex logarithm theorem in the publisher's copy of Wüstholz's chapter, §5, printed pp. 5–6, and its attribution to the 1993 Baker–Wüstholz paper on p. 9.
The theorem allows fixed determinations of the logarithms of nonzero algebraic arguments different from one, with integral coefficients and nonzero resulting form.
For the field \(K\) generated by those arguments, the factor \(1/D\), where \(D=[K:\mathbb Q]\), is outside both maxima defining the modified heights.
The relative logarithmic height of a number is \(D h_{\rm abs}\), and the integer form has relative projective height \(D\log(\max_i|b_i|/\gcd_i|b_i|)\).
This verifies the conversion in the source unit, the \(1/D\) floor in each modified height, and the use of \(-1\) as the second argument.
The chapter gives the constant \(18(s+1)!\,s^{s+1}(32D)^{s+2}\log(2sD)\).
The original theorem is used at statement and applicability depth. [Cambridge publisher's chapter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/07999/excerpt/9780521807999_excerpt.pdf).

For the power estimate in the unit, rounding \(p\arg\xi/(2\pi)\) leaves the imaginary part of \(\Lambda\) in \([-\pi,\pi]\), with \(|2k|\le p+1\).
Exponentiation gives \(\xi^p\), so the nonvanishing hypothesis follows from \(\xi^p\ne1\).
The relative height bounds \(|\log|\xi||\) at the chosen place by applying the absolute height formula to \(\xi\) and \(\xi^{-1}\).
The triangle inequality for the principal complex logarithm then gives \(h'(\xi)\le h_{\rm abs}(\xi)+\pi/D\).
On the disk \(|\xi^p-1|<1/2\) the rounded logarithm is principal, and \(1/z\) has modulus at most two on the segment joining \(1\) to \(\xi^p\).
This yields \(|\Lambda|\le2|1-\xi^p|\), and the complementary case gives the same asserted upper bound for \(-\log|1-\xi^p|\) immediately.
When \(|\xi|=1\), the sharper maximum in the unit follows directly from the argument lying in \([-\pi,\pi]\).

For the large conductors to be excluded, the fourth-index zero profile gives the three moments \(2,0,6\).
Grid evaluation of the congruence and \(v(b_j)>0\) put \(\alpha=-\beta/A\) in the open sign gap, so \(\alpha>-2\).
Summing \((2-b_j)(b_j-\alpha)\rho_j>0\) gives \(-6-4\alpha>0\), hence \(\alpha<-3/2\).
The \(n\) positive numbers \((2-b_j)(4-b_j^2)(Ab_j+\beta)^2v(b_j)\) have sum \(p(4\beta-6A)\) by the same moments.
Their product is at least \(p^2 A^{2n}|B_n(\alpha)|^2\), since each geometric weight has product \(p\) and the product of the \(v(b_j)\) is a positive integral resultant.
Arithmetic-geometric mean therefore gives the lower bound in v29 (1) for any integral polynomial representative of the cofactor.
The upper bounds follow from \(\beta\le2A-1\), which gives \((4\beta-6A)/A^2\le (2A-4)/A^2\le1/4\), as well as \(4\beta-6A<2A\).

The primitive quadratic \(Az^2+\beta z+A\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) by its negative discriminant.
Its two unit-modulus roots give \(h_{\rm abs}(w)=\tfrac12\log A\), including the contribution of the leading coefficient.
A torsion \(w\) would give a rational algebraic integer \(\alpha\) in \((-2,-3/2)\), so the two-logarithm estimate applies with \(D=2\) at every permitted conductor.
The factors in that estimate are bounded by \(\tfrac12\max(\log A,\pi)\), \(\pi/2\) and \(\log(2p)\), respectively.
The integer prefactor of \(\log8\) in \(C(2,2)\) is \(14\,495\,514\,624\).
Multiplying it by \(21/10\) and \(5/2\), then adding one, gives \(76\,101\,451\,777<8\cdot10^{10}\), so the chosen \(K\) absorbs all constants including the additive \(\log2\).

The identity \(|B_n(\alpha)|=|1-w^p|/|1-w|\), with \(|1-w|\le2\), makes the right side of (1) at least \((n/p)e^{-\gamma}A^{-\gamma}\).
For \(p\ge10^{14}\), the bound \(n/p\ge12/25\), decrease of \(\log(2p)/p\), and \(\log(2\cdot10^{14})<33\) give \(\gamma<11/100<1/8\).
At \(A=8\) this lower bound exceeds \((12/25)e^{-1/2}>1/4\).
On \(1\le A\le8\) the bound is at least its value at eight, and on \(A\ge8\) its ratio to \(2/A\) increases from a value greater than one.
Thus it exceeds the required upper bound in both ranges uniformly over all denominators.
A second reviewer independently checked the source-unit substitution, numerical constants and both denominator ranges against the fixed mathematical records.
The proof establishes \(p<10^{14}\) for prime and composite odd conductors without an endpoint inequality or a restriction on the coefficient heights.
The remaining finite conductor range for modular multiplication is outside this exclusion.
The comparison with an ordinary polynomial factor uses the previously reviewed complete exclusion under the additional polynomial identity. [Review of L464 v28](Yin_R_G1107_v65.md#rational-factor-review).
