## Tail exponents for one fixed hyperbolic algebraic unit

At 2026-11-20T22:18 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed-polynomial theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v670.md#statement) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the submitted mathematics and the requested scope, without proof-development discussion or previous findings on this theorem.

Write \(J=[0,W-1]\cap\mathbb Z\).
If the squared tail is less than \(\|P\|_2^{-2}\), convolution bounds every numerator coefficient outside \(J+[0,d]\) strictly below one in absolute value.
Its integrality forces it to vanish, even when the original numerator has Laurent terms of arbitrarily large positive or negative degree.
Monic division now gives \(A=PQ+R\), with \(Q\) supported on \(J\) and \(\deg R<d\).
Partial fractions give the negative coefficients of \(R/P\) from the interior roots and the nonnegative ones from the exterior roots.
In each tail the first square block is a Vandermonde matrix on distinct nodes, and each infinite column has finite squared norm.
The resulting lower and upper comparisons with the weighted sum of squared conjugates hold uniformly in \(W\), and the tails of \(Q+R/P\) are identical to those of \(R/P\).

The product of the weighted conjugate magnitudes is \(e^{-mW}|N(A(\alpha))|\), with each complex pair counted twice.
Its norm factor is a positive integer, so arithmetic-geometric mean gives the unrestricted lower exponent \(2m/d\).
Tails at or above the support cutoff satisfy this estimate after the constant is decreased.
The power basis embeds \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) as a full lattice, and scaling both real coordinates at each exterior complex place gives determinant \(e^{-mW}\).
Minkowski's convex body theorem on a Euclidean ball gives the matching squared norm.
The norm convention at complex pairs changes only a fixed factor.
The corresponding integral proper representative has real Laurent coefficients, so subtracting one nearest integer per index of \(J\) preserves both tails and leaves at most \(W/4\) energy in \(J\).

For a small-tail numerator with coefficient mass \(H\), deleting vanishing subsets preserves its value at every conjugate.
Each prefix of the surviving terms is nonzero at every conjugate, and there are at most \(H\) terms.
At an exterior place of log modulus \(\lambda\), translating a prefix of span \(l\) to degree \(l\) bounds the product at the other places by \(H^{d-1}e^{(m-\lambda)l}\).
Translating back gives the prefix lower bound \(H^{1-d}e^{\lambda b-ml}\).
At a gap \(g\), the ratio of the omitted upper bound to this lower bound is at most \(H^d e^{(m-\lambda)l-\lambda g}\).
If the ratio bound exceeds \(1/2\), the next span is at most \((m/\lambda)l+(d\log H+\log 2)/\lambda\).
Iteration gives a final span \(O_P((1+\log H)B_P^H)\), including when \(m=\lambda\).
This also applies to the inverted unit, whose measure is the same and whose largest surviving exponent is \(-a\).

The original tail therefore controls the two endpoint exponentials up to the common factor in (4), since the surviving polynomial has the same residue at all roots.
The inequality \(a+W-b\le W\) still applies when \(b\ge W\).
Balancing the endpoint distances gives the exponent \(\kappa\).
The estimate \(H\le\|A\|_2^2\le D^2E(A)\) shows that each fixed energy cap makes the loss in (4) a constant.
For a variable cap, a tail at most \(e^{-(\kappa+\eta)W}\) forces \((1+\log H)B_P^H\gg_{P,\eta}W\), whence \(E(A)\gg_{P,\eta}\log W\).
For the matching upper bound, a minimizing numerator exists by the fixed-polynomial finite search, and multiplying it by \(X^j\) preserves energy.
Choosing \(j=\delta_+W/(\delta_-+\delta_+)+O(1)\) equalizes the two exponential decay bounds up to fixed factors, including at the endpoint cap \(H_0=E_*\).

Finally the sums of the positive and of the negated negative logarithmic moduli both equal \(m\).
Comparing each sum with its minimum times its number of terms gives \(\kappa\le 2m/d\), with equality exactly for constant radii on each side.
This condition permits nontorsion quotients between roots of the same modulus.
For example, \(X^3-X-1\) is irreducible by the rational root test and has discriminant \(-23\), a real root \(\alpha>1\), and two nonreal roots of modulus \(\alpha^{-1/2}\).
If the two nonreal roots had equal positive powers, then \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^n)\) for that power would have degree less than three and hence degree one.
The rational unit \(\alpha^n>1\) this would require is impossible.
Both exponent estimates and the necessary logarithmic energy growth follow for the stated fixed input.
