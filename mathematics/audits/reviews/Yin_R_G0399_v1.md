### The full relative group and determinant normalization

A genuine Salem field has signature(2,t-1) over its degree-t totally real trace field, with t>=2.
Its real embedding forces its only roots of unity to be plus or minus1; the same holds for F.
Above every nondistinguished real place the relative norm is a positive squared complex modulus.
At least one such place exists, so -1 cannot be a relative norm.
The source's norm-in-mu group is therefore exactly the norm-one kernel, not a different group of index2.

For a norm-one unit, all complex moduli are1 and the two real values have product1.
A distinguished value of modulus1 forces the actual element to be plus or minus1 by injectivity.
Otherwise a sign and inversion put exactly one real value outside the circle, with positive reciprocal partner.
A proper own subfield would repeat that unique expanding value among the full embeddings, which is impossible.
Thus the resulting element is a full-degree Salem number, and the accepted fixed-field theorem gives E={+/-tau^j}.
The same inverse involution fixes F: the nonidentity real embedding sends the Salem generator to its positive reciprocal, consistently with alpha=tau^a.
No smaller order has been substituted for the full integer ring.

Sun18 deletes one place above each place of F.
Here that deletes every complex place and one of the two real places, leaving one real row of weight1.
The free generator tau gives the absolute determinant |log tau|=log tau.
There is no hidden complex weight2, torsion-unit multiplier or Euclidean sqrt(2) covolume in this convention.

### Two quotient maps and the ordinary regulator

The map u to u/sigma(u) has image in E and kernel U_F, since fixed integral units are precisely the full F-units.
It sends E to the subgroup generated by tau^2, including the torsion element -1 mapping to1.
