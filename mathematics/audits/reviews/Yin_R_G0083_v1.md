### Ordered-cycle determinants and the fixed-rank exclusion

The review checked the determinant expansion directly.
For an isolated cycle, a permutation supported on graph edges is a disjoint union of fixed vertices, edge transpositions and, possibly, one of the two full directed cycles.
Each fixed vertex contributes 1+t, each transposition contributes -t regardless of edge sign, and the two full cycles have exponents k and n-k, with the common sign equal to (-1)^(n-1) times the edge-sign product.
The path recurrence and the subtraction of closing-edge matchings give 1+t^n for the matching part.
Thus both the frustrated formula (t^k+1)(t^(n-k)+1) and the absolute formula (t^k-1)(t^(n-k)-1) hold, including n=3.
Both are cyclotomic products.
Ordering affects k, so arbitrary vertex reordering is not silently an allowed polynomial-preserving operation.

For rank four and E<=3, integrality indeed forces entries in {0,1,-1} with at most three nonzero positions; the 233 cases are exhaustive.
The elementary graph enumeration at t=1 lists all possibilities: two-edge support is either a matching or a path plus an isolated vertex, and three-edge support is a path, star, or triangle plus an isolated vertex.
Their determinants are exactly the listed 9/8 and 5/4/0-or-8 values.
Only signed stars have determinant four; they have polynomial (1+t)^4-3t(1+t)^2 and value zero at -1.
They therefore cannot match (t^2+1)^2.
This excludes every lower-energy endpoint at the same rank and full polynomial, irrespective of intermediate energy, but does not exclude stabilization or discarding neutral factors.

### Integral congruence obstruction

The displayed root basis has Gram entries -1 along consecutive edges and +1 on the closing edge, hence the required frustrated sign product.
Its integer span is the even-coordinate-sum lattice: subtracting half the coordinate sum times the last root leaves a zero-sum integer vector generated by consecutive differences.
This proves the identification without an external root-lattice classification.

Every nonzero vector in this lattice has even positive squared norm, at least two.
The norm-two vectors are exactly the signed pairs of distinct coordinate vectors; they span the lattice.
Their nonorthogonality graph is connected: supports meeting in exactly one coordinate already have nonzero inner product, disjoint supports have a linking root, and orthogonal roots with the same two-coordinate support are linked using a third coordinate.
In an orthogonal integral decomposition every norm-two vector must lie entirely in one summand, since two nonzero components would have total norm at least four.
Connectivity and spanning therefore rule out a nonzero second summand.

For an integrally congruent upper-unitriangular W, its symmetric form remains positive definite.
The 2-by-2 principal minors force strict upper entries to have absolute value at most one.
A disconnected support would give exactly the prohibited orthogonal lattice decomposition.
Consequently E(W)<n forces a connected n-vertex graph with n-1 edges, hence a tree.
In its determinant the coefficient of t^(n-1) is n minus the number of edges, namely one: matchings of size at least two cannot reach that degree.
The target coefficient is zero for 2<=k<=n-2.
Congruence preserves the full polynomial, so this is a contradiction.

If W were switchable, its nonzero entries could all be made -1.
Any support cycle would have a nonzero indicator vector of symmetric-form value at most zero, with chords only lowering the value.
Positive definiteness forces the connected graph to be a tree, yielding the same contradiction.
This proves the no-switchable-endpoint assertion for arbitrary unimodular integral congruences at fixed rank, not merely for the adjacent generators.
Neither this conclusion nor the raw isolated absolute cycle's zero measure asserts a cost obstruction after stabilization or neutral-factor changes.

### All-size path surgery and attachments

The review checked the adjacent pair blocks in both directions, including their unimodular determinants and preservation of upper unitriangular form.
In the forward sweep the accumulated columns are e_2,...,e_(j+1), h_j, e_(j+2),...,e_m, where h_j is the sum of the first j+1 original basis vectors.
The current pair entry is -1 at every forward step.
The final pair entry is +1, giving columns e_2,...,e_(m-1),e_m-h,h under the inverse move.
These formulas hold at the minimal m=3 endpoint as well.

Direct internal products give energy m after each forward step and m-1 before and after the whole sweep.
The coupling calculation can be checked as a single coefficient-matrix identity.
If T is a prefix basis change, then

    T T^T-I=h_j h_j^T-e_1 e_1^T.

For the final basis change it is

    T T^T-I=2hh^T-e_m h^T-h e_m^T-e_1 e_1^T.

For each earlier or later outside coordinate, its coupling row transforms by T; its squared norm therefore changes by the corresponding quadratic form.
Summing over all outside coordinates proves exactly both D21 energy formulas, without an ordering omission.
The blocks B_L, B_R and D are untouched because the basis change is confined to the middle block.

Substitution of u_1=-e, u_2=...=u_(m-1)=0 and u_m=-e+b with e orthogonal to b gives forward excess one and final excess minus one.
The two distinguished cycle attachments have the correct frustrated sign product.
Outside attachments at the distinguished outside vertex and at the last block vertex are permitted; interior block attachments are not part of this special one-unit assertion.
With disjoint pendant coordinates of cardinalities d_i, the prefix cost is 1+sum_(i=2)^(j+1)d_i and the final cost is -1+d_1+2sum_(i=2)^(m-1)d_i; d_m cancels.
The resulting possible increase is a property of this sweep, not an impossibility theorem for every other operation.

### First-escape barrier and its limits

For a simple triangle-free signed graph, adjacent edge endpoints have disjoint external neighbor supports.
The exact local energy formula thus reduces to degree(reflected endpoint)-1.
A nonedge move simply swaps vertices.
A zero-energy edge move reflects a leaf; swapping the two vertices and changing the sign at the new leaf gives the same signed graph.
This statement is about the symmetric signed graph and does not claim equality of ordered bilinear matrices after an arbitrary permutation.

Every zero-energy step consequently preserves simplicity, triangle-freeness, vertex degrees and the signed-graph class.
Before the first departure from that class, the energy remains its initial value.
If each nonleaf vertex has degree at least r+2, the first departing edge move reflects such a vertex and costs at least r+1.
This proves the stated lower barrier.
In the explicit cycle-with-r-pendants family, degree r+2 at cycle vertices and degree one at leaves verify the hypothesis; its frustrated cycle makes it nonswitchable.

The barrier is a necessity for adjacent-mutation/sign paths, not for arbitrary integral congruences, and says nothing about whether a successful path exists.
The sharp one-unit assertion additionally needs the whole input to be simple and triangle-free; that condition is explicit in D21.
The pendant family supplies no small-measure or general scalar-realization coverage.
These scope boundaries are adequate and are part of the review finding.
