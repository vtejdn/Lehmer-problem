# Review of the coefficient test for powers and its reciprocal residue frequencies

<a id="finding"></a>
## The extra dyadic bit is necessary once per pair of distinct reciprocal residue factors

At 2026-11-25T05:16 UTC a reviewer outside the development of [C0901 v167](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v167.md#statement) checked the fixed four sections for Yin.
The review used the fixed proof and its identified antecedent, without access to the author's development, explanations or prior findings about this version.
The global necessary conditions, both local equivalences at squarefree reduction, the two frequency formulas and the global dyadic equality example follow.

The power map on roots is a polynomial map on monic coefficients by integral symmetric functions, including for repeated roots.
The cyclotomic product has the same fixed sign for two polynomials of the same degree.
Its linear variation at a perturbation divisible by \(p^k\) is an invariant integral sum of \(p\) summands which coincide upon reduction at \(\zeta_p-1\).
It is therefore divisible by a further factor \(p\) over the base, and the nonlinear terms are divisible by \(p^{2k}\).
This also works at two, where the root of unity in that calculation is \(-1\).
The coefficient map reduces to the identity modulo \(p\), so iterating increases successive agreement by one digit.
The unique fixed point in each residue class is the polynomial of the Teichmüller lifts with their residue multiplicities, including zero when present.
The fixed point and first iterate agree with the original polynomial to the same requested precision if the latter two agree, by summing the convergent successive differences.
Conversely congruence to the fixed point gives congruence to the first iterate by contraction.
For an in-field root \(\rho^{p^e}=\alpha\) the root is an integral unit and \(\mathbb Q(\rho)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), so the relative powering steps preserve all final conjugates and composing the map \(e\) times yields the original minimum.
The initial minimum of \(\rho\) is in the same residue class; its \(e\) contractions give the asserted \(e+1\) digits without a ramification assumption.
The one-step congruence implies the earlier divisibility because evaluation of \(\Psi_p S-S\) at \(\alpha^p\) gives \(-S(\alpha^p)\).
A nonzero root in a finite invariant multiset for powering is periodic and hence torsion, ensuring that the coefficient content on nontorsion irreducible unit inputs is nonzero.

At squarefree reduction the local factors define unramified extensions and have distinct residue roots in the common unramified splitting field.
Only the matching lifted linear factor of \(S_*\) has positive valuation at a component root of \(S\).
This proves the implication from coefficient agreement to individual root agreement, and multiplying all matched linear factors proves its converse.
The Teichmüller part is uniquely divisible by \(p^e\) within its group.
The logarithm handles the full principal-unit group at odd primes and the subgroup \(1+4\mathcal O\) at two.
In the dyadic quotient the first squaring of \(1+2y\) gives exactly \(1+4(y+y^2)\), and each further squaring shifts that same residue by one binary place.
The resulting Artin–Schreier image is the trace-zero space, including in residue degree one.
After matching that residue, the remaining error in \(1+2^{e+2}\mathcal O\) is an exact \(2^e\)-th power by the logarithm.
The Teichmüller norm is a unit of odd finite order in \(\mathbb Q_2\), hence one, and multiplying the principal conjugates adds their residues modulo the next binary digit.
This yields precisely the signed local constant-term condition.
For the separate global exceptional class, unramified dyadic integer-valued valuations of \(-4x^4\) are congruent to two modulo four and cannot be valuations of a unit.

In even reciprocal degree the Teichmüller polynomial has constant one and reciprocal root multiset, so agreement on the \(n\) free coordinates counts the first \(en\) digits.
A squarefree binary reciprocal reduction of even degree cannot have the self-inverse root one, because its value and derivative there satisfy the stated identity.
Hence the self-reciprocal local factors have their roots paired in distinct inverse pairs, and their norm is exactly one.
A pair of distinct reciprocal factors has inverse constant norms and imposes just one binary trace condition after coefficient agreement.
Lifting a member of any labeled pair by adjusting its constant by \(2^{e+1}\) and forming its monic reciprocal is an involution modulo \(2^{e+2}\).
Hensel uniqueness identifies the adjusted pair in the product, so the involutions commute, preserve all lower digits and toggle independently every specified bit.
This gives \(2^{-en-r}\), as against \(p^{-en}\) at odd primes, and Chinese remaindering on the free coefficients justifies the multi-prime product.
As a direct check at \(e=1\), the two distinct binary cubics \(X^3+X+1\) and \(X^3+X^2+1\) have product \(1+X+\cdots+X^6\).
Among the 64 reciprocal lifts of that product modulo eight, exactly four occur as a squared-root polynomial.
This was checked by running through the \(4^6\) monic lifts \(Q(X)=E(X^2)+XO(X^2)\) of the same binary reduction and taking \(E(T)^2-TO(T)^2\bmod8\).
The four images have nonleading coefficient lists in increasing degree \((1,a,b,1,b,a)\) for \(a,b\in\{1,5\}\), as predicted.

Finally the real quadratic unit in the sharpness example and every indicated even power remain irrational because the two conjugate moduli differ.
The traces start at three and stay congruent to three modulo four under \(t\mapsto t^2-2\).
Factoring \(t_{e+1}+1\) increases its dyadic valuation by exactly one each time, and the difference \(t_{e+1}-t_e\) has the same valuation as \(t_e+1\).
This proves the stated exact content and realizes the global in-field exponent for each \(e\).
The residue frequencies describe local power conditions and supply no converse to the global necessity.
