# Independent review of the denominators and roots of monic derivatives of a Salem trace

<a id="finding"></a>
## The two denominator estimates, trace descent, root confinement and the integer shift

At 2026-11-25T04:27 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v151 independently reviewed for Yin the [fixed statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v151.md#statement) and all four proof sections.
The reviewer had worked on some earlier trace results but received the fixed v151 without its development discussion.
Both bounds in (1), the liminf constant, the large-degree descent and the two root-location tests hold under their stated hypotheses.

All \(n\) trace roots are simple and real, with one \(T\in(2,5/2]\) and the others in \((-2,2)\).
Iterated Rolle interlacing puts all derivative roots strictly between the extreme original roots and at most one above two.
Dividing the derivative by its leading coefficient multiplies an original \(j\)-th elementary symmetric function by \(\binom rj/\binom nj\).
For \(j=1,2\) this gives the unchanged mean and exactly \(V_r=(r-1)V_n/(n-1)\).
The reduced denominators of the resulting rational coefficients give the stated least common multiple.

For a squarefree integer polynomial of degree \(D\) and leading coefficient \(q\), the ordered sum of the logarithmic root distances is at least \(-(2D-2)\log q\).
The distance comparison in the reflected Fourier estimate adds \(2(D-1)\sum_i\log^+|z_i|\) to this leading-coefficient allowance, exactly \(2(D-1)\log M(P)\).
This extends that estimate to reducible, nonmonic inputs without assuming primitive coefficients.
At smoothing radius \(e^{-1/D}\), the off-diagonal smoothing and the diagonal loss are \(O(D\log(2D))\).
For the Salem minimum, reversing the one reflected exterior root in frequencies one and two costs at most \(\alpha^2-\alpha^{-2}<4\).
Pairing reciprocal roots gives the sums \(n\mu,\sum(t_i^2-2)\).
The frequency bounds therefore give both \(\mu=O(\sqrt{\log(2n)/n})\) and \(V_n=2+O(\sqrt{\log(2n)/n})\).

The reciprocal lift of \(q_rG_r\) has degree \(2r\) and leading coefficient \(q_r\).
Distinct trace roots give disjoint inverse-pair sets, and the only possible repeated lift is at \(1\) when the trace is two.
Removing its two copies preserves integrality and the leading coefficient.
The remaining degree is \(D=2r+O(1)\), and its Mahler measure is at most \(2q_r\), because its only possible exterior root is positive and below \(\alpha\).
The deleted roots and the reflection correction affect the second power sum by \(O(1)\).
Comparing the frequency-two estimate with \(r^{-1}\sum_y y^2=\mu^2+(r-1)V_n/(n-1)\) gives the first lower bound in (1) after squaring, with \(\log(2n)/n\le\log(2r)/r\).
If \(r\to\infty,\ r/n\to s\), the power sum divided by \(r\) tends to \(-2(1-s)\).
Its squared contribution to the energy divided by \(r^2\) tends to \(2(1-s)^2\), since the frequency has coefficient \(\rho^4/2\).
The Mahler term there is \((4+o(1))\log(2q_r)/r\), and the smoothing error vanishes, yielding the lower constant \(1/2\).
For the second denominator bound, the mean of the \(\binom r2\) squared distances is \(2rV_n/(n-1)\).
The discriminant of \(q_rG_r\) is \(q_r^{2r-2}\) times their product and is a nonzero integer.
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives exponent \(\binom r2/(2r-2)=r/4\) as claimed.

At \(q_r=1\) the second inequality gives \(r\gg n\), and the first gives \(k=n-r=O(\sqrt{n\log(2n)})\).
For \(B(X)=\prod(2-t_i+X)\), the reciprocals of the \(2-t_i\) exceed \(1/4\), so counting extensions of degree-\((k-1)\) products gives \(B_k/B_{k-1}\ge(n-k)/(4k)\).
By Voutier at degree \(2n\) and \(T-2\ge(\log\alpha)^2\), one has \((T-2)(n-k)>4k\) uniformly in this range eventually.
Thus the coefficient of \(X^k\) in \(H(2+X)\) is negative, exactly the sign of \(G_r(2)\).
The largest root \(y\in(2,T)\) of an integral \(G_r\) is irrational since there is no integer in this interval.
Its other conjugates are in \((-2,2)\), so the quadratic lift is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(y)\) by the discriminant at any such place.
The resulting Salem value is strictly smaller, of degree \(2\deg y\le2r<2n\).
The large-degree conclusion deliberately excludes \(r=1\); for example the irreducible trace \(T^3-4T-1\) has roots in \((-2,-1),(-1,0),(2,5/2)\) and has normalized linear derivative \(X\).

For the barrier step, \(h=(\sum_i1/(z-x_i))^{-1}<z-\max x_i\), so each stated \(a_i,p_i\) is positive and \(\sum p_i=1\).
Writing \(S=\sum a_i\), one gets \(\sum p_i a_i=S-1\) and \(\sum p_i a_i^2=\sum a_i^2-S+1\).
The weighted variance is exactly \(\sum a_i^2-S(S-1)\), which proves the logarithmic derivative inequality.
A smaller fixed shift than the new inverse logarithmic derivative keeps the evaluation point farther right, where the logarithmic derivative of the next real-rooted polynomial is smaller.
This proves the \(k\)-step displacement bound for both the original and the reflected roots.
For shrinking \(\alpha\), the trace distribution tends to the arcsine law on \([-2,2]\), with the extreme trace eventually bounded away from any fixed evaluation point \(z>2\).
The limiting transform there is \(1/\sqrt{z^2-4}\), and substituting \(z=2/\sqrt{s(2-s)}\) gives \(2\sqrt{s(2-s)}\) with error uniform over \(k\ge(1-s)n\).
At \(s<1-\sqrt3/2\) this value is below one and \(z>4\) also bounds the kernel for all \(1<\alpha\le2\); at growing degree the same angular equidistribution follows from the bounded-measure Fourier estimate, with only one exceptional trace.
In an interval strictly inside \((-2,2)\) an algebraic integer with every conjugate there is cyclotomic trace by the unit-circle quadratic lift, and its conductor is bounded by the largest such trace conjugate.
Simplicity gives the uniform count; for the interval \((-1,1)\) the integral norm forces the value to be zero.

For the integer shift one has \(H(2)\le-1\) and \(H'(2)<0\) eventually by the same \(k=1\) coefficient estimate.
The final critical minimum then lies above two and strictly below level \(-1\), and \(H(T)=0\).
Assuming all other negative minima reach \(-1\), alternating maxima and minima supply all roots of \(H+1\) with multiplicity, including two at an interior tangency.
Below the least original trace, even degree gives positive \(H\), and odd degree gives increasing \(H\) with \(H(-2)\le-1\), so no shifted root is below \(-2\).
There is exactly one above two, in \((2,T)\); when \(H(2)=-1\) the endpoint two is a separate root.
The irreducible factor through the root above two cannot contain a rational endpoint, and its lift gives strict decrease in value with degree at most \(2n\).
This last degree bound justifies the stated stronger record hypothesis for the shift.
