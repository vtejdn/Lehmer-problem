## The sixteen real branches give full symmetric geometric monodromy

The proof establishes the statement at its stated uniformity.
The quadratic discriminant has eight simple zeros in the real \(U\)-line, since its four real \(t\)-roots are distinct and \(dt/dU=1+U^{-2}>0\) at their preimages.
A simple zero forbids the discriminant from being a square even over \(\mathbb C(U)\), and the unit middle coefficient excludes a vertical component.
At each double root \(v_0\), implicit differentiation gives \(f'(v_0)=0\) and \(f''(v_0)=-2a/F_U\ne0\) for the Laurent equation used in the proof.
The derivative of the slope there is \(v_0f''(v_0)/u_0\ne0\).
At each of the eight real boundary limits the coefficient of the linear term in \(w\) is one and the edge has a simple zero, so the derivative of the slope at zero is \(\epsilon f'(0)/u_0\ne0\).
All sixteen inverse branches are real, pairwise disjoint and transverse for sufficiently small positive \(r=k/l\).
Their intersection multiplicities exhaust \((4,2)\cdot(4,2)=16\).
The twelve projective boundary points are smooth with a single coordinate of order \(1\) or \(-1\), so each of the zero and pole fibers has local degrees \(l,l,k,k,k,k\), including fixed sheets when \(k=1\).

Two distinct real limiting coordinates of equal modulus must be negatives, and \(t\) then changes sign.
The boundary and interior cannot share such a pair because the discriminant at each boundary value of \(t\) is one.
At an interior sign pair the two double roots are reciprocals and have modulus different from one, so the difference in log moduli is \(2k\log|v_0|+O(kr)\) up to ordering.
At a boundary point put \(c_0=\epsilon u_0/f'(0)\).
Then \(w=c_0r+O(r^2)\) and \(U=u_0(1+\epsilon r+O(r^2))\), giving the correction \(\epsilon k(\log r+\log|c_0|-1)+O(kr)\) to \(-l\log|u_0|\).
The term \(2k\log r\) in the difference at a boundary sign pair dominates its \(O(k)\) remainder.
For different limiting moduli the log difference divided by \(l\) tends to a nonzero constant, and the absolute log modulus divided by \(l\) at each branch tends to \(\big|\log|u_0|\big|>0\).
All estimates use finitely many fixed neighborhoods and depend on the direction through \(r\) after division by \(l\), so one threshold suffices even for bounded \(k\).

Each isolated simple branch value yields a transposition.
The graph of their conjugates is invariant under the transitive geometric group, and the induced transitive action on its components factors through the infinite cyclic fundamental group obtained by filling in these sixteen punctures.
Inertia at zero therefore acts as a single cycle on the components, whose number divides every original inertia cycle length, in particular both \(k\) and \(l\).
The graph is connected by primitivity, and its edge transpositions generate the whole symmetric group on \(2l+4k\) sheets.

On the two distinct phase graphs the periodicity of each lifted argument and the uniform derivative bound yield connected degree-\(l\) covers of the circle.
At each exterior center the cited parameter disk has radius tending to infinity with \(l/k\), and on \(|h|=1\) the chosen local logarithm uniquely recovers \(h=V^{-1}U^{l/k}\).
Thus each exterior graph is an embedded circle with \(T=h^{-k}\), and inversion supplies two disjoint interior graphs.
Their degrees together with those of the phase graphs exhaust the cover degree, establishing the unit-circle permutation independently of the local inertia calculation.
For \(ak+bl=1\), the inverse to \(X\mapsto(X^k,X^l)\) on the torus fiber \(V^k=U^l\) is \(X=U^aV^b\).
Simultaneous inversion acts there by \(X\mapsto X^{-1}\), and the arithmetic factorization of this particular fiber remains open.
