## Outcome of the full review

The pointwise lower bound applies at every prime and positive count \(r\).
For every \(r\to\infty\) with \(r=o(p^{3/8})\), both finite minima have normalized limit \(\lambda\), and one row of the prescribed sign realizes the asserted simultaneous recovery at every sufficiently large prime.
The distinct-degree constraint precludes any larger exponent in power order.

**Exact counting on the lower side.**
At a nonzero admissible powered value \(z\), the graph recovers \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\) and the derivative polynomial has exact order one less than the graph at \(\alpha\).
Both power and rational recovery commute with field embeddings.
The correspondence between successful primitive factors on the two sides is therefore injective in each direction, including for nonmonic factors.
Gauss's lemma puts the entire product of the distinct \(Q_i^{a-1}\) in \(W\) over \(\mathbb Z[Y]\).
The degree bound and the sum of the measures \(p\,m(P_i)\) give the two necessary inequalities, independently of shared row factors and of how many conjugates any one factor has.
Testing the multiplicity and power equation in each retained number field recovers precisely the qualifying factors, and their degrees are unchanged.
Distinct positive degrees sum to at least \(r(r+1)/2\).
Even when no row qualifies or a candidate ties the fallback, \(2\lfloor apr/2\rfloor\ge(a-1)pr\) supplies the pointwise scalar bound.

**Simultaneous construction.**
Fixing the base seed first permits every constant in the substitution degree and box calculation to depend on it.
C390 at \(L=\|P_0\|_1\) gives irreducibility when the dilation exponent is coprime to \(L_0=A_L\).
The exponents \(1+iL_0\) yield different degrees of total size \(O_{P_0}(N^2)\) and preserve the original measure exactly.
Each individual degree is \(O_{P_0}(r)<p-1\) eventually.
Thus every powered minimum has its original degree and measure \(ph\), and the different degrees preclude coincidences across the chosen factors.

For each seed the divided derivatives of the integer polynomials in the coefficient box are algebraic integers.
Multiplying their per-field counts bounds the number of simultaneous tuples even if some of the fields intersect.
Taking the first integer strictly above the displayed exponential threshold makes \((K+1)^n\) exceed this tuple count when \(n>Dt\).
The difference of two colliding vectors gives a nonzero graph because the coefficient positions in the two residue classes are distinct.
In the stated range \(\eta\to0\), \(N/r,t/a\to1\) including the rounding, and \(Dt/n=O_{P_0}(r^2p^{-3/4})=o(1)\).
After division by \(Nph\), the box terms without \(h\) and the norm factor give \(o(1)\), while the denominator correction is \(O_{P_0}(tDt/n)\).
A one-row graph would require the product of all \(Q_i^t\) in that row, contradicting its coefficient cost with a factor of two in the principal exponent.

**Multiplicity losses and the common sign.**
At each powered node of common order \(c_i\), extracting the row gcd puts twice that order into \(W_0\).
The remaining derivative identity contributes at least \(\max(0,t-c_i-1)\), yielding at least \(t+c_i-1\) altogether also when \(c_i\ge t\).
Comparing the product of these powers with the original norm bound gives average loss \(O_{P_0}(a\chi)\).
The proportion with \(c_i>t-a\) is \(O_{P_0}(\chi/\eta)=O_{P_0}(\eta^2)\).
This uses less than the reserved \(N-r\ge3\eta r\) eventually, since \(N/r\to1\), even when \(\eta r\) tends to zero.
At each retained factor the quotient value equation and ordinary coprimality force both row values to be nonzero.
Reciprocity preserves all the powers under the same formal reversal.
After changing both projected signs to the prescribed sign at width \(b\), the two candidate values never both vanish at any retained node.
There are at most \(r\) forbidden scalars among the \(r+1\) integers \(0,\ldots,r\), so one combination works for all the chosen factors.
The losses \(b_0\log2+O(\log(1+r))\) from division and combination are \(o(apr)\).

For \(r=\max(1,\lfloor(\log_2p)/4\rfloor)\), the separate prime-dilation argument has \(D=O_{P_0}(p^{1/4})\), \(t=a+r\) and \(Dt/n=O_{P_0}(p^{-1/2})\).
The unaveraged error from the box denominator is \(O_{P_0}(rp^{-3/8})=o(1)\) after dividing the twice-log norm by \(ph\).
Together with the other box errors and the derivative norm this gives \(\sum c_i\le r+o(1)\).
Integrality implies \(\sum c_i\le r\) eventually and hence every prescribed seed survives with at least \(a\) copies.

**Limiting statements.**
The fixed-seed construction gives normalized cost with limit superior at most \(h\) in the distinct-degree test and hence in both tests.
The argument takes this limit superior before taking the infimum over reciprocal unit seeds.
The degree-ten benchmark yields a strict gap below the fallback for all sufficiently large primes in the stated growing range.
The difference between the two normalizations vanishes there, using \(a\to\infty\) and \(\log(2pb+1)=o(apr)\).
For any qualifying selection from a minimizing row, its mean excess measure over \(\lambda\) is at most \(T_{p,r}-\lambda\).
Consequently all but a vanishing proportion have excess at most \(\sqrt{T_{p,r}-\lambda}\), with equality of every selected measure to \(\lambda\) if that difference is zero.
This concerns different irreducible factors and allows repeated numerical values of the measure.
