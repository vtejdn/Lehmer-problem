## Energy outside a union of moving intervals

At 2026-11-20T22:15 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the full [degree-scale interval claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v669.md#statement) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its dependencies and consulted no development discussion or earlier finding on this claim.

Bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure allow only finitely many monic integral polynomials by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
Thus \(m\to0\) forces \(d\to\infty\), and Voutier's Corollary 2 applies to these nonzero nontorsion units, giving \(dm>2d/(\log(3d))^3\to\infty\).
For each fixed \(t>0\), it follows that \(k=\lfloor t/m\rfloor\to\infty\), \(k/d\to0\), \(q=\lfloor d/k\rfloor\to\infty\) and \(0\le d-N<k\), where \(N=kq\).
Voutier's estimate is an accepted external input at statement scope.

Degree preservation gives a single root of \(P\) in the fiber over \(\alpha^k\).
The Fourier inversion in that fiber gives all section values \(B_r(\alpha^k)=u\alpha^{-r}\) with the same nonzero \(u\), since the integral factor \(P_k(X^k)/P\) and the numerator are both nonzero at \(\alpha\).
Multiplying a rational relation on the translates by \(\alpha^{k-1}/u\) after evaluation produces distinct exponents \(0,\ldots,N-1\).
Since \(N\le d\), evaluation is injective on that space, and the original integer coefficient matrix has a nonzero \(N\)-row minor.
The mean squared norm has the claimed factor \(m\), because each of the \(k\) section norms occurs \(q\) times with multiplier \(km/N\).

The determinant estimate in v667 applies to rationally independent integral Laurent rows without any upper bound on their supports.
I checked it directly by reducing the minor on the first independent columns, which makes the pivot coefficients one and contributes the square of the nonzero integer determinant when the row change is undone.
Reverse orthogonalization preserves the lowest coefficient, and the analytic quotient after reflection of the interior roots gives squared norm at least \(e^{-2km}\) for each normalized row.
For a short numerator the common translated support ends at exponent \(d+q-2\).
Each pivot at or above \(q-1\) therefore has width at most \(d-1\) even after subtracting higher rows, so v621's complex lowest-coefficient estimate applies there.
The remaining fraction of pivots is at most \((q-1)/N\le1/k\).
Replacing their improved factors by the unrestricted ones multiplies the determinant lower bound, after taking its \(N\)-th root, by at least \((1-e^{-4km})^{(q-1)/N}\), which tends to one at each fixed \(t>0\).

For each covering interval its quotient indices and all their shifts by \(0,\ldots,q-1\) lie in an interval of size at most \(w/k+q+1\), including intervals with negative endpoints.
Each coefficient outside this union can occur at most \(q\) times across the translated rows, so the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm on the unobserved coordinates is at most \(NU\).
This is the inequality required even when observation of another translate also removes some coefficients from outside \(I\).
If the union has size \(n<N\), its projection has a kernel of dimension at least \(N-n\) in the domain.
In an orthonormal basis starting with that many kernel directions the sums of squared norms of their images and of the remaining images are bounded by \(NU\) and \(NC\), respectively.
Hadamard's inequality and the two arithmetic-geometric mean inequalities give (2), with the empty block omitted at \(n=0\).

Indeed \(n/N\le (m/t_k)((W/d)(d/N)+s(1+1/q))\), where \(t_k=km\).
For \(m(W/d+s)\le K\), fixing \(t> K\) bounds the limiting dimension fraction by \(K/t<1\).
The continuous right side of (2) is positive on \([0,K/t]\), so its minimum supplies a lower bound depending only on \(C,K\) when \(t\) is chosen from \(K\).
In the vanishing-ratio case the fraction tends to zero for each fixed \(t>0\), giving \(t e^{-2t}\) in general and \(t/(2\sinh(2t))\) for short numerators.
The first attains \(1/(2\mathrm e)\) at \(t=1/2\), and the second tends to \(1/4\) as \(t\downarrow0\) after taking the lower limit in the inputs.
For rounded coefficients one subtracts a finite integral Laurent polynomial from \(A/P\) since its two tails tend to zero.
The resulting numerator stays in the same nonzero residue class, so the unrestricted bound applies with the energy of the distances themselves.
The product of each torsion-quotient class of conjugates is a unit with the same measure at degree \(d/f\), where \(f\) is the class size.
Strict degree records force \(f=1\), which prevents collisions under every positive power.

Both conditional lower bounds and the finite-ratio positivity statement follow.
The result assumes bounded scaled energy and gives no existence assertion for a shrinking family with that property.
