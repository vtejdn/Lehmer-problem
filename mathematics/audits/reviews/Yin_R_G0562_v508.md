## Outcome of the proof review

The exact minimum and witness algorithm holds for every fixed row degree, including for the stated sets of primes under their size promise.
The timed singleton algorithm has polynomial cost in the numerical prime and computes minima along an effective degree schedule tending to infinity.

At cost \(H\le 2^s\), L243 bounds the sum of the selected seed degrees by \(b\) and the sum of their \(p\,m(P_p)\) by \(\log H\).
In particular there are at most \(b\) primes, each degree is at most \(b\), and each monic seed satisfies \(|[X^j]P_p|\le\binom{\deg P_p}j2^{s/p}\).
These bounds include equality at the fallback threshold.
For a singleton the coefficient box is constant in \(p\).
Under the set promise \(2^{\lceil s/p\rceil}\le 2(2+s)^{C\log 2}\), and multiplying at most \(b\) list sizes still gives a polynomial in \(s\).
For any trial monic divisor \(D\) of a candidate \(P\), Gauss's lemma and multiplicativity give integral coefficients and \(M(D)\le M(P)\le\|P\|_1\).
Thus trial division within the stated larger box suffices.
The factor of \(\varphi(n)^2/n\) at each odd prime is at least one, and its factor at two is at least one half, giving the stated finite torsion test.

For any fixed tuple the same two coefficient vectors satisfy the remainder equations at every prime.
Reduction by the monic \(P_p^2\) requires integer additions and multiplications with a norm growth factor at most \(1+\|P_p^2\|_1\) per power through \(pb+1\).
This gives the claimed entry size and permits construction in polynomial time.
Sign inequalities in each vector express its one-norm exactly.
At prescribed degrees \(i,j\), the leading variables enforce those degrees, the Sylvester determinant tests coprimality, and the sum of squares tests all Laurent coefficients of the reflective difference.
This also handles one constant row, and the determinant convention for two constants agrees with rational coprimality when both constants are nonzero.
The total weight degree is at most \(2(i+j)+8\), and all its coefficients depend only on the degrees.

On printed pages 149–150 of DHKW06, the operator \(z_j\partial_{z_j}\) inserts an exponent as a weight, and linearity gives the short generating function for the full polynomial weight.
The proof of Lemma 3.3(i) evaluates the resulting finite sum at the all-ones vector by the rational-function evaluation algorithm, which allows poles in individual summands.
Taking \(k=1\) computes the integer sum exactly.
The rational inequality description permits equalities and lower-dimensional polytopes, and the empty lattice-point set contributes zero.
Nonnegativity makes positivity equivalent to existence of a qualifying pair.
Testing \(T=2^s\) first therefore distinguishes a genuine tie from an unattained cutoff.
Threshold bisection finds the least cost, and successive coordinate bisections in one feasible tuple and degree choice preserve a solution until the integer vectors are fixed.

For the final assertion L245 gives one deterministic singleton algorithm and the same exponent four for every \(b\).
For any fixed \(B\), all runs through \(B\) together cost \(O_B((p+1)^4)\) underlying steps.
Even logarithmic bookkeeping per step for this prefix fits within \((p+1)^5\) for sufficiently large \(p\).
Keeping only the last completed result as the selected output makes an interrupted run harmless.
Before any degree finishes one can return the valid degree-one fallback, since every eligible seed has degree at least two.
Consequently the selected degrees eventually exceed every fixed bound.
C408 then gives the asserted limit without requiring monotonicity or a convergence rate.
