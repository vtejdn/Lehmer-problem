# Independent review of the directional width bound for sparse Laurent polynomials

<a id="finding"></a>
## Torsion roots, degree shares and the scope of the coefficient bound

On November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the [directional bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v542.md#statement) and its proof.
I received the candidate, the requested scope and the univariate input, including its corrected reference, without participating in the candidate's development or receiving a preceding review finding or its development discussion.
The stated inequalities follow from the univariate degree-share theorem and Lawton's specialization theorem at their recorded scopes.

For a torsion zero, take any decomposition into minimal vanishing blocks of the specialized terms.
If the union of their support differences has rank one, every block, after dividing by a Laurent monomial, is a polynomial in the same primitive character and vanishes at the same root of unity.
In coordinates having that character first, reducing each block modulo its monic cyclotomic minimal polynomial gives zero, including for negative powers since the constant term is a unit.
Thus the assumed absence of such a factor forces two independent differences.
Mann's relation bound applies separately in the blocks containing those differences, even if they are different blocks or some root-of-unity phases coincide.
The two corresponding coordinate polynomials are linearly independent over the rationals, so their common polynomial divisor has degree at most \(r-2\).
Bézout's identity for the two coprime quotients bounds their evaluated integer gcd by a fixed nonzero integer.
All roots from a fixed pair therefore belong to one group of size \(O(N^{r-2})\), which controls their number without summing over individual possible orders.
There are finitely many pairs, and the Euler derivatives through order one less than the support size have an invertible Vandermonde matrix after monomial normalization.
This establishes the multiplicity estimate also for \(r=2\), where the bound is constant.

Completing the direction as a row of an integral unimodular matrix preserves the coefficient list, the primitive characters and torus Haar measure.
For each fixed support, the two extreme specialized exponents have difference \(N^{r-1}W_v+O(N^{r-2})\), including when the directional width vanishes.
Distinct exponents eventually stay distinct, all removed factors specialize to products with only torsion roots, and the residual factor has the torsion error just proved.
Consequently the nontorsion degree with repetitions divided by the total degree tends to \(W_v(R)/W_v(F)\) for each direction of positive denominator.
The [univariate theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) includes the summed degree estimate for an integral multiple, covering nonunit factors and content as well as unit factors.
The specializations escape every bounded set of nonzero relations, so the recorded Lawton theorem applies even when there are torus zeros.
Pointwise passage to the limit suffices before taking the supremum.
In one variable the quotient is already the actual degree share.
Width additivity, the zero measure of each cyclotomic character factor and Cauchy–Schwarz give all the stated special cases, including positive-dimensional tori with support in a proper affine subspace.

For the six-term example, equality in \(2=|X^L+1|\) on the unit circle gives \(X^L=1\), and substitution into the original equality gives \(X=1\).
The derivative \(L-2\ne0\) leaves a torsion-free quotient after removing one copy of \(X-1\).
The quotient has one coefficient \(-1\) and \(L-1\) coefficients \(+1\), so its effective support is \(L\).
The disjoint three-term blocks of \(F_L\) give \(K(F_L)=8^2/12=16/3\), while its univariate width share is \((L-1)/(L^2+L)\).
Thus both asserted lower bounds tend to zero at fixed support size six.
This review checks the interfaces with the cited mathematical inputs and does not rederive Lawton's theorem or the univariate coefficient theorem.
