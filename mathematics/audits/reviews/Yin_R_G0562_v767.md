## Determinant, equality case and energy consequences

For \(k\le d\), a relation among the first \(2k-1\) functions gives \(A=PB\) with \(\deg A<d\), forcing both polynomials to vanish.
Moving the pure monomials to the front and removing their projections therefore gives a positive determinant \(F_k=\det H_{k-1}\).
The shift sends the preceding span into the next preceding span separately for each of the two types of function.
The corresponding squared distances \(u_j,v_j\) decrease weakly, with \(v_0\le1\).
The ratios start at \(u_0\) and continue as \(v_{k-2}u_{k-1}\), so their successive geometric means decrease, including the comparison starting at \(q=0\).

Reciprocity and the absence of unit-circle roots pair the \(h\) inside roots with \(h\) exterior reciprocal conjugates.
In the full evaluation matrix the Vandermonde determinant supplies \(|\operatorname{disc}P|\) after squaring, and the diagonal weights supply \(e^{-2dm}/|\operatorname{disc}P|^2\).
The disk-kernel Schur complement scales each remaining row and column by the conjugate pair of disk automorphism factors, yielding \(V/\mathcal D\) for each kernel determinant.
Exterior differences divide the corresponding interior differences in modulus by \(|\alpha_i\alpha_j|\), and each mixed difference has denominator \(|\alpha_j|\).
The two contributions to the discriminant beyond the interior Vandermonde are thus \(Ve^{2(h-1)m}\) and \(\mathcal D^2e^{2hm}\).
This gives the stated power \(e^{-(4d-2)m}\mathcal D^{-4}\), also when \(h=1\).

In the Schur step, Rouché's inequality is strict on the circle, and division by the known zero at zero leaves a monic strictly stable polynomial of degree \(n-1\).
Reversal in the indicated length gives \(p_n=zp_{n-1}+a_np_{n-1}^*\) and \(p_n^*-\bar a_np_n=(1-|a_n|^2)p_{n-1}^*\).
For \(s=p_{n-1}^*\), the polynomials \(p_n\) and \(zp_{n-1}\) are both monic of degree \(n\) and agree at all roots of \(s\).
In the product formula for their resultants against \(s\), the actual leading coefficient of \(s\) therefore occurs to the same power even if reversal lowers its degree.
Multiplicativity removes \(z\) since \(s(0)=1\).
For constant \(s\) the constant is one, so the same step gives \(R_n=(1-|a_n|^2)^n R_{n-1}\).
The identity \(p_h^*(z)=\prod_j(1-\bar\alpha_j z)\) gives \(R_h=\mathcal D\).

With \(|a_h|=e^{-m}\), each earlier factor in \(R_h\) is at most one, attaining one exactly when the corresponding \(a_n=0\).
The inverse recurrence then gives \(p=z^h+a_h\), and applying the forward step to that binomial recovers all the required zero parameters.
This includes the empty set of parameters for \(h=1\).
Conjugation gives real \(a_h\), so the middle coefficient is \(t=a_h+a_h^{-1}\) with \(|t|=2\cosh m\).
Conversely solving the quadratic in \(z^h\) gives the binomial stable factor for every trinomial in the stated class, where integrality forces \(|t|\ge3\).
The initial irreducibility hypothesis remains in this classification.

Taking the \(d\)-th root of the determinant uses \(4h/d=2\).
Since \(4-2/d\) stays bounded, the ratio of the resulting lower bound to \(1/(4m^2)\) tends to one uniformly in \(d\).
Both explicit bounds for the integral quotient energy dominate \(D_q\), one by \(\gamma_k\ge1\) and the other by the maximum containing \(D_q\).
Thus even their minimum cannot supply an \(O(1/m)\) upper estimate along a hypothetical shrinking reciprocal sequence, for any choice of \(q\).
The infimum of the actual energies can be smaller.
For \(h=2^j\) the translated example is Eisenstein at two, since its reduction is \(X^{2h}\) and its constant term is \(-6\).
Its two root radii give \(m=\log(4+\sqrt{15})\), and Haar invariance under \(z\mapsto z^h\) reduces the energy of one to the average of \((8-2\cos t)^{-2}\), namely \(8/60^{3/2}\).
At \(q=2h-1\) both explicit upper estimates grow with \(h\).

For the square estimate, translation makes the least exponent of \(B\) zero and preserves its boundary modulus.
The strict width inequality gives \(\deg B<h\), so \(B/p\) is analytic in the exterior and vanishes at infinity.
Its zero circle mean gives \(\langle B/p^*,p/p^*\rangle=0\).
Writing \(c=p(0)\), the vector \(1-\bar c\,p/p^*\) has squared norm \(1-|c|^2\) and pairs with \(B/p^*\) to give \(b\).
Together with \(|P|=e^m|p^*|^2\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives the first bound in (4), and its application to the nonnegative integrand gives the bound for the numerator \(B^2\).
For \(B=1\), comparison with the maximum of \(1/|P|\) gives the stated upper bound on the circle minimum.
This argument supplies a lower bound on the actual energy for these short squares, with no conclusion for a general integral numerator.
All four numbered assertions and the stated limitations follow under their hypotheses.
