### Frobenius alternatives and signs

Expanding the Laurent trace gives (1), and multiplying \(H_{q,c}(z)\) by \(z\) gives the six monomials and the \(cxy\) term of \(K_c(z,z^q)\), including all coefficient collisions.

The constant coefficient of \(H\) is one, so all three coordinates are nonzero.
Frobenius and symmetry give \(K_c(y,u)=K_c(y,x)=0\).
If \(y=-1\), the unique Frobenius preimage is \(x=-1\), including in characteristic two.
Otherwise \(K_c(y,T)\) has leading coefficient \(y+1\) and constant term \(y(y+1)\).
The repeated choice \(u=x\) gives \(x^{q^2}=x\) regardless of the multiplicity of the root in either polynomial.
For \(u\ne x\), Vieta gives \(u=y/x\), whence \(u^q=u/y=1/x\).
On this orbit Frobenius agrees with \(\tau(x,y)=(y,y/x)\), whose cube inverts both coordinates and whose sixth power is the identity.
The six Laurent terms in \(K_c/(xy)-c\) are permuted by \(\tau\).

The first case places \(z\) in \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), and the second places it in \(\mathbb F_{q^6}\) with \(q^3\)-Frobenius acting by inversion.
The corresponding traces lie in \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\) or \(\mathbb F_{q^3}\), respectively.

Substitution into \(F\) gives \(F(z,z^{q-1})=H_{q,1}(z)\).

In odd characteristic the corresponding relation uses \(K_1(z,-z^q)\), so symmetry and Frobenius alone do not put the preceding and succeeding iterates into the quadratic \(K_1(z^q,T)\) used for the positive sign.
