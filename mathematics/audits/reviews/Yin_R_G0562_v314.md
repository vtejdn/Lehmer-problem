### The Taylor remainder and the comparison integral

Substitution of \(T=2+u\) in the trace differential equation gives \(2j(2j-1)a_{n,j}=(n^2-(j-1)^2)a_{n,j-1}\).
Multiplying these ratios from \(a_{n,0}=2\) yields both the product in (8) and \(2n(n+j-1)!/((2j)!(n-j)!)\).
The recurrence for \(C_n\) gives integrality after translation by two.
The first omitted coefficient is positive through \(d=n-1\), and substitution of \((z-1)^2/z\) for \(u\) doubles the vanishing order of the remainder.
The Laurent exponents of the subtracted block range from \(-d\) to \(d\), so multiplication by \(z^n\) preserves the two end coefficients and gives a reciprocal polynomial.

For each fixed real \(b\), Jensen's formula for \(X^2-bX+1\) gives \(g(b)\), including at the repeated roots for \(b=\pm2\).
Over the compact range of \(b=B(2\cos\theta)\) the positive logarithmic part is uniformly bounded, and the fiber mean \(g(b)\ge0\) bounds the integral of the negative part by that of the positive part.
This also justifies integrating the fiber formula over \(\theta\).
On the arc of half-length \((d+1)/(4en)\), the coefficient bound \(2(en/j)^{2j}\) and \(2-2\cos\theta\le\theta^2\) majorize the tail by \(2\sum_{j\ge d+1}16^{-j}=(32/15)16^{-(d+1)}\).
Everywhere else, \(|2\cos(n\theta)-b|\le4e^{g(b)}\) since \(e^{g(b)}\ge |b|/2\) for \(|b|>2\).
Combining the estimates with normalized arc length \((d+1)/(4e\pi n)\) gives (3), with correction \(\log(4\cdot15/32)=\log(15/8)\).
For \(d=\lfloor5\sqrt n\rfloor\), the limiting lower bound exceeds \((175/66-2)\log2\) by \(e<3\) and \(\pi<22/7\).
For \(d=\lfloor n^{3/4}\rfloor\), its quadratic term grows on the order of \(\sqrt n\).

### Trace zeros and the finite measure bound

For \(1\le j\le d\le n/2\) the numerator in (7) is at least \(3n^2/4\) and its denominator is at most \(4d^2\).
At \(x\ge64d^2/n^2\) the successive term magnitudes in \(B(2-x)\) therefore grow by a factor of at least twelve.
The preceding terms sum in magnitude to at most \(1/11\) of the last, whose magnitude is at least \(2\cdot12^d\).
For \(n\ge16d\), every zero of \(C_n-B\) in \([-2,2]\) consequently exceeds \(7/4\).
The coefficient of \(T^{n-1}\) vanishes by the parity of \(C_n\) and the degree of \(B\).
Vieta's formula rules out having all trace zeros in this interval, counting multiplicity.
A trace outside it has a reciprocal pair of lifts with one root exterior, since the trace of every unit-circle point is real in \([-2,2]\).

For \(n\ge4d\), the same ratio bound is at least three, so \(L\le(12/11)a_{n,d}4^d\) and \(2+L\le3a_{n,d}4^d\).
On the circle the coefficient length gives \(\mu_{n,d}\le\log(2+L)\).
At roots with \(r\ge1\) all central Laurent powers have modulus at most \(r^d\), whence \(r^{n-d}\le L+1\).
Reciprocity gives the common annulus, and the constant coefficient and monicity give \(\sum|\log r|=2\mu_{n,d}\).
As \(\sum\alpha=0\), the phase sum has absolute value at most \(\sum|r-1|\le2e^\ell U\).
Its real part is at least \(n-3K/2\) for \(K\) roots with \(\cos\theta\le1/2\).
At these roots \(|\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2|=|\alpha-1|^2/r=r+r^{-1}-2\cos\theta\ge1\).
The lower powers in \(B\) then have total modulus at most half the leading term, yielding \(n|\log r|\ge\log(a_{n,d}/4)>0\).
The resulting sum over \(K\ge(2/3)(n-2e^\ell U)_+\) proves the factor \(1/3\) in (6).

### All sequences of sublinear degree

When \(d=o(n)\), the factorial bounds in (8) give \(\log a_{n,d}=2d\log(n/d)+O(d)\) with an absolute error constant.
Thus \(\log a_{n,d}/d\to\infty\), and \(U/n\to0\) follows from \(t\log(1/t)\to0\) at \(t=d/n\).
The first \(n-d-1\) coefficients below the leading term vanish.
For fixed \(k>0\) Newton's identities eventually give \(\sum\alpha^k=0\), and the normalized \(k\)-th phase moment has modulus at most \(ke^{k\ell}\mu_{n,d}/n\to0\).
Trigonometric approximation gives uniform limiting angular distribution with multiplicities, so the closed arc \(\delta\le|\theta|\le\pi\) has limiting mass \(1-\delta/\pi\).
On this arc the ratio of each earlier Taylor term to the next in modulus is at most \(16(d/n)^2/(3c_\delta)\), which tends to zero for each fixed \(\delta>0\).
The geometric sum of earlier magnitudes is eventually at most half the last term, uniformly in the roots on the arc.
Since \(d\log c_\delta-\log4=o(\log a_{n,d})\) for fixed \(\delta\), the root equation and the sum of the absolute radial logarithms give \(\liminf\mu_{n,d}/\log a_{n,d}\ge1-\delta/\pi\).
Letting \(\delta\) decrease to zero and applying \(\mu_{n,d}\le U\sim\log a_{n,d}\) proves (5).

For comparison with v161, \(B(Y+Y^{-1})\) has nonzero extreme powers \(\pm d\), and the two \(X\)-terms give at most \(2d+3\) terms altogether.
Its specialization with \(H=n,N=1,\phi=0\) has measure \(\mu_{n,d}\), so v161 (1) applies whenever \(n>2d\) and gives the stated \(O(d^2\log(2n)/n)\) error.
This use relies on the [existing review of v161](Yin_R_G0562_v161.md#residue-progression-measure) for the general estimate.
The constructed lower bound concerns the additive comparison error of the full polynomial.
The repeated root and divergent full measures leave the separable, bounded-measure and individual-factor questions open.
