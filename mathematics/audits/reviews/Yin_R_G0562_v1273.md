# Independent review of common prime-power divisors in the local residue orders

## Partial dyadic norm contributions and full splitting degrees at the coefficient sum

At 2026-11-25T16:22 UTC a separately assigned agent independently reviewed the whole [fixed C0901 v194](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v194.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer had worked separately on partial splitting via discriminants, without developing this proof or receiving its development dialogue, author explanations or earlier review findings on v194.
The assignment included a request to scrutinize the prime-exponent case.
The reviewer checked the exact integer index in the norm infimum and the final claim about all local factors at the primes of the coefficient sum.
The stated inequalities and local degree conclusions hold.

The [trace construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) and [common-power criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) ensure that \(T,Y\) belong to \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(T)\) and that \(\beta\) is a unit.
Indeed Bézout expresses \(\beta\) as a monomial in \(T,Y\), and its absolute values at the embeddings are the positive \(u\)-th roots of those of \(T\).
Thus \(h(\beta^{N/t})=h_t\) with the original degree \(d\).
The [congruence in the integral order](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#periods) gives \(\beta^N-1\in c\mathbb Z[\beta]\) for either parity of \(q\), without requiring a separable binary reduction.
The residue orders of \(T\) and \(\beta\) coincide since \(\gcd(u,N)=1\).

Write \(\gamma=\beta^{N/t}\).
At a selected dyadic embedding, \(\gamma\) reduces to one and \(1+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{t-1}\) is a unit by oddness of \(t\).
Hence \(v_2(\gamma-1)\ge e\), with valuations normalized by \(v_2(2)=1\).
Multiplicities in the embedding count give \(v_2(N_{\mathbb Q(\beta)/\mathbb Q}(\gamma-1))\ge edP_t\); all other finite valuations are nonnegative.
The absolute archimedean product is at most \(2^d\beta^{N/t}\).
The stronger [cyclic norm inequality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) applies with its integer index and explicitly includes powered-field multiplicity.
These give both bounds in (1), with the same argument for any set of primes \(r\mid c\) prime to \(t\), since the geometric sum reduces to the nonzero residue \(t\).
The parity switch at the other member of \(\{p,q\}\) uses the corresponding other congruence.

By the [real-pole estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#real), \(N\log\beta=\log Y-\epsilon\log T=d\log p+O(\sqrt{d\log(2d)\log L})\), as \(0<\log T<1/q\).
Division by \(dt\) gives (2) with its uniform error.
The [bounds on \(q,u,N\)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#words) imply that the error inside the numerator tends to zero uniformly at growing degree.
Hence the height tends to zero when \(t/\log p\to\infty\).
In the power-of-two specialization, \(e\log2=\log c=\log p+O(1/q)\); using the first bound verifies \(t^{-1}+O(1/\log q)\).

For each prime \(\ell>X=(\log d)^{2+\delta}\) dividing \(N\), a divisor \(m\) of \(N\) misses the full factor \(\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\) exactly when \(m\mid N/\ell\), including when the exponent is greater than one.
The exceptional fraction is thus \(P_\ell\).
The bound \(h_\ell=O(\log\log(9d)/X)\), monotonicity of \(h\log(1/h)\) near zero, and the cyclic norm estimate give \(P_\ell=O_\delta(\log\log(9d)/(\log d)^{1+\delta/2})\).
There are at most \(\log N/\log X=O_\delta(\log d/\log\log(9d))\) such distinct primes.
The union bound therefore loses only \(O_\delta(d/(\log d)^{\delta/2})\) conjugates.
On the complement \(N_*\) divides the residue order, which in residue degree \(f\) divides \(2^f-1\), and \(f\) divides the full local degree.
The convention at \(N_*=1\) and the exclusion when \(N_*>\sqrt d\) follow.

For prime \(v\), the real estimate \(v\log\beta/d=\log p+o(1)\) and \(\log\beta<1/q\) give \(v>d\) at large \(d\).
The height bound on \(q\) gives \(d>L\), so \(v\) is odd and no \(r\mid L\) equals \(v\).
The [rank-two trace factorization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v895.md#trace) applies for odd \(v\) and either parity of \(u\).
It yields \((Z+1)^2D_r\), and lifting the divisor of \(\Psi_v\) through \(W=Z+Z^{-1}-2\) gives \(D_r\mid(Z^v-1)/(Z-1)\).
Since \(v\) is prime and prime to \(r\), all roots of this last polynomial have exact order \(v\) and are simple.
They differ from \(-1\), of order two since \(r\) is odd.
The factors of \(D_r\) and the quadratic component therefore lift separately by Hensel factorization.
Each degree-\(f\) simple factor lifts irreducibly, is unramified, and has \(f=\operatorname{ord}_v(r)\); in the degree statement the unramified residue degree already attains the full factor degree.
The two roots of the complementary quadratic each have degree at most two.
Since \(r^f\ge v+1>d\), exactly these two roots count at any indicated integer cutoff.
The order \(f\) divides \(d-2\) by the partition into degree-\(f\) factors and divides \(v-1\) by Fermat.
In the cyclic group \((\mathbb Z/v\mathbb Z)^\times\), the order of the subgroup generated by all of the \(r\)'s is the least common multiple of their orders.
It follows that \(|\Gamma|\mid\gcd(v-1,d-2)\) and \(L^{d-2}\equiv1\bmod v\).

Finally, with \(g=\gcd(v-1,d-2),\ b=(d-2)/g,\ j=(v-1)/g\), one obtains \(bv-jd=b-2j\).
The [period identity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#identities) gives \(kA=v-\epsilon u\) and \(1\le k<2\log_2 L\).
Hence \(|kbA-jd|\le b+2j+bu\ll b(\log(3d))^C\), using \(j/b=(v-1)/(d-2)\) and the preceding bounds on both exponents.
Uniformly in the admitted \(q\), \(\log k=O(\log\log L)=o(\log d/\log^2 L)\) as an upper bound (bounded \(L\) also suffices).
For \(b\) below the smaller claimed cutoff one can thus use \(kb\) in the [binary period repulsion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#statement) with exponent \(1/4\).
Choosing the effective constant small enough that \(b\le d^{1/8}\) makes the upper bound \(o(d^{3/4})\), giving the contradiction and the group index consequence.
The norm bound covers all powers of the large prime divisors that occur; it provides no unconditional positive proportion of dyadically low-degree embeddings.
