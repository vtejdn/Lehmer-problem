# Review of small Mahler measures over solvable extensions of the totally real field

## The label field after base change and the trace formula near zero

On 2026-11-18T18:00 a separately assigned agent for Yin reviewed the [submitted decision in C345 v395](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v395.md#decision-real-label-spectrum-submitted), all its statements and arguments, and the inputs at their scopes of use.
The reviewer took no part in developing the proof and received its fixed text and scope without development discussion or a previous finding on it.
The dichotomy for arbitrary inputs of bounded measure and large compressed degree, the exact least adjunction degree in the nonreal case, and the two descriptions of the near-zero spectrum hold as stated.

For an arbitrary nonzero nontorsion \(x\), the torsion-quotient classes of its distinct rational conjugates are permuted transitively and have a common size \(f\).
Each product is a root of unity times the \(f\)-th power of any class member.
Different classes have products with nontorsion quotient, so the degree of the product \(\gamma\) is \(\deg(x)/f\), and the height is \(f h(x)\).
This checks the unnormalized measure identity even for nonintegral inputs.
The labels then have one or two roots each and \(d/2\le v\le d\).
The compressed splitting group above the faithful label group has abelian kernel acting on these fibers.
The original splitting field over the compressed one is contained in a solvable Galois extension obtained by adjoining roots of unity and radicals of powers of the class products.
Consequently the full rational splitting field \(L\) of \(x\) is solvable over the normal label field \(P\).

The totally real field \(T\) is fixed setwise by every rational automorphism of the algebraic closure and fixed pointwise by complex conjugation.
The finite solvable Galois extensions of \(T\) are likewise permuted and are closed under finite composita, proving the normality of their union \(B\).
All Salem traces and their conjugates lie in \(T\), so a finite compositum of quadratic extensions of \(T\) contains the corresponding Salem splitting field.
If \(P\subseteq T\), the finite Galois group of \(LT/T\) embeds in \({\rm Gal}(L/P)\), giving \(L\subseteq B\).

If \(P\) is not totally real, its normality puts a nonreal element in the specified copy of \(P\).
Complex conjugation then gives a nonidentity element of \(H={\rm Gal}(P/(P\cap T))\).
As \(P\cap T\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\), \(H\) is normal in the faithful label group.
The unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup \(D=(A_b)^u\) therefore lies in \(H\), with all its factors acting on their individual label blocks.
Restriction identifies \(H\) with \({\rm Gal}(PT/T)\).
The finite Galois extension \(PT\cap B\) of \(T\) is contained in one finite solvable Galois extension of \(T\) by finite generation and closure under composita.
The corresponding restriction from \(H\) to its solvable quotient kills the whole perfect group \(D\), since \(b\ge9\) eventually.
Its kernel identifies with \({\rm Gal}(PB/B)\), so no diagonal identification between blocks is introduced by base change.
The lower bound \(b\gg_M d/(\log(3d))^{212}\) gives \(\log b\ge\log d-O_M(\log\log(3d))\).
Multiplying \(\log(b!/2)\ge b\log b-b-\log2\) by \(u\), and using \(ub=v\le d\), gives the stated error.

For the tower obstruction let a finite group have a section \(A_b\), given by a subgroup mapping onto \(A_b\).
For any normal subgroup of the ambient group, the intersection with that subgroup maps either onto \(A_b\) or trivially, by simplicity.
In the second case the map factors through a subgroup of the quotient.
This shows that the absence of this section passes to extensions, in addition to subgroups, quotients and finite products.
At a bounded-degree step the conjugate splitting groups over the preceding normal closure embed in products of groups of order at most \(q!\).
For \(q<b\), \(q!\le(b-1)!<b!/2\).
At a step with solvable relative normal closure the analogous conjugate groups are solvable after base change.
Induction therefore excludes the section from the whole finite normal closure over \(B\).
For a tower reaching \(x\), take a rational conjugate tower for each rational conjugate of \(x\).
All these towers have base \(B\) and retain their relative step bounds by normality.
The compositum of their normal closures over \(B\) thus still excludes the section, but contains \(BL\) and surjects on its subextension \(BP/B\), whose Galois group contains \(A_b\).
This proves the lower bound for the individual element even if its conjugates are not all conjugate over \(B\).
For attainment, the C281 tower has two stages of degrees \(u\) and \(b\), with \(u<b\) eventually, followed by adjunctions with solvable normal closure recovering the actual \(x\).
Compositing each stage with \(B\) bounds the new single-generator degree by the original one, and preserves solvability of the relative normal closures.

Fix \(q\) and take \(M=1\).
The uniform compressed-degree bounds permit a single cutoff above which \(b>q\).
Below that degree cutoff the coefficients of the primitive minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\) are bounded by the binomial coefficients times \(e\), giving just finitely many positive measures to exclude.
Reducing \(\epsilon_q\) further below \(\log2\) and Smyth's logarithmic bound \(\log\theta\), where \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\) and \(\theta>1\), forces the primitive minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\) to be monic with constant \(\pm1\) and reciprocal.
An inverse is consequently a distinct conjugate, and each label consists of that inverse pair because compression eliminated all torsion quotients between distinct roots.
The map from pairs to their traces is bijective, as the roots of \(Z^2-tZ+1\) are uniquely determined by \(t\).
Its equivariance identifies the label field with the splitting field of \(\gamma+\gamma^{-1}\).
The imaginary part of \(z+z^{-1}\) vanishes precisely when \(z\) is real or \(|z|=1\).
The field dichotomy thus gives all three elementwise equivalences, with the conjugate-root condition imposed on \(\gamma\).

Every value under consideration in \(B_q\) has the equal-measure reciprocal representative \(\gamma\) with this root condition.
Conversely let \(z\) be any reciprocal unit satisfying the condition and having positive logarithmic measure in the interval.
Its trace \(t=z+z^{-1}\) is a totally real algebraic integer.
The field \(T(z)/T\) has degree at most two and is Galois, so \(z\in B\).
Since \(z^{-1}\ne z\) is a rational conjugate fixing their trace, the two roots of \(Z^2-tZ+1\) are conjugate over \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
Conversely take any totally real algebraic integer \(t\) for which this quadratic is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
Either root \(z\) is an algebraic integer unit, generates a field containing \(t\), and has exactly two distinct lifts of each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(t)\) as its rational conjugates.
Different embedded traces give disjoint pairs.
A trace \(t'\) in \([-2,2]\) gives zero contribution, and a trace with \(|t'|>2\) gives exactly one exterior root of absolute value \((|t'|+\sqrt{t'^2-4})/2\).
Thus either sign of exterior trace contributes \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|t'|/2)\) once, without any normalization by degree.
A positive sum excludes torsion and gives exactly the claimed converse when the sum lies in the interval.
This establishes a spectrum identity and leaves open whether such positive sums accumulate at zero.
