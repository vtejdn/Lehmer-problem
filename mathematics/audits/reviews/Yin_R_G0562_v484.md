# Review of the small-multiple criterion for two coprime rows

## Independent finding on both perturbed diagonals and their cofactors

At 2026-11-18T20:03 UTC an agent outside the development of [L462 v227](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v227.md#rows) independently reviewed its three sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had previously reviewed the cyclotomic fluctuation construction and received the present criterion as fixed mathematics.
Under (1), both diagonals of the prescribed sign satisfy the exact degree, coprimality and nonvanishing statements, and both actual cofactors satisfy (2).
The trace and determinant costs use the original short rows and remain bounded independently of \(B\).

### Removing common roots and controlling the perturbation

Put \(n=4d\).
The choice \(l=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) in L462 v206 is available even for \(m=2\), and \(d/l\le6\) for all \(m\ge2\).
Before division by any fixed polynomial, the two original rows have logarithmic coefficient sizes \(O(L)\), as do the reduced representatives of both traces.
They may be chosen simultaneously with \(u_i,D_i\ne0\), \(v_i/u_i\notin\mathbb Q\) and nonzero two-row determinant.
Since \(\beta\) has degree \(d\), its conjugates are the \(p\)-th powers of the distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\), with no collisions.
In particular \(Q\) is monic reciprocal of degree \(d\) with constant term one.

For a short row, formal reversal about \(n\) is defined even if an endpoint vanishes.
Subtracting the odd centered value equations at \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) gives
\(\beta^{-n/2}(A-A^\#)(\beta)=-\sigma(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})u\ne0\).
Thus \(E=A-A^\#\) is a nonzero polynomial coprime to the irreducible \(Q\).
If \(E(z)=0\) and \(z\ne0\), reversal implies \(E(1/z)=0\).
The common complex zeros of the input multiples are exactly the zeros of their monic gcd \(Q\), so at each of \(z,1/z\) some multiple is nonzero.
It is unnecessary for the same member to avoid these two roots.

Choosing a basis from the input multiples leaves all common polynomial divisors unchanged, since every discarded member is a rational linear combination of the basis.
The basis has at most \(2d+1\) members.
Retaining these members and adjoining their shifts to degree \(2d\) still gives gcd \(Q\), and all \(4d+2\) or fewer entries have coefficient sum at most \(\exp B\).
Some basis member has nonzero constant term since \(Q(0)=1\), and a shifted member has nonzero coefficient at \(2d\).
These endpoint requirements and the evaluations at \(z,1/z\) are all nonzero linear forms for a general combination.
Their product has degree at most \(2+2n\) because \(E\) has at most \(n\) distinct nonzero roots.
The integer grid with coordinates \(0,\ldots,2n+2\) contains a point where it is nonzero, since a polynomial of degree at most \(2n+2\) in each variable vanishing on that whole grid would be zero by repeated interpolation.
The resulting \(H\) has the required endpoint and nonvanishing properties and \(\|H\|_1\le(4d+2)(2n+2)\exp B\).

The polynomial \(T=H X^{2d}H(1/X)\) has exact degree \(n\) and nonzero constant coefficient, using both endpoints of \(H\).
It is invariant under reversal, is divisible by \(Q^2\) by reciprocity of \(Q\), and is nonzero at every nonzero zero of \(E\).
Each forbidden evaluation of \(A+kT\) there, and either endpoint condition, excludes at most one value of \(k\).
There are at most \(n+2\) excluded values, so the \(n+3\) choices \(0,\ldots,n+2\) suffice.
The difference of the resulting row and its reversal is still \(E\).
Any common nonzero root would belong to the just excluded list, and the nonzero endpoints exclude a root at zero and a loss of degree.
This construction can be carried out separately for the two original rows.

### Original traces, determinant and actual cofactor costs

Both \(T(\beta)\) and \((\mathcal ET)(\beta)\) vanish.
Adding \(kT\) therefore fixes the value and Euler derivative at \(\beta\) in each row, and preserves the two centered equations, their trace representatives and divisibility of both diagonals by \(P^2\).
Equivalently the added diagonal is \(k(1+\sigma x)T(x^p)\), which is divisible by \(P^2\) since \(P\mid Q(x^p)\).
After division by its positive content \(c_i\), the row is primitive and the diagonal is primitive since its two coefficient classes \(0,1\bmod p\) are disjoint.
The constant term of the diagonal is \(A_i(0)\), and its coefficient in degree \(pn+1\) is \(\sigma A_i(0)\), both nonzero.
Thus each final row has exact degree \(4d\), each diagonal has span \(4pd+1\), and \(F_i/P^2\) is a nonzero integral polynomial of degree \(4pd+1-2d\).

The final row values, derivatives and the two reduced trace representatives scale by \(1/c_i\).
The integral odd trace chart gives integral representatives after division, and uniqueness in the degree-\(<m\) Chebyshev basis identifies them with these scaled polynomials.
The remainder \(D_i=v_i+u_i-2(2\sigma-q)U_i'(q)\) also scales by \(1/c_i\).
The original two-row determinant scales by \(1/(c_1c_2)\).
Thus both nonzero evaluations, both nonzero remainders, the nonrational trace ratios and the nonzero determinant persist.
The norms of the row values and the determinant can only decrease in absolute value under this scalar division, giving the claimed normalized bounds from the original determinant and value identities of L462 v206.
The reduced-coefficient estimate gives \(\log|N_+D_i|/m\le\log(12m^3\max(1,\|U_i\|_C,\|V_i\|_C))+(1-1/m)h=O(L)\).
The lift of a reduced first trace has coefficient sum at most \(2m\max(1,\|U_i\|_C)\), so the primitive product of its distinct irreducible factors has \(m(S_{u_i})=O(L)\).

For each row the perturbed coefficient sum is at most \(\|A\|_1+(n+2)\|H\|_1^2\).
Since reversal preserves the coefficient sum and \(B\ge0\), the logarithm of the final diagonal's coefficient sum is \(O(L+B)\).
This bounds \(m(F_i)\).
Removing the two copies of \(P\) and the cyclotomic factors preserves the claimed upper bound for each \(m(R_i)\), by additivity, nonnegative logarithmic measure for integer factors and zero cyclotomic measure.
The bounds therefore apply simultaneously to the cofactors of the very same primitive coprime diagonals carrying the small trace and determinant data.
In the fourth-logarithmic window \(L/p=h+\log(2dp)/p\to0\) on a shrinking sequence, so the additional hypothesis \(B=o(p)\) suffices.
The argument uses this hypothesis to construct the rows and establishes no converse or unconditional existence of such short multiples.

### Cyclotomic and positive-measure controls

For squarefree \(N\), \(H_q=((X')^N-1)/((X')^{N/q}-1)\) is the product, each factor once, of the \(\Phi_b\) for \(b\mid N\) with \(q\mid b\).
An order \(b\mid N\) occurs for every \(q\mid N\) exactly when \(b=N\), proving the claimed monic gcd.
The geometric sum has norm \(q\) and degree \(N-N/q\).
Summing \(-\log(1-1/q)=O(1/X)\) over the \(s\) conductor primes gives \(N/d_N=1+O(s/X)<2\) eventually.
Thus the input degree cap holds and the cost is \(O(s)=O(\sqrt{\log d_N})\).
Every prime in the stated window is eventually smaller than all conductor primes, so the gcd and the family of multiples work throughout that window although \(J(\Phi_N)\) grows faster than every power of \(\log d_N\).

For the positive-measure controls multiplying each \(H_q\) by the common \(P_{0,p}\) multiplies their monic gcd by \(P_{0,p}\).
The degree becomes at most \(e+2d_N\le2(d_N+e)\).
Submultiplicativity of the coefficient norm and \(\prod_\gamma(1+|\gamma|)\le2^e\prod_\gamma\max(1,|\gamma|)\) give the displayed cost since \(m(P_{0,p})=p\,m(P_0)\) when the degree is preserved.
The large angular fluctuation follows from the lower bound for \(J(P_{0,p}\Phi_N)\) in L462 v225.
For fixed \(P_0\) of positive measure the cost estimate retains a positive multiple of \(p\), and the powered control has two rational factors.
This comparison neither invokes the irreducible-input row theorem for that product nor turns the sufficient \(o(p)\) cost condition into a necessary one.
