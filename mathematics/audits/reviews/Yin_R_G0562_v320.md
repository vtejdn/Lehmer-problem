### Clipping and quadrature

For fixed \(a\in[-2,2]\), the clipped ratio in \(L_a\) is at most one.
It is at least \(u/6\) on \(|x|\le4\) and at least \(1/3\) outside.
On either side of zero the numerator is affine or constant on the pieces cut out by \(a-u,a+u\), and the denominator is positive affine.
Each quotient is monotone there, so the total variation of \(L_a\) is \(O(\log(6/u))\) on the whole real line.
Splitting the circle at the zeros of \(b'\) gives \(O(d)\) monotone arcs for nonconstant \(b\), uniformly in its amplitude.
On each of the \(n\) cells in the shifted mesh, the pointwise discrepancy from its chosen endpoint is bounded by the variation on that cell.
Averaging the resulting bound over \(v=nt+\phi\) compares precisely the two clipped integrals since the mean of \(\log(2+|b(t)|)\) agrees on both sides.
The density of \(2\cos v\) is \(1/(\pi\sqrt{4-x^2})\) on \((-2,2)\).
On each half of this range it is bounded by a constant times the inverse square root of the distance from the nearer endpoint.
An interval of length \(2s\) therefore has mass \(O(\sqrt{s})\), uniformly in its center, giving a two-variable clipping loss \(O(\sqrt u)\) by integration against \(ds/s\).

### Flagged periods and logarithmic tails

Each equation for \(b=\pm3\), \(b'=\pm n/4\), or \(b''=\pm n^2/4\) has at most \(2d\) solutions for nonconstant \(b\), since a periodic derivative cannot be identically a nonzero constant.
On each resulting arc of the first flagged set, \(b'\) has fixed sign and magnitude greater than \(n/4\), while \(b\) stays in an interval of length six, giving arc length at most \(24/n\).
On each arc of the second set, \(b''\) has fixed sign and magnitude greater than \(n^2/4\), while \(b'\) stays in an interval of length \(n/2\), giving arc length at most \(2/n\).
Each arc of either length and each threshold point meets only a bounded number of mesh periods, including both closed neighbors at an endpoint.
Hence only \(O(d)\) periods are flagged.

A remaining period stays entirely in \(|b|>3\), where \(|f|>1\), or entirely in the region where both derivative bounds hold.
In the latter case write \(x=nt+\phi\) and split at \(|\sin x|=1/2\).
On the pieces with large sine the difference has derivative of fixed sign and magnitude at least \(3/4\).
On each complementary piece its second derivative has fixed sign and magnitude at least \(\sqrt3-1/4>1\).
There the portion with \(|f'|\le\sqrt s\), using the \(x\)-derivative, has length at most \(2\sqrt s\).
On each of the two remaining monotone portions, \(f\) can spend at most \(2\sqrt s\) in \([-s,s]\).
These bounds control the relative sublevel measure on a full unflagged period by \(O(\sqrt s)\).
Across a half-period the fast summand changes by four and the slow summand by at most \(\pi/4\), so \(\sup |f|\ge(4-\pi/4)/2>1\).

Appending the next unflagged period to each cyclic run of flagged periods gives a cover of the flagged set with total length at most twice the flagged length.
Every enlarged interval contains a point where \(|f|\ge1\).
If all periods are flagged, \(n=O(d)\) and the full period of length \(2\pi\) has the same supremum bound from the Fourier coefficient of modulus one at \(n\).
On each lifted interval \(J\), the \(K\ge2\) frequencies are distinct and purely imaginary in the exponential-polynomial convention.
The exponential factor in the measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality is therefore one.
For \(E_s=\{t\in J:|f(t)|\le s\}\) of positive measure it gives \(|E_s|/|J|\le A s^{1/(K-1)}\) with absolute \(A\).
The same estimate holds trivially when \(|E_s|=0\).
Integrating this bound against \(ds/s\) from zero to \(u\) gives at most \(A(K-1)u^{1/(K-1)}|J|\).
This layer integral computes the clipping loss even when a zero has multiplicity greater than one.
The enlarged runs cost \(O(dK/n)\) and the unflagged periods cost \(O(\sqrt u)\).
Taking \(u=(d/n)^2\) yields (1), since \(\log(6n^2/d^2)=O(\log(2n/d))\).
When the derivative hypotheses hold globally the unflagged-period estimate applies directly to every period, and \(x\log(2/x)\to0\) for \(x=d/n\to0\).
Constant \(b\) gives equal means by the measure-preserving map \(t\mapsto nt+\phi\).

### Sharpness, amplitude and integral examples

The Taylor lower bound in v314 gives \(\liminf(I-\mu)>0\) at \(d=\lfloor5\sqrt n\rfloor\).
When \(d/\sqrt n\to\infty\), its positive quadratic term dominates the bounded subtraction and gives \(I-\mu\gg d^2/n\).
The actual total-measure asymptotic \(2d\log(n/d)\) applies for \(d=o(n)\), in particular at the displayed square-root width.
For general coefficients, \(K\le2d+3\) makes the upper bound tend to zero throughout \(d=o(\sqrt n)\).

The sharp Bernstein inequality applies first to \(b\) and then to \(b'\), both of degree at most \(d\), so that \(d\|b\|_\infty\le n/4\) bounds \(\|b'\|_\infty\le n/4\) and \(\|b''\|_\infty\le dn/4<n^2/4\).
The alternate Cauchy bound follows on both sides of the unit circle by applying the maximum principle to \(z^db\) and its reciprocal polynomial.
The radius \(1/d\) in the complex \(t\)-plane gives factors \(ed\) and \(2ed^2\) for the two derivatives.

With integral \(B\), the Laurent polynomial \(J(z)=-B(z+z^{-1})\) is symmetric and integral, and \(I=m(Y+Y^{-1}+J)\).
The fiber quadratic gives the displayed \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) formula, and \(|z^n|=1\) on the circle gives \(\mu=\log M(P)\).
In each constant zero case the two roots of \(w^2-Bw+1\) are roots of unity, including the double roots for \(B=\pm2\).
For \(B=\pm C_j\), \(1\le j\le d<n\), direct multiplication gives the indicated signed binomials of degrees \(n-j\) and \(n+j\).
Thus the positive branch \(I\ge c_2\) of v156 applies to every noncyclotomic input.
For \(d\le c\sqrt n\) the term \(dK/n\) is at most \(2c^2+3c/\sqrt n\) and \(d\log(2n/d)/n\le c\log(2n)/\sqrt n\).
Choosing \(c\) first and \(n\) second gives \(\mu\ge c_2/2\), and the same eventual floor follows directly in the derivative-controlled \(d=o(n)\) range.

In the sign-polynomial recurrence the two coefficient blocks are disjoint and \(|A_j|^2+|B_j|^2=2^{j+1}\) on the circle by the parallelogram identity.
The resulting \(b(t)\) is a sum of \(d\) distinct signed trace pairs at frequencies \(1,\ldots,d\), so for \(d\ge2\) it lies outside all the zero cases of v156.
Its sup norm is at most \(2\sqrt{2d}\), and \(n\ge16e\sqrt2\,d^{3/2}\) ensures the required amplitude bound and \(d/n\to0\).
Taking the least integer above that lower bound realizes \(d\asymp n^{2/3}\).
