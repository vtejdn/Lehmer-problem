# Primitive actions on minimal blocks of conjugate labels

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T15:58 UTC I independently reviewed for Yin the complete [C345 v268 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v268.md#decision-primitive-standard-actions-submitted).
I used the fixed submission, its mathematical dependencies and the cited journal originals without participating in development of this result.
The proof establishes both stated alternatives as necessary conditions, uniformly over minimal blocks at bounded logarithmic measure and sufficiently large compressed degree.

### Sparse graphs on a block

For an arbitrary block \(B\), C345 v266 gives one pair \(a,b_0\) whose pointwise stabilizer has every other orbit of size at least \(c_M d/\log^{K}(3d)\).
Transitivity makes the valency of an invariant simple graph constant.
With valency at least two, the neighborhood of \(a\) contains a label \(z\ne a,b_0\) and contains the entire orbit of \(z\) under the pair stabilizer.
A graph of valency one partitions \(B\) into invariant matching edges.
Each edge is a block for the whole group because translates of \(B\) are equal or disjoint.
Even when \(B\) is itself an edge, the uniform lower bound of C345 v264 on every nontrivial block size excludes it.
Since \(b\le d\), an upper bound \(O(b^{1-\epsilon}\log^C(2b))\) with fixed \(\epsilon>0,C\) contradicts the lower bound as \(d\) grows.

### Normal subgroups and the degree of the diagonal graph

The action induced on a minimal nontrivial block is faithful and primitive.
The radical result in C345 v267 applies to this block, so every minimal normal subgroup \(N\) is nonabelian and transitive.
Its minimal normal subgroups in one \(H\)-conjugacy orbit generate \(N\) by its minimality under \(H\).
Distinct ones commute, each has trivial center, and intersection with the product of the others is therefore trivial.
Normal subgroups of an individual direct factor are normal in the product, so each factor is simple.
The factors \(T_i\) are conjugate under the point stabilizer \(H_x\) because \(N\) normalizes each factor and \(H=NH_x\).

For \(L=N_x\), the product \(P\) of the projections of \(L\) contains \(L\).
If the projections are proper, \(H_x\) normalizes \(P\) and \(PH_x\cap N=P\) because \(H_x\cap N=L\le P\).
Thus \(PH_x<H\), and maximality of \(H_x\) gives \(P\le H_x\), hence \(P=L\).
The point set \(N/L\) is consequently the full product of the coordinate coset spaces, all of equal size, and \(H_x\) permutes the coordinates through the factor isomorphisms.
Translations by \(N\) act separately within the coordinates.
This meets the Cartesian exclusion of C345 v266 whenever \(k\ge2\), including \(L=1\).

If all projections are surjective, successively forgetting the last factor gives a full or trivial kernel in that factor.
In the latter case the homomorphism to the last simple group maps exactly one earlier diagonal factor isomorphically onto it, as the images of the distinct factors are commuting normal subgroups.
An independent singleton factor in \(L\) would force all \(T_i\le L\) by \(H_x\)-transitivity, impossible in a nontrivial action.
There are at most \(k/2\) diagonal factors of \(L\).
For \(t=|T_i|\) this gives \(b\ge t^{k/2}\).
The graph formed by all nontrivial one-coordinate moves is invariant under \(H\), since \(N\) is normal and \(H\) permutes the \(T_i\).
Inverses provide the reversed edges, and the graph has an edge since the \(T_i\) generate a transitive group on more than one point.
For \(k\ge3\), its valency is at most \(k(t-1)\le 2\log_2(b)b^{2/3}\), which is excluded by the graph argument.

For \(k=2\) in the subdirect case there is a single diagonal and \(t=b\).
A permutation commuting with transitive \(N\) is determined by its image at \(x\).
The kernel of the map into \(\operatorname{Aut}(N)\) thus has order at most \(b\).
For a product of two isomorphic nonabelian simple factors an automorphism permutes the factors and acts by isomorphisms on each.
The generating-pair corollary of GK00, applied to \(T_i\) itself, gives \(|\operatorname{Aut}(T_i)|\le t^2\) by evaluation on the two generators.
This proves the asserted \(2b^5\) bound, and gives \(b^3\) when \(k=1\) and \(N\) is regular.
C345 v264 gives \(\log|H|/\log b\gg V(d)\to\infty\) uniformly, excluding both.
In the remaining simple nonregular case any nontrivial \(C_H(N)\) would be normal and transitive.
An element of \(N_x\) commuting with a transitive group fixes every point, contradicting faithfulness and nonregularity.
Conjugation therefore embeds \(H\) in \(\operatorname{Aut}(N)\) with \(N\) acting as its inner automorphism group.

### Almost simple actions and equal-sized partitions

I checked the definition of subspace action and Theorem 1.3 on printed pages 497–500 of LS99, and Theorem I with its generating-pair corollary on printed pages 744–745 of GK00, at published-statement scope.
The three possibilities of Theorem 1.3 are the alternating or symmetric subset or partition action, the classical subspace action, and an absolutely bounded base size.
The third gives a polynomial order bound by the orbit of an ordered base.
The classical definition covers the specified proper single subspaces, the graph-automorphism configurations for the linear groups, and the two orthogonal stabilizers in even-characteristic symplectic groups.
The accidental alternating and classical isomorphisms concern only bounded alternating degrees.
For subsets of size \(j\), taking complements covers \(j>n/2\), and C345 v266 excludes the nontrivial subset sizes between two and \(n/2\).

On partitions into \(a\) equal parts of size \(s\ge2\), the transposition graph is invariant even for the alternating action, since conjugating a transposition by an even permutation yields another transposition.
A transposition across different parts changes the partition, so the graph is nonempty and its valency is at most \(\binom{n}{2}\).
For a matched pair of parts, selecting one of the two matched letters at each of their \(s\) positions determines a new first part and its complementary second part.
Precisely complementary selections are identified by forgetting their order.
Disjoint original pairs of parts have disjoint unions, so their \(2^{s-1}\) choices multiply.
For \(a,s\ge2\) one has \(\lfloor a/2\rfloor\ge a/3\) and \(s-1\ge s/2\), giving \(b\ge2^{as/6}\) uniformly.
Thus the graph valency is \(O(\log^2(2b))\), also excluded.
The singleton and complementary singleton subsets give the natural action, while the degenerate partitions have degree one.

The bounded compressed degree in the excluded cases leaves finitely many primitive polynomials because all coefficients have absolute value at most \(2^d e^M\).
Compression preserves the original measure, and the same finite set of positive values at every bounded degree forces \(d\to\infty\) along a sequence of positive measures tending to zero.
I found no defect in the submitted scope.
