## The coset estimate retains the ramification weight and the compressed degree

At 2026-11-24T23:38 UTC an agent outside the development of [C0901 v77](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v77.md#statement) independently reviewed the complete fixed mathematical text for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and the requested scope without its proof-development discussion.
The finite coset inequality, its uniform consequence for individual lifts and its class-size interpretation hold at the stated scopes.

Distinct conjugate classes form an orbit on a positive-radius sphere for an invariant inner product.
Each chosen point is an exposed vertex even after restricting the orbit to the entries lying in \(F\).
The initial affine simplex has distinct sums of \(s\)-subsets because a collision would give a nonzero balanced affine relation.
Each additional point lies beyond a facet of the preceding full-dimensional hull.
Taking it together with \(s-1\) distinct members of an affine basis of that facet gives \(\binom r{s-1}\) new averages beyond the same facet, distinct by affine independence.
This proves the stated \(D_s\) including \(s=1\); its lower bound follows from \(\binom{r+1}s=(r+1)\binom r{s-1}/s\).
The corresponding products are actual units of \(F\), with height at most \(s m/d\).

The classes of the determinant nodes are \((I-qg^{-1})[u_j]\); the kernel is zero since \(g\) has finite order and \(q>1\).
If a right coset hits the specified inertia coset above \(P\), an element \(x\) of the intersection satisfies \(x^{-1}u_j=g^{-1}u_j\).
At \(v=P\cap F\) the Teichmüller lift \(\zeta_j\) exists in the original completion, so \(v_p(u_j-\zeta_j)\ge1/e_v\).
The lift \(x\) acts as \(q\)th powering on prime-to-\(p\) Teichmüller representatives.
In particular \((x^{-1}\zeta_j)^q=\zeta_j\), and taking integral powers of local units does not reduce their congruence valuation.
Dividing by a local unit consequently gives \(v_p(z_j-1)\ge1/e_v\), independent of ramification in the compositum.
This argument works at \(p=2\) and for every \(f\ge1\); for residue field of order two the sole Teichmüller representative is one.

At a hit the conditional probability is \(|I_P\cap H|/|I_P|\).
Indeed the right cosets are \(I_Px\) and \(Hx\), and the relative inertia group has order \(e(L_P/F_v)\).
The local-degree weights of a normal field are constant on primes above \(p\) and sum to one there.
Thus averaging the hit contribution \(1/e_v\) gives exactly the law claimed for \(X\in Hg\).
Every node difference is integral everywhere and gains the indicated valuation at every hit.
The nonzero Vandermonde determinant therefore pays \(\binom{D_s}{2}\Pr(X\in Hg)\log p\) at the finite places.
The Hadamard factor \(D_s^{D_s/2}\) and row rescalings yield after division by \(\binom{D_s}{2}\) exactly \(\log D_s/(D_s-1)+2s(q+1)m/d\).
If the chosen entries generate \(F\), agreement of the inverses on them amounts precisely to the right coset.

For the global bound take \(F=L\) and one actual representative of each of the \(n\) classes.
Equal fiber sizes and the unit norm make their sum of classes zero, so their affine dimension is the full multiplicative rank \(b\).
The action on the class lattice gives \(n\le3^{b^2}\), while the product in each torsion class gives an actual unit with degree \(n\) and measure \(m\).
No degree-one nontorsion rational unit exists.
Finiteness in bounded degree and at bounded measure forces \(n\to\infty\) if \(m\to0^+\).
Taking four factors then gives \(D_4\gg n(\log n)^{3/2}\) and \(D_4\le n^4\) once \(b\ge4\).
The normalized determinant error tends to zero and the remaining term is \(O((q+1)m)\), proving the fixed-\(p,f\) consequence without a separate numerical height lower bound.

For \(b\ge20\), \(D_{11}\ge n(b/2)^{10}/(11\cdot10!)\) and \(D_{11}\le n^{11}\) give exactly the displayed \(C_0/(\log n)^4\) estimate after multiplication by \(n\).
Voutier's Corollary 2 applies to the compressed unit itself.
Using \(\log(3n)\le2\log n\) in this rank range gives \(C_0/(\log n)^4<4C_0m/\log n\).
This tends below \(m\) at an effective degree threshold, so \(23\) works there.
The elementary inequalities \(4C_0<2\cdot10^{14}\) and \(n\le3^{361}\) for \(b<20\), with \(s=1\) and Voutier at \(n\ge2\), verify the global \(10^{15}\).
The bounds are uniform in \(p,f,g\), and \(n<d\) would give the same measure in a lower degree, contradicting first attainment at \(d\).
Finally the prime-average law is uniform conjugation of a uniformly chosen element of one fixed inertia coset; its mass at \(g\) is the intersection fraction divided by the conjugacy class size.
The shrinking-sequence and gap implications follow directly, with unramifiedness required only for the stated singleton-coset formulation.
