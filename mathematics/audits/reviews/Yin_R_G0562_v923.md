For any line, all binary weights from zero to \(L\) fit in the same embedding boxes with \(R_\sigma=L\max(1,|\sigma\alpha|)^{L-1}\).
The diameter in a complex coordinate is at most \(\sqrt{2}/2<1\), so a nonzero difference in one box would have a nonzero integral norm of absolute value less than one.
Counting real coordinates and both components of each conjugate pair gives the stated exponent \((L-1)m+d\log(8L)\).

Evaluation on a line has rational rank \(\min(d,L)\) by minimality of \(P\).
In a basis adapted to the evaluation kernel \(U\), zero records in all directions force every tensor factor of each surviving basis word to lie in \(U\).
The resulting space has dimension \(D=(L-d)_+^J\).
A rank-\(D\) minor of its coordinate matrix gives an injective projection of every affine translate onto \(D\) positions, including the singleton case \(D=0\).
Restricting the mask preserves the bound \(2^D\).
At positive rate \(c\), this forces \(L>d\) and \((c/\log2)^{1/J}\le1-d/L\).
The estimate \(1-e^{-x}\le x\) gives the second bound with \(x=J^{-1}\log((\log2)/c)>0\).

A nonzero last-direction line of a signed difference meets at least \(w_L\) distinct nonzero slices, each obeying all the remaining line equations.
Iteration gives \(w_L^J\) differing entries, and the absence of any ternary relation rules out every nonzero difference.
The tensor power of a minimizing ternary relation attains equality on the full grid because its positive and negative parts are admissible binary arrays and all its line records vanish.
For a fixed digit \(j\), each flattened line contributes a monomial times \(T(X^{L^j})\) with \(P\mid T\) over the integers by monic division.
The divisibility persists upon summing all such lines, and uniqueness of radix expansions prevents cancellation between grid positions.

For a balanced flattening of shape \(a\times b\), choosing \(t\) basis rows at rank \(t\) costs at most \(\binom at 3^{tb}\) possibilities.
Those rows determine pivot columns where restriction of the row space is injective, so each remaining row has at most \(3^t\) possibilities.
Summation through rank \(R\) and a union bound over all groupings give \(2^{J+a}3^{R(a+b)}\) bad differences.
As \(a\le b\), the second factor costs at most \(\kappa ab/8\) in the logarithm for the chosen \(R\).
For fixed \(J\ge2\), \((J+a)/(ab)\to0\), so the total cost is at most \(\kappa ab/4\) for sufficiently large \(L\).
Differences from a selected array identify their other endpoint uniquely, so deleting all its bad differences discards at most \(\exp(\kappa ab/4)\) arrays, including the selected array.
Division of the initial size by the deletion bound leaves at least \(\exp(\kappa ab/2)\) selections.
Their integral ranks exceed \(\lfloor\kappa a/(16\log3)\rfloor\) in every grouping, and each product of one-variable arrays has matrix rank at most one.
