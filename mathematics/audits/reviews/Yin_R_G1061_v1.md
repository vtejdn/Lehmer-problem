### 1. Scope, finite coefficients and actual diagonal finiteness

Finite positive successor sets imply that only finitely many paths of any fixed length start at a specified state, even without a uniform degree bound.
The assumed finite entrance and exit sets make A finite; finite outgoing degree alone would not establish the exit-set condition in the general theorem.
For the quartic, each state also has at most three predecessors because the three possible predecessors are (y-e)/beta.
All path probabilities remain those of the original substochastic kernel.

For a length-n digit word, beta>1 makes beta^n-1 nonzero and fixes at most one starting point x(w).
Requiring the complete cyclic orbit to lie in D minus K excludes nonintegral fixed points and every removed state.
Summing over the 3^n original words therefore gives exactly the displayed d_n, with 0<=d_n<=1.
This proves finite coefficients and only the stated trivial bound; it does not prove alpha<=1/beta.

### 2. Complete actual nonclosure arithmetic, BC1--BC4

The bound beta>17/10 gives beta^2-beta-1>0, and beta<18/10<2 gives 0<b<beta^(-1)<a<1.
Reducing by the quartic gives beta*a-1=b and beta*b-1=-beta^(-1).

The complete predecessors of 1 are 0, beta^(-1) and 2 beta^(-1) algebraically.

In BC2 the six exterior edges have weight (1/4)^5(1/2)=1/2048, and the two additional 1/4 entrance and exit edges give 1/32768 at chronological length 8.
