### Which discriminant products can become squares

Irreducibility of \(P\) forces irreducibility of its monic integral trace polynomial \(f\), since any proper factorization of \(f\) would lift to a proper factorization of \(P\).
The two integers \(f(2),f(-2)\) have absolute value one, by \(P(1)=f(2)\) and \(P(-1)=(-1)^nf(-2)\).
Their residues modulo four agree, hence so do their signs.
Their product is \(\prod_i(y_i^2-4)=1\), making each \(\delta_i=y_i^2-4\) a unit and the number \(n-s\) of negative factors even.
The nonvanishing also excludes traces at the two endpoints.

If \(s=1\), transitivity realizes every possible position of the unique positive sign and forces no group hypothesis beyond irreducibility.
If \(0<s<n\), \(n\ge3\), and the group contains \(A_n\), all \((n-s)\)-subsets occur as the negative set.
Indeed, either that set or its complement contains two letters whose interchange corrects the parity of any permutation of the labels carrying one set to another.
For a nonconstant exponent vector \(w\), choose \(u,v\) with different coordinates.
There are \(n-2\) other letters, from which \(n-s-1\) can be chosen.
Adjoining \(u\) or \(v\) to this fixed set gives opposite parities of intersection with the support of \(w\).
Thus every nonempty proper discriminant product has both signs, even when \(s>1\), whereas the empty and full products equal one.
For any field generated by the selected traces, the embeddings giving opposite signs restrict to embeddings on that field and extend to embeddings of each larger totally real \(E\) in the statement.
Total reality ensures these extensions are real.

Writing \(M=E(\sqrt h)\), total imaginarity says that \(\tau(h)<0\) at every real embedding of \(E\).
In characteristic zero, \((u+v\sqrt h)^2\in E\) forces \(uv=0\).
Hence the only classes of \(E^\times/E^{\times2}\) killed in \(M^\times/M^{\times2}\) are \(1\) and \([h]\).
Their representatives are respectively totally positive and totally negative.
Every nonempty product among the selected \(j\le n-1\) classes retains both signs in \(E\), so none is killed.
The multiquadratic correspondence now gives degree \(2^j\) over \(M\), with elementary abelian Galois group.
This argument allows \(E\) to be nonnormal and \(M\) to ramify over \(E\) at finite primes.

### Local behavior, class groups and signatures

Over the completion at any finite prime of \(E\) or \(M\), \(X^2-y_iX+1\) is integral with unit discriminant.
Its reduction has distinct roots in a finite extension of the residue field.
Hensel lifting in the corresponding unramified local extension splits the polynomial, also in residue characteristic two.
The splitting field over \(M\) of the chosen quadratics is therefore unramified at every finite place.
At a real embedding of \(E\) a quadratic lift becomes complex exactly where \(\delta_i<0\), whereas \(M\) has no real places.

Gross and McMullen's Proposition 3.1 asserts finite unramifiedness for the original reciprocal extension, and Proposition 3.2 gives the unit-discriminant criterion for its quadratic equation.
In Section 5, equation (5.1) gives the quadratic Artin character of a principal ideal as the product of its generator's signs at the ramified real places.
Proposition 5.1 states Artin surjectivity for a finite abelian extension unramified outside the designated real places, from ideal classes modulo principal ideals with a generator positive there.
On the totally imaginary base \(M\) the list is empty, giving a quotient of the ordinary class group.
These uses agree with the author edition on [printed pages 8–9](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/papers/home/text/papers/unim/unim.pdf#page=9) and [printed pages 13–14](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/papers/home/text/papers/unim/unim.pdf#page=14).

On \(E\), the principal ideal of a unit is the identity, so its sign vector is orthogonal to each column \((1_{\tau(\delta_i)<0})_{\tau}\).
For \(E\) containing every trace, a sum of columns vanishes exactly when the corresponding discriminant product is positive at every embedding.
The mixed signs exclude every nonconstant exponent vector, and the total product gives the all-one relation.
The column rank is consequently \(n-1\), so these are \(n-1\) independent restrictions on the unit signatures.
The Artin quotient over \(M\) factors through \({\rm Cl}(M)/2{\rm Cl}(M)\), giving the asserted lower bound on ordinary 2-rank.

### Quartic directions, the Lehmer example and bounded degree

For a primitive direction with \(4\mid b\), \(a\) is odd.
The high-slope endpoint calculation gives absolute value one at both endpoints of the cyclotomic-free core, which passes to its monic rational irreducible factors by integral divisibility.
Removing cyclotomic factors is essential here since the original parent contains \(\Phi_4\), with value two at both endpoints.
A single negative exterior root giving a Salem number after the sign change has all traces real, and changing sign preserves the absolute endpoint values.
The factor degree \(d=2n\) satisfies \(d/(b/a)=1+o(1)\) in the single-exterior case along the indicated shrinking sequence.
The quotient for every totally imaginary quadratic extension of the normal trace field follows with \(j=n-1\).
The field of an ordered \(j\)-tuple of distinct traces has degree equal to its orbit size, bounded by \(n!/(n-j)!\), and the same quotient argument works over its quadratic extensions.

For the given quintic \(f\), expansion of \(X^5 f(X+X^{-1})\) reproduces the displayed degree-ten Lehmer polynomial in the cited input, and \(f(-2)=f(2)=-1\).
The signs at \(-2,-3/2,-1,0,1\) are respectively \(-,+,-,+,-\), with \(f(-3/2)=3/32\), while \(f(3)=159>0\).
There are thus four distinct roots in \((-2,2)\) and one in \((2,3)\).
Modulo two the quintic has no linear factor and its remainder modulo the only irreducible quadratic \(Y^2+Y+1\) is \(Y\), proving irreducibility.

This verifies the irreducible Salem root configuration directly.
Taking \(j=1\) over a quintic trace field forces even class number after every totally imaginary quadratic extension, and \(j=4\) over the splitting field gives a quotient of order sixteen.

If one such extension of the normal trace field has 2-rank bounded by \(B\), the lower bound gives \(2n\le2(B+1)\).
