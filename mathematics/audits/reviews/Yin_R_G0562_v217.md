Since \(P\) is monic of degree \(d=n+g\deg A\) with unit constant coefficient, multiplication by \(x\) and by \(t=x^g\) are integral automorphisms on the rank-\(d\) lattice.
The characteristic polynomial of multiplication by \(t\) consequently expresses \(t^{-1}\) and all positive powers of \(t\) in the span of \(1,t,\ldots,t^{d-1}\).
In the Laurent polynomial ring, the free summands \(X^{in}\mathbb Z[X^g,X^{-g}]\), for \(0\le i<g\), exhaust the monomials without overlap by coprimality.
Thus multiplication of \(A(T)Y-B(T)\) by this basis gives every relation from \(P\), and passage to the additive quotient by the first summand removes both constant terms in the endpoint relations.

For distinct roots, the two Vandermonde determinants give \(I_g(P)=\prod_{i<j}D_g(z_i,z_j)\), including when the powered roots collide.
Both sides are polynomials in the original roots, so the same identity holds on the diagonals.
In the ordered-pair product the diagonal contributes absolute value \(g^d\), using \(\lvert\prod z_j\rvert=1\).
Computing that product instead by evaluating \(P(\zeta z_j)\) gives exactly \(g^d\lvert\prod_j A(z_j^g)\rvert^{g-1}\), since exponentiation by \(n\) permutes the nonidentity \(g\)-th roots of unity.
Reversing the monic resultant gives \(\lvert\prod_j A(z_j^g)\rvert=\lvert\operatorname{Res}(A,B)\rvert^g\).
A root of \(A\) of multiplicity \(m\) contributes \(gm\) evaluations even at zero, and constant \(A=1\) gives a product of one.
