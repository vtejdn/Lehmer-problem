# Independent review of the logarithmic-gradient criterion and radical denominators

## Common components, individual radical degrees and the sharp example

At 2026-11-25T05:19 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v164 independently reviewed its fixed statement and four argument sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof without its development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings on v164.
The criterion for simple components, the effective finite exclusion, the denominator bound for every radical and both families follow at their stated scope.

Write \(G\) for a simple irreducible component, \(H=\bar F/G\), and take all gradients in the function field of \(G\).
There \(\nabla_{\log}\bar F=H\nabla_{\log}G\), with \(H\ne0\).
If a logarithmic derivative of \(G\) is a nonzero multiple of \(G\), the Newton polygon of the quotient translates the polygon of \(G\) into itself at every point of the quotient polygon, forcing the quotient to be constant.
If both derivatives vanish modulo \(G\), the two resulting coefficient comparisons put the entire support in one residue class modulo \(p\).
Over the perfect constant field this would make \(G\) a Laurent unit times a \(p\)-th power.
Thus the logarithmic normal is well-defined at the generic point.
Differentiating before reducing gives \(\Theta_j\overline{\delta_pF}=-(\Theta_j\bar F)^p\) on \(G\).
If \(G\mid\overline{\delta_p F}\), the left-hand gradient is proportional to the gradient of \(G\).
Consequently the ratio of its two normal coordinates, in a chart with nonzero denominator, is fixed by Frobenius and belongs to \(\mathbb F_p\).
A perpendicular vector over \(\mathbb F_p\) gives a logarithmic derivative divisible by \(G\), and comparison with its constant multiple of \(G\) puts the support on the asserted affine line.
The same proof applies to the signed polynomial itself, including in characteristic two.

An integer triangle in the support of a nonbinomial geometric component has nonzero determinant divisible by \(p\) when that support is collinear modulo \(p\).
Its area is bounded by the parent area through the Newton polygon sum.
In characteristic zero the monomial-cleared squarefree polynomial over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(Y)\) is coprime to \(X\partial_XF\), since it has no factor \(X\) and characteristic zero makes the factors of positive \(X\)-degree separable.
A nonzero resultant coefficient survives away from finitely many primes and excludes repeated factors of positive \(X\)-degree there.
Factors of \(X\)-degree zero are geometric binomials.
A binomial in any direction contributes a segment summand to the polygon and hence an edge parallel to that segment.
For every such primitive direction the row polynomials over \(\mathbb Q[z^{\pm1}]\) have no common nonzero root, so a denominator-cleared Bézout identity excludes all such components away from the primes dividing its nonzero integer right-hand side.
Keeping the vertices and excluding the primes up to twice the area proves the effective finite assertion.
For a full-rank lattice substitution, the coset monomials give a finite free module over the image Laurent ring.
Quotienting by the two pulled-back equations therefore preserves finite length over the ground field, including when the lattice index is divisible by \(p\).

If \(\beta=\rho^p\) with \(\rho\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\), then \(\mathbb Q(\rho)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and the distinct conjugates of \(\rho\) have distinct \(p\)-th powers.
The product of the rotations of its primitive minimum \(Q\) has the roots of \(S(Z^p)\) with matching multiplicities.
Its coefficients are rational algebraic integers, and reduction at any prime ideal above any rational prime leaves a product of nonzero polynomials.
Gauss's lemma therefore gives equality up to sign with the primitive \(S(Z^p)\), and its endpoint absolute values are the \(p\)-th powers of those of \(Q\).
At \(1-\zeta_p\) the product reduces to \(\bar Q(Z)^p\), while the other side becomes \(\bar S(Z)^p\), since the leading sign is positive for odd \(p\).
Injectivity of Frobenius gives \(\bar Q=\bar S\).
The product with the unrotated factor omitted is also integral rational and reduces to \(\bar S^{p-1}\).
Writing these two integral factors as \(S+pU\) and \(S^{p-1}+pV\), the terms surviving in their defect modulo \(p\) are multiples of \(\bar S\), since \(p-1\ge1\) and \((1-\varepsilon)/p\) is integral.
This gives divisibility with the full multiplicities of \(\bar S\), even for a nonmonic minimum.

The product formula for the polynomial defect passes this divisibility to any integral Laurent multiple of \(S\).
The coprime signed specialization of the parent gives a surjection from the two-equation Laurent quotient to \(\mathbb F_p[Z^{\pm1}]/(\bar S)\), since a Bézout relation for \(a,b\) produces \(Z\) in the image.
After removing boundary monomial factors, plane Bézout bounds the length by \(p d_F^2\), while the target dimension is exactly the difference of its surviving extreme exponents, including zero for a unit Laurent monomial.
Outside the finitely many primitive tie directions the endpoint coefficients of the parent have absolute value \(q\), by the equality of endpoint moduli along every edge.
Both endpoints of \(S\) divide the corresponding parent endpoints.
Their valuations are multiples of \(p\), so \(v_p(q)<p\) ensures that both survive.
They also survive for an algebraic unit with no restriction on \(q\).
The fixed edge degree bounds then contradict the length estimate for sufficiently large primitive directions, uniformly over the finitely many tested primes and signs.
The unit version uses only the modulus-one hypothesis, and the unrestricted version uses torsion edge roots.

Clearing a Laurent monomial gives a nonzero integral relation of length at most \(L\).
The bounded denominator theorem excludes in-field \(p\)-th roots for \(p\nmid R_L\), and preserves its full valuation bound at the retained primes.
The signed family also supplies a relation and the same degree estimates for \(-\beta\), by the substitution \(Z\mapsto-Z\).
Thus the additional fourth-power obstruction at a nonexceptional dyadic prime is excluded because \(\beta=-4u^4\) would give \(-\beta=(2u^2)^2\).
For each nonexceptional \(p^j\Vert g\), Capelli's criterion gives degree \(p^j\) for \(\alpha^{g/p^j}\) over \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
The tower law forces \(p^j\mid e\), and the remaining denominator \(g/\gcd(g,e)\) divides both \(g\) and the part of \(R_L\) supported on the retained primes.
Multiplying the height identity by the absolute field degrees gives \(m(\alpha)/m(\beta)=e/g\).
When \(g\) is coprime to \(R_{\rm exc}\), the relative degree equals \(g\), so the degree of the minimum of \(\alpha\) equals that of \(S(Z^g)\).
The earlier edge and denominator estimates were used at their reviewed interfaces, and the specialization argument was rechecked here.
The number-field binomial criterion was checked at the statement level in Jones and Phillips, Theorem 2.2, on journal page 48. [Published text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/9B22D4A35CB8C35FCE9EBB8BEEF4A38F/S0004972717000648a.pdf/an-infinite-family-of-ninth-degree-dihedral-polynomials.pdf#page=2)

For the reciprocal family, a rational root of \(Y^2-PY+1\) and its inverse are both integral over the integrally closed Laurent ring in \(X\).
It must be \(cX^n\), whose sum with its inverse cannot have both the nonzero constant and the nonconstant term present in \(\bar P\).
The monic quadratic has no vertical component, and its irreducibility makes the geometric Laurent component simple, also in characteristic two.
The three surviving lattice points form a triangle of determinant one, while the four primitive binomial edges have endpoint moduli one.
This removes every candidate prime.
For the displayed choice of \(P\), the sign change between one and positive infinity gives a positive real unit root.
In its embedded real field torsion consists of signs, and any nonprimitive exponent on its unit line would yield an in-field prime root after choosing the positive generator.

For \(X-(1+Y)^p\) the reduced equation is monic linear in \(X\), so it cuts out a simple irreducible torus component of normalized area \(p\).
Putting \(A=(1+Y)^p\) and \(B=1+Y^p\) gives \(A\equiv B\bmod p\), hence \(A^p\equiv B^p\bmod p^2\).
Evaluation at \(X=A\) therefore annihilates the reduced defect and gives the asserted common component.
The vertical edge has the sole root \(-1\), and the other two edges are primitive binomials with unit endpoint coefficients.
For \(a>p\) coprime to \(p\), a root \(\beta>1\) exists by the sign change of \(Z^a-(1+Z)^p\).
For \(ia+jp=1\) the element \((1+\beta)^i\beta^j\) lies in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and has \(p\)-th power \(\beta\), hence relative degree one and exactly the stated factor \(1/p\) in logarithmic measure.
Such \(a\) are unbounded.
