## Independent finding on arbitrary middle gaps

At 2026-11-17T12:32 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin outside proof development reviewed the complete [C345 v231 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v231.md#decision-uncentered-five-terms-submitted).
The reviewer received the fixed Claim and the scope of the assignment without proof-generation dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or previous review conclusions on v231.
The following checks support both inequalities, the two product reductions in either orientation, and the asserted height dependence.

For the primitive integral input, the coefficient ratio of v229 satisfies \(K\le 5H\).
At sufficiently small positive measure its irreducible factor is a reciprocal monic unit up to sign, \(dtm\ge1/20\), and comparison with the leading term in both orientations gives \(wt\le L\).
In particular \(0<t\le m\le et\) by summing the positive conjugate logarithms.
Write \(h=m/d\).
Multiplication of the equations at a root and its inverse cancels the two opposite powers of \(X^D\), giving \(T_{A+cX^n}=T_B\) at that root.
The difference has exponents among \(0,\pm a,\pm n,\pm(n-a),\pm b\), so a nonzero difference gives \(\max(n,b)h\gg1\) by the bounded-support consequence of v186.
Reversing interchanges the end binomials and replaces \(n\) by \(D-n\).
If \(wh\) reaches a sufficiently small fixed positive constant, \(wh\le20Lm^2\) gives \(m\gg L^{-1/2}\), enough for both claims.
Otherwise the two nonzero differences force \(\Delta h\gg1\) for \(\Delta=\min(n,D-n)\).

For an identically zero difference, comparison of extreme coefficients gives \(n=b\) and \(A_0c=B_0B_1\).
Each of the positive interior differences \(a,n-a\) carries a nonzero summand, so equality of the traces requires \(n=2a\) and \(A_1(A_0+c)=0\).
Thus \(c=-A_0\), \(B_1=-A_0^2/B_0\), and the constant term gives \(A_1^2=(B_0+B_1)^2\).
In particular \(k=A_0/B_0\) is nonzero and \(k^2\ne1\).
Expanding (3) and dividing by \(B_0\) gives \(k+\sigma(1-k^2)X^a-kX^{2a}-k^2X^{D-2a}+X^D\), with \(D>4a\) from the original order.
The four coefficients of the bracket in increasing exponent order are \(\sigma,-k,-\sigma k,1\), which acquire the common multiplier \(\sigma\) under reversal.
In the middle-power product of v230 the four coefficients of the first factor in increasing order are \(sz,-s,1,-z\), which acquire the multiplier \(-s\) under reversal.
In either product a root of the other binomial has a positive power equal to a rational number different from \(1\) and \(-1\), which excludes algebraic units.
The reduced numerator and denominator of \(k\) have absolute values at most \(H\).
For the v230 parameter \(z\), the two bottom coefficients of the expanded product are \(-Cz,C\), giving the same bound even when the original input is reversed.
Clearing the reduced denominator therefore gives a four-term polynomial of coefficient height at most \(H\).

At \(\beta=\alpha^{-1}\), where \(|\alpha|=e^t\), the two orientations give (5) and \(\max(|A(\beta)|,|B(\beta)|)\le 11H^2e^{-\Delta t}\).
Set \(Z=\beta^f\) as in the Claim.
If \(\lambda^v\ne\mu^u\), the product of their reduced denominators is at most \(H^{u+v}\).
Comparison with \(Z^{uv}\) by factoring differences of powers costs at most \(11H^2(vH^{v-1}+uH^{u-1})e^{-\Delta t}\).
Since \(u,v\le w\), the rational lower bound gives \(\Delta t\ll wL\), including \(H=1\).
With \(wt\le L\) this yields \(\Delta t^2\ll L^2\).
If the rational powers agree, Bézout's identity \(iu+jv=1\) gives the common value \(\gamma=\lambda^i\mu^j\) with powers \(\gamma^u=\lambda,\gamma^v=\mu\), and every common root in the \(Z\) variable equals \(\gamma\).
For \(\gamma=\pm1\), the first binomial approximation has error at least \(1-e^{-at}\gg t\).
Thus \(\Delta t\ll L+\log(1/t)\), and multiplication by \(t<1\) gives \(\Delta t^2\ll L\).
In both cases \(\Delta h\gg1\) gives \(dt^2/m\ll L^2\), and \(dtm\ge1/20\) gives \(t\ll m^2 L^2\).
The inequality \(m\le et\) now yields \(emL^2\gg1\).

For a nontorsion common root, write \(\gamma=p/s\) in lowest terms with \(s>0\), and put \(Q=\max(|p|,s)\).
Then \(2\le Q\) and \(Q^{\max(u,v)}\le H\) by taking the reduced rational powers.
Writing \(A_1=q_A s^u\) gives \(A_0=-q_Ap^u\).
Division by \(sZ-p\) gives coefficients of magnitude at most \(|q_A|Q^{u-1}\le H\), including the constant-cofactor case \(u=1\).
The same reasoning applies to the second cofactor.
All three factors have degree \(O(L)\), and uniqueness and simplicity of the common root make them pairwise coprime over \(\mathbb Q\).
For any positive-degree pair \(U,V\), the coefficient matrix of \((M,N)\mapsto MU+NV\) with \(\deg M<\deg V,\deg N<\deg U\) is a Sylvester matrix of size \(O(L)\).
The adjugate applied to the constant basis vector supplies integral multipliers for its nonzero determinant.
The minors have size at most \((O(L)H)^{O(L)}\) by determinant expansion, so the two multipliers on the unit disk have size at most \(\exp(O(L^2))\).
The determinant has magnitude at least one, which gives the claimed lower bound \(\delta=\exp(-CL^2)\) on \(\max(|U|,|V|)\) throughout the disk.
A constant factor is a nonzero integer and gives the same bound directly.

If \(\Delta t\ll L^2\), then \(dt/m\ll L^2\) by \(\Delta h\gg1\), hence \(m\gg1/L\) by the radial lower bound.
Above a sufficiently large multiple of \(L^2\), both products with \(C_0\) have magnitude less than \(\delta^2/2\).
The separation of the two cofactors first gives \(|C_0|<\delta/2\), and separation of each cofactor from \(C_0\) then bounds both cofactors below by \(\delta\).
Their degrees and heights give the common upper bound \(O(LH)\).
The errors relative to the two nonzero middle terms of (5) are bounded respectively by \(10H^2e^{-(D-n)t}/|c|\) and \(10H^2e^{-nt}/|c|\), both less than \(1/2\).
Thus one may divide, cancel \(C_0\), and compare the absolute values in (5) to obtain \((D-2n)t+O(1)=\log|C_A/C_B|=O(L^2)\).
Outside the exclusions of v230 its degree estimate gives \(1\ll h(L/t+|D-2n|)\ll mL^2/(dt)\le20m^2L^2\).
This proves the stronger gap and gives both original inequalities using \(t\le m\le et\) and \(L>1\).
At bounded \(e\) one gets \(m\gg1/(eL^2)\), including \(e=1\) for a Salem factor, and the absolute smallness cutoff can be absorbed into these lower bounds since \(L\ge\log 5\).
