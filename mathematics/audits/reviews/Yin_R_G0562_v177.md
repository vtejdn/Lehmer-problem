Conversely \(u^A=(1+u)^B\) excludes \(0,-1\), so Bézout exponents \(hB+jA=1\) give \(z=u^h(1+u)^j\) with both required powers.
The derivative test in v176 applies with \(a=A,b=B\), since a multiple root would satisfy both \(|z|^{A-B}=B/A<1\) and \(|z|^B=A/(A-B)>1\).
Thus the correspondence accounts once for every root of each monic polynomial.
The roots \(u\) have product of absolute value one by the constant term of \(J\).
For a primitive cube root \(\omega\), one has \(1+\omega=-\omega^2\).
The squared modulus in (2) is therefore \(|1+\omega^{2B-A}|^2\) for odd \(B\) and \(|1-\omega^{2B-A}|^2\) for even \(B\), giving \(4,1\) and \(0,3\) respectively according as \(3\mid 2B-A\) or not.

When this value vanishes, the two common roots in the \(u\)-coordinate correspond to primitive sixth roots \(z\), since \(z\) belongs to the sixth roots and \(z^A=1+u\) has order six.
On removing these pairs, the factor \(\prod|u|\) is still one.
The product of \(|\Phi_3(u)|\) on the remaining roots is \(|J_0(\omega)|^2\), and the product of \(|\Phi_6(z)|\) there is \(|g(\zeta)|^2\).

The identities \(\omega(1+\omega)J'(\omega)=\omega^A[A(1+\omega)-B\omega]\) and \(\zeta G'(\zeta)=A(1+\omega)-B\omega\) show that the quotient of products is one.
