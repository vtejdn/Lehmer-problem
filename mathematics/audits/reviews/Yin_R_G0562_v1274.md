## Exact evaluations, the cyclotomic factors and dyadic degrees

At 2026-11-25T16:24 UTC a separately assigned agent reviewed the complete [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v195.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer had worked independently on norm constraints for the moving rational pole and received the subject and review scope for these fixed-pole examples without their development dialogue, construction details, author explanations or previous findings on the claim.
Both families, the uniform local count and the quantifiers on its subsequence hold.

For \(d=r-1\ge4\) the integer-pole identity follows by telescoping the geometric sum.
On the circle the Möbius factor has argument derivative \(3/|2-z|^2\le3<d\) and winding number one.
Consequently the argument of its quotient into \(z^d\) increases through \(2\pi(d-1)\), giving exactly \(d-1\) simple roots on the circle for \((Z-1)F\).
The sign change of \(F\) between one and two gives an exterior root, and reciprocity supplies its inverse, accounting for the remaining degree.
The [Salem factor argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v296.md#barrier) applies because this unit must have a contracting conjugate, which is uniquely the displayed inverse, and its trace in the quadratic case would lie strictly between two and \(5/2\).
For fixed \(1<x<2\), the identity is negative at \(x\) for large \(d\) and positive at two, so \(T\to2\).
Then \(0<2-T=(2T-1)T^{-d}\) decays exponentially and \((2/T)^d\to1\).
This gives the constant three as well as the exact value \(F(2)=3\).
Modulo two the polynomial is \((Z^r-1)/(Z-1)\).
Any cyclotomic cofactor would reduce to roots of order \(r\), so its integer order would be \(2^j r\), of degree at least \(d\).
A proper cofactor cannot have that degree, and the other factors would all be cyclotomic by Kronecker.
The binary polynomial is separable, and its Frobenius orbits each have length \(\operatorname{ord}_r(2)\ge\log_2(r+1)\), which proves the claimed exact zero count at the cutoff.

For the cubic, the lack of rational roots, negative discriminant and unit norm give the required nonreciprocal Pisot minimal polynomial.
The nonreal pair has modulus \(\rho^{-1/2}\), and \(P(1)<0<P(4/3)\).
The [inspected construction statement](../literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#smy15-u002) covers both signs for sufficiently large \(n\), so it applies to the minus sign here.
Its underlying original proof was not independently re-audited and no effective onset is used.
Over \(\mathbb F_2\) the two distinct reciprocal cubics have no residue roots in \(\mathbb F_2\), hence are coprime.
Neither contributes a pole at a zero of \(A_n\).
The numerator of the derivative of their quotient is \(X^4+X^2+1\).
At a primitive cubic root \(\xi\) of unity the quotient equals \(\xi\), while \(\xi^{2^k}=\xi^2\) for odd \(k\).
This excludes all possible repeated roots of the binary polynomial, so every local factor is unramified and no repeated cyclotomic factor occurs in characteristic zero.

The reduction of a cyclotomic polynomial of order \(2^a m\), \(m\) odd and \(a\ge2\), repeats each of the reductions of its odd part, since primitive roots with the same odd part differ by 2-power torsion.
Thus each characteristic-zero torsion root has the form \(\sigma y\) for \(\sigma=\pm1\) and odd-order \(y\).
Applying the square-map automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(y)\) to \(y^n=R_0(\sigma y)\) and comparing to the square of this equality gives \(R_0(\sigma y^2)=R_0(\sigma y)^2\).
There are no torsion poles, and at zero the two sides have values \(-1,1\), respectively, for either sign.
There are therefore finitely many possible odd orders, independent of the odd integer \(k\).
The specified progression forces \(2^k\equiv2\) at each such order, including the trivial order automatically, and \(2^k\equiv2\bmod5\).
Consequently the torsion roots on that progression are exactly those of \(X^2P-P^*=X^5-1\).
After their simple removal the established construction leaves the unique Salem minimal polynomial, of degree \(n-2\).
At the pole \(\rho^3=\rho+1\) gives \(\rho^4=\rho^2+\rho=\rho^5-1=-P^*(\rho)\), proving \(S(\rho)=1\).
For each fixed \(x\in(1,\rho)\) the \(x^nP(x)\) term tends to minus infinity, locating \(T\to\rho\) from below.
Since \(P'(\rho)=3\rho^2-1>0\), the defining equation and the mean value theorem give \(T^n(\rho-T)\to\rho^4/P'(\rho)\).
It follows that \(n(\rho-T)\to0\); replacing \(T^n\) by \(\rho^n\) preserves the limit and shifting the exponent from \(n\) to \(d=n-2\) divides it by \(\rho^2\).

For fixed \(k,f\), the two coincident subdivisions among the \(m+1\) multiples of \(k\) modulo \(f\), \(m=\lceil\sqrt f\rceil\), give a single choice \(1\le a\le m,\ |b|\le\lceil f/m\rceil\) shared by all the roots in \(\mathbb F_{2^f}\).
Along each such root all iterates of \(R\) are finite since they agree with Frobenius iterates.
For \(b\ge0\) compare \(R^{\circ a}\) and \(X^{2^b}\), of distinct degrees \(3^a\) and \(2^b\).
For \(b<0\) the degree of the \(2^{-b}\)-th power of \(R^{\circ a}\) exceeds one, so it differs from \(X\).
In each case denominator clearing gives at most \(1+3^a2^{|b|}+2^{|b|}\) zeros.
This is bounded by \(C\exp(C\sqrt f)\) with absolute \(C\), independently of \(k,f\).
Summing over \(f\le B\) may overcount and gives the required upper bound by squarefree lifting.
Its logarithm is \(O(\sqrt B+\log(2B))\), which yields each claimed specialization.
For every prescribed \(b\) there are arbitrarily large \(k\equiv1\bmod\operatorname{lcm}(H,1,\ldots,b)\) on the chosen progression.
In every field of degree \(f\le b\) the equation then reduces to \(z^2=R(z)\).
Its solutions reduce the already removed factor \(X^5-1\), coprime to the remaining factor modulo two, proving the growing empty-cutoff subsequence.
The fixed poles in these two families remain strictly above one, and no varying-\(q\) realization is inferred.
