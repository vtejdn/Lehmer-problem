# Independent review of modular trace factors on the full odd grid

At 2026-11-16T10:05 a reviewer for Yin independently examined the complete [G0895 v2 statement and argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v2.md#statement).
The reviewer took no part in developing v2 and received no proof-development discussion, author explanations or previous finding on v2.
The external logarithmic-form theorem was checked against the displayed statement in §5, printed pp. 5–6 of Wüstholz's [Cambridge chapter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/07999/excerpt/9780521807999_excerpt.pdf), and against the [local source unit](../literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form).
This inspection covers the source hypotheses, height definitions, degree dependence and application to the chosen logarithms.
The theorem is accepted at this statement-level scope, with the original 1993 proof outside this review.

## Finding

The two-sided uniform estimate holds with arbitrary coefficient heights and counts every root of the lift with multiplicity.
In the strict chamber it implies the stated \(149/100\) upper limit for factors modulo \(B_n\), including the supplied products with bounded individual degrees and unrestricted total degree.

For \(\zeta^p=1\), the modulus of \(F^*(\zeta)\) is \(|F(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})|\).
Each trace node occurs twice in the product over \(\zeta\ne1\).
The degree of \(F^*\) is \(2d\), and its highest and constant coefficients both equal the leading coefficient of \(F\).
For a root \(\xi\) outside the unit disk, \(|\zeta-\xi|/|\xi|=|\zeta^{-1}-1/\xi|\), and inversion permutes the sampled nodes.
The normalized product for each root, inside or outside the disk, is consequently \(H_p(w)\).
This yields the denominator \(p-1=2n\) in the exact logarithmic identity and the upper error \(2d\log p/(p-1)\), with the leading coefficient included in \(m_F\).

At the omitted node \(w=1\) one has \(H_p(w)=p\).
A torsion root of order \(q>1\) has \(q\nmid p\), so \(|1-w^p|\ge2\sin(\pi/q)\ge4/q\) and \(|1-w|\le2\).
At a prime power \(\ell^a\) the ratio \(\varphi(\ell^a)^2/\ell^a\) equals \(\ell^{a-2}(\ell-1)^2\).
It is at least one for odd \(\ell\) and at least \(1/2\) for \(\ell=2\), proving \(q\le2\varphi(q)^2\).
Thus \(\log H_p(w)\ge-2\log \varphi(q)\) for each such torsion root, including \(q=2\).

For a nontorsion \(w\) with \(|w|^p\le1/2\), the triangle inequality gives \(H_p(w)\ge1/4\).
In the remaining case \(p\log|w|\in[-\log2,0]\).
The choice of the nearest multiple of \(2\pi\) gives a linear form \(\Lambda=p\log w-2k i\pi\) with \(|2k|\le p+1\), \(|\Im\Lambda|\le\pi\) and \(e^\Lambda=w^p\ne1\).
Wüstholz allows both \(w\) and \(-1\) as arguments, arbitrary fixed determinations of their logarithms and any integer coefficient vector different from zero, including \(k=0\).
The field is precisely \(\mathbb Q(w)\), of degree \(D\).
The field-relative height of \(w\) is \(\log M(S_w)=m_w\) even for a nonmonic primitive minimal polynomial.
The two modified heights are \(O((1+m_w)/D)\) and \(\pi/D\), since \(|\log w|\le\pi+\log 2\).
Dividing the coefficient vector by its greatest common divisor only decreases its maximum, so the modified form height is \(O(\log(2p))\).
Multiplying these estimates by \(C(2,D)=O(D^4\log(2D))\) gives precisely the asserted \(D^2\log(2D)\log(2p)(1+m_w)\) loss.
On the stated rectangle \(|1-e^{u+iv}|^2\) bounds a positive absolute multiple of \(u^2+v^2\), since \(e^u\ge1/2\) and \(\sin(|v|/2)\ge |v|/\pi\).
The resulting lower bound for \(|1-w^p|/|1-w|\) loses at most a further absolute factor.

Inversion preserves the Mahler measure of the primitive irreducible polynomial because reversal exchanges its leading and constant coefficients and replaces every nonzero root by its inverse.
Hence \(D\le2d\) and \(m_w\le m_F\) follow from integer factorization, with every complementary factor of measure at least one.
The sum over all \(2d\) roots accounts for the third factor of \(d\).
Replacing \(1/(p-1)\) by \(O(1/p)\) is uniform over all the specified \(p\), and a constant polynomial has the stated exact sampled norm.

For the modular conclusion the [odd-grid identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product) gives \(|B_{n-1}(b_j)|^2=2+b_j\) and \(\prod_j(2+b_j)=|B_n(-2)|=1\).
The [trace-coordinate congruence](../../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#original-domain) then identifies \(\Delta\) with the absolute resultant of \(h\).
Strict signs exclude zeros of \(h\), \(F\) and \(G\) at every node.
Evaluation of \(h\equiv FG\bmod B_n\) gives \(\Delta=\Delta_F\Delta_G\), with both factors positive integers because \(B_n\) is monic integral.
This works across the entire grid for composite as well as prime conductors.
If \(\varepsilon_p\) denotes the coefficient of \(1+m_F\) in the lower error, the resulting inequality is \((1-\varepsilon_p)m_F\le n^{-1}\log\Delta+\varepsilon_p\).
Since \(\varepsilon_p\to0\), the [adopted resultant ceiling](../../claims/Yin_C_G0893_v1.md#decision-normalized-residue-tests-reviewed) gives the required upper limit without any prior bound on \(m_F\).

For a supplied factor with positive logarithmic measure, its lift either has a nonunit leading coefficient or contains a monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor up to sign.
In the latter case that irreducible factor has degree at most \(2p\), so the [existing degree-dependent estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#transfer) gives a cost at least \(c/(\log p)^3\).
This reasoning also applies to reducible supplied factors, and a nonunit integer constant already costs at least \(\log2\).
If the full measure exceeds \(149/100+\delta\), the factors preceding the first crossing of \(149/100+\delta/2\) have bounded total logarithmic measure.
Including the crossing factor therefore requires at most \(O_\delta((\log p)^3)\) factors even if its measure is very large.
Their total degree is \(O_\delta(p^{1/3-\epsilon}(\log p)^3)\), so its cube times \(\log(2d)\log(2p)\) is \(o(p)\) whenever that degree is positive.
The case of degree zero uses the exact constant-factor identity.
The omitted factors multiply the given modular cofactor, proving modular divisibility of the subproduct and contradicting an infinite sequence of such crossings.
The aggregate assertion requires modular divisibility of the supplied product; individual divisibilities alone do not give this hypothesis.
The earlier wider degree range and transition-root conclusions retain their [adopted scope for ordinary polynomial divisors](../../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#decision-trace-factor-transfer-reviewed).
Existence of a shrinking sequence in the chamber remains open.
