# Ramified matrix congruences and the prior bound for ordinary radicals

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review and prior coverage

At 2026-11-21T05:09 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin completed an independent review of [C345 v766](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v766.md#statement).
The reviewer took no part in developing this claim and recommends mathematical acceptance, with the prior coverage below.
The distinct proof ingredients are the ramified coefficient congruence and its use with a variable root-of-unity multiplier.

For any of the stated fixed graph units there is an integer relation of coefficient length at most \(L_R\).
The existing [bounded-denominator theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) thus gives \(r\mid R_{L_R}\) on every ordinary radical branch, irrespective of \(W_0\).
It gives \(t\ge g/R_{L_R}\) and \(m(\alpha)\ge m(\beta)/R_{L_R}\).
Taking \(H_R=R_{L_R}\) already gives the two divisibilities and square-root bound in (1), since \(d_0\mid t\) and \(g=r d_0\).
For (2), the cubic height inequality over \(\mathbb Q\) gives \(m(\beta)\gg G(d)\), while \(J(d)\) is bounded below by a positive absolute constant.
These facts and the earlier fixed denominator imply the displayed bounds involving \(m(\beta),G(d),J(d)\).
The uniform upper bound on \(m(\beta)\) follows at once by Mahler measure of the bounded-length relation.
Thus all stated bounds for ordinary radicals were already available, including a stronger linear lower bound on their degrees.

For the direct proof, if the translated test vanished at \(\gamma/\zeta\), that value would be a nontorsion unit of height \(h(\beta)/p\) on a coefficient conjugate of the original graph.
The [fixed-graph height limit](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#height) gives the same strictly positive limiting value for \(v h(\beta)\) and \(v h(\gamma/\zeta)\) on any unbounded slope sequence exhibiting this failure.
The factor at least two between them excludes the failure uniformly even with unbounded torsion orders and varying primes.

In \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{p^c})\) with \(c\ge1\), Eisenstein after translation gives the unique prime over \(p\), residue field \(\mathbb F_p\) and ramification index \(p^{c-1}(p-1)\).
For the valuation with \(w(p)=1\), the first lift \(x^p-x\) has value at least the reciprocal ramification index.
The binomial bound \(\min(pb,1+b)\) multiplies that lower bound by \(p\) until \(1/(p-1)\), then increases it by one per step.
Thus the successive scalar powers indexed by \(\ell,\ell-1\) differ by a multiple of \(p^{\max(0,\ell-c)}\) in the full integer ring.
This includes \(p=2,c=1\), and the rational integer congruence gives the sharper case \(c=0\).
For a trace of a matrix power, cyclic orbits of based words of least period \(p^j\) supply \(p^j\) copies of the repeated edge-weight product.
The matched words in the shorter trace have identical edge weights, giving divisibility by \(p^{j+\max(0,a-j-c)}\).
Words first occurring at length \(p^a\) contribute multiples of \(p^a\).
Exterior-power traces then give the congruence for every coefficient of the characteristic polynomial.
These arguments work for matrices of arbitrary finite size over the stated ring.

The norm and Bézout argument has its root of unity in the original field \(F\) and its order divides \(W_0\).
Removing its prime-to-\(p\) part is permitted because powering by \(p^a\) is invertible on that cyclic subgroup.
The three elements \(z=\zeta\beta,\gamma,\theta_0\) generate \(F\) over \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta)\) by their successive powering relations.
Hence the relative companion matrix gives the two minimal polynomials themselves, with no lost multiplicities.
Monic division of the translated integral polynomial and evaluation at \(\gamma\) produce the stated multiple of \(p^{a-c}\), nonzero by variable-phase separation.
The absolute norm contributes the exponent \([F:\mathbb Q]\), while the embeddingwise coefficient-length bound incurs \(\deg(P)h(\beta)/p\) after taking this root.
The resulting bounded excess at every prime yields \(r\mid H_R w_0\) for the direct choice of \(H_R\).

Over \(E_0=\mathbb Q(\xi)\), adjoining \(\theta\) gives \(F\) because \(\beta=\theta^r/\xi\).
The relative degree is therefore \(d/\varphi(w_0)\).
All odd primes outside the divisors of \(w_0\) are unramified, and each conjugacy class in the cyclotomic field has size one.
Since \(w_0\le2\varphi(w_0)^2\le2d^2\), the exceptional-prime term in the [relative cubic bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#statement) is absorbed by \(\log(2d)/\log\log(5d)\), even when the relative degree is small.
This gives \(r\varphi(w_0)\ll m(\beta)/G(d)\).
The uniform bound on \(m(\beta)\) gives \(w_0\ll_R\log^6(2d)\), so the recorded totient estimate bounds \(w_0/\varphi(w_0)\) by \(O_R(J(d))\).
Together with \(r\mid H_Rw_0\), this proves (2) by the direct route.
