## Restrictions on irreducible binary reductions and prime periods

At 2026-11-25T16:01 UTC a separately assigned agent reviewed the whole [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v191.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer received the target and its direct inputs without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings on this corollary and did not develop its proof.
Both restrictions hold for the degree and the binary period of the same genuine integral trace-pole minimum.

The [trace-pole construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) supplies the monic reciprocal Salem minimum to which the [binomial degree cap](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#statement) and the [period repulsion inequality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#statement) apply.
Indeed, divisibility of the binary reduction by \(Z^N-1\) implies \(A\mid N\) in the full quotient, so \(A\ll d P_d\) uniformly in \(q\).
For irreducible binary reduction of degree \(d>1\), the class of \(Z\) is a nonidentity element of \(\mathbb F_{2^d}^{\times}\).
Its orbit length \(d\) is the least \(j>0\) with \(A\mid 2^j-1\), so \(d\mid\varphi(A)<A\).
The first repulsion test therefore applies with \(b=1\) and \(n=\varphi(A)/d\).
If \(r\) is the least prime dividing \(A\), at most \(A/\ell\) elements modulo \(A\) are multiples of each prime \(\ell\mid A\).
Taking their union bounds \(A-\varphi(A)\) by \(A\log_2(A)/r\).
Together with repulsion and the binomial cap this proves \(r\ll d^\eta P_d\log(3d)\).
For every \(\varepsilon>0\) one may first fix \(\eta<\varepsilon\) in the allowed range and then absorb the logarithmic factors into \(d^{\varepsilon-\eta}\).
This yields \(\log r/\log d\to0\), incompatible with \(r\ge(d+1)^{1/a}\) for \(A=r^a\) with bounded positive exponent.

When the order \(A\) is prime, \(d\le A\) by divisibility of \(\bar F\) into \(Z^A-1\).
Thus \(A\) is odd at large \(d\) and every factor of \(\bar F\) occurs once.
All nonidentity \(A\)-th roots have the same Frobenius orbit length \(f=\operatorname{ord}_A(2)\).
Counting the orbits selected by \(\bar F\), and then those in the whole group of \(A\)-th roots, gives \(d=tf+\delta\), \(0\le\delta\le1\), and \(I=(A-1)/f\ge t\ge1\).
The identity \(tA-Id=t-I\delta\) gives absolute value at most \(I=t(A-1)/(d-\delta)\ll tP_d\).
The repulsion constant can be decreased uniformly in \(q\ge2\) so that the cutoff is at most \(d^{1/8}\), since \(\log(2q+1)\ge\log 5\).
If \(t\) did not exceed the cutoff, repulsion at \(b=t,n=I\) would give the contradictory lower bound \(d^{1-\eta}\), whereas \(d^{1/8}P_d=o(d^{1-\eta})\) for the entire stated range of \(\eta\).
This proves the claimed uniform factor-count restriction.
The argument uses the two prior inequalities at their stated scope and makes no further assertion about collisions of dyadic roots.
