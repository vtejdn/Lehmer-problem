## Denominators, degree and radicals

At 2026-11-25T14:51 UTC a reviewer outside the proof development checked the complete [claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement) for Yin, including both argument sections and the two direct inputs below.
The height inequality, the equivalence with integrality, both prime thresholds and the uniform divergence for integer monomials follow under the stated hypotheses.

Normalize each extension of the rational valuation by \(v(r)=1\), counting all \(d\) embeddings, so the multiplicity at a place of \(K=\mathbb Q(T)\) is its local degree.
The Gauss norm of the primitive polynomial gives \(\sum_{v(T)<0}-v(T)=v_r(A)=\alpha\).
Pairing these embeddings with their distinct inverses recovers at least the absolute value of their signed valuation sum in the finite part of \(d h(T^aY^b)\).
Both \(E=p-qT\) and \(E'=pT-q\) have norm \(L^2\).
For \(r\mid q\), \(E\) has valuation zero outside the negative set, while \(E'\) has valuation \(v(T)\) on it.
For \(r\mid p\) the two roles interchange, and away from \(pq\) the two valuations agree on the negative set.
This gives the three coefficient terms in (1), including their signs before taking absolute values.
For \(T^aY^b\ne1\) the positive real inverse pair contains exactly one image of modulus greater than one and the other \(d-2\) images have modulus one.
The multiplicity of this exterior image is \([K:\mathbb Q(T^aY^b)]=1\), proving the required degree for the individual Mahler measure.
For \(T^aY^b=1\) the local and archimedean height estimates apply directly and force the right side to vanish.

Since \(Y>T>1\), a relation requires opposite signs and \(|a|>|b|>0\), so each prime dividing \(A\) would contribute strictly positively.
In the integral case \(w=T+T^{-1}-2\) has degree \(D\) by inversion, lies in \((0,1/(pq))\) at the distinguished place and has its other images in \((-4,0)\).
The integral element \(\delta=(p-qT)(p-q/T)\) has trace-field norm \(L^2\), so the product of its other conjugates proves \(L^2/\delta\) integral.
These are the hypotheses of the positive real branch of the [graph criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion), giving the coprime positive exponents in the claim.

For \(r\mid q\) the possible cancelling exponents are multiples of the pair defining \(Y/T\).
On the negative set put \(s=-v(T)\).
There \(v(Y/T)=-v(E)\), the sum of \(v(E)\) is zero, and its negative part is exactly \((s-e)_+\), also when \(s=e\).
Inversion gives a finite height contribution, in units of \(\log r\), of at least \(2\sum(s-e)_+\ge 2\alpha-de\).
For \(r\mid p\), the remaining monomial \(YT\) has valuation \(v(E')\) on this set, and the same bound follows.
For \(r\nmid pq\) the only cancellation in the first estimate has \(a=0\).
On the unit embeddings, \(\sum v(E)=2l+\alpha\); there \(qE'+pE=L\) bounds each \(\min(v(E),v(E'))\) by \(l\).
Summing \(v(E)-\min(v(E),v(E'))\) proves the bound \(\alpha-(d-2)l\).
Inverse pairing preserves the height cost for either sign of a nonzero power.

Any nonzero local valuation extends to a field containing a radical, where taking an \(n\)-th root divides the value by \(n\) and multiplying by a root of unity preserves it.
An algebraic number with such a valuation has either leading or constant coefficient of absolute value at least two in its primitive integer minimum, and its multiplicative Mahler measure bounds both.
For integer monomials the estimates grow with \(\alpha\) uniformly in the exponent pair at fixed \(p,q,d,r\).
The [one-prime construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place) gives \(A=r^\alpha\) for arbitrarily large admissible \(\alpha\) at each of its fixed degrees.
The divergence applies to these integer monomials, while the radical assertion is the nonunit conclusion and its measure floor.
