# Review of the trace evaluation modulo eight for short offsets

## Quadratic lifting and exclusion of all admissible offsets

At 2026-11-17T13:50 UTC an agent for Yin independently reviewed the [fixed v246 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v246.md#decision-five-term-second-trace-digit-submitted).
The reviewer previously checked the squarefree conductor factorization of v243 and the rational trace restrictions of v244, took no part in developing v246, and received its fixed text and scope without development discussion or earlier findings on v246.
The local formula holds for every stated radical, and the modulus-eight and modulus-four tests together exclude the specified rational lift throughout the stated interval.

For \(R=\operatorname{rad}(t),\ H=t/R\), the \(H\)-th power maps every primitive \(3t\)-th root to a primitive \(3R\)-th root.
Conversely an \(H\)-th root of any such \(\xi\) has order \(3t\), since at each prime \(p\mid H\) its order must have valuation \(v_p(H)+1\), and its other prime parts are unchanged.
All fibers are separable and disjoint in characteristic two.
The full parent reduction is squarefree, so the polynomial \(X^H-\bar\xi\) singles out exactly one monic degree-\(H\) divisor over the unramified lifting ring.

Holding \(Z=X^k\) and \(W=X^{H-k}\) fixed, one has \(P_l=f(U)-2L_0(U)\) for \(U=X^H\), since \(U^{2M-1}W=X^{2n-k}\).
The cube root \(u=\xi^M\) is primitive, as \(M=R(n/t)\) is prime to three.
With \(D=f'(\xi)=M(u/\xi)(1+2u)\), the constant in the Taylor expansion vanishes, and division by the unit \(D\) uniquely determines the successive corrections \(a=L_0(\xi)/D\) and \(b=(L'_0(\xi)a-Ea^2)/D\) at the required precision.
Substitution of \(U=\xi+2a+4b\) cancels the linear and quadratic terms modulo eight.
In \(4b\) the monomials \(ZW\) and \(W^2\) may be replaced by \(\xi\) and \(\xi X^{H-2k}\), respectively, because their differences have a factor \(U-\xi\), which vanishes modulo two in the proposed quotient.
All correction exponents \(k,H-k,2k,H-2k,0\) are less than \(H\) when \(0<2k<H\).
Thus \(G_\xi\) is monic of degree \(H\), and the Taylor identity proves actual divisibility in the quotient modulo eight.
The complementary residual factor is coprime, so uniqueness of Hensel factorization identifies this polynomial modulo eight.

Writing \(c=M\bmod4\) and \(e=(c-1)/2\), inversion of \(D\) gives \(a\equiv c(1-2u)(\xi u^{-1}Z+uW)\bmod4\).
For the quadratic term, \(E\equiv(u^2+eu)/\xi^2\) and \(L'_0(\xi)\equiv u^2 W/\xi^2\bmod2\).
In the order \(Z^2,ZW,W^2\), the coefficients of \(b\) are consequently \(\xi(u^{-1}+eu^{-2}),1,\xi^{-1}(1+(1+e)u^2)\) modulo two.
This recovers (4) after reducing the monomials.
Since \(x\) has order six and \(k\) is odd with \(3\mid k\), the values of \(Z,W,Z^2,X^{H-2k}\) at \(x\) are \(-1,-v,1,v\), with \(v=x^H\).
Separating \(v\) and \(-\xi\) in \(G_\xi(x)\) yields the respective coefficients
\[
 A=-3+2cu-4eu^2,\qquad B=1+(4-2c)u^2+4eu .
\]
For \(c=1\) their sum is \(-2+2u+2u^2=-4\).
For \(c=3\) it is \(-2+10u-6u^2\equiv-4\bmod8\), using the same cube-root identity.
The unit \(B\equiv1\bmod2\) then gives \(A/B\equiv4/B-1\equiv3\bmod8\), with the sign needed for \(3v-\xi\).
As \(\xi\) varies, each primitive cube root occurs as \(u\) exactly \(\varphi(R)\) times.
The scalar product is the \(\varphi(R)\)-th power of an odd rational integer, hence one modulo eight since \(R>1\) is odd and \(\varphi(R)\) is even.
The resulting factor has degree \(2H\varphi(R)\), so its trace at \(x+x^{-1}=1\) equals its value at \(x\) multiplied by \(v^{-\varphi(R)}\).

Now \(v=-y\) for a primitive cube root \(y\), and \(3v\equiv y+4y\bmod8\).
The reference \(y^{-\varphi(R)}\Phi_{3R}(y)\) is \(1\) for composite squarefree \(R\), and \((-1)^{f_p}p\) when \(R=p\), by the signed integral evaluation in v245.
The normalized linear Taylor term is the logarithmic derivative at \(\bar y\), which is regular since \(R>1\).
Its summands over the roots \(zr\) of \(\bar\Phi_{3R}\) are \((1+(z/\bar y)r)^{-1}\), with \(r\) primitive of order \(R\) and \(z\) either primitive cube root.
At \(z=\bar y\), pairing \(r,r^{-1}\) gives \(\varphi(R)/2\bmod2\).
For the other ratio \(u_0=z/\bar y\), the sum is \(\sum_{s\mid R}u_0^s\) by the squarefree divisor decomposition in v245.
For composite squarefree \(R\) this vanishes by pairing subsets at a prime \(1\bmod3\), or by the even multiplicity of each residue when all primes are \(2\bmod3\).
Also \(\varphi(R)/2\) is even in this case.
For prime \(R=p\), these two terms are exactly \(e_p\) and \(f_p\).
This proves (2) for arbitrary allowed \(p\), with no dependence on the choice of primitive cube root.

As an independent arithmetic check, direct reduction of \(v^{-\varphi(R)}\Phi_{3R}(3v)\) in \((\mathbb Z/8)[y]/(y^2+y+1)\) gives \(7,3,1\) for \(R=5,7,35\), using either \(v=-y\) or \(v=-y^2\).
I also lifted the two complementary factors of the full parent directly at \(k=3\).
At each precision \(2^a\), \(a=1,2\), I divided the product error by \(2^a\), solved for corrections to the two monic factors modulo two, and verified their final product modulo eight.
Normalizing the lifted conductor factor at a primitive sixth root gave the following residues for both values of \(M\bmod4\).

| \(R\) | \(t\) | \(n\) at \(M\equiv1\bmod4\) | \(n\) at \(M\equiv3\bmod4\) | Trace at one in both cases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 125 | 125 | 875 | 7 |
| 7 | 49 | 343 | 49 | 3 |
| 35 | 245 | 1715 | 245 | 1 |

In the antichain the first even index contributes the unique pure seven-power and the largest even index the unique pure five-power, since \(b_a=0\) precisely when \(A-a=0\) or \(1\).
Every mixed level has radical \(35\), so the product is \(7\cdot3\equiv5\bmod8\).
The strict bound \(H_t=t/R_t>L/245\) justifies the quadratic expansion for every \(k<L/490\) divisible by three.
For \(3\nmid k\) the v245 modulus-four test applies since \(k<\min H_t\).
Either result contradicts the exact rational trace value required by v244.
Finally \(\sqrt n\le L\) places the whole closed interval below \(L/490\).
These conclusions apply to the specified binary divisor.
