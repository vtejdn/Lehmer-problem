### Trace and the first pair

The Schur step gives a real monic strictly stable \(B\) of degree \(2\ell\), hence \(|B(0)|<1\), including when zero is a root.
The inverse \(KE=q\Delta-\Delta^*\) makes both endpoints of \(E\) zero, and the coefficient of \(w^\ell\) in the parity equation for \(V\) is then \(S=(L/N)(q+B(0))\).
For \(1\le j<q-1\), multiplication by \(L/N>1\) gives \(S>q-1\), and the upper endpoint \((L/N)(q+1)=q+1+j/(q-1)\) is strictly below \(q+2\).
Integrality of \(V\) leaves precisely \(q,q+1\) as candidates.

In the antecedent's elimination identity, reversal of \(w\Delta\) in formal degree \(\ell+1\) gives \(w\Delta^*\).
Canceling \(w\) after polynomial elimination gives (4) over \(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\) even when \(N\) is a zero divisor in that ring.
The coefficient of \(w^\ell\) on the right is \(-\Delta_0+q\Delta_{\ell-1}=0\), hence \(L\mid N^2S\).
For \(h=\gcd(L,N^2)=\gcd(L,j^2)\) this gives \(L/h\mid S\).
At the first occupied pair only \(q^2y-qx\) contributes on the right, since both coefficients at the preceding pair vanish.
As \(h\mid N^2\) and \(q\) is coprime to \(N\), one obtains \(h\mid qy-x\) and then \(L\mid S(qy-x)\).
Subtracting \(Ly\) gives the determinant congruence \(L\mid Wy-Sx\).
This reasoning also covers a middle coefficient paired with itself and allows either coordinate of a noncentral pair to be zero.
For \(\ell=1,2\) the outer zeros already force \(\Delta=0\).

The two values \(W=1-j,q+1-j\) both satisfy \(|W|<S\).
If the determinant is nonzero then \(L\le(|W|+S)H_0\), and \(L\ge q^2\) while \(|W|+S<2(q+1)\).
Thus \(H_0>(q-1)/2\), which exceeds both claimed thresholds for \(q\ge16\).
If the determinant is zero, division by \(k=\gcd(W,S)=\gcd(L,S)\) shows that all its integer solutions are multiples of \((W/k,S/k)\).
The assumption on the pair excludes the zero multiple.

### The two primitive pairs

For \(S=q+1\), the coordinates of a nonzero multiple have the same sign since \(W=q+1-j>0\).
Here \(k=\gcd(j,q+1)\) divides both \(N\) and \(L\), and reduction of (4) gives \((1-w)(\Delta^*+\Delta)=0\bmod k\).
Its coefficient at \(i\) gives \(k\mid x+y\), so \(k\le|x+y|\le2H_0\), with the lower bound using the common sign.
Since \(S/k\le|y|\le H_0\), this yields \(2H_0^2\ge q+1\).

For \(S=q\), the case \(j=1\) would require \(q^2\mid q\) by \(L\mid Sj^2\).
For \(j>1\), write \((q,j-1)=k(\beta,\nu)\), where \(\beta>\nu>0\) are coprime, and put \(\lambda=L/k=q\beta+\nu\).
The congruence \(L\mid qj^2\) now yields \(\lambda\mid\beta j^2\) and coprimality gives \(\lambda\mid j^2\).
From \(k\beta^2\equiv-\nu\bmod\lambda\) one obtains \(\beta^2j\equiv\beta^2-\nu^2\bmod\lambda\), hence \(\lambda\mid(\beta^2-\nu^2)^2\).
The positive multiple on the right is strictly below \(\beta^4\), whereas \(\lambda>q\beta\).
Thus \(q<\beta^3\), and the second primitive coordinate gives \(H_0\ge\beta\).

### Content and excess

For either sign of exact reversal, the right side of (4) factors as \((\varepsilon q-1)(q+\varepsilon w)\Delta\).
It vanishes modulo \(h\), and multiplication by the linear polynomial with unit leading coefficient is injective on \((\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z)[w]\) by the leading-term test.
Consequently \(h\mid(q-\varepsilon)c_\Delta\), also trivially when \(h=1\).
Multiplication by \(S\) and subtraction of \(Lc_\Delta\) yield \(L\mid c_\Delta(W-\varepsilon S)\).
The strict bound \(|W|<S\) rules out a zero multiplier for both signs, and \(|W-\varepsilon S|\le2(q+1)\) gives (2).

The positive branch inequality applies for all \(j\ge1\).
When \(j\ge q-1\), its lower bound exceeds \(1/2\) because \(q>4+q^{-1}\).
When \(1\le j<q-1\), \(H_0\le A_0\) gives \(q<A_0^3\) or \(q\le2A_0^2-1\) according to the trace.
Thus the branch denominator \(q+2+q^{-1}\) is strictly below \(Q_{A_0}+3\), and \(j\ge1\) proves (3).
The high-branch estimate covers the same constant since \(A_0\ge1\).
When \(\Delta=0\) the inverse forces \(E=0\), so \(B=D(z^2)\) satisfies the even-descendant theorem at the same \(q=d^2\), giving its strict \(1/327\) bound.
Taking the smaller of the two lower bounds gives the stated denominator \(\max(327,Q_{A_0}+3)\).

The earlier Schur and parity equations were checked at the interfaces used here.
The branch inequality and the previously proved even-descendant theorem were used at their stated hypotheses without a fresh audit of their full proofs.
The conclusion bounds \(\theta-q\) for the specified Pisot inputs and leaves the unrestricted Mahler measure question open.
