## Affine valuations on residue classes for a fixed reciprocal base

On 2026-11-16T17:16 a separately assigned agent for Yin independently checked the [valuation theorem](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v86.md#valuation), its proof, its fixed-polynomial extension and both examples.
The reviewer took no part in their development and received the fixed ledger location without its development discussion, author explanations or earlier review conclusions.
The review found all four sections valid under the stated hypotheses.

The relative quadratic involution indexes the embeddings of \(F\) by inverse root pairs, so the trace product defining \(V_k\) is valid even when some of its factors coincide after exponentiation.
The absolute norm from the full field equals \(V_k^2\) because the omitted monomial is a unit and the traced element belongs to \(F\).
The \(h\) roots reducing to one form \(h/2\) entire pairs.
For the other pairs, multiplication over each orbit of Frobenius on the inverse residue pairs telescopes to \(+1\) or \(-1\) according to the orientation at first return.
The orbit multiplicities are precisely the residual factor multiplicities, which gives the constant \(\epsilon\) after normalization.
This computation of the value at zero is exact in the two-adic splitting field.

All roots are local units, and repeated squaring of each principal part takes it into \(1+4\mathcal O\) by the valuation inequality in the proof.
One common exponent \(n_0\) therefore suffices for convergence of the logarithms and for exponentiation throughout the closed unit disk in \(t\).
The finitely many odd root-of-unity orders admit a common period for doubling, so a class modulo \(T\) fixes every \(\lambda_i\) simultaneously.
At \(t=2^{k-2-n_0}\) ordinary integral powers agree with the exponentials and reproduce \(U_k\).
The signed resultant estimate applies to \(Q\) with exponent \(J/2\) and sign \(-1\), since this irreducible nontorsion unit has positive measure and no root of unity.
It gives \(\log W_k=(J/2)m(Q)+O_Q(\log J)\) after removing the eventual fixed power \(2^h\).
Thus \(E_a(t)-\epsilon\) is nonzero as a function on every residue class.

Expanding the \(b\) two-term factors and adding the constant leaves at most \(2^b+1\) exponentials before merging equal exponents.
In characteristic zero the derivatives of orders \(0,\ldots,r-1\) at zero give an invertible Vandermonde matrix on \(r\) distinct exponents.
Since the constant term vanishes, the first nonzero Taylor coefficient consequently has an index \(e_a\) between one and \(2^b\).
After division by \(t^{e_a}\), continuity and the ultrametric inequality give its constant valuation at all sufficiently small sampled arguments.
Although that coefficient belongs to a possibly ramified field, its valuation is an integer by subtraction from the integer valuation of \(U_k-\epsilon\) at any sufficiently large sampled index.
The convergence \(U_k\to\epsilon\) makes \(v_2(U_k+\epsilon)=1\) eventually.
These facts give (1) and the \(O_Q(\log J)\) bound over the finite collection of classes.
The degree-dependent criterion for substitutions samples a subsequence of these same base norms because odd powering permutes primitive dyadic roots.
The thresholds and intercepts may vary with \(Q\).

For a fixed rational polynomial in the shifted norms, the substitution \(t\mapsto 2^{r_i}t\) lands in the same disk and advances the class by \(r_i\).
Polynomial operations preserve finite exponential sums.
If the sum is nonzero, its order at zero can now be zero, and its leading coefficient still has integral valuation by evaluation at rational sample values.
If it vanishes identically, all sufficiently late samples on the class vanish.
Replacing selected arguments by their squares covers the indicated \(W_k\) as well.

In the quadratic example \(b_1=7\) and \(b_j\equiv7\bmod8\) thereafter.
The factorization \(b_{j+1}+1=(b_j-1)(b_j+1)\) raises the valuation by one at each step and yields \(v_2(W_k-1)=k+1\) from \(k=3\).
When all roots reduce to one, every factor is even in \(t\), giving an even positive slope on the single class.
For the quartic, \(Q\bmod2=(x-1)^4\), its trace roots are \(1\pm\sqrt3\), and the negative relative discriminant at the second real place proves relative irreducibility.
The first trace produces an exterior root less than \(5/2\), giving the stated degree, Salem property and measure bound.
Direct iteration gives the successive pairs \((S_n,P_n)=(4,-8),(28,4),(772,-1532)\) for \(n=1,2,3\).
Thus at \(n=3\) the normalized differences are \(192,-384\).
Under the proposed induction \(v_2(s)=2n,\ v_2(r)=2n+1\), the unique lowest valuations in the next pair are \(2n+2\) from \(-2r\) and \(2n+3\) from \(12r\).
This proves \(v_2(U_k-1)=2k-3\) and \(v_2(W_k-1)=2k-2\) beginning at \(k=5\).
