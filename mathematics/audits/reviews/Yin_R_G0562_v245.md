### The rational trace tests and signs in the reference values

Since \(n\) and \(k\) are odd, \(l=n-k\) is even and \(\gcd(n,l)=\gcd(n,k)=1\).
Removing \(X^n-1\) from \(X^{3n}-1\) in the binary reduction leaves squarefree roots of precisely the orders \(3t,\ t\mid n\).
In particular the product of any selected complete levels omitting \(t=1\) has a coprime complement.
Hensel factorization supplies its unique monic dyadic lift.
Monic reciprocal normalization fixes this lift by uniqueness, and constant coefficient \(-1\) is impossible since it would force a root at one while the parent takes value \(-1\).
This is precisely the scope of the [earlier lift argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts).

For a rational lift the hypotheses on \(l\), the entire selected conductors and the omission of order three are exactly the [trace-evaluation hypotheses](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#traces).
There the unit evaluations at the primitive third and sixth roots give \(T(-1),T(1)\in\{-1,1\}\); \(T(2)=T(-2)=1\) and reduction of the respective arguments modulo three force both signs to be positive.
The first-correction formula used here is the sum over all selected primitive roots \(\eta\), with each root once, obtained by adding the two terms in each inverse pair in the [Hensel trace computation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#digit).
In particular its denominator \(1+\eta+\eta^2\) is nonzero, since \(t>1\).

For a primitive complex cube root \(z\), the arguments \(-z,z\) have traces \(1,-1\) respectively.
The half-degree of \(\Phi_{3t}\) is \(\varphi(t)\), which is even.
At \(-z\), the numerator \(\Phi_t((-z)^3)=\Phi_t(-1)=1\) because \(2t\) has at least two distinct prime divisors.
The divisor product for its denominator only involves factors \(1+z^d=-z^{\pm1}\), and their Möbius exponents sum to zero.
After the trace normalization all that remains is a real cube root of unity, equal to one.
At \(z\) the numerator is \(\Phi_t(1)\), equal to \(p\) for \(t=p^j\) and one otherwise.
In \(\Phi_t(z)\) the ratio of the contributions from exponents congruent to two and one modulo three is \((1-z^2)/(1-z)=-z^2\).
The sign in \(T_t(-1)\) is thus \((-1)^{a_t}\), since every other phase is a cube root of unity and the value is real.
For \(t=p^j\), the two nonzero Möbius terms at \(p^j,p^{j-1}\) give opposite residues precisely for \(p\equiv2\bmod3\).
For several primes the subset pairing by a prime congruent to one modulo three preserves the residue; if there is no such prime, the two residues each occur \(2^{\omega(t)-1}\) times.
Both cases yield even \(a_t\) for composite radical.
The magnitude \(p\) at each prime-power conductor contributes a minus sign modulo four precisely for \(p\equiv3\bmod4\).
Hence the full reference values are \(1\) at one and \(1+2(E+F)\bmod4\) at minus one.

The trace representations commute with reduction, so the difference of lifted and reference trace polynomials is \(2V\) in \(\mathbb Z_2[Y]\).
Since \(V(-1)\equiv V(1)\bmod2\) and both references are odd, the multiplicative correction modulo four is identical at the two arguments.
Exact rational values of one therefore require \(E+F\) even without imposing any further bound on the offset.
Among the primes prime to six, the two indicator conditions have odd sum precisely in the residue classes five and seven modulo twelve.

### Finite fibers and the excluded offsets

Let \(t=HR\) with \(R=\operatorname{rad}(t)>1\).
Every prime of \(H\) already divides \(R\), and \(3\nmid HR\).
It follows prime by prime that all \(H\) solutions of \(\eta^H=\xi\) for a primitive \(3R\)-th root have order \(3t\), with no additional roots or multiplicities.
They are simple because \(H\) is odd.
The identity \(g=1+\sum_{\omega}(1+Z/\omega)^{-1}\) follows by putting the two primitive cube-root fractions over \(1+Z+Z^2\) in characteristic two.
Using \(\eta^l=\xi^{n/H}\eta^{-k}\), the constant part vanishes on a fiber when \(0<k<H\).
In the finite geometric expansion of each other part, all sums of \(\eta^{j-k}\) vanish apart from \(j=k\), at which the fiber gives \(H=1\) in the field.
The denominator \(1+\xi/\omega^H\) is nonzero because \(\xi\) has a nontrivial \(R\)-part.
This proves (4) with the given power of \(\omega\) and no missing factor from the size of the fiber.

Decomposing \(\xi=zv\) is bijective by coprimality of \(3,R\), and \(R\mid n/H\) removes \(v\) from the numerator.
At \(u=1\) the two fractions for \(v,v^{-1}\) add to one, and there is no self-inverse primitive \(R\)-th root.
Thus \(K_R(1)\) is odd only for prime \(R\equiv3\bmod4\).
At a nontrivial cube root \(u\), the sum over \(v^s=1\) is \(1/(1+u^s)\) by the geometric expansion and \(s\) odd, and every Möbius weight of squarefree \(R\) equals one in characteristic two.
Since \(u^s\ne1\) and \((1+u^s)^{-1}=u^s\), divisor pairing or equal residue counts give zero for composite \(R\).
For \(R=p\), \(u+u^p\) equals one just for \(p\equiv2\bmod3\).
Taking the two cases \(z=\omega^{\pm H}\) gives the character sums with exponents \(n-k\) and \(-n-k\), respectively.
Each has residue one when its exponent is prime to three and zero when divisible by three.
This yields exactly the displayed residue at trace one, and adding the two reference parities gives the stated complementary indicators at trace minus one.

In the antichain of v243, every selected \(t_a>L/7\) and every radical is at most 35.
There is just one pure seven-power level at \(a=0\) for large \(A\); the only last two indices with \(b_a=0\) are \(A-1,A\), of which exactly one is even.
The pure five-power and seven-power levels give \(E=F=1\); all mixed levels have zero contributions.
For \(3\nmid k\) exactly one of \(n\pm k\) is a multiple of three, forcing both residues to be three.
For \(3\mid k\) neither exponent is divisible by three and both residues are one.
The interval upper endpoint is at most \(L/500<L/245\), and its length \(\sqrt n/1000\) tends to infinity.
For large \(A\) its admissible offsets are precisely the integers prime to 70, and exactly two thirds of those classes are prime to three over a full period of 210.
The bounded endpoint error in counting complete periods gives the asserted limiting proportion for this congruence test.

### Prime-square parents

A cyclotomic factor of the parent would have order \(3t\) or \(6t\), since squarefree binary reduction excludes larger two-parts.
The \(n\)-th power of its root has order three or six, while its \(l\)-th power has odd order because \(l\) is even.
The polynomial equation would require this last trace to be zero or one, which requires order four or six.
This rechecks the torsion exclusion at every \(n\) used here.
Even \(l\) also makes \(P_l(-e^v)/(-e^v)^n=-2\cosh(nv)-4\cosh(lv)+1<0\).
At a positive \(x\ge2\), \(x^n-2x^l\ge0\) and \(1+x^{-n}-2x^{-l}>0\), so there is no real root there.

For \(n=p^2\) the squarefree reduction has only the three full levels, and \(\bar\Phi_3\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Exactly one rational irreducible factor \(U\) represents it, by monicity and squarefreeness.
Its normalized reciprocal again represents the same residual quadratic, so \(U\) is fixed by the permutation of rational irreducible factors under normalized inversion.
It cannot be a quadratic: constant \(-1\) would give \(X^2-1\), while a nontorsion quadratic with constant one stable under inversion has integral trace of absolute value at least three, hence a real root of modulus at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
The real-root exclusions apply to either sign of that root.
The product of every remaining factor is stable under normalized inversion regardless of whether any one of its irreducible factors is reciprocal.
It is reciprocal, since the anti-reciprocal sign would force a root at one.
Its binary roots are a union of complete joint Frobenius-inversion orbits and avoid the order-three orbit.
By the group hypothesis the two remaining orders each form a single such orbit.
A nonconstant complement must therefore consist residually of exactly one full level, since containing both would give the impossible degree two for \(U\).
That one level contributes odd \(E+F\) by the congruence of \(p\), contradicting (1).

At \(p=5\) the orders of two modulo \(5,25\) are \(4,20\), which are also its orders after adding the modulus three.
The residue of \(-1\) modulo five requires exponent two modulo four and hence cannot realize \(-1\) modulo three.
Adjoining inversion doubles the two orders to \(8,40=\varphi(75)\), with \(8=\varphi(15)\) in the first case.
At \(p=7\) the odd orders \(3,21\) modulo \(7,49\) become \(6,42\) on adding the modulus three.
Inversion is already outside the generated group modulo seven; doubling gives the full sizes \(12,84\).
The lifts of the orders from \(p\) to \(p^2\) follow from \(2^4-1=15\) and \(2^3-1=7\) respectively.
These verifications cover every admissible offset and do not imply a statement for arbitrary odd inner exponents.
