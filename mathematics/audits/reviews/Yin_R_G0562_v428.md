## Independent whole-proof review of the odd-power exclusions and the binomial induction

At 2026-11-18T15:55 UTC an agent outside development of C388 independently reviewed its [submitted decision](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v388.md#decision-all-cubic-dilations-submitted), all four argument sections and the two finite certificates for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed inputs and scope without development dialogue or earlier findings on the odd-power argument and had independently reviewed the dyadic input.
The proof establishes irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) for every positive dilation of each nontorsion factor in every primitive positive direction, including the factors at small slopes and small degrees.

If \(\alpha=\gamma^p\) in \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), integrality of both \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) implies integrality of \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma^{-1}\), and the field inclusions give \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=K\).
Fix an embedding at \(p\), write \(x=\bar\alpha\), and let the local root field have ramification index \(e\) and residue degree \(h\).
The minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb Q_p\) of the embedded \(\alpha\) has degree \(eh\), and all its roots reduce into the Frobenius orbit of \(x\).
Its reduction has coefficients in \(\mathbb F_p\), so its \(eh\) occurrences distribute equally across the orbit, whose size divides \(h\).
As that polynomial divides the full monic parent locally, the multiplicity \(m_x\) is at least \(e\), including when \(\bar\alpha\) generates a proper subfield of the local residue field.

For \(u=\gamma^a,\ v=\gamma^b\), the identity \(F(u^p,v^p)=0\) gives \(\overline{F(u,v)}=0\).
The value of \(F(u,v)\) has rationally normalized valuation at least \(1/e\) if nonzero.
When \(m_x<p\), its \(p\)-th power divided by \(p\) therefore has strictly positive valuation.
The integral polynomial \((F(U^p,V^p)-F(U,V)^p)/p\) vanishes at the residue point, where the unit identity \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\) permits substitution of \(V=-g/B\).
Clearing \(B^p\) gives \(H_p(\bar u)=0\), hence \(H_p(x^a)=0\) by Frobenius.
The small-prime calculation thus applies to every residue with \(m_x<p\), whether the local field is unramified or ramified.

For \(p=3,5,7\) the reviewer independently divided the correction by successive gcds with \(TgB\) and the listed prime-to-\(p\) cyclotomic factors until no common root remained.
The remaining correction was constant in all three cases.
After excluding the invalid graph coordinates, the reviewer also recomputed the maximum critical multiplicities outside the torsion list and the total ones on the list for every slope.
These give exactly the three rows of J171, including zero torsion budget at the zero slope in characteristic three.
When \(p\mid a\) the reduction is simple throughout by primitivity and the derivative formula.

The prime-to-\(p\) lift of \(x\) then has precisely the required \(a\)-th and \(b\)-th powers by uniqueness, so it is a torsion root of this same parent above \(x\).
At least one occurrence at each such residue is thus reserved for torsion.
Summing the remaining multiplicities gives \(d\le\sum_x(m_x-1)\le6\), even if the characteristic-zero parent has repeated factors.

It still generates \(K\), so its reciprocal nontorsion minimal polynomial has even degree \(2,4\) or \(6\) and constant one.

For a prime \(p\), the product of the roots of a proper degree-\(j\) monic factor of \(X^p-z\) belongs to the base field and has \(p\)-th power \(z^j\).
Since \(1\le j<p\), Bézout then gives a \(p\)-th root of \(z\) in that field.
If \(X^m-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(K\), the norm of any root equals \((-1)^{m+1}\alpha\).
A \(p\)-th power at the next step would force this norm to be a \(p\)-th power in \(K\), contrary to the odd-prime exclusions or, at two, C380's exclusions of both signs.

Distinct base factors have disjoint inverse images, the map is unramified at their nonzero roots, and every lift of a root of unity is torsion.
Taking the greatest common divisor of any two positive exponents proves the claimed classification with multiplicities and equality of the two sets of positive measures.
