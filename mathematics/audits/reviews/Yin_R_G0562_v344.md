### Fiber roots and logarithmic integrals

The reflection \(i\mapsto n-i\) has a single fixed residue modulo the odd prime \(p\), which the invariant set of three residues must contain.
With this residue represented by \(s\), reflection sends \(s+e\ell+pj\) to \(s-e\ell+p(d-j)\).
This verifies the reversal of the shifted rows for either sign of reciprocity.
The row shifts are Laurent units, so they preserve the gcd.
At a circle point where \(a\ne0\), division by \(\omega\) gives the real equation \(b/\omega+2\Re(aY/\omega)=0\) for unit roots.
It has two distinct solutions exactly on \(E\), one double solution at equality and no solution under strict middle-row dominance.
In the last case self-inversiveness pairs a root with its reciprocal conjugate, so the two roots have a common argument and moduli \(R,R^{-1}\) with \(R>1\).

Since \(\gcd(p,\ell)=1\), disintegration over \(x^p=X\) assigns each of the \(p\) points \(Y^p=X^\ell\) weight \(1/p\).
On that grid \(\prod_Y|Y-u|=|u^p-X^\ell|\), so subtraction of the fiber Jensen formula gives (3) including the normalization for the outer root.
The grid mean \(d_p\) is integrable because it averages the integrable circle logarithm of the nonzero diagonal polynomial.
The fiber mean \(j\) is bounded above by the logarithm of a uniform bound on \(|q_X(Y)|\), and Jensen gives \(j\ge\log|a|\) away from finitely many points.
This proves its integrability and permits fiber integration of the logarithm, whose positive part is bounded.
The logarithm of the nonzero Laurent discriminant is integrable as well.
On \(E\) the nonnegative loss integrand is \(2\log 2-p(d_p-j)\) almost everywhere, so isolated diagonal zeros give finite integrated loss.

For either root off the unit circle the normalized factor in (3) is at least \(1-R^{-p}\ge 1-R^{-2}\).
Here the root separation is \(R-R^{-1}\), and \(j=\log(|a|R)\), giving the lower bound \(\log|\Delta|-2j\) for \(p(d_p-j)\).
On \(E\) the root separation is \(|\Delta|^{1/2}/|a|\) and \(j=\log|a|\).
Consequently the integral of \(p(d_p-j)\) exceeds or equals \(m(\Delta)-2m(F)-L_E+2\int_E\log(2/|\alpha-\beta|)\,d\mu\).
The extra integral is finite by the preceding log-integrability bounds and is nonnegative because the roots lie on the unit circle.
The zeros of \(a\Delta\) are a finite exceptional set, and the upper estimate follows from the bound \(2\) for each normalized factor.
Clearing Laurent powers from \(\Delta\) preserves its measure, and its nonzero integral leading coefficient gives \(m(\Delta)\ge0\) by Jensen.

### Gap, factorization and contact

The three shifted primitive rows meet the hypotheses of the [projected-support gap](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#statement).
For its zero-measure alternative, a cyclotomic factor specializes either to one of measure zero or to a nonzero integer constant, since the diagonal is nonzero.
The specialized product thus has measure zero or at least \(\log 2\).
This recovers the gap for \(E=\varnothing\), and for \(0<m(P)\to0\) it eventually forces \(m(F)\ge\eta_3\).
Rearranging (1) gives \(L_E/p\ge(1-2/p)\eta_3-m(P)\) along this tail, proving (2).
The argument uses the accepted projected-support theorem at this interface and the [cyclotomic specialization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#gap).
Bounding \(L_E\) from above on general elliptic arcs remains open.

Gauss's lemma over the Laurent UFD gives two primitive linear factors when the discriminant is a square in its fraction field.
Both coefficients of each linear factor survive on the diagonal in distinct residue classes \(0,\ell\), so each factor remains a Laurent nonunit.
Since \(P(0)\ne0\), irreducibility in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) also excludes this factorization after localization at \(x\).
A double root makes the primitive factors associates.
Conversely, the square of a two-row input with coprime rows \(A,B\) has rows \(A^2,2AB,B^2\) up to Laurent shifts, with unit gcd and distinct residues even for \(p=3\).
Centering at the cross term makes its quadratic discriminant zero.

Under weak middle-row dominance, a circle zero of \(b\) would be common to \(a,b,c\).
Such a zero is algebraic and nonzero, so its primitive integral minimal polynomial would divide all three Laurent rows, contradicting their unit gcd.
Now \(F(x^p,x^\ell)/b(x^p)\) is a nonzero real analytic circle function taking values in \([0,2]\), so any zero has even order in the circle parameter.
The parametrization \(x=e^{it}\) has nonzero derivative and the remaining factors are nonvanishing, hence this order equals the complex multiplicity of the corresponding zero of \(P\).
Characteristic-zero separability rules out such a zero for irreducible \(P\), proving the necessary presence of \(E\) for a Salem input with circle conjugates.
