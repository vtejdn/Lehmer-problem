## Finding

At 2026-11-17T05:28 UTC an independent agent reviewer for Yin examined the whole [C345 v168 statement and proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v168.md#statement).
The reviewer took no part in developing the proof and received the fixed claim and review assignment without proof-generation dialogue, exploration notes or author explanations.
The initial findings were formed from the claim, its mathematical inputs and the published sparse-divisor statement before consulting the earlier reviews of the two project inputs.
The proof establishes both inequalities in (1) and the individual-divisor gap for every stated \(F\) and every nonzero specialization, with constants depending only on \(F\).

### Localization at exceptional angles

Multiplication by a Laurent monomial preserves divisibility up to associates and preserves Mahler measure after specialization.
An irreducible factor with nonzero constant term is an ordinary polynomial divisor of the normalized specialization, since it is coprime to \(X\).
For a monic unit the sums of the positive and negative parts of the radial logarithms both equal \(m(A)\).
Thus a single choice of \(\varepsilon_F\) puts every exterior root and every inverse of an interior root in the prescribed annulus.
The exterior zeros of the two leading coefficients form a finite set separated from the unit circle.
All off-circle factors therefore have a uniform positive minimum on the chosen closed annulus.
For the interior roots the identity \(F^\vee(\alpha^{-1},\alpha^{-n})=\alpha^{-w-nR}F(\alpha,\alpha^n)\) supplies the correct fiber equation, with the angle negated.

Dividing the equation by the \((R-1)\)-st power of \(|z|^n\) bounds \(|D_G(z)|e^{y_i}\) by the sum of the suprema of the lower coefficients.
When \(D_G\) has \(b_G>0\) circle zeros counted with multiplicity, this gives \(\operatorname{dist}(z,Z_G)\ll_F e^{-y_i/b_G}\).
If \(y_i\ge1\), that distance is at least \(e^{y_i/n}-1\ge1/n\), giving the uniform logarithmic cap.
The chord on projecting \(z\) to the circle is no longer than its original distance to the circle zero.
This gives the claimed arcs with \(b=\max(1,b_F,b_{F^\vee})\), including repeated zeros and coincident centers.
For a leading coefficient with no circle zero the fiber equation already bounds \(y_i\) by a constant, so that side contributes no large-\(s\) tail.

### The two estimates on conjugates

In [C345 v108, equation (4)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases), reflection preserves conjugate phases, and the ordered off-diagonal logarithmic distances have nonnegative sum by the nonzero integral discriminant.
With \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\) the diagonal cost is \(O(d\log(2d))\), and the total phase-replacement cost at frequency \(\ell\) is at most \(2\ell m\).
For the triangular test, splitting the sum at \(1/h\) bounds \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell|\widehat\psi_h(\ell)|^2\) absolutely uniformly in \(h\).
Cauchy–Schwarz through \(\ell=d\), followed by the Fourier tail \(O(1/(dh))\), gives the stated absolute error for every center, independent of reciprocity.
If \(e_d\le1/2\), the test with \(h=2\max(\delta,e_d)\) is at least \(1/2\) on the closed arc and its error is \(O(e_d)\), since \(e_d^2=\log(2d)/d\) and \(m<1\).
The trivial bound \(1\le 2e_d\) covers the remaining degrees.

I checked the unit case of [C855 v2 Part A](../../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#argument) through the original-degree descent.
For a reciprocal power-stable endpoint the polynomials \(P_2\) and \(P_4\) have simple disjoint roots by degree preservation and the distinct positive heights of the two powers.
The simple roots make \(\sqrt{P_2P_4}\) nonrational, and the modulo-four congruence and the integral coefficients of \(\sqrt{1+4x}\) give its branch at zero integral Taylor coefficients.
The shifted Hankel criterion supplies infinitely many nonzero integer determinants.
The common-disk Green-function comparison bounds the logarithmic capacity increment by the sum of the single-slit increments \(2\log\cosh(v/2)\).
All squared and fourth-powered endpoints lie in the resulting hull, so the normalized square root evaluated at \(1/z\) continues on its simply connected exterior.
The contour Vandermonde estimate on exterior level curves bounds the quadratic logarithmic determinant growth by that hull's logarithmic capacity.
Together these facts give \(0\le\Phi_1(t)\) for every \(t\ge0\).
Reciprocal pairing and \(\log\cosh x\le x^2/2\) give \(\Phi_1(1/d)\le10\sum_i(u_i)_+^2-1/(2d)\), hence \(d\sum_i u_i^2\ge1/10\).
At a nonreciprocal endpoint Cauchy–Schwarz and the accepted Smyth bound give \(\sqrt{d\sum_i u_i^2}\ge2m>1/\sqrt{10}\).
For a degree drop, each embedding of \(L=\mathbb Q(\alpha^q)\) has \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):L]\) extensions of equal modulus on \(\alpha\).
The relative norm \(\gamma\) multiplies the corresponding logarithm by \(e\), while each conjugate of \(\gamma\) occurs \(f=[L:\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\) times in \(L\).
Its degree is \(d/(ef)\), so its degree-weighted sum of squares is the original sum with its degree weight divided by \(f^2\).
The norm is still a nontorsion unit since \(\gamma^q=\alpha^{qe}\), and iteration lowers the degree strictly.
The endpoint bound therefore applies in the original degree with all complex embeddings counted.
The existing [C855 whole review](Yin_R_G1086_v1.md#raw-p2-whole) and [C345 angular review](Yin_R_G0562_v108.md#cyclotomic-parent-exclusion) cover the respective fixed inputs.
The classical capacity identities, shifted Hankel criterion and Smyth bound retain their established source scope.

### Integration and the individual-factor consequence

The pointwise cap first gives \(1/10\le d^2 C_1(F)^2\log^2(2n)/n^2\).
Using also \(d\le w+Rn\), one gets \(e_d\ll_F \log(2n)/\sqrt n\), so \(e_d\log^2(2n)\) is bounded for every \(n\ge1\).
Above the fixed threshold the proportion of roots is bounded by the sum over the fixed finite set of centers of \(O_F(e^{-s/b}+e_d)\), and vanishes above the cap.
The integral against \(2s\,ds\) gives a bounded contribution from the exponential and a contribution \(O_F(e_d\log^2(2n))\) from the arc error.
This proves the mean-square estimate and then \(1/10\le C_Fd^2/n^2\), yielding the stated linear lower bound.

I checked the printed statement of [Dobrowolski's Lemma 1, p. 203](../literature/Dob06/readme.md#edition) against its use here.
The numerator of the increment is \(0.31\) times the degree of the monic divisor with nonzero constant term and no cyclotomic factor, and its denominator is the factorial of the number of nonzero coefficients of the actual multiple times that multiple's degree.
A parent supporting such a divisor has at least two collected terms.
Their number is at most \(k\), and \(w+Rn\le(w+R)n\), so the increment is at least \(0.31c'_F/(k!(w+R))\) throughout the small-measure case.
Taking the logarithm gives a fixed positive constant.
The source inspection covers the published statement and this application, without a new audit of its proof.
For other irreducibles the threshold \(\varepsilon_F\), the leading or constant coefficient bound \(\log 2\), and the rational-prime bound cover all positive measures.
Width zero reduces to the finite factorization of a fixed polynomial up to a Laurent monomial, including the vacuous case of no positive factor.
Additivity and nonnegativity of integral factor measures give the same gap for arbitrary divisors with contents, multiplicities and Laurent units allowed.
