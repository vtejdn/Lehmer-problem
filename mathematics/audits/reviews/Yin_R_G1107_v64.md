## Factor measures, deletion of grid nodes and separation from the endpoint

A reviewer outside the development of C895 v1 independently checked its full statement and proof for Yin on 2026-11-16T09:53 UTC.
The assignment identified the fixed target and the points to inspect, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings on C895.
The earlier independent reviews of the odd grid and the normalized residue law cover the respective inherited hypotheses and estimates. [Odd-grid review](Yin_R_G1106_v4.md#odd-grid-review), [normalized-law review](Yin_R_G1107_v57.md#normalized-residue-review).

The strict cut and endpoint on the full odd grid, with \(1<M(P)\to1\), give \(\kappa=o(p)\) and total residue variation \(O(1+\kappa)=O(p)\) by the near and far pole estimates in L464 v5.
In the integral coordinate basis, \(\rho_j=\nu_j h(b_j)\), with \(\nu_j=(4-b_j^2)/p\).
Exact semicircle quadrature applies to \(E_i h\) through degree \(2n-2=p-3\), and its continuous integral is \(a_i\) by orthonormality.
Thus \(|a_i|\ll p(i+1)\), and summing \(|a_i|(i+1)\) gives \(\|h^*\|_\infty\ll p^4\).
The lift respects products, and the integral cofactor has measure at least one, so \(\log M(F^*)=O(\log p)\) uniformly over divisors.
Each coefficient of \(h^*\) is bounded by its circle supremum.
In dividing by \(z-\xi\), every quotient coefficient is a sum of at most \(p+1\) input coefficients multiplied by powers of \(\xi\) when recursing from the high end, or by inverse powers when recursing from the low end.
Choosing the direction according to \(|\xi|\) makes each multiplier have modulus at most one.
Division through \(2d\) roots with multiplicity, followed by division by the leading coefficient of modulus at least one, yields the claimed \(O((d+1)\log p)\) bound for \(\log^+\|G^*\|_\infty\).

After rotating a nearest grid point to \(1\) and replacing an exterior root by its reciprocal conjugate, the normalized distances are \(|\zeta-w|\) with \(|w|\le1\) and \(|\arg w|\le\pi/p\).
Their product over \(\zeta\ne1\) is the modulus of the geometric sum through \(w^{p-1}\), also when \(w=1\) by evaluation of that sum.
Each term projects onto the middle ray with factor at least \(\cos((p-1)\pi/(2p))=\sin(\pi/(2p))\ge1/p\).
The constant term already has modulus one, so the claimed lower bound includes ties between nearest grid points.
Deleting any further node costs at most a factor two per root since \(|\zeta-w|\le2\).
Multiplying these bounds for all \(2d\) roots gives \(M(F^*)^{p-s}\) times the normalized lower bound, including the full leading coefficient on each surviving node.
Removing \(1\) and pairing conjugates then gives exactly the half-grid factor \(1/(2n)\) in (4).
For \(d=0\) this step simply counts the copies of \(|F|\).

Strictness makes the all-node cofactor resultant a nonzero integer even when the conductor is composite.
Omitting at most \(s\le4d+1\) half-grid evaluations costs at most \(s\log^+\|G^*\|_\infty\) in its log lower bound.
The original geometric factors have product \(p|B_n(a)|\) and absolute values at most \(16\), so deleting their values costs at most \(s\log16\).
Finally \((p-s)/(2n)=1-(s-1)/(p-1)\), and the logarithm of the divisor measure is \(O(\log p)\).
These losses together give \(O((d+1)^2\log(p)/p)\) in (5), uniformly in the divisor and its root locations.

In the stated degree range the error tends to zero and the deleted proportion tends to zero.
On a subsequence realizing the upper limit for the chosen divisors, the five-profile compactness and C893 give a joint weak limit \(\lambda\) for the trace and the positive variable \(X=p(b-a)\rho\).
Deleting a set of vanishing mass preserves this weak limit for the surviving subprobabilities.
The bound on the full first moments controls the positive logarithmic tails also after deletion.
For each \(T>0\), \(\max(\log X,-T)\), extended to \(X=0\), is continuous and its integral passes to the limit by this tail control.
Letting \(T\) increase gives the required upper bound by \(\int\log X\,d\lambda\) for the surviving averages.
The normalization by \((t+2)(t-x)^2\) has zero mean logarithm under the arcsine trace marginal, including coincident endpoint zeros.
C893 bounds the resulting \(\int\log u\,d\eta\) by \(\log(149/100)\) on each of the five profiles.
This proves (1), and the leading coefficient of \(F^*\), equal to that of \(F\), consequently has modulus one eventually.

A primitive factor with nonunit leading coefficient costs at least \(\log2\) in logarithmic measure.
Otherwise a lift of positive measure has a noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor of degree at most \(2p\), after choice of sign.
Its constant term is a unit, since the monic lift has constant term of modulus one.
The accepted numerical Dobrowolski estimate for the total measure gives \(\log M\gg(\log p)^{-3}\) in large degree by \(\log(1+v)\ge v/2\) for \(0\le v\le1\).
In bounded degree, the integral coefficient bounds and the measure-one characterization give the same weaker estimate.
Before first crossing the fixed lower threshold \(149/100+\delta/2\), at most \(O_\delta((\log p)^3)\) such positive-cost factors can occur, even with repetitions.
Including the crossing factor adds one, and the total degree is \(O_\delta(p^{1/2-\epsilon}(\log p)^3)=o(\sqrt{p/\log p})\).
This contradicts (1) if the original product exceeds \(149/100+\delta\) infinitely often.
A constant divisor equal to the content gives eventual primitivity.
For bounded factor degrees the lift's coefficient bounds give finiteness up to sign, since the trace lift determines the original polynomial.
In degree one eventual monicity forces a rational root to be integral, and for \(|y|\ge3\) the exterior modulus is at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).

Strict opposite signs of \(h\) at the adjacent poles force an interior zero.
For any such \(y\), its primitive minimal polynomial divides \(h\) by Gauss's lemma.
The field \(\mathbb Q(y)\) is real in this embedding, whereas the quadratic lift \(\xi\) is nonreal, so its degree is \(D=2d_y\) and the lifted polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The neighboring angles give \(|1+\xi|\le(2\kappa+1)\pi/p\).
If \(d_y\le p^{1/3}\), (1) gives a monic minimal polynomial \(S\), after choice of sign, with \(M(S)\le2\) eventually along these indices.
Its full Vandermonde product is at least one by the nonzero integral discriminant.
The factors involving \(\xi\) have product at most \(2^{D-1}M(S)\), giving (6).

Confluence of the \(k\) rows at \(-1\) divides out their mutual Vandermonde and yields the derivative rows normalized by their factorials.
This leaves exactly \(k\) copies of each factor \(|1+\xi_i|\) for \(i\ge2\).
The norms of the remaining \(D-1\) rows contribute at most \(N^{(D-1)/2}M(S)^{N-1}\), while the derivative rows contribute at most \(N^{k/2+k(k-1)/2}\).
Combining Hadamard's bound with (6), dividing by \(k=\lceil\sqrt D\rceil\), and using \(S(-1)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\) gives \(-\log|1+\xi|\ll\sqrt D\log(2D)\).
Here \(S(-1)\ne0\) by irreducibility and the nonreal root.
Thus \(L=\log(p/\kappa)\ll\sqrt D\log(2D)\), with absolute constants.
For \(D<L^2\) substitution of \(\log(2D)\ll\log(2+L)\) proves (2), and \(D\ge L^2\) gives it directly.
The omitted case \(d_y>p^{1/3}\) satisfies the bound as well because \(L\le\log p\).
All estimates apply to any choice of a zero in the gap, and \(L\to\infty\) by endpoint localization.
The proof establishes the factor restrictions and degree growth conditionally on the stipulated shrinking sequence.
The source lower bound is used at its existing statement and applicability scope.
