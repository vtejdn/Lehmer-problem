## Independent review of the dichotomy and its degree bounds

At 2026-11-17T07:53 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the complete [C345 v182 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v182.md#decision-four-coefficient-compression-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and the requested scope without proof-development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings on v182.
The arbitrary-coefficient dichotomy, the uniform endpoint estimates and both degree consequences follow under the stated hypotheses.

For sufficiently small positive \(m\), \(e^m<\min(2,\theta)\), where \(\theta\) is the plastic number.
An irreducible constant has measure at least two, and an irreducible polynomial with zero constant term is associated to \(X\) and has measure one.
For all other irreducibles the absolute values of the two endpoints are bounded by \(e^m\), so after choosing its sign \(S\) is monic with unit constant term and every root is an algebraic unit.
Smyth's theorem gives reciprocity, and positive measure excludes roots of unity.
The factor therefore survives removal of monomial and content from its multiple.
Clearing denominators in any nonzero rational dependence supported on at most three of the four powers would contradict the sharp three-term factor gap, including for a shifted binomial.
Two independent relations would give such a dependence by eliminating one nonzero coefficient.

Multiplying the rational coordinate matrix of the four powers by the invertible embedding matrix preserves its rank three.
More generally, at rank \(s-1\), the signed maximal minors from any \(s-1\) independent rows span the same line as the primitive rational kernel vector.
At a finite place the ultrametric inequality bounds every minor by the product of the row maxima, and at an infinite place the determinant expansion costs at most \((s-1)!\).
The two endpoint powers \(1,\alpha_j^D\) give the row maximum \(\max(1,|\alpha_j|_v)^D\) even at a contracting place.
With local-degree weights summing to one over the archimedean places, the extra contribution is at most \(\log((s-1)!)\).
The product formula cancels the single proportionality scalar across all places, and conjugate invariance gives \(Dh(\alpha)\) for each selected row.
At each finite rational prime a primitive integer vector has maximum absolute coordinate one, so its logarithmic projective height equals \(\log H\) for \(H\) the largest ordinary absolute coefficient.

Write \(r=e^t>1\) for the largest root modulus and \(q=Dm/d\).
In both orientations the extreme term has absolute coefficient at least one, so the triangle inequality gives \(e^{wt}\le3H\) for the larger endpoint gap \(w\).
Dobrowolski's Lemma 1 on the original four-term multiple gives \(q\ge0.31/(2\cdot4!)\) whenever \(e^m-1\le2m\).
For an original root, Part A of C855 v2 gives \(1/10\le d\sum_i\log^2|\alpha_i|\le2dtm\), using reciprocal pairing, the exterior maximum \(t\), and the sum \(m\) of positive logarithms.
Consequently \(Dt=q(dt/m)\gg m^{-2}\) and
\[
 \frac{w}{D}\le\frac{\log(3H)}{Dt}
 \le\frac{\log18+3q}{q}\frac{m}{dt}\ll m^2.
\]
These bounds are uniform in the polynomial, coefficients and cofactor.
They imply \(N-c\ge D/2\) and \(\log(4H)<(N-c)t\) throughout a single absolute small-\(m\) range, which proves the strict estimate \(|B(\alpha)|<1/2\).

Multiplying the two equations \(P(\alpha)=P(\alpha^{-1})=0\) after moving the lower binomial across the equality cancels both monomial factors and gives \(T_B(\alpha)=T_E(\alpha)\).
If the Laurent difference is nonzero, it cannot be a nonzero constant because it vanishes at \(\alpha\).
Shifting by its actual largest positive exponent produces a reciprocal integral multiple of degree at most \(2w\) with between two and five nonzero terms after cancellation.
Here Lemma 1 gives \(w\ge0.31d/(4\cdot5!\,m)\).
Combining this with the two bounds for \(w\) and \(dtm\) yields \(D\gg d^2t/m^2\gg d/m^3\) in this branch.
The only exterior conjugate of a Salem number is the positive real Salem number itself, so \(t=m\) gives \(D\gg d^2/m\).

In the identity branch the extreme Laurent coefficient of each binomial product is nonzero, forcing \(a=c\).
Equal endpoint products and equal sums of squares determine the squares of both the sum and the difference of the two coefficients.
The four resulting sign choices amount exactly to a common sign and a possible interchange of the pair, including equal or opposite coefficients.
An interchange factors \(P\) as \(B(X)(X^N\pm1)\), and neither binomial can have the given small positive-measure factor.
For the remaining order the primitive content gives \(\gcd(u,v)=1\), the ordering of disjoint blocks gives \(N>a>0\), and \(u>v\) would give \(|B(\alpha)|\ge u r^a-v>1\).
Equal absolute coefficients again give the excluded binomial factorization.
At the positive Salem root the choice \(\epsilon=+1\) would give \(B(\alpha)>1\).
