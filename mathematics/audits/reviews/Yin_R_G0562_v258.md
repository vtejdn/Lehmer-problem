# A uniform bound for the missing prime powers in a residual period

### Multiplicities and the probability estimate

For \(J=p^e J'\) with \(p\nmid J'\), the polynomial \(X^J-1\) in characteristic \(p\) is the \(p^e\)-th power of the separable polynomial \(X^{J'}-1\).
A residual factor of order \(t\) and multiplicity \(\mu\) is therefore present with all its multiplicity exactly when \(t\mid J'\) and \(\mu\le p^e\).
This proves both the formula for \(b\) and \(b\mid B\).
Monicity preserves total degree in reduction, so the weights \(\mu\operatorname{ord}_t(p)/d\), using orbit size one at \(t=1\), define a probability distribution even with repeated roots.
The gcd at \(J\) has degree at least \(d\delta(J)\), since partially present blocks can only increase the gcd.

The \(i+1\) integral factors of \(X^{p^iJ}-1\) in the successive \(p\)-power decomposition all reduce to multiples of \(X^J-1\).
Multiplication by each has nullity at least \(d\delta(J)\) on the reduction of the monic quotient algebra, so its nonzero determinant is divisible by \(p^{d\delta(J)}\).
Noncyclotomicity ensures nonvanishing in characteristic zero, and the absolute resultant is at most \(2^d\exp(p^iJm)\) by the root formula for a monic polynomial.
These steps also apply to nonunit roots.
For \(u>0\), the event \(Y_q\ge u\) is \(b\mid B/q^{\lceil u/\log q\rceil}\) when this quotient is integral, and is empty otherwise.
The equivalence uses the full \(p\)-part of \(b\) even when \(q=p\).
The floor choice of \(i\) gives \(p^i\le e^{u/2}\) and \((i+1)\log p>u/2\), including the case \(i=0\).
The ceiling gives \(J/B\le e^{-u}\), so the resultant inequality proves the uniform tail (4).

For \(u\ge u_0=2\log(2+K)\), one has \(K e^{-u/2}<1\), giving the bound \(4/u\).
The union of the events \(Y_q>T=u_0+32r\) has mass at most \(4r/T\le1/8\).
The integral of the truncated tail is at most \(u_0+4\log(1+32r/u_0)\) per prime.
Outside a further set of mass at most \(1/4\), Markov bounds the sum of the truncations by four times \(r\) times that expression.
Since \(u_0\ge2\log2\), this is \(O(r(\log(2+K)+\log(2r)))\) with an absolute constant.
On the intersection, of mass at least \(5/8\), no truncation changes the sum.
The proof needs no independence of the coordinates and covers every chosen subcollection of primes.

### An attained period and the five-term estimate

Taking all primes gives a represented factor with \(B/b\le e^A\).
With \(h=\lceil\log_p\mu\rceil\), one has \(\mu\ge p^{h-1}\), and \(\mu=1\) when \(p\nmid B\).
For \(t\mid B_0\), reduction of unit groups onto \((\mathbb Z/t\mathbb Z)^*\) is surjective by prime-power lifting and the Chinese remainder theorem.
The ratio of totients is at most \(B_0/t\), since each prime dividing \(B_0\) but absent from \(t\) contributes an extra factor \(1-1/q\) to this ratio after division by \(B_0/t\).
The order of \(p\) upstairs divides its order downstairs times the kernel size.
It follows that \(d\ge\mu f_t\ge (p^{h-1}t/B_0)f\), which is (2) after division by \(B\) and the bound on \(B/b\).
If \(p\nmid B\), \(h=0\) and the exact \(\mu=1\) removes the loss of \(p\).
The trivial unit group at \(t=1\), including \(B_0=1\), gives the same comparison with the stipulated order one.

For every \(0<l<n\), binary reduction gives \((X^{2n}+X^n+1)(X^n-1)=X^{3n}-1\), so divisibility includes all multiplicities for even as well as odd \(n\).
The constant term and monicity make every noncyclotomic irreducible factor a nontorsion unit polynomial.
For each of the four consecutive cuts, a vanishing side of at most two terms would force torsion.
Summing the resulting gap inequalities at width \(2n\) and coefficient length seven gives \(m\le6(\log7)d/B\).
At \(J=B\) one has \(\delta(J)=1\), so \(i=1\), \(p=2\) give \(m\ge(\log2)d/(2B)\).

Since \(3\mid B_0\), \(r_0=\omega(B_0)\ge1\).
All \(r_0\) odd prime powers have even totients, and their lcm loses at least \(2^{r_0-1}\) from their product.
That lcm is a multiple of \(f\), hence \(r_0\le1+\log_2 I\).
Euler's product gives \(\varphi(B_0)/B_0\ge2^{-r_0}\), so \(f/B_0\ge2^{-r_0}/I\).
With \(r\le r_0+1\) and fixed \(K=6\log7\), the exponent \(A\) in (2) is \(O(\log(2I)\log(2+\log(2I)))\).
The costs from \(2^{r_0}\), from \(I\) and from the factor \(p=2\) all fit the same exponent, even at \(I=1\).
Thus \(d/n=3d/B\) and the lower measure estimate give (3) uniformly in \(n,l\).
Any bounded subsequence of \(I\) would give a positive lower bound for the corresponding measures, proving the claimed divergence along a shrinking sequence.
