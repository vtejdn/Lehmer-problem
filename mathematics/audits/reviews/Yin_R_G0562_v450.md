### The base fields and the conjugation action

Every rational automorphism of the algebraic closure preserves the property of having only real conjugates, hence preserves \(T\).
It permutes the finite solvable Galois extensions of \(T\), whose union is a field because their finite composita have solvable Galois group.
This proves normality of \(B/\mathbb Q\) as well.
The introduction on PDF page 3 identifies \(T\) with the common fixed field of the involutions of the absolute Galois group of \(\mathbb Q\). [Fried, Haran and Völklein](../literature/FHV93/readme.md)
A finite abelian extension of \(\mathbb Q\) gives an abelian Galois extension after base change to \(T\), so \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\subseteq B\).
For a Salem polynomial every inverse-pair trace has only real conjugates, since each pair consists of real reciprocal roots or a conjugate pair on the unit circle.
Adjoining the two roots above each trace requires only square roots over \(T\), so its full splitting field belongs to \(B\).

Use the joint rational signed action at sufficiently large \(\max(a,b)\), and write \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)\).
Base change to \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\) realizes its whole commutator subgroup \(\prod_i W_i\), as the maximal abelian subextension is \(M\cap\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\).
Each coordinate subgroup \(W_i\) is normal in \(G\), since \(G\) preserves each rational factor individually and conjugation preserves both parities.
The uniform large-degree condition is available in all primitive directions, including bounded slopes, by the individual degree lower bound on this irreducible rank-two graph.
Here irreducibility of \(g(U)+VB(U)\) follows from its linear degree in \(V\) and \(-Ug(U)+(1-U)B(U)=1\).

Complex conjugation fixes \(T\) pointwise, so its restriction \(c\) lies in the normal subgroup \(C=\operatorname{Gal}(M/(M\cap T))\).
For \(i\in J\) its trace permutation \(\tau_i\) is nonidentity.
A permutation centralizing \(A_{n_i}\) must preserve every triple, by conjugating its three-cycles, and the intersection of the triples through any given letter is just that letter.
Choose a three-cycle that fails to commute with \(\tau_i\) and lift it in \(W_i\), with identity actions elsewhere.
The commutator with \(c\) belongs to \(C\cap W_i\) by normality of both groups, and its projection on labels is nontrivial.
As \(C\cap W_i\) is normal in \(W_i\), its projection is the full simple \(A_{n_i}\).

To recover a pair flip \(e_j+e_k\), take two more labels \(l,m\) and lift \((j\,k\,m)\) in \(C\cap W_i\).
Conjugation on sign vectors depends only on the label permutation.
Its commutator with \(e_j+e_l\) is \(e_j+e_k\) and still lies in \(C\cap W_i\).
The pair flips span \(V_i\), proving \(W_i\subset C\), also when simultaneous reversal is an even flip.
The same commutators with pure three-cycles show \([A_{n_i},V_i]=V_i\).
Thus \(W_i\) is perfect, and the normal closure in \(W_i\) of its natural copy of \(A_{n_i}\) contains \(V_i\) and equals \(W_i\).
These arguments act on one labeled coordinate at a time, including when some degrees coincide.

### The intersection and the factors at large slope

Restriction identifies \(C\) with \(\operatorname{Gal}(MT/T)\).
The intersecting field \((MT)\cap B\) is a finite Galois extension of \(T\), contained in a finite compositum of solvable Galois extensions by choosing generators.
The restriction map from \(C\) to its solvable Galois group therefore kills every perfect \(W_i\) for \(i\in J\).
Its kernel identifies with \(\operatorname{Gal}(MB/B)\) by Galois base change.
Conversely this last group lies in \(\prod_i W_i\) because \(B\) contains the maximal abelian base.
For \(i\notin J\) the trace polynomial has only real roots, so each trace belongs to \(T\) and its quadratic lifts all lie in \(B\).
These two inclusions prove (1), also if \(J\) is empty.
For \(i\in J\) the label action is transitive and an even flip using a spare label interchanges the roots in a prescribed pair.
This gives irreducibility over \(B\).

The rational parity fields identify \(E\) with the fixed field of the product of all \(W_i\).
Inside that product the subgroup fixing \(M_i\) pointwise is precisely the kernel of the \(i\)-th coordinate action, since the roots of \(P_i\) generate \(M_i\).
Taking all such kernels for \(i\notin J\) identifies \(E\prod_{i\notin J}M_i\) as the fixed field in \(M\) of the surviving product.
The same subgroup fixes \(M\cap B\), which proves (2).
At sufficiently large slope every factor contains a nonreal exterior root by the partition result.
For \(z=re^{it}\) with \(r>1\) and \(\sin t\ne0\), \(\Im(z+z^{-1})=(r-r^{-1})\sin t\ne0\).
Thus all the coordinates survive, and the intersection reduces to \(E\).
The rational nontorsion factors remain irreducible over every intermediate field between \(\mathbb Q\) and \(B\), including the compositum of all Salem splitting fields.
For each individual root \(z\), irreducibility preserves \([B(z):B]=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\), which proves the stated linear disjointness.

### Arbitrary towers and the Mahler measures

Fix an active index \(i\) and write \(n=n_i\).
If a subgroup \(U\) of an extension of finite groups maps onto \(A_n\), the image of the intersection of \(U\) with the kernel is normal in \(A_n\).
When the image is full, the kernel has an \(A_n\) section, and when it is trivial, the quotient has such a section via the image of \(U\).
This proves extension closure for groups without an \(A_n\) section, in addition to closure under subgroups and quotients.
For \(q<n\) all solvable groups and \(S_q\) have this property, the latter by \(q!\le(n-1)!<n!/2\).
At a finite tower step the relative normal closure has solvable group or group inside \(S_q\), according to the allowed adjunction.
After the preceding field is replaced by its normal closure over \(B\), the conjugates of the step give a compositum whose group over that field embeds in a finite product of subgroups of the relative groups.
Induction thus excludes an \(A_n\) section from the group of the whole normal closure \(N/B\), even for towers leaving \(MB\).

The field \(N\cap MB\) is Galois over \(B\), and its group is both a quotient of \(\operatorname{Gal}(N/B)\) and an image of \(\prod_{j\in J}W_j\).
The natural \(A_n\) in the active \(W_i\) must map trivially, since any nontrivial homomorphism on this simple group would embed a copy of it.
Normal generation kills all of \(W_i\).
As \(W_i\) moves \(y\), this intersection cannot contain \(y\).
For the opposite inequality, adjoining all roots of every active trace polynomial uses degrees at most the maximum active \(n_j\), and adjoining the roots above those traces uses quadratic extensions.
The subgroup fixing the resulting field in \(MB\) consists of exactly the inactive coordinates.
Every inactive coordinate fixes \(y\), so the tower contains it, proving the exact minimum in (3).

At large slope a root of \(P_i\) has just its own coordinate active.
Its trace and one quadratic lift achieve the upper bound using only this root's trace.
The measure belongs to \(M_i\), since it is the positive product of the conjugate exterior pairs.
There are \(2k_i\) occupied labels and at least one unused label at sufficiently large slope, uniformly by the degree estimate.
The signed-subset calculation therefore realizes every choice of those labels and their signs in \(W_i\), and its distinct products remain distinct over \(B\).
This gives degree \(2^{2k_i}\binom{n_i}{2k_i}\), with just the \(i\)-th coordinate active.
In a product of measures from distinct factors the automorphisms of each chosen coordinate change only its own nonzero term, giving the specified active set.
Thus \(q_i=n_i=\deg P_i/2\) for either the single root or the single measure.
The uniform degree asymptotic gives \(2q_i/b=(k_i/a)(1+o(1))\), and the primitive criterion identifies \(q_i=o(b)\) along large slopes with the existence of factors in the full family having positive log measure tending to zero.
The equivalence leaves the existence of such allocations open.
