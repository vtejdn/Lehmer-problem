For a fixed powered pair choose a nontorsion coordinate \(W=\omega\tau^j\).
Extending the exterior embedding to a field containing \(W,\omega,\tau\) gives \(|j|\log\tau\le\max_{\sigma}|\log|\sigma W||\), even for \(j<0\).
Since \(|j|h(\tau)=h(W)>0\) and \(d h(\tau)=\log\tau\), both \(d\) and \(h(\tau)\) are bounded in terms of \(W\).
Northcott's theorem therefore gives infinitely many distinct pairs from the hypothesized family, and their smooth lifts eventually avoid every finite set.
The uniform section height bound also forces \(d_i\to\infty\) for distinct Salem numbers.
Their off-circle fraction \(2/d_i\) tends to zero, so the face theorem applies both to constant primitive directions and to unbounded primitive norms.
On a subsequence approaching one signed normal the short exponent satisfies \(k_i/l_i\to0\).
Exact equality \(k_i=0\) would force torsion by the full-circle fiber theorem.
A bounded primitive norm on a further infinite subsequence would force one fixed primitive direction equal to this normal and hence \(k_i=0\).
Thus phase descent applies for arbitrary common dilations, bounds the phase orders through the real embedding, and permits constant phases after extraction.
The separate coordinate inversions preserve the stabilizer exponent and give positive \(k_i,l_i\).
The two bounds on \(k_i\log\tau_i\) yield \(1<R<\infty\), divergence of \(l_i\log\tau_i\), and \(k_i\to\infty\) if \(\tau_i\to1\).

The primitive irreducible equation has degree \(D\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(U)\) in \(V\), so \(D\) is the degree of the map \(U\).
The width is \(Dl_i+O_F(k_i)\), since the maximum and minimum \(V\)-exponents are \(D\) and zero and the range of \(U\)-exponents is fixed.
The individual section height formula now gives \(l_i h(\tau_i)\to m(F)/D\) and \(h(U(P_i))\to0\).

The exponent \(s\) of \(H\) makes both \(\zeta^s\) and \(\theta^s\) fixed by \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_i/M_i)\), so these roots of unity lie in the real field \(M_i\) and are signs.

The resulting translated inversion sends infinitely many distinct affine points back to the curve, hence extends to an automorphism exchanging marked poles and zeros.
Composing the chosen embedding on the right with \(\gamma\) gives all \(f\) lifts of the interior embedding.
Their limits are \(\rho(h_\gamma)I Q_+\), with \(\rho(h_\gamma)\in H\) by the integral character equations defining \(H\).

At a finite nonzero \(U\)-value the equation in the chart \(1/V\) cuts out \(A_D(U)=0\) on its boundary.
The leading coefficient is a nonzero polynomial, so this closure has no boundary component there.
Surjectivity of normalization gives at least one marked pole above every such root, including repeated roots, and the same argument using \(V\) covers the zeros.
The full-circle fiber theorem excludes all circle conjugates, and hence the height at every marked point is positive.
Choose disjoint neighborhoods of the marked points whose closures avoid \(|U|=1\) and all other zeros and poles of \(V\).
Over a finite Galois field \(K\) defining the marked points, sum the point formula over one rational orbit \(\mathcal O\).
An embedding permutes the points in this orbit, so the sum of neighborhood indicators selects the same union for each embedding of \(K\).
Every embedding of \(L_i\) has \([KL_i:L_i]\) extensions, which cancels the corresponding factor in the normalization.

Each visiting lift contributes exactly \(l_i\log\tau_i\).
The \(f\) individual limits are marked points on each side, so the count for each fixed union stabilizes even if several lifts have the same limit.
Dividing the sum by \(fd_i\) gives \(n_{\mathcal O}m(F)/(fD)=|\mathcal O|e h(U(Q))/D\).
