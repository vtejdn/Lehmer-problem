### Cubic divisibility

Write \(z=\alpha^p,\ s=z+z^{-1},\ q=\alpha+\alpha^{-1},\ D=s^2-4,\ \delta=z-z^{-1}\).
Powering preserves the full field, and its inverse involution has fixed field both \(\mathbb Q(q)\) and \(\mathbb Q(s)\).
The [first-row derivative identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v233.md#different) gives \(2l=DT'(s)c\), where \(c\in\mathbb Z[s]\).
The recurrence for \(W=W_p(q)\) gives \(\alpha^{-1}=(W_{p+1}(q)-z)/W\).
The coordinates on \(1,z\) are unique over the trace field.
Thus \(g(z)=-\alpha^{-1}u\) gives \(B(s)=u/W\) for \(g=A(t)+YB(t)\).
The Euler derivative at \(z\) is \(\delta A'(s)+zB(s)+z\delta B'(s)\).
Adding its image under inversion gives \(sB(s)+DB'(s)=q(l+u/p)\) with the stated positive sign on \(DB'\).
Differentiating \((\alpha-\alpha^{-1})W_p(q)=\alpha^p-\alpha^{-p}\) as a Laurent identity gives \((q^2-4)W'_p(q)=ps-qW\).
Since \(D=(q^2-4)W^2\), substitution gives precisely \(2W'u=pW^3(qT'(s)c-2B'(s))\).
Modulo \(W\), the product \((q^2-4)sW'\) equals \(4p\).
Inverting \(2p\) therefore makes \(W'\) invertible modulo \(W\), and an inverse lifts modulo \(W^3\) by a finite geometric sum.
This proves the second assertion of (2).
Taking determinants in the two pairs of coordinate containments gives both statements in (3), with the factor of two kept over the unlocalized trace order.

### Exact local columns and arithmetic global approximation

For \(\lambda\nmid2p\operatorname{disc}T\), the monogenic order \(S_\lambda\) is a product of unramified integer rings, \(T'(s)\) is a unit, and \(S_\lambda=R_\lambda\) by the index-discriminant identity.
Given \(u\in W^3S_\lambda,\ l\in D S_\lambda\), the local Laurent expression \(H_0(t)+(Y-Y^{-1})H_1(t)\) has derivative \(\delta H_0'(s)+sH_1(s)+DH_1'(s)\).
Prescribing \(H_0=u,\ H_1=H_0'=0,\ H_1'=l/D\) at \(s\) realizes the two coordinates of \(f\).
For \(g\), put \(v=l+u/p\) and \(\alpha^{-1}=q/2-\delta/(2W)\).
The four prescriptions in v237 give the required value and derivative separately in their invariant and anti-invariant coordinates.
In particular \(v/W\) is integral, and the last derivative is \(ql/(2D)-W'u/(2p W^3)\), also integral.
All these assignments lift to polynomials with \(\mathbb Z_\lambda\) coefficients because adding \(T\) times a polynomial adjusts any desired derivative without changing the value.
The [bounded representatives modulo the squared minimum](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#jets) work over this base since the polynomial is monic with unit constant term.
The global coefficient equations are then a finite rational linear system on integral coefficients.
Clearing fixed denominators and applying flat base change to the kernel and its value image identifies the constructed local solutions with the completed global column lattice.
The first section supplies the opposite inclusion, and the coordinate vectors \((W^3,0),(0,D)\) give the full ideal \(W^3D S_\lambda\).

The global lattice spans all rational trace coordinates by the same interpolation over the field with denominators cleared.
Global columns are dense in the completed lattice, and the condition of attaining the valuation of \(W^3D\) is open in each of its finitely many components.
Consequently two global columns can retain all these valuations after further variations in congruence cosets of full rank.
For trace degree at least two, the vanishing of a value and the proportionality of the two rational coordinate lists are proper algebraic conditions, avoided in these cosets by Zariski density.
Nonzero determinant is already forced by the prescribed valuations.
The [endpoint corrections](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#endpoints) preserve all these jets and impose the seed gcd because \(b(z)\ne0\) for \(u\ne0\).
For the normalized graphs the value and centered Euler derivative at \(\alpha\) both vanish by (1) and its image under inversion.
Thus all the additional row conditions can coexist with the exact localized determinant ideal when coefficient heights are unrestricted.

### Transfer from the contraction ideal to the two endpoint evaluations

At \(\lambda\mid b_\epsilon\) the [cyclotomic contraction exponent](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#contraction) is the maximum over primitive nodes of \(v_\lambda(P_0(\epsilon\zeta^j))=k\), using \(v_\lambda(\lambda)=1\).
At a maximizing node, good reduction of the monic \(P_0\) gives just one congruent root and all other factors are units.
At that embedding the distance of \(\alpha\) to the node has valuation \(k\).
Because \(\lambda\nmid2p\), powering to \(z\) preserves that distance to the corresponding sign, and the reduction of the node has order \(p\) or \(2p\).
In particular \(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}\) is a unit and \(v_\lambda(\delta)=k\), giving \(v_\lambda(W)=k\) and \(v_\lambda(D)=2k\).
The cubic divisibility and first-row derivative identity now give \(v_\lambda(u)\ge3k,\ v_\lambda(l)\ge2k\) without any unramified trace-discriminant assumption.
The same value and derivative bounds hold for the two entries of \(g\) using (1) and for \(f,g\) at \(z^{-1}\) by inversion.
Multiplying by \(Y^{2e}\) preserves the respective bounds on values and ordinary derivatives since \(z\) is a unit.

In the integral expansion of \(M=a,b\) about \(z\), every term evaluated at \(z^{-1}\) except possibly the quadratic already has valuation at least \(3k\).
The value at \(z^{-1}\) also does, and its distance from \(z\) has valuation exactly \(k\).
The quadratic coefficient must therefore have valuation at least \(k\), which forces \(v_\lambda(M(\epsilon))\ge3k\) on evaluation at the sign.
Both signs and all primes under consideration give (5).
The gcd hypothesis prevents the two evaluations at a single sign from both being zero, proving (6) by the coefficient sum.
For fixed \(P_0\), the prime \(p\) eventually avoids \(P_0(\epsilon)\), and every fixed prime dividing \(2\operatorname{disc}P_0\) has only finitely many residual orders of roots of \(P_0\).
These primes stop dividing \(B_\epsilon\), so \(b_\epsilon=B_\epsilon\) eventually.
The [fixed-input squeeze on the contraction exponent](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#fixed-input) gives \(\log B_\epsilon/(p-1)\to h/2\) at either sign.
This yields (7) at fixed seed, and the [height-to-measure estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v234.md#height) applies to the [short prescribed-offset representatives](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#short), where the normalized height discrepancy tends to zero.
The argument supplies no uniform fixed-prime onset for varying seeds.

### Explicit quartic verification

The trace polynomial is \(q^2-q-5\).
Its smaller real root lies strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), so \(q^2-4\) fails to be a square at that embedding, giving the quadratic extension above the quadratic trace field.
The other trace is between \(2\) and \(3\), so just one exterior reciprocal root contributes, giving the stated positive measure less than one.
Trial division through \(64\) and \(2520\) gave primality of \(4099\) and \(6353449\), respectively, while \(4097=17\cdot241\) and \(4098\) is even.
I independently used repeated squaring of the integer matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) modulo \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda^2\).
Its trace at the \(p\)-th power gives the powered trace and its lower-left entry gives \(W_p(q)\).
Both displayed residue roots satisfy the quadratic and have nonzero \(q^2-4\).
Their powered traces are \(2,4977833\), so the powered trace discriminant is a unit at \(\lambda\).
The stated lift reduces to \(356245\) and satisfies the quadratic modulo \(\lambda^2\).
At that lift the lower-left entry is \(3045055622924=479276\cdot6353449\) modulo \(\lambda^2\), with \(479276\) a unit.
Hence \(v_\lambda(W)=1,\ v_\lambda(D)=2\) in that component and (4) gives exact determinant valuation five there.
The original root has nontrivial order dividing \(p\) because its trace avoids \(\pm2\) and its powered trace is \(2\).
Thus it has order \(p\), intersects a positive primitive node at good reduction, and (5) forces the asserted integer cubes in both evaluations at \(1\).
