For an ordered pair \((b_0,a)\), conditioning on a place \(P\) gives a uniform translate of the inertia orbit on \(b_0\).
The orbit-stabilizer index is \(e_v\), the ramification index in the root field at the place below \(P\).
Consequently a possible hit has conditional probability exactly \(1/e_v\).
In the integral case the unique Teichmüller lift of the residue of \(b_0\) belongs to the completion of that root field, so its distance from \(b_0\) has valuation at least \(1/e_v\) in the specified normalization.
Every indicated lift sends this representative to its \(q_p\)-th power by residue compatibility and uniqueness.
The ultrametric inequality then gives the claimed difference valuation.
For \(|b_0|_P>1\), the conditional hit also gives \(|a|_P>1\), and the inverse difference is exactly the normalized chordal distance.
Thus the genuine local saving is at least the conditional hit probability times \(\log p\).
Summing these estimates for all pairs after averaging over places counts the expectation of \(|A\cap yB_p|\), since each \(y\) acts injectively.
Overlap between different pairs of residue classes needs no independence assumption.

The powered node families at distinct primes have different positive heights, and none has the original height.
The remaining possible coincidence occurs inside a single powered family, precisely where injectivity is assumed.
For Salem conjugates, sending one member of an equal-power pair to the positive exterior root would give two different roots of its exterior modulus, which is impossible.
The confluent Vandermonde product contains every required cross pair exactly \(T\) times.
At a finite place each node of multiplicity \(t\) has exponent \(t(S-t)\le t(S-1)\) in the product of the local maxima before the chordal savings.
At an infinite place the Euclidean norm of the \(j\)-th divided derivative row costs at most \(S^{j+1/2}\max(1,|z|)^{S-1}\).
In particular the logarithmic coefficient for the \(T\) rows at an original node is \(T^2/2\).
The local degrees of the splitting field are equal over \(p\), allowing the uniform average over \(P\) in the product formula.
These computations give exactly (1), also for subsets which are not stable under the splitting group.

A selected conjugated lift can land in \(A\) when at least one of the \(e_v\) images lands there, so the loss in replacing its indicator by the full-coset probability is at most \(1-1/e_v\), even conditionally on the selected place.

The test primes with \(p\equiv1\bmod7\) give asymptotically one sixth of the interval's logarithmic mass and split in the chosen cubic field.
The three residue prescriptions are independent prime ideal conditions there.

The other two local factors split in unramified extensions, with cyclic Frobenius acting transitively by irreducibility of the reductions.
The additional split primes exceed \(n\), have enough distinct linear factors and can be chosen outside the finite test set.
After the Chinese remainder conditions, dividing the congruence lattice by arbitrarily large integer denominators prime to its support preserves all these local data while allowing simultaneous prescribed open conditions at all real embeddings.
This produces a genuinely totally real splitting field normal over \(\mathbb Q\).

In the splitting group the auxiliary primes produce elements supported in one coordinate, and conjugation by an element above the cubic rotation moves their supports cyclically.
The projected kernel is transitive and the \((n-1)\)-cycle makes the stabilizer of its fixed point transitive on the other letters.
Hence conjugating an isolated transposition by the kernel gives every isolated transposition on that coordinate.
The kernel therefore contains \(S_n^3\), and surjection to the cubic group gives the full cyclic product-action group.
The action on triples is faithful.
Any overgroup strictly containing the indicated point stabilizer contains a base-group element moving its distinguished point in some coordinate, after multiplying by a rotation from the stabilizer.
Conjugation supplies a second point stabilizer in that coordinate alone, and the two generate \(S_n\) since the union of the transposition graphs is connected for \(n\ge3\).
Rotation supplies the remaining coordinate factors, proving maximality and hence the primitivity of the specified field of degree \(n^3\).

At a test prime the two moving coordinates are conditionally independent uniform \(n\)-cycles after conditioning on the fixed coordinate.
For \(1\le k<n\), raising to \(k\) permutes the class of \(n\)-cycles since \(n\) is prime.
For any particular point the three choices of fixed coordinate give disjoint image sets, each of \((n-1)^2\) equiprobable possibilities.
In the triangle only the third coordinate is constant.
Its strictly differing row sizes and strictly differing column sizes imply that every setwise stabilizer fixes both of its varying coordinates pointwise, so the triangle cannot be a nonsingleton subgroup orbit.
When the third coordinate is fixed, removing a point's row and column removes at most \(2n\) of the \(n(n+1)/2\) triangular points.
The resulting conditional fraction divided by \((n-1)^2\) is at least \(1/3\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
The unconditional overlap is therefore at least \(1/9\), although no element in the prescribed test class stabilizes the triangle.
The logarithmic weight of that class of primes suffices since \(1/54>1/100\).

The box in the integral embedding lattice has a nonzero lattice point by the difference-of-points volume argument, with only the first coordinate allowed to have large absolute value.
Its nonzero integral norm forces that absolute value to exceed two, and the sign may be chosen positive.
The unique exterior coordinate forces the integer to generate the whole totally real field because embeddings of a proper subfield repeat with equal multiplicity.

Its conjugates over the other embeddings are unit-circle pairs, so the positive exterior root has the required trace field and is Salem.
For every such Salem generator, taking complete inverse pairs above the triangle preserves normalized overlap exactly, including at primes ramified upstairs, because all lifts project to the residue automorphism at the restricted unramified place.
All restricted places are equally frequent, independently of the chosen generator.
Now \(d/r=2n^2/(n+1)\), and (2) yields (3) with an absolute constant.
This construction demonstrates the specified behavior on a family of trace fields and makes no uniform assertion about the occurrence of the overlaps in other fields.
