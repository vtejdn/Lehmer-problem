## The partial splitting inequality and the sharp Salem cost

The partial splitting bound, its applications and the sharp growth of the logarithmic measure in the constructed Salem family hold.

For an algebraic unit of local degree \(ef\le B\), a residue of exact degree \(r\) implies \(r\mid f\) and bounds the ramification index by \(\lfloor B/r\rfloor\).
The unique lift with multiplicative order prime to \(p\) of that residue lies in each of the fields containing the relevant unit, by simple-root lifting of \(Z^{p^r-1}-1\).
Using this common center gives the difference valuation at least \(1/\lfloor B/r\rfloor\) for any two counted roots in its residue class.
No bound on the ramification of their compositum is needed.
Writing \(w_\alpha=1/\lfloor B/r\rfloor\), the unordered pairs yield \(w_\alpha n_\alpha(n_\alpha-1)\) in the squared discriminant, and other pairs contribute nonnegatively by monic integrality.
Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(w_\alpha^{1/2}n_\alpha\) and \(w_\alpha^{-1/2}\) gives the denominator \(S_{p,B}\); the removed diagonal term is at most the total number \(n\) of counted roots.
Expanding the floor as a sum of ones and applying \(\sum_{j\le m}p^j\le2p^m\) gives \(S_{p,B}\le(2+2B p^{-B/2})p^B\le5p^B\).
On the complex side Hadamard's inequality with the unnormalized Vandermonde gives the factor \(d^d M^{2d-2}\) for the absolute discriminant.
At each prime its valuation is nonnegative, so the multiplicative factorization of the nonzero integer discriminant gives the sum of the positive contributions with their logarithmic weights.
This proves the asserted estimates for monic squarefree polynomials, including reducible polynomials and roots uncounted by the local unit condition.

Inserting \(n\le d,\ 2^B\le\sqrt d\) at the half-logarithmic cutoff gives \(n^2=O(d^{3/2}\log d)\) at bounded measure.
If \(2^B\log d=o(d)\) the same argument gives \(n^2/d^2\to0\).
Conversely \(n\ge\delta d\) forces a positive lower bound of order \(\delta^2 d^{3/2}\) for \((2d-2)\log M\) once \(d\log d\) is absorbed.
In a genuine integral Salem pole realization the positive root itself is the measure, so the estimate applies at bounded measure even without the more restrictive graph equation.
The earlier [two-center trace discriminant argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v275.md#cluster) supplies the same archimedean determinant bound at its stated scope.
The [graph bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v190.md#statement) gives the stronger \(O(2^B)\) count when its power identity is present.
The comparison with [Fili and Pottmeyer, arXiv v2, introduction p. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.01498v2) accurately identifies their field of algebraic numbers whose conjugates all lie in each specified finite normal local extension.
None of their numerical bounds is needed in this argument.

For the constructed example, the oddness of \(B\) excludes any nonidentity residue orbit paired with itself by inversion.
The two levels \([a](1+2[b])\) and \(5[a](1+2[b])\) have difference valuation two for a fixed \(b\), while reduction modulo four gives valuation exactly one for distinct \(b\)'s with the same \(a\).
Inverses have identical distances and lie over different residue orbits.
The full Frobenius-stable set has \(2Q(Q-2)\) points and a reciprocal monic polynomial with constant one.
Its derivative at each point has valuation \(2+(2Q-2)=2Q\).
For a perturbation modulo \(2^{4Q+1}\) the derivative retains this valuation and Hensel's inequality places a root in the same unramified field at distance of valuation at least \(2Q+1>2\).
The neighborhoods of this depth about the template points are pairwise disjoint.
This supplies \(d\) distinct roots of the perturbed polynomial without assuming any simple roots in the residual polynomial.

Over \(\mathbb F_3\) a primitive \((3^D+1)\)-st root has orbit length \(2D\), since no proper divisor of \(2D\) can exceed \(D\) and none of the positive \(3^j-1\) for \(j\le D\) reaches its order.
