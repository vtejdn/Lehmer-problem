## Growth of the least integer in the row ideal

The least positive integer in the row ideal satisfies (1) for every stated row at the computable width, and the proof establishes both divergences, the coefficient in (2), and the equivalence with a zero unrestricted infimum.
The exponent interpretation uses the class of one and works even for an infinite additive group.

For unequal labels, put \(x=v(d)\) and \(y=v(pq(c-a))\).
The smaller Smith valuation of the two-column matrix is \(x+\min(x,v(2))\), so subtraction from \(4x\) gives the claimed larger valuation.
When \(x>v(q(c-a))\), the adjugate jet identity gives \(v(m)+2y\ge3x-\min(x,v(2))\), which implies the asserted divisibility.
In the complementary case \(y\ge x\) suffices, including at primes dividing the exponents.
For equal labels, monic division at the two distinct nodes and the differentiated contact give \(d\mid V(z)\) and hence \(d\mid m\).
Contraction to \(\mathbb Z\) rounds each lower bound upward after division by the ramification index, clips at zero and takes the maximum above each rational prime.
Conversely these local conditions suffice by ideal factorization, and the nonzero product \(C^3\) supplies a finite divisor search.

For fixed indices and exceptional exponents, the relation between vectors of units modulo torsion determines at most one reduced prime ratio unless both vectors vanish.
In the latter case the positive equal Weil heights within either list force the absolute exponents to agree, which also excludes a zero exponent.
Primitivity then yields actual equality or inversion on the exceptional subgroup.
After the finitely many exceptional ratios for the two signs are removed, each sign has at most \(\min(k_1,k_2)\) solutions because either powered conjugate fixes the other uniquely.
For the remaining pairs, Proposition 2 bounds the finite-place gcd by an arbitrary multiple of a height of order \(\Sigma\), apart from finitely many points of bounded gcd.
The number of index pairs is fixed, giving uniformity over the dyadic primes for each fixed pair of seeds.

At each finite prime the largest weighted valuation retains at least the sum of all weighted valuations divided by \(2k\), less the sum of the cross-group minima.
Allowing all such pairs bounds the loss even as the maximizing indices change with the prime.
The unequal-label corrections cost \(O(\log(2\Sigma))\) for fixed seeds, and \(\sum_{\mathfrak l\mid\ell} e_{\mathfrak l}f_{\mathfrak l}=[L:\mathbb Q]\) yields the rational logarithm on summing the local inequalities.
The diagonal weight changes from three to one precisely for equal seeds, giving the subtraction of twice the diagonal logarithm in (5).

For each candidate width, exact algebraic comparisons and the finite divisor search determine its success at the current prime.
In the repeated-seed case the excess of \(3eq-2(q-p)\) over \(3e\Sigma/2\) is \((3e/2-2)(q-p)\ge0\).
Thus in both seed cases the fixed-seed error in (5) is eventually smaller than the slack in each fixed-width test, and the greatest passing width tends to infinity.
Every choice of qualifying contacts occurs on that list by the degree and weighted measure bounds.
The class products of the smaller-measure seed form a single orbit of distinct units of degree \(d=e_*/k_*\), and multiplication within a constant-modulus class preserves its contribution to the logarithmic Mahler measure.
Using \(k\le k_*\) and \(p\ge\Sigma/3\) gives the asserted uniform coefficient \(1/2\).
The compressed unit has positive measure and degree at least two, so both Voutier bounds and the strict inversion apply also at \(\eta=10^{-3}\).
Along every sequence in the claim, \(T_\eta\sim (4\eta)^{-1/3}\log((4\eta)^{-1/3})\) dominates \(\log(1/\eta)\), giving divergence after multiplication by \(\eta\) as well as the stated lower limiting coefficient.
The contact inequality forces a zero infimum for such a sequence, and the two-prime minima at the selected growing width eventually beat the initial candidate when the infimum is zero.
