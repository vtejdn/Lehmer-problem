The full statement holds with the given order of quantifiers, including the freedom to choose the prime and the compatible residue after choosing the degree.

For \(s=p/q\), the identities \(s+s^{-1}-2=1/M\) and \(q^{2D}s^D=M^D\) give the required pole functional on reciprocal coefficients.

At a prime power \(r^b\Vert M\), repeated binomial expansion raises the valuation of \((1+x)^{r^j}-1\) from \(b\) to \(b+j\).
The sole exceptional case in this computation is \(r=2,b=1\), excluded by \(4\mid q\).
Thus every target \(1+2x\) occurs, and reducing the congruence modulo \(r^{b+1}\) gives \(D\equiv2\bmod r\).
Consequently \(L\) is \(1\bmod r\) on the primes of \(q\) and \(2\bmod r\) on the odd primes of \(p\).
The extra congruences for the remaining primes up to \(q\) also give \(L\equiv1\bmod r\), and all the moduli involved have distinct prime bases.
This leaves an unbounded progression of degrees independent of \(B,\ell\).
I checked the illustrative powers \(5^{6982}\bmod256=9\) and \((-4)^{6982}\bmod625=616\).

Thus the logarithm of the capacity of the extended star is \(-(\log4)/L+2\log\cosh(g/2)\).
The lower bound at \(k=1\) tends to \(2\log\cosh(2U)>0\) at fixed \(q\), and applies to all the remaining \(k<q\) by monotonicity in \(R\).
In the neighborhood, each of these finitely many powered angle lists can be matched in cyclic order to its original list.
