## The all-prime intersection and the density of directions

The freshman's dream gives coefficientwise divisibility by \(p\) in the definition of \(J_p\).
A common residue root is nonzero because the constant coefficient of the section is one.
At its Teichmüller lift each factor belongs to \(pO\), and the product belongs to \(p^2O\).
For any lifted point above the curve, writing its value as \(y=pz\) gives \(J_p=\sigma(z)-p^{p-1}z^p\), which reduces to \(\sigma(z)\) even for \(p=2\).
The product rule and chain rule at the common root give the tangent equation, and primitivity supplies a projective direction modulo every prime.

I checked the component exclusion used from v805.

The middle coefficient prevents vertical components, and the weights of the constant, fourth-degree and middle monomials exclude every nonzero constant logarithmic tangent on the resulting irreducible curve.
At two and five the displayed graph pairs are distinct, and each graph has a simple zero or pole at a nonzero unramified coordinate as claimed.
Thus neither coordinate logarithmic partial vanishes generically on any component, and their ratio cannot belong to \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).
The derivative identities for \(J_p\) would force this ratio to equal its \(p\)-th power on any shared component.
The corner coefficients exclude boundary components of \(F\), so the bidegrees bound the total intersection length by \(16p\).
At a smooth eligible point the rational tangent vector annihilates both differentials, since the second evaluation is the \(p\)-th power of the first.
At a singular point the differential of \(F\) vanishes, so the linear parts again have rank at most one.
In each case the local intersection algebra has a nonzero cotangent space and length at least two, proving \(\#Z_p\le8p\) also in characteristic two.

Away from the listed exceptional primes both roots in \(t^2\) avoid the branch parameters, and a zero of the leading coefficient of the quadratic has unit linear coefficient.
Smoothness consequently gives one tangent congruence of index \(p\) per eligible point.
The character \((k,l)\mapsto v^k/u^l\) maps onto the group generated by the coordinates, of order \(h\) prime to \(p\), so its kernel and the tangent congruence intersect in index \(ph\).
The box densities for these lattices exist, and restricting their union to primitive pairs costs at most the reciprocal of the primitive-pair density.
For \(h\le Y\), the short monomial relation from the pigeonhole argument has both exponents bounded by \(\lceil\sqrt Y\rceil\).
After dividing out their greatest common divisor, the primitive normal defines translates with a nonzero rational tangent, including when the original relation has inseparable multiplicity.
The component exclusion and the bidegree bound therefore give \(O_F(\sqrt Y)\) distinct curve points per relation and \(O_F(Y^{3/2})\) in all.
Dyadic summation and the large-order bound give \(O_F((\sqrt Y+p/Y)/p)\), hence the claimed \(O_F(p^{-2/3})\) upon taking \(Y=p^{2/3}\).

At 19 the two irreducible quadratic quotients give \(u=v^2\) and \(u=v^8\), and the tests at exponents 20, 10 and 4 establish order twenty in both quotients.
