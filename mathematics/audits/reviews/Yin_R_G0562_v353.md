### Nonvanishing and covering multiplicity

After shifting each polynomial by a monomial, the fast exponents lie in \([0,h]\) for \(G\) and \([0,s]\) for \(B\).
Reduction of \(pi+bj\) modulo \(p\) recovers \(j\) in either interval, and the weight then determines \(i\).
Twisting by \(w^j\) with \(|w|=1\) changes no coefficient to zero.
Consequently every twisted diagonal of \(B\) has the same positive span \(d_B\), and the ordinary product on the diagonal gives \(d_B\le d_G\).
By comparing a monomial of \(G\) with one at its minimum base exponent zero, all base degrees are at most \((d_G+bh)/p\le d_G/p+h\).

The normalized factor \(B\) is separable and irreducible in \(\mathbb Q(X)[Y]\), by its positive fast width, nonzero constant row, Gauss's lemma and characteristic zero.
For \(D=pX\partial_X+bY\partial_Y\), distinct weights imply \(DB\ne0\).
If \(DB=QB\) in the Laurent ring, support containment and additivity of both coordinate widths force \(Q\) to be a single monomial with scalar coefficient.
The support of its translate of \(B\) can lie in the support of \(B\) only for zero translation, while a scalar derivative quotient would require one weight throughout \(B\).
Thus writing \(G=BV\) with \(B\nmid V\) gives \(B\nmid DG\).
In the same way \(B\nmid G_Y\), since \(B_Y\) is nonzero of smaller degree and \(B\) divides \(G\) simply.
Both resultant tests hold over the fraction field, as clearing a base denominator cannot introduce \(B\).
For a derivative \(T\) of actual degree \(e\) with prescribed degree \(q\ge e\), the product \(A_B^{q-e}\operatorname{Res}_Y(B,T)\) is a nonzero integral polynomial in \(X\).
This covers degree drops for both \(DG\) and \(G_Y\), including \(q=0\) when \(h=1\).

Discard the finitely many circle zeros of the two resultants, discriminant and nonzero row and extreme coefficients, and cut the circle once if necessary.
On a regular interval a simple unit branch stays on the circle, since reciprocal conjugation gives a second local root through the same initial value and simple-root continuation is unique.
Implicit differentiation at this root gives the signed phase derivative \(-DG/(uG_Y)\).
Both numerator and denominator are nonzero there.
For fixed \(w^p=v\), the \(p\) solutions to \(x^p=X\) give all \(p\) possible \(u=w x^b\) once.
Every regular point in the inverse image of \(v\) therefore gives a distinct root of the twisted diagonal, with at most \(d_B\) such roots.
On an interval with a lifted phase the derivative has constant sign, and change of variables counts every visit to each angle modulo \(2\pi\).
Exhaustion by compact subintervals gives (3) with normalized arc measures, including for the integrable singularity \((2/|1-v|)^\alpha\), \(0<\alpha<1\).

### Logarithms on fibers with both types of roots

The measurable-set inequality applied to \(G(X,e^{it})\) with the \(k\) occupied frequencies gives a tail \(\min(1,Ae^{-t/(k-1)})\) for the sublevel set at distance \(t\) below its log maximum.
The area under this tail is \((k-1)(1+\log A)\), which bounds the difference of the log maximum and the fiber mean and proves (5) by the coefficient integral.
The one-row estimate is an equality.
For a row of degree \(a\), its logarithmic derivative on the circle is a sum of \(a\) terms \(X/(X-z)\), with repetitions included.
Subadditivity of the square root and the uniform bound \(\int|\sin t|^{-1/2}\,d\mu<\infty\) after rotation of each \(z\) give \(\int Z^{1/2}\le1+Ck(d_G/p+h)\).
Taking twice the logarithm of this upper bound proves (6), since \(k\le p\), \(h<p\) and \(p,d_G\ge1\).

For \(T=DG\) or \(G_Y\) use respectively \(q=h\) or \(h-1\) and put \(t_i=\log|T(X,u_i)|-q\log^+|u_i|\).
The formula for the prescribed-degree resultant \(R_T\) gives exactly \(\sum_i t_i=\log|R_T|-q\,m_X(B)\).
Its right side is integrable, since each fiber mean has an integrable lower bound from its leading row and a uniform upper bound from the torus supremum.
The nonzero polynomial \(R_T\) has integrable logarithm and \(m(R_T)\ge0\).
For the first derivative the coefficient of \(Y^j\) is \(pXa'_j+bj a_j\), bounded in modulus by \(p(Z+h)|a_j|\).
For the second derivative it is enough to bound each \(j|a_j|\) by \(h|a_j|\).
Since \(|u_i|^\ell/\max(1,|u_i|)^q\le1\) for \(0\le\ell\le q\), these estimates and (5) bound every \(t_i\) above by \(m_X(G)+Ck+\log[p(Z+h)]\) or \(m_X(G)+Ck+\log h\), respectively.
The positive parts of these bounds are integrable.
The identity \(\sum_i t_i^-=\sum_i t_i^+-\sum_i t_i\) then gives integrability of the negative parts even after restriction to any measurable subset of the roots.
On neutral branches \(|u_i|=1\), so this proves absolute integrability of the logarithms of the speeds.

To estimate the negative log speed, use the negative logarithm of the full \(DG\) product, restore its off-circle terms, and add the neutral \(G_Y\) terms.
Exactly \(s\) copies of \(m_X(G)\) enter the two upper bounds together, for either sign of \(m_X(G)\).
Discarding \(-m(R_{DG})\le0\), integrating (6) and using \(Z+h\le Z(1+h)\) give (8).
When the mean neutral count \(M\) is positive, (3) bounds the integral of \((2/|1-v|)^\alpha J\) by \(C_\alpha d_B\).
Its log positive part is integrable by this bound, and its log negative part is bounded by \((\log J)^-\).
Jensen for the branch measure divided by \(M\) therefore gives (4) and proves finiteness of \(L(B)\).
For \(M=0\) the defining loss is zero.
Taking \(\alpha=1/2\) and using \(M\le s\), \(-M\log M\le1/e\), and \(d_B\le d_G\) gives the multipliers \(2h\) and \(2s\) in (1).

### Original and padded degrees and arbitrary offsets

For the unpadded lift \(s=h\) and \(B=G\), combining (1) with the v345 lower bound gives the coefficient \(1-(6h-2)/p\) of \(m(G)\).
Because \(m(G)\ge0\), decreasing it to \(1-6h/p\) is valid, and \(2h\log h\) is absorbed by \(O(h\log(2pd))\).
For fixed \(k\), \(h\log(2p)/p\to0\), whereas \(m(G)\ge\eta_k>0\).
Inequality (9) then forces \(h\log(2d)\ge c_kp\) on a shrinking sequence, and the prime-lift span bound gives the second inequality in (10).

For the padded lift, the accepted factor comparison gives \(m(B)=m(G)\ge\eta_k\), \(s\le h\), and a nonnegative discriminant measure.
The coefficient after substitution is \(1-(2h+4s-2)/p\), which tends to one, and the errors other than \(O(s\log(2d_G)/p)\) tend to zero for fixed \(k\).
Consequently \(s\log(2d_G)\ge c_kp\) eventually.
With \(G=B\), the row count is at most \(s+1\) and the resulting error is \(O((s^2+s\log(2pd_B))/p)\), including the discriminant cost.
If \(s^2+s\log(2D)=o(p)\), then \(s=o(\sqrt p)\), \(s\log p=o(p)\), and \(d_B\le D/(1-24s/p)\le2D\) eventually.
The surviving factor would force a positive lower bound on \(m(P)\) in this regime, including bounded \(s\) with \(\log(2D)=o(p)\).

In the two-row graph, a circle zero of \(f\) or \(g=f^*\) would be common to both by real coefficients and reversal, contradicting coprimality.
The sole root has modulus one and logarithmic differentiation gives the speed \(|K/(fg)|\) for either sign.
Here \(K(0)=-Sf(0)\operatorname{lc}(f)\ne0\), so all its logarithms are integrable and \(m(K)\ge\log(S|\operatorname{lc}(f)|)\) by Jensen at zero.
The coprimality of \(p,S\) suffices for the same covering argument with no restriction on \(S/p\).
The two extreme diagonal exponents \(0,pn+S\) survive every twist, giving span \(d=pn+S\).
The conditional grid product gives \(p(m(J)-m(f))=\log2-L\), since the fiber mean is \(\log|g|\) and \(m(g)=m(f)\).
Equations (3) and (4) with one branch therefore give (11).
The coprime nonconstant seed cannot be associated to its reversal, so the whole-polynomial plastic threshold gives \(m(f)\ge\log\theta\).
Inserting these estimates in (11) yields the displayed bound for \(\log(d/S)\) when \(\alpha p>2\).
After division by \(p\), the nonnegative \(\log|\operatorname{lc}(f)|\) can be discarded, and \(p\to\infty\) followed by \(\alpha\uparrow1\) proves (12).
The degree restrictions leave open sequences in which the indicated degree grows fast enough.
