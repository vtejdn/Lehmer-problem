## From distinct digit ideals to independent split-prime coordinates

The coefficient of the newest evaluation in the length-\(j\) signed product is the leading coefficient of \(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}(2^i-T)\), including the empty product at \(j=1\).
Successively solving for each newest ideal with exponent \((-1)^{j-1}\) gives equality of the two generated groups over the integers.
Inversion by \(\tau\) kills every fixed valuation and pairs the remaining ones with opposite signs.
Each split pair occurring in this group therefore occurs in some signed generator, with exactly one prime above it in that generator's reduced denominator.

In the real expansion the positive exponents on the majority roots sum to \(\log R\), with at least two summands, giving strict coefficient positivity by strict superadditivity of \(\sinh\) on positive arguments.
The upper bound \(4\sinh(a\log R)/a\le2mR^a\) gives the stated budget after filtering.
For \(a>h\) all factors of \(P_h(2^{-a})\) lie in \((0,1]\), while one factor vanishes at each of the preceding orders.
The later dyadic terms at order \(a\) have total weight \((B-1)/(2^a-1)\) relative to the current term, with strict inequality below one at every \(a>h+1\).
Thus the leading nonzero digit difference dominates all later differences, even when its absolute value is one.
At \(q=32\) the quotient's absolute log is bounded by \(2m(2R/32)^{h+1}/(1-R/32)<m\), also for \(h=0\), since \(R<2\).
An ideal collision gives a unit with reciprocal values at the two real embeddings and modulus one at the other embeddings.
Its positive absolute real logarithm equals its logarithmic Mahler measure times the extension degree from its own field to \(K\).
This contradicts least degree because \([K:\mathbb Q]=2u<d\).
The arguments in all \(B^k\) products range from exponent five through \(5+(k-1)+h=s\).

Every conjugate root in either integral evaluation has modulus at most \(R\), since the exceptional root has modulus \(R\), the other exterior roots have product of moduli \(R\), and the remaining blocks are neutral.
The absolute norm of either evaluation is consequently at most \((2^j+R)^d<2^{d(j+1)}\) for \(j\ge1\).
The norm of each prime is at least two, so both nonnegative valuations, and the absolute value of their difference, are bounded by \(d(s+1)\).
Keeping one row per split pair preserves the rank, and a nonzero minor of order \(r_s\) selects a projection injective on the whole rational span.
In any projected coordinate, varying a single digit on \([0,B-1]\) gives width at most \((B-1)d(s+1)\sum_i|p_i|\), regardless of signs.
Adding these widths and using \(\sum_i|p_i|=\prod_{i=1}^h(1+2^i)\le2^{h(h+3)/2}\) bounds the number of integral vectors by the product of one plus each width.
Comparison with \(B^k\) gives (1), with the stated harmless enlargement of \(B-1\) to \(B\).

For \(L=\log_2(2ds)\) and \(h=\lfloor\sqrt L\rfloor\), the stated threshold gives \(h+4\le s/2\), and \(h+1\ge\sqrt L\).
The denominator in (1) is bounded by \(2+h+h(h+3)/2+\log_2 k+\log_2(d(s+1))\), which is \(O(L)\) with an absolute constant because both logarithms are at most \(L\).
This proves the claimed rate and the lower bound on distinct split primes for the full signed prefix.
