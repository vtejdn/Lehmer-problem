## The half atom, the residual Fourier coefficients and the balanced measures

At 2026-11-20T21:27 UTC a reviewer outside development of [the claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v656.md#statement) independently inspected its full argument, its integer tests and both atom bounds.
The reviewer had independently examined [the moving Taylor word](Yin_R_G0562_v791.md#finding), a direct input, and received no development dialogue, author explanation or prior review of the present claim.

For the chosen \(N=\lceil\sqrt{h/m}\rceil\), both \(h/N\) and \(mN\) tend to zero.
Truncating the original window to \(h\) and applying the moving-word theorem with this new \(N\) gives the same digits eventually, since both integer words approximate the same moments uniformly to \(o(1)\).
Write \(t_i=q_i z^{-u_i}\).
At a good step \(w t_{i+1}=t_i\), including the factor \(\sigma\) from the signed period.
In \(N(1-w)D_N\) the two endpoint terms contribute at most two, and each of the at most \(2h\) breaks contributes at most two.
Since \(\|D_N\|_\infty\le1\), the ratio \(\|HD_N\|_2^2/\|H\|_2^2\to1/2\) forces asymptotic mass at least \(1/2\) in every closed arc neighborhood of \(1\), because the multiplier tends uniformly to zero on its complement.
The closed-set inequality for weak convergence and continuity from above give \(\lambda(\{1\})\ge1/2\).

For each fixed \(k\), at most \(2kh\) possible starts of \(k\) steps are spoiled by breaks.
Some run therefore ends at an index less than \(N\), and the normalized stripped remainders at its ends differ by a nonzero integral short polynomial.
Their signed translates of \(H\) differ, up to a unitary monomial, by \((1-w^{-k})H\).
Each stripping error has norm at most \(\sqrt{N-1}\).
Thus \(\|(1-w^k)H\|_2\ge\sqrt L-2\sqrt{N-1}\), using \(|1-w^{-k}|=|1-w^k|\).
Divide by \(\|H\|_2\), use \(L/\|H\|_2^2\to1\), and pass to the chosen weak limit with the fixed continuous test in \(w\).
This gives \(\operatorname{Re}\widehat\lambda(k)\le1/2\).
The pushforward by \(\sigma z^h\) commutes with conjugation, so these moments are real.

The residual measure \(\eta=2\lambda-\delta_1\) is positive of total mass one.
All its positive-index moments are nonpositive, so testing against the nonnegative Fejér kernel bounds the monotone sums of \((1-k/n)_+(-2\widehat\eta(k))\) by one.
The resulting absolutely convergent cosine series has constant coefficient one and coefficient \(\widehat\eta(k)\) at both \(k\) and \(-k\).
Fourier uniqueness gives precisely the displayed continuous density, which lies in \([0,2]\), hence the atom of \(\lambda\) has mass exactly \(1/2\).

Conversely every specified coefficient sequence gives a convex mixture of Haar measure and densities \(1-\operatorname{Re}w^k\) for the residual part.
Each latter constituent integrates \(|B|^2\) to half the squared integral coefficient norm of \((1-X^k)B\), by Parseval.
This product is nonzero and vanishes at one, so its squared norm is at least two.
If also \(B(1)=0\), the product has a double zero, which rules out norm squared two (a difference of distinct monomials has a simple zero at one) and three (three signed units have odd sum).
Haar itself gives squared norm at least two for such a \(B\), and at least one otherwise.
These bounds, with the atomic term \(|B(1)|^2/2\), prove the integer test for every measure in the asserted family.
No existence of polynomial inputs giving any member of the family follows from this converse.

In a balanced division translation of the short numerator leaves its squared boundary modulus unchanged.
Put \(a=DQ\), \(b=(1-D)Q\).
At equality the projection reconstruction gives \(H=-ca+o(m^{-1/2})\) in norm, with \(c=(1/\sqrt2)/(2-\sqrt2)=(1+\sqrt2)/2\).
The excess of squared integral coefficients above the support count is \(o(1/m)\), so the Fourier block underlying the commutator has scaled squared norm \(o(1)\) at each lag \(kh\) with fixed \(k>0\).
The orthogonal components \((1-D)X^{kh}a\) and \(DX^{kh}b\) of the commutator, with opposite signs, are separately \(o(m^{-1/2})\).
Cauchy–Schwarz annihilates the two scaled cross moments against \(w^k=\sigma^k z^{kh}\).
Complex conjugation interchanges the terms at negative \(k\), and their constant moments vanish by orthogonality.
The scaled cross measures have bounded total variation, so trigonometric approximation gives weak convergence to zero after pushforward, even with \(h,\sigma\) varying.
Extracting a limit \(\xi\) of the pushed-forward positive measures \(m|b|^2d\tau\), the decomposition \(Q=a+b\) yields \(\rho_Q=c^{-2}\rho_H+\xi\).
The same calculation for \(F=(1-c)a+b+o(m^{-1/2})\) gives \(\rho_F=(1-1/c)^2\rho_H+\xi\).
The total short mass tends to \(1/2\), giving \(\rho_H(\{1\})=1/4\).

The binomial \(1-\sigma^k X^{kh}\) is coprime to every admissible \(P\), including reducible \(P\), so the unrestricted lower bound applies after multiplying \(A\) by it.
The resulting inequality \(2K-2\operatorname{Re}\widehat\rho_F(k)\ge K\) and the Cesàro limit of \(w^k\) bound \(\rho_F(\{1\})\le K/2\).
Positivity of \(\xi\) gives the lower bound \(1/(4c^2)\) on the quotient atom.
Subtracting the two decompositions gives its upper bound \(K/2+(2/c-1)/4=(\log 2)/2\), since \(2/c-1=4\sqrt2-5\).
Both statements require the nonzero signed period.
