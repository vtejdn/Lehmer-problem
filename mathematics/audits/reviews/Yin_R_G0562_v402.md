## Energy at all annular widths and all exterior frequencies

At 2026-11-18T14:07 UTC an agent outside the development of [this row example](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v175.md#rows) independently checked both sections from the fixed input, without development discussion.
The reviewer had previously checked the [Dirichlet identity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#identity) and its [radial count formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#radial).

Set \(w=z^k\).
Each linear row in \(w\) has coprime coefficients, and their coefficient determinant is \(a^2-(a-1)(a+1)=1\).
The absolute resultant after substitution is its \(k\)-th power, with equal degrees so that homogenization gives the same result.
On \(|z|=1\) the squared-modulus difference is \(4a(1+\Re w)\), including equality precisely at \(w=-1\).
The row maximum therefore agrees with \(|V|\) throughout that circle, and every zero of \(V\) lies strictly inside it.
Its mean logarithm is \(\log(a+1)\), giving total energy \(2k\log(a+1)\).

On the \(t\)-fiber, \(w=(at-(a-1))/(a-(a+1)t)\).
For \(|t|=1\) neither numerator nor denominator vanishes.
Taking their squared moduli yields the stated quotient, which decreases in \(1-\Re t\) because its two affine coefficients are ordered strictly.
The endpoints \(t=1,-1\) give respectively radius one and \(((2a-1)/(2a+1))^{1/k}\).
Writing \(s=\log|z|\), the angular mean has zero derivative below the smallest logarithmic root radius and above zero.
Within that interval its derivative lies between \(0\) and \(k\) almost everywhere.
Integrating the square over the entire real line therefore gives at most \(k\log((2a+1)/(2a-1))\).
For any \(\tau>0\) the two disjoint intervals adjacent to zero in the radial count proof bound \(\tau(n_-^2+n_+^2)\) by this same mean-mode energy.
This remains true for widths depending on \(a,k\).
When \(a=\lceil e^p\rceil\), division by \(kp\) leaves total energy \(2\log(a+1)/p\to2\) and a bound \(\log((2a+1)/(2a-1))/p\to0\) for the mean mode, uniformly in all integers \(k\ge1\).

For \(r=|w|>1\) the squared-modulus difference is \((2a+1)r^2+4a\Re w+2a-1\).
Its minimum over the angle is \((r-1)((2a+1)r-(2a-1))>0\).
It follows that on the exterior \(g=\log(a+1)+\Re\log(1+qz^{-k})\), where \(q=a/(a+1)<1\).
The logarithm series gives the continuous boundary values even at the \(k\) equality points of the two rows, and its coefficients decay geometrically for each finite \(a\).
The boundary circle itself has area zero in the energy integral.
For a real mode \(A\Re z^{-m}\), the squared gradient in polar coordinates is \(A^2m^2r^{-2m-2}\) with \(r=|z|\), giving normalized exterior energy \(A^2m/2\).
Orthogonality in angle and convergence in energy justify adding these norms at frequencies \(kj\) with amplitudes \((-1)^{j-1}q^j/j\).
Their sum is \((k/2)\log((a+1)^2/(2a+1))\), whose ratio to the total tends to \(1/4\).
The first \(J\) positive modes have energy at most \(k(1+1/2+\cdots+1/J)/2\), a vanishing fraction of the full exterior energy for each fixed \(J\).

The small radial contribution here means the energy of the angular mean, the quantity that controls the annular counts.
The nonconstant exterior modes themselves have radial derivatives, whose energy equals their angular derivative energy by the sine and cosine integrals.
Thus the example distinguishes the annular counts from the total energy on integral rows of unit resultant.
Its construction provides no double-root witness on a specified nontorsion input.
