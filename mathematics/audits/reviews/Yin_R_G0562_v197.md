For a real adjacency eigenvalue \(t\), the two roots of \(X^2-tX+1\) have product one, and the larger modulus when \(|t|>2\) is \(\exp(\operatorname{arcosh}(|t|/2))\).
The total measure is the sum of these nonnegative contributions with their spectral multiplicities.
The absolute-value Rayleigh bound identifies the largest eigenvalue with the operator norm, and the squared norm of the adjacency matrix applied to a vertex basis vector is that vertex's degree.
Thus every degree is at most \((2\cosh B)^2\), including when all eigenvalues lie in \([-2,2]\).
Every finite graph with index greater than two contains a vertex-minimal induced graph of index greater than two.
This witness must be connected, and deleting a vertex and interlacing show its second eigenvalue is at most two.
It therefore qualifies for the small-witness classification quoted in McKee and Smyth, without any condition on the second eigenvalue of the original graph.
The finitely many witness graphs on at most ten vertices have a common positive lower bound \(\eta\) for their largest eigenvalue's contribution.
Deleting each witness together with its current neighbors makes their induced union block diagonal even in the original graph.
If there are \(k\) witnesses, interlacing forces \(k\) eigenvalues of the original graph to contribute at least \(\eta\) each.

When the deleted set \(S\) is nonempty, connectedness bounds the number of remaining components by the number of edges leaving \(S\), at most \(\Delta|S|\).

This gives the claimed bound on branch vertices in terms of \(B,\Delta\), also when nothing remains.
When \(S\) is empty the same classification applies directly.
In a connected graph of order at least two with \(b\) branch vertices, summing \(\deg u-2\) gives at least \(-2\), so at most \(2+\Delta b\) vertices are leaves.
After suppressing the degree-two interiors, counting incidences with multiplicity gives \(2e\le\Delta v\) even for returning paths.

The quadratic for \(t\) has a simple root there, so this accounts for every copy in \(P_G\).
