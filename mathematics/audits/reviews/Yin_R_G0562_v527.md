## Outcome of the proof review

For each fixed seed at its own degree, the claimed eventual periodicity holds for all positive integer exponents.
On every noncolliding progression genuine coprime rows exist eventually, and the comparison of the exact limiting rate with \(\log 2\) is strict.
The finite decision uses the specified certified group data and gives no effective onset.

### Relations, torsion and the two signed lines

The exponent map \(\mathbb Z^s\to G/G_{\rm tors}\) is surjective with kernel the stated relation lattice.
Its quotient is free, so this lattice is saturated and an integral basis extends to a unimodular basis of \(\mathbb Z^s\).
The images in \(G\) of the relation basis generate all of \(G_{\rm tors}\), including any torsion generated only by a product of representatives.
The complementary basis maps to a basis modulo torsion.
For any positive annihilator \(M\), its \(M\)-th powers generate \(G^M\) and are independent there; all are ordinary algebraic units.
The rank is positive because each conjugate is nontorsion.

If two powered conjugates coincide at \(p=a+Mn>0\), their ratio belongs to \(G_{\rm tors}\), its \(M\)-th power is one, and its \(a\)-th power is one.
The first identity gives equality of the exponent vectors in the free basis, and the second gives equality of the monomial constants, also when one or both roots are inverse representatives.
Conversely equal monomials impose equality at every positive exponent of the progression.
Coprime rows would recover both original roots as the same quotient of their values at the common node.
For example, \(P=X^4-4X^2+1\) has representatives \(\alpha=\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}\) and \(-\alpha\).
The square root has degree four since a square \((u+v\sqrt3)^2=2+\sqrt3\) with rational \(u,v\) would require \(u^2=1/2\) or \(3/2\).
Here the relation lattice is generated by \((1,-1)\) and \(M=2\) suffices.
The two representatives collide at every even exponent, whereas on the odd class the four Laurent nodes are \(\pm\alpha T,\ \pm\alpha^{-1}T^{-1}\) with \(T=(\alpha^2)^n\).

In a noncolliding class the \(e\) distinct Laurent monomials are distinct elements of the rational function field, regardless of their multiplicative relations.
Setting the free parameter \(N=0\) makes the signed value and derivative conditions equivalent to vanishing at all these nodes.
Their product polynomial in the coefficient variable is monic reciprocal with constant one, so each signed kernel has dimension one and the mixed edge expression in raw cofactors is nonzero.
The invertible parameter change \(N=a+Mn\) preserves nonzero polynomial coefficients.
Collecting equal torus exponents before specialization gives a polynomial-coefficient recurrence whose distinct bases have nontorsion ratios.
The local recurrence bound makes the rank witnesses, mixed edge expression and any removed nonzero common factor eventually nonzero along the actual progression.
Signed reversal gives both constraints at the inverse conjugates, identifying the specialized lines with the rational remainder kernels.
The \(e\) distinct powered nodes have the original degree, so the hypotheses of the two-line formula all hold.
In particular its nonzero edge gives a nonzero reflected determinant for mixtures of both lines, and the formula yields actual integral coprime rows including the parity restriction.

### Height and cutoff comparison

The polynomial coordinates must first be reduced on the chosen torus and residue class.
Clearing a common Laurent monomial and then dividing the polynomial gcd leaves no common nonconstant factor.
A surviving translation of the exponent supports has mean archimedean weight zero by the product formula for the basis units.
For each embedding the log maximum of the evaluated coordinates is \(n\) times the support maximum plus \(o(n)\).
Indeed division by the \(n\)-th power of a largest base in each entry permits application of Xiao's Lemma 4.1 even with several dominant terms.
The elementary upper bound on the normalized log modulus is \(O(\log(2n))\).

A nonzero constant entry bounds the finite common valuations by its fixed norm.
Otherwise one can choose an integral linear combination of entries avoiding all irreducible factors of a fixed nonzero entry, since a finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot contain all integer coefficient choices.
The common divisor of this pair bounds that of the tuple.
At \((a+Mn,\gamma_1^n,\ldots,\gamma_r^n)\) all finite almost-unit cost comes from the positive integer \(a+Mn\), giving \(\log(a+Mn)\), while the projective height of the point with leading coordinate one is at least \(n h_{\rm W}(\gamma_1)>0\).
For each fixed sufficiently small positive tolerance, a nonzero Laurent equation of Xiao's exceptional closed set vanishes at only finitely many of these points by the same recurrence argument.
The sum of coordinate heights is \(O(n)\), so taking the limit superior before letting the tolerance go to zero yields common finite contribution \(o(n)\).
These statements concern a fixed seed and fixed progression data.

The complex embeddings in the archimedean sum are counted individually, so its normalization is \(1/d_L\).
Projective height subtracts the common finite valuations from the archimedean sum.
Conversion from \(n\) to \(p\) contributes the additional \(1/M\), giving exactly \(R_{\varepsilon,a}\).
The height of the rational line is the logarithm of the coefficient maximum of its primitive integral generator; the one-norm costs only a bounded factor.
The two-line cost formula is between one half and twice the larger generator one-norm, so the limiting cost uses the maximum of the two rates.

For each embedding the ordering of the finitely many weights is the ordering of the positive real algebraic numbers \(|\sigma(\boldsymbol\gamma^{\mathbf u})|^2\).
The product of the chosen maxima is \(\exp(2Md_LR_{\varepsilon,a})\).
Each term is an ordinary algebraic unit times its complex conjugate and hence an ordinary unit.
Neither product can equal \(2^{2Md_L}\), whose reciprocal is not an algebraic integer.
These exact real algebraic comparisons decide the sign of \(R_a-\log2\), including when either support has ties.
Convergence and strict separation give the eventual answers, and the restriction to odd primes only takes a subsequence of those answers.

At fixed larger width \(w\), the same evaluation argument at \(N=0\) gives kernel dimension \(w+1-e\).
Its maximal minors, with complementary column signs, represent the exterior product of its rational kernel.
A basis of its saturated integral lattice has primitive wedge since the lattice is a direct summand of \(\mathbb Z^{w+1}\).
Its Euclidean length is the covolume, within a factor at most \(\sqrt{\binom{w+1}{e}}\) of the largest absolute Plücker coordinate.
The support calculation therefore gives the covolume rate; at rank greater than one it leaves the individual successive minima undetermined.
