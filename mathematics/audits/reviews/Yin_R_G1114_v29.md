## Distinct nontorsion preimages

The nonzero constants and coprimality make the rational map degree \(r+|k|\) and leave zeros and poles in \(\mathbb C^\times\).
If \(R(-z)=R(z)\), comparison of the lowest Laurent terms forces even \(k\).
Then \(F\mid F(-z)\) and \(G\mid G(-z)\), and equality of degrees and constant terms makes each polynomial even.
Iteration halves \(r\) each time, and every nonzero torsion point downstairs has exactly the corresponding power of two distinct torsion lifts.

For \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}\eta,\operatorname{ord}w)\) with \(w\) of odd order, the applicable exponents \(2,L/2+2,1+L/2\) are units modulo \(L\) in the three stated cases.
In the two even cases \(\eta^{L/2}=-1\) and \(w^{L/2}=1\).
The resulting conjugacies give precisely the three cleared equations, including the sign of the offset.
Either squaring identity would preserve the nonempty finite set of nonzero zeros and poles under inverse image by \(z\mapsto\pm z^2\), where every point has two distinct preimages.
The remaining sign identity was removed by the even descent.
Consequently the degrees \(4r_0,4r_0,2r_0\) give \(10r\) after lifting.
The graph polygon has two rows of width \(r\) at distance one, hence area \(r\), consistent with the cited all-orders comparison.

Across \(s\) disjoint target fibers there are \(sD\) preimages with multiplicities.
For \(k\ne0\) the local degrees at zero and infinity are both \(|k|\) and their images avoid the selected targets.
Riemann–Hurwitz therefore leaves at most \(2D-2-2(|k|-1)=2r\) excess multiplicities across the selected fibers.
For \(k=0\) the total excess is \(2r-2\) and omission of zero and infinity costs at most two distinct points.
Subtracting at most \(10r\) torsion points gives \(sD-12r\), including \(r+13|k|\) for \(s=13\).

In the half-degree construction \(r=\ell+1,\ k=-\ell,\ D=d+1\).
The two allowed constant terms in the encoded seed ensure a choice coprime to its reciprocal, and its coefficients give \(z^\ell f+g=(z+1)P\) exactly.
Both \(\mu_7\) and \(\mu_3\cup\mu_5\) have seven elements and are invariant under inversion.
Thus the convention that a zero of \(wf+z^\ell g\) maps to \(1/w\) gives the same unions of points in either case, with lower bound \(7(d+1)-12(\ell+1)=d-5>0\).
For the seventh-power polynomial the leading coefficient has modulus one, and a nonzero nontorsion root forces measure greater than one by Kronecker's theorem.
At \(y^d=w\), substitution into \(wf+z^\ell g\) gives \(y^\ell(y+1)P(y)\).
The count supplies positivity for the phase polynomial and leaves the size of the original cyclic resultant unresolved.

## Quantitative convergence and its transfer

Under the identity map the lattice condition of Proposition 4.10 is automatic.
With \(\alpha=1/2,\ \epsilon=(\delta/L)^2\), the one-variable constant and exponent yield \(24u^2(\delta/L)^{1/[2(u-1)]}\), also for complex coefficients.
The monomial case has the exact elementary logarithmic error recorded in the ledger.
Setting \(\delta=L\) bounds the largest coefficient of \(Df\) by \(e^{24s^2}M(f)\), and reversal up to sign gives the same bound for \(Dg\).
This use of the earlier proposition is independent of the later constant objection recorded in the source unit.

The two shifted integral vectors have unequal orders at zero when \(k\ne0\), and at \(k=0\) their independence follows by canceling \(D\).
Their nonzero integral minor has modulus at least one.
Replacing its first vector by the phase difference leaves the determinant unchanged and bounds it by \(2H\) times the largest remaining coefficient.
Hence each phase polynomial is nonzero, with coefficient maximum at least \(1/(2H)\), even when some terms cancel.
For \(t=2s,\ L_0=1/(2H)\), regularization at \(\delta=L_0\rho\) costs \(O_t(\rho^{1/[2(t-1)]})\) uniformly in the phase.
The regularized means are Lipschitz with constant \(sH/(2\delta)\) since the polynomial perturbation on the circle has norm at most \(sH|w-v|\).
Sampling centered arcs at \(j\)-th roots costs \(O_{s,H}(1/(j\rho))\).
Balancing proves the claimed exponent \(1/(2t-1)=1/(4s-1)\).

The discrete product is \(D^j V_j\) and \(m(D)=0\).
On the circle the two terms in the continuous phase integral have equal modulus, so the inner Jensen integral equals \(\log|Df|\) almost everywhere.
Subtracting from a constant upper bound permits Tonelli's theorem across the logarithmic singularities, and the resulting mean \(m(f)\) is finite.
These arguments require only the term and measure bounds in the statement and apply to both signs and all offsets.

For the moving Pisot units the seed measure is \(\theta\).
A fixed sufficiently large \(j=j(s,\theta_0,\Theta)\) gives \(m(V_j)\ge(j/2)\log\theta_0>\log2+1\).
The cited full-resultant transfer at this fixed multiplier gives the claimed lower bound after division by \(d\) when \(r^3\log(2r)\log(2d)=o(d)\), including sequences of unbounded negative offsets.
For the multinacci seed the multiplier \(1-z\) has measure one and gives exactly three nonzero coefficients, as required.

For the fixed eighth multiple, the monomial exponents of an eighth power of an \(s\)-term polynomial arise from weak compositions of eight among those terms.
Thus \(D^8V_8\) has at most \(K_8=2\binom{s+7}{8}\) terms, allowing all collisions and cancellations.
For the coprime Pisot seeds G0462v27 gives \(m(V_8)>0\) at each offset, and \(m(D^8V_8)=m(V_8)\).
Theorem 2 of Dobrowolski and Smyth gives a positive lower bound \(\eta_{K_8}\) for this nonzero integer polynomial, uniformly without a degree or coefficient-height bound.
This uses the source statement at its recorded scope and gives no numerical value for the bound.
The multiplier eight is absolute, the rate depends only on \(s\), and the stage at which the transfer error divided by \(d\) falls below \(\eta_{K_8}/2\) can depend on the moving-seed sequence.
The resulting lower bound for \(d^{-1}\log C_{8d}\) contradicts uniform decay at \(n\le8d\) along any sequence with original logarithmic measures tending to zero.
This application of G0391 requires only a positive normalized rate.

If the original reciprocal \(P\) has at most \(s_0\) terms, \((z+1)P\) has at most \(2s_0\), so the explicit coefficient formula for its seed gives at most \(2s_0+2\) terms.
With fixed upper bounds on \(s_0\) and \(M(P)\), regularization controls the largest coefficient of \(P\), hence its coefficient sum and the measure of the encoded seed by the encoding's coefficient-sum estimate.
That seed is monic, integral and coprime to its reciprocal, so it has a nonreciprocal irreducible factor with nonzero roots to which Smyth's bound applies.
Thus the sparse encoding has a uniform positive lower bound for \(m(V_j)/j\) for large fixed \(j\).
For \(d\ge6\), put \(t=2s_0+2\).
The seventh phase polynomial \(f^7+(z^\ell g)^7\) for the original sign has at most \(K_7=2\binom{t+6}{7}\) terms.
The first finding gives \(d-5>0\) distinct nonzero nontorsion roots and positive total logarithmic measure.
The same fixed-support theorem therefore gives \(m(V_7)\ge\eta_{K_7}>0\), equivalently a rate of at least \(\eta_{K_7}/7\) after division by seven, without any upper bound on \(M(P)\) or its coefficient height.
The encoded seed can have several exterior roots and its degree is \(d/2+1\), beyond the cited transfer range.
The original-resultant estimate above therefore covers the specified moving Pisot units, while the general sparse reciprocal application supplies the phase estimate.
