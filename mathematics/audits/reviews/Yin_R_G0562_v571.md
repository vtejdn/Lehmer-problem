### Exact jets and their ranks

Write \(E=x\,d/dx\).
At nonzero \(c\) the matrix taking ordinary derivatives of orders \(0,\ldots,t-1\) to the \(E^l\)-jets is triangular with nonzero diagonal \(c^l\).
Vanishing to order \(t\) is therefore equivalent to vanishing of \(Q(E)\) at \(c\) for every \(\deg Q<t\).
Multiplication by a monomial at \(c\) preserves the order before this test.
In the reversed graph, multiplication by \(x^{nk}\) changes the exponents at offset \(j\) to \(-nj,1-nj\), respectively.
Writing \(c^{-n}=R^{-n}z_c\) gives the two matrix entries \(Q(-nj)z_c^j\) and \(-cQ(1-nj)z_c^j\).

Suppose the log row norm divided by \(n\) is bounded by \(C\), and include every radius needed in the proof in the cutoff condition \((J+1)\min\log R>C+2\).
For fixed jet order \(l\), the maximum of \((1+nj)^l R^{-nj}\) over \(j>J\) is attained at \(J+1\) eventually.
This gives exponential decay in the original, unshifted sum after applying the norm bound.
Replacing the exponent index \(j\) by \(j-k\) in the derivative only changes the polynomial estimate, since \(k\le J\).
Division by the largest contribution of the maximizing block has exponential slope \(-L(\log R)\le0\).
The finite discarded terms have a strict slope deficit.
Consequently the absolute residual in each retained equation remains exponentially small, uniformly in the formal width along the extracted sequence.

For half-order \(v>0\), the coprime ideals \((s^v)\) and \(((s-1)^v)\) give \(2v\) independent Hermite interpolation conditions on a polynomial of degree below \(2v\).
The specified \(Q_{e,l}\) have leading term \((s-e)^l\) at \(e\), with error divisible by \((s-e)^v\), and vanish to order \(v\) at the other center.
Thus substituting \(s=e-Xj\) and dividing by \(X^l\) gives \((-j)^l\) at zero on the selected coordinate and zero on the other coordinate.
This includes \(l=0\) and \(j=0\).
The factors \((-1)^l\) and \(-c\) are nonzero and can be removed from the relevant equations at the specialization.
Since \(l<v\), the divided entries are polynomials and the real equations at \(X=n\) still have exponentially small errors.
For each coordinate the specialized equations now require zeros of multiplicities \(v_c\) at the phases \(z_c\).
At a separating positive exponent, the distinct phases give a divisor of degree \(m=\sum_c v_c\).
The homogeneous test at degree \(m-1\) has zero kernel.

I checked the residue-class quantifier in the determinant argument.
Choose a common modulus killing all torsion ratios of its phase bases and divisible by every original collision order.
Each class containing a separating exponent has a separating representative \(a_0\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\).
At \(X=0\) and phases of \(a_0\) the determinant just computed is nonzero.
At least one coefficient polynomial therefore remains after grouping the terms by equal \(q\)-th powers.
The surviving bases have modulus one and pairwise nontorsion ratios.
The [fixed-coefficient recurrence estimate with polynomial factors](../literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives eventual nonvanishing and inverse determinant of modulus at most \(\exp(o(n))\) on the finitely many admissible classes.
Polynomially bounded cofactors suffice.
If the maximizing interval had width less than \(m\), this inverse would send the residual vector to a coefficient vector tending to zero, despite its maximum coordinate being one.
Summing the requisite jumps \(m\log R\) counts each exterior root with its half-order and gives \(S=\sum_i u_i h_i\).

### The last equation at one odd-order node

After lowering the order of \(P_i\) by one, the base half-orders cost \(S-h_i\).
At this limiting cost the integrated jump formula forces exactly the base jumps, no other positive breakpoints and \(\gamma_0=0\).
The integral initial pair \(w_0\ne0\) has coordinate heights \(o(n)\).
Across the block at \(R\), scaling by \(gR^{-nj}\) matches its last coefficient to the first coefficient at the next radius, since the next product \(g\) includes \(R^{-nm}\).
At formal \(X=0\), the coordinate polynomials with prescribed constant pair \(y_0\) are \(y_0 L_R(Y)/L_R(0)\), where \(L_R=\prod_c(Y-z_c)^{v_c}\).
This uniquely solves the \(2m\) equations on the remaining pairs also when \(y_0=0\), so these Cramer determinants obey the same subexponential inverse bounds.
Thus the errors stay exponentially small after finitely many transfers.

Put \(v=u_i-1\) and \(Q(s)=s^v(s-1)^v\).
The remaining order \(2v+1\) permits \(Q(E)\), whose degree is \(2v\).
At the two centers the coefficients of \(X^v\) in \(Q(-Xj)\) and \(Q(1-Xj)\) are respectively \(j^v\) and \((-j)^v\).
In particular the terminal functional on the exact specialized solution is proportional to \(y_{0,1}-(-1)^v c_*y_{0,2}\).
Its scalar is nonzero because the Euler derivative of order \(v\) of \(L_R\) at the chosen phase equals \(z_{c_*}^v L_R^{(v)}(z_{c_*})\), which is nonzero at an exact order-\(v\) zero.
For \(v=0\) it is ordinary evaluation and \(L_R(z_{c_*})\ne0\), including an empty block.
The preceding transfers multiply the pair by nonzero scalars at zero.
Clearing their polynomially bounded determinants leaves the same nonzero terminal direction there.

On an infinite admissible class the grouped coefficients of the cleared row, after application to \(w_0\), have height \(o(n)\).
The small-coefficient estimate of v436 forces every such coefficient to vanish along the extracted sequence, since their sum with the powered phase bases is exponentially small.
The sum of the grouped polynomial rows and each of its conjugates therefore annihilate the same nonzero rational pair at infinitely many \(n\).
Their polynomial determinant vanishes at zero.
The terminal specialization gives \(c_*=\tau(c_*)\) for every automorphism of a containing normal field, since \((-1)^v\) is rational.
This contradicts \(c_*\) being an exterior algebraic unit.
Failure of an eventual uniform strict gain above \(S-h_i\) would supply the sequence just excluded.

### Genuine rows, all widths and the fixed-order budget

In the integral jet count the total weighted log measure is \(2S\) and the dimension lost is \(J=\sum_i2u_i\deg P_i\).
At width \(w\), putting \(l=2(w+1)>J\) gives leading normalized log norm \(Sl/(l-J)\), with the fixed derivative overhead \(O(\log n/n)\).
A sufficiently large fixed \(w\) leaves the required slack below all strict gains.
At a separating \(n\), the minimum of \(c^n\) has the original degree and log measure \(nh_i\).
Its pullback under \(X\mapsto X^n\) removes one order at the \(P_i\) roots and none at the other selected roots.
Thus a common powered minimum in the two rows would, upon restoration to a largest quotient row, require original slope at least \(S+\eta_i-w\log(2)/n\).
This rules out common vanishing.
The graph forces both values nonzero at each selected node, and the two reciprocal projections preserve all orders with an integer linear combination avoiding the finitely many numerator zeros.
The width-one sign adjustment and the reciprocal padding multiplier cost only bounded norm factors.
This first proves the fixed-width estimate and then the upper limit at any diverging width, without a growth restriction for that limit.
The existence of a least norm follows by integrality.
At an exponent with colliding powers the two distinct original roots would be recovered as the same rational-function value, which is impossible.

For common even order \(a\), take \(u_i=a/2\).
The strict budget is \(2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2nb+1)<(a-1)nB\), with \(B=(\log2)/4\).
The sum condition \(\sum_i h_i<(1-1/a)B\) is exactly the slack \(2S<(a-1)B\).
Each individual value is small enough to admit a reciprocal unit representative, and the norm replacement preserves that measure while giving a unit whose positive powers all generate its field.
Distinct measures and those preserved degrees preclude collisions across the orbits.
Now \(\log b=o(n)\) keeps the width term within the strict slack also after padding.
If \(\lambda<(1-1/a)B/[r(r+1)/2]\), factorization supplies a positive irreducible value \(h\) below this threshold, and its first \(r\) positive integral multiples are distinct irreducible values with sum below \((1-1/a)B\).
This gives the stated contrapositive for every infinite subsequence.
The fit of the strict budget requires fixed \(a\), and the proof supplies no infinite sequence of exclusions.
