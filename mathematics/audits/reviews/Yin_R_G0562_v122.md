## Complex fibers, translated sources, pole measures and exceptions

At 2026-11-17T02:08 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin inspected the complete C345v122 submission fixed at 2026-11-17T02:07 UTC.
The reviewer had no part in its development and received the fixed submission without its development discussion or an earlier verdict on this version.

The C345v117 normalization gives \(B^m S(A/B)=\prod_{S(y)=0}(A-yB)\), a monic integer polynomial of degree \(sm\).
Its value at a pole \(b_i\) is \(A(b_i)^m\ne0\).
Each root \(u\), with its multiplicity, contributes the reciprocal quadratic \(z^2-uz+1\) to the full lift.
Its contribution to logarithmic measure is the nonnegative \(\ell=\log |z|\) for a larger-modulus member of the pair, also for coincident roots at an endpoint.
Writing \(z=e^{\ell+i\theta}\) gives \(\Re u=2\cosh\ell\cos\theta\) and \(\Im u=2\sinh\ell\sin\theta\).
The addition formula implies \(\sum\sinh\ell\le\sinh(\sum\ell)\le\sinh L\) on each fiber.
The imaginary part of the sum of its \(s\) trace roots is \(k\Im y\) by the coefficient of degree \(s-1\), so all source imaginary parts tend to zero with \(L\).

For \(x\ge t=2\cosh L+2\sinh L+\delta\), the absolute argument of each \(x-u_j\) is at most \(|\Im u_j|/(t-2\cosh L)\).
Their sum is bounded by \(2\sinh L/(2\sinh L+\delta)<1<\pi/2\), hence the real part of the product is strictly positive.
It is exactly \(A(x)-(\Re y)B(x)\) because \(A,B\) are real at \(x\).
On the negative half-line, applying the same computation to \(u_j-x\) introduces \((-1)^s\).
This localizes the entire real fiber over \(\Re y\), including when the original fiber has a multiple nonreal root or \(L=0\).
The derivative and one-sided pole limits of \(g\) give one real solution on every branch, placing every pole between the first and last solutions and giving the strict comparison at \(-t,t\).
Direct subtraction of the pole terms then gives
\[
  D<\frac{2t}{k}-\sum_i\frac{2t w_i}{t^2-b_i^2}
     \le\frac{2t}{k}-\frac{2W}{t}.
\]
For the affine case \(s=1\) the sum is empty, while \(k=1\) would already be an excluded integer translation.

Taking \(L,\delta\to0\) gives limiting width at most two for \(k\ge2\) and at most three for \(k=1\), because the latter has positive integer \(W\) from division by monic \(B\).
In particular width \(13/4\) is an eventual bound with room at both thresholds.
After centering, \(q=13/16\) places the real parts in \([-2q,2q]\).
The monic quartic in the submission takes the values \(2q^4\cos(4\theta)\) there, and \(2q^4<1\).
Uniform continuity on a fixed compact rectangle thus gives the required single \(\rho<1\) throughout a sufficiently thin complex neighborhood.
The column of degree \(4j+h\) is monic in the original variable even when the midpoint is nonintegral, and has Euclidean norm at most \(8\sqrt m\,\rho^j\).
The accumulated exponent \(\sum_{i<m}\lfloor i/4\rfloor=m^2/8+O(m)\) dominates the \(O(m\log m)\) logarithm of the other factors.
The Vandermonde determinant's modulus is at least one by the nonzero integral discriminant of the monic irreducible source, proving the absolute degree bound for the source along the proposed sequence.
Translating by the nearest integer bounds all conjugates uniformly and retains \(A-NB\) monic, coprime to \(B\), with precisely the same poles and slope.
Only finitely many translated source polynomials remain, and a fixed one on a subsequence must be totally real as its imaginary parts tend to zero.
It is still irreducible of degree at least two, exactly the hypotheses of the C345v120 strict span bound.
Its fixed \(D>2\) excludes infinitely many \(k\ge2\), then excludes \(W\ge2\) by \(2t-4/t\to2\).

In the resulting \(k=W=1\) case, every coefficient in the inverse-power expansion of \(x+c-g(x)\) is integral by monic division.
These are precisely the moments of the positive unit-mass pole measure.
For fixed \(T>2\), eventually \(t_n<T\) and all pole measures are supported in a fixed closed interval strictly inside \((-T,T)\).
The function \(2T/(T^2-x^2)\) decreases in the parameter \(T>|x|\), so weak convergence and the strict width estimate give its limiting integral at most \(4-D\).
Sending \(T\downarrow2\) by monotone convergence gives \(\int4/(4-x^2)\,d\mu\le4-D<2\), including exclusion of endpoint atoms.
For each fixed \(j\) and more than \(j\) distinct poles, the monic polynomial \(V_j\) cannot vanish at all poles.
Thus \(\int V_j^2\,d\mu_n\) is a positive integer, as in C345v121, and its limit is at least one by weak convergence on a common compact interval.
The pointwise Cesàro means of \(\sin^2((j+1)\theta)\) tend to \(1/2\) for every \(0<\theta<\pi\).
Dividing by \(\sin^2\theta\) is justified by the limiting integral bound.
The average of the limiting moments is consequently at most \((4-D)/2<1\), contradicting their lower bounds.
This excludes simultaneous \(L\to0\) and \(2sm\to\infty\).
Failure of the asserted pair of uniform constants would give exactly such a sequence by taking \(L\le1/n\), \(2sm>n\).
At bounded degree and bounded measure the coefficient bounds give finitely many monic full outputs, so their positive measures have the stated gap and the zero cases form a finite set.
This conclusion concerns full outputs, allowing multiple descriptions of the same output after a source translation.

For \(g=x+c\), \(S(Y)=H(Y-c)\) gives the inverse trace \(H\) exactly.
In particular a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity for primes \(p\ge5\) has an irreducible trace polynomial of degree \((p-1)/2\), and this construction gives its full cyclotomic lift in unbounded degree even with irrational sources.
For \(S(Y)=Y\), the choice \(A=H,\ B=H'\) for monic squarefree totally real \(H\) is coprime, has slope \(1/\deg H\) and has simple poles by strict Rolle interlacing.
Differentiating \(H'/H\) at each pole gives \(H''(b)/H(b)<0\), so the residue \(H(b)/H''(b)\) has the required sign.
This yields \(H\) as the full inverse trace, including Salem trace polynomials.
Independently, choosing \(H(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(s\theta)\) gives a nontranslation for each \(s\ge2\) and the neutral full lift \(z^{2s}+1\).
Both exclusions are therefore substantive even for the degree bound at measure one.
