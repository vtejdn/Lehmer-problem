## Verification of the pencil and the odd-prime obstruction

Substitution into the two binary graphs interchanges their equations under inversion because the torus coordinate \(t\) is inversion invariant.
Adding their equations at a common point would give \(V=1\), where either equation has value one.
The first section polynomial has constant term one and degree \((q+1)^2\), and its derivative can vanish at a root only at zero or one, both excluded.
Distinct first-graph roots have distinct traces since the other root with a given trace is its inverse on the second graph.
This accounts for the full degree of the monic trace polynomial.

Multiplying the difference of the two invariant values by \(XYZ\) yields \((X+Z)((Y^2+1)XZ+Y^2+Y+1)\).
At \(Y=X^q,Z=X^{q^2}\) the product over the labels is therefore \((1+X^{q^2-1})G\).
The constant term one and the monomial pairwise differences separate all labels, and the simple roots of \(X^{q^2-1}-1\) remove exactly one copy even at double intersections.
The squared-coordinate map permutes the remaining roots by squaring their unique label.
A root over a label in \(\mathbb F_q\) has relative degree at most \(2(q+1)\), hence absolute degree at most \(2a(q+1)\), and its absolute degree is divisible by the absolute label degree.
The proper subfield labels number at most \(\sum_{j\le a/2}2^j\), each contributing at most \(2(q+1)\) roots.
The first factor sum reaching \(B\) is less than \(B+2a(q+1)\), eventually below \(n/2\) since \(a(q+1)=o(B)\) and \(B=o(n)\).
At an odd integer value of the polynomial all modular divisors evaluate to one, and at a nonzero even value the integer divisors one and two supply both residues.
The census imposes no condition at a zero value.

For the zero label, a nonzero trace \(s\) gives norm \(s+\sqrt{s}+1\), and applying the absolute trace to its quotient by \(s^2\) cancels the terms \(s^{-1}\) and \(s^{-2}\), leaving \(\operatorname{Tr}(s^{-3})\).
Trace one gives precisely two conjugates and trace zero gives none with that prescribed nonzero relative trace.
The singleton at zero trace and the character count therefore remove \(1+q-S_a\) distinct points.
Cubing permutes the field for odd \(a\).
For even \(a\), orthogonality in the square of the sum retains exactly the four elements satisfying \(v^4=v\), each contributing \(q\) since its cube is zero or one and \(a\) is even.
The congruence \(S_a\equiv1\pmod3\) selects the stated sign.
The numerator obtained by putting \(Xw(X)-Yw(Y)\) over a common denominator is \((X-Y)D_0(X,Y)\), with no pole at a remaining root or its conjugate.
Thus \(xyz\) is fixed by the relative Frobenius and \(x^{q^3}=x\), while the diagonal equation excludes every base-field root.
For label one the shifted norm-power equation has two nonzero simple targets, neither equal to one, each with \(q+1\) simple nonzero shifted preimages.
In even degree all these points lie in the quadratic extension by norm surjectivity.
In odd degree none lie there, and none lie in the cubic extension since it contains no primitive cube root.
The divisibility \(3(q+1)\mid q^6-1\) follows from \(3\mid q^4+q^2+1\) and \(q+1\mid q^2-1\), giving relative degree six.

For odd \(p\ne5\), the quadratic discriminant in the geometric reduction has a simple zero away from the ramification of the substitutions, and adjoining the independent variable \(X\) preserves irreducibility.
The two generic roots are nonzero and distinct, and their ratio cannot be torsion since the sum of that ratio, its inverse and two equals the nonconstant \(1/(A_0B_0)\).
An equality on the correspondence would make the coefficient-twisted function in its second argument invariant under scaling by this ratio, so its divisor would be supported at zero and infinity.
Invariance then forces the exponent of the resulting monomial to vanish, and independence of \(X,Y\) rules out the resulting equality since composition of nonconstant rational functions is nonconstant.
This also handles functions of just one coordinate and inseparable rational functions.
Clearing denominators preserves generic nonvanishing on the quadratic, so its resultant with the cleared equality is nonzero over the rational function field in \(X,Y\).
At any defined solution both polynomials vanish at the specialized \(Z=x^{q^2}\), forcing the polynomial resultant to vanish even when a leading coefficient specializes to zero.
The finitely many coefficient twists bound both partial degrees uniformly, and for sufficiently large \(q\) distinct exponent pairs give distinct exponents after \(Y=X^q\).
The finitely many smaller \(q\) can be absorbed in the implied constant.
The degree of the monic section is \(2(q+1)^2\) and its constant term is one.
The derivative gcd bound applies since \(\gcd(q,q^2+1)=1\), and bounds the excess of degree over the distinct torus-root count also when a multiplicity is divisible by the characteristic.
These checks establish the submitted conclusions at the stated scopes.
