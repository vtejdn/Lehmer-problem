## Exact norm, irreducibility at every exponent and the degree cap

At 2026-11-24T20:28 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v22.md#statement), both proof sections of C0901 v22 and the [twenty-factor certificate](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0186/readme.md#below-two).
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical and computational inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings on v22.
The stated strict upper bound holds for every permitted \(B,N\), including with the specified linear total-degree cap.

The supplied verifier reproduces both degrees, the value at one and the claimed integer one-norm.
For an independent calculation the reviewer used the two multiplicity tables directly and formed \(e_j=\sum_{n:\,j\mid n}c_n\mu(n/j)\).
Since all orders exceed one, the product for \(C\) is \(\prod_j(1-X^j)^{e_j}\).
The degree is \(\sum_n c_n\varphi(n)=3908\), and \(\sum_j e_j=0\) gives \(C(1)=\prod_j j^{e_j}=71052345981129072096647871486492672000000000000=2^{104}3^{15}5^{12}\).
Starting from the exact coefficients of \(L^{160}\), multiplication by \(1-X^j\) uses subtraction of the \(j\)-th previous coefficient in descending order, and division uses addition of that coefficient in ascending order.
All operations were performed with integers through degree \(5508\), which suffices by the independent degree count.
The resulting polynomial is monic and reciprocal, its coefficients sum to \(C(1)\), and the sum of their absolute values is

The strict margin is \(7\cdot2^{160}-10H=16039460550527003259659901684548799459482779712>0\).
It gives the exact strict logarithmic comparison in (1), and the triangle inequality bounds every circle modulus by \(H\).

For the positive Salem root \(\alpha\) of \(L\), the first catalogue entry, any putative prime root \(\beta\in K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) would generate the same field, since \(\alpha=\beta^p\).
It would be a unit of total measure \((\log\alpha)/p\).
Voutier's degree bound and the catalogue bound \(\alpha<49/37\) give \(p<17\) as in the power certificate, leaving \(2,3,5,7,11,13\).
For each of the six exponents \(p\) in that order, the reviewer recomputed \(L(a)\), \(L'(a)\) and \(a^{(\ell-1)/p}\) modulo the specified prime \(\ell\).
All six first values were zero, the derivatives were \(3,48,29,29,3,14\), and the final residues were \(22,37,57,20,13,65\).
In each case \(p\mid\ell-1\), so Hensel lifting gives an embedding into \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) at which the residue of \(\alpha\) cannot be a \(p\)-th power.
This excludes \(\alpha\in K^p\) for every prime \(p\).
The real embedding of \(K\) containing the chosen \(\alpha>1\) also excludes \(\alpha=-4u^4\), including when \(4\mid N\).
Thus \(Y^N-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(K\) for every \(N\ge2\), and any root has absolute degree \(10N\) because its \(N\)-th power generates \(K\).
It follows that \(L(X^N)\) is its full minimal polynomial, with \(N=1\) supplied by the original Salem minimum.
All \(N\) lifts of each original root together contribute its original positive logarithmic modulus, so substitution preserves total logarithmic measure and reciprocity.

Every primitive root \(\zeta\) at an order in \(B\) has \(\zeta^N=1\), giving \(P_N(\zeta)=L(1)=-1\) and \(C(\zeta^N)=C(1)\ne0\).
The first equality gives unit absolute resultant and the second excludes every forbidden order from the monic cyclotomic product \(C(X^N)\).
For \(a=160j+r\) with \(0\le r<160\), the indicated witness has norm at most \(H^j9^r\) and total degree \(N(5508j+10r)\), at most \((5508/1600)a(10N)\) including \(j=0\).
Its log norm divided by \(a\) has upper limit at most \((\log H)/160\), with no dependence on \(B\) or \(N\) in the norm estimate.
Every allowed product has norm at least \(\exp(a\,m(L))\) by additivity of measure.
Under the linear total-degree cap the admissible set is nonempty at every \(a\), and multiplying two witnesses respects the cap, so the constrained log minima are also subadditive.
Taking the full forbidden set of divisors of \(N\) in the power-substitution formula gives equality with the order-one rate on \(L\), while forbidding only \(B\) permits at least the same cofactors.
The comparison to the original order-four rate concerns a different input of the same measure.
The proof supplies a strict upper bound and leaves the exact order-one rate open.
