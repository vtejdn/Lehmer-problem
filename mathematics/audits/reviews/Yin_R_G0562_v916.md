# Orders of torsion translations and relative degrees of powers

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T05:57 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete [C345 v775 argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing this proof.

Put \(H=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\), \(K=\mathbb Q(z)\).
The extension \(H(z)/H(z^j)\) is the splitting field of a separable binomial, and its automorphisms act faithfully by multiplication by \(j\)-th roots of unity fixed by the base.
Its order \(l\) divides \(j\), so \(\delta=z^l\) is fixed and generates \(H(z^j)\) over \(H\) because its \(j/l\)-th power is \(z^j\).
The degree of \(z\) over \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\) is at least \(l\) by scalar extension and at most \(l\) by \(X^l-\delta\).
This exactly cancels the factor \(l\) in \(h(\delta)\), for nonintegral \(z\) as well.
For a finite \(L/\mathbb Q\), the identity \([HL:H]=[L:L\cap H]\) follows on a finite Galois closure of \(L\) by using the normal subgroup fixing its intersection with \(H\).
Its application to \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\) and \(K\) gives equal degrees for their two abelian intersections, and inclusion then gives equality of the fields.
Thus \([\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]=sD_j\) with the original \(s\), even when \(z\) itself generates a larger field.

The rational-base Amoroso–Delsinne estimate at \(\delta\) has relative degree \(D_j\), so multiplication by \(sD_j\) leaves the stated factor \(sG_4(D_j)\).
Under integrality and \(m(z)\le1\) the positive power \(\delta\) meets exactly the hypotheses of C345 v726 with that same abelian subfield and total measure, which gives the cubic factor.
The relative degrees over positive powers form a set of positive integers with an attained minimum.
A bounded minimum along a subsequence would give a fixed positive lower bound on total measure.

Multiplicative translation preserves the property that a geometric component is a subtorus coset.
A binomial component of the original rationally irreducible polynomial would force all conjugate components to have parallel supports and their product to have zero Newton area.
Consequently the complex translated curve has finitely many torsion points, and the Beukers–Smyth bound applies with its original Euclidean area \(V\).
The orbit of the ordered pair over \(K\) has size \([K(\xi,\eta):K]\).
The two translations generate the full cyclotomic field of the least common multiple \(w\) of their orders, since they generate the group \(\mu_w\).
This field is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\) and its intersection with \(K\) lies in \(K\cap H\), giving \(\varphi(w)\le22Vs\).

Specializing along the primitive direction gives a nonzero equation for \(z^g\) over \(H\), since an identically zero section would again give a coset component.
Its width bounds its ordinary polynomial degree after monomial removal and is \(O_F(n)\), including either coordinate axis.
Positivity on compact intervals and eventual decay and monotonicity of \(G_3,G_4\) give \(G_i(D_g)\gg_F G_i(n)\).
When \(m(z)<\log2\), the positive primitive leading coefficient and the absolute constant coefficient of its minimal polynomial are both one, by the product formula for polynomial measure and reciprocity.
Thus every shrinking sequence eventually qualifies for the cubic bound and forces \(n\to\infty\), giving the asserted little-oh bound on \(\varphi(w)\).

For growing \(n\), C345 v769 supplies both the uniform scale \(h(z)\asymp_F (gn)^{-1}\) and the exact norm degree \(\varphi(w)W\), with \(W\asymp_F gn\).
For a root of an arbitrary nontorsion irreducible rational factor, the coefficient-conjugate section containing it has the same orders and area.
Dividing its measure lower bound by its individual height and the norm degree yields the stated positive fraction of order \(G_4(n)\), or \(G_3(n)\) under the monic integral small-measure hypothesis.
Summing the degree fractions counts repeated factors with their actual multiplicities.
These estimates allow the individual fraction to decrease along growing primitive directions.
