## The compressed degree, the two independent units and the residual families

At 2026-11-16T22:41 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the complete C345v65 submission dated 2026-11-16T22:36.
The reviewer received the fixed proof without development dialogue, author explanation or an earlier verdict on it and had no role in its development.

The maximal torsion-quotient classes in C345v61 have common size \(f\) and constant modulus on each class.
Their products therefore preserve the total exterior product, multiply the individual class log moduli by \(f\), and give one distinct root per class in an irreducible reciprocal polynomial without further torsion collisions.
In particular radial rank one survives compression.
In the rest of the argument \(d=|\Omega|\) and the number of exterior roots both refer to that polynomial.
Its distinct roots give distinct vectors in the torsion-free lattice tensored with \(\mathbb R\), so \(d\) counts the weights, with no stabilizer multiplicity.

For the positive generator \(r\) of the log-modulus group, the integral weights \(w(x)=\log|x|/r\) have greatest common divisor one.
The identities \(x\overline x=\kappa^{w(x)}\) give the actual unit \(\kappa=e^{2r}\) by Bézout.
Choosing any exterior root gives \(w(x)h(\kappa)\le2h(x)=2\log M(P)/d\).
The C345v63 reflection at \(a=[\kappa]\) acts on exact units as \(x\mapsto x\kappa^{-w(x)}\).
Its signed translates preserve the original weight orbit, have integral pairings and span the ambient space by the Kronecker argument for a lattice element orthogonal to all translates.
If two translates give the same root vector, their conjugate reflections agree on all weight vectors and hence on the corresponding actual roots by compression.
Their two lifts raised to the transported pairing exponents therefore agree, and the greatest common divisor of those exponents is one.
This is the uniqueness needed to bound the joint field degree by the number of images of an ordered pair of vectors.
Each lift is a conjugate of \(\kappa\) or of its inverse and hence has height \(h(\kappa)\).
Two linearly independent vectors give multiplicatively independent lifts.
Positivity of \((a,[x])\) is equivalent to \(|x|>1\) at the original embedding.

The finite group generated by Galois and inversion acts isometrically and transitively on the \(d\) weights and transitively on the \(t\) root components.
Reflections from different components act independently because each fixes the orthogonal complement of its component.
For common support size \(p\) and \(B\) distinct supports, their union meets every component, so \(Bp\ge t\).
For any one support, the separate Weyl actions give at least the product of the individual projection orbit sizes, all on that same support.
Different supports give disjoint sets of weights.

In \(A_{N-1}\), \(N\ge3\), a nonzero sum-zero vector has a nontrivial level set, whose placements give at least \(N\) permutations.
In \(D_N\), \(N\ge5\), for \(1\le j<N\) nonzero positions, permutations and parity correction at a zero position give at least \(\binom Nj2^j\) weights.
With all positions nonzero the \(2^{N-1}\) even sign changes are distinct.
These lower bounds are at least \(N\), and for \(p\ge2\) one obtains \(d\ge BN^p\ge tN^p/p\ge tN^2/2\).
The last inequality follows from \(N^{p-2}\ge p/2\) for \(N\ge3,\ p\ge2\).

For \(p=1\) all \(t\) components are supports.
In type \(A\), the lines having one exceptional coordinate are intrinsically the lines of shortest vectors in the dual root lattice.
Indeed the coordinate differences there are integers and \(N\|v\|^2=\sum_{i<j}(v_i-v_j)^2\), whose shortest nonzero patterns have one exceptional coordinate differing by one from the rest.
If one weight is on such a line, every weight is on one of its translates at the same distance from the origin.
Against \(a=e_1-e_2\) only the positive orientation of the first exceptional coordinate and the negative orientation of the second can contribute positively, with all other components orthogonal.
Thus this case has at most two exterior roots.
Otherwise either all coordinates are distinct or some level has multiplicity \(2\le j\le N-2\), giving at least \(\binom N2\) placements also for the small case \(N=3\).
In \(D_N\) the intrinsic coordinate axes are distinguished by \(2(N-1)\) roots pairing to two with \(\pm2e_i\), versus six for a four-position vector of squared length four.
Again only two fixed-length points on those axes pair positively with \(a\).
Off the axes, \(2\le j\le N-2\) gives at least \(\binom N2\) placements, and \(j=N-1,N\) gives at least \(2^{N-1}\ge\binom N2\).
The assumption of at least three compressed exterior roots therefore yields \(d\gg tN^2\) in both classical series for \(p=1\) as well.

Two roots of pairing \(-1\) in one component are independent.
For an \(A_{N-1}\) root the second root has \(2(N-2)\) possible choices with that pairing out of the \(N(N-1)\) roots.
For a \(D_N\) root these counts are \(4(N-2)\) choices and \(2N(N-1)\) roots.
Thus the field of the ordered pair of actual lifts has degree \(D\le8tN^3\ll d^{3/2}\), since \(t\ge1\).
For \(D_4,E_6,E_7,E_8\) an adjacent pair also exists, and there are at most 240 roots per component.
The \(D_4\) model has 24, and the exceptional embeddings in the \(E_8\) lattice have at most \(4\binom82+2^7=240\) norm-two vectors.
Consequently \(D\le240^2t\le240^2d\) without a coordinate-axis assumption in \(D_4\).

For components of type \(A_1\), support size one gives at most one fixed-length weight positive on the axis of \(a\).
With at least three exterior roots one therefore has \(p\ge2\).
Take roots on two axes in one support.
Their ordered Galois images lie over an ordered pair of distinct axes within some support, with at most four sign choices per ordered pair.
Thus \(D\le4Bp(p-1)\), while independent reflections on the \(p\) occupied axes give \(d\ge B2^p\).
Since \(p(p-1)/2^p\) is bounded, this also supplies two independent lifts with \(D\ll d\), even though every separate root component has rank one.

Theorem 1.6 of Amoroso and David concerns the product of individual absolute logarithmic heights for multiplicatively independent algebraic numbers and uses the degree of their joint field.
The reviewer checked its statement and the exponent in the [authors' text](https://amoroso.users.lmno.cnrs.fr/files-heightGmn/Crelle.pdf#page=4) and used the published source unit at statement and applicability depth.
The argument takes the dimension to be two in every application, with \(\kappa(2)=3(3!)^2-2=106\).
Thus the product \(h(\kappa)^2\) has a lower bound \(c(2)D^{-1}(\log(3D))^{-212}\).
Taking square roots and combining with the height upper bound gives \(\log M(P)\gg dD^{-1/2}(\log(3D))^{-106}\).
All the ordered-pair degree bounds have absolute constants, so \(D\ll d^{3/2}\) also gives \(\log(3D)\ll\log(3d)\).
This proves the asserted \(d^{1/4}/(\log(3d))^{106}\) lower bound.
Its value is positive at every positive integer degree and tends to infinity, so it has positive infimum over the possible compressed degrees.

With one compressed exterior root, complex conjugation forces it to be real.
Changing its sign if necessary preserves Mahler measure and gives a Salem number or a reciprocal quadratic unit greater than one.
In the latter case the integer trace is at least three, giving a fixed positive gap.
With two real exterior roots, proportional positive logarithms give equal even powers after integer rescaling.
Since the roots are conjugate with the same nonzero height, the two positive exponents must coincide, contradicting the absence of torsion quotients.
The only remaining two-exterior case is therefore one nonreal conjugate pair.
Both this whole family and the Salem family have radial rank one before any compression, giving necessity of the two residual gaps.
Conversely those gaps, the quadratic bound, and the proved estimate for at least three compressed exterior roots cover the original class by exact preservation of measure.
The reviewer finds the quantitative bound and the equivalence proved as submitted.
