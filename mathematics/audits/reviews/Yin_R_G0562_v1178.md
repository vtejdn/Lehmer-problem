# Review of the inverse bound for the measure of any integer polynomial

<a id="finding"></a>
## Arbitrary factorization, merging actual constituents and primitive equality

At 2026-11-25T01:59 UTC an agent outside the development of v118 independently reviewed the [full statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#statement), its three proof sections and the cited fixed inputs for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed version without its development discussion.
The quantitative bound and primitive equality case extend to arbitrary nonzero integer polynomials with the stated sufficiently small positive total measure.

**Taking all factors with their original multiplicities.**
Removing any power of \(X\) preserves measure.
Both the leading coefficient and the resulting nonzero constant coefficient have absolute value at most \(M(P)<2\).
Their integrality forces both to have absolute value one, eliminating integer content and giving monic irreducible factors up to sign, all with unit constant coefficient.
The torsion factors contribute zero logarithmic measure.
Taking one root \(w_j\) per occurrence of a positive-measure factor gives \(m=\sum_j m(w_j)\) with every summand positive, so every one satisfies the same smallness cutoff.
This works uniformly without a bound on the length of the list.
The exterior-product characterization gives \(|gM(w_j)|\le M(w_j)\), with equality only if \(g\) fixes that actual exterior product.
An equality \(|gA|=A\) requires equality separately at each occurrence.
Thus \(A>1\) is a strict Perron unit and no two distinct conjugates of \(A\) share a torsion class, by transporting one to the distinguished embedding.
Every positive power has the same degree and field.

**Gluing the constituents as subspaces.**
Use the absolute Galois action, or its common finite image on the finite list at hand.
Each chain contributes a direct sum of pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible subspaces, of strictly descending dimensions along the chain.
The leading module of each term \(z\) is faithful for its actual compressed splitting field \(L_z\), also in the oriented case with a nonzero space of block constants.
Consequently isomorphic leading modules from different chains have equal kernel fields.
The two-field comparison applies uniformly to the corresponding small-measure terms, because every such term has measure at most \(m\) and positive bounded-degree measures cannot tend to zero.
It forces matching, which makes the actual conjugate spans equal by taking the spans of proportional conjugate vectors.
In that common multiplicity-free space there is a unique simple subspace of highest dimension.
Thus the two leading modules coincide inside the rational multiplicative class space.
Conversely matching terms have this same leading subspace.
This comparison prevents a second isomorphic copy from entering the combined space, even if two chains have only a common terminal part or arise from repeated factors.

After removing the coincident subspaces, the sum is direct by semisimplicity.
The orbit span of \([A]\) is precisely the sum of its nonzero components, since all the remaining types have multiplicity one.
The kernel field of this orbit span is the compositum of the active \(L_z\), by intersection of kernels.
It is the ordinary splitting field of \(A\) because \(A\) is already compressed.
At least one component is active since \(A>1\), and the largest active compressed degree \(d\) is well-defined for the finite support.
Its chosen term \(z\) satisfies \(0<m(z)\le m(w_h)\le m\) for an actual source occurrence \(h\).

**Quantitative estimate without a coordinate match.**
Put \(D=\deg A\).
For \(D\le d^6\), the leading projection has orbit size at most \(D\).
The sign-support or majority-coordinate argument marks at most 36 labels, so the stabilizer of the full \(A\) has orbit of size at most 72 on a root \(z'\) above a marked label.
The full class nonmatch makes \(A,z'\) multiplicatively independent.
Their joint absolute degree is at most \(72D\), and the two-coordinate height product yields \(m(A)m(z)\gg d/(\log(216D))^{212}\), sufficient for (3).

For \(D>d^6\), all other active representatives are unmatched and have degree at most \(d\), including ties.
The unmatched-field theorem on the labels of \(z\) permits a lift of a supported three-cycle fixing all those splitting fields.
Its fourth power has order three, fixes the off-triple actual roots, and acts nontrivially on the leading module.
Its conjugates in the active compositum still fix each of the other normal fields, and there are at most \(16\binom{d/2}{3}\) possibilities for their actions on \(z\)'s roots.
If every conjugate preserved the rational line of \([A]\), the only rational cube root of one being one would force the normal closure to fix the nonzero projection and then its irreducible span.
This contradicts the nontrivial action.
For a conjugate \(\tau\) witnessing independence, the stabilizer of \(A\) sends \(\tau A\) through at most that conjugacy-class size.
The joint degree is \(O(Dd^3)\), without dependence on the number of input factors.
Applying AD99 to \(A,\tau A\) of equal height gives \(m(A)^2\gg D/(d^3(\log(3D))^{212})\).
Eventual monotonicity in \(D\), followed by the Voutier lower bound on \(m(z)\), proves (3) also in this range.
The choice \(m(z)\le m<1\) keeps the degree cutoffs and numerical constants uniform over all chain lengths and multiplicities.

The forward degree inversion uses \(t=\log d>(4m)^{-1/3}\log t\), so \(t>(4m)^{-1/3}\log((4m)^{-1/3})\) at small \(m\).
Substitution into the logarithm of (3) proves (1) for every fixed \(\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\).
Under \(m(A)\le B\), (3) gives \(\log d\le\log B+O(\log\log B)\) for \(m<1\).
Applying Voutier's strict inequality at this degree gives the coefficient \(1/4-o(1)\) in (2).

**Match, equality and recurrence exclusion.**
For a match choose \(z'\) with class on the line of \([A]\).
Complex conjugation fixes that class, hence fixes \(z'\) by its compression.
The point stabilizers of the two classes coincide and each is the stabilizer of its actual number, so \(\mathbb Q(A)=\mathbb Q(z')\) at this real embedding.
The only torsion in this field consists of the two signs.
The positive primitive unit \(\rho>1\) on the common line satisfies \(A=\rho^j\) and \(z'=\pm\rho^k\) with \(j\ge1,\ k\ne0\).
Every positive power of \(\rho\) generates the common field, since fixing that power fixes the nonzero proportional class of \(A\) and hence fixes \(A\).
Degree-times-height therefore gives \(m(\rho)=m(z)/|k|\le m(w_h)\).
The strict Perron property descends from \(A\) to \(\rho\), and the fixed Smyth threshold forces reciprocity for this small unit and hence its powers.

If \(A\) is primitive in its own field, \(j=1\).
The single real exterior value then gives \(\log A\le m(A)=m(\rho)\le m(z)\le m(w_h)\le m=\log A\).
All are equal, so all the positive log measure comes from the single occurrence \(h\).
A repeated positive-measure irreducible factor would have supplied a second positive occurrence in the same sum.
Also \(M(A)=A\) and \(\deg A=d\to\infty\) as \(m\to0\).
Reciprocity therefore gives the inverse interior root, all remaining roots neutral, and a genuine real Salem number.
In particular a sufficiently small primitive Salem value of an integer polynomial must be in this equality case: its own logarithmic measure equals its logarithm and cannot satisfy the growing lower bound.
Arbitrary monomial and cyclotomic factors remain possible.
For nonnegative recurrences with the fixed coefficient-sum bound from v117, the unique positive root has nonreciprocal minimum and its own measure is bounded by that of the parent.
This triggers (1) against any integer-polynomial preimage at sufficient proximity to one, independently of the preimage factorization.
