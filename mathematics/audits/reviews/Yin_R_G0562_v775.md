## The finite estimate and its small-measure constant

At 2026-11-20T20:20 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently checked the full [claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#statement), the reflected polynomial and short-width estimate it uses, and the cited comparison and rounding applications.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and the requested scope without development dialogue, author explanations or prior findings about this proof.
The finite lower bound holds for every admissible numerator and denominator, including reducible polynomials and polynomials with repeated roots.

In the division at position \(j\), the low truncation of \(A/P\) matches \(A\) below \(j\) after multiplication by \(P\), and it contributes nothing at or above \(j+d\).
The high truncation matches \(A\) at and above \(j+d\) and contributes nothing below \(j\).
Both assertions include the empty truncations at the endpoints.
The leading and constant units of \(P\) give integrality, and a difference of two remainders of degree below \(d\) cannot be a nonzero Laurent multiple of \(P\) because widths add under multiplication.
Each remainder represents \(X^{-j}A\) modulo \(P\) and is nonzero even when \(P\) shares a proper factor with \(A\).
The two truncations use fixed expansions for all \(j\), so crossing one place increases the difference of their support counts by zero, one or two.
Between its initial nonpositive and final nonnegative values this difference must lie within one of zero at some window.
The short-width estimate applies at that window even when the first nonzero remainder coefficient has positive exponent.

Reflection of each interior root places that root in \(O^*\) with the required multiplicity, giving analyticity of both \(U\) and \(V\) and diagonal magnitudes \(t=e^{-m}\).
The exterior roots of \(O\) put \(R/O\) in \(H^2\), while \(R/O^*\) is regular outside the closed disk and vanishes at infinity.
Thus \(Y\) is orthogonal to \(H^2_-\) and \(JH^2\), which are themselves orthogonal because \(J\) is analytic.
On the circle \(Y+Z=U X^{-j}A/P\), giving the required norm identity.
At the selected negative positions only inputs at the same or lower positions can contribute, and at the selected nonnegative positions of \(\bar VQ\) only inputs at the same or higher positions can contribute.
Starting at the corresponding extreme includes every contributing occupied input.
Each \(l\)-dimensional compression has all singular values at most one and their product equals \(t^l\).
This bounds each selected output norm below by \(t^l\) times its input norm.
The two squared bounds add by orthogonality, for independent truncation lengths and also when one side is empty.

For \(n>k\) there are at least \(k\) integer coefficients of absolute value at least one on each side, which gives \(2kt^{2k}\) for \(k>0\).
The case \(k=0\) follows from positivity, and the empty quotient gives \(E=r^2\).
For \(1\le n\le k\), \(u^2=1-t^{2n}\le1/2\).
If \(ub\ge\sqrt C\), completing the square leaves \((1-u^2)b^2\ge C\).
Otherwise the quadratic increases in \(r\) above \(\sqrt C\).
The geometric sum gives \(4Cu^2<2n\), while balancing and integrality give \(b\ge\sqrt{2n-1}\).
The derivative of \(b^2-\sqrt{2n}\,b\) is positive on this range, and \(\sqrt{2n(2n-1)}<2n-1/2\) bounds the loss by \(1/2\).

Finally \(2kt^{2k}\ge k\ge(\log 2)/(2m)-1\), and \(m\le\log2-1/2\) makes this last expression at least \(1/(4m)-1/2\).
Since \(C\le1/(4m)\) and \(mC\to1/4\), the estimate is uniform in the numerator, degree and root multiplicities.
The leading constant \(1/4\) improves the previous \(\log2-1/2\) on its irreducible domain.
For \(k=0\), (1) gives only a trivial finite bound.
Nearest-integer rounding subtracts a Laurent multiple of \(P\) and therefore preserves the nonzero residue class.
In the matching application \(B^2\) is coprime to \(P\), so both the original square and every rounded representative satisfy the estimate.
