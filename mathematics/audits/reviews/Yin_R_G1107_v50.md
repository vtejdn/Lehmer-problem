Thus \(D(b_j)/B_n'(b_j)=(4-b_j^2)/p\), proving the formula and nonnegativity for the neutral residues even at a zero of \(E_{s-1}\).

Multiplication by \(t-a\) therefore leaves moment equality for test polynomials through degree \(L-3\).

Consequently \(\prod_j (4-b_j^2)=p\) and \(\prod_j|D(b_j)|=1\) follow from the two endpoint evaluations of \(B_n\) and \(D(b_j)^2=2+b_j\).

For fixed \(\epsilon>0\), an approximation error \(\eta\) on \(1/(u+\epsilon)\) costs at most \(\eta(\mathbb E X_j+\mathbb E|G_j|)\le128\eta\) in the moment comparison.
Taking the sequence limit before sending \(\eta\) to zero gives the expectation bound for \(Y_j\).
The identity \(\log Y_j=\log X_j-\log(f_s(b_j)+\epsilon)\), the product bound and the same quadrature give (4).
All grid denominators are at least \(\epsilon\), including at common zeros of the neutral trace factors and \(B_n\).
On the continuous interval the factors \(2+2\cos\theta\), \((2\cos\theta)^2\) and \(4\sin^2(s\theta)\) each have logarithmic mean zero.
Hölder's inequality with three exponents equal to three bounds the mean of the product raised to the power \(-1/12\).
The first two resulting integrals have at most inverse square-root singularities, and the third has the same integral at each integer \(s\) by periodicity.
This gives the claimed uniform \(O(\epsilon^{1/12})\) bound for the regularized logarithmic mean, which permits \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) after the sequence limit.

For \(y\ge1\), integrating \((u-1)/u\) from \(1\) to \(y\) bounds \(\psi(y)\) below by \((y-1)^2/(2y)\).

Thus the stated Cauchy–Schwarz step uses only the bounded first moment of \(Y_j\) and makes the limiting mean distance of \(X_j\) from \(f_s(b_j)\) zero.
Since \(\mathbb E X_j=\mathbb E G_j\) and \(f_s\ge G_j\), the mean distance of \(G_j\) from \(f_s(b_j)\) also tends to zero.

Hence the factor multiplying \(2+a\) is \(4p/(p-1)\), forcing \(a\to-2\) along the assumed sequence.

This contradicts integrality uniformly over all coefficient heights.
