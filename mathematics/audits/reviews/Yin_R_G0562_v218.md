## Finding on the three local factorizations and the conditional global estimates

The reviewer took no part in its development and received no development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or earlier findings on this submission.
The review used the fixed proof and its cited inputs, with the reviews of the earlier two-grid and lifting results available for those dependencies.
The local factors exist for both signs at all three primes, the measure estimate applies to every rational divisor of the specified degree, and the four resultant values apply to a divisor realizing the prescribed reductions.
Existence of either kind of rational divisor remains open.

For fixed \(h\), the field contains all conjugates of the radicals and of \(\zeta_{2h}\).
Complete splitting therefore gives the prime-field reductions at both ends of the proposed gap.
Away from \(2h\), the reductions of the \(2h\)-th roots of unity remain distinct, so the reduction of the chosen primitive root has order \(2h\).
For each base \(\ell=2,3,5\), an \(h\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) has order dividing \((p-1)/h\) for either split prime \(p\).
The fixed-field Chebotarev density and the prime number theorem give \((\delta_h+o_h(1))x/\log x\) split primes in \([x,2x]\), with \(\delta_h>0\).
The shortest internal gap is at most \(x\) divided by one less than their count.
Thus \(g=O_h(\log x)\), \(2h\mid g\), and \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) follows for any requested \(C\) by choosing \(x\) after \(h\).
This argument uses the established prime-counting inputs at their stated interfaces and does not re-audit their original proofs.

Since \(g\) is even and coprime to \(q,r\), the torsion classification removes exactly \(X+\varepsilon\).
At 2 and 3 the remaining \(q\)-grid is simple and disjoint from \(X^g+1\), as every root on the grid has order divisible by \(q\).
At 5 each root on the corresponding grid has order divisible by \(r\), whereas roots of \(X^{2g}+1\) have orders dividing \(4g\).
Writing a grid root as \(-\varepsilon\zeta_p^j\), Frobenius and inversion act on \(j\in(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times\) by multiplication by the residue characteristic and by \(-1\).
The orbit length is the order of the subgroup generated by the base and \(-1\), hence is the least common multiple of the multiplicative order and two.
The even integer \((p-1)/h\) is divisible by this length, so the number \(J\) of orbits is divisible by \(h\), and \(2J/h\le J\).
The selected degrees are \(2(q-1)/h\) at 2 and 3 and \(2(r-1)/h\) on the grid at 5.
At 5 the missing degree is \(2b\).
Writing \(Z=X^{2b}\), the complementary quotient of \(Z^h+1\) by \(Z+1\) is nonzero at \(-1\), since its value is \(h\bmod5\).
Thus \(X^{2b}+1\) takes its full multiplicities and is coprime to the complement even when \(5\mid b\).

For \(v=v_2(g)\), an exceptional root of order \(m\) satisfies \(m/\gcd(m,2g)=2\), so \(v_2(m)=v+2\).
Its Frobenius-inversion orbit has size divisible by the order of the image generated by \(5,-1\bmod 2^{v+2}\).
The identity \(v_2(5^{2^i}-1)=i+2\) follows by induction since \(5^{2^i}+1\) has valuation one.
Consequently 5 has order \(2^v\), and inversion doubles the subgroup order since powers of five are \(1\bmod4\).
Every reciprocal exceptional divisor over \(\mathbb F_5\) has constant multiplicity on each orbit, so its degree is a multiple of \(2^{v+1}\).
The deficit for numerator one is \(g/h\), whose dyadic valuation is \(v\).
This excludes numerator one under the stated restrictions of a common fraction on the two grids and zero exceptional degree at 2 and 3.

Inverse pairing of each selected multiset gives constant one, and none of its roots equals \(1\) or \(-1\).
Unique monic coprime Hensel lifting applies even when the first reduction has repeated roots, because only coprimality between the two reductions enters the linear correction equation.
Normalized reversal fixes each lift by uniqueness, and evaluation at 1, where the first lift has unit value, forces its constant coefficient to equal one at all three primes.
The complement is then reciprocal with constant one by cancellation.
Both factor degrees agree across the three characteristics, so coefficientwise Chinese remaindering preserves the product and its reciprocity at every positive triple of precisions.

A monic rational divisor is integral by Gauss's lemma.
All its irreducible constituents have positive integer shares \(s_i\) in the partition of \(g\), so there are at most \(g\), and their summed degree error is at most \(g/[3(2g+1)^2]\).
Adding \(2(g-1)/(hr)\) still gives less than \(1/g\) for \(r\) in the stated large-delay range.
The fractions \(\sum_i s_i/g\) and \(2/h\) both lie in \(g^{-1}\mathbb Z\), so they agree.
In particular there are \(2g/h\) exterior roots in each of the two blocks.
The individual measure errors sum to \(O(g^2\exp(-cr/(2g+1)^2))\), with absolute constants since the block primes are fixed.
As \(h\to\infty\) one has \(g\ge2h\), so this error is \(o(1/h)\).
The positivity of the constituent measures ensures that any rational divisors of this degree along such a sequence, including reducible ones, would give positive measures tending to zero.

Now assume the rational divisor realizes the selected reductions.
Its common degrees with the two integral grid polynomials are \(d,d,d-2b\) at 2, 3 and 5, respectively.
The even exponent \(g\) eliminates the extra dyadic term on the \(r\)-grid, giving absolute norms \(6^d\) and \(5^{d-2b}\).
Evaluating the parent on \(X^g=2,3\) gives absolute values 15 and 40, respectively.
By multiplicativity these exclude every other prime from the binomial resultants.
At 2 and 3 the \(q\)-part of the order survives the \(g\)-th power and rules out any common root with the binomials.
The same argument excludes the \(r\)-grid at 5.
Of the roots of the exceptional factor, exactly \(b\) counted with multiplicity reduce to each specified value of \(X^g\), because \(X^b=\pm2\) and raising to the odd power \(h\) permutes \(\{2,-2\}\) in characteristic five.
A lifted root \(z\) above this multiset has \(z^r+\varepsilon\) a unit by disjointness and exclusion of \(-\varepsilon\).
With \(Y=z^g\), the identity \((z^r+\varepsilon)(Y-2)(Y-3)=-5\varepsilon(Y^2-1)\) gives valuation exactly one for the vanishing linear factor even in a ramified extension, with \(v_5(5)=1\).
Monicity ensures that the reduction counts the lifted roots with multiplicity, yielding valuation \(b\) and absolute resultant \(5^b\) for each binomial.
