## The proof and its sharpness example

Reviewed at 2026-11-20T19:34 UTC for Sheng Yin.
I checked the complete fixed claim in version 625 independently of its development, with the statement and proof as inputs and without development dialogue, author explanations or previous review conclusions.
The argument proves both degree estimates uniformly over the stated class of complex polynomials, and the reciprocal integer family attains the exponent \(1/2\).

At zero the Lagrange basis element for \(\rho\) equals \(-P(0)/(\rho P'(\rho))\), which gives (3) with its stated sign.
The sums of the positive and negative logarithmic radii have equal absolute value \(m\), so every \(y_\rho\le m\) and the sum of the \(x_\rho\) is two.
Dividing \(|c_\rho|\) by its summand in \(T\) gives \(\sqrt{|1-r_\rho^{-2}|/m}\le\sqrt{2}e\sqrt{x_\rho}\).
This proves (4) and, by the constant interpolation identity, the absolute lower bound on \(T\).

The selected roots have \(t_0h_0\le2\) even when \(J=0\), while every later group has \(t_j<2y_\rho\) at each of its members.
On the \(j\)-th circle each reflected pole lies strictly outside and the boundary modulus of its factor is \(\sigma_j/r_\rho\).
Summing the logarithmic bounds therefore costs at most \(2t_0h_0+(1+\lambda)4m+2m\le4+(4\lambda+6)m\).
On the smaller disk the Cauchy tail is bounded by \(M_j e^{-(n_j+1)\lambda_jt_j}/(1-e^{-\lambda_jt_j})\).
At a selected root, the growth of the preceding product from the unit circle is at most \(e^{t_jD_{j-1}}\), precisely the loss covered by the term \(D_{j-1}/\lambda_j\) in the truncation order.
The later factors stay within their smaller disks, and the discarded roots lie within every such disk since \(m\eta\le\eta\).
Including the factor for each index gives total weighted error at most \(2Be^\varepsilon C_0T\varepsilon<1\), which contradicts (3) whenever \(D_J<d\).

Iteration of the recurrence gives
\[
 D_J\le(1+1/\lambda)^J\bigl(1+A/\eta+J+A/(\lambda\eta)\bigr).
\]
Here \(J=O(1+L)\), \(A=O(1+L)\), and the geometric sum of the reciprocal later radii parameters \(1/t_j\) is at most \(1/\eta\).
For the prescribed \(\lambda\), both \(\lambda m\) and \((\log R)/\lambda\) are \(O(1+\sqrt{m\log(2+R)})\), and \(1+L=O(1+\log R)\) uniformly in \(m\).
These estimates give (1), including when there is only one group.
For \(R\le\sqrt{2d}\), bounding the logarithmic factors in (1) by their values at \(\sqrt{2d}\) and using \(R\le(1+C_0)T\) gives (2).
The complementary range follows from the same comparison with \(T\), and the logarithms of both correction factors are \(o(\log d)\) uniformly for \(m\le1\).

In the real quadratic field, a cube root of \(\alpha\) would be an integral unit of norm one and have trace strictly between two and three.
The relative norm of \(\alpha^{1/q}\) is \(\alpha\) at each odd-degree induction step, so a cube in that extension would give the excluded cube in the base field.
The resulting tower gives degree \(2q\) over the rationals.
All roots are simple, the two sets of radii are \(\alpha^{\pm1/q}\), and the logarithmic Mahler measure is \(\log\alpha\).
Using \(|Q'(\alpha^{\pm1})|=\sqrt5\) and \(|1-\alpha^{\pm2/q}|\sim2\log(\alpha)/q\) in the stated derivative formula gives \(T/\sqrt q\to(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})/\sqrt{10}=3/\sqrt{10}\).
This confirms sharpness at fixed positive measure.

The proof uses elementary interpolation, the maximum principle, Cauchy's coefficient bound and field norms.
The reference to the integral numerator estimate explains the application and supplies no step in the degree proof.
