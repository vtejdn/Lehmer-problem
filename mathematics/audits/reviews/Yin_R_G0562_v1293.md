## Absolute degrees, splitting and the exceptional traces

The adjacent-record construction supplies positive exponents on late rows, strictly dominant units \(A,B\), and \(D^{-1}\log|N(2-x)|\to\log 2\).
The distinguished conjugate of \(x=A^aB\) alone attains its modulus, since equality forces an embedding to fix both factors.
Thus \(x\) generates \(F=\mathbb Q(A,B)\), and carrying either member of a pair of distinct conjugates to \(x\) shows that their quotient is nontorsion.
The Salem height formula \(h(A)=\log(A)/d_A\) and \(a\log A<\log 2\) bound the height of each quotient by \(2h_*\), with \(h_*\to0\) since the strict record degrees tend to infinity.
The difference of the two translates is a unit times one minus the quotient, so the stated small-height bound controls the normalized log norm of their ideal gcd uniformly over the pair.

I checked the normalization at ramified as well as unramified primes.
For a fixed embedding of a normal closure into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), exactly \(e_Q f_Q\) embeddings of \(F\) induce \(Q\), and each gives \(2-x\) absolute \(p\)-adic valuation \(v_Q(2-x)/e_Q\) when \(v_p(p)=1\).
After the first embedding is chosen uniformly, its expected contribution from \(Q\), multiplied by \(\log p\), is \(f_Q v_Q(2-x)\log p/D=w_Q\).
Given that embedding, exactly \(e_Q f_Q-1\) of the other \(D-1\) embeddings induce the same place and therefore give the same valuation.
Keeping these pairs gives the claimed lower bound on the averaged gcd contribution, even if some other pairs also have equal valuations.
The gcd upper bound already includes all finite places, so summing the lower bound over \(p\) introduces no prime-dependent constant.

When both records are Salem, all embeddings of the two trace fields have real image, hence so do the embeddings of their compositum \(L\).
The nonreal conjugates of \(A\) preclude \(A\in L\), and the trace quadratic gives \([L(A):L]=2\).
The late-row relative inversion fixes \(L(A)=\mathbb Q(A,B+B^{-1})\) and sends \(B\) to \(B^{-1}\), giving \([F:L(A)]=2\).
The splitting field over \(L\) of the two quadratics is therefore of degree four, so its action realizes all four choices of inversions.
For any of its three nonidentity automorphisms the image of \(x\) differs from \(x\).
The preceding absolute gcd estimate thus bounds the weight discarded from primes with stabilizer and from all but a maximal-weight prime in each free orbit, using the orbit argument with these three gcds.
Furthermore \([F_Q:\mathbb Q_p]\ge [L_{\mathfrak l}:\mathbb Q_p]\) at every \(Q\mid\mathfrak l\), with equality on split quartets.
If every local factor of \(L\) over \(p\) has degree at least \(\delta [L:\mathbb Q]=\delta D/4\), the absolute-degree multiplier is at least \(\delta/8\) for sufficiently large \(D\).
This yields the simultaneous estimate for any set of such primes.

Writing \(u=A^a\) and \(v=B\), the traces of \(uv\) and \(u/v\) have sum \(TS\) and product \(u^2+u^{-2}+v^2+v^{-2}=T^2+S^2-4\).
The two factors \(5-2(uv+(uv)^{-1})\) and \(5-2(u/v+v/u)\) then give \(W=4T^2-10TS+4S^2+9\), and completing the square in either variable gives the displayed square identities.
The norm tower identifies divisibility of the absolute norm by \(p\) with the existence of \(\mathfrak l\mid p\) at which \(W\) vanishes.
For \(p>3\) the two identities force each nonzero discriminant to be a square modulo \(\mathfrak l\).
When both are nonzero, all roots lift by Hensel's lemma and \(F=L(u,v)\), since every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(A)\) fixing \(u\) must carry \(A\) to the unique conjugate of modulus \(A\).
Consequently all four local factors equal \(L_{\mathfrak l}\).
Starting from a signed product equal to \(2\) in the residue field, inverting just one factor changes that value because each factor differs from its inverse.
Inverting both gives \(1/2\ne2\), so precisely one of the four primes contributes.
The ideal norm formula \(v_{\mathfrak l}(W)=\sum_{Q\mid\mathfrak l} f(Q/\mathfrak l)v_Q(2-x)\) gives the stated equality of valuations.

If the discriminant of \(v\) vanishes, then \(S=\pm2\) and the conic becomes \((4T-5S)^2=0\) after multiplication by four.
Exchanging the variables gives the other exceptional pair condition, including in characteristic five where \(5/4=0\).
At \(p=2\) the norm polynomial is \(1\) modulo \(2\), and at \(p=3\) it reduces to \((T+S)^2\), where simultaneous inversion can preserve the product \(2\).
The restriction \(p>3\) in the uniqueness assertion covers these cases.
