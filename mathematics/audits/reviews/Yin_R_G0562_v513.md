## Independent finding on single signs, the exceptional prime list and division at both endpoints

At 2026-11-18T22:07 UTC an agent outside the development of [L462 v249](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v249.md#signs) independently checked all four sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had earlier reviewed the quartic collision bound and finite row-kernel method, and received this proof without its development dialogue.
Finiteness for each sign, the explicit odd-prime exceptions for the two positive seeds, the lower bound for every nonzero integral pair and the resulting width-five exclusions hold.

### Finiteness for each fixed sign

The four conjugates are distinct and \(\eta\) is nontorsion by irreducibility, so \(d_i,\delta_i,t_i+2\varepsilon\) and \(S_1-S_2\) are nonzero for positive \(p\).
In particular the two powered traces lie respectively in \((2,\infty)\) and \((-2,2)\).
For an equal-edge nonzero row the antisymmetric trace factor is a nonzero constant, since otherwise the symmetric quadratic would have two distinct double zeros.
Thus the Hermite secant identity of L462 v246 applies to a single sign.
On the chosen local branch, differentiating \(d^2=t^2-4\) gives
\[
 dH_\varepsilon'(t)=1-\frac{t(t-2\varepsilon)}{t^2-4}
                  =\frac{2\varepsilon}{t+2\varepsilon}.
\]
Together with \(dS/dt=p\delta/d\) and \(d\delta/dt=pS/d\) this gives (1), including the coefficient \(1/p\).
Multiplication by the nonzero \(p\delta_1\delta_2\) gives the stated \(E_\varepsilon(p)\).
Its coefficients are fixed algebraic numbers once the seed, roots and sign are chosen, and each summand after expansion has base \(\rho^i\eta^j\ne0\) and a polynomial prefactor of degree at most one.
Terms sharing a base can be combined.

I checked the exponential-polynomial lemma in section 1 of [Lech's journal edition](../literature/Lec53/readme.md#edition), printed pp. 417–418, against the source unit.
Its characteristic-zero field, integral indices and allowance for polynomial prefactors apply directly here.
Infinitely many positive zeros therefore give an entire positive tail of an arithmetic progression of zeros, including if the exponential polynomial were identically zero.
At each zero, the secant in (1) differs from \(H_1\) by \(O_P(\rho^{-p})\) because \(\delta_1-S_1=-2\rho^{-p}\) and \(S_2,\delta_2\) stay bounded.
Also \(S_1/\delta_1=1+O_P(\rho^{-2p})\).
Rearranging gives \(H_2 S_2/\delta_2=H_1+O_P(1/p)\), without bounding \(1/\delta_2\) in this estimate.
Along a progression of step \(r\ge1\), \(\eta^{2r}\) has infinite order and its rotations are dense on the circle on every tail.
On a subsequence with \(\eta^{2p}\to-1\), the direct quotient \(S_2/\delta_2=(\eta^{2p}+1)/(\eta^{2p}-1)\) tends to zero, whereas \(H_1=(t_1-2\varepsilon)/d_1\ne0\).
This contradicts the progression and proves finiteness separately for each sign and fixed seed over all positive integer exponents.
The proof needs only necessity of (1) for an equal-edge row.

### The prime sieve and the exact five kernel computations

For \(p\nmid 2\operatorname{disc}(T)T(2)T(-2)\), the difference \(t_1-t_2\) and both \(t_i\pm2\) are units above \(p\), so each \(d_i,H_i,K_i\) is integral there and \(d_i\) is a unit.
Frobenius in the residue field gives \(S_1-S_2\equiv(t_1-t_2)^p\) and \(\delta_i\equiv d_i^p\).
Reduction of \(E_\varepsilon(p)\) leaves \((S_1-S_2)\delta_1\delta_2(K_1+K_2)\), with all three factors before the last sum units.
Summing the two fractions \(K_i\) indeed gives \(2\varepsilon(j+4\varepsilon)/T(-2\varepsilon)\).
Thus a good odd prime for which an equal-edge row exists must divide \(j+4\varepsilon\).
When the latter integer vanishes, only the progression argument has been used for finiteness.

For \(T_A\) the discriminant and endpoint values \((\operatorname{disc}T,T(2),T(-2))\) are \((13,-1,3)\), and for \(T_B\) they are \((8,-1,7)\).
The restriction integers for the two signs are respectively \(-390,234\) and \(-672,224\).
Their odd prime divisors give exactly the two candidate lists in the text.
For each surviving prime and both signs I independently took the five remainders \(x^{pi}+\varepsilon x^{p(4-i)+1}\) modulo \(P^2\) by polynomial division.
I computed the rational nullspace of each resulting \(8\)-by-\(5\) coefficient matrix and cleared the denominators with the free coordinate positive.
Each matrix had rank four and every resulting integral generator had coefficient gcd one.
The complete vectors agreed with the existing program's oriented generators.
Their last-minus-first coordinates were as follows.

| Positive seed | \(p\) | Plus | Minus |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(A\) | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| \(A\) | 5 | 0 | 80 |
| \(A\) | 13 | 3835 | 12415 |
| \(B\) | 3 | 0 | 48 |
| \(B\) | 7 | 28826 | 2842 |

Hence the plus lines at \((A,5)\) and \((B,3)\) are the only nonzero rational equal-edge lines for these positive seeds at odd primes.
For the optional variable changes to \(P(-X)\), odd \(p\) sends \(A(Y)\) to \(A(-Y)\), preserving the width-four edge difference and exchanging the two signs.

### Degree four without a row gcd or a modulus condition

For any nonzero row pair the two components \(U\pm V_4^*\) cannot both vanish, since their sum is \(2U\) and their difference is \(2V_4^*\).
Reversing the graph about \(4p+1\) preserves \(P^2\), so each component gives the double divisibility for its own sign.
The union of the two exceptional sets of integers is finite for fixed \(P\).
Thereafter any nonzero component has nonzero edge difference and the finite-place inequality of L462 v246 applies to that individual integral row.
Its error term is independent of its coefficients.
The exterior value and derivative bound gives \(|a_4-a_0|\ll_P pH/\rho^p\), using \(\|U\pm V_4^*\|_1\le2H\).
Combining the inequalities and absorbing \(\log p\) proves the squared lower rate for every such pair, including a pair fixed up to sign by reversal or a pair with common factors.

### Degree reduction at \(1\) and \(-1\)

At \(\xi=\pm1\), one has \(V_5^*(\xi)=\xi V(\xi)\) and \((U+V_5^*)(\xi)(U-V_5^*)(\xi)=D(\xi)\).
If \(D\ne0\) neither component vanishes identically, so \(D(\xi)=0\) yields \(A_\varepsilon=(Y-\xi)C\) with \(C\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) nonzero of degree at most four.
Direct formal reversal about degree five gives \((A_\varepsilon)^*_5=(1-\xi Y)C_4^*=-\xi(Y-\xi)C_4^*\), even when the actual degree drops.
Thus the signed graph factors as
\[
 (x^p-\xi)\big(C(x^p)-\varepsilon\xi x C_4^*(x^p)\big).
\]
The first factor has only roots of unity whereas \(P\) has none, so the second factor keeps both copies of \(P\).
Synthetic division by \(Y-\xi\) gives signed partial sums of coefficients of \(A_\varepsilon\), each of magnitude at most \(\|A_\varepsilon\|_1\).
There are at most five resulting coefficients, hence \(\|C\|_1=\|C_4^*\|_1\le10H\).
The degree-four result applies even though this new signed pair has equal row moduli.
The loss \(\log10\) is absorbed in \(o_P(p)\), proving the stated endpoint bound for either \(\xi\).

For the positive quartic seeds of C345 v410, \(2\log\rho>\log2\), so this lower rate eventually exceeds the box cutoff.
The minimum \(Q\) of \(\rho^p\) cannot vanish at \(1\) or \(-1\).
Consequently vanishing of the quotient at an endpoint is exactly \(D(\xi)=0\).
For its reciprocal form \(cY^2+dY+c\), these two excluded cases are respectively \(d=-2c\) and \(d=2c\).
Combining them with the earlier \(c\ne0\) condition gives a true quadratic of nonzero discriminant with neither root an endpoint.
The size estimate remains \(2|c|+|d|\le4(2/\rho)^{2p}\).
The result leaves existence in this remaining range open.
