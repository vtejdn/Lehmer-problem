## Fixed factors, both prescribed signs and the two-value consequence

At 2026-11-19T00:59 UTC I reviewed all four sections of the [fixed-list argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v269.md#rate) for Sheng Yin.
I received the fixed proof and questions to check, worked outside its development, and examined its dependencies before consulting the earlier findings on those dependencies.
The least one-norm has logarithmic rate \(\tfrac12\sum_i t_i m(P_i)\) under the stated hypothesis on distinct inverse-pair representatives.
The two-value and dyadic applications satisfy that hypothesis after the indicated choices of signs.

### The integer contraction and coefficient lower bound

For sufficiently large \(p\), reduction of \(\Phi_p\) to \((X-1)^{p-1}\) in characteristic \(p\) excludes \(p\) from the resultant, since \(F(1)\ne0\).
At \(\ell\ne p\) the cyclotomic extension is unramified and the primitive nodes have distinct reductions.
The exponent of \(\ell\) in \(B_p(F)\) is the largest normalized valuation of \(F\) at these nodes, whereas its exponent in \(N_p(F)\) is their sum.
Reciprocity gives the same valuation at two distinct inverse nodes, proving \(B_p(F)^2\mid N_p(F)\).
In a fixed splitting field of degree \(d\), dividing \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}\Phi_p(x)\) by \(e_{\mathfrak l}\) converts it to the valuation normalized at \(\ell\).
If positive, the entire valuation comes from one node, since the other differences are units there.
The largest \(t_x\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}\Phi_p(x)\) thus contributes at most \(e_{\mathfrak l}v_\ell(B_p(F))\).
Summing against \(\log N\mathfrak l/d\) recovers \(\log B_p(F)\) on this upper side by \(\sum_{\mathfrak l\mid\ell}e_{\mathfrak l}f_{\mathfrak l}=d\).
The corresponding sum over all representatives equals \(\tfrac12\log N_p(F)\) because inverse representatives give identical ideals.

I checked Proposition 2, inequality (2.8), and the absolute-value conventions in the [authors' preprint, pp. 2, 3 and 7](../../literature/pdf/CZ05.pdf#page=7).
For an independent pair \(x,y\), the pairs \((x^p,y^p)\) are distinct, independent ordinary units in this one field and have maximum absolute logarithmic height \(p\max(h_{\mathrm{abs}}(x),h_{\mathrm{abs}}(y))\).
The normalized sum of the finite-place minima for their differences from one is bounded by the full nonnegative place sum in that proposition.
Its exceptional set is finite for each fixed positive \(\epsilon\), giving \(o(p)\).
I accepted Proposition 2 at its stated scope without rechecking the Subspace Theorem proof.
For a dependent pair the differences generate an ideal containing \(\eta^p-1\), including for negative exponents since both bases are units.
Its norm stays bounded as \(\eta^p-1\) ranges over finitely many nonzero elements for all large primes.
Replacing \(x^p-1\) by its divisor \(\Phi_p(x)\) only decreases the individual valuations.
Pairwise minima bound the loss on replacing the sum by its maximum, also after taking the largest fixed multiplicity as a common factor in the bound.
The [fixed-input norm estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#local) now gives the claimed limit for every fixed subproduct, including those obtained by lowering one multiplicity.

After dividing out the common endpoint zero, the determinant \(\zeta^{-a}-\zeta^a\) is a unit at \(\ell\ne p\), even when \(\ell=2\).
The two evaluations therefore force both endpoint integers to be divisible by \(B_p(F)\), and at least one is nonzero.
The Hasse derivative bound gives the explicit inequality \(\log H_*\ge\log B_p(F)-b_0\log2\), including a graph with just one nonzero row.
This lower bound is uniform in rows for the fixed input.

### Attaining the lower rate

The integral box proof counts \(t_i\) jets at the \(i\)-th root, with only a value condition when \(t_i=1\).
Multiplication of the fieldwise upper counts requires no disjointness of fields.
The leading normalized cost is \(Wl/(2(l-J))\).
For diverging \(b_0=o(p)\) the full normalized upper bound tends to \(W/2\).
For a fixed \(b_0\) it has the stated leading limit, which can first be made strictly below \(W/2+\min(\gamma,\min_i h_i/2)\).

Powering is injective on the finite combined root set for all sufficiently large primes.
The powered minimum \(Q_i\) therefore has the original degree and \(m(Q_i)=ph_i\).
Its pullback \(Q_i(X^p)\) is simple at each root of \(P_i\) and misses all other prescribed roots.
If both rows contain \(Q_i\), their quotients give a nonzero graph divisible by \(F/P_i\).
The larger quotient row has one-norm at least \(\exp(p(W-h_i)/2-o(p))\), with the integer bound one sufficient for an empty product.
Its degree is at most \(b_0\), so its logarithmic measure loses at most \(b_0\log2\) from this log norm.
Restoring \(Q_i\) would raise the original graph's normalized cost to at least \((W+h_i)/2-o(1)\).
The strict margin in the box construction excludes all such common zeroes, including if one row were identically zero.

At each powered node at least one of \(U+V^*\) and \(U-V^*\) is nonzero because their sum is \(2U\).
The reciprocal powers divide each corresponding signed graph by reversal.
Multiplication by \(Y+1\) or \(Y-1\) moves both to the same prescribed sign at width \(b_0+1\) and preserves the nonzero tests since the powered nodes are nontorsion.
Each orbit then forbids at most one integer slope, so the \(k+1\) choices \(0,\ldots,k\) suffice.
The row cost grows by a constant depending only on \(k\).
The admissible one-norms form a nonempty set of positive integers at every sufficiently large prime, so the minimum exists.

### Choice of representatives and size of the conditional gap

Within an all-power reciprocal orbit, equal positive absolute heights force the primitive exponents in a dependence to have equal absolute value.
A torsion product then gives a torsion quotient with the inverse conjugate.
Injectivity under every power makes a torsion quotient possible only for identical conjugates, leaving different inverse pairs independent.
Across two dependent orbits, orienting the second root positively fixes the coprime exponents by the ratio of absolute heights.
For each first root the positive match is unique by the same torsion-quotient argument.
Galois transitivity therefore makes all positive-match phases conjugate, and inverse matches merely invert the relation.
When the common conjugacy class is \(\{1\}\), one of the two primitive exponents is odd, and negating that orbit gives phase \(-1\) at every match.
Negation commutes with conjugation and inversion and preserves degrees and measures.
For the dyadic list of a positive-measure all-power reciprocal unit, dependence forces the underlying conjugates to agree up to inversion by the same argument.
After orientation, the higher power divided by the corresponding even power of the lower signed root is \(-1\).

Double factors give \(W=2\sum_i h_i\), and the strict test at a fixed width requires \(2\log\|A\|_1/p+\log(2pb+1)/p<B\).
A fixed positive margin is available precisely for the asserted sufficient condition \(2\sum_i h_i<B\).
Padding to a larger width multiplies the row norm by at most two, so \(\log b(p)=o(p)\) suffices for the padded test even when \(b(p)/p\) grows.
Under the hypothesized infinite exclusion, the pair \(h,2h\) forces \(3h\ge B/2\), and factorization transfers this bound to the unrestricted infimum.
If \(\lambda<B/4\), some positive irreducible value lies below \(B/4\) and there can be only one such value.
It attains \(\lambda\), every other positive irreducible value is at least \(B/2-\lambda\), and two positive factors contribute at least \(2\lambda\ge B/2-\lambda\).
An integral content of absolute value at least two already costs \(\log2\).
This gives isolation for the full positive spectrum.
The growing-order test retains its separate previously established conditional bound.
The review establishes neither the infinite exclusion nor a starting prime uniform in the input field or list.
