## Counting full completions and their positive gaps

At 2026-11-20T20:56 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently checked the [submitted statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v645.md#statement), its four argument sections and the two ledger calculations cited below.

Both norm inequalities and both residue-order criteria hold with the quantifiers and uniformity stated.

Fixing the smallest of the \(r-4\) prescribed positions at zero selects one representative of each translation class.
A two-term sum of nonzero integer multiples of distinct powers cannot vanish, since a nontrivial rational power value \(\alpha^u\in\mathbb Q\), \(u>0\), would equal \(\alpha^{-u}\) under inversion.
The product of a two-term sum with its image under inversion determines the positive gap by the reciprocal quadratic, and the sum then determines the smaller position.
This applies first to the surviving pair and then to the erased pair in the prescribed order.
Consequently the number of distinct surviving sums equals the number of full completions, even for a relation with a proper zero subsum.

Put \(D=\binom n2\).
In the order \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), which contains \(\alpha^{-1}\), the differences of any \(n\) surviving sums lie in \(Q\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Translation into \([0,W']\) preserves that divisibility, and the nonzero determinant divided by \(Q^D\) has nonzero integral norm.
At each embedding the disk bound follows from applying Hadamard to rows of the power matrix after dividing each point by the disk radius.
The resulting norm is at most \(n^{dn/2}S^{dD}\exp(mW'D)\), giving (1).
The function \(\log n/(n-1)\) decreases to zero on \(n\ge2\), with maximum \(\log 2\), so the strict cutoffs asserted for the number of completions follow.

Applying inversion to the common residue preserves its congruence in the same order.
The trace differences multiplied by \(ce\) belong to \(Q\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Cancelling \(g=\gcd(Q,|ce|)\) in the domain and using an integer Bezout identity for \(ce/g\) and \(Q/g\) gives divisibility of each difference by \(Q/g\), including when \(Q/g=1\).
Two positive gaps with equal traces give equal or inverse powers, so distinct gaps give a nonzero determinant.
In the monic trace polynomial basis the positive-degree columns contribute \(2^{n-1}\) per embedding and the growth exponents sum to \(WD\).
The product formula for the unit gives total logarithmic radial growth \(2mWD\).
Division by \(dD\) yields \(2\log 2/n\) for the extra column factor in (2).

For an eligible root of order \(s\), the two reciprocal evaluations are valid for Laurent exponents because the root is nonzero.
Eliminating \(y\) gives \(cVx^2+(e^2-c^2-UV)x+cU\), which vanishes identically precisely in the stated exceptional case.
Otherwise each of its at most two roots gives at most one admissible \(y\).
In the exceptional case the fixed ratio \(y/x=-c/e\) has square one, including in characteristic two.
It gives at most one difference residue modulo \(s\) and forces \(s\mid2(j-i)\).
These arguments use only field evaluation and hold at a root of any multiplicity.
Once one ordinary solution is chosen per test, any two compatible pairs have the same \(i\bmod M_0\) and the same \(j-i\bmod M\), regardless of common divisors among the orders.
The order of the labels fixes the side of zero for \(i\), giving at most \(W\) consecutive possible integers for each of \(i\) and \(j-i\).
This proves (3), also when no ordinary tests are present.

For each realized completion the least common multiple of the exceptional orders divides \(2(j-i)\), hence \(M\le 2W M_0\).
The first criterion excludes exceptions for every choice of fixed positions, and the second forces \(M_0>W\) whenever those positions have a completion.
A single order exceeding \(2W\) also excludes its exception.
Otherwise a minimal collection crossing the chosen threshold \(X\) has \(M\le2WX\), since the removal of any one order leaves a least common multiple at most \(X\).
Each indispensable order uniquely supplies a maximal prime power of \(M\), so the number of tests is at most \(\omega(M)\).

The remaining prescribed labels occupy one ordered subset of \([1,W]\) of size \(r-5\), including the empty subset when \(r=5\).
This proves (4) uniformly in the other inputs.
