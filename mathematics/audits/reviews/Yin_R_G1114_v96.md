## The multiplier degree, the first fractional coefficient and the branch-two family

At 2026-11-16T19:24 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Yin examined all four sections of L462v115 and the uses of the earlier results identified below.
The reviewer took no part in developing this proof and received no development discussion, author explanation or earlier verdict on it.

Multiplying both sides of the inverse representation by \(H\) preserves \(J\), all its charged atoms and the measure of the integral denominator.
The complex sparse height bound applies to \(F=H\Pi/j\) because its constant and Mahler measure are one, and hence bounds its coefficient absolute sum using just the number of terms.
Since the one-sided moments of \(J\) are bounded, the integer coefficients of \(B=HB^\sharp\) are bounded even when those of \(H\) have no prescribed bound.
If the original normalized numerator is integral, division by \(1-z^2\) and the integral basis \(1,z^k+z^{-k}\) of symmetric Laurent polynomials show \(C/j\) is monic integral, so \(G=1\) contradicts the trace multiplier obstruction on any escaping integral subsequence.
Antireciprocity puts the first fractional index \(d\) at most \(h\), and convolution by \(H\) with unit constant preserves that index and the residue modulo one there.

If \(d\ge c n\) with \(n=\deg(H\Pi)\), then \(2h+2\le n\le h/c\), and the integer Taylor prefix of \(B/F\) has length comparable to \(h\).
The moving-index nonvanishing test is therefore of degree \(o(h)\) on the original \(2h\) positively charged points.
The binomial prefix model and both cutoffs apply with polynomial degree \(O(h)\) and bounded circle norms for \(UF,VF\).
The integral \(UB\) then has bounded circle \(L^1\) norm, hence bounded support, contradicting \(M(UB)=M(B)>1\) tending to one.
This verifies precisely \(d=o(n)\) for arbitrary multiplier degrees.

Under failure of the distance estimate one may fix a limiting nonintegral coefficient at \(e=O(d)\).
The bounded number of exponents, their stabilized relations, logarithmic truncation and initial face exposure give frozen early specializations \(T\) of constant one with \(M(T)\to1\).
At each pair \(s,S\) with \(s/S\to0\), all omitted offsets are at least \(\eta S\) for some fixed \(\eta>0\).
The coefficient errors in replacing the early part of \(FJ\) by \(TJ\) are uniformly \(o(1)\) throughout the tested prefix.
Consequently the ordered base coefficients and all fixed positive layers at \(kt+O(s)\) inherit integral limits from \(B\) when \(s/t,t/S\to0\).
The face has the nonintegral coefficient at \(e\), so the density exclusion and last harmonic argument apply, and their character multipliers give exact zeros for the integer polynomial \(UB\) on the intermediate interval.
This step uses the positive weak limit of the measures and is valid above the original degree.

For the initial location-uniform bound, tests of degree \(o(d)\) still have degree \(o(h)\), giving the short integral model and the block Bessel bound, including the intervals meeting offsets of \(F\).
At a later scale, the initial sum bound for \(PUB\) and its uniform coefficientwise approximation by \(PUTJ\) bound the weighted Gram operator on each fixed \(k\)-point set by \(O(1)+k\,o(1)\).
Each coefficient of \(PUTB\) belongs to a fixed finite real set, since \(T\) uses the frozen tuple, the induction multipliers have bounded support with coefficients in fixed finite sets, and \(B\) has bounded integer coefficients.
This gives a positive lower bound for the modulus of a nonzero coefficient and justifies the Bessel count away from offsets.
The previous all-interval estimate for \(PB\) controls each length-\(O(s)\) transition by translating it through the finitely supported \(UT\).
At degree scale \(n\), boundedly many intervals now cover the product, and each specialized face has \(M(T)\ge1\) by its constant.
Thus \(M(PB)>1\) tends to one with bounded term count, fixed finite coefficient set and unit constant, contradicting the fixed-tuple endpoint argument after extraction.
At \(d\), distance from the integers is at least the reciprocal of the reduced denominator, which divides \(j\).
This gives both denominator and bounded-branch conclusions, and \(j=(\theta-q)(q+\theta^{-1}-H_q'(0))\) with \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\) also gives the asserted bound on \(q\).

For the cubic construction the discriminant is \(-268\), the real root is in \((3,5)\), and the conjugate pair has squared modulus \(3/\theta_*\).
Equality in \(|P-P^*|\le2|P|\) on the circle occurs precisely at \(z^3=-1\) since \(P+P^*=-2(z^3+1)\) and \(P\) has no neutral root.
For \(6\mid N\) these three points are roots, and the order-\(N\) derivative term makes all three simple eventually.
The Rouché count for \(0<t<1\), persistence of strictly exterior roots at \(t=1\), and the real sign change on a fixed small bracket of \(\theta_*\) give a unique simple exterior root converging to \(\theta_*\).
The monic quotient has nonzero constant 2, so an additional monic integral factor supported strictly inside the disk would have a nonzero integer constant of modulus less than one.
The linear Taylor coefficient of \(f_N/f_N^*\) is \(-5-2(-5)=5\), which gives \(j=2\).
The denominator \(D\) has the odd-degree factor \(1+z\), and substitution into \(K_q B^\sharp=\Pi\) gives the stated trace identity.
Direct expansion gives the low block \(2-2z-z^2+2z^3-z^4\) and its negative reflection about degree \(N+3\) at the top.
The disjoint blocks have ten terms in total.
Dividing the low block by \(1+z^3\) gives \(2-z-z(1+z)/(1+z^3)\), hence four nonzero coefficients per six exponents away from the ends of the prefix.
The original support grows linearly, while the excess tends to the strictly positive \(\theta_*-2\).
Also \(L_N=(1+z^3)f_N\) has bounded support, so the cited sparse seed criterion applies independently.
