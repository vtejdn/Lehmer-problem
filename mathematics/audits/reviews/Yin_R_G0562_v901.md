# Every radical branch of a unit on a fixed graph

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:48 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the fixed [C345 v761](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v761.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the claim and received its fixed argument without its development discussion or a prior verdict.

Write \(t=[F(\alpha):F]\) and \(d_0=\gcd(g,t)\).
Taking the relative norm of \(\alpha^g=\beta\) gives the exact equality \(b^g=\beta^t\) in \(F\).
After setting \(r=g/d_0,\ j=t/d_0\), the quotient \(b^r/\beta^j\) is a \(d_0\)-th root of unity \(\omega\) in \(F\).
For \(Aj+Br=1\) this gives \((b^A\beta^B)^r=\omega^A\beta\), including negative Bézout exponents since all the elements are units.
Thus the twist \(\xi=\omega^A\) lies in the specified group and has order at most its cardinality.

For a fixed graph unit \(z\) with an \(r\)-th root in a relative extension of degree at most \(E\), each \(p^a\mid r\) gives a \(p^a\)-th root \(\theta_0\) in that extension.
For \(K=\mathbb Q(\theta_0)\) there are exactly \(e=[K:\mathbb Q(z)]\le E\) embeddings above each conjugate of \(z\).
The characteristic polynomial of the \(p^a\)-th power of the integer companion matrix for \(\theta_0\) is therefore \(S_z^e\), where \(S_z\) is the minimal polynomial of \(z\).
The previous matrix power has eigenvalue \(\gamma=\theta_0^{p^{a-1}}\).
The [integer matrix congruence](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#congruence) applies also at two and makes \(S_z(\gamma)^e\) divisible by \(p^a\) in \(\mathcal O_K\).
Since \(S_z\) is monic and divides the integer graph test \(P\), the same holds for \(P(\gamma)^e\).
This element is nonzero by the [separation of every prime root](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#radicals), which imposes no field or sign condition on the chosen \(\gamma\).
Its norm gives \(p^{a[K:\mathbb Q]}\le|\operatorname N_{K/\mathbb Q}(P(\gamma)^e)|\).
Using the coefficient length at each embedding and \(h(\gamma)=h(z)/p\) bounds the normalized right side by \((L_S\exp(\deg(P)h(z)/p))^e\) after taking the \([K:\mathbb Q]\)-th root.
The product \(\deg(P)h(z)\) and the length \(L_S\) are uniformly bounded for the fixed graph at large slope.
The resulting common upper bound for every \(p^a\) gives the claimed divisibility for \(r\) by taking a single least common multiple.

The transformed equation for \(\delta=\xi\beta\) uses precisely \(S(Z)=\xi^vR(\xi^{-u}Z)\).
Bounded order of \(\xi\) leaves finitely many graphs even as \(u,v\) vary.
Here \(\mathbb Q(\delta,\xi)=F\) since \(\xi\in F\) and \(\beta=\delta/\xi\), so \([F:\mathbb Q(\delta)]\le[\mathbb Q(\xi):\mathbb Q]\le W\).
This verifies the relative-degree hypothesis for the root obtained by the norm argument and permits common constants for all twists.
The height identity \(h(\alpha)=h(\beta)/g\) and degree identity \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=t[F:\mathbb Q]\) give the exact measure ratio \(t/g\), which is at least \(1/r\).
Every irreducible factor of \(P_\beta(X^g)\) contributes a branch with \(g\)-th power exactly \(\beta\) by extending an embedding carrying the powered root back to \(\beta\).
Any real embedding of \(F\) injects its torsion into \(\{\pm1\}\), even if the chosen embedding or radical is nonreal.
The [positive real formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) has the distinct hypothesis of a positive radical in the distinguished embedding.

In the example, a primitive \(2^{k+1}\)-th root \(\zeta_k\) gives \(x_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}\) of degree \(2^{k-1}\).
All its conjugates lie in \([-x_k,x_k]\) with a unique maximum \(x_k>0\), so the distinguished conjugate of \(a_k=1+x_k\) is the unique one of largest modulus.
Consequently each positive power of \(a_k\) generates the full field by counting extensions of the distinguished embedding.
From \(x_j^2=2+x_{j-1}\) the quadratic norm of \(a_j\) is \(1-x_j^2=-a_{j-1}\).
The sign has norm one at each further quadratic step, so the norm from \(F_k\) to \(F_2\) is \(-a_2=-(1+\sqrt2)\), itself a unit.
Thus \(\beta=-a_k^{2^k}\) is a generating unit, and its negative value excludes every even radical in this real field.
An odd \(r>1\) would give an \(r\)-th power \((1+\sqrt2)^{2^k}\) by norm to \(F_2\), since both the exponent and \([F_k:F_2]\) are even.
Coprimality then gives an \(r\)-th root \(y\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) of \(1+\sqrt2\), necessarily a unit with \(1<y<1+\sqrt2\).
Its rational coordinate is integral in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\).
It equals \((y+1/y)/2\in(1,\sqrt2)\) for norm one and \((y-1/y)/2\in(0,1)\) for norm minus one, ruling out both signs.
Finally \((a_k\zeta_k)^{2^k}=\beta\) and adjoining this root recovers the nonreal quadratic extension \(F_k(\zeta_k)\).
The exact measure ratio is \(2/2^k\), proving the claimed limitation on a conclusion based solely on in-field roots.

## Comparison with the stronger bounded-length radical theorem

At 2026-11-21T05:06 UTC the reviewer checked the earlier [denominator theorem for all complex radicals](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) against the graph application.
The integer graph norm polynomial in v758 has bounded coefficient length, so that theorem gives an integer bounding \(g/\gcd(g,t)\) for every branch and every root field, independently of its roots of unity.
Its proof uses an affine action on the roots followed by descent to the signed powers in the original field.
Thus v761 gives another proof of a special case, and its principal separate example is the negative dyadic unit showing why an argument from untwisted in-field powers alone fails.
The counterexample ranges over arbitrary units and supplies no contradiction to the bounded-length theorem.
