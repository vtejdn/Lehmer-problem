## The fourth-root bound

Review date (UTC) 2026-11-20T19:33.
An agent working for Sheng Yin independently checked the whole proof of [the degree bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v627.md#statement).
The reviewer had no role in its development and received no exploration notes, proof-generation discussion, author explanations or prior conclusions about this proof.
The argument establishes the absolute bound \(T(P)\ge c d^{1/4}\) for the stated complex polynomials and the divergence assertion for hyperbolic unit minimal polynomials.

At \(z=0\) the Lagrange basis polynomial for \(\rho\) has value \(-P(0)/(\rho P'(\rho))\), with every denominator nonzero.
The positive radial logarithms sum to \(m\) and the negative ones to \(-m\), so each \(x_\rho\le1\) and their sum is two.
The exact ratio \(|a_\rho|^2/w_\rho^2=|1-r_\rho^{-2}|/m\) is bounded by \(2e^2 x_\rho\), using the derivative of \(u\mapsto e^{-2u}\) on \([-1,1]\).
Thus the light nodes carry at most \(\kappa T\sqrt\eta\) absolute interpolation weight, and at most \(2/\eta\) nodes exceed the cutoff.

For the circle-zero factor, write \(M=\lfloor N/4\rfloor\).
On \(|t|\le 2\pi/N\) the first \(M\) terms in the cosine sum give \(U_N(t)\ge N^2t^2/(512\pi^2)\), since \(N-j\ge3N/4\), \(\sum_{j=1}^M j^2\ge M^3/3\), and \(M\ge N/8\).
The substitution \(s=Nt\) makes the integral of the resulting logarithmic upper bound \(O(N^{-1})\).
Outside this arc, \(\sin(|t|/2)\ge |t|/\pi\) gives \(|D_N|\le1/2\), so \(-\log(1-|D_N|^2)\le 4|D_N|^2/3\).
Orthogonality gives the full-circle mean of \(|D_N|^2\) as \(1/N\).
The scalar Fejér–Riesz statement on page 2 applies to the nonnegative, nonzero Laurent polynomial \(U_N\) of degree \(N-1\) and supplies the factor with no open-disk zero. [Dritschel and Rovnyak, Section 1](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/james-rovnyak/files/p71.pdf#page=2)
For the root-pair argument in the claim, clearing the Laurent power gives a polynomial of degree \(2(N-1)\) with nonzero constant coefficient.
Its reciprocal-conjugate pairs and even multiplicities on the circle give the claimed factor degree and leave a positive scalar to be fixed at a point where \(U_N>0\).
Each exterior or circle zero contributes zero to the mean of \(\log|p_N/p_N(0)|\), including the integrable singularity at a boundary zero.
Consequently \(|p_N(0)|\ge e^{-C/N}\), while \(U_N(0)=0\) and \(U_N\le1\) give the prescribed zero and the disk norm after normalization.

For a node \(r_\rho<R\), its reflection \(\beta=R^2/\bar\rho\) satisfies \(|\beta|>R\).
On \(|z|=R\) one has \(|z-\beta|=(R/r_\rho)|z-\rho|\), giving the normalized quotient in the claim modulus \(R/r_\rho\).
Removing the factor \(1-z/\beta\) and inserting \(1-z/\rho\) preserves the degree bound and value at zero.
The exterior case \(r_\rho\ge R\) uses the disk bound directly.
The total logarithmic norm of their product is at most \(Ch/N+hm\eta+m\le C+3\), because \(h\eta\le2\).
This norm controls all surviving nodes, including those on the contour, by the maximum modulus principle.

The constant interpolation identity rules out \(h=0\) for the chosen \(\eta\).
If the product had degree below \(d\), its value one at zero and its vanishing at the \(h\) discarded nodes would force \(1\le B\kappa T\sqrt{\eta}=1/2\).
Hence \(d\le h(\max(8,h)-1)\le8h^2\le32(2\kappa B)^4T^4\).
Each product of a subset of roots has modulus at most \(e^m\), which bounds every integer coefficient in bounded degree.
Only finitely many such integral polynomials have bounded degree and \(m\le1\), and each hyperbolic unit polynomial has \(m>0\).
It follows that \(m\to0\) forces \(d\to\infty\).
The comparison with [the earlier divergence statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#statement) uses its stated conclusion only.
