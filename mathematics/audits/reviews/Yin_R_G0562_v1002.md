## A single finite exceptional set suffices

The proof establishes the full statement.
For nonaxial slopes the logarithmic one-forms have no simultaneous torus zero, since the curve is smooth and embedded in the torus.
At a point with slope \(l/k\) the form \(dU/U\) is therefore nonvanishing, and \(dT/T=k(g-l/k)dU/U\) has a simple zero at each unramified point of that slope fiber.
The degree-sixteen slope map has only finitely many branch values in characteristic zero.
The boundary points have one unit coordinate and the other coordinate of order one in absolute value, so each sign choice gives the same degree and the same multiset of zero multiplicities.
If a critical value were torsion then primitivity gives a parametrization of its fiber as a torsion translate of \((X^k,X^l)\), and tangency gives a multiple section root, excluded by v852.

The rational critical scheme has length sixteen, bounding the degree of each entire point by sixteen, while elimination in either coordinate gives height \(O(\log(2N))\) uniformly in both signs.
The quotient coordinates thus have the same height bound and lie in one field of degree at most \(16^2\).
With \(d=a^l/b^k\) torsion the two Bézout identities give \(a/\gamma^k=d^j\) and \(b/\gamma^l=d^{-i}\).
Height invariance under torsion and homogeneity under signed powers give the equality with \(Nh(\gamma)\).
Northcott finiteness at degree at most 256 and height at most one, together with Kronecker's characterization of height zero in the multiplicative group, gives the uniform positive gap required here.
For torsion \(\gamma\), Northcott applied to the two torsion coordinates yields a fixed finite set of translation pairs.
The coefficients at \((0,0),(1,0),(2,1)\) of the displayed model are \(1,-7,1\), respectively.
A translation preserving the curve must preserve its irreducible Laurent equation up to a unit, whose monomial exponent vanishes by finiteness of the support.
Those three coefficients force successively the scalar, \(a\) and \(b\) to equal one.
Each remaining translate meets the curve in finitely many torus points, and smoothness supplies a single projective logarithmic tangent at each point.
A rational projective tangent has exactly two primitive integral representatives, giving the claimed finiteness even with both signs allowed.

The orbit cover for the normal subgroup generated by nonzero finite inertia is connected and unramified on the punctured parameter line.
It is totally ramified over zero if its degree is greater than one, so that degree divides every zero multiplicity and hence divides \(\gcd(|k|,|l|)=1\).
The resulting transitive subgroup generated by transpositions contains every edge transposition of a connected graph and is the full symmetric group.
For distinct translating scalars the nonzero finite branch loci are disjoint even when comparing copies of the same critical value, since that value is nonzero.
A loop supported around a branch of just one factor gives a transposition there alone, and surjectivity onto that factor supplies all its conjugates in the compositum group.
These elements generate the full product, for any finite number of factors.
Its intersection with the cyclic Kummer field is Galois over the parameter field and unramified at all those places, so its quotient group kills a generating family and is trivial.
This proves the claimed group after every positive Kummer pullback without adding exceptional directions.
