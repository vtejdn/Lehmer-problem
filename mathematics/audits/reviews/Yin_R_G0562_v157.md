## Finding on the complete argument

The proof establishes (1)–(4) in their stated ranges and the subsequent power-sum conclusions for every cancelling product of asymptotically minimum degree.

For each neutral or added root \(z\), the vector with entries \(z^i\) contributes a positive outer product, including when the added multiset is not conjugation invariant.
Removing these terms leaves the symmetric reciprocal-pair matrix whose action on \(x+y\) is multiplication by \(q+\sinh(aq)/\sinh a\).
For the normalized eigenvector the shifted inner products satisfy \(|w_j|\le1\), and the full autocorrelation has \(\ell^2\) norm at most \(\|h\|_1\|h\|_2=\|h\|_1\).
The finite geometric sums give \(\|x\|_1^2/\|x\|_2^2=\coth(a/2)\tanh(aq/2)\).
Since \(\|x+y\|_2^2\ge2\|x\|_2^2\), the two bounds on the off-diagonal form give exactly the minimum in (1), including the empty prefix at \(q=1\).

Multiplication by \(2\sinh a/N\) gives \(\Delta_q/N=O_{A,C}(a)\) or \(O_{A,C}(\sqrt a)\) under the respective budgets.
The squared geometric tail with ratio \(e^{-2a}\) then gives \(O_{A,C}(a)\) or \(O_{A,C}(1)\).
Under the weaker budget \(\sinh(aq)=O_C(N\sqrt a)\) for small \(a\), so \(q/N\ll_C\log(2N)/(aN)\).
Because \(N\ge d\), \(\log(2N)/N\le\log(2d)/d\) for \(d\ge2\).
Dobrowolski's Theorem 1 (Acta Arithmetica 34, 1979, p. 391) applies because the Salem algebraic integer has measure \(e^a\) and infinite order.
Its contrapositive gives \(a\gg_A(\log(2d))^{-3}\) at large \(d\), and finiteness of bounded-degree integer polynomials of bounded measure forces \(d\to\infty\) when \(a\to0\).
These bounds give the claimed uniform limit even for unbounded added degree.
At a fixed \(q\), every copy of \(X^q-1\) contributes zero to the earlier moments and \(q\) to the \(q\)-th moment and the degree, leaving the reflected exterior excess unchanged.

The formal logarithmic derivative identifies cancellation with the exponents \(-c_l\) at every \(l\le k\).
Reversing the integral packet for either sign realizes those exponents as a monic cyclotomic product.
Its degree estimate uses absolute values throughout.
The bounds \(k=O_{A,B}((\log(2d))^4)\) and \(e^{ak}\ge d^{1/2+\delta}\) absorb each polynomial-in-\(k\) error involving \(\sqrt{kd\log(2d)}\) or \(e^{ak/2}\).
At the top end \(\sum_{j\in J}e^{aj}=V(1-e^{-aH})\) with \(aH\to\infty\).
In the test identity \(|w_l/l|\le1\), so the total replacement cost for \(lc_l\) is \(o(e^{ak})\) for every signed test, and indices below \(J\) contribute \(O(Ve^{-aH})\).
The constant test thus gives a negative moment of size \((1+o(1))V\), while the uniform absolute conductor bound makes its size at most \((1+o(1))D\).
Together with the packet upper bound this proves (2).

At a small active conductor, uniqueness of \(j\) follows from separation of fractions with denominators at most \(R\).
For \(m=n/(n,j)\) the factor \(\mu(m)/\varphi(m)\) reaches \(-1\) only at \(m=2\), with a gap of at least \(1/2\) elsewhere.
The large-conductor contribution per unit degree is uniformly \(o(1)\).
Thus \(D\sim V\) bounds the total degree off the good conductors and the lost scalar weight on them by \(o(D)\).
At a good conductor \(n=2^{v_2(j)+1}w\), all odd local factors force the odd part of the test divisor to equal \(w\).
The negative masses over its two-primary levels sum to \(-\varphi(w)\sum_{t=0}^{v_2(j)}\varphi(2^t)=-\varphi(n)\).
The same grouping on the prescribed side gives every \(n\mid 2j,\ n\nmid j\) with positive coefficient \(\varphi(n)\) before the overall minus sign.
Arbitrary signs on the separate \((j,n)\) coordinates therefore bound their total absolute discrepancy by \(o(D)\).
Projection onto \(n\) can only decrease the discrepancy, and restoring bad conductors and rounding change it by \(o(D)+O(k^2)=o(D)\).
The resulting multiplicities give (3) and taking their minima gives the asserted common divisor.

For a uniform root of \(X^j+1\), the order has exact two-adic valuation \(v_2(j)+1\) and its odd component is uniform on the cyclic group of size \(\operatorname{odd}(j)\).
When \(a\to0\), geometric weighting of \(j\) gives uniform residues modulo every fixed modulus, since \(aH\to\infty\).
The contribution from \(2^h\) is one for \(h=0,1\) and \(2^{1-h}\) for \(h\ge2\).
At an odd \(p^t\), summing \((1-p^{-1})p^{-v}(1-p^{t-1-v})\) for \(v\ge t\) gives \(p^{1-t}/(p+1)\).
The joint law factors first at bounded valuation by the Chinese remainder theorem and then in the limit since the discarded tails have arbitrarily small mass.
This gives (4), including \(u=1\).
