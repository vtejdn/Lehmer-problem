## The second positive index and the first possible support

### Position of the second term

The [forward trace construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) gives a degree-\(h\) integral numerator of leading coefficient two with all zeros in \((-2,2)\).
Thus \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^hC(z+z^{-1})\) has degree \(N=2h+2\), leading coefficient \(-2\), constant two and all roots on the circle.
It satisfies \(z^N\Pi(1/z)=-\Pi(z)\), so the midpoint coefficient vanishes and each nonzero coefficient below the midpoint is paired with its negative above it.
In particular \(M(\Pi)=2\) and the [sparse height estimate](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives a coefficient bound uniform in the exponents under a fixed support cap.
An escaping sequence has \(h\to\infty\) since \(q-1\mid C(2)\) and \(C(2)\le 2\cdot4^h\).

By the [first odd coefficient theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#statement), whose [independent review](Yin_R_G0562_v11.md#odd-prefix-review) includes this sparse family, that coefficient is eventually present at a positive index \(o(h)\).
Its presence supplies a second positive support index, since \(N\) is always in the support.
If this second index were at least \(\eta h\) along a subsequence, the first odd coefficient would be the sole positive-index term below \(\eta h\) eventually.
Write it \(a z^d\), where \(d=o(h)\), and extract a fixed odd \(a\) by the height bound.
For \(|a|=1\), agreement with \(2+a z^d\) through \(\lfloor\eta h/2\rfloor\) satisfies every hypothesis of the [arbitrary-offset bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#statement), in particular \(1\le d\le h\) eventually.
For \(|a|\ge3\), the circle with \(r^d=9/10\) gives \(|2+a z^d|\ge |a|(9/10)-2\ge7/10\), while the remaining terms have total modulus \(O((9/10)^{\eta h/d})=o(1)\).
The \(d\) roots of the binomial have modulus \((2/|a|)^{1/d}<r<1\), and Rouché's theorem would place the same number of roots of \(\Pi\) in that disk.
This contradiction proves that the second positive support index divided by \(h\) tends to zero.
At most five terms means at most four by anti-reciprocity, and in this case at most one support index can lie strictly between \(0\) and \(h+1\).
Hence \(q\) is uniformly bounded on the stated support class.

### Two lower frequencies

With exactly six terms the lower half consists of the constant and two terms \(a z^d,b z^e\) with \(d<e<h+1\).
The upper half is forced, \(e=o(h)\) along an escaping sequence, and at least one of \(a,b\) is odd eventually.
After extracting fixed coefficients, write \(d=gm,\ e=gn,\ \gcd(m,n)=1\).
For \(n\to\infty\), a bounded relation \(ud+ve+wN=0\) must have \(w=0\) eventually, since \(d,e=o(N)\).
The remaining primitive relation is \((n,-m,0)\), up to sign, so no nonzero bounded relation can persist.
These are the growing minimum-relation hypotheses of [Lawton's theorem, Theorem 14 and Example 15 in the inspected source](../literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002).
Clearing Laurent monomials preserves Mahler measure before and after specialization.
The specialization of \(2+a x+b y-t(2+a x^{-1}+b y^{-1})\) is exactly \(\Pi\), so the limiting multiplicative measure is two.
For fixed \(x,y\) on the unit torus, the constant and linear coefficients in \(t\) have equal modulus \(|2+a x+b y|\).
Jensen integration in \(t\), followed by \(y\), therefore gives
\[
 \log 2=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max\{|2+a x|,|b|\}\,dm(x)
 \ \ge\int_{\mathbb T}\log|2+a x|\,dm(x)
 =\log\max\{2,|a|\}.
\]
The usual integrable logarithm for a nonzero Laurent polynomial permits the torus integrations, including the measure-zero fibers where both \(t\)-coefficients vanish.
This forces \(|a|\le2\).
If \(|b|>2-|a|\), the maximum strictly exceeds \(|2+a x|\) on an open arc, making the first inequality strict.
Since the rightmost value already equals \(\log2\), we obtain \(|a|+|b|\le2\) and hence \(|a|=|b|=1\).

When \(m,n\) are fixed the specialization vector is \((g,N)\).
Its shortest relation in the sup norm has size \(N/\gcd(g,N)\ge N/g\to\infty\), also for bounded \(g\).
The same integration in \(t\) gives \(M(P)=2\) for \(P=2+a x^m+b x^n\).
Equality with \(|P(0)|\) excludes every root in the open disk, and \(|b|\le M(P)=2\).
If \(|a|>2+|b|\), comparison with \(a x^m\) on the unit circle gives \(m>0\) interior zeros.

It remains to rule out \(|b|=2\).
Equality with the leading coefficient leaves no roots outside the circle either.
Real coefficients now make \(P\) reciprocal up to the factor \(\operatorname{sgn}(b)\).
Its unique nonzero middle term forces \(m=n-m\) and \(a=\operatorname{sgn}(b)a\), hence \(b=2\) and \((m,n)=(1,2)\).
The unit-circle quadratic \(2+a x+2x^2\) then has \(|a|\le4\), with \(a\) odd by the earlier oddness condition.
Using \(N-2g=2(h-g+1)\) and \(X_g(z+z^{-1})=z^g+z^{-g}\) in the exact reciprocal difference gives
\[
  C(x)=(2X_g(x)+a)U_{h-g}(x/2).
\]
Since \(U_{h-g}(x/2)\) is monic integral, Gauss's lemma assigns every primitive nonmonic irreducible factor of \(C\) to \(G=2X_g+a\), with its multiplicity.
Thus \(G\) is an admissible integral multiplier for the [trace obstruction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement), with \(G(2)=4+a\ne0\) and \(\|G\|_{[-2,2]}\le4+|a|\le8\).
The remaining positive leading coefficient \(c\) in that theorem is at least one, so \(\|G\|^2/c\le64\) uniformly in \(g\).
The [exact branch derivative](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(\theta-q\to0\) at \(j=2,\ q\to\infty\), contradicting the divergence required of these ratios.
Consequently \(|b|=1\) and \(|a|\le3\).
The fixed-primitive-pair conditions are necessary and leave open the existence of corresponding Pisot inputs.
