The seed equation gives \(|r-a|=(ar-1)/r^n<a/r^{n-1}\), which is strictly positive.
For \(a=b^k\ge4\) the upper bound \(a/(a-1)^6\le4/729\) gives \(r>a-1/4\).
If the positive \(k\)-th root \(\beta\) belongs to the seed field, powering shows that it generates the field and gives a bijection of conjugate sets.
Its neutral conjugate and its complex conjugate form an inverse pair, so irreducibility makes the minimum reciprocal and identifies its sole interior root as the positive \(\beta^{-1}\).
This is the signature argument of v680 applied to the Salem root classified in v681.
Since \(\beta/b>15/16\), the geometric sum in \((\beta^k-b^k)/(\beta-b)\) exceeds \((31/16)b^{k-1}\), using only its first two terms even when \(k=2\).
At the exterior conjugate this gives the factor \((16/31)b r^{1-n}\) in the upper bound for the absolute value of the norm.
The interior conjugate costs less than \(b\), and the at most \(n-1\) neutral conjugates each cost at most \(b+1\).
The ratio \((b+1)/(b^2-1/4)\le8/(5b)<1\) follows from \((3b+1)(b-2)\ge0\).
Thus the nonzero integral norm would be less than or equal to \((8/5)^6/31=262144/484375<1\).
Repeating with base \(b^{k/\ell}\) and exponent \(\ell\) excludes every prime divisor of \(k\).
The divisor and substitution theorem v205 then covers every stated dilation, allowing arbitrarily high powers of these primes in the dilation exponent.

At an odd \(\ell\) the roots of the two signed congruences are disjoint units and lift uniquely from \(\mathbb F_\ell\) to modulus \(\ell^2\).
Their counts are two for the positive sign and either two or zero for the negative sign according as \(4\mid\ell-1\) or not.
For each fixed cutoff, the uniform prime-count input and the Chinese remainder theorem give the product of the surviving fractions in the reduced residue classes.
The finitely many prime divisors of the modulus do not affect the limiting proportion.
Up to \(Z=(\log X)^2\) the count has at most \(4Z\) separate uniform errors of order \(X/(\log X)^6\).
The resulting error is \(o(\pi(X))\), and the sum of the main terms beyond any fixed cutoff is bounded by the stated tail times \(\operatorname{li}(X)\).
Beyond \(Z\) through \(X^{2/3}\), summing the integer bound \(4(X/\ell^2+1)\) costs \(O(X/Z+X^{2/3})\).
For the larger squares, \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) have no common odd divisor, so neither can carry \(\ell^2>X^{4/3}\) for large \(X\).
For \(p^2+1=t\ell^2\) one has \(t\le2X^{2/3}\).
At square \(t\), the two positive integer factors of \(t\ell^2-p^2=1\) would both have to be one, forcing \(p=0\).
For nonsquare \(t\), the inverse of any norm-minus-one unit in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt t]\) remains in that ring.
The ratio of two distinct positive solutions ordered by \(x+y\sqrt t\) has norm one and thus even integral trace at least four.
This gives multiplicative spacing at least \(2+\sqrt3\) throughout \((1,2X+1)\), uniformly also for nonsquarefree \(t\).
Summing the resulting \(O(1+\log X)\) count over \(t\) gives \(o(\pi(X))\).
These tail estimates justify passage from fixed cutoffs to all odd primes, with a positive product since every factor is positive and its deficits are summable.
Ked07 is used at its inspected absolute-error scope, with no effective numerical density cutoff required.

For an admissible \(p\ge5\), the two neighbors contribute only powers of two to the lcm defining \(H(p^2)\), and the central entry is exactly \(p\).
The perfect-power obstruction excludes two from \(R\), and v716 excludes the seed prime \(p\) at both signs and all delays.
Thus \(R\mid H(p^2)\) forces \(R=1\).
The exception bound in v703 counts seeds with a failure at any delay or sign already, so substitution of \(X^2\) and injection of \(p\mapsto p^2\) keep the bound \(\exp(O(\sqrt{\log(X+1)}))\).
Discarding \(p=3\) adds at most one.
This yields the same relative density among primes for admissibility together with the all-delay conclusion.
The only other original factors have roots \(\pm1\), whose preimages at all dilations remain roots of unity.
The unique noncyclotomic factor therefore has measure in logarithmic units \(\log r>\log(p^2-1)\), whereas \(H(p^2)\ge p\) gives the claimed vanishing earlier lower estimate uniformly in both sign and delay.
