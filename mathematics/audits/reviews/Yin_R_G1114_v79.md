## The complete prime-order construction and its degree-window representatives

On 2026-11-16T17:19 UTC agent-S independently inspected the fixed C0899v4 [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v4.md#statement), its three argument sections and all seven direct input scopes for Yin.
The reviewer was separately developing a two-adic trace argument and received the new Claim without proof-development notes, author explanations, mathematical summary or a prior verdict on this construction.
The reviewer had independently examined the earlier prime-substitution interface and used the dyadic transport result in a different investigation.
The simultaneous prime count, restrictions on every contributing characteristic, exact residue degrees and valuations, uniform norm estimate, transport and both versions of the gap equivalence hold as stated.

The Chebyshev estimates give \(\log M\asymp y\) and allow \(s,u\) in their distinct prescribed intervals, beyond the primes in \(M\).
The residue modulo \(s\) is nonzero and all the selected classes modulo \(u\) are nonzero, so every CRT class is reduced for the full modulus \(Msu\).
The inspected least-prime theorem applies uniformly to these composite moduli.
Distinct classes modulo \(u\) yield \(u-1\) distinct primes, all at least \(M-1\) and at most a constant times \((Msu)^{5.18}\).
In particular \(y\asymp\log p\) with absolute constants and the count is exponential in \(y\).

The congruence \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) makes the two-part of \(p-1\) equal to two, and \(p\equiv-1\bmod q\) excludes every odd prime \(q\le D\) from \(p-1\).
For \(f\le D\), the order of any solution of \(\ell^f\equiv1\bmod p\) therefore divides two.
For prime candidates \(ip\pm1\le yp-1\), the coefficient \(i\) must be positive and even once \(p\) is large.
An odd prime factor of \(i+1\le y+1\) eliminates \(ip-1\), and an odd prime factor of \(i-1\) eliminates \(ip+1\) for \(i>2\).
The chosen prime \(s\) eliminates \(2p+1\), and in each case the divisor is smaller than the candidate.
This gives \(\ell>c p\log p\) after adjusting the same absolute \(c\) used in the statement.
The logarithm of the integer used for the last exclusions is bounded by \(\log|Q(1)|+2\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|\), with \(|Q(1)|\ge1\) by nontorsion.
The root-product estimate \(\log|Q(1)|\le D\log2+m(Q)\) and the nonzero discriminant bound leave only \(O(D\log(2D)/y)\) removed orders, negligible against \(u-1\) uniformly for \(D\le y\).

Modulo \(p\), the only possible common root with \(\Phi_p\) is one, already excluded via \(Q(1)\).
At any other primitive-resultant contributor, a common root has exact order \(p\) and lies in an extension of degree at most \(D\), so its characteristic is in one of the required classes.
A discriminant prime in either class would have had \(p\mid\ell^2-1\), which the final exclusion rules out.
The index of the order generated by \(\alpha\) is therefore prime to \(\ell\), giving the entire residue field from the reduced root.
Its Frobenius orbit length is \(\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)\), which is one or two according to sign.
At the contributing prime ideals \(\alpha-1\) is a unit, so \(\Phi_p(\alpha)\) and \(\alpha^p-1\) have identical valuations there.

The signed estimate for \(z^p-1\) applies because a nontorsion irreducible polynomial has no torsion root.
Extracting the divisor \(Q(1)\) gives the claimed logarithmic error.
Relative to \(p\,m(Q)\) its error is bounded by a constant times \(D^3\log^4(2D)\log(2p)/p\), in addition to \(O(D\log^3(2D)/p)\) for \(Q(1)\).
Both vanish uniformly because \(D\le y\ll\log p\), and replacement of \(p\) by \(p-1\) in the main term costs only relative \(1/p\).

The open interval for \(a\) contains a prime uniformly by the dyadic lower count.
Reducibility of \(z^a-\alpha\) over \(K\) gives an \(a\)-th root \(\gamma\in K\) by the prime-degree factor-product argument.
Then \(\gamma\) is a unit and generates \(K\), since its power already generates \(K\).
Its measure in logarithmic units is \(m(Q)/a\), so the degree bound gives \(a\ll\log^3(2D)\), incompatible with \(a>p/(2D)\) uniformly.
The extension tower gives irreducibility of \(Q(z^a)\), unchanged measure and \(d<p<2d\), while powering permutes the primitive \(p\)-th roots and preserves \(U_p\).
At a base contributor the characteristic exceeds \(a,p\), and \(z^a-\alpha\) has simple roots after reduction.
Only one of them lies in the cyclic group generated by the order-\(p\) residue of \(\alpha\), while the others have order \(ap\).
The unique returning root lifts by Hensel to the base completion, so the returning prime above has identical residue field, completion and integral valuation normalization.
For the lifted root \(\tau\) with \(\tau^a=\alpha\), the geometric quotient of \(\tau^{ap}-1\) by \(\tau^p-1\) reduces to the unit \(a\).
Conversely a returning residue upstairs has \(a\)-th power of order \(p\) downstairs.
This checks preservation of the complete contributing valuations, irrespective of the other local factors.

An absolute gap supplies a uniform lower bound on \(m(Q)\) in the forward direction.
In the weaker converse one fixes each \(Q\) before taking its unbounded sequence of representatives, so \(d>p/2\) and the norm asymptotic force \(m(Q)\ge c_1/2\) for that base with a universal constant.
Irreducible factors with nonunit leading or nonzero constant coefficient have measure at least \(\log2\), and a factor \(z\) contributes zero.
This verifies transfer to the unrestricted positive-gap question and leaves its absolute lower bound open.
The review checked applicability of the retained least-prime and degree estimates without auditing their external source proofs anew.
