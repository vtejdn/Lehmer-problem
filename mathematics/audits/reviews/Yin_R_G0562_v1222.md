# Independent review of iterations for a product of small real Salem numbers

<a id="finding"></a>
## Marked embeddings, quadratic lifts and the least trace field

At 2026-11-25T05:08 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v162 independently reviewed for Yin the fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v162.md#statement) and its four proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or previous findings on v162.
The least-field criterion, its uniformity in the number of inputs and their exponents, and the two persistent factors in the divergent case follow in both parameter ranges.

Write \(K_i=\mathbb Q(x_i)\) and \(t_i=x_i+x_i^{-1}\).
The trace field \(F_i=\mathbb Q(t_i)\) is totally real, of degree \(n_i\ge2\), and \(K_i/F_i\) is quadratic.
Precisely the marked embedding of \(F_i\) takes \(t_i\) outside \([-2,2]\).
A Salem number has a unique strictly largest conjugate modulus, attained at itself, so every positive power generates the same embedded field.
If two inputs in distinct root fields were multiplicatively dependent, the equality of suitable nonzero powers would identify these fields.
Their measures are their actual positive values, allowing use of the absolute rank-two comparison in v47.
In a repeated root field the proper-subfield property identifies the common trace field, and its norm-one unit group modulo sign is cyclic by the unit ranks.
Each marked value greater than one is a positive integral power of its positive primitive generator.
Replacing repeated sources by that generator can only decrease their total unpowered log sum.

For \(F=\prod_i F_i\) in the sense of field compositum, the marking probabilities are counts of extensions of embeddings.
Conditioning on the identity on \(F_i\), the additional condition on \(F_j\) fixes \(F_iF_j\) pointwise.
Thus the conditional probability is \(1/[F_iF_j:F_i]\) even if the trace fields intersect or are nonnormal.
Set \(q=[F_iF_j:\mathbb Q]\) and \(D=[K_iK_j:\mathbb Q]\), so \(D\le4q\le4n_in_j\).
The rank-two inequality gives \(m_i m_j\ge c(2n_i)(2n_j)/(D\log(3D)^{212})\), and consequently bounds \(n_i/q\) by \(C m_i m_j\log(12n_in_j)^{212}/n_j\).
For \(n_j\ge n_i\) the factor multiplying the two log measures is at most \(C L(n_i)\).
Since \(m_i\le S\), it suffices to take \(C\delta^2 L(2)\le1/8\) or \(CH^2 L(\min n_i)\le1/8\).
The second choice is possible uniformly above a degree threshold because every fixed power of a logarithm grows more slowly than its argument.
These choices are independent of the number of sources.
Equal embedded trace fields would give conditional probability one, contrary to (1), and restriction to the compositum of any subfamily preserves its relevant probabilities.

For a nonempty index set \(J\), select one of its least-degree fields \(F_i\).
Among the embeddings marking \(i\), at most \(1/8\) also mark some other index of \(J\).
There is also an embedding marking none of \(J\), since \(\Pr(E_j)\le\Pr(E_j\mid E_i)\) for each other \(j\) by the elementary degree bound \([F_iF_j:F_i]\le n_j\), and \(\Pr(E_i)=1/n_i\le1/2\).
At these two embeddings the product of the corresponding discriminants \(t_j^2-4\) has respectively \(|J|-1\) and \(|J|\) negative factors.
In particular it is negative at one real place of the totally real compositum and cannot be a square.
This rules out every nonempty squareclass relation, including odd support sizes.
Successive adjunctions therefore give degree \(2^s\) over \(F\): an element in a preceding multiquadratic extension whose square is nonzero in \(F\) lies in a simultaneous sign eigenspace, spanned over \(F\) by a single square-root monomial.
All choices above each trace embedding are consequently available.
The root compositum equals the field of the powered product, since equality in its conjugate modulus upper bound requires every positive-power factor to attain its unique maximum.
Thus the lifts enumerate the conjugates with the multiplicities used in computing Mahler measure.

For a given base embedding, only marked indices have nonzero real log modulus.
Each has its two real reciprocal logarithms, and the independent signs of the unmarked indices merely repeat these same logs in the sum of positive parts.
Collecting coefficients gives the displayed integer \(c_i\), using exponent \(a_i c_i\) on the actual positive value \(x_i\).
For a fixed choice of the other signs, the contribution at a marked index is the difference of two indicators, at \(T+U_i\) and \(T-U_i\), with the same strict positive threshold.
It is always zero or one and equals one for an exclusive marking, also when some other lifts have ties.
This proves (2) with its factor \(2^{s-1}\), so no cancellation can undo the lower bound from an exclusive marking.
After deleting indices with coefficient zero the same field and lift argument applies to the remaining powered product, whatever its current log measure.

If \(F_0\) is least by inclusion, its other marking events lie inside \(E_0\).
At most \(1/8\) of \(E_0\) is lost to their union, so \(c_0>0\) and \(c_j/c_0\le(1/8)/(7/8)=1/7\) for any other survivor.
The sum of their weighted log contributions relative to \(U_0\) therefore decreases geometrically, including across deletions.
Once that ratio is below one the sign of every sum with a marked coordinate is decided by the sign at zero.
Flipping any other marked coordinate leaves the indicator unchanged, giving exponent zero for that index.
The remaining positive power of \(x_0\) is fixed because it is itself the only exterior conjugate.

If there is no least field, finiteness gives two incomparable minimal fields \(F_u,F_v\), chosen in order of the two smallest degrees among minimal fields.
The first has degree no larger than any source and therefore an exclusive marking by (1).
Every source of strictly smaller degree than \(F_v\) contains \(F_u\), since it must contain a minimal field of that smaller degree or below.
Its event is contained in \(E_u\), which covers at most half of \(E_v\) by incomparability and \([F_uF_v:F_v]\ge2\).
The events of degree at least \(n_v\) other than \(v\) cover at most a further \(1/8\) of \(E_v\).
This also covers tied degrees and gives an exclusive marking of \(v\).
Restricting either witness embedding keeps it exclusive after any deletions that keep the two sources.
With at least two survivors, (2) multiplies both marked exponents by at least two at every step, giving divergence.
The criterion classifies the iterates of these products under the common size or degree conditions, including the one-source case and the corresponding two-source specialization of v157.
Existence of Salem numbers tending to one remains open.
