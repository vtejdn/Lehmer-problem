## Actual roots, character stabilizers and the two sign obstructions

The reviewer received the fixed claim and scope without proof-development dialogue or prior findings about v83.
The measure bound with the actual block stabilizer, the exact real-conjugate proportions and both directions of the odd-relation criterion hold.

**The actual half-block product.**
The commuting involutions on \(V\) generate the elementary abelian image \(C_0\), whose rational sign characters give a direct-sum decomposition of the conjugate classes.
Inflate these characters to the actual group \(C\), which may have a kernel on \(V\).
For \(O=Cw\), the product \(b\) of its distinct actual members is fixed by \(C\).
Every \(g^{-1}cg\) fixes \(b\), hence all conjugates of \(b\) are real.
It is an algebraic unit in \(D(w)\), so the sharp totally real transfer and \(m<\log\phi\) give \(b=\pm1\).
The block average therefore gives \(D e_1[w]=[b]=0\).
Some nontrivial projection must be nonzero because \(w\) is nontorsion.

For each active \(\chi\), the stabilizer \(C_w\) of the actual root is contained in \(\ker\chi\), since \(e_\chi[t w]=\chi(t)e_\chi[w]\).
Thus the positive and negative cosets of \(\ker\chi\) descend to exactly \(D/2\) distinct roots each, regardless of the action kernel or of torsion-equivalent actual roots.
The character-weighted group sum, divided by \(|C_w|\), gives
\[
         D e_\chi[w]=[y_\chi]-[b/y_\chi]=2[y_\chi]\ne0 .
\]
Any \(t\in C\) of negative sign exchanges the halves exactly, so \(t(y_\chi)=b/y_\chi\) as actual units.
Some conjugation \(t=g^{-1}cg\) has negative sign since the conjugations generate \(C\).
Consequently \(g(b)=g(y_\chi)\overline{g(y_\chi)}\) is positive.
Combined with \(b=\pm1\) this proves the essential equality \(b=1\) and removes the possible sign in the inverse relation.
Every conjugation now sends \(y_\chi\) to itself or its inverse, and one sends it to its inverse.
This makes its trace totally real, its minimum reciprocal and one image neutral.
Kronecker's theorem gives an off-circle image, which is real because its trace is real, and reciprocity supplies a real exterior conjugate.

**The stabilizer cost.**
By normality of \(C\), \(G\) acts transitively on its blocks in the conjugate set of \(w\).
All blocks have size \(D\), and the stabilizer of \(O\) has index \(d/D\).
For \(g\in H\), \(gw\) lies in \(O\) and has the same active set as \(w\), so conjugation by \(g\) permutes \(S\).
The stabilizer of \((O,\chi)\) in \(G\) is the stabilizer \(H_\chi\) of \(\chi\) inside \(H\).
Its index is exactly \((d/D)[H:H_\chi]=(d/D)a_\chi\) by the subgroup index formula, and \(a_\chi\le s\).
This uses no normality of \(H\) or \(H_\chi\).
In a transported block, changing the starting root either preserves or interchanges the two actual halves, giving at most two possible product values per block-character pair.
Hence \(\deg y_\chi\le 2(d/D)a_\chi\).
The ordinary product contains exactly \(D/2\) conjugates of \(w\), so \(h(y_\chi)\le (D/2) h(w)\).
The factor \(D/2\) cancels against the degree bound, giving \(0<m(y_\chi)\le a_\chi m(w)\).
In particular this estimate charges the original unit's total measure at its actual degree, also when that unit includes a torsion twist satisfying the hypotheses.
For a sequence one may choose the minimizing character on each input.
At \(a_\chi=1\) and \(D>2\) the comparator has smaller degree and no larger measure, contradicting strict record minimality or first attainment.
The comparator itself also has commuting conjugations on its class span because of its totally real trace and its reciprocal conjugates.
Since \(D\) is even, only \(D=2\) remains, in which case the actual positive half consists of \(w\) alone.

**Signs and real proportions.**
For an active \(\chi\), the image \(g(y_\chi)\) is real precisely when \(\chi(g^{-1}cg)=+1\).
A negative sign gives a unit-circle image that cannot be real, because a conjugate equal to \(1\) or \(-1\) would make \(y_\chi\) torsion.
Uniform averaging on \(G\) weights every distinct conjugate equally, so \(p_\chi\) is exactly its real-conjugate fraction.
It is positive since a nonneutral real conjugate exists.
Every occurring character is a transport of an active one because the orbit of \([w]\) spans \(V\).
Both \(D\) and \(p_\chi\) are unchanged by such transport, even for characters from another block.
Applying the real-place height estimate \(h(y_\chi)>p_\chi^2/(100\log(8/p_\chi))\) and the actual half-block height cost gives the stated strict bound with \(50\) in the denominator.

For fixed \(g\), the projections of \((1+g^{-1}cg)[w]\) onto the active eigenspaces are \((1+\chi(g^{-1}cg))e_\chi[w]\).
The direct sum makes this class nonzero exactly when at least one of the active signs is positive.
After applying \(g\), the class is that of the positive real number \(\lvert g(w)\rvert^2\), which is torsion precisely when it is one.
The union probability is therefore exactly the nonneutral fraction \(\delta\) of the original unit.
The union bound gives \(\max_{\chi\in S} p_\chi\ge\delta/s\), and monotonicity of \(p^2/\log(8/p)\) gives (2).

In an odd product relation of length \(h\), at least one of its characters has positive sign at every conjugation.
The union bound gives a participating character with \(p_\chi\ge1/h\), so \(m>(d/D)/(50h^2\log(8h))\).
Because \(d/D\ge1\), this excludes bounded odd relation lengths along any shrinking sequence, also for relations among characters from different blocks.
In the absence of odd relations, the assignment of one to each occurring character over \(\mathbb F_2\) respects every relation and so defines a linear functional on their span.
Extending it to \(C_0^\vee\) and using finite-dimensional duality supplies an element of \(C_0\) acting by sign \(-1\) on every occurring eigenspace.
Conversely this element rules out an odd relation by evaluation.
This is a common inversion on \(V\), with no assertion that its lift in \(C\) must invert every actual conjugate without a torsion factor.
