## The two unique minimizers and the finite limits at growing degree

The review uses the proved Salem factor and residue criteria in v478 and v392 at the interfaces specified below.

Among divisors of \(N=2mn+1\), the cofactor test of v478 removes the nonidentity orders dividing \(a=m-1\) or \(b\), where \(b=(m-2)/2\) for even \(m\) and \(b=1\) for odd \(m\).
Its additional exclusion of \(3\) accounts for the sole possible nonidentity order of weight one.
Thus the forbidden orders form the union of the divisor sets of \(a,b,3\).
On a single prime \(p\), the forbidden exponents are precisely the integers from zero through \(t_p\).
For support containing at least two primes, a pair \(pq\) dividing neither \(a\) nor \(b\) is permitted and is minimal exactly when both singleton primes are forbidden.
If every pair divides one of \(a,b\), their coprimality puts the whole support in one integer, say \(c\).
A divisor on that support which fails to divide \(c\) contains some \(p^{v_p(c)+1}\), already permitted since \(v_p(c)\ge1\), the other integer is coprime to \(p\), and \(v_p(3)\le1\).
These alternatives exhaust the minimal divisors, including pairs in which \(3\) divides neither \(a\) nor \(b\).
The admissibility test then follows because each permitted order dividing \(N\) contains a minimal permitted divisor.

For each listed \(e\), the specified congruences give \(eu\equiv1\bmod 2m\) and exclude every prime up to \(e\) from \(u\), including those dividing \(2m\).
Every divisor of \(eu\) at most \(e\) consequently divides \(e\), so minimality under divisibility makes \(e\) the numerical minimum throughout an infinite progression.
For \(p\ge5\), the class \(m\equiv-1\bmod p\) has \(p\nmid a,b\), since neither \(2\) nor \(3\) vanishes modulo \(p\).
The class \(m\equiv1+p^{j-1}\bmod p^j\) gives \(v_p(a)=j-1\) and \(p\nmid b\), including \(p=3,\ j=2\).
For distinct odd primes, imposing even \(m\), \(p\mid m-1\) and \(q\mid m-2\) gives \(p\mid a,\ q\mid b\), with each prime absent from the other integer.
These progressions realize every stated type along unbounded \(m\), and the fixed-\(m\) list excludes any other type.

Expanding \((x-1)(x^N f_m-f_m^*)\) gives the displayed \(G_{m,N}\), with the sign of \(A_{m,n}\) for \(x>1\).
After dividing by \(x^{N+m-1}\), the two positive error terms are \(x^{1-m}\) and \(x^{1-N}\), both uniformly small in \(n\) at each fixed \(1<x<2\).
The remaining error tends uniformly to zero as well.
Together with the unique exterior root and \(\sigma_{m,n}<2\), the negative limit \(x-2\) proves uniform convergence of the root to two.
At \(x=\varphi\), the terms \(1-\varphi^{m-3}+\varphi^{m-N}\) in \(G_{m,N}(\varphi)/\varphi^N\) already have negative sum for \(m\ge4\), as \(N-m\ge m+1>1\).
Hence these roots exceed \(\varphi\), while the \(m=3\) roots increase strictly to it, proving uniqueness of the input minimum.
Direct substitution into \(A_{3,1}(x)=x^6+(1-x-x^2)(1+x^3)\) gives \(-41/64\) at \(3/2\).
For \(m=3\) the minimal divisors are the primes of \(6n+1\), all at least five, and \(5\mid6n+1\) first at \(n=4\).

Strict increase for \(m=3\) therefore singles out precisely \((3,4,5)\).

Conversely a finite parameter set bounds all input degrees.
