### Recovering the direction and the factor

The parent has unit endpoints.
Its monic integral factors have unit constant terms, so every conjugate of each root is an algebraic unit and Kronecker's theorem gives at least one exterior root of each nontorsion factor.
Large slope gives simplicity, reciprocity and a unique pole lift for each exterior root by the individual estimates in C345 v401.
A common zero \(v\) of the two blocks would be nonzero and satisfy \(v^s=v+1,\ v^{s-1}=-v\), giving \(v^2+v+1=0\) and \(v^{s-2}=-1\), incompatible for a cube root of unity.

Let \(A,B'\) be integers with \(aA+bB'=1\).
For \(v=x^a\) the section equation makes \(x^b=-g(v)/B(v)\) nonzero, hence \(x=v^A(-g(v)/B(v))^{B'}\).
The inclusions \(\mathbb Q(v)\subseteq\mathbb Q(x^{a/q})\subseteq\mathbb Q(x)\) are therefore equalities.
Every conjugate of \(x\) gives a different projected conjugate, preserving the degree, the number of exterior roots and reciprocity.
If two projected minimal polynomials agree, choose a common exterior root \(y\) and set \(v=y^q\).
Then \(\log|v|>0\), and \(\log|g(v)/B(v)|/\log|v|\) recovers \(b/a\) for both directions.
Reduction of this positive fraction recovers \(a,b\) uniquely, and the displayed Bézout formula recovers the same \(x\), proving that the original factors agree.
This also proves distinctness for different factors within one section.
The product from any finite collection of distinct direction-factor pairs is consequently monic, integral and squarefree.

### Why the weighted collision count leads to a global bound

The pole weight \(\omega_\lambda\) is repeated \(q\) times over each original pole, so its sum over all projected lifts is \(q\).
Each projected exterior root has the form \(y=\lambda\exp(u/q)\) with \(|u|\ll_s|\tau_j|^{-\rho}\).
The modulus of \(\lambda\) is at most \(\max_j|\tau_j|\), and \(\log|y|\ge(\min_j\log|\tau_j|)/(2q)\) for all sufficiently large \(R\), uniformly in \(q\).
For distinct roots at the same lift this bounds their difference by \(O_s(|\tau_j|^{-\rho}/q)\) and \(|y\bar y'-1|\) below by a positive constant depending on \(s\) times \(1/q\).
Their disk distance ratio is \(O_s(\exp(-\rho I\omega_\lambda))\), regardless of whether they came from the same factor.
The map \(y\mapsto1/\bar y\) carries every such pair to a distinct interior pair with the same ratio.

Write \(M\) for the logarithmic measure of the projected product.
Taking the \(a/q\)-th power scales the logarithmic measure of each factor by exactly \(a/q\) since the roots project without repetitions.
The individual pole and degree estimates therefore give \(M=(IW/q)(1+O_s(e^{-c_sR}))\), \(D\asymp_s\rho W\) and \(D\le(1+\eta)\rho W(1+O_s(\varepsilon_R))\).
The disk Vandermonde bound for the product of normalized reflected distances is \(D^D\) after squaring.
The discriminant has absolute value at least one.
There are \(N_\lambda(N_\lambda-1)\) ordered exterior pairs at lift \(\lambda\), each with its interior counterpart.
These facts give the stated factor \(2\) on the collision side and \(D\log D+2(D-1)M\) on the other.
The lower bound \(\omega_\lambda\ge\omega_*=\min_j\omega_j>0\) absorbs the absolute loss in the logarithm of each distance ratio at relative cost \(O_s(1/R)\).
After division by \(2\rho I\), the degree logarithm costs \(O_s(W\log(2D))\), whereas the measure term is at most \((1+O_s(\eta+\varepsilon_R))W^2/q\).
Adding the diagonal contribution \(\sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda N_\lambda=W\) and subtracting \(W^2/q\) gives exactly the weighted variance (2).
In particular its coefficient of \(W^2/q\) before centering tends to one as the interval width and \(\varepsilon_R\) tend to zero.

Assigning each qualifying factor to one lift and one sign prevents cancellations in the accumulated defect.
For that group the chosen term alone contributes at least \(\omega_*\delta^2W^2/q^2\).
With \(q,\delta\) fixed, sufficiently narrow relative intervals and then sufficiently large \(R\) absorb the first error in (2).
Every nonempty finite group then has \(W\ll_{s,q,\delta}\log(2D)\ll_{s,q,\delta}\log(2RW)\), which gives \(W\ll_{s,q,\delta}\log(2R)\) by absorbing \(\log W\).
There are finitely many intervals independent of \(R\) and at most \(2qt\) groups in each.
Since \(h(P)\ge\omega_* k(P)\), the resulting bound applies to the sums of \(k(P)\) over every finite subcollection, hence to the entire collection without a bound on \(a\).
At a missing projected lift the relative defect equals one, and \(k(P)<qt\) forces such a missing lift.

The count of exterior poles is \(t=s-1-2\lfloor(2s-1)/6\rfloor\).
It gives \(t=1\) at \(s=4\) and \(t\ge3\) at even \(s\ge6\).
At sufficiently large slope the only real exterior root, when present, is negative and occurs for even \(s\) and odd \(a\).
If it becomes a Salem number after sign change, its factor has exactly one exterior root and therefore misses a pole when \(t\ge3\).
Each such direction contributes at least one to the exterior count for \(q=\delta=1\), including \(a=1\).
For \(s=4\) and fixed \(q\ge2\), the same argument counts all factors with \(k(P)<q\) on directions with \(q\mid a\).
Here the implied constant may depend on \(q\), whereas the earlier quartic count was uniform in odd \(q\) on its narrower Salem class.

### Growing values of \(q\) for an individual factor

Using a singleton in (2) gives \(D=\deg P,\ W=h(P),\ \eta=0,\ \rho=R=r\).
Dividing by \(\omega_* h(P)^2/q^2\) bounds the largest squared relative lift defect by the right side of (3), with constant depending only on \(s\).
The Laurent graph is irreducible by coprimality of the two coefficients of its linear polynomial in the second variable, and \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\) witness support rank two.
The primitive direction has height \(b\) at large slope and dilation one.
The primitive degree bound therefore gives \(\deg P\gg_s b(\log\log b/\log b)^3\).
Together with \(\deg P\asymp_s r h(P)\) this gives \(h(P)\gg_s a(\log\log b/\log b)^3\).
Also \(\log(2\deg P)\ll_s\log b\) by the parent degree.
Substitution proves (4), including its uniformity over every factor and divisor \(q\mid a\).
When its right side tends to zero it excludes zero occupancy at every projected lift.

For a missing original pole, the global count at \(q=\delta=1,\ R=r\) gives \(h(P)\le k(P)\ll_s\log(2r)\).
Combining this with the preceding lower bound gives \(a\ll_s \log(2r)(\log b/\log\log b)^3\).
Since \(r\le b\), it follows that \(\log a=O_s(\log\log b)\), hence \(\log r\sim\log b\) and \(\log\log r\sim\log\log b\).
This gives the claimed fourth power of \(\log(2r)\) and inverse third power of \(\log\log(3r)\).

In the example, \(\gcd(a,a^2+1)=1\), and for large \(j\) the exponent of two defining \(q\) is nonnegative and at most \(2j\).
The ceiling changes \(q\) by at most a factor of two from \(2^j/j^2\), giving \(q\asymp\sqrt a/(\log a)^2\).
Since \(r=a+1/a\), the first term of (4) is \(O((\log a)^{-3/2})\) and the second is \(O_s((\log\log a)^{-3})\).
Thus all projected counts have the stated relative asymptotic for all nontorsion factors in this family.
Here \(a\asymp r\) is outside the earlier first-exponent range \(a\ll_s\sqrt{R/\log(2R)}\) in a dyadic window containing \(r\).
The conclusions concern distribution of the roots among lifts and give no uniform positive lower bound for the measure of each factor or proof of irreducibility.
