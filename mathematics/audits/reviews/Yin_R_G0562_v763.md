Review date (UTC): 2026-11-20T19:31.

A reviewer who took no part in developing the proof inspected the fixed claim and its cited mathematical inputs without development discussion, exploration notes, author explanations or earlier review findings for this claim.
The proof establishes (1) for every Salem number under the stated hypotheses.

For the cited power-basis construction, inserting \(q=2s\), \(h=2\) and tolerance \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\) gives \(2s\) neutral factors of size at most \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\) and two real factors of size \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-s/2})\).
Their product bounds the absolute norm uniformly.
Since the matrix of multiplication by \(c\) on \(R\) has determinant \(N(c)\), the possible principal ideals occur among finitely many sublattices.
Within each ideal the quotient by its chosen generator and its inverse both lie in \(R\).
Squaring gives a unit positive at both real embeddings and preserves the required exponents in (2).
For the period selection, \(q\le H^s\) gives \(H^{-1}\le q^{-1/s}\), and an infinite bounded subsequence of differences as \(H\to\infty\) would give a positive common root-of-unity exponent.
Thus \(n=q+1\) is unbounded, with \(z^n\to z\ne 1\) at every neutral conjugate.

The trace pairing in the two indicated power bases has determinant \(\pm1\) by column reversal, so \(R^\vee=P'(\alpha)^{-1}R\) holds in the digit order.
Multiplication by the unit \(u\) preserves \(R\), giving the asserted conductor ideal exactly.
The norm of its generator has magnitude \(\asymp_P\alpha^n\) along the selected periods, which ensures nontriviality.
In fact the phase sequence also has least period \(n\) eventually.
A proper least period \(h\mid n\) would annihilate the character under multiplication by \(\alpha^h-1\), by testing the phases on the powers spanning \(R\).
The conductor equality would imply \(|N(\alpha^n-1)|\le |N(\alpha^h-1)|\ll_P\alpha^{n/2}\), contradicting the lower bound.
Taking the fair-digit expectations gives exactly the \(n\) absolute cosine factors.

At the two ends, replacing \(1/(z^n-1)\) and \(z^n/(z^n-1)\) by their values at \(z\) has error \(O_P(n^{-1/s})\) before multiplication by \(b_z\).
The opposite real tail has size \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-s}\alpha^{J-n})\), and \(\alpha^J\ll_P\varepsilon^{-s}\) controls this uniformly.
The trace differences for \(L_j\) and \(U_l\) follow from \((r-1)f_r=b_r\) and \((\alpha-1)f_\alpha=b_\alpha\), respectively.
The signs of \(P'\) at the two real roots make both \(f_\alpha,f_r\) positive.
The involution exchanges the two terms in \(W_p\) up to the common factor \(\alpha\), so \(W_p>0\) and its image differs from itself.
Subtracting \(W_p\) from the rational trace gives an irrational approximant in \(K\) at both ends.
Multiplication by \(D_P\) clears the denominators even for negative exponents, since \(\alpha\) is a unit.
Every field embedding bounds the resulting approximant by \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-2s})\) using \(\alpha^{J+1}\ll_P\varepsilon^{-s}\).
The same bound holds for the nearest doubled half-integer.
The norm of the nonzero cleared difference then gives separation \(\gg_P\varepsilon^{2s(d-1)}\).

Writing \(a=2s(d-1)\), the ratio of the neutral endpoint error bound to this separation is \(O_P(A^{1-a})\) since \((a-1)\delta_d=1/s\).
Choosing \(A\) once and taking large \(n\) also absorbs \(\varepsilon^{-2s}\alpha^{-n}\).
The \(2J\) end factors therefore cost \(O_P((\log n)^2)\) in negative logarithm.
A fixed sufficiently large \(J_0\) puts all the middle phases within \(1/4\) of zero, where the cost is bounded by a constant times the square.
The two squared geometric tails cost \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-2s}\alpha^{-2J})=O_P(1)\), and the neutral terms cost \(O_P(n\varepsilon^2)\).
As \(s\ge1\), one has \(1-2\delta_d\ge 3/5\), with equality in degree four, so the stated power absorbs the endpoint cost.
The earlier qualitative result concerns the same binary law and serves as a comparison only.
The constants and the sequence of periods depend on the input polynomial, and the positive Mahler gap remains open.
