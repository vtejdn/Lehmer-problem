# Independent review of the two-thirds lower bound for separated blocks

## Finding

Review time (UTC) 2026-11-28T07:42.
An agent outside proof development independently examined the entire fixed [width argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0904_v7.md#width) for Yin.
The review used the proof and its cited mathematical inputs without exploration notes, development dialogue, separate author explanations or a prior verdict on this argument.

Removing a monomial preserves the full polynomial, its degree, the exterior-root count \(k\) and the end-block log measure \(m\), while increasing \(n\) and decreasing \(r\).
Any common irreducible divisor of the remaining \(Q,Q^*\) would have degree below \(\deg P\) and divide \(P\).
Conjugation therefore excludes unit-circle zeros, and every irreducible factor of \(Q\) satisfies the hypotheses of Smyth's bound, including the nonzero constant term.
The constant block gives \(X^n\pm1\).
Thus \(m\ge m_0=\log\theta\) and \(k\ge1\) in every admissible case.
For the record application, the unique exterior root of the degree-ten competitor lies between \(1\) and \(19/16\) by the two endpoint signs, and \((19/16)^3-(19/16)-1<0\).
The source results used here were checked for their statements and applicability at the cited units.
Their original proofs are outside this review.

The two reciprocal-resultant normalizing factors over all ordered root pairs have product \(\exp(2rm)\).
Same-side pairs give the modulus \(|1-w_i\bar w_j|\), and opposite-side pairs give the pseudohyperbolic numerator \(|w_i-w_j|\), bounded by that modulus.
The resultant is a nonzero integer, so expansion of the logarithms bounds \(\sum_{l\ge1}|S_l|^2/l\) by \(2rm\) with all multiplicities.
The one-point kernel bound follows by pairing \(S_l/\sqrt l\) with \(\sqrt l e^{-ly}\).
For two points counted in the same interval, the kernel numerator is \(\gg T\) and the denominator is \(O(T^2)\).
Summing these positive contributions gives the stated occupancy estimate, since \(l e^{-2lT}=O(T^{-1})\).

For the choice of radius, the disk identity bounds the potential of each exterior root with \(\rho\ge1/2\) by \(O(yK(\bar v w))\).
When \(\rho<1/2\), radial alignment minimizes \(\rho\), and \(2a-1-2a^4+a^5\ge0\) for \(7/8\le a=e^{-y}<1\), since \(a^4-a^3-a^2-a+1<0\) there.
This yields \(x_i\le4y\le8T\).
The distance estimate \(|v-w|\le2y\) and the lower bounds on the moduli also confine the arguments to an arc of length \(O(T)\).
Each radial logarithmic singularity has uniformly bounded normalized \(L^2[T,2T]\) norm after rescaling.
As only boundedly many intervals meet the arc, the maximum of the interval sums controls the singular contribution uniformly in the angle.
The integral of the squared maximum is bounded by the sum of their squared norms, which gives the required common radius outside the finite set of singular radii.

For a monic \(F\), the circle mean of \(\log|F|\) at radius \(e^y\), less \(y\deg F\), is the sum of \(\log^+(|\gamma|e^{-y})\) over its roots.
Hence domination by \(z^nQ\) with relative error at most \(\delta\) leaves \(m(P)\ge m-ky+\log(1-\delta)\).
The choices of \(c,\delta\) make this strictly exceed \(\log\lambda\).
When \(m\le k\), dividing the domination threshold by \(T=cm/k\) gives terms of orders \(r\sqrt{k/m}\), \(k\sqrt{r/m}\) and \(k/m\), each \(O_\lambda(r\sqrt{k/m})\) by \(k\le r\) and \(m\ge m_0\).
When \(m>k\), the cited radial average of the separate exterior-root potentials gives \(O(k)\) on one circle with \(c\le y\le2c\), and \(n=O_\lambda(k+1)\) follows.
Consequently \(d=O_\lambda(r^{3/2})\), and \(r=o(d)\) forces \(m/k=O_\lambda((r/d)^2)\).
The number of factors with multiplicities is at most \(m/m_0\), so those with at most \(H\) exterior conjugates account for at most \(Hm/m_0\) exterior roots.

For \(Q(X)=R(X^\ell)\), the circle of log radius \(y/\ell\) covers the entire circle of log radius \(y\) under the power map.
The kernel estimates and the radial comparison apply to \(R\) because it is monic with nonzero constant term and coprime to \(R^*\).
The radial loss for \(Q\) is at most \(k_Ry\), while the growth from its monomial multiplier is \(ny/\ell\).
This gives the compressed estimate in both measure ranges with constants depending only on \(\lambda\).
A common divisor \(b>1\) of \(n,\ell\) produces an integral polynomial of degree \(d/b\) and the same measure by power substitution, contradicting strict degree minimality.
The estimates for \(k_R/m\) and \(q\) follow from \(k_R\le q\) and \(m\ge m_0\).
The proof establishes all the stated conclusions, including the necessary conditions on a hypothetical narrow sequence.
Existence or exclusion of such sequences remains open.
