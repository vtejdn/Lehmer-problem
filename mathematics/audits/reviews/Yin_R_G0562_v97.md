The triangle inequality bounds the normalized distance from above as well, giving comparability to the Mahler measure for both factors, even constants or factors with zeros at zero.
The cyclotomic Möbius product contributes bounded upper and strictly positive lower factors there because \(\sum_{j\ge1}(3/4)^j<\infty\).
Regardless of which summand contains \(\Phi_n\), the relative scales on each circle thus differ from \(\rho_i^j M(V)/M(U)\) by factors bounded above and below in terms of \(H\).
Their comparison parameters have ratio at least \(2^j\), so for large \(j\) some circle has a summand of at least twice the other's modulus throughout.
That larger summand has modulus at least a fixed multiple of \(M(U)\), because the low summand always has this lower bound.
Reverse triangle inequality and monotonicity of the logarithmic circle mean with radius give (1).

For \(k>2d\) and large \(d\), the higher summand alone has degree \(k+\deg B\).
In the reversal its low polynomial is \(B^*\), and the other offset \(k+\deg B-d-\deg T\ge k-d-H\) tends to infinity.
Applying (1) both ways bounds \(M(T)\) and \(M(B)\).
Reversal preserves measure if a zero at the origin lowers the reversed degree, since the moduli agree on the circle.
Bounded measure and degree give finitely many coefficient vectors for either integer factor, and a fixed pair contradicts C345v95 on every unbounded-prime-count subsequence.
If \(k\le d/3\), the low summand alone reaches the degree \(d+\deg T\) for large \(d\).
Its reversal leaves \(T^*\Phi_n\) low and places \(B^*\) at exponent at least \(2d/3-H\).
In the remaining range \(d/3<k\le2d\) the original orientation already works.
Thus the degree bound \(D=O_H(d)\), a shift tending to infinity and a low factor \(T\) chosen from a finite set hold on the subsequence used in the proof.
These conclusions do not require the high factor to have bounded coefficients.

With the low factor now fixed, stripping its fixed power of \(X\) leaves the fixed nonzero integer constant term, since \(\Phi_n(0)=1\) for \(n\ge2\).
Every fixed prefix is eventually unaffected by the high summand and has bounded coefficients by the finite Möbius factors of exponent up to the prefix length.
The inverse power sum of each fixed order is therefore bounded.
Reflecting the inside roots as in C345v95 costs at most \(2j e^{j m(P)}m(P)\) for the order-\(j\) power sum, using \(\sum_{|\alpha|<1}\log|\alpha|^{-1}=m(P)-\log|c|\le m(P)\).

In the localized test of C345v95, the \(l=0\) term retains at least the full diverging positive correlation.

Thus the bounded test, nonnegative weight and bounded signed mean \(\int W L_n\) give divergence of \(\int W L_n^+\) uniformly in every projected angle.

Differentiating \(\log|z-\alpha|\) twice at \(z=e^{i\theta}\) gives the real part of \(\alpha z/(z-\alpha)^2\).
Its modulus is at most \(R/((1-R)^2+4R\sin^2((\theta-\gamma)/2))\) when \(\alpha=Re^{i\gamma}\ne0\), including the limits of roots approaching the circle from either side.
Away from the \(\delta\)-neighborhoods the sum of these bounds for \(B\) is \(O(H/\delta^2)\), and the choice \(\delta=C_H/\sqrt d\) makes it at most \(d/16\).

The original circle-root contribution \(S\) dominates both \(d/4\) and \(4/\ell^2\), hence the remaining negative curvature is at least \(S/2\).
Deleting at most \(H\) circular neighborhoods, even with coincidences or overlap, splits each lifted original root arc into at most \(2H+1\) intervals.

This proves the uniform band bound after summing the original arc lengths and gives the stated restricted cancellation loss by the exponential integral and logarithmic series.

The normalized logarithm of each linear root factor has mean zero and positive part at most \(\log2\).
Since every nonzero integer polynomial \(F\) has \(m(F)\ge0\), its integrated negative logarithmic part is at most \((\deg F)\log2\).
This bounds the weighted negative parts of \(T,B\) uniformly despite arbitrary height for \(B\).
On the removed neighborhoods at least one weight factor is \(O(\delta^4)\), while every other factor is bounded even when projected roots coincide.
Using \(L_n^+\le d\log2\) and the preceding negative-part bound for \(P\) costs \(O_H((d+D)\delta^4)=o(1)\).
The resulting contradiction between the two weighted integrals proves the uniform divergence.
When no roots are projected the complement is the whole circle and the same estimates apply.

After using divergence above a fixed prime-count cutoff, this gives a single gap depending only on \(H\).
If one polynomial factor vanishes, the measure equals that of the other bounded-degree integer polynomial, and bounded-degree finiteness at measure at most two suffices.
