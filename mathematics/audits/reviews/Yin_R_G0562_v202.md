## Simultaneous prime tests and descent of measure

At 2026-11-17T09:55 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed v202 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v202.md#decision-common-power-certificates-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed mathematics without development discussion, exploration notes, author explanations or previous findings on this submission.
All three conclusions hold for either sign, with the size hypothesis on the normalized blocks and no upper bound on the dilation.

Since \(\gcd_i b_i=1\), v198 gives a single simple irreducible noncyclotomic factor \(S_N\) and bounds the whole cyclotomic degree by \(10D\).
The uniform error in its logarithmic measure is \(O(W\exp(-cN/W^2))\); increasing the prime-dependent threshold constant makes this smaller than half the fixed positive main term.
Every root \(\beta\) is a unit because \(P_N\) is monic with constant coefficient \(\varepsilon\).

For prime \(q\), a nontrivial proper factor of \(X^q-u\) gives a product \(v\) of \(r\) roots in the base field, \(0<r<q\), with \(v^q=u^r\).
If \(u\ne0\), Bézout for \(q,r\) gives a \(q\)-th root of \(u\) in that field.
At the next step of an irreducible tower \(X^t-\beta\), a root has norm \((-1)^{t+1}\beta\); if the root were a \(q\)-th power upstairs then its norm would be a \(q\)-th power downstairs.
Ruling out both signs for every selected prime therefore permits arbitrary repetition and mixing of the primes, including \(2\); for odd primes the signs are equivalent.
The degree multiplication at every step is valid because the field of a radical contains its power, and the empty product \(h=1\) poses no exception.
For the real-sign test the two disks at opposite real centers for any even \(b_i\) are exterior, disjoint, and invariant under conjugation.
Each contains a unique simple real root of the sign of its center, and both are roots of the same irreducible \(S_N\).
Thus each of \(\beta,-\beta\) is negative in some real embedding, excluding both squares in \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).

A \(q\)-th root \(\gamma\) of \(\beta\) inside \(K\) generates \(K\) and is a nontorsion unit, since both \(\gamma\) and its inverse are integral over the algebraic integers.
Hence its logarithmic measure is \(B/q\) at the unchanged degree \(d\).
I checked [Dobrowolski's Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-34-4-391-401) on printed p. 391: its contrapositive at a fixed \(\varepsilon=1/2\) bounds \(M(\gamma)-1\) below at large degrees.
Taking logarithms gives the weaker \(m(\gamma)\ge c_1/(\log(2d))^3\); the coefficient bound for monic polynomials of bounded degree and \(M\le2\), together with Kronecker's theorem, extends this to all degrees after decreasing the absolute constant.
This is the accepted theorem statement, not a review of its original proof.
The strict cutoff \(q>B(\log(2d))^3/c_1\) now excludes a \(q\)-th power, giving \(C_1=1/c_1\).

For any finite \(T\), including the empty set, the chosen \(m=4\prod_{q\in T}q\) is greater than one.
At large \(z\) divisible by \(m\) and by every prime on a prescribed finite list, \(\Phi_m(z)>1\) is coprime to \(z\).
A prime divisor \(\ell\) is therefore new and prime to \(m\); separability prevents its root \(z\bmod\ell\) from belonging to \(\Phi_r\) for any proper divisor \(r\) of \(m\).
Its order is \(m\), so such primes \(\ell\equiv1\bmod m\) are unbounded, and finiteness of each bounded totient range gives \(\varphi(\ell-1)>10D\).
Even if a seed equals \(\ell\), the two nonzero reductions \(A,A^*\) exclude at most \(2D\) elements, leaving a primitive \(a\).
Fixing the unique exponent class modulo \(\ell-1\) that makes \(P_N(a)=0\) leaves its derivative affine in \(N\bmod\ell\) with nonzero slope.
Exactly \(\ell-1\) of the \(\ell\) classes survive, hence by coprimality the resulting density among all delays is \(1/\ell\), unchanged by the finite initial cutoff.
If a cyclotomic factor \(\Phi_j\) vanished at \(a\), then \(\ell-1\mid j\) by its order, also when \(\ell\mid j\); the prime-factor formula gives \(\varphi(j)\ge\varphi(\ell-1)>10D\).
Thus the simple root belongs to \(S_N\), lifts with unit derivative to \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), and realizes the required field embedding.
A \(q\)-th root of either sign in \(K\) would have unit image and reduce to a \(q\)-th root of the corresponding sign of \(a\).
For \(q\mid\ell-1\) the generator \(a\) is not such a power; the signs give the same test since \(-1=(-1)^q\) for odd \(q\), and \(-1\) is a square for \(4\mid\ell-1\).
These are simultaneous tests over \(K\), independent of the exponent of each \(q\) in the dilation.

For an individual radical \(\alpha^h=\beta\), let \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):K]\).
The height identity \(h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)=h_{\rm abs}(\beta)/h\) gives \(m(\alpha)=eB/h\).
All roots above \(S_N\) are nontorsion and simple, and all roots above \(U_N\) are torsion, so \(\sum e=h\) over the irreducible noncyclotomic factors.
If \(S_N(X^H)\) is irreducible, \(\alpha^{h/H}\) has degree \(Hd\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(K\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha^{h/H})\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) gives \(H\mid e\).
This gives the claimed integer partition, including the full-degree simple factor for \(H=h\).
For \(\eta=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/K}(\alpha)\), the identity \(\eta^h=\beta^e\) makes \(\eta\) nontorsion and a unit.
Writing \(d_\eta=[\mathbb Q(\eta):\mathbb Q]\le d\), the same height rule gives \(m(\eta)=(d_\eta/d)m(\alpha)\).
The absolute lower bound at degree \(d_\eta\) is at least \(c_1/(\log(2d))^3\); since the sum of the factor measures is \(B\), it gives the stated bound on their number as well.
The lower bound depends on the normalized degree, and the residue argument certifies the specified classes without asserting their necessity.
