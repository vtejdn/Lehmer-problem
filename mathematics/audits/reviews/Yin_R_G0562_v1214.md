## Exceptional destinations, separate sign weights and joint weak limits

At 2026-11-25T04:35 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v154 independently reviewed for Yin the fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v154.md#statement) and both proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its inputs without development discussion or previous findings on v154.
The reviewer developed the separate five-term source v152; the present audit independently checks the application of its fixed orbit and radial-limit statements only.
The common half-arclength lower bound, agreement of the two angular limits and the stated angular-radial independence follow.

First take \(2\le e\le E\) fixed as a range along a subsequence.
In a half with nonzero baseline, the unmarked coefficients all correspond to root logs of the same strict sign.
The half has total log zero because its product is torsion, so the sum of the marked absolute logs is at least the unmarked absolute sum.
In a half with zero baseline all unmarked logs are zero.
This proves \(f_S\ge1/2\) after summing; in the independent model zero baselines give \(f_S=1\).
The orbit of any selected root contains the two orientations of every destination label with the same multiplicity.
On the labels in \(S\), one of those orientations contributes to each strict sign with magnitude \(|t_i|\,|\log|a_j||\), with both contributions zero at a neutral label.
Dividing by the full signed orbit size proves \(\mathbb E[(t_i\ell_i)_\pm{\bf1}_{E_i}]=f_S |t_i|h_1/2\) separately for each sign.

Conditional on the signed image of label \(j\), another label from its block has uniform position among the other \(b-1\) by the supported point stabilizer.
For a label in a different source block one may also fix its block transport and use the independent alternating permutations in that block.
Therefore \(\mathbb P(E_i\mid ga_j)\ll e/(b-1)\) for \(i\ne j\), and \(\mathbb E[|\ell_j|{\bf1}_{E_i}]\ll e h_1/b\).
On the pairwise disjoint events that exactly label \(i\) lands in \(S\), replacing \(L_\pm\) by \((t_i\ell_i)_\pm\) costs at most the sum of \(2|\ell_j|\) over \(j\ne i\).
To extend that expression back to \(E_i\) one loses at most \(2|\ell_i|\) on each overlap with \(E_j\).
The reversed conditioning covers this second error, giving the claimed total \(O_E(h_1/b)\) for either sign under any test between zero and one.
Dropped events have nonnegative weight.

For each retained \(i\), there is a distinct exceptional source position to move because \(e\ge2\).
Holding the signed image of \(i\) and all other nonmoving positions fixed, the varying signed coordinate has a uniform destination in a half with only \(O(E)\) deletions, or in a full block up to \(O(E)\) deletions using a spare zero-coordinate flip.
In the oriented case the baseline monomial is invariant under these same within-half permutations.
The conditional phase is therefore a fixed rotation times a moving phase to an exponent in \(\{\pm1,\pm2\}\).
The v149 fixed-frequency bound survives finitely many deleted positions uniformly in the rotation and fixed data.
This applies also after restricting to \(E_i\), since that event and \((t_i\ell_i)_\pm\) depend on the held label.
Trigonometric approximation gives an arclength mean plus \(o(1)\) for every fixed continuous angular test in this averaging.

The resulting lower bound for the unnormalized sign measure is \(f_S h_1(\sum_i|t_i|)\langle\phi\rangle/2-o_E(h_1)\).
Its denominator is \(\mathbb E L_\pm=\mathbb E|L|/2\) by the unit norm, at most \(h_1\sum_i|t_i|/2\), and at least a constant times \(\sqrt e\,h_1\) by v149.
Thus the normalized lower bound is \(f_S\langle\phi\rangle-o(1)\) for each sign.
If \(f_S\to1\), applying this to \(1-\phi\) gives the reverse limiting inequality.
Using the same moving position without an event yields \(\mathbb E[t_i\ell_i e^{ih\arg gA}]=o_E(h_1)\) for \(h\ne0\); summing gives the vanishing difference between the two normalized sign harmonics.
The constant harmonic agrees exactly.
For general \(e\), any sequence can be further restricted to bounded \(e\) or \(e\to\infty\).
Together with the bounded densities and growing-support Haar limit of v149 this proves all angular statements, including the half-arclength lower bound on open arcs by weak compactness.
These arguments use compression, the supported signed or pure oriented permutations and, in the oriented model, torsion half products, so the stated extension beyond strict records has the needed hypotheses.

For the joint law, when \(e\to\infty\) and \(e\le\sqrt b\), deleting the one moving coordinate changes \(Z\) by \(O(e^{-1/2})\) in expected absolute value using the same first-moment lower bound.
A bounded Lipschitz radial test can therefore be frozen at the remaining log, and the conditional angular harmonic vanishes uniformly.
For \(e>\sqrt b\), the cost of freezing it at the mean over the \(K\) compensated swaps is \(O(K/\sqrt e)=o(1)\), since \(K=O_h(\log(3d))\) and \(b\) grows faster than every fixed power of \(\log(3d)\).
The residual test is bounded, so the mean conditional harmonic bound \(\gamma_h^K\) suffices without independence assumptions on the swap destinations.
The identity \(\mathbb E|Z|=1\) gives tightness, and the limiting mixed integrals against bounded Lipschitz radial tests and nonconstant circle characters all vanish.
The constant character recovers the radial marginal, so these tests determine precisely its product with arclength.
In the five-term application v152 gives \(e=J_p\to\infty\) and \(\sqrt p\,\mathbb E|L|\to 2\sqrt{I_2/(6\pi)}>0\) by its positive-part mean and symmetry.
Multiplying \(Z\) by that deterministic scale returns the radial Gaussian with the specified variance, leaving the uniform angular factor independent.
