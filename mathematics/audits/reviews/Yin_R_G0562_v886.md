# Radical degrees forced by the limiting height at a four-term Salem relation

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:02 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the submitted [C345 v746](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v746.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the claim and received its fixed text without its development discussion or a prior verdict.
The review checked the full relative-orbit argument, the classification of positive radicals in a Salem field, the simultaneous extension to all divisors and the hypotheses for the four-term application.

Positive total measures tending to zero force \(d_i\to\infty\) by finiteness of monic minimal polynomials of bounded degree and bounded measure.
Equality of two conjugate powers of a Salem number would produce two conjugates of exterior modulus by sending the first to the exterior conjugate.
Thus \(\alpha_i^l\) generates \(K_i\) for all positive \(l\), and its large degree precludes equality to \(B_i\).
Fix \(k\) and extract a subsequence of multiples of \(k\) with constant seed \(B\) and relative degree \(s\) for \(c=B^{1/k}\).
Since \(Z_i=\alpha_i^{a_i/k}\) generates \(K_i\), the field \(H(Z_i)\) for \(H=\mathbb Q(c)\) is indeed the degree \(sd_i\) compositum \(K_i(c)\).
At the chosen real embedding, factoring the difference of \(k\)-th powers bounds \(|Z_i-c|\) by \(|\alpha_i^{a_i}-B|\).
Thus the distinguished place in the product formula contributes at most \((-b_i m_i+O(1))/(sd_i)\).
At finite places \(|Z_i|_v=1\), and summing \(\log^+|c|_v\) with the normalized place weights of the compositum gives exactly the finite part of \(h(c)\) as an upper bound.

Over each fixed embedding of \(H\), the images of \(Z_i\) constitute a full relative Galois orbit with no multiplicities, since \(H(Z_i)\) is the entire compositum.
Each such orbit carries the same fraction \(1/[H:\mathbb Q]\) of all embeddings when complex embeddings are counted separately.
Its points have height \(a_i m_i/(kd_i)\to0\), degree \(d_i\to\infty\) and modulus at most \(\exp(a_i m_i/k)\), which is bounded.
Every subsequence therefore admits a further sequence of distinct points to which the [fixed-field equidistribution theorem](../literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution) for the square map applies.
The cutoff \(\max(\log|z-\tau c|,-U)\) is bounded and continuous on this common disk and admits a continuous extension to the sphere, so deleting one truncated term costs \(O_U(1/(sd_i))\).
All conjugates of the totally real seed \(B>1\) differ from both \(1\) and \(-1\), since a rational conjugate could only equal \(B\) itself.
Consequently each circle has positive distance from its \(\tau c\), and at a sufficiently deep fixed cutoff the circle means are exactly \(\log^+|\tau c|\).
The untruncated other terms are bounded above by the truncated ones, giving the claimed upper limit including the archimedean part of \(h(c)\).
Thus \(h(B)/s\le h(B)/k\), by the exact long-exponent limit and the height identity for a positive radical.
Since \(h(B)>0\) and \(s\le k\), equality forces \(s=k\) along every fixed-\(k\) subsequence where this is relevant.

The nontrivial automorphism \(\iota\) over the real trace field fixes \(B\) and maps its positive real \(j\)-th root \(u\in K\) to \(\pm u\), whence \(u^2\in F\).
Each \(E=\mathbb Q(B)\)-conjugate of \(u^2\) is real and satisfies \(X^j=B^2\), so the degree of \(E(u^2)\) over \(E\) is at most two.
With \([F:E]>2\), the distinguished embedding of \(E(u^2)\) extends at least twice to \(F\).
Only one of the trace embeddings has real extensions to \(K\), because the Salem trace has a single conjugate above two and the others lie strictly between minus two and two.
On a nonreal extension \(\sigma\) of the other embedding, \(\sigma\iota=\bar\sigma\), as holds on the Salem generator.
If \(\iota(u)=-u\), the nonzero purely imaginary value \(\sigma(u)\) has negative square, contradicting \(\sigma(u^2)=u^2>0\).
Hence \(u\in F\), and all its conjugates over \(E\) belong to \(\{u,-u\}\).
This gives \(u^2\in E\), as well as \(u\in E\) for odd \(j\).
The exponents of positive radicals in each fixed real number field are finite by bounded degree and height.
They are closed under least common multiples because the two integral exponents obtained by multiplying \(1/j,1/l\) by \(\operatorname{lcm}(j,l)\) are coprime, and Bézout combines the corresponding positive powers.
This closure, closure under divisors and the preceding inclusions give exactly the doubling test in (2).

For large indices the trace degree \(d_i/2\) and the bounded seed degrees give \([F_i:E_i]>2\), leaving a fixed finite list of possible prime divisors of \(e_{K_i}(B_i)\).
The fixed-prime degree conclusion excludes each member of that list whenever it divides \(a_i\), simultaneously eventually since the list is finite.
For any \(k\mid a_i\) and \(u=B_i^{1/k}>0\), the absolute value of the real constant term of its monic minimal polynomial over \(K_i\) is \(u^s\), because all complex roots have modulus \(u\).
Both this value and \(u^k\) belong to the chosen real field, so \(u^{\gcd(s,k)}\in K_i\).
If \(s<k\) this produces a divisor greater than one of both \(a_i\) and \(e_{K_i}(B_i)\).
Hence \(s=k\) at every divisor, including unbounded ones.
The same degree over \(E_i\subseteq K_i\) follows, and its preservation in the compositum gives linear disjointness.
The [four-term input](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement) supplies exactly the bounded short power, finite trace-field seed list, exponential approximation and limiting long-power height required here.
