## Fourth-root lifts and reciprocal endpoint factors

At 2026-11-21T09:26 UTC an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin recommends acceptance of the entire [v814 claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v814.md#statement).
The reviewer had no role in its development and used the [previously reviewed finite supports](Yin_R_G0562_v954.md#digits) and [lifted-value implication](Yin_R_G0562_v954.md#proof).

Bézout's identity gives \(\langle x\rangle=\langle x^k,x^l\rangle\), so the two possible residues at three form one closed point of degree two.
The table requires \(3\mid l\) and \(l\equiv3k\bmod4\), with both exponents odd by primitivity.
At the order-four lift put \(U=\xi^k\), so \(V=-U\), \(t=2U\), and \(Dt=0\).
Substitution in the normalized section gives \(L=-27\), \(DL=-16lU\), and in fact \(D^2L=-28(k^2+l^2)\): the second derivatives of \(t\) and \(V-V^{-1}\) contribute respectively \(k^2t\) and \(l^2(V-V^{-1})\), while the other second-derivative terms vanish.
Thus \(D^2L\) is a unit, and since \(2\xi^2\) is a unit the reduced section has order exactly two at the residue.

In a collision each factor has a simple root at that residue and hence a unique lift in \(O=\mathbb Z_3[i]\).
The valuations of the distances of the lifts from \(\xi\) are positive integers with sum three, so they are one and two, and their mutual distance has valuation one.
The product rule for the normalized section has a unique term of valuation one, obtained by differentiating the factor with distance of valuation two, whence \(v_3(l)=1\).
Over \(\mathbb Z_3\) the component of \(S\) at the closed point is a monic quadratic lifting \(X^2+1\) modulo three, by coprime Hensel factorization.
Its root generates \(O\) because one and its residue are a basis modulo three; evaluation of \(T\) at this lift has valuation one by the mutual distance and simplicity.
The quotient on that component is therefore \(O/3O\), while \(T\) is a unit on every other component of \(S\).
This gives additive group \((\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2\) and resultant valuation two by the determinant of multiplication by \(T\) on the monic quotient by \(S\).
In the absence of a collision that determinant is a unit.

For any reciprocal irreducible factor the dyadic graph argument applies for every primitive direction: inversion exchanges disjoint graphs defined over the prime field, forcing local splitting at every dyadic trace place.
The signed endpoint product is therefore an odd dyadic square; the other possible odd prime, 23, is minus one modulo eight, and neither sign of five is one modulo eight.
Hence the sum of the two endpoint valuations at five is even for each irreducible factor.
When \(l\) is odd the negative endpoint is a unit and the positive endpoint has total valuation two, counted with multiplicity.
When \(l\) is even, \(k\) is odd, and simultaneous vanishing of \(l-2k,l+2k\) modulo five would force \(5\mid\gcd(k,l)\).
At one of the two endpoints the normalized trace derivative \(\eta\,2(l-2\eta^k k)^2\bmod5\) is thus nonzero.
A simple trace root belongs to exactly one trace factor occurring once, since the trace representation takes products to products and neither rational endpoint is a root in characteristic zero.
That factor carries the entire endpoint valuation two; the parity result then forces all individual valuations at the opposite endpoint to be even as well.
At each occupied endpoint the total valuation two therefore belongs to exactly one irreducible factor of multiplicity one, which cannot occur on both sides of the factorization.
The two endpoints exhaust the possible collisions at five by the finite supports and Bézout's identity.
The empty supports at \(2,7,11,17\) and eventual reciprocity from v805 give the stated steep-sequence consequence.
