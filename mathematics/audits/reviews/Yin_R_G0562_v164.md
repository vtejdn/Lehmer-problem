## Whole-proof finding

Both inequalities hold under their stated hypotheses, with absolute constants.
The prime conductor enters the height estimate through the count of excluded primes and the use of one Frobenius on the coefficient field at every place over a good prime.

### Power collisions and the determinant

Write \(L=K(\alpha)\) and \(L_p=K(\alpha^p)\).
Each embedding of \(L_p\) over \(K\) extends to exactly \(m=[L:L_p]\) embeddings of \(L\), so the power map on the relative orbit has fibers of size \(m\).
If \(m=p\), the tower law gives \(p\mid D\).
If \(1<m<p\), every root over \(L_p\) is \(\alpha\) times a \(p\)-th root of unity.
For \(um+vp=1\) the quantity \(N_{L/L_p}(\alpha)^u(\alpha^p)^v\) lies in \(L_p\) and equals \(\alpha\zeta_p^b\).
A trivial multiplier would imply \(\alpha\in L_p\), so division by \(\alpha\) puts a primitive \(p\)-th root in \(L\).
For distinct such odd \(p\ne\ell\), the compositum with \(K\) has relative degree \(\prod(p-1)\) by the totient formula, including when \(\ell=2\).
Each factor is at least two, hence at most \(2\log_2D\) primes of the two types must be removed beyond \(2,\ell\).
For \(D=1\) the power maps are already injective.

Applying an extension of the inverse Frobenius preserves the internal distinctness of the good powered orbit.
Absolute heights \(h(\alpha)>0\) and \(p h(\alpha)\) distinguish every pair of different packets.
Confluence gives precisely the powers \(m_um_v\) of the node differences because division of the rows by the derivative factorials removes the within-node constants.
The entry at column \(n\) in row \(j\) is \(\binom nj u^{n-j}\) when \(n\ge j\), and zero otherwise.
Summing \(j+1/2\) over rows gives \(D(T^2+t)/2\).
At finite places, the local bound for an unordered pair is \(\max(1,|u|_v)\max(1,|v'|_v)\), where \(v'\) denotes the second node.

Fix a place over a retained prime \(p\).
The restriction of \(\sigma_p\) to the residue field of the underlying prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\) is \(x\mapsto x^p\), and \(p\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p}=\mathfrak p\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p}\).
Dividing the minimal polynomial by a coefficient of largest local modulus gives a primitive local polynomial \(g=cf\).
The coefficients of \(g(X^p)-(\sigma_p^{-1}g(X))^p\) then lie in \(p\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p}\).
Evaluating at \(z_i=\sigma_p^{-1}a_i\) uses only that \(z_i\) is a root of \(\sigma_p^{-1}g\).
The bound \(p^{-1}\max(1,|z_i|_v)^{pD}\) therefore holds even if the chosen extension of the automorphism moves the place or \(|z_i|_v>1\).
The Gauss norm identity for \(g\) replaces \(1/|c|_v\) with \(\prod_j\max(1,|a_j|_v)\).
Multiplication over \(i\) and the cross multiplicity \(T\) save \(p^{-DT}\) against precisely the ordinary pair bounds for this packet.
All other pairs retain their finite-place bounds, including at the conductor and at primes dividing denominators.

Use the ordinary complex modulus and \(|p|_v=p^{-1}\) with local degree weights divided by the common field degree.
Both the archimedean total and the total above a single \(p\) have weight one.
In the product formula for the nonzero determinant the height contribution divided by \(D\) is bounded by \((S-1)(T+\sum p)h(\alpha)\).
Enlarging \(S-1\) to \(D(T+t)\) gives exactly the height coefficient allowed in (3).
The retained preprint of Amoroso and Delsinne, §3.2 and (4.16), confirms the attribution of this determinant method.
The saving and its normalization here follow from the displayed local estimates for this family of fields.

### Uniform parameters

Put \(L_1=\log(2D)\), \(L_2=\log\log(5D)\).
On \(D\ge1\), both \(L_1/L_2\) and \(L_1/L_2^2\) have positive lower bounds by continuity and their limits at infinity.
Thus \(T\gg A\) and \((1+\log D)/T^2\ll A^{-2}\).
For \(X=T^2\log(2T)\), the prime number theorem gives \(\pi(2X)-\pi(X)\asymp X/\log X\asymp T^2\) uniformly above an absolute threshold.
After the exclusions one still has \(t\asymp T^2\) for sufficiently large absolute \(A\).
Also \(\log(2T)\gg L_2\) since \(\log(L_1/L_2)/L_2\to1\) and the bounded range is covered by \(T\gg A\).
Relative to \(T^3\log(2T)\), the archimedean cost is therefore \(O(L_1/[T\log(2T)]+1/T)=O(1/A)\).
The remaining coefficient of \(Dh(\alpha)\) is \(O(T^2\cdot T^4\log(2T))\).
Fixing \(A\) yields \(D h(\alpha)\gg T^{-3}\gg (L_2/L_1)^3\), also for bounded \(D\) and unbounded \(\ell\).

### Fibers, specialization and induction

For a fixed two-variable lift \(F\), a nontorsion horizontal irreducible curve has only finitely many torsion points by the maximal torsion subvariety theorem.
All sufficiently large primitive prime grids avoid their projections and the zeros of the end coefficients of \(F\) and of the curve's defining factor.
The selected root then has a full orbit of \(D\le R\) distinct roots over the single field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\).
At every finite place, integral specialized coefficients and Gauss norm multiplicativity bound the sum of the roots' local positive logarithms by minus the logarithm of the leading coefficient.
Averaging Jensen's formula over archimedean embeddings and applying the product formula proves that the grid mean dominates the sum of the absolute heights of all roots, hence \(D h(\alpha)\).
For the opposite comparison, each fixed lower truncation of the logarithm is continuous and majorizes the grid integrand.
Equidistribution followed by removal of the truncation gives \(m_2(F)\ge\limsup_\ell m_{\rm grid}(F)\).
The function \(\log\log(5x)/\log(2x)\) is positive on \(x\ge1\) and eventually decreasing, so comparison through \(D\le R<H\) loses at most an absolute factor.
The grid-order threshold may depend on \(F\) without affecting this bound.

After dividing univariate exponent differences by their gcd, two of \(H>B^{k-1}\) residues lie in one of \(B^{k-1}\) boxes.
Their parameter difference is invertible modulo the prime \(H\), and some resulting vertical exponent is nonzero by primitivity.
This gives at least two rows, \(R<2H/B<H\), and no merged coefficients under the exact specialization.
I checked the reverse estimate used here in v161.
A folded root within distance \(v<1/(4R)\) of the specialized circle point gives a true root at distance at most \(2v\).
The segment derivative bound and Parseval then give \(|P|/\|P\|_\infty\ll R\sqrt k\,v\).
For \(k\ge3\), the measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality with exponent \(1/(k-1)\) bounds the regularization excess by \(O(k/H)\) per root at the stated scale.
Averaging over the \(H\) preimages contributes \(O(Rk\log(2H)/H)\), independently of the base exponents.
For \(k=2\) both measures equal the logarithm of the larger coefficient modulus.

Write \(L_H=\log(2H)\asymp k\log(A_0k)\) for \(k\ge2\).
The cubic horizontal lower bound at \(H\) dominates a constant times \(L_H^{-4}\).
The error divided by this weaker bound is \(O(A_0^{-8}k^{-2}\log^5(A_0k))\), uniformly small since \(\sup_{k\ge2}k^{-2}\log^5 k\) is finite.
With \(A_0\) fixed, the remaining half of the cubic bound is \(\gg k^{-3}\), using \(\log\log(5H)\gg\log(2k)\).
If every horizontal component is torsion, conjugate primitive characters give factors \(\Phi_e(X^aY^j)\) with \(0<j\le R\).
Their specialized exponents \(Ha+Nj\) cannot vanish, so they contribute zero measure, with all multiplicities allowed.
Their leading coefficients in \(Y\) are monomials, so the top row retains exactly the original measure and has strictly fewer terms.
This permits descent with the same constant down to a monomial, whose positive measure is at least \(\log 2\).
For several variables the power substitutions eventually separate all terms and converge against continuous functions by the character criterion.
The measurable-set inequality and \(\|P_N\|_\infty\ge1\) give the integrable tail \(O((k-1)e^{-s/(k-1)})\) beyond a fixed logarithmic cutoff \(s\), uniformly in the substitution index.
Consequently the measures converge for each fixed polynomial, preserving the alternative \(\{0\}\cup[c'/k^3,\infty)\), a closed set.

For the two external theorems used in the polynomial argument, the reviewer checked the statements and their applications at Bilu, Theorem 5.1(a), and Bownik and Speegle, Theorem 2.1.
Their original proofs lie outside this review.
The cubic estimate tends to zero with growing \(k\).

## Second independent whole-proof finding

A second agent outside proof development checked the [v164 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#decision-prime-conductor-cubic-gap-submitted) and its necessary mathematical inputs.
The coordinator received the completed finding at 2026-11-17T04:58 UTC, under the human record author Yin.
The reviewer received no proof-development conversation, author explanation or earlier v164 findings and delivered the conclusion before reading the first finding above.
Both the uniform relative height estimate and the cubic bound for the support of the original polynomial follow with absolute constants.

I checked the two mechanisms for collisions of powered conjugates separately.
The relative norm and Bézout identity give a primitive \(p\)-th root in \(K(\alpha)\) whenever \(1<[K(\alpha):K(\alpha^p)]<p\).
The products of the degrees \(p-1\) for distinct such odd primes away from the conductor divide \(D\), while a full \(p\)-element fiber requires \(p\mid D\).
After these exclusions, absolute heights separate the original packet from the inverse-Frobenius packets and distinguish packets belonging to different primes.

The decisive valuation estimate takes place in the coefficient ring \(\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p}\), where \(p\) generates the maximal ideal for every chosen \(p\).
Scaling the minimal polynomial \(f\) to a polynomial \(cf\) of Gauss norm one gives the coefficientwise congruence modulo \(p\) at every such \(\mathfrak p\).
At any extending valuation, evaluation at \(z=\sigma_p^{-1}a_i\) costs at most \(\max(1,|z|_v)^{pD}\), and \(|c|_v^{-1}=\prod_j\max(1,|a_j|_v)\) supplies exactly the factors from the denominators of the original orbit.
Consequently the cross differences for this prime gain \(p^{-DT}\) even when the nodes have denominators or the splitting field ramifies above \(p\).
The normalized derivative rows contribute \(D(T^2+t)/2\) to the exponent of \(S\) at infinity, and the local-degree weights above every individual rational prime sum to one.
For the proposed \(T\) and \(X\), the number of discarded primes is \(O(T^2/A^2)\) since \((\log\log(5D))^2/\log(2D)\) is bounded.
The prime number theorem supplies \(t\asymp T^2\), the archimedean term takes at most \(O(A^{-1})\) of the left side of (3), and the coefficient of \(Dh(\alpha)\) is \(O(T^6\log(2T))\).
These inequalities yield \(Dh(\alpha)\gg T^{-3}\) uniformly down to \(D=1\).

In the support argument I checked that the entire relative orbit belongs to the same specialized fiber, that the leading coefficient pays for all finite-place height terms, and that truncation gives the required upper bound on the limsup of primitive grid means.
Finiteness of torsion points on the fixed nontorsion horizontal curve makes the excluded projections finite.
Prime compression preserves all \(k\) coefficients, and exact averaging of the reflected roots over preimages, followed by the measurable-set estimate at the stated regularization scale, bounds the reverse specialization error independently of the base exponents.
The error times \((\log(2H))^4\) is \(O(A_0^{-8}k^{-2}\log^5(A_0k))\), uniformly small for sufficiently large \(A_0\).
The numerator \(\log\log(5H)\gg\log(2k)\) then gives the cubic bound.
In the torsion case \(0<j\le R<H\) and \((H,N)=1\) preclude \(Ha+Nj=0\), so every horizontal cyclotomic factor specializes to measure zero and the strictly shorter top row retains the original measure.
The univariate bound passes to all finite dimensions because the substitutions eventually separate the \(k\) terms and have uniformly integrable logarithms for that fixed \(k\).

I checked the inverse-Frobenius determinant against §4.2.2, Lemma 4.5 and (4.16) in Amoroso and Delsinne.
Their general Theorem 1.3 has fourth power on the denominator logarithm.
The \(p^{-DT/2}\) estimate in their Lemma 4.5 suffices for the same parameter order here, and the stronger local estimate in v164 follows directly from its cross-difference calculation. [ESI preprint](../literature/AD07/readme.md#edition)
I checked the measurable-set inequality in Bownik and Speegle, Theorem 2.1, and used the recorded statement of Bilu, Theorem 5.1, for torsion finiteness.
The review covered these external statements and their applications.
A full audit of the source proofs, numerical optimization of the constants and questions of publication priority lay outside its scope.
