## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T02:41 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed [C345 v727](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of the whole statement.
The agent took no part in developing v727 and received the fixed claim and the specified mathematical inputs without the author's development discussion or an earlier verdict.
The review included the nonintegral determinant of v164, the class-product mechanism of v714, and the finite estimate and small-total-measure scope of v726.

The Galois action over the given finite base permutes the torsion-quotient classes transitively.
Writing \(l\) for their common size, each product has the form \(z^l\xi\), with \(z\) an element of that class and \(\xi\) torsion.
Two such products having torsion quotient would imply that a power of the quotient of their representatives is torsion, hence that the representatives are in the same class.
There are exactly \(D/l\) conjugate products, and their positive powers are still distinct.
At every place of a field containing \(\alpha\) and the torsion factor, \(|\beta|_v=|\alpha|_v^l\) for the chosen product.
This yields \(D'h(\beta)=D h(\alpha)\) with finite-place denominators included.
All powered conjugates in a single twisted set thus stay distinct, while absolute heights \(h(\beta)>0\) and \(p h(\beta)\) distinguish the sets, for any extensions of the chosen lifts.

At each qualifying place the lift preserves the place and acts as residue Frobenius.
Scaling by the inverse of a coefficient of maximum absolute value makes the Gauss norm one, both for the scaled relative polynomial and for its inverse twist.
The coefficient difference after the indicated two power operations is in the maximal ideal by reduction in characteristic \(p\).
With \(|p|_v=p^{-1}\) this gives \(p^{-1/e_p}\) even at a wildly ramified base prime.
Evaluation at \(z_i=\sigma_p^{-1}a_i\) costs at most \(\max(1,|z_i|_v)^{pD'}\).
Factoring the scaled polynomial recovers \(|c_v|_v^{-1}=\prod_j\max(1,|a_j|_v)\) by multiplicativity of Gauss norm.
Taking the product over \(i\) consequently saves \(p^{-D'/e_p}\) on the cross differences relative to their individual local maxima, irrespective of the sizes of the roots.

The orbit of the chosen place under the centralizer has size \(|C_G(\sigma_p)|/|C_G(\sigma_p)\cap G_v|\), against \(|G|/|G_v|\) places overall.
Its proportion is at least \(1/b_p\), since conjugation by the centralizer preserves the chosen lift and transports its residue action.
The local polynomial can be scaled anew at each of these places.
Equal local degrees in the Galois base and the sum of relative local degrees in a common splitting field preserve this lower bound for normalized weights.
The determinant contains the cross differences to power \(T\).
For a node of multiplicity \(m_z\), the powers in the full nonarchimedean pair bound total \(m_z(S-m_z)\le m_z(S-1)\).
At infinity the divided derivatives of orders \(0\) through \(T-1\) contribute \(T^2/2\) to the exponent of \(S\) at each original node, with another \(1/2\) at each added node.
After applying the product formula and dividing by \(D'\), the height term is bounded by \((S-1)(T+\sum p)h(\beta)\).
This gives precisely the stated finite upper estimate and its prime contributions \(T\log p/(e_pb_p)\).

For \(X=T^2\log(2T)\), the interval contains at least a fixed multiple of \(T^2\) primes once the absolute parameter \(A\) is large.
Since \(u\le T^2/A^2\), deleting the exceptions leaves that same order of magnitude.
Then \(\log S\ll\log(2D)+\log(2T)\), and \(T\log(2T)\gg AH\log(2D)\) as well as \(T\gg AH\), uniformly through \(D=1\).
This absorbs the archimedean logarithmic term into half the prime contribution.
The remaining coefficient is \(O(T^6\log(2T))\), yielding \(Dh(\alpha)\gg1/(HT^3)\) with the claimed dependence on \(H,u,D\).

In an abelian base every lift has a singleton class, and excluding its ramified odd primes gives the asserted sparse-ramification bound.
If the finite abelian Galois group has exponent dividing \(b\), its inertia at \(p\nmid b\) has order prime to \(p\) by Cauchy's theorem.
The kernel of the residue uniformizer character is a \(p\)-group by its higher ramification quotients, so tame inertia embeds in the cyclic residue multiplicative group.
Its order therefore divides \(b\).
Taking \(H=b\) and at most \(b\) exceptional primes yields the absolute factor \(b^{-4}\), since \(\sqrt{b+1}\ll b\) and \(\log(2D)/\log\log(5D)\) is bounded below.
For an infinite abelian base the finitely many coefficients of the relative minimal polynomial generate a finite abelian subextension of the same exponent bound, and any factorization there would persist over the full base.
This preserves the degree, including for the compositum of quadratic fields where every finite quotient has exponent dividing two.
For the integral specialization with the base in the root field, multiplying \(Dh(\alpha)\) by the base degree gives the ordinary total logarithmic measure.
The comparison with v726 uses its stated small-total-measure range, while the proof of v727 derives its finite estimate directly for arbitrary heights.
