### The degree and discriminant of a competing unit

For \(L=\mathbb Q(w)\) of degree \(2n\) containing a quadratic field \(D\), the absolute discriminant of \(L\) is \(|d_D|^n\) times the positive integer norm of the relative discriminant.
The polynomial discriminant has absolute value \(|d_L|[\mathcal O_L:\mathbb Z[w]]^2\).
In the \(2n\)-column Vandermonde determinant the row at a conjugate \(z\) has norm at most \(\sqrt{2n}\max(1,|z|)^{2n-1}\).
Squaring the product of these bounds and taking the \(n\)-th root gives \(4n^2 M(w)^{4-2/n}\).
Writing \(|\Delta|=a u^2\) with \(a\) squarefree, the fundamental quadratic discriminant has absolute value \(a\) or \(4a\).
The positive integers \(s\) for which \(|\Delta|/s\) is an integer square have least member \(a\), which justifies the exact loop up to \(\lfloor4p^2(33/25)^4\rfloor\).

In the odd parity branch, an admissible unit \(y\) with fewer exterior roots than the input must have \(2c\).
Writing \(w=y^j\in F\), one has \(b(y)=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q(w)]\,b(w)\).
The original \(p\)-label action already accounts for \(2c\) exterior roots of \(w\), forcing this index to be one.
The field generated by a suitable conjugate of \(y\) thus has degree \(2p\) and contains the quadratic twist field, so the parameter in the discriminant bound is \(n=p\).
The quadratic field is determined by the squareclass of \(\operatorname{disc}Q_p\) even if \(\mathbb Z[t]\) has nontrivial index in the trace field's ring of integers.
The product of the \(x_i-x_i^{-1}\) squares to \(Q_p(2)Q_p(-2)=1\), eliminating the total coordinate sign character and leaving only the permutation sign for a nontrivial quadratic character.

### The small prime degrees

An independent exact Sturm count for \(Q_p\) at \(p=5,7,11,13,17,19,23\) gives respectively \(4,4,8,8,12,12,16\) roots in \((-2,2)\), and exactly one above \(2\) in every case.
The endpoints both have value \(-1\), and the odd strictly increasing \(C_p(t)-t\) on the two exterior real intervals gives no roots below \(-2\).
The degree-five trace therefore gives one exterior root of the input, the minimum for a nontorsion unit by Kronecker's theorem.
For the other six primes the numbers of transpositions on the trace labels are \(c=1,1,2,2,3,3\), respectively.
They satisfy \(0<c<(p-1)/4\) and \(2c+1<\min(4c,(p+1)/2)\).

Any transitive subgroup on the \(p\) labels that contains a conjugate of this involution is primitive and contains a \(p\)-cycle.
The affine and projective fixed-point estimates in v315 exclude those cases since the involution fixes \(p-2c>(p+1)/2\) labels.
In degrees 11 and 23 it is odd.
The ATLAS generators inspected in the Jones source unit have even sign for every exceptional entry in the relevant actions, and the other four degrees have no such entry.
This argument applies to a transitive image of an output-pair stabilizer as well as to the original group.
Consequently the v315 stabilizer alternatives and their numerical thresholds remain valid in all six degrees.
The surviving order-\(p\) action uses the subplastic bound, and the mixed signs on at least five fixed labels supply the even sign subgroup.
The required transitivity on pairs and the perfectness of \(A_{p-1}\) hold down to \(p=7\).
The v316 character argument therefore gives the input count \(2c+1\) in the even branch, and the degree-\(2p\) quadratic-field obstruction above in the odd branch.
Smyth's bound makes this argument applicable to all nontorsion units in the stated budget.

### Strict measure bounds and integer tests

Specializing \(F(X,Y)\) at \((z^p,z)\) and multiplying by \(z^p\) gives \(P_p\).
The fiber width is two, giving the one-sided estimate \(\log M(P_p)-I\le 2\log(2)/p\).

At each midpoint the cosine upper polynomial is evaluated at a rational *lower* bound on the angle and the hyperbolic cosine lower polynomial at the proposed upper bound on the integrand.
The four rational comparisons hold.
They give \(I\le383/1500\), and the fourth-order lower sum for \(\log(13/10)\) exceeds \(383/1500+14/2510\) by \(32047/30120000\).

For each even-degree monic iterate, \(f(u)f(-u)\) has the squared roots after replacing \(u^2\) by the new variable, with all multiplicities preserved.

I ran both programs as printed.
For the 52 primes the first successful iterations \(j=8,9,10,11,12\) occurred respectively \(3,4,21,18,6\) times.
The cutoff was \(33/25\) at \(7\) and \(13/10\) everywhere else.
