## Restriction of the conjugation average, exact signed products and the strict-record coefficient

At 2026-11-24T23:49 UTC an agent independently reviewed for Yin the subspace estimates, rational generation and asymptotic assertions in the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v82.md#statement) and their proofs in the final C0901 v82.
For these results the reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion and checked the explicit commuting hypothesis in the final strict-record assertion.
The reviewer subsequently proposed the additional integral orbit-product construction and checked it as a contributor.
The assertions about at most \(\deg w\) products and the exponent of the quotient lattice belong to that separate development and are outside this independent finding and its formal bindings.
The subspace height bound, its improvement for commuting involutions, both generation equivalences, and the strengthened asymptotic rank conclusions hold.

On the rational irreducible \(G\)-module \(W\) the average of the conjugates of \((1+c)/2\) is \(aI_W\).
Indeed this central group-algebra element is scalar on each irreducible complex summand, with rational scalar equal to its \(c\)-fixed dimension divided by its dimension.
The rational eigenspaces are \(G\)-invariant and rational irreducibility gives the common scalar, determined by the trace on \(W\).
For the given \(U\), each \(g^{-1}cg\) individually lies in \(C\), and hence preserves \(U\).
Restriction of the already computed average is therefore \(aI_U\), even when \(g\) does not preserve \(U\).
Taking traces on \(U\) gives mean fixed dimension \(as\), so at least a fraction \(a\) of the conjugations have positive fixed dimension there.
The finite image of \(C\) preserves the full lattice \(U\cap\Lambda\), with \(\Lambda\) the lattice of original conjugate classes.
Reduction modulo three injects that image into \(\mathrm{GL}_s(\mathbb F_3)\), giving \(q\le3^{s^2}\).
Among the at most \(q\) image matrices there is one with nonzero rational fixed vector and probability at least \(a/q\).
Its one fixed vector lets the selected product from v68 use the same actual unit at all these embeddings and gives height at most \(m(w)/2\).
Garza's increasing bound gives the claimed \((a/q)\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-q/a})\) floor, with uniform convergence \(a\to0\) when \(s\) is uniformly bounded and \(m(w)\to0\).

If the conjugations commute on \(W\), their image consists of simultaneously diagonalizable involutions over \(\mathbb Q\).
Any rational line in a common character space is \(C\)-invariant, and the trace calculation above gives exactly \(a\) for the fraction acting as \(+1\) there.
The selected unit has actual real fraction at least \(a\).
The stronger real-place height estimate for that unit, with \(2h\le m(w)\), yields the strict \(a^2/(50\log(8/a))\) bound.
Also \(a>0\) on every nonzero rational constituent since its realizing nontorsion monomial has a conjugate of modulus different from one and hence positive radial rank by v62.

For commuting conjugations on all of \(V\), use the rational invariant inner product and any nonzero rational \(v\) in a character space \(V_\chi\).
For each \(u\in C\), the pairing of \(v\) with \(u[b]\) is \(\chi(u)\) times the pairing with \([b]\).
Thus \(u\) permutes the actual positive and negative root sets separately if \(\chi(u)=1\), and interchanges them if \(\chi(u)=-1\), leaving the zero-pairing set invariant.
The quotient of the two products is therefore taken exactly to itself or its inverse.
This argument counts all roots even when some share a class modulo torsion.
The sum of the absolute pairings with the spanning root classes is positive and equals \((v,[z(v)])\), so \(z(v)\) is nontorsion.
The sum of the heights of all roots is \(m(w)\), and inversion preserves height, giving the bound on each signed product.
If these classes had a proper span in \(V_\chi\), the rational orthogonal complement there would contain a nonzero \(v\), contradicting that same positive pairing.
Finite basis selection gives full rational rank with generators in the ordinary group of conjugates.
Their classes therefore span the rational conjugate module, and after adjoining all roots and torsion they give exactly \(D(w)\).

Trivial character products are fixed by every conjugation over \(\mathbb Q\), which makes every conjugate real.
For a nontrivial character some conjugate involution inverts the actual product, since these involutions generate \(C\).
This supplies an inverse conjugate as well as an embedding at which conjugation is inversion, hence modulus one.
Every conjugate involution fixes the trace \(z+z^{-1}\), so that trace is totally real.
Kronecker and nontorsion ensure an off-circle conjugate, which must be real since the trace is real.
Reciprocity then gives an exterior real conjugate.
Conversely, on each conjugate class of a totally real generator all conjugate involutions act as \(+1\).
For a reciprocal generator with totally real trace each such involution fixes the trace of each conjugate and can only fix or invert that conjugate, giving scalar signs on its line.
The involutions accordingly commute on every spanning direction and hence on \(V\).
This argument remains valid if an actual radical or torsion twist lies in a larger field because the Galois action on the rational classes factors through the original splitting group.
The compressed splitting group is precisely the image on \(V\), since different compressed conjugates have different classes and their classes still span \(V\).
Thus commutation in that field's splitting group is equivalent to the claimed action on \(V\).

For nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), its conjugate span \(V_x\) is a nonzero rational \(G\)-submodule.
Its radial-rank fraction is the \(c\)-fixed dimension fraction and is a dimension-weighted average of the constituent fractions \(a\).
The strict commuting-case floor excludes any fixed positive lower bound on those fractions when \(m(w)\to0\), uniformly in all the constituents and all \(x\).
For a shrinking sequence of strict degree records with the commuting hypothesis on every original module, v80 supplies \(r\ge (2/\log2)(\log d-O(\log\log(3d)))\) on each nonzero constituent, where \(d=\deg w\).
The negative dimension on a constituent inside \(V_x\) is \((1-o(1))r\) uniformly, and the argument rank of \(x\) is at least this large.
This gives precisely \((2/\log2-o(1))\log d\) with the original record degree and for every nontorsion element of its divisible group.
