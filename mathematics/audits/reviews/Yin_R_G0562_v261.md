### Exact root counts and labels

On the disk \(|U-2|<1\) each indicated determination of \(U^{1/k}\) is holomorphic with nonzero derivative, and the \(k\) determinations are distinct at every \(U\).
Dividing the parent by \(x^{2q-k}\) gives exactly the stated analytic perturbation of \(U-2\).
On the half-radius boundary its absolute error for \(b\ge5\) is at most \(16/81+512/19683+512/6561=5936/19683<1/2\).
Thus each branch contains one root counting multiplicity.
The changes of variable and the division preserve multiplicity, so these are \(k\) distinct simple exterior roots, since \(|x|^k>3/2\).
Reciprocity supplies \(k\) further distinct interior roots.
The circle trace takes values at most \(-1\) on the even mesh points and at least \(3\) on the odd ones.
The open arcs between consecutive points, including the last arc ending at \(2\pi\), are disjoint on the circle.
They give \(2(q-k)\) further distinct roots, exhausting the degree and forcing simplicity everywhere.
Conjugation takes \(j\) to \(-j\) and leaves only \(j=0\) fixed because \(k\) is odd.
Uniqueness forces \(U_0\) to be real in the positive disk, and the zero branch gives \(x_0>1\).

### Uniform expansion

At a branch root put \(\delta=|U_j-2|<1/2\).
The perturbation bound there gives \(\delta\ll(3/2)^{-b}\) and hence \(b\delta\ll1\) absolutely.
Every \(V\) on the segment from \(2\) to \(U_j\) satisfies \(|V|^{-b}\le2^{-b}(1-\delta/2)^{-b}\ll2^{-b}\).
Substituting this estimate at \(U_j\) gives \(\delta\ll2^{-b}\) and sends the last term of the branch equation to \(O(4^{-b})\).
The derivative of the remaining power has modulus \((b-1)|V|^{-b}\), so replacing its value at \(U_j\) by its value at 2 introduces \(O(b4^{-b})\).
The value being subtracted is \(2^{1-b}\exp(-2\pi iqj/k)\), with no ambiguity from changing the branch.
Expanding \(\log|U_j|=\log2+\Re((U_j-2)/2)+O(\delta^2)\) then gives the stated cosine coefficient.
All constants are absolute, and addition over the actual exterior roots of a monic factor costs at most its exterior count \(e\) in the remainder.

### Factors, measures, degrees and primes

Squarefreeness in characteristic two allows torsion orders only \(3,6,3q,6q\), by the stated cyclotomic order argument.
In the odd-order cases the vanishing equation forces \(x^l+x^{-l}=0\), and in the even-order cases it forces \(x^l+x^{-l}=1\).
But \(l\) is even and \(x^l\) has odd order, excluding the required orders four and six.
Every irreducible monic factor therefore has an exterior root by Kronecker.
Only the factor containing \(x_0\) can have fewer than two exterior roots, giving the bound \(1+(k-1)/2\).
At \(k=1\) there is one factor, hence the positive root has the one inverse inside the circle and \(2(q-1)\) other conjugates on it.
In particular its minimal polynomial is \(P\).

The parent has width \(2q\), five nonzero terms, length seven and two end gaps equal to \(k\).
For every nontorsion unit divisor the all-cut bound consequently gives \(m\le2(\log7)d/q\), with no additional hypothesis on initial sums needed at five terms.
The entire binary reduction divides \(X^{3q}-1\), giving \(m\ge d(\log2)/(6q)\) by the period bound with one doubling.
The primitive degrees in \(\bar S\) sum to \(fN(S)\), with the only possible remaining contribution \(2\epsilon(S)\).
Therefore the left side of (2) is exactly \(d/[2(q-1)]\).
The strict annular bounds give \(e\log(3/2)<km<e\log(5/2)\), establishing the comparison in both directions.
When \(I/k\to\infty\), \(e\ge1\) forces \(N(S)\to\infty\) since the subtracted term \(2\epsilon(S)/f\) is bounded.
The end-gap estimate \(d\gg\sqrt{qk}\) also applies before imposing \(q/k\ge5\), so \(m\to0\) forces \(q/k\to\infty\).
In (1) the corrections divided by \(e\) then tend to zero uniformly, so \(e/k\sim m/\log2\), forcing \(k\to\infty\).
The other stated necessities follow respectively from (2) and the moving-prime bound.

For each fixed \(h\) the split-prime input gives arbitrarily large \(q\equiv1\bmod2h\) for which \(2\) is an \(h\)-th power modulo \(q\).
Thus its order divides \((q-1)/h\), and the order modulo \(3q\) is its least common multiple with two.
The claimed \(I\ge h\) follows.
Choosing \(h\) separately for each prescribed \(k\), then selecting \(q\ge5k\) beyond the previous prime, uses no uniformity in the splitting field.
The same choice with \(k=1\) and unbounded \(h\) gives the Salem family with unbounded binary orbit counts.
