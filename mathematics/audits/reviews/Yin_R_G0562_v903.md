# Torsion quotients of conjugates on a fixed rational graph

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T05:00 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin completed an independent review of [C345 v763](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v763.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of the complete claim.
The reviewer took no part in developing the proof.

For \(X=\beta^u\) the graph gives \(v h(X)/u=h(R(X))\le D h(X)+C_R\) without an integrality hypothesis.
The bound tends to zero with the reciprocal of the slope and applies uniformly to every nontorsion solution.
Both maps are finite at the specified arguments even when the torsion multiplier belongs to an external field.
Clearing their denominators yields a polynomial of degree at most \(2D\) over \(E(\eta)\).
If it is nonzero, adjoining \(X\) to \(\mathbb Q(\eta)\) costs degree at most \(2D[E:\mathbb Q]\), since the same degree bound holds after first adjoining \(E\).
The [relative height theorem in rank one](../literature/AM16/Yin_U002.md#am16-u002) applies to this varying finite abelian base with a uniform constant and gives a strictly positive lower bound at the stated relative degree.
This proves the single identity threshold independently of the order of \(\eta\).
The relative height theorem is accepted at its recorded statement scope.

The clarified comparison with [the plane-curve result](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#curve) and [its extension across absolute components](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#twists) was checked separately.
The distinct conjugates of the numerator-denominator graph are a transitive set of absolutely irreducible components.
A one-dimensional support would give a torus coset whose graph has a single value over a generic first coordinate, hence a monomial.
Thus the existing results already imply bounded multiplier orders and stabilization even across coefficient conjugates.
The explicit sign changes at algebraic Möbius poles, bound for external translates and example of degree loss give the additional conclusions under review.

A nonmonomial map has a zero or pole in the multiplicative group.
The image of this one point under multiplication by \(a\) must be among the finitely many zeros or poles of the same kind of a coefficient conjugate.
The map identity uniquely determines \(b\) once \(a\) and the conjugate are fixed.
Taking the least common multiple of the orders of the torsion pairs bounds the orders of both \(\eta^u\) and \(\eta^v\).
Coprimality of the original exponents then bounds the order of \(\eta\).

An automorphism fixing \(\beta^k\) has multiplier in both \(\mu_k\) and \(\mu_N\), so it fixes \(\beta^{\gcd(k,N)}\).
The reverse inclusion of stabilizers, the relative-degree bound and stabilization after powering by \(N\) follow in a finite normal closure.
For a twist \(\xi\beta\), every embedding fixing it extends to an absolute automorphism and sends \(\beta\) to a torsion translate with multiplier in \(\mu_N\).
The product then determines the image of \(\xi\), bounding the degree by the number of possible multipliers without any restriction on the order or original field of \(\xi\).

A rational Möbius map has just one zero and one pole on the projective line.
A finite nonzero one is fixed by any permitted input scaling, so the only pair is \((1,1)\).
For the algebraic pole map the images of \(1/B\) and \(B\) force \(a/B=1/B'\) and \(aB=B'\), respectively.
These give \(a^2=1\), and direct substitution gives \(b=a\) for either fixed sign.
The only possible nonidentity multiplier is minus one, with both exponents odd and \(B'=-B\).
If the even power field is proper, its relative degree is exactly two by the preceding general bound.

In the degree-loss example the translated polynomial is the pure leading power modulo two and has constant coefficient \(-2\).
Eisenstein's criterion proves irreducibility, and evenness gives two distinct conjugates above their common square, which attains the index bound two for this root.
For the constant monomial example the golden ratio is a positive nontorsion unit.
Its positive \(v\)-th root has relative degree \(v/\gcd(v,r)\) above its field by the [positive radical degree formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement), which is unbounded for fixed finite \(r\).
