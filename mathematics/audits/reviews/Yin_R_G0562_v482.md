## Independent finding on the first absolute moment and the prime window

At 2026-11-18T19:59 UTC an agent outside the development of [L462 v225](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v225.md#window) independently checked all four sections for Sheng Yin.
The conductor count, the mixed moments through order four for the truncated sums and the first absolute moment bounds hold.
They apply at every prime in the stated window, and the positive-measure comparison holds with the distinct unpowered and powered measures specified in the final section.

### Avoiding short relations at every induction step

Fix \(D=2^s\), \(T=D^2\) before selecting the primes.
Suppose \(B\) is the divisor set of the product constructed so far and \(q\) is a prime distinct from those already selected.
The two sets \(B,qB\) are disjoint by unique factorization.
On each side of a proposed relation among distinct elements, the contributing old divisors are still distinct after removing the common \(q\) from the second side.
Each nonempty side thus has nonzero weighted sum by induction, even if the same old divisor is used once on each side.
A relation meeting just one side is impossible, and for two nonempty sides the equation \(u+qv=0\) determines at most one \(q\).
For length \(k\le4\) there are at most \((2|B|\cdot8T)^k\) ordered choices of labelled old divisors and nonzero signed coefficients.
Allowing permutations and choices exceeding the total coefficient budget only enlarges this count.
Summing over \(k\) gives \(O((DT)^4)=O(\exp(12s\log2))\) excluded candidates at each step.
The lower bound \(\gg X/\log X\) for the primes in \([X,2X]\) exceeds this number plus \(s\) if \(X=\exp(Cs)\) with fixed \(C>12\log2\) and \(s\) sufficiently large.
The estimate is uniform in the choices already made, so the induction supplies all \(s\) distinct conductor primes.

For this \(N\), \(\log\varphi(N)=\sum_{q\mid N}\log(q-1)=Cs^2+O(s)\).
Thus every conductor prime is larger than \((\log(3d))^A\) eventually for each fixed \(A>0\), where \(d=\varphi(N)\).
For every prime in that range powering permutes the primitive \(N\)-th roots, so the minimal polynomial remains \(\Phi_N\).
With \(y=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil\), all primes of \((y,2y]\) are covered, including its upper endpoint, by taking for example \(A=5\).

### Truncation and fourth-order products

The boundary logarithm \(f(t)=\log|1-e^{it}|\) has mean zero and the coefficients \(-1/(2|k|)\) for \(k\ne0\).
The singularity at zero is logarithmic, so \(f\in L^4\) for normalized angular measure.
The Fejér kernel is a probability kernel and gives \(\|f_T\|_4\le\|f\|_4\).
With the stated multiplier, Parseval gives exactly
\[
 \|f-f_T\|_2^2=\frac{T}{2(T+1)^2}+\frac12\sum_{k>T}\frac1{k^2}=O(T^{-1}).
\]
The two first Fourier modes alone keep \(\|f_T\|_2\) bounded below for \(T\ge1\).

The Möbius divisor identity holds away from the finitely many zeros of the numerator and denominator factors and hence almost everywhere for the logarithms.
The \(D\) summands in the finite Fejér replacement \(S\) have signs of absolute value one.
In any product of at most four summands the Fourier indices have absolute value at most \(T\).
Collect the indices at repeated occurrences of the same divisor and discard the zero totals.
A nonempty remaining combination has at most four distinct divisors and sum of absolute coefficient values at most \(4T\).
The conductor property prevents it from contributing to the constant coefficient.
Hence the integral factors into one integral per distinct divisor with its actual multiplicity.
This also handles four distinct divisors, one repeated divisor and two different divisors, and the two-pair case.

The second moment is consequently \(D\|f_T\|_2^2\).
In the fourth moment each divisor accounts for one fourfold occurrence, and two distinct double occurrences contribute \(6\binom D2\|f_T\|_2^4\).
Every other pattern has a singleton with mean zero.
This gives exactly \(D\|f_T\|_4^4+3D(D-1)\|f_T\|_2^4\), with all contributing signs positive.
Hölder interpolation between \(L^1\) and \(L^4\) gives \(\|S\|_1\ge\|S\|_2^3/\|S\|_4^2\gg\sqrt D\), while Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\|S\|_1\le\|S\|_2\ll\sqrt D\).
Multiplication of a uniform angle by an integer \(b\ge1\) preserves its law.
The total \(L^1\) error in undoing the truncation is therefore at most \(D\|f-f_T\|_2=O(1)\).
Since the full logarithm is centered, \(J(\Phi_N)\) is half its \(L^1\) norm, proving \(J(\Phi_N)\asymp2^{s/2}\).
The relation \(\log d=Cs^2+O(s)\) absorbs the multiplicative constant into a positive exponent \(c\) and yields \(J(\Phi_N)\ge\exp(c\sqrt{\log d})\).
Dividing by the largest possible \(p\le2y\) still gives a quantity tending to infinity.

### The second-moment comparison

Lalín and Sinha's Theorem 5 gives \(m_2(\Phi_N)=(\pi^2/12)\,2^{\omega(N)}\varphi(N)/N\), matching \(V(\Phi_N)\) since the mean logarithm vanishes.
One can check the specialization directly from the Fourier coefficients: \(\int f(bt)f(ct)\,d\mu=\pi^2\gcd(b,c)^2/(12bc)\).
For squarefree \(N\) the signed divisor double sum then factors prime by prime, with local multiplier \(1+1-1/q-1/q\) at each \(q\mid N\).
This reproduces \((\pi^2/12)D\varphi(N)/N\).
The first absolute moment lower bound uses the fourth-moment estimate above. [Lalín and Sinha, Theorem 5](https://www.iiserkol.ac.in/~kaneenika/higherlehmerfinal.pdf#page=4)

### Positive-measure comparison at all primes of the window

A fixed irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial \(P\) has no common root with \(\Phi_N\), so the base product is squarefree and has \(m(P\Phi_N)=m(P)>0\).
Two distinct roots of \(P\) can collide under prime powering only when their quotient has that particular prime order.
There are finitely many such exceptional primes for fixed \(P\).
At every other prime the \(e\) powered roots are a transitive orbit of \(e\) distinct nontorsion conjugates.
For \(p\nmid N\), adjoining the powered cyclotomic roots therefore gives precisely \(P_p\Phi_N\).
Its logarithmic Mahler measure is \(p\,m(P)\) by the archimedean root formula.

For any complex root \(\gamma\), its centered logarithmic contribution is of the form \(\log|1-z e^{-it}|\) with \(|z|\le1\).
Its \(L^2\) norm squared is \(\frac12\sum_{k\ge1}|z|^{2k}/k^2\le\pi^2/12\), including the boundary.
The centered logarithm of any monic degree-\(e\) polynomial thus has \(L^1\) norm at most \(e\pi/\sqrt{12}\).
Half the reverse triangle inequality gives \(J(P_p\Phi_N)\ge J(\Phi_N)-O(e)\) uniformly in the roots and in \(p\).
For fixed \(P\), the window for total degree \(d+e\) eventually lies above every exceptional prime of \(P\) and below the least prime dividing \(N\), and \(\log(d+e)\sim\log d\).
Thus every prime in that window satisfies the same superlogarithmic lower bound.
Given hypothetical irreducible inputs with \(m(P)\to0\), the conductor can be chosen separately so that \(\sqrt D/e\) is arbitrarily large and both window conditions hold for each input.
It is the unpowered reducible products that preserve the input measures on this conditional sequence.
The argument supplies no assertion that its powered measures tend to zero and no construction of shrinking positive input measures.
