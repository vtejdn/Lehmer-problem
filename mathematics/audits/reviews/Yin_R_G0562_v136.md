## Odd power sums, the integral cofactor budget and the plastic boundary

At 2026-11-17T03:14 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the complete C345v136 statement fixed at 2026-11-17T03:12 UTC.
The reviewer had no role in developing this version and had separately checked the earlier C345v133 result used in the solvable-group case.

At any odd order \(n\le k\) Newton's triangular identities determine the power sum from the common leading coefficients, including when the output has repeated or zero roots.
For the Salem input the real power sum is at least \(e^{na}-(d-1)\).
The output's nonnegative powers \(u_i=n\log^+|\beta_i|\) have sum at most \(na\), assigning zero to the root at zero.
If all \(u_i\le na/2\), the secant bound for \(e^u-1\) on this interval gives \(\sum(e^{u_i}-1)\le2(e^{na/2}-1)\).
The hypothesis \(na-\log(2d)\to+\infty\) makes the resulting upper bound \(d+2(e^{na/2}-1)\) smaller than the input sum.
There is therefore precisely one root of modulus above \(e^{a/2}\), even counting multiplicity.
Conjugation makes it real and simple; the sum of the absolute \(n\)-th powers of all other roots is at most \(d-2+e^{na/2}\), so its sign is positive by oddness.
These estimates also establish strict uniqueness of its modulus without any integrality assumption.

With \(\tau\) this root, the first subtraction of power sums gives \(1-(\tau/\sigma)^n=O(de^{-na}+e^{-na/2})=o(1)\).
Because \(1<\tau\le M(Q)\le\sigma\), this implies \(n(a-\log\tau)=o(1)\).
The remaining logarithmic measure is at most \(a-\log\tau\), bounding the other absolute \(n\)-th powers by \(d-2+\exp(n(a-\log\tau))=d-1+o(1)\).
A second subtraction followed by \(-\log(1-u)\sim u\) proves the coefficient \(2+o(1)\) in (2).
All error bounds use the input parameters alone, so changing the other output coefficients does not affect uniformity.

At degrees at most \(d\) an irreducible integer monic factor of positive logarithmic measure contributes \(\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\), by Dobrowolski at growing degree and coefficient finiteness at bounded degree.
Every factor of a monic integral \(Q\) is integral after monic normalization, and the factor containing the simple root \(\tau\) occurs only once.
Under the separate residual budget (4) no other positive-measure factor can occur.
Kronecker then leaves cyclotomic factors and the possible zero root.
This part uses (4) in addition to (1).
When \(a\to0\) and \(k\ge\epsilon d\), Dobrowolski on the input gives \(a\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\), implying both conditions.
The total secondary cost \(E\) is then at most \(\exp(-(\epsilon+o(1))da)\).
The distinguished factor is a unit eventually because its nonzero integer constant has magnitude at most its Mahler measure \(<2\).
Smyth's nonreciprocal theorem then forces reciprocity; the directly applicable source location is Smy08-U002.
If a secondary real exterior conjugate \(\gamma\) occurs, its reciprocal is within \(E\) of its signed unit endpoint.
Evaluation there has nonzero integer modulus at least one, whereas the coefficient derivative bound and C345v73 give an upper bound \(E\exp(O(\sqrt d\log(2d)))=o(1)\).
For any positive surplus of the distinguished factor, the solvable-group estimate of C345v133 is \(O(d^{2/3}\log(2d)+d^{3/4}(\log(2d))^{3/4})=o(da)\), contradicting the same exponential budget.
The sole remaining exterior root and its reciprocal give a Salem number since a reciprocal quadratic would have integral trace at least three, impossible as \(\tau\to1\).

For the boundary, the formal factors with constant one determine the integral exponents \(c_j\) successively.
Because \(F(w)=\prod_{f(\alpha)=0}(1-\alpha w)\), equating logarithmic derivatives gives \(\sum_{j\mid e}jc_j=\sum_\alpha\alpha^e\).
The non-Pisot conjugates have modulus less than one and all proper divisors of \(j\) are at most \(j/2\).
Möbius inversion therefore yields the stated \(\theta^j+O(j\theta^{j/2}+j)\) for \(jc_j\).
Only finitely many \(c_j\) can be negative.
Replacing each inverse binomial by its finite geometric series retains the first \(k\) coefficients since \(jh>k\), and changes the signed formal degree by \(jh=O(k)\) per replacement.
The geometric leading sum now gives \(D_k\sim\theta^{k+1}/(\theta-1)\).

Taking \(d_k\equiv4\bmod120\) as specified adds only \(O(k)\) to \(D_k\), and \(d_k-3\equiv1\bmod120\) gives an admissible positive completion which is itself the Salem minimal polynomial for large \(k\).
Its reversed leading series agrees with \(1-w^2-w^3\) through order \(k\).
The reversed cyclotomic product is monic with constant \(+1\) or \(-1\); multiplying by \(z^{h_k}+U_k(0)\) sets the constant to one and leaves the required prefix intact because \(h_k>k\).
The roots' inversion symmetry and the new constant one then imply exact reciprocity and matching trailing coefficients.
As an independent exact check, using the recurrence for the three conjugate power sums gave \(D_{10}=44,\ D_{16}=311\) before padding and matching prefixes and suffixes at \(d_{10}=124,\ d_{16}=364\).
At \(d_k\to\infty\), the root satisfies \(\sigma_k\ge\theta\) and \(f(\sigma_k)=O(\theta^{-(d_k-3)})\); the nonzero slope at \(\theta\) gives \(k|\log\sigma_k-\log\theta|=o(1)\).
Combining this with the asymptotic for \(D_k\) proves the signed boundary constant \(\log((\theta-1)/\theta)\).
This shows sharpness of the scale up to an additive constant, with source measures tending to the plastic number.
