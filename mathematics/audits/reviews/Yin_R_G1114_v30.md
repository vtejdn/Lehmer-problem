## The positive delay-one return and its coefficient consequences

The arguments establish all four sections, including the sharp infimum, the exclusion for \(q\ge12\), and the eventual antireciprocity in the stated relative limit.

### Boyd's inequality and bounded support

The degree is at least two since a positive rational Pisot integer has minimal polynomial with negative constant.
Taking the absolute product of the conjugates gives \(q<\theta\), and evaluating their real linear and paired complex factors gives \(f(1)<0\).
Thus \(D(1)<0\), and monicity gives a real root \(\sigma>1\).
For \(0<t<1\), the strict Rouché comparison for \(zf+tg\) counts \(r\) interior roots in degree \(r+1\).
Any collection of exterior roots of the limit persists with multiplicities in small disks exterior to the circle, so their total multiplicity is at most one.
Consequently \(M(D)=\sigma\).
Reciprocity of \(D\) now gives a unique interior root \(\sigma^{-1}\).
Every monic integral factor has absolute constant one, so the factor of \(\sigma^{-1}\) must also contain \(\sigma\) to balance its product of root moduli.
If these were its only roots then \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) would be an integer greater than two.
This excludes the quadratic case when the sum tends to two and leaves circle conjugates as required in the Salem conclusion.

I checked the sign in Boyd's Section 4, equations (26)–(28), and the hypotheses of Section 6, Lemma 5, equation (38), on printed page 1256.
The parameters are \(q=|f(0)|>1\), \(\theta=q+\delta\), delay one and sign \(\operatorname{sgn} f(0)\), which selects \(zf+g\) here.
The paper permits the designated value one when there is no exterior root, and the preceding root count supplies an actual exterior root in this application.
The displayed upper bound in (38) applies without a smallness assumption on \(\delta\).
Its factor \(((q+1)/q)((q+1)/(q-1))\) decreases on \(q\ge2\) and is at most \(9/2\).
Hence \(\delta\to0\) gives \(\sigma\to1\). [Boyd's published paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/247185150_Pisot_and_Salem_Numbers_in_Intervals_of_the_Real_Line)

For a cyclotomic product \(C\), \(m(CD)=m(D)=\log\sigma>0\).
The fixed-support gap applies to this possibly reducible integral polynomial, and \(\sigma\ge e^{\eta_K}\) gives the stated lower bound on \(\delta\) by monotonicity on \((1,\infty)\).
A product of cyclotomic polynomials has reversal \(\pm C\), including the sign from factors \(z-1\).
Therefore an \(s\)-term \(Cf\) makes \(CD=zCf\pm(Cf)^*\) a polynomial with at most \(2s\) terms.
These two implications give divergence of both support minima along every sequence with \(\delta\to0\).

### Dominance and the four-term cutoff

The Rouché comparison with the dominant monomial counts exactly its exponent in interior roots, which here is \(r-1\).
Its coefficient is negative because strict dominance makes its sign the sign of \(f(1)\).
Integrality gives \(A\ge q+B+2\), including \(B=0\) in degree two.
After division of the root equation by \(\theta^{r-1}\), the intermediate coefficients cost at most \(B/\theta\), while \(q/\theta^{r-1}<1\).
This gives the stated strict \(\theta-q>1+B(1-1/\theta)\).
A single negative coefficient suffices because \(f(1)<0\) forces its absolute value to exceed the sum of all positive coefficients.
For \(z^2-(q+2)z+q\), the values at zero and one have opposite signs, and the second root is \(>q+1\).
The interior root in \((0,1)\) excludes a monic linear factorization over the integers.
The quadratic formula gives \(\theta-q=1+2/(\sqrt{q^2+4}+q)\), attaining the limiting infimum through Pisot examples as \(q\to\infty\).

For the four-term family, \(f(q)=q(1-q^k)<0\) and the identity at \(\theta\) gives \(0<\delta=q(\theta^k-1)/\theta^{r-1}<q/\theta<1\), since \(k\le r-2\).
In \(D\) the terms at \(z^r\) and \(z\) cancel.
The other two interior terms have coefficient \(-q\) each, combining to \(-2q\) at \(2k=r-1\).
Hence the height is at least \(q\) and the support has at most four terms, so DS17 gives \(M(D)\ge q/4\), also in the three-term case.
For \(q\ge12\) this gives \(\sigma\ge3\) and \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\ge4/3\).
The upper bound from Boyd and \(\delta<1\) is strictly below \(13^2/(12\cdot11)=169/132<4/3\).
Thus the contradiction applies uniformly in \(r,k\) at and above the stated cutoff.

### The normalized integral pattern

The coefficient maximum of \(A\) is at least one and that of \(f/q\) is at least \(1-h\ge1/2\).
For either polynomial the fixed-support regularization error is \(O_s(t^{1/[2(s-1)]})\) for \(t\le1\), by (2) of L462v31 and its separate single-term formula.
This use permits unbounded exponents and coefficient heights.
The derivative of \(x\mapsto\tfrac12\log(x^2+t^2)\) has maximum \(1/(2t)\) on \(x\ge0\).
Since the pointwise difference in moduli is at most \(h\), the two regularized means differ by at most \(h/(2t)\).
Taking \(t=h^{2(s-1)/(2s-1)}\) gives the asserted power, and at zero perturbation the two polynomials coincide.

For a Pisot seed \(m(f/q)=\log(\theta/q)\), so \(\theta-q=o(q)\) implies \(m(A)\to0\).
The fixed-support gap forces \(m(A)=0\) eventually, and Kronecker gives the integral cyclotomic factorization up to sign and an initial power of \(z\).
The uniform coefficient error at most \(h\), \(q\to\infty\), and integrality imply \(A(0)=1\), its coefficient at \(r\) is zero and all coefficients above \(r\) vanish eventually.
Boyd gives \(\sigma\le2+(9/2)\delta=o(q)\).
The bound for at most \(2s\) coefficients gives \(\|D\|_{\mathrm{coeff},\infty}\le2^{2s-2}\sigma=o(q)\).
Each coefficient of \((zB+z^r B(1/z))/q\) has absolute value at most \(2h\).
Consequently every integer coefficient of \(zA+z^rA(1/z)\) has absolute value less than one simultaneously for sufficiently large indices.
Its vanishing gives \(A=-z^{r-1}A(1/z)\), and its nonzero constant fixes the degree \(r-1\) and leading coefficient \(-1\).
Evaluation at one gives the factor \(z-1\).

For \(A=1-z^k-z^{r-1}\), reversal already disagrees with \(A\) at the endpoints.
It also disagrees with \(-A\) in the interior whether or not \(k=r-1-k\).
If every irreducible factor were reciprocal up to sign, their product would be as well.
Thus \(-A\) has a nonreciprocal monic irreducible factor to which Smyth applies, giving the stated plastic-number lower bound for \(M(A)\) by multiplicativity.
The remaining existence question permits unbounded minimum support under cyclotomic multiplication.
