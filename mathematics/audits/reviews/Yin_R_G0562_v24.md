## Sparse normalized lifts on arbitrary positive branches

At 2026-11-16T18:32 UTC, a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Yin inspected all four substantive sections of fixed C345v24 and the stated direct inputs at their used scopes.
The reviewer had no role in developing this proof and received no proof-generation discussion or author explanation.
The finding follows from checking the arguments, including the adaptations to a varying rational tail, without adopting previous proof verdicts.
The external inequalities and specialization theorem were inspected at retained statement and applicability depth.

The trace roots give \(M(\Pi_i/j_i)=1\) in either degree parity, so the sparse height estimate applies even when \(j_i\) varies.
It bounds the full normalized coefficient sum, while the first nonintegral index \(d_i=o(h_i)\) and the inverse-measure identities hold under escape with this bounded supremum.
The initial coefficient set is finite because both the numerators in reduced form and their positive denominators are bounded there.
Its fixed common denominator \(m\) after extraction is at least two because \(d_i\) eventually belongs to the prescribed interval.
For \(D=1\) this already rules out the sequence.

For the far coefficients, convergence of the finite ordered tuple gives uniform circle closeness to the specialization of the frozen Laurent polynomial, even if some nonconstant terms disappear.
The constant one gives circle supremum at least one for both polynomials.
I checked the application of the measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality on a full period.
For \(2\le k\le K\) nonzero terms with imaginary frequencies, the exponent \(1/(k-1)\) gives the weaker common exponent \(1/(K-1)\) on \(0<t\le1\).
A constant monomial with value one satisfies the needed truncation bound directly.
Integrating \(A_0t^{1/(K-1)}\,dt/t\) on \((0,\delta)\) yields \(A_0(K-1)\delta^{1/(K-1)}\).
The truncated logarithm is \(\delta^{-1}\)-Lipschitz in the input modulus, so the two mean logarithms differ by a quantity tending to zero after taking the limits in the stated order.
Lawton's theorem then gives \(M(F_\infty)=1\) on the stabilized lattice.

There are finitely many comparability classes of exponents between \(d_i\) and \(h_i\).
At successive scales \(s\ll S\), evaluations of chosen offsets are \(O(s)\), whereas every omitted exponent is bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \(S\).
A nonzero nonnegative rational combination of the omitted terms cannot lie in the rational span of the chosen ones by evaluating at late inputs.
Rational separation yields the exposing functional even when some late limiting coefficients are zero.
The face bound from Jensen and the exposed constant give equality of its normalized measure with one.
Its coefficients are fixed inside the prescribed interval, and Lawton gives \(M(A_i)\to m\) for the integer face specializations.
After dividing by the face content, its primitive constant \(m'\) remains at least two because the coefficient at \(d_i\) is fractional in \(A_i/m\).

I checked that the base tests of the actual \(B_i\) at \(O(s)\), and the positive mixed tests at \(kt+O(s)\) with \(s\ll t\ll S\), only use \(A_i/m=P_i/m'\) inside the common prefix, where \(P_i\) here denotes the primitive face specialization.
The doubled moments give the integer transform of each row weighted by \(2P/m'\), and the zero-layer computation gives the transform of \(2|P|^2\mu\).
For each graph slope \(H=(2P/m')f\) is integer Laurent.
I checked the division argument on almost every exposed circle slice.
Jensen excludes interior poles of \(H/P\), reality pairs nonzero poles under reciprocal conjugation, and integrability excludes poles on the circle.
The unique leading monomial in the exposed direction gives polynomial division with Laurent rational-coefficient remainder, which vanishes on almost every slice and hence identically.
Primitivity then gives \(2f/m'\) integral Laurent.
A nonzero slope would have mass at least \(m'/2\), exceeding one or exhausting it in the excluded Haar case.
The bound at the last possible full-dimensional harmonic gives \(M(H_k)<1\), since \(M(P)=m'\) and the geometric mean of the continuous marginal density is less than one.
The weighted rows vanish above \(P=0\), so annihilators of the remaining fixed torsion cosets give the same cyclotomic product for all intermediate tests.
After diagonal extraction, integrality yields the simultaneous zero interval for \(U_iB_i\).

At the initial scale, formal division gives integer Taylor coefficients for \(J_i\) below \(d_i\).
The local models use the vanishing negative atom, the \(2h_i\) positive charged lifts and \(d_i\le h_i\), all available on varying branches.
Their Gram bounds and, in the reciprocal model, the transition intervals of width at most its binomial exponent give the required estimate for \(P_iB_i\) on every interval of length \(d_i\).
When \(d_i\) is bounded, bounded individual coefficients suffice.

For one induction step, multiplying the zero interval by the preceding \(P\) loses at most \(\deg P\) at its lower end.
The bound already known at scale \(s\) gives the bounded coefficient sum of \(C=PUB\) in the residual initial interval.
I checked the two-sided identity for \(V=PUA\) using \(C=(V/m)J\) on the prefix.
At \(|k|\le S/(4L)\), every nonnegative index of \(C\) tested by \(V^*C+VC^*\) is at most \(|k|+\deg V<S/L\), and the constant coefficient of \(J+J^*\) is two.
The resulting absolute sum of the signed weighted moments bounds the Gram norm uniformly on translated blocks.
On a fixed number of characters the error from the positive-part correction tends to zero.
The Bessel test counts nonzero coefficients of the integer polynomial \(VB\), while the full normalized numerator, including its unrestricted tail, appears only in bounded partial multipliers.
On an offset transition \(VB=(UA)(PB)\), so the induction bound at \(s\) applies to boundedly many translates on an interval of length \(O(s)\).
This proves the next all-location bound, retaining a multiplier of bounded coefficient sum and degree \(O(S)\).

At \(h_i\) boundedly many intervals cover the resulting product because \(B_i\) has degree \(2h_i+2\).
For the fixed number \(b\) of scale steps its constant is \(m^b\).
The cyclotomic factors have measure one, each integer face factor has \(M(A_i)\ge m\) tending to \(m\), and \(M(B_i)>1\) tends to one.
Thus the measure of the integral bounded-term product approaches its constant \(m^b\) strictly from above, contradicting the endpoint gap.
For every fixed threshold \(D\), applying the uniform bound to arbitrarily small fixed \(\eta\) gives a least violating index \(o(h_i)\) on every bounded-support escaping sequence.
A diagonal choice of thresholds gives denominators tending to infinity at sublinear indices.
On a fixed branch every reduced denominator divides \(j\), so bounded support is impossible on any escaping subsequence, also for finitely many branches.
Finally, \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\) in the branch derivative identity bounds \(q\) at fixed \(j,K\) once the positive excess is bounded below.
I find the theorem and the stated qualitative consequences valid.
