## Relative orbit sizes, heights and the two growth estimates

At 2026-11-25T05:07 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v159 independently reviewed for Yin the fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#statement) and its three proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its inputs without development discussion or prior findings on v159.
The reviewer developed the earlier exterior-product argument in v95 and checked its application here from the actual conjugates.
All bounds and the second-moment identity follow for the indicated increasing degrees, with uniformity in the number of records and the stated restrictions on the exponents.

A strict decrease of the record minimum rules out any compression lowering degree at unchanged measure.
High-degree strict records have measure less than two by comparison with an earlier unit of measure less than two, so their extreme coefficients have modulus one.
The two pure models in v84 therefore apply to the reciprocal records.
Two matching compressed conjugate classes on a rational line have the same point stabilizer for the two proportional nonzero vectors and thus equal compressed degrees.
Strict increase of degree puts every preceding source splitting field in the unmatched base for the new source in v87.
This comparison uses source degrees, irrespective of the degrees of the derived values.

The positive measure of a unit equals, up to rational sign, the product of its strictly exterior conjugates.
Among subsets of the same cardinality only the exterior set has maximal product of moduli.
It follows that a conjugate of the value with maximal modulus equals the value itself.
In particular its Galois stabilizers as a number, as a class modulo torsion and after any positive integral power agree.
An automorphism fixing a powered prefix product must attain maximal modulus in every factor separately.
Its fixed-field calculation identifies the prefix field with the compositum of the individual embedded value fields.

In the relative group fixing the preceding splitting fields, lift any supported three-cycle on the new labels using v87.
In the oriented representation its fourth power removes uniform signs and acts as the same three-cycle.
In the signed model the relative image is normal in the full signed action.
Commuting the lift with an available full-group flip on one moved coordinate and one spare coordinate gives the double flip on that moved coordinate and its image.
Conjugating by the full group yields every supported double flip in each block.
The fourth power of the lift fixes coordinates off the triple without signs. On the triple it has order three, thus its sign product is positive.
An even flip corrects these signs in the relative group.
The resulting actions give independent pure alternating factors, enlarged by independent even flips in the signed model.

Let \(t\) blocks be occupied by the exterior-product class.
A nonzero sum-zero block has a nonempty proper color class, and alternating permutations realize every subset of its size because parity can be corrected within the subset or its complement.
The same support count applies on a partially occupied signed block, and a fully occupied signed block has \(2^{b-1}\) images under even flips alone.
Each block thus supplies at least \(b\) images, independently.
For the reverse comparison fix coordinate orders in all blocks.
A full-group transport specifies at most \(u^t\) ordered destinations, one permutation parity per occupied block and at most one more binary datum per occupied block, the common sign or the parity of the individual signs.
Two images with the same data differ only by supported actions in \(J_i\) on the occupied destinations.
Writing \(n\) for the orbit size under \(J_i\), this gives \(b^t\le n\) and \(D_i\le(4u)^t n\).
It follows that \(n\ge b\) and \(D_i\le n^{1+\log(4u)/\log b}\).
Over the preceding value field the new value has relative degree at least \(n\), since that field lies in the compositum of previous splitting fields, and at most \(D_i\).
Multiplication gives (1).
At fixed ceiling the polylogarithmic bound for \(u\) makes the relative loss in each logarithm tend uniformly to zero for every degree above the smallest one.
Summation preserves the same relative error for every \(k\).

With uniform Galois elements in a common finite normal field, conditioning on the preceding field restrictions makes the new element uniform on a coset of their kernel.
On the new class space the subgroup average vanishes because independent flips remove all fixed signed coordinates, and independent alternations leave only block constants, excluded by the sum-zero conditions.
Thus the conditional mean of its real log modulus vanishes on each coset.
Prefix sums are a martingale, giving monotone mean absolute values and vanishing cross moments.
For units the mean absolute log modulus is twice the absolute Weil height.
This proves (2) using the degree product, as well as \(s_i h(A_i)\le2h(C)\) by the triangle inequality for the difference of two prefix sums.

In oriented coordinates block centering preserves the class of the exterior product, and the log vector is already centered because the oriented half product is torsion.
In either model the ordinary inner product evaluates its conjugate log modulus.
The full-group orbit average of the operator \(v_iv_i^{\mathsf T}\) commutes with the orthogonal action and has trace \(\|v_i\|^2\), so irreducibility makes it scalar on \(W_i\).
This proves (4) with denominator the dimension of that space.
A conjugate subset product uses any given source root at most once and therefore has logarithm between \(-m_i\) and \(m_i\) by the unit product formula.
Consequently \(S_k^2\le T|S_k|\) with \(T=\sum s_i m_i\), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the other side of the height bounds.

Since \(A_1>1\) is itself an exterior conjugate of its minimum, \(\log M(A_1)\ge m_1\).
Equation (2) of v150 gives \(b_2>d_2/[32m_2^2(29920\log(8d_2))^{36}]\), and Voutier gives \(m_1>2/(\log(3d_1))^3\).
Their product implies (3), including consecutive degrees of record attainment.
For the last estimate put \(A=(4s)^{-1/3}\) with \(s=m_i\).
Voutier and \(u\ll(\log(3d))^{212}\) give \(\log b\ge A\log A-O(\log(A\log A))\) uniformly at small \(s\), as in v89.
The leading coefficient of \(s^{-1/3}\log(1/s)\) is \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\).
For unpowered factors \(m_1\ge m/k\), so the remaining \(k-1\) measures have mean at most \(m/k\).
Convexity and decrease of \(s^{-1/3}\log(1/s)\) for small \(s\) give the asserted sum bound by Jensen.
In \(\log\log M(C)\) the loss from the first factor is at most \(\log(k/m)\), whose ratio to the main scale is at most \((m/k)^{1/3}/(k-1)\).
This proves uniformity in \(k\) at any strict margin in the constant.
These lower growth estimates permit the faster two-record behavior of v155 and retain its open first-measure condition.
