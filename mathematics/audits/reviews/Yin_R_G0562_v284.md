## Mathematical finding

All stated conclusions are supported under the given hypotheses.
No mathematical gap was found in the inspected scope.

The triangular transformation preserves the first nonintegral coefficient depth and full integrality.
Pairing inverse primitive roots cancels their powers of the root of unity, giving exactly the rational square in the resultant identity.
Thus integral resultants, including zero, give integral trace norms.

For a denominator prime, minimal scaling leaves a nonzero reduction of degree at most \(k=n-j-1\).
An integral trace norm forces a common reduced trace root, because its scaled resultant has positive valuation.
Distinct odd primes give disjoint trace-root sets even in characteristic two: a trace determines the unordered pair of roots of \(T^2-xT+1\).
The ramified prime contributes only the trace \(2\), which would force either member of that pair to be \(1\).
Consequently \(k+1\) tests contradict the degree bound.
The same argument with irreducible-factor degree proves the single-test criterion; the Frobenius orbit calculation and the bound \(\ell>B^k+1\) exclude every possible orbit of length at most \(k\).
The case \(k=0\) is covered without any nonempty range of exponents to check.

For sharpness, a prime divisor of \(\Phi_M(M)\) cannot divide \(M\), and reduction away from \(M\) preserves the primitive-order condition.
This proves the asserted simultaneous splitting prime without a prime-distribution theorem.
Simple roots lift to \(\mathbb Z_p\), and distinct integer representatives greater than \(2\) can be chosen to the stated precision.
For each test, one factor in the scaled norm supplies its entire required denominator valuation; every other factor is integral.
The rational norm has no other denominator prime, hence is an integer.
The first nonintegral coefficient occurs at depth \(n-t>j\), including the empty-test case.
The two root sets of the constructed \(S\) are disjoint and simple and avoid \(\pm2\), so passage through \(Z+Z^{-1}\) preserves separability.
Integrality of torsion traces excludes the only possible additional zero of the norm.

For the odd prime power, the trace identity implies that \(F_{p^r}(2+Y)\) reduces to \(Y^e\), while its constant coefficient is exactly \(p\).
Eisenstein therefore gives total ramification and \(v_p(x-2)=1/e\).
When \(k<e\), the least unit-coefficient index \(b\le k\) in the shifted scaled polynomial gives a unique term of minimal valuation \(b/e<1\).
Every conjugate gives the same value, so the trace norm of \(S\) has valuation \(b-ae<0\), contradicting the test.
When \(e\le k\), the proposed \(X^n+F_{p^r}(X)/p\) has its first nonintegral coefficient at depth \(n-e>j\) and takes the values \(x^n\) on primitive traces.
These values are nonzero because an odd-order root of unity cannot have zero trace.
Their norm is a nonzero integer, proving the necessary converse with denominator only \(p\).
