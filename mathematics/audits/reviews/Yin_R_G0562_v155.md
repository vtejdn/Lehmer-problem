For the radical reduction I verified the relative binomial step in the chosen real embedding.
All Salem powers have full degree, so \(U=\mathbb Q(\sigma^h)=\mathbb Q(\sigma)\subset\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The absolute norm of \(\beta=|\alpha|\) over \(U\), meaning the absolute value at this embedding of the relative field norm, is \(\beta^{m_0}\in U\).
Hence the degree-\(m_0\) binomial is the minimal polynomial over \(U\), and its full root orbit gives \(m_0\mid j\) whenever \(\beta^j\in U\).
The power of \(\gamma=\beta^{m_0}\) generates \(U\), and the base embeddings give one exterior, one interior and \(d-2\) neutral conjugates.
There is a nonreal neutral conjugate because \(d\ge4\) and a conjugate equal to \(1\) or \(-1\) would force torsion.
Complex conjugation and irreducibility then force reciprocity, so the degree bound applies to this genuine Salem generator.

The relative polynomial of \(\alpha\) has real coefficients at this embedding and roots on \(|z|=\beta\).
The constant term supplies \(\beta^m\in U\), and the identity \(z^mH(\beta^2/z)=c_mH(z)\) gives the displayed coefficient symmetry.
Thus \(m_0\mid m\) and \(m_0\mid2i\) at each nonzero coefficient, proving the substitution by \(M=m_0/\gcd(m_0,2)\).
The norm polynomial accounts for all \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\) embeddings and is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\); its coefficient polynomial before substitution is consequently the minimal polynomial of \(\lambda=\alpha^M\).
Here \(U\subset\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) also follows directly from \(\lambda^k=\sigma^{hM}\).
Each base embedding has \(t=m/M\) lifts after compression, giving degree \(dt\), exactly \(t\) exterior conjugates and total measure \(L=tu\), where \(u=M\log\beta\ge(\log\gamma)/2\).
Thus \(L\to0\) forces \(d\to\infty\) and \(t\ll\log^3(2d)\), which is within the count range at degree \(dt\), with no bound on \(M\).
Finally, summing the full \(M\)-th-root packets shows that the original earlier energy at index \(Mi\) is exactly \(M^2\) times its compressed counterpart at \(i\), and the endpoint power is \(M\) times the compressed power.
The original statement follows for arbitrary torsion multiples as soon as the common positive powers agree.
