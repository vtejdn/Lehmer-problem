### The ordinary pair

The labels are distinct projective points in the rational vector space generated by the block modulo torsion.
Indeed any rational proportionality gives an equality of powers up to torsion.
Equal positive heights make the exponents equal in absolute value, so the representatives are equal up to sign in that vector space.
The rank bound from the fixed C345 v264 is uniform for every block of at least two labels when \(m\le M\) and \(d\) is sufficiently large.
The projective points span, so rank at least three supplies a noncollinear triple.

One can avoid all marked points by a projective hyperplane and work in its affine chart.
A generic linear projection to a plane preserves distinctness of the finite set and noncollinearity of each originally noncollinear triple, since each forbidden collapse is a proper algebraic condition.
For the minimum positive point-to-spanned-line distance \(\delta\) in the plane, put the foot at coordinate zero on the line.
Among three distinct points on it there are two on the same closed half-line, in order \(B_1,C_1\) away from the foot.
Then \(0<|B_1C_1|\le|\mathrm{foot}\,C_1|<|PC_1|\).
The distance from \(B_1\) to \(PC_1\), computed using the two area formulas for triangle \(PB_1C_1\), is positive and less than \(\delta\).
Thus the minimizing line contains only two marked points.
Their original line is ordinary since collinear triples remain collinear under the projection.
Any third label gives an independent triple in the lattice modulo torsion and hence three multiplicatively independent conjugates.

For a third label \(z\), write \(t_z=|H_{a,b_0}z|\).
The number of ordered label triples in its orbit is at most \(u b(b-1)t_z\), by first fixing a translated block and then an image of the ordered pair.
Lifting the three labels costs at most \(w^3\), so \(J\le d w^2(b-1)t_z\le4d^2t_z\) for the relative joint degree.
The separate bound \(J\le d^3\) follows by the tower law.
Delsinne's Theorem 1.6 for dimension three therefore gives \(m^3/d^3\gg1/(4d^2t_z\log^{K_3}(3d))\), absorbing the fixed factor from \(\log(3d^3)\).
The pair came from the configuration before \(z\) was chosen.

### The four actions

In the pair design each pair has a unique line, and its pointwise stabilizer preserves the other \(k-2\) points on that line.
An exterior point lies on \(k\) different lines to its \(k\) points, and counting its lines by their disjoint sets of other points gives \(k\le (b-1)/(k-1)\).
This supplies an orbit of size \(O(\sqrt b)\) for every pair.
Finite projective lines have \(q+1\) points, affine lines have \(q\), and in the stated dimensions each is proper and determined by two points.

For two ground-set subsets differing in at least two elements on each side, exchanging one element on each side gives a third subset in at most \(k^2\) ways.
When they differ by a single element, discarding a common element and retaining both distinctive elements gives \(k-1\ge1\) possibilities, all distinct from the original pair.
In the complement-extended action, intersecting the pair stabilizer with the ground permutation subgroup has index at most two, which at most doubles either orbit bound.
The inequality \(\binom{2k}{k}\ge2^k\) follows by choosing one element from each of \(k\) disjoint pairs, and \(n\ge2k\) gives the stated logarithmic bound.

In the Cartesian power, changing to the second tuple in a single differing position gives a third tuple if there were two or more such positions.
The two fixed tuples determine the replacement value after each image of the position, so there are at most \(a\) images.
If just one position differs, changing any other position gives at most \((a-1)(s-1)\) possibilities.
Here \(a\le\log_2 b\) and \(s\le\sqrt b\), including the endpoint \(a=s=2\).
All three constructions give a third orbit of size \(O(\sqrt d\log(2d)+\log^2(2d))\) at the ordinary pair.
This contradicts (1) for \(d\to\infty\) at fixed \(M\), because every fixed logarithmic power grows more slowly than \(d^{1/2}\).

In the fourth class an independent triple exists by rank growth and every other triple is its image under a lift of an element of \(H\), preserving independence.
After quotienting by a chosen label, all remaining projective points are nonzero and distinct, and their span has rank at least three for sufficiently large \(d\).
An ordinary pair in this quotient gives independent quadruples with the original label and every remaining fourth label.
Restricting the triple design at that original label gives a pair design with parameters \(b-1,k-1\), hence \((k-1)(k-2)\le b-2\).
The unique subset through the selected triple supplies a fourth label with at most \(k-3\) possible images under its pointwise stabilizer.
The number of images of the four roots is at most \(u b^3w^4(k-3)=d b^2 w^3(k-3)\ll d^{7/2}\).
It is also at most \(d^4\).
The dimension-four product inequality gives \(m^4\gg\sqrt d/\log^{K_4}(3d)\).
In the binary affine example the two independent differences of any distinct triple extend to a basis, giving triple transitivity and a unique affine plane of size four, proper in dimension at least three.

In all four cases the coefficient bound for the primitive minimal polynomial includes its leading coefficient and gives finitely many compressed numbers when \(d\) and \(m\) are bounded.
The finding covers finiteness of measure values for the four specified actions and the uniform pair estimate for general blocks.
The unrestricted symmetric action on individual letters is compatible with a single orbit of size \(b-2\) outside a pair.
