## The nonunit gap and convergence along the entire sequence

At 2026-11-16T19:31 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Yin checked the statement, both proof sections and all twelve dependencies in the submitted dyadic-limit decision of C345v28.
The reviewer took no part in proof development and received no development dialogue, author explanation or prior verdict on this claim.
The [review of the two ledger proofs](Yin_R_G1114_v97.md#positive-return) records this reviewer's checks of the positive measure, integral limits, width computation and reciprocal completions used in the assembled argument.

Failure of a uniform nonunit gap would supply a sequence of strictly positive excesses tending to zero.
By C345v27 it would eventually have delay two, and a further subsequence would have norm two.
More generally, from every subsequence of a delay-two escape one can extract distinct seeds with a fixed norm in \(\{1,2\}\) and a convergent bounded pole parameter separated from one.
The opposite sign and reciprocal seeds would have measure at least the seed, so they disappear eventually.
The surviving polynomials each have measure equal to their unique positive exterior root.
At each fixed derived quotient the boundary modulus loss supplies the nonzero absolutely continuous part required for the Herglotz classification.
If the resulting \(K\) is nonconstant, its width-one defect gives a sufficiently late first discrepancy with unique continuation of either sign.
The exhibited completions realize both signs with a monic seed multiple, and the cyclotomic factor has measure one for every actual output.
Thus every sufficiently late output on that extracted subsequence has the measure of a polynomial with at most fourteen terms, contradicting the fixed-support gap.
This uses a single fixed profile per subsequence, so no bound uniform in the profile on the first discrepancy threshold is needed.

The remaining \(K=1\) forces norm one, the pole \(1/2\), and the quotient \(1/(1-2z)\).
It excludes any infinite nonunit subsequence and, together with C345v27 at all larger delays, gives one positive gap for both signs over \(N\ge2\).
Any subsequence omitting a neighborhood of the proposed limit would itself admit a further subsequence converging to that limit.
This proves convergence of the entire sequence in the stated topology and of the pole parameter to two.
Coefficient convergence near the origin is eventual equality by integrality, and the first discrepant coefficient exists for each late seed because the finite seed quotient has boundary modulus one.
Consequently \(s_i\to\infty\), and the actual positive measure at each late input supplies the asserted Fejér estimate with \(T_i=M_i+M_i^{-1}\).
On printed p. 1255 Boyd identifies the dyadic germ as a point of the second derived set with delay-two value one.

The submitted statement and full assembled argument pass this independent review.
They give necessary conditions for a delay-two escape and leave its existence open.
The earlier \(N\ge3\) gap and the external compactness, width, uniqueness and sparse-gap results were used within their cited scopes.
