### Confluent inertia and zeros and poles

The graph equation and the assumed numerator value imply that the denominator is nonzero at each powered node.
Different contacts give different powered nodes by recovery of \(x_i=R(x_i^p)\).
Division by the denominator and the nonzero derivative of the power map preserve the required order of contact.
The domain and range rotations put all inverse-power germs on the same positive-real branch, because the common ray is a condition on the original \(x_i\).
Unitary rotations and changes of local coordinate with nonzero derivative give invertible triangular changes of jet basis.
Entries of order \(k,l\) in the Hermitian kernel use only the respective Taylor coefficients through orders \(k,l\).
The prescribed contact of order at least \(m_i\) therefore suffices for the stated \(m_i\) jets.

For \(0<\alpha<1\) I checked the scalar integral by the substitution \(t=su\).
Its divided difference at \(z,1/w\) supplies the integrand \(t^\alpha/((t+z)(1+tw))\) with the exterior factor \(c_\alpha w^\alpha\).
Expanding a determinant and averaging over the permutations of the integration variables yields the integral of two Cauchy determinants over the ordered simplex.
The ordering removes the permutation factorial.
The first determinant has positive Vandermonde numerator \(\Delta(z)\Delta(t)\), the second has numerator \((-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\Delta(t)\Delta(w)\).
All remaining factors are positive and the scalar integrals converge at both ends.
On compact interior node sets the two denominators together contribute at least a constant times \(\prod_j(1+t_j)^{2n}\), and the squared Vandermonde costs at most \(\prod_j(1+t_j)^{2n-2}\).
This gives an integrable product bound for taking the confluent limit, and strict positivity survives since the integrand is positive throughout the open ordered simplex.
Divided differences within each ordered group converge to factorial-normalized derivatives, leaving only strictly positive differences between distinct groups.
Every leading submatrix is another such configuration, including a partially retained last group.
The ratios of consecutive nonzero principal determinants have signs \((-1)^{k-1}\) at step \(k\), proving the full signature.
As a separate check I expanded the rational kernel in the original coordinates at the two nodes \(1/3,2/3\), taking three jets at the first and two at the second.
For each of \(p=2,3,5\) exact rational arithmetic gave the leading determinant signs \(+,-,-,+,+\).

The reduced rational function has no boundary zeros or poles since the two original polynomials match in multiplicity there by real-coefficient reversal.
Removing the finite disk Blaschke factors of its interior zeros and poles leaves a function and inverse holomorphic through the closed disk with boundary modulus one.
The maximum principle on both gives the unimodular constant.
The rank-one identity for a single factor, followed by the product identity, bounds the positive kernel rank of either product by its degree even on normalized derivative data.
The denominator product is nonzero at every contact node, so multiplication by it is an invertible congruence on the complete jets.
For a difference of positive semidefinite matrices the number of positive directions is bounded by the rank of the first and the number of negative directions by the rank of the second.
The two disk counts add to the rational degree, since the interior and reflected exterior divisor sets of the reduced product cannot cancel, with zero reflected to infinity.
The rational degree is at most the original formal width \(b\), giving \(b\ge M\).

### Angular scope and coefficient size

For the square map the pulled-back inverse kernel on all disk jets equals \((1-u\bar v)/(1-(u\bar v)^2)=1/(1+u\bar v)\).
The Cauchy denominator is strictly positive because each diagonal factor is real positive and each off-diagonal pair is a squared absolute value.
Its numerator is \((-1)^{n(n-1)/2}|\Delta(u)|^2\).
Taking the conjugate divided differences on the second variable cancels all within-group powers even for nonreal coalescing points.
The remaining cross-group squared distances are positive, and the power map is locally invertible at every selected point.

For the cubic example the rational-root test gives irreducibility, and the opposite extrema at \(-1,1\) and nonzero constant term give three distinct real nonzero roots.
The two-by-two determinant has a negative leading term from exponents \(0,1\).
In the three-by-three determinant the candidate exponents \(0,1,3\) give a linear dependence, whereas \(0,1,4\) give a nonzero Vandermonde times \(3\).
Their coefficient signs are \(+,-,-\), so this next term is positive, with no other exponent triple of the same total degree.
Higher terms are of larger order in the common scale, by absolute convergence.
This verifies inertia \((1,2)\) for sufficiently small rational scale.
The cubed points are distinct because cubing is injective on the real line, and their reciprocals all lie outside the disk.
Galois symmetry of the six prescribed values and uniqueness in degree at most five give a rational interpolant, whose scalar clearing preserves the graph equations and the nonzero numerator values.
This supplies actual signed value data, without claiming contact multiplicity greater than one or a size bound.
