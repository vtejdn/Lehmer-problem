## The degree, measure and bounded-coefficient conclusions hold

At 2026-11-21T15:45 UTC a separate agent acting for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the fixed C0345v866, including all four argument sections and every class of roots in its statement.
The reviewer developed the precursor C0345v865 and took no part in developing C0345v866 or its four-term arguments.
The isolated mixed-area bound and difference-body estimate shared with that precursor already have a separate [independent review](Yin_R_G0562_v1013.md#finding).
This finding checks their application here and the new quotient, common-component and parent arguments.

Primitivity of the affine relation makes its last three coordinates primitive, so the quotient of the three-dimensional exponent lattice is torsion-free of rank two.
The four projected points span this quotient, and equality of two points would force equality of their original exponents.
Their relation module is precisely the rank-one module generated by the chosen relation, including when one coefficient vanishes.
The four projected face determinants have absolute values \(|\lambda_i|\) by expanding the determinant with the primitive kernel vector and two edge differences.
Along a line in that direction, increasing the line parameter decreases exactly the barycentric coordinates with negative \(\lambda_i\), so the upper endpoint belongs to one of their zero faces.
Ties occur on projected edges of area zero, and a face with \(\lambda_i=0\) has zero projected area.
Summing the negative face determinants thus gives \(2A=L/2\), also for a collinear triple or a projected interior vertex.
Two distinct pairs of opposite edge normals would require four extreme vertices giving a parallelogram, whose unique primitive relation is an excluded permutation, including its negative.

Conjugacy carries the point to its inverse and conjugates its coefficients, and normality of \(H\) makes the second equation defined over \(H\).
An edge normal of a two-dimensional common factor must be an edge normal of both polygons, by taking exposed faces in the two Minkowski sums.
Two nonparallel such normals would force the excluded parallelogram.
Consequently any common geometric component is a single primitive-character binomial, even when the original polynomial is reducible or has repeated factors.
For a point on it the character exponent \(ak+bl\) cannot vanish, since vanishing would force \(\gamma=1\) and put the whole parametrized torus in the component, contradicting the four distinct exponents with nonzero coefficients.
Over \(H\) each conjugate of \(\gamma\) contributes a distinct factor with the same primitive segment, whose product has lattice length \([H(\gamma):H]\).
A supporting face in the perpendicular direction contains a translate of that segment, and any off-line lattice vertex has positive integral lattice height, giving the bound \(2A\).
Changing the sign of the power of \(z\) preserves its field.
This argument includes intersections lying on several common components.

Away from the common locus every relative conjugate remains isolated, and removing the geometric gcd preserves every such point.
The residual mixed area is bounded by that of the original polygons, and the overlap integral yields \(|N-N|\le6A\), including a polygon with a parallel edge pair.
Bézout's identity then identifies the orbit size with the relative degree of the positive gcd power, giving the claimed bound \(4A=L\).
Compression supplies the cubic estimate for integral reciprocal roots of small measure, uniformly for \(D\le L\) by eventual monotonicity and compactness.
A nonunit has measure at least \(\log2\), a nonreciprocal unit satisfies Smyth's gap, and measures above one are covered because \(G_3\) is bounded.
Taking cube roots and substituting the preliminary lower bound on \(\log L\) back into \(\log\log L\) gives the exponential length obstruction with one absolute constant for all admissible choices.

For the bounded-coefficient result, fix the rational coefficient tuple in increasing exponent order.
The hyperplane and its inverse are irreducible and nonassociate by their simplex polygons, so their finitely many common components over the algebraic closure have dimension at most one in the three-torus.
The cited BMZ theorem supplies a bounded nonzero character at the point of untwisted powers on each component, and taking the maximum gives a bound for the fixed coefficient tuple.
A nontorsion parameter turns the character identity into an exact integer exponent relation, and adjoining the negative sum after dividing out the gcd makes it primitive.
If the support has no parallelogram identity it is admissible, independently of the signs of the coefficients.
Otherwise strict exponent ordering permits only least plus greatest equal to the sum of the middle two, giving the indicated square support.
In the split case \(ad=bc\) a positive power of the root is rational by one of the two linear binomials.
In the nonsplit case the primitive linear equation and its reverse have the same square support, so any associating Laurent monomial is constant.
Comparing the corner coefficients forces a common multiplier \(+1\) or \(-1\), in both cases making the entire parent reciprocal up to sign and monomial as excluded.
All other sign patterns give coprime equations and mixed area two, so a positive power has relative degree at most two.
These alternatives also cover every nonunit and nonreciprocal root by the preceding gaps.
Taking the minimum over finitely many ordered primitive tuples and capping it at \(\log2\) handles positive-measure integral factors by additivity and integer content.
