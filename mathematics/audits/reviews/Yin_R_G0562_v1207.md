## The two cubic-residue factors, the uniform period bound and the explicit exclusions

At 2026-11-25T04:05 UTC an agent outside development of [C0901 v147](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v147.md#statement) independently reviewed for Yin the revised statement and all three proof sections against the listed inputs.
The reviewer had developed the earlier odd-prime result v123, which is used at the Laurent polygon and the odd-prime congruence interfaces; this review concerns the new assertions of v147.
The initial dilation sentence omitted the nontorsion condition: \(a=2,b=3,u=1,v=2\) already gives the unit root \(-1\) of zero log measure.
The revised sentence explicitly assumes a nontorsion unit root \(z\), so \(z^g\) is nontorsion and the quantitative conclusion applies to that primitive power.
The exact local classification, both global lower bounds and the two types of whole-family exclusion hold at the stated scopes.

Dividing by \(X^u\) when \(a\) is even gives \(C=b,E=a,j=-u,N=v-u\); when \(b\) is even no division is needed and \(C=a,E=b,j=u,N=v+u\).
In both cases reduction on the unit torus gives a scalar multiple of \((X^h-1)^e\) and \((j,N)=1\).
At an odd-order lift \(\zeta\) the constant is \(2C+E(\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j})\).
It has valuation one for \(r\ge2\), and for \(r=1\) its reduction after division by two vanishes precisely if \(\bar\zeta^{2j}+\bar\zeta^j+1=0\).
The order of \(\bar\zeta\) divides \(h\) and is coprime to \(j\), so the only exceptional residues are the primitive cube roots.
They comprise one Frobenius orbit of length two when present.
Away from these centers residual multiplicity \(e\) and the constant of valuation one give just the edge of width \(e\) and drop one, also for \(e=1,2\).

At an exceptional center \(\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j}=-1\), and \(2C-E\) is divisible by four, nonzero since \(E=2C\) would violate coprimality with \(|C|\ge2\).
When \(4\mid N\), the translated linear coefficient divided by two has nonzero residue: \(CN/2\) vanishes and \(j,N-j,E/2\) are all odd, leaving the residue sum one.
Hence the slopes are \(1-v_2(2C-E)\le-1\) and \(-1/(e-1)>-1\); the intermediate integral valuations below \(e\) are at least one and the endpoint at \(e\) has valuation zero.
Multiplication by the Laurent-clearing unit series has zero weighted minimum for each positive weight and preserves these negative-slope edges.
Both examples with \(N=12\) give \(C=3,E=2\) and \(v_2(2C-E)=2\).

Over the unramified field generated by \(\zeta\), the exceptional width-one edge has one root with multiplicity one and thus gives a linear factor.
A width \(w=e\) or \(e-1\) of drop one gives \(w\) roots counted with multiplicity at distance valuation \(1/w\).
The degree of each root there is at least \(w\) by the value group and at most \(w\) by the size of this slope class, which contains every conjugate over the unramified field.
Therefore there is exactly one such totally ramified factor.
The local root field over \(\mathbb Q_2\) itself contains \(\zeta\) by unique Hensel lifting of the odd-order residue root in its residue class.
Restriction of conjugation across the Frobenius orbit now gives a single factor of total degree \(fw\) and residue degree \(f\) per slope on that orbit.
The two exceptional factors have degrees \(2,2(e-1)\), and all ordinary ones have degrees divisible by \(e\).
A rational monic unit minimum selects whole local factors; when \(s\ge2\), their possible sums have the stated three residues and only the degree-two exceptional factor alone can give positive total degree below \(e\).

Monic division shows the reduced minimum divides the reduced parent, and its constant remains one modulo two.
The residue orders contributing to the minimum thus divide \(h\), each with multiplicity at most \(e\).
Their least common multiple \(h_0\) is odd, so \(X^{e h_0}-1\) contains every represented root with sufficient multiplicity.
In \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\) this gives \(\beta^{e h_0}=1+2y\), hence \(\beta^{2e h_0}-1=4(y+y^2)\).
Its norm is nonzero by nontorsion, has absolute value at least \(4^D\), and is at most \(2^D\exp(2e h_0\,m(\beta))\).
For \(i=v_\ell(h_0)>c_\ell\), some residue order is divisible by \(\ell^i\), so it cannot be three even at \(\ell=3\).
Its Frobenius degree is divisible by \(\ell^{i-c_\ell}\) by binomial lifting at the odd prime, and the ordinary factor at that center has degree at least \(e\ell^{i-c_\ell}\), including when \(s<2\).
For \(i\le c_\ell\) and \(D>2\), the local result or \(e\le2\) gives \(D\ge e\).
Since \(h_0\mid H\ell^i\), substitution in the norm inequality gives \((\log2)/(2H\ell^{c_\ell})\) in both cases.
The only possible remaining nontorsion unit degree is two, where the integral trace and norm give \(m\ge\log\varphi\).
For an integral divisor, a nonunit extreme coefficient or nontrivial content costs at least \(\log2\); otherwise any positive contribution comes from a monic nontorsion unit factor.

Along the specified near-pole sequence, the individual estimates for \(\beta=z^g\) give \(J\le u\) eventually, \(m(\beta)\sim (J/u)\log(1+\delta)\) and \(v h(\beta)=\log|b|+o(1)\), with \(v/u\to\infty\) and \(|b|\to\infty\).
Thus \(m(\beta)\to0\), \(D\to\infty\), and \(D\sim (J/u)v\log(1+\delta)/\log|b|\).
The first estimate of (2) follows from \(h=N/e\ge N/D\) and \(N/v\to1\); the second follows directly from (1) and the equivalent for \(m(\beta)\).
The fixed-set comparison at two selected primes indeed has a logarithmic coefficient-height loss.

When \(N=2^s,\ s\ge1\), the only center is one and it is ordinary.
If \(b\) is even the local irreducible factor consumes the full degree \(N\).
If \(a\) is even, the other roots have normalized dyadic valuations of absolute value \(r/u\) by the two side segments through \((0,r),(u,0),(v,0),(u+v,r)\).
In any proper rational factorization some factor omits the unique multiplicity-one unit-root factor and hence every dyadic conjugate of its root \(\kappa\) has such absolute valuation.
The absolute-log form of the product formula then gives \(2h(\kappa)\ge(r/u)\log2\).
The determinant \(a^2-b^2\ne0\) ensures the graph map is defined; bounding its two linear forms at the infinite places and by one times the input maximum at the finite places gives \((v-u)h(\kappa)\le\log(|a|+|b|)\).
These inequalities contradict precisely (3).
Primitivity and \(|a|\ge2\) then prevent an algebraic-unit root of the whole irreducible polynomial and of its dilations.

In the last family the three congruence moduli are coprime with product \(30Q\), so the specified interval of length \(30Q\) always supplies a representative.
They give \(v_2(v-5)=s,\ v_3(v+5)=t,\ (v,5)=1\).
The ordinary dyadic degrees force \(2^s\mid D\).
For odd \(r\) with \(3\nmid r\), expansion of \((-1+3)^r+1\bmod9\) gives \(v_3(q+1)=1\).
The odd-prime index \(v+5\) is even and the reciprocal sign is positive, excluding sign centers at three.
The earlier exact congruence with valuation one and \(t\ge2\) thus gives \(3^t\mid D\), for either choice of the coefficient signs.
Consequently \(D\ge Q\), whereas \(D\log((q+1)/2)\le3(v+5)/q<96Q/q\) is impossible for \(q\ge128\).
The lower bound \(v\ge Q\) permits both single-prime cofactors to grow by taking \(s,t\) large after \(r\).
Every putative nontorsion unit at dilation would have supplied such a primitive unit root, so its exclusion is global and the positive-divisor cost follows.
