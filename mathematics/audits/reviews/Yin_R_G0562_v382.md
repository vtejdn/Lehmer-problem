# Cyclotomic divisors, heights and degrees for the reciprocal cubic seed

<a id="reciprocal-seed-review"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T12:30 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin examined all four sections of [the reciprocal-seed argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v158.md#cyclotomic) and the source interfaces bound below.
The reviewer had no role in developing this argument and received or consulted no development discussion, author explanation or earlier verdict on it.
The claimed cyclotomic classification, individual factor estimates, eventual squarefreeness and square-root comparison all hold for their stated positive odd exponents.

**Cyclotomic divisors.** For \(t=\xi^n\), the order of \(t\) divides \(M=\operatorname{ord}\xi\) and retains its full power of two because \(n\) is odd.
The exponents \(2\) for odd \(M\), \(2+M/2\) for \(M\equiv2\bmod4\), and \(1+M/2\) for \(4\mid M\) are units modulo \(M\) in their respective cases.
Applying the resulting automorphisms to \(\xi=-g(t)/b(t)\) is legitimate since \(b\) has no unit-circle zero.
I checked the three cleared numerators by exact polynomial expansion, obtaining respectively \(-2tL,\ 2\Phi_{12}\Phi_{30}\), and \(-2(t^2-1)(t^4+1)\).
In the first case \(L(1)=-1\) excludes order one and every odd prime power, and the totient bound leaves only \(15\) among the other odd orders.
Division by \(\Phi_{15}\) gives the stated degree-six remainder with leading coefficient \(-2\).
In the second case order \(12\) has too large a power of two, while the identity \(g+t^9b=-(t^4+1)\Phi_{30}\) would make \(\xi=t^9\) of order \(10\) if \(t\) had order \(30\).
In the last case \(t\) is primitive of order eight, and \(g+tb=-(t^4+1)\) gives \(\xi=t\), equivalent at these roots to \(n\equiv1\bmod8\).
Conversely these roots all occur in that congruence class.
The derivative there reduces to \((-3n-1)t^3+(n-1)t^2-(n-1)\), of degree strictly less than \(\deg\Phi_8=4\) and nonzero for every positive \(n\).
This accounts for multiplicity, including \(A_1=-\Phi_8\).

**Factor height.** The constant and leading coefficients of \(A_n\) have absolute value one, so \(\alpha\) and \(\beta=\alpha^n\) are units.
The resultant of \(g,b\) is \(-1\), and evaluating an integral Bézout relation gives \(1=b(\beta)u\) with \(u\) an algebraic integer.
Thus \(b(\beta)\) is a unit, and the two maps \(\beta=\alpha^n,\ \alpha=-g(\beta)/b(\beta)\) give equality of the fields.
The uniform lower estimate for \(\max(|g(z)|,|b(z)|)/\max(1,|z|)^3\) follows from coprimality and the limit one at infinity.
Taking the embedding average and using the zero log norm of \(b(\beta)\) gives \((3-1/n)h(\beta)\le-\log c_0\).

For the opposite bound, all nontorsion \(\beta\) avoid every proper algebraic subgroup of \(\mathbb G_m\), so any sequence with heights tending to zero satisfies Bilu's strictness hypothesis.
For \(n\ge3\) the six exponents \(0,1,n,2n+1,3n,3n+1\) are distinct, hence \(M(A_n)\le\sqrt6\) bounds every conjugate of \(\alpha\) in modulus.
The corresponding \(\beta\)-orbits satisfy \(|g|\le\sqrt6\,|b|\), a closed set disjoint from the zeros of \(b\) on which \(|b|\) has a positive lower bound by growth at infinity.
Truncating \(\log|b|\) at both ends as in the submission thus gives a bounded continuous function, bounded above on this set by \(\log|b|\) and equal to it on the circle.
Its orbit means are at most zero by the unit norm, whereas Jensen's formula gives its Haar mean \(m(b)=m(g)=\log\theta>0\).
Bilu's theorem therefore supplies the contradiction, proving a positive floor on \(h(\beta)\) uniform in \(n\) and in the chosen factor.
The alternative fixed-function inequality applies because the coprime cubics have nonzero roots and their ratio cannot be a Laurent monomial.
Finally \(m(P)=d h(\beta)/n\) gives both bounds for the individual factor.

**Degree and multiplicity.** Each nontorsion unit has degree at least two.
Voutier's corollary in this actual degree, with \(m(P)\ll d/n\) and \(d\le3n+1\), first yields \(d\gg n/(\log n)^3\).
It follows uniformly that \(\log d=\log n+O(\log\log n)\), justifying use of the sharper Voutier bound with the logarithms of \(n\).
Summing the resulting minimum degrees against the available \(3n+1\) bounds the count with multiplicities.
Differentiation at a repeated zero gives \(n\beta f'(\beta)=f(\beta)\).
After clearing denominators the obstruction has degree six with leading and constant coefficients one.
Equality of the two root fields bounds \(d\) by six, contradicting the uniform degree estimate for sufficiently large \(n\).
The cyclotomic simplicity check accounts for the remaining roots.

**Lifts and scope.** If a square-root tower has \(j\) steps of relative degree one, the tower law gives \(d'=2^{k-j}d\).
The power law for absolute height then gives \(m(P_\gamma)=2^{-j}m(P)\).
The two-sided factor estimate and \(d/n\le3+1/n\) prove both directions of the lift criterion.
For a fixed base unit, the norm of its lifted root gives \(2^k\mid [\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q(\alpha)]s\), where \(s\) is the saturation index of that base's logarithmic unit ray.
This bounds \(2^j\) by \(s\) for that base.
The estimates leave open whether a fixed positive fraction of the seed degree always lies in one nontorsion factor.
Bilu's and Voutier's theorems were used at the statement and applicability scopes of the cited source records.
