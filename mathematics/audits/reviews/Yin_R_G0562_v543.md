## Outcome for the fixed-width and growing-width tests

All four sections hold with the stated strict budgets and for either prescribed sign.
The existence of one fixed width is asserted separately for each count \(r\).
The divergence conclusion uses widths tending to infinity.

**Powers and the original test.**
For a positive measure less than \(\log 2\), integral content costs zero, and factorization supplies a primitive irreducible positive contribution at most the original measure.
An input with arbitrarily small slack above the infimum therefore suffices in each strict hypothesis.
C421 supplies a representative \(\delta\) of that contribution \(h\) with \(\deg(\delta^k)=\deg(\delta)\) at every positive \(k\).
The absolute height power formula gives exactly \(ih\) and \(nih\) for the total measures at the original and powered nodes in the construction.
Within either list these values are positive and distinct, which also precludes collisions between different powered orbits.
If \(T_r\lambda<B\), the list of the first \(r\) multiples of a suitable \(h\) has total measure \(T_rh<B\), so C421 realizes it eventually at every integer, in particular at every sufficiently large prime.
Contraposition gives \(\lambda\ge B/T_r\) for the original growing-multiplicity test, with \(B/T_2=(\log2)/12\).

**Counting and retaining at fixed width.**
For an integral coefficient cube with side parameter \(K\), field \(i\) contributes at most \((6lK)^{d_it_i}(1+L)^{d_it_i(t_i-1)/2}\exp(Lt_i\cdot ih)\) divided-derivative tuples.
Multiplying these bounds gives the exponents \(J,J',LH_0\) even though the powers in this application generate the same field.
With \(l>J\) the pigeonhole denominator is \(l-J\), and the difference polynomial is nonzero since the two support classes are disjoint for \(n\ge2\).
Taking \(l\) large before increasing \(n\) bounds the leading term of \(2\log\|F_0\|_1/n\) by \(H_0l/(l-J)\).
All remaining terms at this fixed width are \(O(\log(1+n)/n)\).
The chosen slack makes the upper bound strictly less than \(2H_0\) eventually, ruling out a single nonzero row by the powered product of order \(t_i\) at each node.

If the common polynomial gcd has order \(c_i\), its square divides the differential polynomial, and the quotient contributes at least \(\max(0,t_i-c_i-1)\) further orders.
This gives at least \(t_i+c_i-1\) in both ranges of \(c_i\), and the nonzero least coefficient permits the measure estimate.
Since \(S=T_Nh\), subtraction gives \(h(\sum_i i c_i-T_N)\le\eta/2+o(1)<h\) eventually.
The difference in parentheses is integral, which gives the exact budget \(\sum_i i c_i\le T_N\).
After division any index with \(c_i\le t_i-2\) retains two orders, and the coprime rows cannot vanish simultaneously at its powered node.
The two projections, multiplied by the appropriate \(Y\pm1\), have one prescribed sign at width \(w_0+1\).
At each retained node at least one projected value is nonzero, so at most one slope is forbidden there, and \(0,\ldots,N\) suffice jointly.
The row norm factor \(4(1+N)2^{w_0}\) is constant with respect to \(n\).

For a larger prescribed width the reversal of \(Y^k(Y+1)^e\) at width \(2k+e\) equals itself and the only possible roots of the multiplier are zero and minus one.
The powered nodes are nonzero and nontorsion, so this multiplication preserves the nonvanishing tests and the contacts at either sign.
Its one-norm is at most two, independently of \(k\).
The additional budget term \(\log(2nb(n)+1)/n\) tends to zero under the stated hypothesis, and eventual \(b(n)\ge w\) suffices even without monotonicity.

**Two costs and the converse.**
The weighted budget individually implies \(c_i\le\lfloor T_r/i\rfloor\), proving retention of all indices at the first cost.
For the second, losing \(3r+1\) indices costs at least \(2T_{3r+1}\), and \(2T_{3r+1}-T_{4r}=r^2+7r+2>0\).
The first costs at \(r=1,2\) are 3 and 11 and are smaller than the respective second costs.
The bound \(3T_{4r}=24r^2+6r\) supplies the asserted quadratic growth bound for the minimum.
Either construction uses \(H_0=C_rh<B\) when the smaller cost is selected.
Conversely, the differential product bounds the sum of one representative per distinct value strictly below \(B\) for arbitrary \(n,b\).
A positive gap therefore uniformly bounds the counts, whereas a zero gap realizes each count on every sufficiently late term of the specified width sequence.
The logarithmic prime-width example satisfies \(p>4b+1\) eventually, and the qualifying unit minima and scalar normalization meet C422's local hypotheses.
