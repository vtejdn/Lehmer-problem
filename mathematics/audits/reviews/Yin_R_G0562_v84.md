## Equal bins, cyclotomic portions and the density over varying node sets

The degree-preserving power result leaves one exterior trace above two and \(k\) distinct neutral traces whose folded angles have joint uniform limiting distribution on \([0,\pi]^k\).
All torsion trace nodes lie in \([-2,2]\), so the exterior trace fills the top interval.
Success therefore requires exactly that every lower bin receive at least one of the \(k\) neutral samples.
The boundaries lie in coordinate hyperplanes at the node angles and have measure zero.
These facts allow the occupancy probability to be used for the natural density at the given embedding.

Samples in the unrequired interval can be reassigned to the lower bins while preserving every previous success.
For lower probabilities of total mass one, conditioning on the other bins and the positions of the samples assigned to a selected pair fixes whether the other bins are filled.
If the pair receives \(a\ge2\) samples and has mass \(s>0\), the probability of filling both is \(1-(p_i/s)^a-(p_j/s)^a\).
Convexity makes averaging the two probabilities favorable, and an empty pair or fewer than two samples cannot contribute a success.
The probability is continuous on the simplex, so minimizing the sum of squares among maximizers forces all entries equal.
For \(g=1\) one just puts the entire mass in the required bin.
The equal-bin probability is the number \(g!\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}k\\g\end{smallmatrix}\right\}\) of surjections divided by \(g^k\).
The recurrence for \(U_{g-1}(x/2)\) gives a monic integer polynomial with the distinct interior nodes \(2\cos(j\pi/g)\), \(1\le j<g\).
Adjoining \(2\) leaves exactly \(g\) lower bins of angular length \(\pi/g\), including the case of the single polynomial \(x-2\).

Inside a common cyclotomic field \(L\), a primitive order-\(q\) trace has stabilizer given by the two signs in the units modulo \(q\) for \(q\ge3\).
Indeed equality of two traces identifies the two lifts up to inversion.
The regular quotient orbit thus has \(\varphi(q)/2\) elements, with distinct orders disjoint and \(q=1,2\) each giving a separate singleton.
Restriction onto \(G_q\) is surjective since the trace field is a Galois subextension of \(L/\mathbb Q\).
The image of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) has index at most \([K:\mathbb Q]\) in that quotient.
It preserves the selected subset, which must then be a union of its regular-action cosets.
This argument allows \(K\) to vary and requires the occupancy condition only at the selected embedding.

The CRT decomposition and odd-prime-power cyclicity give at most \(\omega(q)+1\) generators after allowing the 2-primary factor two generators.
For \(a\ge3\), successive squaring of \(5\) raises the 2-adic valuation of \(5^{2^j}-1\) from \(j+2\) to \(j+3\).
Hence its order modulo \(2^a\) is \(2^{a-2}\), giving all units congruent to one modulo four, and multiplication by \(-1\) gives the rest.
Passing to a quotient retains this generator bound.
For index \(j\le D\), there are at most \((j!)^{\omega(q)+1}\) homomorphisms to \(S_j\), at most \(j\) candidate stabilizers per homomorphism, and at most \(2^j\) unions of their cosets for the transitive actions.
The coset action of every index-\(j\) subgroup occurs among these.
Thus \(C_D^{\omega(q)+2}\) bounds the number of possible portions and every nonempty one has size at least \(\varphi(q)/(2D)\) for \(q\ge3\).

Absorbing the finitely many small primes gives \(C_D^{\omega(q)}\ll_{D,\delta}q^\delta\) and \(q/\varphi(q)\ll_\delta q^\delta\).
For a portion of size at most \(x\) this restricts \(q\) to \(O_{D,\delta}(x^{1/(1-\delta)})\).
Summing the first bound over that range and then choosing \(\delta\) small gives \(B(x)\ll_{D,\nu}x^{1+\nu}\) for every \(\nu>0\), including both endpoints.
A node set maps injectively to the collection of its nonempty portions, one per order and of total weight equal to its size.
Counting all item subsets of weight at most \(g\) can only increase that count.
The convergent product \(\prod_E(1+e^{-t|E|})\) therefore bounds the count after multiplication by \(e^{tg}\).
Layer integration gives \(\sum_E e^{-t|E|}=\int_0^\infty te^{-tx}B(x)\,dx\ll_{D,\nu}t^{-1-\nu}\).
The specified \(t\) bounds the logarithmic count by \(O_{D,\nu}(g^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)})\), giving the claimed exponent for every \(\epsilon>0\).
The resulting finite union has null boundary and its measure depends only on \(k,g,D\).
Its lower bound comes from the equal grid and its upper bound is the candidate count times the single-grid maximum, capped at one.

The event that the first \(g\) labeled samples are all distinct has probability \(g!/g^g\) and ensures occupancy.
For the upper estimate, an injection assigning one witness to each bin has probability \(g^{-g}\) and there are \(k!/(k-g)!\) such injections.
With \(h=k-g=o(k)\), Stirling's estimate gives \(-k+o(k)\) for the logarithms of both bounds, since \(h\log(k/h)=o(k)\) for positive \(h\) and \(g\log(k/g)=O(h)\).
The logarithmic candidate count contributes \(o(k)\).
For \(1\le k/g\le C\), conditioning independent Poisson counts on their sum \(k\) gives the equal-bin multinomial law.
The event of a positive count at every bin has unconditional probability at most \((1-e^{-C})^g\), and the conditioning event has probability of order \(k^{-1/2}\).
After the subexponential candidate factor, this gives an exponential upper bound in \(g\) for fixed \(C,D\), with the factorial lower bound providing the other direction.
When \(k/g-\log g\to+\infty\), the union bound on empty bins for the rational grid is at most \(g e^{-k/g}\to0\).
All limits refer to densities for individual inputs and give no uniform estimate for the first successful exponent.
