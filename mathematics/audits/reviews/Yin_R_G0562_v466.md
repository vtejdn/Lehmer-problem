### Independence between rational factors

The three support points \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\) give the full lattice, and I obtained \(\operatorname{Res}(g,B)=-1\).
Thus the graph is primitive and linear in \(V\), hence absolutely irreducible.
For the primitive directions in question, C253 gives \(h(x)\ll1/N\) and uniform growth of each degree, and C251 with projected gcd one excludes distinct torsion twists of roots, even across different rational factors.
The field generated by a root over \(L=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) therefore has the same degree and is the same field after any positive power.
Reciprocity permits replacing a root by its inverse.
For a proposed dependence take \(x^v/z^u\) torsion with \(u,v>0\) coprime.
Then \(w=x^i z^j\), where \(iu+jv=1\), has \(w^u/x,w^v/z\) torsion.
All roots of unity lie in \(L\), giving \(L(x)=L(z)=L(w)\) of relative degree \(D_0\le 4a+b\le5N\).
The AD07 rational-base bound gives \(h(w)\gg1/(N(\log(2N))^4)\), hence \(u,v\ll(\log(2N))^4\).
At \(x\), the same theorem with its actual relative degree gives \(D_0\gg N/(\log(2N))^4\).

For \(c,e\ne0\), the constant coefficient in \(V\) of \(F(cU^q,eV^q)\) has a simple nonzero zero, since \(g(0)\ne0\), \(g\) is separable and \(q\ge1\).
At that zero the leading \(V\)-coefficient is a unit by coprimality.
Eisenstein and primitivity over \(\mathbb C[U]\) therefore apply for every \(q\).
If \(u\ne v\), the two indicated irreducible curves have different \(V\)-degrees, so their intersections number at most \(25uv\).
All \(D_0\) conjugates over \(L\) of \((w^a,w^b)\) are distinct intersection points, since the two powers recover \(w\) and the coefficients of the twists lie in \(L\).
The lower degree bound outgrows \(25uv\).
When \(u=v=1\), torsion-quotient exclusion leaves only equality after the possible inversion.
This proves the required independence, allowing application of AD99 at rank two with joint absolute degree \(J\le25N^2\).
Its exponent \(2\kappa(2)=212\) and the two height upper bounds give \(J\gg N^2/(\log(2N))^{212}\).

C397 applies at \(s=4\), and C391 gives simplicity of the roots for all sufficiently large \(r\).
The projections of the commutator of the trace splitting group are precisely the \(A_{n_i}\), which are simple since all \(n_i\) grow uniformly.
A proper subdirect product identifies at least two isomorphic factors diagonally.
Their degrees agree by group order, and for \(n_i\ge9\) the identification is a permutation of the natural labels.
After relabeling, normalization of the diagonal means that the quotient of the two coordinate permutations centralizes \(A_{n_i}\) and hence is the identity.
Matched traces then have the same stabilizer in the full compositum and generate the same absolute degree-\(n_i\) field.
Each inverse-pair lift has degree at most two over this field, giving joint degree at most \(4n_i=O(N)\).
Lifts in distinct rational factors are unequal even up to inversion, so this contradicts the preceding lower bound.
The entire product of independent alternating actions follows.

### Units, signs and exact integral testing

I expanded \(D(t+t^{-1})=B(t)B(t^{-1})\) directly.
The resultant with \(B(X^a)\) has absolute value \(|\operatorname{Res}(g,B)|^a=1\), since at every root of \(B(X^a)\) the section specializes to \(g(X^a)\).
All factors in the resultant product are algebraic integers, so each is a unit, also at the inverse root.
A full trace orbit therefore has a rational unit as its product.
For a circle root its eigenvalue is positive, and the product at a pair of nonreal conjugate traces is positive by complex conjugation.
At a real exterior root \(x\), the eigenvalue is \(-x^{b-4a}B(x^a)^2\).
For large \(r\) the real-root count gives just one such \(x<0\) if \(a\) is odd and none if \(a\) is even.
For even \(b\), primitivity forces odd \(a\), and precisely the orbit containing this negative root has product \(-1\).
For odd \(b\) all orbit products are positive.

The powers \(x^a,x^b\) recover \(x\) and \(x^a\) determines \(x^b\) on the graph, so the \(x^a+x^{-a}\) values separate inverse pairs.
Equality of two \(D\)-values in one block would, by double transitivity and Galois equivariance, make the nonconstant quartic \(D\) take one value at more than four distinct arguments.
For a proper nonempty subset of a block, a three-cycle can move one selected entry to an unselected entry and leave their other contributions to the product unchanged.
This works for either size of the smaller side, since \(n_i\ge9\), and the independent action fixes all other blocks.
The product changes by the quotient of two distinct nonzero eigenvalues.
Hence exactly unions of whole blocks have rational products, and these products are their signs.
Separable \(H\) makes its multiplication algebra split over the trace splitting field, with one coordinate per trace.
This diagonalizes \(M\) even if two values from different blocks coincide.
Exterior powers have one basis vector for each subset, with its product as eigenvalue.
Extension of scalars preserves the two kernel dimensions, giving both generating identities including the empty and full subsets.
In the unsigned generating polynomial the first nonconstant coefficient counts the blocks of the smallest degree, so division by their factors iteratively recovers all degrees with multiplicity.

For \(R=|t|\ge4\) one has \(R^4+R+1<2(R^4-R^3-1)\), incompatible with \(R^r=|g/B|\) for \(r\ge1\).
Inversion supplies the other endpoint, so each \(B(t)\) and \(B(t^{-1})\) has magnitude at most \(321\).
There are exactly \(\binom nj\) eigenvalues of \(\wedge^jM\) with multiplicities and each has modulus at most \(321^{2j}\).
The integral determinant is thus strictly smaller in absolute value than the prescribed modulus under (3).
Reduction of the multiplication matrix, its exterior powers and their determinants commutes with reduction modulo \(3^K\), including when \(H\) has multiple roots in the residue field.
This argument gives an exact vanishing test for both signs.

### Exterior counts and shrinking sequences

For the single simple exterior pole \(-\beta\), let \(I=\log\beta>0\).
The reciprocal identity on the circle and Jensen give \(m(B)=I\).
The quartic graph meets L198's coprimality, unit-endpoint and support hypotheses.
C391's allocation and L198's individual height estimate thus give \(a m_i=s_i(I+O(e^{-cr}))\) and \(b m_i/d_i=I+O(r^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\log(2d_i)/d_i})\) with common constants for all factors.
Since the least \(d_i\) tends to infinity, comparison first gives \(d_i/r\sim s_i\) uniformly.
Substitution back bounds the absolute error by a constant times \(s_i/\sqrt r+\sqrt{s_i\log(2d_i)/r}\).
Using \(s_i\le a,\ d_i\le4a+b\) proves (4).
Under \(r/a^2\to\infty\) the first term tends to zero and the second does too, since \(a\le\sqrt r\) eventually and \(\log(2(4a+b))=O(\log(2r))\).
Eventually the error for every factor is less than \(1/2\), giving a unique nearest integer.

For even \(b\), the least detected negative degree is the degree of the unique negative-sign block.
Its exterior count \(s_i\) is odd as every nonreal exterior root is paired by conjugation.
Its degree \(j=d_i/2\) satisfies \(j/r=(s_i/2)(1+o(1))\) uniformly as the slope grows.
Consequently \(j<r\) holds exactly when \(s_i=1\), even when larger odd counts vary with the input.
In that case the reciprocal factor of \(-\sigma\) has degree exceeding two eventually and all remaining conjugates beyond the exterior root and its inverse lie on the circle.
Changing sign gives a Salem number, and conversely the minimal polynomial of a Salem number has only one exterior root.
The exterior estimate now gives \(m_i\ll1/a\) already for large slopes and \(m_i\sim I/a\) as \(r\to\infty\).
The degree and height lower bounds from C253 give \(m_i\gg(\log\log N/\log N)^3\).
Thus \(a\ll(\log N/\log\log N)^3\) and \(r/a^2=b/a^3\to\infty\) when \(N\to\infty\).
A sequence of these \(\sigma\) tending to one forces \(N\to\infty\), since bounded \(N\) yields only finitely many roots.
Consequently \(r\to\infty\) there and the asymptotic for \(m_i\) gives \(\sigma\to1\) exactly when \(a\to\infty\) on the escaping sequences in question.

Finally the primitive cyclotomic degree is bounded by 13 by L212, so \(n=(4a+b-O(1))/2\sim b/2\).
As \(r\to\infty\), \(d_i/r\sim s_i\) makes \(j_i=n_i=o(n)\) equivalent to \(s_i=o(a)\).
A detected degree \(1\le j=o(n)\) contains at least one full block of that size or smaller, and each small block alone gives a vanishing determinant with its own sign.
C391's equivalence for common dilations transfers exactly this small-block criterion to arbitrary positive directions.
Existence of the shrinking sequence remains open.
