**Position of the exterior roots.**
The reciprocal minimum has two exterior roots and their two distinct interior inverses.
The exterior roots belong to distinct inverse pairs since they have the same modulus greater than one.
Complex conjugation thus induces a single transposition on the inverse-pair labels, fixing every remaining label by inversion on its circle roots.
A transposition across two different blocks would move only one point of either block and keep another point in place, contrary to preservation of the block partition.
The two labels therefore lie in one block even when no degree-record hypothesis is available.
Distinct conjugates modulo torsion and the stated degree threshold allow the two alternatives on the actual roots to be used directly.
If a torsion-class product is taken first, the exterior-pair hypothesis must be checked on that product.

**Orbits and the field norm.**
There are \(N=u\binom b2=n(b-1)/2\) eligible unordered label pairs.
In the first alternative, two-transitivity orders any pair in its block, and transitivity on blocks reaches all such blocks.
The even inversion subgroup gives each of the four sign assignments on the destination pair by using a third coordinate for parity.
The multiplicative independence of one representative per label distinguishes the resulting \(4N\) products, while the ordered pair of roots has \(8N\) images.

In the second alternative each block has a neutral label, since \(b\ge9\).
Complex conjugation fixes that label and takes either of its two roots to the inverse, so it interchanges the canonical halves of the block.
In the half containing \(\alpha\), the representatives on the two special labels are therefore \(\alpha\) and \(\bar\alpha^{-1}\).
Every selected half product has modulus one at the chosen embedding and every conjugate of such a product is another half product.
Kronecker's theorem gives \(u\) relations modulo torsion, one independent constant vector per block.
The rank lower bound \(n-u\) makes them the entire rational kernel, with no need to assume least degree to obtain equality here.
The ratio vectors \(e_i-e_j\) are pairwise distinct in the quotient since their blockwise sums vanish and the only blockwise constant with all these sums zero is zero.
The alternating subgroup realizes the \(b(b-1)\) ordered choices in each selected half, and simultaneous inversion yields a ratio already counted.
This gives \(2N\) product values.
For the ordered roots there are \(2n\) first-root choices, each with \(b-1\) choices for the second in the opposite half, by the point stabilizer in the alternating action.
Hence the ordered orbit has \(4N\) members.

The stabilizer of the ordered pair consists exactly of the automorphisms fixing both generators of \(E\), so its index is \([E:\mathbb Q]\).
The index of the product stabilizer gives \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\).
Their ratio is two in each case.
Complex conjugation preserves \(E\), fixes \(\beta\) and exchanges \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\), so its fixed field there is exactly \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
The relative norm and trace are \(\beta\) and \(\alpha+\bar\alpha\), respectively.
Division of the ordered orbit size by \([K:\mathbb Q]=2n\) yields the asserted extension degrees \(2(b-1)\) and \(b-1\).
Realness here refers to the chosen embedding of the fixed field.

**Heights and both measure multipliers.**
For a unit \(y\) the product formula gives \(h(y)=\tfrac12\operatorname{mean}_g|\log|g y||\) in a normal field containing \(y\).
For \(x^q=\xi y\) with \(y\) the indicated integral monomial, this computes \(h(x)=h(y)/q\) even when \(x\) or \(\xi\) lies outside the splitting field.
The preimage of the special pair is uniform on the \(N\) choices by transitivity and equality of stabilizer fiber sizes.
Conditional on a given ordered preimage, parity-corrected flips give all four signs with equal multiplicity in the first alternative.
Their mean absolute linear form on \(t,t'\) is \((|t+t'|+|t-t'|)/2=\max(|t|,|t'|)\).
In the oriented alternative the signs are opposite at the distinguished pair and transport can only reverse them together, giving \(|t-t'|\).
This recovers the factor \(\log R/(2N)\) in both formulas, including torsion twists and arbitrary positive radical exponents.
The description of the oriented kernel gives precisely one rational representative with every block sum zero after subtracting its blockwise averages.

Counting the exterior conjugates of \(\beta\) directly gives a separate check of its total measure.
In each alternative the pair of distinguished labels contributes one conjugate of modulus \(R^2\).
There are \(2(b-2)\) pairs meeting exactly one distinguished label.
Each gives two products of modulus \(R\) in the independent case and one ratio of modulus \(R\) in the oriented case.
Every remaining product has modulus at most one.
Since \(m(\alpha)=2\log R\), this gives \(m(\beta)/m(\alpha)=2b-3\) and \(b-1\), respectively.
These multipliers alone give no uniform positive lower bound for \(m(\alpha)\).
