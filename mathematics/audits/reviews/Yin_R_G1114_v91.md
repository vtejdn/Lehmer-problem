This proves equality without any comparability among the unchosen scales.

In the division lemma, positivity and distinctness of the exposing exponents ensure that both endpoints of each polynomial slice have nonzero fixed modulus.
The constant is \(m\), so the log measure is bounded below by \(\log m\) on each slice, and continuity and the Haar mean force equality everywhere.
Thus every slice polynomial is zero-free in the open disk.

After multiplying the Laurent numerator by a suitable power of the exposed variable, the quotient has no pole at zero because the denominator there is \(m\).
Division in that variable is valid in the rational Laurent ring of the base coordinates because the leading coefficient is a scalar times a monomial.
The remainder specializes to zero almost everywhere, hence vanishes identically by Fourier uniqueness.
Gauss's lemma for the primitive \(P\) returns the quotient to the integer Laurent ring, including when \(P\) is reducible or has repeated zeros.

The ordered Taylor test uses the genuine constant one at index zero and doubled moments at positive indices.
Every fixed character shift is \(O(D_i)\), so each positive mixed test at \(kt_i+\lambda_i(g)\) lies in the available prefix for \(D_i\ll t_i\ll H_i\).

Separating distinct slopes and applying Fourier decay therefore leaves an integer Laurent density \(H=(2P/m)f\) for each grouped slope.
The positive component has finite mass after division off \(P=0\), and mass directly above \(P=0\) contributes zero to these rows even when that set has positive dimension.

If it is nonzero, its integer mean is positive, so its slope has mass at least \(m/2\).

This recovers the excluded Haar marginal.

With these slopes gone, the constant and first harmonics are the only ones possible for the conditional pushforward at the last nonzero positive harmonic.

Consequently that integer Laurent row has measure bounded above by \(M(P)M(f_0)/m<1\), since the absolutely continuous marginal density can have geometric mean one only for the Haar probability.

## The scale induction and the gap at bounded coefficient denominator

On 2026-11-16T18:31 UTC the same reviewer independently checked all five sections of the fixed [bounded-denominator result](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#gap).

I find the claimed uniform positive excess valid for each pair \(K,m_{\max}\), including varying branch and degree, and the two fixed-branch consequences follow.

The least common denominator \(m\) divides \(j\), and normalization gives an integral polynomial \(F\) with constant and Mahler measure \(m\).
The sparse height estimate fixes the finite coefficient tuple along a subsequence and bounds the absolute coefficient sum of \(F/m\).
The inverse-measure representation is for the original input at its genuine \(j\), so \(\alpha\to0\), \(B(0)=1\), \(1<M(B)\to1\) and \(B=(F/m)J\) all survive this rescaling.
The first nonintegral index \(d=o(h)\) is an actual lower exponent, and integer division through \(d-1\) gives integral Taylor coefficients of \(J\) before it, even if there are earlier nonzero integral numerator terms.

For unbounded \(d\) the local models apply because tests at \(o(d)\) and their fixed differences are in the integral range, the negative atom is at one, and the \(2h\) distinct charged lifts exceed the degree of any annihilator in the moving-index proof.
For the small-\(u\) reciprocal model the squared binomial kills its atom measure and leaves the Haar part with density at most four.
The Fourier differences required on blocks of diameter \(\kappa d/4\), even after a shift by \(u\), are within the exact matching interval.
In a transition piece of diameter strictly less than \(u\), all nonzero differences have zero moment.
Splitting at all original and translated endpoints handles overlapping transition intervals, and the endpoint coefficients are bounded.
The other models either have bounded Haar density or match the zero prefix on the shorter blocks.
For fixed \(k\) distinct indices the positive-part Gram differs from the signed one in operator norm \(O(k\alpha\|P\|_\infty^2)\), and the individual errors in the signed integrals tend to zero uniformly over the locations.
Choosing \(k\) after fixing the limiting Gram and multiplier bounds gives the all-location coefficient bound in (1).
Bounded \(d\) follows by direct coefficient counting.

All scale ratios can be stabilized at once since there are finitely many offsets.
At successive classes \(s\ll S\), the integral polynomial \(A\) includes every lower offset of size \(O(s)\), including \(d\), while all other exponents and the entire reflected half are at least \(\eta S\).
The exposed-face argument and the specialized shortest-relation condition give \(M(A)\to m\).
Its primitive constant \(m/\operatorname{cont}(A)\) is at least two, because a chosen coefficient of \(A/m\) is nonintegral.
The fixed-\(j\) statement of v105 requires checking its underlying proof here.
That proof uses the normalized integral-prefix equality, the fixed primitive face including its measure equality, and the original signed measures with negative masses tending to zero.
They are available after fixing \(m\) for the rescaled polynomial \(F\), independently of whether the original \(j\) stays bounded.
Its intermediate tests use \(O(s)\) and \(kt+O(s)\) for \(s\ll t\ll S\), giving the binomial product \(U\) on the claimed interval with the constant scale factor adjusted to \(D\asymp s\).
The finite number of later extractions preserves all earlier estimates.

I rederived the weighted Gram identity using \(C=PUB\), \(V=PUA\) and \(C=VJ/m\) on the available nonnegative prefix.
The zero interval of \(UB\) shortens by at most \(\deg P=O(s)\) under multiplication by \(P\).
The entire surviving initial section of \(C=U(PB)\) is controlled by a bounded number of translates of the previous length-\(s\) intervals.
For each support index \(a\) of \(V\), the two coefficient indices in \(V^*C+VC^*\) are \(k+a\) and \(a-k\).
Both are either negative, where the one-sided sequences vanish, or within the controlled positive prefix for \(|k|\le S/(4L)\).
Convolving the two finite polynomials against \(J+J^*\) therefore gives exactly \(2\int v^k|V(v^{-1})|^2\,d\omega\) after restoring the factor \(m\), with the two Taylor constants summing to two at zero.
Summing the absolute right-hand side over these \(k\) costs at most twice the prefix sum of \(C\) times \(m\|V\|_{\rm coef}\).
This bounds all absolute row sums of the weighted signed Gram matrix on every block of that diameter, uniformly in its location.

Outside the intervals \([e,e+\deg V]\) every active shift contributes its genuine positive-index moment with the same partial multiplier on that piece.
The fixed-\(k\) Bessel test gives a bound on the number of nonzero integer coefficients there.
On the exceptional intervals, including their endpoints, \(VB=(UA)(PB)\) is a bounded weighted sum of translates of the old polynomial.
Each interval and each translated copy has length \(O(s+1)\), covered by boundedly many old intervals wherever it falls.
There are at most \(K\) exceptional intervals, and the remaining portions of any length-\(S\) interval require only boundedly many Gram blocks.
Thus the induction recovers the absolute coefficient bound on all intervals at scale \(S\).

At scale \(h\) the degree bound covers the nonzero polynomial \(PB\) by a bounded number of these intervals.
Each of the fixed number \(b\) of steps multiplies its endpoint constant by \(m\), and its multiplier measure by \(M(A)\ge m\) with \(M(A)\to m\).
The initial multipliers have constant and measure one, so \(PB\) has constant \(m^b\) and measure strictly above \(m^b\) tending to that value, contrary to the sparse endpoint gap.
All subsequence constants may depend on the assumed counterexample sequence, which is sufficient to rule out a vanishing infimum for fixed \(K,m_{\max}\).
On fixed branch \(j\), one can take \(m_{\max}=j\), and the derivative bound gives \(q\le j/c(K,j)+2\).
