## Local factors and degrees of rational divisors

At 2026-11-17T12:50 UTC an agent for Yin independently checked the [fixed C345v237 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v237.md#decision-odd-prime-reciprocal-degree-submitted).
The reviewer had previously investigated a related example in residue characteristic two.
The reviewer did not develop the characteristic-three or larger odd-prime arguments under review and received their fixed text and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or earlier findings on this submission.
The two local tests, their precise divisibility conclusions and the asserted measure bounds hold.

After dividing the parent by its leading \(p\)-adic unit, Hensel lifting separates the distinct monic residual powers.
Any nontrivial polynomial splitting of a lift over \(\mathbb Q_p\) has monic integral factors, since roots of a monic integral polynomial have nonnegative valuation.
Their positive-degree reductions would both be divisible by the same residual irreducible \(g_i\).
In the unramified extension containing its Teichmüller root \(\xi_i\), both factors would then take values in \(p\mathcal O\).
Every complementary lifted factor is a unit there, so \(v_p(F(\xi_i))=1\) excludes such a splitting.
In the single-residue argument from v180 one can equivalently translate a packet to an Eisenstein polynomial after passing to the unramified splitting field.
Here, retaining the entire Frobenius orbit over \(\mathbb Q_p\) gives degree \(e_i\deg g_i\), including \(e_i=1\).
When the valuation test holds at every orbit, rational divisor degrees are sums of the local degrees because all monic lifted factors are distinct irreducibles.

At \(p=3\) the assumed divisibilities remove the inner terms of the parent in the residue field.
The prime-to-three part of the degree-\(2n\) binomial has simple roots of exact orders \(2^{v+2}t\) for all \(t\mid h\), each with multiplicity \(3^u\) in the full reduction.
Indeed the \(2^{v+1}h\)-th power must be \(-1\), forcing exactly this two-part and allowing precisely the odd parts dividing \(h\).
At the lifted root, \(\xi^n\) is a fourth root of unity of exact order four, so its sum with its inverse is exactly zero.
If \(9\mid c\), the remaining value divided by three is nonzero in residue whenever \(y=\bar\xi^l\) lacks order four.
Its two-part under \(v_2(l)\ne v\) already precludes that order.
When \(v_2(l)<v\), the two-part is at least eight.
Membership \(y+y^{-1}\in\mathbb F_3\) would make the third power map preserve the two roots \(y,y^{-1}\) of their quadratic, so \(y^3=y^{\pm1}\), impossible for that two-part.
Thus cancellation with any scalar \(c/3\bmod3\) is impossible, since \(b/3\) is a unit.
In each case one obtains valuation one throughout.

The order of three modulo \(2^{v+2}t\) is a multiple of its order modulo \(2^{v+2}\).
At \(v=0\) the latter is two; for \(v\ge1\) it is \(2^v\) by \(v_2(3^{2^j}-1)=j+2\) for \(j\ge1\).
In particular every local degree is at least \(3^u2^v=n/h\) and is divisible by the claimed integer.
When \(h=1\), the \(\varphi(2^{v+2})\) simple classes before introducing multiplicity \(3^u\) therefore give two full orbits of length \(2^v\) if \(v\ge1\), and one orbit of length two if \(v=0\).
This recovers the exact numbers and degrees of local factors.

For \(p\ge5\), reducing \(P/a\) with \(c/a\equiv1\) gives the \(p^u\)-th power of \(X^{2\cdot3^v h}+X^{3^v h}+1\).
Its distinct roots have order \(3^{v+1}t,\ t\mid h\), because raising them to the \(3^v h\)-th power gives primitive cube roots.
Each lift therefore satisfies \(\xi^n+\xi^{-n}=-1\) exactly, giving the term \(c-a\) with the stated sign.
The alternative \(p^2\mid b,\ v_p(c-a)=1\) forces valuation one without a condition on \(l\).
Otherwise \(v_p(b)=1\) and \(v_3(l)\le v-w\) force the order of \(y=\bar\xi^l\) to have three-part at least \(3^{w+1}\).
When \(p\equiv1\bmod3\), the three-part in \(p-1\) is \(3^w\); when \(p\equiv-1\bmod3\), \(v_3(p^2-1)=v_3(p+1)\).
Thus neither \(p-1\) nor \(p+1\) admits the order of \(y\).
Frobenius cannot permute \(y,y^{-1}\), so the residue of the divided trace term lies outside the prime field and cannot cancel \((c-a)/p\).
If \(v<w\) the offset inequality is empty, consistently leaving the second coefficient alternative available.

All the orbit sizes are divisible by \(\operatorname{ord}_{3^{v+1}}(p)\).
Writing \(f=\operatorname{ord}_3(p)\), an exponent that fixes \(p\bmod3\) must be divisible by \(f\).
Expanding successive cubes and taking powers prime to three gives \(v_3((p^f)^j-1)=w+v_3(j)\).
This yields the asserted \(f3^{\max(0,v+1-w)}\), including \(v=0\), and its lower bound \(f3^{1-w}3^v\).
For \(p\) primitive modulo nine, \(f=2,\ w=1\), so at \(h=1\) the required local degree equals the full \(2n\).
The first offset condition becomes \(v_3(l)<v\) as asserted.

The v186 width bound applies at width \(2n\) with effective support at most five, giving \(m\gg d/(2n)\) with an absolute constant for each nontorsion irreducible unit factor.
Integral irreducible factors with a nonunit endpoint and nontrivial content already give the absolute lower bound \(\log2\).
The inequalities \(h\ge1,\ f3^{1-w}\le2\) then suffice for every positive-measure integral divisor by multiplicativity.
At the stated examples \(P(1)=-4\) and \(-2\), respectively, and the leading coefficients are positive.
For \(l=1,\ v\ge1\), the respective offset inequalities do hold at \(p=3\) and \(p=5\) with \(h=1\).
The review checked the indicated uses of the earlier local and width results without undertaking another proof audit of their general antecedents.
