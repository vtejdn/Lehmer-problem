## Explicit prefix bound

The neutral trace gives the exact lift \(F_s(z)(1-z^r)/(1-z)\): the identities for \(z^{s+1}t^2E_{s-1}(t)\) and \(z^m B_m(t)\) give the two factors separately.
On the grid \(B_m(b_j)=B_{n-1}(b_j)E_{s-1}(b_j)\) follows from \(r=p-2s\) and the sine formula, while \(B_{n-1}(b_j)/B_n'(b_j)=(4-b_j^2)/p\).
Thus the neutral residues are nonnegative, so a strict negative block rules out \(H=0\).
For \(d=\deg H\), the perturbation changes coefficient \(n+1-d\) by \(\operatorname{lc}(H)\ne0\) and leaves every earlier coefficient alone; this index is strictly below \(r\) because \(n+1=(r+1)/2+s<r\).
The longest matching index is therefore \(L=n-d\le n\), and it suffices to treat this index.

Since \(B_n\) is monic with precisely the \(n\) distinct grid roots, the partial fractions of \(H/B_n\) have residues \(\rho_j-\rho^0_j\).
Expansion at infinity makes the first nonzero moment the one of index \(L-1\), with value \(\operatorname{lc}(H)\).
Thus a polynomial of degree at most \(L-3\) times \(b-a\) annihilates the difference, including for the constant test when \(L\ge3\).
The separator lies strictly between \(b_{c+1}\) and \(b_c\), so every \(X_j>0\), while \(a>-2\) and \(\rho^0_j\ge0\) give \(G_j\le f(b_j)\) even at neutral zeros.
For \(D=B_{n-1}\), \(D(b_j)^2=2+b_j\); the values \(B_n(2)=p\) and \(B_n(-2)=(-1)^n\) give \(\prod_j|D(b_j)|=1\) and \(\prod_j(4-b_j^2)=p\).
The absolute product of the \(Q(b_j)\) is at least one because it is the magnitude of a nonzero integer resultant, with no restriction on the orders of the grid points.
Also \(\prod_j|a-b_j|=|B_n(a)|\ge1\), since the sine numerator at the angular midpoint has absolute value one.
These facts in \(X_j=(b_j-a)(4-b_j^2)Q(b_j)/D(b_j)\) prove \(\mathbb E\log X\ge0\).
For \(L\ge3\), \((t-a)T_{L-2}(t)\) is monic of degree \(L-1\), even at \(L=3\) where \(T_1(t)=t\).
The moment of this polynomial against \(\rho-\rho^0\) is the nonzero integer above, and \(|T_{L-2}|\le2\) therefore implies \(\mathbb E|X-G|\ge p/(2n)>1\).

I checked the inverse construction by writing \(2+t=|1+z|^2\), \(t^2=|1+z^2|^2\) and \((4-t^2)E_{s-1}^2=|1-z^{2s}|^2\) on the unit circle.
Each signed binomial has magnitude at most two, so \(0\le f\le64\).
For \(x=\sigma z^k\), the squared modulus of the truncated geometric sum has conjugate frequencies in \(k\mathbb Z\) of magnitude at most \((N-1)k\).
Because its coefficients are real and symmetric, it is a real polynomial in \(t\) of at most that degree.
Its numerator and denominator have no unit-circle zero for \(0<u=\lambda^N<1\) and \(0<\lambda<1\).
Writing \(F_k=|1-x|^2\) and \(\epsilon=(1-\lambda)^2/\lambda\), the exact inverse is \((F_k+\epsilon)(1+u)^2/|1-ux^N|^2\).
This lies between \(F_k+\epsilon\) and \(q(F_k+\epsilon)\), with \(q=((1+u)/(1-u))^2\).
Using \(u<3\cdot10^{-9}\) and \(\epsilon<2\cdot10^{-12}\) gives \(q-1<2\cdot10^{-8}\) and \(4(q-1)+q\epsilon<10^{-7}\).
Telescoping the three products costs at most \(3\cdot25\cdot10^{-7}<10^{-4}\), since every individual factor in either product is less than five.
Thus \(fw\le1\) and \(1/w<65\), including points where one or more neutral factors vanish.
The degree of the whole test is at most \((N-1)(2s+3)\).

For \(g=\gcd(k,p)\), the map \(z\mapsto z^k\) has kernel size \(g\) on the \(p\)-th roots and image the entire group of order \(p/g\).
The full denominator product is consequently \(|1-(\lambda\sigma)^{p/g}|^g\le2^g\), also when the grid has imprimitive roots or \(k\) and \(p\) are not coprime.
Removing \(z=1\) divides by \(|1-\lambda\sigma|\ge1-\lambda\); the remaining log sum is twice the upper-half sum, so its mean on the \(n\) nodes uses the denominator \(2n=p-1\).
With \(g\le k\), summing the three resulting bounds and using \(|1-u x^N|\ge1-u\) gives precisely the stated upper bound for \(-\mathbb E\log w\).
Under the contradiction hypothesis \(L\ge (N-1)(2s+3)+3\), the degree of \(w\) is admissible and \(p-1=2n\ge2L\).
The three terms in that log estimate are bounded respectively by \(3(10^{-6})/(1-10^{-6})<3.00001\cdot10^{-6}\), \(12u/(1-u)<4\cdot10^{-8}\) and \((0.7+3\cdot14/7)/(N-1)<3.4\cdot10^{-7}\).
Their sum is strictly below \(4\cdot10^{-6}\).

Finally \(Y=Xw>0\), \(\mathbb E Y=\mathbb E Gw\le1\), and \(\mathbb E\log X\ge0\) together give \(\mathbb E(Y-1-\log Y)\le-\mathbb E\log w\).
Integrating \((y-1)/y\) from one to \(y\) gives \(y-1-\log y\ge(y-1)^2/(2\max(1,y))\), which suffices for the stated Cauchy–Schwarz bound \(\mathbb E|Y-1|<0.004\).
Writing \(X-f=(Y-1)/w+(1/w-f)\) yields \(\mathbb E|X-f|<0.2601\).
The constant moment test and \(G\le f\) give \(\mathbb E|f-G|=\mathbb E(f-X)\le\mathbb E|f-X|\), so the triangle inequality yields \(\mathbb E|X-G|<0.522\), contradicting its integer lower bound.

I found no gap: every matching index at a strict cut satisfies \(L<(2s+3)(20\,000\,000-1)+3\), without an endpoint test.
Only the indicated finite identities of the earlier claim enter this proof; its separate asymptotic approximation is unnecessary here.
The bound concerns the exceptional parents and integral trace perturbations in the stated degree range; it gives neither a sharp matching length nor a bound on Mahler measure.
