## The fixed-input limit and the primitive witnesses

On 2026-11-18T10:14 a separately assigned agent for Yin checked the whole [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement) and found its limit and both existence assertions proved by the submitted argument.
The reviewer had no role in developing the proof and received its fixed text and dependency locators without development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings about this claim.
The earlier review consulted concerns the already adopted fixed-base common-modulus theorem.

Fix a valuation over \(\ell\ne p\).
Removing the largest common power of \(X-\epsilon\) from the two rows removes only copies of \(x^p-\epsilon\) from the cyclotomic part of the multiple, since \(P\) and \(R\) have no torsion zeros.
At least one row evaluation survives, and two terms cannot vanish on all \(p-1\) primitive conjugates since their coefficient polynomial of degree at most \(p-1\) would be divisible by \(\Phi_p\).
The remaining cyclotomic factors have constant valuation on these nodes by the unit quotients of conjugate root differences in the [local estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v356.md#local).
After dividing the row evaluations by a coefficient of minimum valuation, evaluations at any two distinct nodes cannot both vanish modulo the valuation ideal.
Indeed the two residues of the exponents differ modulo \(p\), and the reduced ratio of the two nodes has order \(p\).
This checks the needed local estimate directly, including the case of a single surviving coefficient.

The interpolation matrix on the \(p-1\) primitive nodes has unit determinant at \(\ell\), so the valuation of the denominator clearing \(1/P(\epsilon\zeta)\) equals the largest evaluation valuation.
The inverse pair giving this maximum has distinct indices because \(p\) is odd.
Applying the local estimate there and summing the deficits shows that the valuation of the primitive norm of \(R\) is at least \((p-1)v_\ell(B_\epsilon)-v_\ell(N_\epsilon)\).
The additional value \(R(\epsilon)\) is a nonzero integer.
In the trace order \(A=\mathbb Z[\zeta+\zeta^{-1}]\), the decomposition \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]=A\oplus\zeta A\) gives \(N_\epsilon=H_\epsilon^2\) and the same exponent \(B_\epsilon\) on each summand of the quotient.
Every common integer root modulo \(g\) gives a quotient onto \(\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\), so \(G_\epsilon\mid B_\epsilon\mid H_\epsilon\).

The [selected common-modulus decision](../../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#decision-common-root-modulus-reviewed) and its [independent review](Yin_R_G1114_v80.md#common-root-modulus-review) cover all sufficiently large prime orders for each fixed reciprocal unit.
Both \(P(X)\) and \(P(-X)\) meet the hypotheses because their even degree, irreducibility, reciprocal root pairing and measure agree.
I checked Proposition 2, inequality (2.8), and the conventions on pp. 2–3 and 7 of the [Corvaja–Zannier preprint](../literature/CZ05/readme.md#arxiv-v2).
For independent conjugate powers in the fixed splitting field, the finite-place gcd sum is bounded by the full nonnegative sum after changing sign, and the distinct pairs have heights linear in \(p\).
The finite exceptional set for each positive tolerance gives the required \(o_P(p)\) overlap.
Dependent conjugates outside the inverse pairs differ up to inversion by a nontrivial torsion twist, whose \(p\)-th power differs from one for all sufficiently large primes.
The common-modulus decision supplies the resulting rates, including the fixed-input primitive norm estimate.
The external proposition is accepted at the level of its statement and application.
As \(P(\epsilon)\ne0\), reduction of \(\Phi_p\) excludes \(p\) from the resultant eventually.
Thus each \(\log D_\epsilon/(p-1)^2\) tends to \(m(P)/2\).
Multiplicativity of resultants combines the two divisibilities even at primes common to both integers.
The single root-product bound for \(x^{2p}-1\), with the leading coefficient raised to \(2p\), contributes at most \((\deg R)\log2+2p\,m(R)\).
Since \(\deg R=o(p^2)\) uniformly in the prescribed pair, this gives the claimed lower rate.

In the coefficient count the \(d\) real coordinates include two for each complex pair, and a box of side at most \(1/2\) gives complex differences of modulus at most \(1/\sqrt 2\).
A nonzero algebraic integer cannot have all its conjugates this small.
The count of distinct values therefore has the stated exponent \((pb+r)m(P)\), with each complex modulus counted twice.
The displayed choice of \(H\) gives a strict collision, and its upper bound by twice the unrounded exponential yields (3) by the coefficient sum.
After division by \(p\), the leading coefficient in (3) is \((b+r/p)/(2b+2-d)\to1/2\) uniformly in \(r\).

For all large \(p\), the \(d\) conjugate \(p\)-th powers are distinct, so their monic minimal polynomial \(Q_p\) has logarithmic measure \(p\,m(P)>0\).
Any row vanishing at \(\alpha^p\) is divisible by \(Q_p\) over the rationals, with integral quotient for an integral row by monicity.
The substituted factor \(Q_p(x^p)\) has the same measure as \(Q_p\).
Thus the strict bound \(m(F_0)<p\,m(P)\) excludes a single occupied row and excludes \(Q_p\) from the common row gcd.
Division by the primitive integral gcd preserves integrality by Gauss's lemma and preserves \(P\) since the divisor is nonzero at \(\alpha\).
The shift to the prescribed residues can multiply a wrapped row by \(x^p\), which is a unit over the indicated Laurent ring.
The shift increases the degree by at most \(p-1\), and discarding the other factors gives \(m(R)\le m(F_0)\).
At each fixed cap the coefficient bounds for \(R\) in terms of its degree and one witness's measure give finitely many possible \(R\) at or below that measure, hence attainment.

Taking the prime limit separately for each \(P\) identifies the infimum of these limiting rates with half the infimum of the positive measures of reciprocal unit minimal polynomials.
Factoring off monomials and using additivity, the endpoint bound \(\log2\) for nonunit leading or constant coefficient and Smyth's plastic bound for nonreciprocal unit factors give the claimed equivalence with the unrestricted gap.
The existence of a positive infimum over the reciprocal inputs remains open.
