## Relative norms and archimedean block sums

For a unit \(\alpha\), every conjugate of \(N_{K/E}\alpha\) is again a unit.
Each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(N_{K/E}\alpha)\) has exactly \([E:\mathbb Q(N_{K/E}\alpha)]\) extensions to \(E\).
Multiplying the images of \(\alpha\) over each extension block and using \(\max(0,\sum x_i)\le\sum\max(0,x_i)\) gives the stated multiplicity and upper bound for logarithmic Mahler measure.
All block sums vanish precisely when all archimedean conjugates of the norm have modulus one.
Kronecker's theorem then makes the norm torsion, while a nonzero sum gives a nontorsion element of degree at most \([E:\mathbb Q]<[K:\mathbb Q]\).
For \(E=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\) with \(k>0\), the values above a given embedding of \(E\) have equal modulus, giving equality in the measure bound and the power-norm formula at the cited scope.

## Proper subfields and oriented products for genuine Salem numbers

Complex conjugation fixes the real pair and flips every other pair.
Transitivity on pairs supplies a conjugate that omits each designated pair, and multiplying two such elements gives their double flip.
For even \(r\) the sum of the \(r\) flip patterns also supplies the all-ones vector and hence every single flip.
These are precisely the kernel inclusions needed in the argument.

A block of at least two individual roots containing \(\alpha\) but omitting its inverse would contain a root of another pair.
For \(r\ge3\), flipping the pair of \(\alpha\) together with a third pair fixes that other root, so the image block intersects the initial block and must coincide with it.
For \(r=2\) the single flip of the pair of \(\alpha\) does the same.
Both force \(\alpha^{-1}\) into the block.
For \(E\subsetneq K\) the block of embeddings above the inclusion of \(E\) has size \([K:E]>1\).
The conjugation over \(E\) taking \(\alpha\) to its inverse maps \(K\) onto \(K\) because \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^{-1})=K\).
Its fixed field is \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) by the nontriviality of the involution and the quadratic trace equation.
Hence every such \(E\) lies in the totally real trace field and norm transitivity gives \(N_{K/E}\alpha=1\).

For a proper block of \(m\) inverse pairs, its \(r/m\) translated supports partition the pair set.
At least one unused coordinate permits all \(2^m\) orientations on each support even when only even flips are available.
Independence modulo torsion distinguishes all these exponent vectors.
The sole support containing the real pair supplies \(2^{m-1}\) images of modulus \(\alpha\), and all remaining images have modulus at most one.
This gives degree \((r/m)2^m\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(2^{m-1}\log\alpha\), equivalently absolute logarithmic height \(m\log\alpha/(2r)\).
For \(m=2\) the degree equals \(2r\), and it exceeds \(2r\) for \(m>2\).

## The degree-eight example and its four independent square classes

For \(t_1=1+\sqrt{-2+\sqrt5}\), the field \(\mathbb Q(t_1)\) contains \(\sqrt5\), and \(-2+\sqrt5\) has norm \(-1\) in the real quadratic field.
It cannot be a square there, so \(t_1\) has degree four.
I checked the two real traces lie strictly inside \((-2,2)\) and that the other traces form one nonreal pair.
The map \(t\mapsto(t-1)^2+2\) has two fibers of size two over \(\pm\sqrt5\), which the trace group permutes.

I expanded \(T=t^4-4t^3+10t^2-12t+4\) and checked the four residues \(4,5,26,27\) modulo 29.
In that order the derivatives modulo 29 are \(16,27,2,13\).
Thus each root lifts in its own residue class in \(\mathbb Q_{29}\), and the four lifts realize an embedding of the whole splitting field \(F\).
Exactly the fourth class is \(-2\), and none is \(2\).
The product \(\prod_i(t_i+2)=T(-2)=116\) has valuation one, while the three factors away from the fourth class are units.
All \(t_i-2\) are units, so the valuation parity vector for the \(u_i=t_i^2-4\) has a single nonzero coordinate.
Trace transitivity transports this valuation to give each of the four coordinate vectors.
Applying these four valuations to a putative square product forces every exponent to be even.

The independent square classes give the full group of \(2^4\) flips over \(F\).
In the inductive argument the existing radical monomials have distinct characters, so an element whose square is in \(F^\times\) belongs to one such eigenspace.
Consequently an independent next radical doubles the degree.
The full lift splitting field contains \(F\) via the trace map, and automorphisms of \(F\) extend to it.
Its action is transitive on the eight lifts by trace transitivity and the single flips.
Thus the reciprocal monic polynomial \(z^4T(z+z^{-1})\) is irreducible, and its two exterior, two interior and four unit-circle roots are all conjugates of a nontorsion unit.
The individual-root block argument now puts every proper subfield in the fixed field of the trace involution, giving norm one in every case.

Applying a single flip to a torsion relation and taking the quotient forces the exponent on that pair to vanish.
This proves independence modulo torsion and distinguishes the eight images of every positive power.
For a monomial supported on \(m\) pairs, the translated supports cover all four pairs, giving at least \(4/m\) distinct supports and \(2^m\) sign choices per support.
The resulting degree lower bound is at least eight for every \(1\le m\le4\).
The specified two-pair support has just two translates and four sign choices per translate.
The block of real traces contributes only unit-modulus products.
In the nonreal block the conjugate traces have equal exterior modulus \(\lambda\), giving precisely one product of modulus \(\lambda^2\), one of modulus \(\lambda^{-2}\), and two of modulus one.
The oriented product and \(\alpha\) consequently have degree eight, Mahler measure \(\lambda^2\), and absolute logarithmic height \(\log(\lambda)/4\).
Writing an exterior lift as \(\lambda e^{i\theta}\) gives \(|\Im t|=|(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})\sin\theta|>2\) for a nonreal trace of this example, hence \(\lambda>1+\sqrt2\).
