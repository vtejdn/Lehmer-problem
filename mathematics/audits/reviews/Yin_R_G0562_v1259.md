## Uniqueness, precision and the two residue obstructions

At 2026-11-25T14:49 UTC, an agent outside proof development independently checked the statement, all four argument sections and the cited input interfaces of [C0901 v181](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and scope without its development dialogue, author explanations or earlier conclusions about this version.
The stated uniqueness and finite reconstruction hold, as do the binary candidate bound and the one-prime comparison.
The specified cubic residue is excluded modulo four, and the family at sufficiently large \(q\equiv4\pmod8\) is excluded modulo eight.

For a genuine realization, \(1<T<p/q\) gives \(Y>0\), and Bézout expresses \(\beta\) in \(\mathbb Q(T)\).
All conjugate absolute values of \(\beta\) are determined by those of \(T=\beta^u\), so the degree is \(d\) and the logarithmic Mahler measure is \(\log\beta\).
Voutier's stated Corollary 2 and \(u\log\beta=\log T<1/q\) give \(u<(\log(3d))^3/(2q)\).
The additive index \(2q+1\) identifies the two power orders modulo two.
In the common quotient the graph equation gives \(\beta^N=1\) for the indicated choice of \(N=v\mp u\).
Since \(\gcd(u,N)=1\), \(T\) and \(\beta\) have the same order \(a\) there, \(a\mid N\), and \(k=N/a\) is prime to \(u\).
Writing \(\beta^a=1+2h\) shows \(\beta^{2a}-1=4(h+h^2)\) in the degree-\(d\) integral order.
Its nonzero norm is a multiple of \(4^d\), whereas the single expanding conjugate gives the strict upper bound \(2^d\beta^{2a}\).
Together with \(N\log\beta\le\log(YT)<d\log(2q+1)\), this gives \(k<2\log_2(2q+1)\).

In the monic \(2\)-adic quotient both \(z\) and \(p-qz\) are units by reduction modulo two, and substituting the graph relation gives \(W=\beta^N\) for a genuine lift.
For either parity choice direct subtraction gives \(W=1+cE\) with \(\bar E=z+z^{-1}\).
Multiplying \(\bar E\) by \(z\) and \(\bar E(1+\bar E)\) by \(z^2\) gives respectively \(z^2+1\) and \((z^2+1)(z^2+z+1)\).
Their degrees are below \(d\ge6\), so both classes survive even if the binary modulus has repeated factors.
The free power basis makes coefficientwise divisibility exact.
For \(e=1\), \((W^2-1)/4\) reduces to \(\bar E(1+\bar E)\).
At valuation \(s\ge2\), squaring multiplies the difference from one by twice a unit, and an odd power multiplies it by a geometric sum congruent to one modulo two.
This proves the order \(2^{b-e}\) for every \(b\ge e\), including \(b=e\).
The denominator of \(W\) cancels in the actual dyadic power order, so \(z^{ak}=W^u\) is necessary in the presented finite ring.

Elimination for two surviving primitive pairs gives a determinant divisible by that exact order and of absolute value strictly below \(KU\).
With \(2^{b-e}>KU\) the determinant vanishes, and positivity and primitivity identify the pairs.
Modulo two, the fixed order \(a\) and the ranges of \(u,k\) give at most \(KU\) possible pairs.
For a fixed pair, the logarithm of the graph coordinate vanishes at zero and has strictly increasing derivative \(L(1-s^2)/(1-L^2s^2)\) in \(x=\log T\), where \(s=\tanh(x/2)\) and \(0<s<1/L\).
Its secant slope from zero is therefore strictly increasing, which identifies at most one exterior root and one monic minimum across all grids.
The degree-dependent bound on \(v+u\) supplies the early rejection cutoff for \(a\), and factoring the indicated integer polynomial and imposing the exact pole, signature, degree and residue gives the finite decision.
On feasible heights \(q<(\log(3d))^3/2\), the admissible choice \(b=e+1+\lfloor\log_2(KU)\rfloor\) is \(O(\log\log(9d))\).
This is a precision for each coefficient.
With \(q,d\) supplied and reciprocity used, a direct encoding of the \(D\) variable coefficients occupies \(Db\) bits.
For any odd prime \(\ell\), the one-prime approximation applies with \(B=2^b\) to every compatible integral pole representative, and its exact denominators \(\ell^j\) distinguish infinitely many rational lifts.

For the cubic at \(q=2\), the four trace evaluations are \(-25/3,2/3,-1/3,25/216\), and the four rational-root candidates all give nonzero evaluations.
The strictly negative quadratic discriminant at a negative trace embedding proves irreducibility of the reciprocal lift.
The two binary factors have orders \(3,5\), respectively.
Independent monic reduction modulo four gave \(z^{15}\) the ascending coordinates \((3,0,2,2,0,2)\).
Modulo two the reciprocal relation gives \(z+z^{-1}=z^2+z^3+z^5\), linearly independent from one.
In the necessary identity modulo four the two independent coordinates hence force \(k\) and \(u\) even.

For the large-height family, each principal-unit group for \(r\mid q\) remains cyclic with the specified generator at the larger exponent \((J+3)v_r(q)\), since \(v_2(q)\ge2\).
The resulting exponent \(D\) still has residue two modulo \(r\), and the congruences at primes dividing \(p\) and the additional rotation conditions remain compatible by the Chinese remainder theorem.
The factors \(p^l-q^l,p^m-q^m\) give the specified pole modulo \(q^{J+3}\) and \(p^{J+1}\) separately.
Their product modulus contains \(2^{e+1}M^{J+1}\), since \(2^{e+1}\mid q^2\).
The free coefficients have evaluation increments of gcd \(M^{J+1}\), so multiplying a Bézout correction by \(2^{e+1}\) gives the required integral representative with the same residue and zero prefix.
The one-prime construction therefore imposes this residue on its strict capacity and signature examples for every prescribed odd denominator prime.

The binary product \((Z^l-1)(Z^m-1)\) has roots of odd orders generating \(\operatorname{lcm}(l,m)\), and has maximum multiplicity two, attained at one.
Thus its exact order is \(a=2\operatorname{lcm}(l,m)\).
The integral factors \((Z-1)^2\) and \(1+Z+\cdots+Z^{l-1}\) of the product each give a quotient map over \(\mathbb Z/2^{e+1}\).
In the first quotient \(z=1+t\), the coefficient of \(t\) is free, and \(z^2-1=2t\) gives \(W=1\).
It follows that \(2^{e+1}\mid ak\), forcing even \(k\).
In the second quotient \(l\mid a\) gives \(z^{ak}=1\), and \(z\bar E=z^2+1\) survives reduction modulo two by degree.
Here \(W^u=1\) forces even \(u\).
These two consequences contradict primitivity, in particular modulo eight when \(q\equiv4\pmod8\).
