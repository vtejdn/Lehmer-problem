## Finding

Both conclusions hold.
For either sign and every sufficiently large delay, one of the 62 signed tests gives the claimed positive logarithmic Mahler measure certificate.
On the stated height-one subsequence, the absolute resultant at the exact degree is uniformly \(o(2^d)\) even when the phase is selected after the polynomial.

**Removal of cyclotomic factors.**
The cubic \(f\) has one real root \(\theta>1\), and its other two roots have modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}\).
On the circle \(|g|=|f|>0\), so Rouché's theorem for \(z^Nf-t\sigma g\), \(0<t<1\), counts \(N+2\) roots inside and one outside.
At \(t=1\) two strictly exterior roots counted with multiplicity would persist for nearby \(t<1\).
For fixed \(1<s<\theta<t'\), the signs of \(f(s)\) and \(f(t')\) dominate the bounded perturbations for large \(N\), giving one simple real exterior root converging to \(\theta\).
The reciprocal of \(D\) is \(-\sigma D\), and \(D\) is monic with unit constant term.
Consequently the exterior root and its inverse belong to the same irreducible factor: without the inverse the product of the moduli of that factor's roots would exceed one.
Kronecker's theorem applies to each remaining factor.

For torsion order \(m\), the exponents \(2,m/2+2,m/2+1\) are coprime to \(m\) in the odd, twice-odd and four-divisible cases, respectively.
For the resulting conjugate \(\xi\eta^j\), the two equations \(\eta^N=\sigma G(\eta)\) and \((\xi\eta^j)^N=\sigma G(\xi\eta^j)\) give the stated multiplier \(\xi^N\sigma^{j-1}\).
The cleared terms \(g(\xi z^j)f(z)^j\) and \(f(\xi z^j)g(z)^j\) have degree at most \(6j\).
From \(G(z)=-1+z+O(z^2)\), the rational functions have different linear terms for \(j=2\); for \(\xi=-1,j=1\), their constant terms differ at odd \(N\) and their linear terms differ at even \(N\).
Only parity and sign vary in these three nonzero polynomials, so the union of their possible torsion roots is finite independently of the delay.
Differentiating \(z^N-\sigma G(z)\) at a repeated torsion root gives \(N=\eta G'(\eta)/G(\eta)\); neither \(f\) nor \(g\) vanishes there, and this finite set of values excludes repetitions for all large \(N\).
At fixed \(N,\sigma\), the sum of the three degrees is at most \(12+12+6=30\).
It follows that \(0\le h=\deg H\le30\), so \(N-1=d+h-4\) lies in the claimed inclusive interval of 31 exponents.

**The signed test.**
At the nodes \(z^{N-1}=-\sigma\) the value is \(-\sigma(zf+g)\).
The trace equation for \(zf+g\) gives one reciprocal positive pair with \(\lambda>2\) because \(u_+>2+1/2\), and a unit pair with trace \(u_-\).
If that pair were torsion, every conjugate of its trace would be a sum of a root of unity and its inverse and would belong to \([-2,2]\), contrary to the conjugate \(u_+\).
The node product therefore never vanishes.
The simple torsion roots removed with \(H\) range over finitely many roots of unity, so their powers take finitely many values and the product removed is uniformly bounded above and away from zero.
The resulting monic resultant has the four factors displayed in the proof, with exponential contribution \(\lambda^n\) from the exterior root.
The [logarithmic-form specialization](../literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle) applies at \(p=2n\) to a fixed algebraic nontorsion \(\gamma\), giving \(|\gamma^{2n}-1|\gg n^{-C}\).
Each signed factor \(|\gamma^n+\sigma|\) is at least half this value, since the complementary factor is at most two.
This proves the \(O(\log n)\) logarithmic error for both signs; the inverse root contributes only \(O(1)\).
Subtracting \(d\log2\) and dividing by \(n\) gives the certificate since \(d=n+O(1)\).
Testing both signs at all 31 exponents needs only the degree and the polynomial itself.
The external logarithmic-form theorem is accepted at the statement and applicability level; its original proof was not independently reviewed.

**The exact-degree obstruction.**
The [height-one subsequence](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v23.md#irreducible) takes \(N\) divisible by all possible torsion orders, so its torsion roots satisfy \(f-g=(z-1)(2z^2+3z+2)=0\).
The quadratic would give the nonintegral rational trace \(-3/2\), and the root 1 is simple since \(D'(1)=7-N\).
Division by \(z-1\) gives the stated height-one quotient of degree \(d=N+2\).
At \(y^d=w\), multiplication by \(y^2\) thus gives exactly \(y^2(y-1)S(y)=wf(y)-y^2g(y)\).
To check the coefficient calculation independently, I used the matrix \(C\) of multiplication by \(z\) modulo this monic quintic in the basis \(1,z,\ldots,z^4\).
It has ones on the subdiagonal and last column \((w,w,1,-w,-1)^\mathsf{T}\).
Computing \(\det(XI-C^6)\) over \(\mathbb Z[w]\) reproduced all six displayed coefficients of \(J_w\); their coefficient absolute sums as polynomials in \(w\) are \(1,13,20,20,13,1\).
Their squares sum to \(1140\), so Jensen and Parseval give \(M(Q_w)^{12}=M(J_w)^2\le1140<2^{12}\) uniformly on the circle.

Multiplying the node identity over \(y^d=w\) contributes modulus one from \(\prod y^2\), \(|w-1|\) from \(\prod(y-1)\), and \(|A(w)|\) from \(\prod S(y)\).
Interchanging the root products of the two monic polynomials on the other side gives \(\prod_r|w-r^d|\le32M(Q_w)^d\), including at \(w=1\).
The coefficient bound from circle integration applies to \((w-1)A(w)\); summing coefficients up to degree \(k\) gives minus the \(k\)-th coefficient of \(A\) for \(0\le k\le d\).
The two factors of \(d+1\) therefore suffice to bound \(\sup |A|\), without division near \(w=1\).
Since \(1140^{1/12}<2\), this gives \(o(2^d)\) uniformly in the phase and hence defeats the stated factorwise test also for a varying positive number of phases.
This comparison concerns the factorwise cost on that subsequence and gives no impossibility for other auxiliary costs or for other exponents.
