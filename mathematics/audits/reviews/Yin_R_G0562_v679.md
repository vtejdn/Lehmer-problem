# Independent review of the simultaneous Frobenius cycle construction

<a id="finding"></a>
## Good reduction and the nontorsion quotient

On November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the [primitive section and its cycle bounds](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v545.md#statement).
I received the fixed claim and the requested review scope without participating in the proof development.
The construction proves all the stated conclusions, with the logarithmic size estimate applying to the displayed least common multiple.

At a common zero of the two seed polynomials, substitution gives \(t^{s-1}=-t\) and \(t^s=t+1=-t^2\), so \(t^2+t+1=0\) and \(t^3=1\).
In odd characteristic, including three, cubing \(t^s=-t^2\) gives the contradiction \(1=-1\), as it does in characteristic zero.
In characteristic two, \(1\) is not a zero of \(T^2+T+1\), so \(t\) has order three and \(t^s=t^2\) would force the excluded residue class of \(s\).
Over the complex numbers the coprime coefficients make the linear polynomial in \(Z\) primitive and irreducible over the Laurent polynomial ring in \(T\), and inverting \(Z\) preserves irreducibility.
Its four extreme exponents give a rectangle of area \(s\), excluding association to a torsion binomial.
The [Beukers–Smyth theorem](../literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) therefore bounds its torsion points by \(22s\).
Since \(b-as^3=1\), the parameter is recovered as \(x=x^b/(x^a)^{s^3}\), so all distinct complex roots of any cyclotomic factor count separately against this bound.

Write \(A\) for the specified least common multiple.
Taking \(a\) to be any positive multiple of \(A\) gives \(a\) even, \(b\) odd and \(F(-1)=0\).
The leading term of \(F\) is \(-X^{b+as}\), so division by \(-(X+1)\) gives a monic \(H\) of the claimed degree.
The prime divisors prescribed in \(A\) eliminate all derivative terms from the inner substitutions, and \(b\equiv1\pmod p\).
As \(F(0)=-1\), a repeated root would yield a common seed zero, already excluded at every selected prime.
At each \(j\le D\), the two power substitutions on \(\mathbb F_{p^j}^{\times}\) give \(1\) and \(x\), respectively, leaving just the root \(-1\) of \(F\).
This root is simple even at two, so the quotient has no root in these fields.
At two the same argument runs through \(j=22s\), excluding reduction of any cyclotomic divisor of \(H\) because its monic reduction has an irreducible constituent of positive degree at most \(22s\).

The finite residue splitting field has a corresponding unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\), in which all simple roots lift uniquely and exhaust the roots of \(H\).
The completion of the global splitting field at any place over \(p\) is generated by these roots and hence is unramified.
Reduction gives a Frobenius-equivariant bijection of root sets, with the orbit of each residue root having length its degree over \(\mathbb F_p\).
Thus each orbit has length greater than \(D\), and every nonconstant rational factor contains an entire orbit.

Bounding the logarithm of the least common multiple by the sum of logarithms gives at most \(Y(1+D(D+1)/2)\log Y+(22s)(22s+1)\log(2)/2\).
This yields the asserted absolute bound for \(a=A\), while its multiples yield the unbounded family for fixed parameters.
Taking \(Y=D=s\) along the allowed residue classes gives growing cutoffs.
For a factor with \(k\le Ka\) exterior roots, \(b/a=s^3+1/a\) fits the cubic slope range, so the cited [conditional degree estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) gives \(d\asymp as^2\) at large \(s\).
Whether such a factor exists remains open.
