## Independent whole-proof finding

The review was performed at 2026-11-17T19:15 UTC on [C345v290](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v290.md#statement), including every argument section and the corrected distinction between a circle-crossing endpoint and the imposed lower endpoint one.
The reviewer was separately assigned, did not develop the proof, and received the fixed candidate, necessary dependencies, review scope and applicable rules without substantive development dialogue or an earlier review conclusion.
Initial findings were formed directly from those inputs.
The existing [C345v289 review](Yin_R_G0562_v289.md#pisot-cyclotomic-perturbation-height) was then consulted to confirm the accepted height and lower-bound interfaces used below.

The complete stated result is supported.
The endpoint clarification has been independently rechecked, and no unresolved mathematical gap remains in this fixed argument.

For a real monic cyclotomic product with constant coefficient minus one, reversal in degree \(k\) is \(-C\), including repeated factors.
In particular \(C(1)=0\), and direct reversal gives the displayed expression for the reciprocal monic degree-\(k+2\) return \(H\).
A common root of \(C,U\) would remain on the unit circle for every parameter.
At a feasible parameter, conjugate pairing of the interior factors gives \(f_q(1)=U(1)<0\).

With no circle root of \(f_q\), the circle map \(Zf_q/f_q^*\) has winding \(2I-k\), even if numerator and denominator share factors away from the circle.
At a unit root \(\zeta\) of \(H\), differentiating \(1+Zf_q/f_q^*=H/f_q^*\) along the circle gives exactly \(\Psi'=H'(\zeta)/f_q(\zeta)\).
The signed crossing argument in [L462v34](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v34.md#crossings) applies to this map without an integrality assumption on \(q\).
For feasibility its winding is \(k\), while \(H(1)<0\) and reciprocity already use at least two degrees for a positive exterior root and its inverse.
Thus at most \(k\) circle-root multiplicities are available, and equality in the signed crossing bound forces exactly \(k\) distinct simple upward crossings.
The two remaining roots must be precisely one simple exterior root and its inverse.
More generally, when \(H\) has \(r\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity and simple circle roots, its \(k+2-2r\) crossings give winding \(k+2-2r-2\ell\).
Solving for \(k+1-I\) proves the exact exterior count \(r+\ell\).

At \(C(\zeta)\ne0\), the anti-reciprocal and reciprocal identities give the claimed reality of both \(t_\zeta\) and \(b_\zeta\), and simplicity gives \(b_\zeta\ne0\).
The crossing is positive precisely when \(b_\zeta/(t_\zeta-q)>0\).
At \(C(\zeta)=0\), coprimality gives the stated parameter-independent nonzero denominator \(U(\zeta)\).
Every circle root of \(f_q\) also annihilates \(H\), so these tests omit no possible crossing or forbidden parameter.
Their strict half-line intersection is exactly (2), and \(U(1)<0\) makes its unique exterior root positive by the intermediate value theorem.
The integer count follows by counting integers strictly between the endpoints.
A finite threshold endpoint gives a circle root; the imposed endpoint one is excluded only by \(q>1\) unless it is also such a threshold.
For integer parameters, any monic integer factor not containing the exterior root would have nonzero integer constant coefficient of modulus less than one, which proves irreducibility.
If the nonempty interval were unbounded above, arbitrarily large feasible integers would contradict [C345v289](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#statement) with fixed \(k,C,U\).

For the stability criterion, on the circle Rouché gives \(f_q-qf_q^*\) the interior root count of \(f_q^*\), since \(q>1\).
The exact identity \(f_q-qf_q^*=ZA_q^*\), with reversal of \(A_q\) in formal degree \(k\), then shows that one exterior root of \(f_q\) is equivalent to degree exactly \(k\) and strict disk stability of \(A_q\).
Indeed a degree drop in \(A_q\) introduces an extra zero at zero in its formal reversal and is incompatible with this count.
In the reverse direction, stability first rules out circle zeros of \(f_q\), so the use of Rouché is justified rather than assumed.
The sign \(A_q(1)=(1-q)U(1)>0\) makes its leading coefficient positive, and the strict product-of-moduli inequality yields \(q^2-1+u_1>q^2-1\).
Expansion of \(f_q/f_q^*\) at zero gives \([Z](f_q/f_q^*)=q^2-1+u_1\), because \(c_1=-c_{k-1}\).
Thus the branch index equals \(u_1>0\), and the accepted first-branch statement of [C345v7](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) applies at \(u_1=1\).

For the quartic family, expansion gives \(H=Z^5-2Z^3-2Z^2+1=(Z+1)(Z^4-Z^3-Z^2-Z+1)\), independently of \(A\).
Its trace polynomial is \(X^2-X-3\), with the stated one exterior trace and one interior trace \(x\).
At minus one, the threshold and derivative ratio are \(A-3/2\) and \(-3/2\).
At the other circle roots, the relations \(Z^2=xZ-1\) and \(x^2=x+3\) give
\[
 t_\zeta=1+\frac{1-A}{x+1}
         =1+\frac{3+\sqrt{13}}2(A-1),\qquad
 \frac{H'(\zeta)}{C(\zeta)}
     =\frac{x+2}{x+1}(2x-1)>0.
\]
There is no common root of \(C,U\), and \(U(1)=-1\), so the exact interval, its integer example \(2,3,4\) at \(A=2\), and branch \(A-1\) follow.
The norm gives \(\theta>q\), and the displayed exterior equation gives \(0<\theta-q\le A/q\).
Consequently \(q/A\to c\in[1,g]\) implies \(\theta/A\to c\) and \(\theta-q\to1/c\).
Integer parameters can approach either open endpoint at distance bounded independently of \(A\), so both limiting endpoint ratios and all intermediate ratios are realized.
Finally \(1/g=(\sqrt{13}-3)/2=T-2>0\).

For varying degree, the Pisot-minimal-polynomial assumption forces \(q=f_q(0)\) to be an integer, and eventually \(q\ge2\).
The height of \(U=qV+W\) is at most \(M(q+1)\), so [C345v289, exterior evaluation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#height) gives \(\delta=O_{M,c}(1)\) when \(q\ge ck\).
The geometric tail of the bounded coefficients gives \(U(\theta)/\theta^{k-1}=qv+O_M(1)\), where \(v=[Z^{k-1}]V\) is an integer.
Since \(C(\theta)>0\) and \(\delta>0\), this excludes \(v\ge1\) for sufficiently large degree.
If \(v=0\), the missing highest coefficient in \(V\) gives \(|U(\theta)|=O_M(\theta^{k-1})\), and the product lower bound on \(C\) then gives \(\delta=O_{M,c}(1/q)\).
Along any such subsequence this contradicts the accepted uniform bound \(\delta>b_{k+2}\) from [C345v289, return](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#return), because \(k^{-1}/b_{k+2}\to0\).
Thus \(v\le-1\) eventually.
The product upper bound on \(C\) now gives
\[
 \delta\ge\frac q\theta(1+1/\theta)^{-k}(1-O_M(1/q)),
\]
whose lower limit is at least \(e^{-1/c}\).
When \(q/k\to\infty\), the same estimates may be taken with a fixed lower ratio bound, giving \(\theta=q+O_M(1)\), while \(C(\theta)/\theta^k=1+O(k/q)\).
Dividing the root equation by \(\theta^k\) therefore gives \(\delta=-v+O_M(k/q)\).
The bounded negative integers \(v\) imply that every convergent excess subsequence in this regime has a positive integer limit; convergence of the entire sequence is not asserted.
Repeated factors of \(C\) do not affect any product bound used here.

For inverse coverage, comparison of the coefficient of \(Z^{k+1}\) in \(H=Zf+f^*\) gives \([Z^k]f+q=[Z^{k+1}]H\).
The stated matching coefficient of \(C\) removes the degree-\(k\) term in \(f-(Z-q)C\), and its constant is zero because \(C(0)=-1\).
This proves the claimed coverage conditional on the matching choice of \(C\), without asserting existence of that choice for every return.
