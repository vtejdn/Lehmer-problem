For the coefficient field of the relative minimal polynomial, the polynomial remains irreducible over the smaller field generated by its coefficients, since any factorization there would persist over the larger field.
The tower law therefore gives equality of the fields.
Restriction of the embeddings of the root field accounts for each root of the rational minimal polynomial once in the norm product.

Taking absolute norms gives the claimed direction of the inequality.

For the nontorsion constant, the embedded conjugate difference has magnitude equal to twice the hyperbolic sine of the absolute logarithm of the modulus.
The unit product formula converts the sum of positive logarithms to half the sum of absolute logarithms, and each positive logarithm is bounded by the corresponding polynomial measure.
For the torsion constant, the reciprocal conjugate polynomial has the same constant, and the first differing coefficient survives division by that unit with its absolute norm unchanged.
The quotient of Blaschke products in C0345v274 applies at each embedding because the numerator product uses the interior roots of the original polynomial, the denominator product uses its reflected exterior roots, and circle roots cancel in the quotient.
The two products have the same positive value at zero, equal to the exponential of the negative embedded measure.
Comparing their first unequal coefficients after the roots-of-unity average and Schwarz–Pick estimate gives the factor four in the hyperbolic sine bound.
In both cases Jensen's inequality applies to the logarithms of the nonzero embedded differences, since the inverse hyperbolic sine of the scaled exponential is increasing and convex.

The compositum of a given subfield and its image under inversion is stable under inversion.
Ordinary conjugation preserves both generators as fields, and its action commutes with every embedding of the compositum because this holds on each generating field.
Its fixed field is therefore totally real, and when either generator field is CM the compositum is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of this fixed field.
Any disagreement on the original subfield forces disagreement on the compositum, whose degree is then at least two.
The nontorsion bound at degree two and discriminant norm one is exactly the logarithm of the golden ratio, while the torsion bound there is strictly larger by strict concavity of the inverse hyperbolic sine.
Inversion has fixed field generated by the trace because the root has degree two over that field and inversion moves it.
