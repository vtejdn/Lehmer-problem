At 2026-11-17T10:03 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v203.md#decision-primitive-delay-power-fields-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the submitted argument and its dependencies without development discussion, exploration notes, author explanations or earlier findings on this submission.
Both signs satisfy (1)–(3) for coprime \(g,n\), and the torsion-quotient criterion and intersections (4) hold at an arbitrary common divisor in the stated delay range.

The leading and constant coefficients give a unit, and the roots of the two blocks have moduli respectively above and below one.
Thus both evaluations at \(\gamma=\alpha^g\) are nonzero, and \(\alpha^n=r(\gamma)\) together with Bézout's identity recovers \(\alpha\) in \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
Taking \(u_i=gv_i\) gives exactly the block gcd \(g\) and width parameter \(W\) of the [reviewed allocation estimate](Yin_R_G0562_v198.md#prime-block-factorization).
Multiplying its absolute error by \(g\) gives \(O(W^2\exp(-cCW^7))\), uniformly smaller than half the sum of the prime logarithms for sufficiently large \(C\), even for the smallest part \(s=1\).

For the upper height estimate use the homogeneous coordinates \([A_0^*(\gamma):A_0(\gamma)]\) of \(\pm\alpha^n\).
At finite places both coordinates are integral, and at each archimedean place their maximum modulus is at most \(\prod_i(1+p_i)\max(1,|\alpha|)^{W-1}\).
Summing gives \((n-W+1)h(\alpha)\le\log\prod_i(1+p_i)\).
The parent has nonzero end coefficients and at most \(2^{k+1}\) terms even after coefficients are collected, so the [reviewed sparse-divisor bound](Yin_R_G0562_v186.md#quadratic-divisor) gives the opposite inequality \(h(\alpha)\gg1/(n+W-1)\).
Thus \(h(\alpha)\asymp 1/n\) and \(d=m/h(\alpha)\asymp ns/g\).

If \(\zeta\alpha\) is another root with \(\zeta\) torsion, clearing the denominators in \(r(\zeta^g\gamma)=\zeta^n r(\gamma)\) gives a polynomial over \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) of degree at most \(2(V-1)\).
An identity would make rotation by \(\zeta^g\) permute the poles separately on each circle, since \(p_i^{v_j}\ne p_j^{v_i}\) for \(i\ne j\).
The stabilizer on the \(i\)-th circle is \(\mu_{v_i}\), so \(\gcd(v_i)=1\) forces \(\zeta^g=1\), and the nonzero function then forces \(\zeta^n=1\).
For coprime \(g,n\) this leaves only \(\zeta=1\).
For \(\zeta\ne1\) the resulting nonzero polynomial would give relative degree at most \(2(V-1)\) for the nontorsion \(\gamma\).
The [rational-base form of the Amoroso–Delsinne bound](../literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) would imply \(h(\gamma)\gg1/(V\log^4(4V))\), whereas \(h(\gamma)=g h(\alpha)\ll g/n\).
Since \(gV\le2W\) and \(V\le W\), these inequalities contradict the ninth-power threshold with a uniform choice of \(C\).
This exclusion covers roots in different factors as well as conjugates.
Consequently all conjugates of \(\alpha\) have distinct \(j\)-th powers, so \([\mathbb Q(\alpha^j):\mathbb Q]=d\) and the inclusion into \(K\) is equality for every \(j\).
Adjoining \(F\) preserves each equality.

For \(h_0=\gcd(g,n)\), write \(W'=1+(g/h_0)(V-1)\).
The bound \(W/W'\ge(1+h_0)/2\ge\sqrt{h_0}\) implies \(n/h_0\ge C(W')^9\).
Taking \(h_0\)-th powers therefore maps any two nontorsion roots with torsion quotient to the same root of the coprime parent.
Conversely each fiber consists precisely of a full \(\mu_{h_0}\)-orbit.
For each \(j\), \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\) contains \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^{jh_0})=\mathbb Q(\alpha^{h_0})\), and \(j=h_0\) attains this lower field.
The same two observations after adjoining \(F\) prove the second intersection.

For the relative degree identity, take a finite Galois closure \(M\) of \(K\) with group \(G\) and write \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\).
The image \(N\) in \(G\) of the absolute Galois group over \(F\) is normal and fixes \(M\cap F\).
Its orbit of \(\alpha\) has size \([N:N\cap H]=[NH:H]=[K:K\cap F]\), since \(M^{NH}=K\cap F\).
This size is \(\delta=[F(\alpha):F]\).
The same source bound gives \(\delta h(\alpha)\gg b(\delta)\gg b(d)\) by positivity on compact intervals and eventual decrease.
Dividing \(d h(\alpha)=m\asymp s/g\) by this lower bound proves (3).
Finally \(d\le n+W-1\le2n\) and \(b(2n)\asymp b(n)\), so the bound for \(g\) follows even when \(d\) is bounded.
The estimate allows a share that decreases with \(n\).
