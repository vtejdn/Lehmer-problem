Divisibility of the independent short integer has limiting proportion \(1/n\), yielding the displayed limiting divisor probability.

For all \(n\le2U\) the upper-bound sieve gives the proportion of eligible prime totals at most \(O(1/\varphi(n))\), and the short-integer divisibility proportion is \(O(1/n+1/U)\).
The bounded mean of \(n/\varphi(n)\) then yields \(\sum_{n>T}1/(n\varphi(n))=O(1/T)\) and \(\sum_{n\le2U}1/\varphi(n)=O(\log(2U))\).
This bounds both the sum of divisor-event tails and the probability of \(g>T\), since \(g\) itself would be such a divisor.

The resulting series converges absolutely by \([K_n:\mathbb Q]\ge\varphi(n)\), and tightness gives total mass one.
No Chebotarev estimate for a varying field is used.

A subfield of an abelian extension is normal; when generated by a real monomial it is a real field, and all conjugates of that monomial lie in it.

The squarefree terms in the Möbius series for \(a=1\) therefore give exactly \(\prod_p(1-1/(p^2(p-1)))\).
