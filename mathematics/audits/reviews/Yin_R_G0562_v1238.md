# Independent review of the trace moment search over all grids

<a id="finding"></a>
## Completeness of the search and its finite exclusions

At 2026-11-25T10:49 UTC, a reviewer outside proof development checked the full statement and both arguments of C G0345 v892, the search program and its reported finite ranges and controls.
The review began from the claim, its fixed dependency and the native code, without proof-development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or an earlier finding for v892.
The prior review of v891 was consulted only for the dependency concerning rational separators and the Salem lift.

For a solution the constant term gives \(\prod_i|v_i|\ge 1/w\), so evaluation at \(h\) gives \(0<h-w<K/M^{n+1}\).
Each of the \(j\) terms of the derivative sum is at most \((p/q)^{j-1}\) on \(1\le z\le p/q\).
The resulting error is less than \(Kn^2/(M^2q^{2(n-1)})\le K/M^2<1\), also for \(n=q=2\).
The real reciprocal pair therefore contributes strictly between \(E_j-1\) and \(E_j\), and each of the remaining \(N\) pairs contributes between \(-2\) and \(2\), inclusive.

The rational-separator construction in v891 applies to every polynomial with the specified root counts.
Its irreducible Salem lift has no root of unity, so the absolute resultants at both signs of unity are positive integers.
Pairing conjugate roots gives the factors \(2-x_i\) and \(2+x_i\), respectively, and the exterior factors are \(\phi_j(w)-2\) and \(\phi_j(w)+2\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean bound gives \(\sum x_i\le N+\log(\phi_j(w)-2)\) and \(-\sum x_i\le N+\log(\phi_j(w)+2)\), including the case of a negative logarithm.
Monotonicity and \(E_j+2<4(p/q)^j\) give both strict bounds in the second moment interval.

In each pole evaluation the coefficient of \(s_j\) has exact divisibility \(|b|^j\) with unit quotient modulo \(|b|\), even for composite \(|b|\).
The preceding digit enforces the divisibility needed at the next step, and the two bases have coprime absolute values.
Newton's identity gives a bijection from the possible next digits to integers in the resulting class modulo \(jM\).
The first window has integer diameter at most \(4N\), and the second admits at most one representative once its length is at most \(jM\).
These observations give both branching cutoffs, including the empty-product case.
Since \(\lfloor4N/(jM)\rfloor=\lfloor r/j\rfloor\), multiplying the bounds through any depth gives at most \(\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1+r/j)=\binom{2r}{r}\).
The triangular change of coefficients is integral with diagonal one.
The exact pole check, squarefreeness and open-interval root counts then give sufficiency, and the prescribed-grid sign tests impose precisely the optional separators.

For the finite search I rederived the next digit using the trace coefficients directly.
If \(c_i=[x^{n-i}]H\), the residue of \(c_j\) is \((K-\sum_{i<j}c_iM^i)/M^j\bmod M\).
Translating by the already determined coefficients of \(z^i(1-z)^{2(n-i)}\) gives the required residue of \(s_j\) independently of the program's two local pole evaluations.
I implemented that recurrence with rational window endpoints, enumerated all five degree ranges, and compared the stopping stage for each of the 5,175 height-degree pairs with a fresh run of the reviewed program.
Every stage agreed, including the last Newton sums \(12,13,12,11,14\) at heights \(5,6,8,12,40\), respectively.
At each height the only cubic reaching the final check was \(x^2(x+4)\), whose endpoint roots exclude it.
Every other degree in the census lost all prefixes by a Newton step.
Consequently at height \(40\) the fourth Newton sum excludes 16 degrees, while the reported stage-four count of 17 also contains the cubic.

For \(q=2,n=3,K=109\), both searches returned precisely \(x^3+6x^2+8x-1\).
Its exact scaled pole is \(109\), and the signs at \(-4,-3,0,1/6\) alternate.
On these three grid nodes its interpolation weights are \(3/50,4/19,1/2\), so its moment matrix is positive definite.
At \(K=25\) the weights of \(x^2(x+4)\) are \(0,18/19,0\), giving rank one and a zero second minor.
The companion matrix program reproduces these two outcomes.
The general matrix criterion is reviewed separately.
I found no defect in the v892 decision procedure or in the five finite exclusions.
Existence at other heights and degrees remains open under this evidence.
