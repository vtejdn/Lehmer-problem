### Exact setting, convergence and source interface

The interior statement concerns a real r by r matrix B with r>=2, spectral radius less than one, and a cyclic vector v, with

    T(B,v)={sum_(j>=0) epsilon_j B^j v : epsilon_j in {0,1}}.

Choose q with spectral radius(B)<q<1.
Finite-dimensional Jordan estimates give a constant C with norm(B^j)<=C q^j after enlarging C; polynomial factors from nontrivial Jordan blocks are absorbed by the larger q.
Consequently the digit series converges uniformly over all choices, and its image of the compact digit space is compact.
No Euclidean operator-norm contraction or diagonalizability is required.

The retained primary [HS16 v4](../../literature/pdf/HS16.pdf), Theorem 1.1 on p.1, has the determinant threshold abs(det B)>=2^(-1/r), the real contracting-matrix setting and cyclic vector.

### Negative finding and the endpoint replacement

Under the ordinary linear span of path images, the source's Proposition 1.5 is false.
In R^2, the constant paths at e_1 and e_2 have images spanning R^2, whereas their Minkowski sum is the single point e_1+e_2.
The source's p.4 choice of a path merely containing v therefore cannot be justified by that unrestricted proposition.
This useful negative finding is retained, together with the distinction between rejecting that statement and validating the corrected interior proof.

For the actual replacement, let gamma_i start at 0 and end at v_i, with the r endpoint vectors linearly independent.
The rule gamma_i(s+k)=gamma_i(s)+k v_i for 0<=s<=1 and integral k is well-defined at the adjoining endpoints and gives a continuous extension to R.
With V the invertible matrix of endpoint columns,

    F(t)=sum_i gamma_i(t_i)=Vt+g(t)

has continuous Z^r-periodic g.
Hence C_g=sup_t norm(g(t)) is finite.
For any y, the map

    H_y(t)=V^(-1)(y-g(t))

maps the closed Euclidean ball of radius R into itself whenever R>=norm(V^(-1))(norm(y)+C_g).
Brouwer's finite-dimensional fixed-point theorem gives t=H_y(t), so F(t)=y.
This establishes surjectivity rather than assuming it from the endpoint data.

Writing each coordinate t_i=s_i+k_i with s_i in [0,1] gives

    R^r=union_(k in Z^r)(S+Vk),
    S=gamma_1([0,1])+...+gamma_r([0,1]).

The set S is compact, being the continuous image of [0,1]^r.
If S had empty interior, every closed translate S+Vk would be nowhere dense, contradicting the Baire category theorem on R^r.
The replacement therefore holds without path simplicity, differentiability, rectifiability or separation.
Brouwer and Baire remain the explicit foundational inputs already accepted by the fixed unit; their original proofs are outside this review.

### Residue classes, cyclicity under powers and scalar endpoints

The uniformly convergent digit sums can be grouped by residues modulo r, and every selection of the r residue digit sequences is allowed.
This gives both inclusions in the exact identity

    T(B,v)=sum_(j=0)^(r-1) B^j T(B^r,v).

The determinant hypothesis implies invertibility and abs(det(B^r))=abs(det B)^r>=1/2.
It does not imply that v stays cyclic for B^r on the original space.
The fixed proof correctly uses

    W=span{(B^r)^k v : k>=0}.

This is a nonzero B^r-invariant subspace, with v cyclic on W.
The induced map on the quotient has all eigenvalues of modulus less than one, and determinant factorization gives

    abs(det(B^r|_W))>=abs(det(B^r))>=1/2.

If W is the full space, the quotient determinant is the empty product 1.
If W is proper, that quotient determinant has modulus strictly less than one.
These facts justify the inequality in the required direction.
They also verify the nondegenerate cyclic hypotheses for applying the accepted Corollary 1.8 on W when dim W>=2.

When dim W=1, write B^r v=a v.
Then 1/2<=abs(a)<1.
For a>=1/2, the scalar binary attractor is [0,1/(1-a)].
For a<=-1/2, it is [a/(1-a^2),1/(1-a^2)].
For each proposed interval I, the two contracting images aI and 1+aI overlap or touch exactly under the indicated inequality and their union is I.
The unique compact attractor is therefore that interval.
Both scalar cases contain 0 and 1 and give the necessary path from 0 to v.
The same argument supplies the separately stated r=1 interior case.

The cyclicity of B implies that v,Bv,...,B^(r-1)v are linearly independent.
Indeed, a dependence ending at the first dependent power supplies a recurrence that keeps every later power in a proper subspace, contradicting cyclicity.
The path from 0 to v and its r images under B^j consequently have independent endpoint vectors.
The endpoint lemma puts an open subset into their Minkowski sum, which lies in the displayed residue decomposition.
This completes the adopted interior argument, including threshold equality and the possible loss of cyclicity under powers.

### Expanding conversion and the exact entropy consequence

For the real expanding cyclic pair (A,v), B=A^(-1) has the required spectral radius.
The inverse-power attractor is

    S={sum_(j>=1) epsilon_j A^(-j)v}=A^(-1)T(B,v).

An invertible real linear map preserves nonempty interior, and the determinant condition converts exactly to

    abs(det A)^r<=2.

For a monic irreducible algebraic-unit polynomial p with positive expanding dimension r, the full real expanding Minkowski space has one real dimension per real expanding conjugate and two per nonreal conjugate pair.
Its complexification has the distinct expanding conjugates as eigenvalues.
Evaluation of 1,lambda,...,lambda^(r-1) at those conjugates gives the Vandermonde rank r, so the expanding embedding of 1 is cyclic.
The absolute real determinant is the product of the moduli of every expanding conjugate, counting a nonreal pair twice, and equals M(p).
Thus the sufficient condition is exactly r m(p)<=log 2, with m(p)=log M(p).
The zero-dimensional situation is outside the positive-dimensional interior statement.

Interior supplies positive finite r-dimensional volume of S.
The direct fixed C G0239 interface gives, by splitting a binary address after n digits,

    A^n S=D_n+S,

Division by the positive volume gives abs(D_n)>=M(p)^n.
