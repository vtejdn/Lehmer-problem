# Uniform height gaps for prime-power radicals of the integer Salem families

## Independent review of the short congruence and the count of exceptional seeds

At 2026-11-21T00:48 (UTC), an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the complete submitted C345 v698 and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in its development and inspected the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on v698.
The numerical constant \(0.427800\) has the published-evaluation scope of the cited height bound.

For either sign, \(a\ge3\) and \(n\ge7\), the classification gives \(G_a=UP\) with \(U\) the product of the distinct factors \(X-c\) at the specified signs \(c^{n-1}=\epsilon\), so \(n-1\le d\le n+1\).
Both binomials in \(G_a\) vanish at each such \(c\), and division by their common monic factor respects integral specialization.
If \(\beta=r^{1/p^e}\in K\), then \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=K\), and the images of \(\beta\) correspond bijectively under powering to the images of \(r\).
There is a unique exterior image, and a neutral image and its complex conjugate force reciprocity by irreducibility.
Thus \(\beta\) is a Salem unit, \(M(\beta)=\beta\), and the powered root multiset gives \(P\) in characteristic zero.
On reducing at a prime over \(p\), the \(p^e\)-th powers of the elementary symmetric functions of the roots of \(S\) give their original residues, with the coefficient signs preserved also in characteristic two.
Consequently \(S\equiv P\bmod p\), so every indicated \(G_c/U\) annihilates \(\beta\) modulo \(p\mathcal O_K\).

For \(p\ge11\), putting \(k=\lfloor(p-1)/4\rfloor\ge2\) gives \(2k(k+1)+1>4k+3\ge p\) and \(4k<p\).
The collision of two residue classes in the radius-\(j_p\) diamond gives a nonzero vector of norm at most \(2j_p\), with \(2(|u|+|v|)\le4j_p<p\).
If \(p\mid u\), both coordinates are divisible by \(p\), and this strict bound makes both zero.
At seven the pairs for residues \(\pm3\) satisfy \(2(\pm3)\equiv\mp1\), and the five other classes have representatives of norm at most three.
At five the trace restriction applies throughout the tower because \(5<n\), and it leaves only \(0,\pm1\) for \(a\bmod5\).
Thus \(D_{u,v}\) has degree \(n+1\), nonzero constant term and circle maximum strictly smaller than \(p\) in all three ranges.
For a nonzero evaluation, the Salem case of the height transfer applies to divisibility in \(\mathcal O_K\), giving the term \(c_0d/(n+1)\).
Independently, taking norms of that evaluation and using its coefficient sum gives \(p^d\le(4j_p)^d\beta^{n+1}\) for \(p\ge11\), including the original degree in the denominator of (2).

For a zero evaluation, the monic rational quotient \(G_{v/u}/U\) has the full degree of \(\beta\) and annihilates it, hence equals \(S\).
The coefficient of \(X^n\) in \(US\) proves integrality of \(b=v/u\), while \(p\nmid u\) gives \(b\equiv a\bmod p\).
The denominator \(\beta^n-\epsilon\beta\) is positive, and subtracting it from the numerator gives \((\beta-1)(\beta^n+\epsilon)>0\), so \(b\ge2\).
At \(x=5/3\) the upper bound \(x^n(x-b)+bx-1\) decreases with \(b\) and equals \((7-x^n)/3<0\) at \(b=2\).
The factorization \(US\) identifies the root beyond \(5/3\) with \(\beta\), including at seed two.
Thus \(\log\beta>\log(5/3)>1/2>c_0d/(n+1)\) throughout the zero case.

For \(p=2,3\), the zero representative of \(a\) gives the scalar congruence at \(M=n+1\).
At \(c=\pm1\), put \(V=X^n+\epsilon c\), so \(G_c=(X-c)V\).
If \(X-c\mid U\), then \(G_c/U\mid V\), giving \(M=n\).
Otherwise \(c^{n-1}=-\epsilon\), which makes \(X-c\mid V\) and \(G_c/U\mid V^2\).
Reduction and the Frobenius identity for \(V^p\) give \(M=np\), with a nonzero scalar also at \(p=2\).
In this last case \(U\) omits the factor \(X-c\), so \(\deg U\le1\) and \(d\ge n\).
Consequently \(d/M\ge1/p\) here and \(d/M\ge3/4\) in the first two cases.
The scalar bounds for the nontorsion unit \(\beta^M\) are \(c_0\) and \(\tfrac12\log(5/2)\), respectively, for absolute logarithmic height.
The latter constant follows from the circle maximum of \((X-c)^9(X+c)\) at \(\sin^2(u)=9/10\) after changing the sign of the variable if needed.
Since \(h(\beta^M)=M\log\beta/d\), the two constants in (1) follow without assuming the power preserves degree.
Writing \(\delta=\log(5/2)/6\) gives \(\delta<1/6<c_0/2\), while \(d/(n+1)\ge3/4\).

The positive-unit factor formula supplies the largest radical index \(R\) and gives \(\log r/\gcd(h,R)\) for the least factor measure in logarithmic units at dilation \(h\).
Every prime power dividing \(R\) yields its own positive field radical and hence is at most \(X/\delta\), with \(X=\log r\).
This gives divisibility by \(\ell_B=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)\), where \(B=\lfloor X/\delta\rfloor\ge1\).
For \(u=\lceil j/2\rceil\), every prime crossing a power threshold in \((u,j]\) does so just once, and its factorial valuation in \(\binom{j}{j-u}\) includes a positive contribution at that power.
Thus \(\ell_j\mid\ell_u\binom{j}{j-u}\), and at odd \(j\) the equality of the two central coefficients saves the factor two required for the induction \(\ell_j\le4^j\).
It follows that \(\ell_B^{-1}\ge r^{-\log(4)/\delta}\), while \(U(X^h)\) has only torsion roots.
For another admissible \(q>1\), the same prime-power gap gives \(q/p^{v_p(q)}\ge\delta/\log(r^{1/q})\) simultaneously at every \(p\mid q\).

For the growing-seed estimate, the primes of multiplicity at least two number at most \(\sqrt{X/\delta}\), and each full prime power contributes at most \(\log(X/\delta)\) to \(\log R\).
The log contribution of primes of multiplicity one up to \(T=16X/\log X\) is at most \(T\log4\).
For \(p>T\) and sufficiently large \(X\), one has \(p>\sqrt X\), \(j_p\le\lceil\sqrt p\rceil\le2\sqrt p\) and \(X/p<(\log X)/16\).
The quantitative nonzero case instead requires \(\log\beta_p\ge(3/4)((\log X)/4-\log8)\), exceeding \((\log X)/16\) once \(\log X>6\log8\).
For each surviving prime, its exact seed satisfies \(2\le b_p<\beta_p+1\le X^{1/16}+1\), using the original interval when \(b_p\ge3\).
At fixed \(n,\epsilon\), each such \(G_{b_p}=US_{\beta_p}\) has a unique exterior root, and the positive \(p\)-th radicals are pairwise distinct, so these primes inject into the stated interval of integer seeds.
Their total log contribution is at most \((X^{1/16}+1)\log(X/\delta)\).
Both error contributions are \(O(X/\log X)\) at effective absolute thresholds, and \(r>a-1\) makes these thresholds uniform as \(a\to\infty\).
The resulting bound for every \(h\) is (4), while the absolute gap applies to the prime-power radical indices.
