## Relative chief factors, exact tower level and the two gap conditions

At 2026-11-25T01:21 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v108.md#statement) and both proof sections of C0901 v108, at the final SHA-256 0DF40C62F48923B523B7C66CC7831B3B1C84E8E67F130F0979BEB1AD64BD5580.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and mathematical inputs without development discussion or earlier findings on v108.
The final statement correctly restricts (2) and the sharp tower threshold to \(P\not\subseteq A\).
The membership and rank formulas, the totally real base specialization, (4), and both directions of the final gap reduction hold.

For any algebraic base \(A\), the class of finite groups defined by the bound on nonabelian chief-factor rank passes to quotients by taking a chief series through the kernel.
For a finite subdirect product project away the last coordinate.
Its kernel is identified with a normal subgroup of that last factor, with exactly the action by conjugation of the whole factor since the projection onto it is surjective.
Its chief sections thus retain their ranks from a series of the last factor through that subgroup, and the sections in the quotient are handled by induction.
The condition is also preserved by attaching a solvable normal kernel.
A finite Galois extension of \(A\) inside the defining compositum lies inside a finite compositum of the defining fields, and hence its group satisfies the same condition.
This works when \(A\) has infinite degree over \(\mathbb Q\).
For the solvable closure of \(U_h(A)\), a finite Galois quotient over \(A\) has a solvable normal image of the kernel over \(U_h(A)\), and its remaining quotient comes from \(U_h(A)\).
This proves solvable closedness by the same finite criterion.
Normality of \(A/\mathbb Q\) ensures that every rational conjugation carries the generating family of \(U_h(A)\) to itself.

Under restriction to \(P\), \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(AP/A)\) identifies with \(\operatorname{Gal}(P/(P\cap A))\), since \(P/\mathbb Q\) is finite Galois.
The intersection is rationally normal, so \(H\) is normal in the rational label group \(G\).
For \(P\not\subseteq A\) this subgroup is nontrivial and contains the unique minimal normal subgroup \(D\) of \(G\).
If \(G_i\) stabilizes a block then \(P^{G_i}=E_i\), and in \(AP/A\) the stabilizer \(H\cap G_i\) fixes precisely \(AE_i\).
The orbit therefore has size \([AE_i:A]\), equal to \(v_A\) on every block because \(G\) acts transitively and normalizes \(H\).
In particular \(v_A\mid u\).
On one such orbit, the product of the corresponding \(A_b\) factors is minimal normal under \(H\).
Any \(H\)-normal subgroup within it is normal under the entire direct product \(D\), hence is a product of individual factors by simplicity and trivial centers, and its support must be a union of \(H\)-orbits.
Successively quotienting by the disjoint orbit products preserves this argument on all remaining orbits.
This gives exactly \(u/v_A\) chief sections of rank \(v_A\) up to \(D\).

For \(H/D\) the kernel on the blocks embeds in the blockwise sign quotient and is abelian.
Its remaining image is a subdirect product of the images on its \(v_A\)-point orbits.
Each of those finite groups embeds in \(S_{v_A}\).
For any simple nonabelian \(S\), a chief section \(S^j\) there has \(j\le v_p(v_A!)<v_A\) for a prime divisor \(p\) of \(|S|\).
The subdirect-product argument gives the same strict bound for the whole image, also when \(v_A=1\) and there is no such section.
Thus \(AP\) occurs exactly from level \(v_A\) if \(P\not\subseteq A\).
Membership of the actual \(y\) is equivalent to inclusion of \(P\) in the chosen level, since that level is rationally normal and the full rational splitting field of \(y\) has solvable kernel over \(P\), including before torsion-class compression.
If \(P\subseteq A\), that solvable kernel suffices already at level zero, proving this separate case of (1).

For \(P\not\subseteq A\), put \(U=U_{v_A-1}(A)\).
The fixed field of \(D\) in \(AP/A\) belongs to \(U\), by the rank bound above \(D\), so the restriction subgroup on \(P\) over \(U\) is contained in \(D\).
It is nontrivial by (1) and rationally normal in \(G\) since \(U/\mathbb Q\) is normal.
Minimal normality in \(G\) now makes it all of \(D\), even when the relative group \(H\) has several block orbits.
This covers \(v_A=1\) by taking \(U_0(A)\).
For \(P\subseteq A\) the restriction is trivial at every level, which is why the final qualification is needed.

After isolating \(D\) over \(U\), a finite tower of solvable extensions and single adjunctions of degree \(<b\) has normal closure with no section \(A_b\).
A single adjunction's normal closure has group inside \(S_{b-1}\), of order less than \(|A_b|\), and the absence of this simple section survives products of conjugates, subgroups, quotients and extensions.
Every nontrivial quotient of \(D\) has a section \(A_b\), so the resulting normal tower fields intersect \(UP\) trivially over \(U\), including after taking their directed union.
For \(h<v_A\) any allowed tower from \(U_h(A)\) base changes to one over \(U\) preserving the degree and solvability bounds.
The union of all such towers over \(U\) is rationally normal, so containing \(y\) would force it to contain \(P\), impossible.
Conversely, stabilizing first blocks and then their individual labels requires successive indices at most \(u\) and \(b\), respectively, with \(u<b\) under the original threshold.
The primitive element theorem followed by the solvable kernel attains \(y\) with bound \(b\) already from \(\mathbb Q\), and hence over all the stated bases.
No choice of \(A,h\) changes that initial degree threshold.

For \(T=\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\), the relation \(v_T=1\) is exactly \(E\subset T\), equivalent to \(B\subset T\) by rational normality, while \(P\subset T\) also implies \(B\subset T\).
Thus (1) gives both directions of (3) for the field defined over \(T\).
If \(B\not\subseteq T\), rational normality of \(B\) makes ordinary complex conjugation nonidentity on its blocks, hence interchanges two whole blocks.
This requires \(b\) disjoint label transpositions, and for reciprocal units each such transposition accounts for exactly one nonreal conjugate pair of exterior roots.
On exchanged formal coordinate spaces, \(c^2=1\) gives a fixed graph of dimension \(b\) in the independent sign case.
On exchanged block sum-zero spaces the dimension is \(b-1\), and these spaces inject into the class module in the oriented case regardless of the block constants.
The fixed-space interpretation of radial rank yields the second inequality of (4), and the independent two-label height estimate yields its third at the compressed degree and actual measure.

Unrestricted positive measures tending to zero can be tested on nonzero nontorsion algebraic unit minima: nonunit extreme coefficients cost at least \(\log 2\), and a noncyclotomic irreducible factor suffices for an integral polynomial.
Smyth gives reciprocity eventually.
Taking products in each torsion class preserves total measure and the whole conjugate span, hence radial rank, and these compressed degrees go to infinity by coefficient finiteness.
If there is a gap on \(\mathcal F\), the compressed sequence eventually lies outside it.
The field criterion then gives a nonreal block field and all inequalities of (4), with the constant in the last estimate fixed by the two-label theorem.
A gap on that remaining class rules out this sequence, proving sufficiency.
An unrestricted gap applies directly to both subclasses, proving necessity.
This includes the large compressed Salem and complex Salem cases inside \(\mathcal F\) and leaves the gap on that field as an actual requirement.
