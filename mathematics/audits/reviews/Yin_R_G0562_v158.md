## Finding on the two one-sided errors and the integral gap

At 2026-11-17T04:31 UTC, an agent outside proof development independently inspected the complete [v158 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v158.md#decision-uniform-phase-quadratic-transfer-submitted) for Yin.
The fixed submission and its necessary dependencies were the mathematical inputs.
The reviewer had independently inspected v154 and v156 and received no development dialogue or separate author explanation for v158.

**Vertical modes.**
For \(|a|\le1\) the two roots are conjugates on the circle, while for \(|a|>1\) reflection gives two copies of \(\operatorname{sgn}(a)e^{-g(a)}\).
For \(|v|=1\), each exterior root \(y\) contributes a factor \(|y||1-v/y|\) to \(|v-y|\), and each interior root contributes \(|1-\bar yv|\).
This proves the factorization including the double roots at \(a=\pm1\).
Jensen's formula makes the vertical log mean exactly \(g(a)\).
The oscillation of \(\cos(j\arccos a)\) contributes total variation \(2j\), and each exterior tail contributes one.
The real-valued trigonometric polynomial \(a(t)\) is monotone on at most \(2s\) intervals of the circle because its nonzero derivative has at most \(2s\) zeros there.
Thus periodic integration by parts at the nonzero frequency \(jn\) bounds the normalized integral of \(c_j(a(t))e^{ijnt}\) by \(O(s/n)\).
The factors \(e^{ij\phi}\) have modulus one, and summing the absolutely convergent series \(\sum_{j\ge1}r^j/j\) gives (3).
All these bounds use only the degree and the real values of \(J\) on the circle.

**Logarithmic singularities.**
The inequality \(|1-rz|\ge\sqrt r\,|1-z|\) on the disk costs \(-\log r\) for the two factors together in the upper bound on \(m_\phi-m_{\phi,r}\).
With \(r=1-u/2\) this is \(O(u)\) throughout \(0<u<1\).
For the opposite direction the triangle inequality bounds the discrepancy of one factor by \(\log(1+\epsilon/D)\) when \(D>0\).
If \(D<v\le1/4\), then the radius of the associated disk point exceeds \(1-v\).
Both disk points have radius \(e^{-g}\), so the original modulus is at most \(2v/(1-v)\le4v\), regardless of how the roots are labeled.
The outer Fourier coefficient of magnitude one guarantees \(\|A_\phi\|_\infty\ge1\), and its frequency cannot collide with another term since \(n>s\).
On a full period the cited measurable-set Turán–Nazarov theorem has \(k\) distinct exponents of real part zero with nonzero coefficients, exponential prefactor one and exponent \(k-1\).
Applied to \(\{|A_\phi|\le4v\}\) when that set has positive measure, it gives \(\Pr(D<v)\le C v^{1/(k-1)}\) with an absolute \(C\).
The same bound is immediate for a null set, and it excludes a positive-measure zero set for either factor.

For \(h=1/(k-1)\), integrating the distribution function against \(\epsilon/(v(v+\epsilon))\) gives the mean of \(\log(1+\epsilon/D)\).
The part above \(1/4\) costs at most \(\log(1+4\epsilon)\).
Splitting the remaining integral at \(\epsilon\), or extending the rescaled integral to infinity, gives \(O(\epsilon^h(1/h+1/(1-h)))\).
The real nonconstant degree-\(s\) polynomial has both frequencies \(\pm s\), distinct from the two outer frequencies, so \(k\ge4\) and this cost is \(O(k\epsilon^h)\).
In particular the logarithm is integrable at every multiplicity permitted by the statement.
For \(\epsilon=(u/(2k))^{k-1}\), the removal cost is \(O(u)\), while (3) costs \(O(u(k-1)\log(2k/u))\).
Together with the upper choice this gives the two distinct bounds in (1) for every phase and every \(0<u<1\).

**Integral specialization.**
At \(\phi=0\), multiplying the Laurent specialization by \(z^n\) gives \(P\) with the same circle log mean.
If the two-variable measure vanished, continuity and Jensen's formula would give \(|J|\le2\) everywhere, leaving only the v154 zero profiles.
For a nonconstant profile in that list the specialization factors as \((z^{n+s}\pm1)(z^{n-s}\pm1)\).
This rules out every \(P\) containing a noncyclotomic factor even when roots repeat.
The absolute positive lower bound from the trace theorem of v156 therefore applies before specialization.
Choosing the absolute \(c_0\) smaller than half that bound divided by the lower-error constant gives (2).
When \(L\to0\) through these positive measures, the condition in (2) must eventually fail.
Since \(k\le2s+3\le5s\), \(\log(2kn/s)\ll\log(2n)\), first \(ks\log(2n)\gg n\) and then \(s\gg\sqrt{n/\log(2n)}\) follow.

The complete submitted estimates and integral consequences hold in their stated ranges.
The Turán–Nazarov estimate was used at the statement level of the recorded Bownik–Speegle Theorem 2.1, which attributes it to Nazarov.
Its original proof was outside this inspection.
