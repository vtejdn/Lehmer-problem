## Finding on the modulus and all its radical fibers

The proposed modulus works for every order and every choice of root, including when \(g\) has repeated prime factors or \(g\mid M\).
The degree, Mahler measure, reciprocity and square-degree conclusions all follow with the stated uniform choice of \(B(C)\).

For a reducible prime binomial, choose a proper **irreducible** factor of degree \(t\).
The root then has degree \(t<r\), and its norm \(u\) satisfies \(u^r=y^t\).
If \(bt+cr=1\), then \((u^b y^c)^r=y\).
For the two-power induction, a square root of \(z\) in \(K(z)\) makes both \(N_{K(z)/K}(z)=-y\) and \(N_{K(z)/K(t)}(z)=-t\) squares in their respective fields.
The second sign uses \(s\ge2\) and the relative degree \(2^{s-1}\), obtained by dividing the full degree by \([K(t):K]=2\).
If \(w^2=-y\) in \(K\), then \((w/t)^2=-1\) in \(K(t)\), so a square root of \(-t\) there gives a fourth root of \(y\) in a quadratic extension, contrary to quartic irreducibility.
For each odd prime step, either sign of an \(r\)th power is an \(r\)th power, so the norm rules out a loss of degree even when a prime repeats.
These arguments prove the needed detection criterion directly, in agreement with the stronger explicit quartic exception in [Capelli's criterion](../literature/JP18/Yin_U001.md#jp18-u001).

For every \(a\mid g\), the element \(y\) and its inverse are integral because their \(a\)th powers are units.
Consequently \(N_{K/F}(y)\) is a unit, and its \(a\)th power \(x^l\) makes its height exactly \(lh/a>0\).
If \(y=\theta^r\) in \(K\), the same reasoning gives a unit norm of height \(lh/(ar)\ge\mu\), hence \(r\le h/\mu\) since \(l\le a\).
At a reducible step every irreducible factor has degree at most \(s-1\), so the new ratio \(l/a\) along the path specified by the original \(\gamma\) is at most \((s-1)/s\) times its old value.
Here \(s\le B\), including the exceptional \(s=4\).
After \(J\) losses the upper bound would be strictly less than \(\exp(-J/B)\le1/B\), since \(\lceil\log_2B\rceil\ge\log B\).
This contradicts the lower bound for the unit norm.

Every factor accumulated in \(a\) divides \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)\), so the terminal \(a\) divides both \(M\) and \(g\).
The identity \(\beta^{m/a}=y\) gives degree bounds \(m/a\) and \(g/m\) on the two stages above \(K\).
Terminal irreducibility gives their product \(g/a\), forcing equality in each bound.
This applies to each \(\beta^m=x\) by choosing any \(k\)th root of \(\beta\).
The monic \(P_\beta(T^k)\) has the full resulting degree, and the product of the Mahler factors of the \(k\) roots over a conjugate \(b\) is \(\max(1,|b|)\).
The complete fibers are inversion-stable as a union exactly when their base conjugates are inversion-stable.
Squaring decreases the degree exactly when the conjugates contain the negative of the chosen root, since the extension over its square has degree at most two.
Negation stays in one fiber for even \(k\) and sends the fiber over \(b\) to the fiber over \(-b\) for odd \(k\).
In particular the prescribed degree \(d\) in the [compressed-graph test](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v474.md#progressions) can be compared directly with \(k\deg P_\beta\).

I checked the normalization on p. 1 and Corollary 2 on p. 4 of [Voutier's author edition](../../literature/pdf/Vou96--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=4).
The only rational units are \(1,-1\), so each nontorsion unit of \(F\) has degree \(2\le f\le e\).
Since \(f(\log(3f))^3\) increases on this range, the corollary gives the stated common lower bound for every such unit, including both norms used above.
The strict inequality \(eh<2\) then gives \(h/\mu<(\log(3e))^3\le(\log(6C))^3\).
The proof of Voutier's corollary is accepted at statement scope.
The compressed-graph comparison concerns the remaining radical test under its stated bounds.
