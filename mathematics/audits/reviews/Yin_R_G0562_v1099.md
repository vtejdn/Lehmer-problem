## Nearly linear degree and exact automorphisms at bounded measure

At 2026-11-24T21:39 an agent outside proof development independently reviewed for Yin the complete [rational-map and automorphism claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v42.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed argument and assignment without development dialogue or previous conclusions on this version.
The degree bounds hold in the independent, dependent and torsion cases, and the automorphism formulas hold with the stated large-degree, reciprocity, power-field and compatibility hypotheses.

Write \(K=\mathbb Q(x)\) and \(d_y=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\).
The nonconstant reduced rational map supplies a nonzero polynomial \(A-yB\) vanishing at \(x\), of degree at most \(t\) over \(\mathbb Q(y)\).
Thus \([K:\mathbb Q(y)]\le t\).
For independent \(x,y\) the joint field is exactly \(K\), and the rank-two specialization of Amoroso–David, Theorem 1.6, has logarithmic exponent \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\).
Multiplication of the absolute-height product by \(dd_y\) gives \(m(x)m(y)\gg d_y/(\log(3d))^{212}\).
Since \(d_y\ge d/t\), the upper bound \(M^2\) gives (1) with constants independent of the rational coefficients. [Theorem 1.6, published edition](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/1944852)

If both coordinates are nontorsion and dependent, divide their integer relation by the gcd of its exponents in the quotient by torsion.
The resulting \(\xi=y^b/x^a\) is a root of unity in \(K\), and \(bu+av=1\) gives \(z=x^u y^v\) with \(z^b=x\xi^v\) and \(z^a=y\xi^{-u}\).
Consequently \(z\) is nontorsion and its absolute height has both asserted scaling laws, with \(|a|\) for either sign.
For \(f=[\mathbb Q(\xi):\mathbb Q]\), the relevant relative degree is \(D=[\mathbb Q(\xi,z):\mathbb Q(\xi)]\le d/f\).
The rational-base consequence of Amoroso–Delsinne gives \(D h(z)\gg b(D)\gg b(d)\) for this finite abelian base, with an absolute constant.
The second inequality uses positivity on bounded intervals and eventual decrease of \(b\).
The source's rational-base specialization uses a fixed auxiliary quadratic field to retain a valid absolute factor. [Theorem 1.3](https://www.esi.ac.at/preprints/esi1848.pdf)
This yields \(fb\ll m(x)/b(d)\) and \(f|a|\ll (d/d_y)m(y)/b(d)\), so the cost of the torsion multiplier field is accounted for.
For \(a>0\) a clearing polynomial is \(A^b-\xi X^aB^b\), and for \(a<0\) it is \(X^{|a|}A^b-\xi B^b\).
Either identity of rational functions would force every zero and pole of \(R\) to be at zero or infinity, and comparison of the scalar and exponent gives precisely an excluded rational map \(\pm X^k\).
The nonzero polynomial has degree at most \(bt+|a|\), proving \(d/f\le bt+|a|\) because \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\subseteq K\).
Using \(d/d_y\le t\) now yields \(d\,b(d)\ll t(m(x)+m(y))\).

For torsion \(x\) and nontorsion \(y\), the relative degree of \(y\) over the cyclotomic field \(K\) is one, giving an absolute positive height lower bound and \(d_y\ll_M1\).
For torsion \(y\) and nontorsion \(x\), the relative degree of \(x\) over the cyclotomic field \(\mathbb Q(y)\) is \(d/d_y\le t\), giving the claimed lower bound on \(t\,m(x)/d\).
When both are torsion the \(d\) embeddings give distinct torus points by their first coordinates.
Over \(\mathbb C[X^{\pm1}]\), the graph equation \(A-YB\) is primitive by coprimality and irreducible by linearity over \(\mathbb C(X)\).
Inverting \(Y\) preserves this irreducibility since \(A\ne0\).
A torsion coset component would thus be the whole graph, and its degree-one projection to \(X\) forces a primitive binomial relation with \(Y\)-exponent of absolute value one.
The resulting monomial multiplier would be a rational root of unity, hence a sign, again excluded.
A rational monomial with nontorsion multiplier has no torsion point.
The Beukers–Smyth finite alternative therefore bounds the number of points by \(22\) times an ordinary Newton area at most \(t\).
This gives \(d\le22t\), with no order or parity restriction.
The three external height and point-count results were used at theorem-statement scope. [Beukers–Smyth, §4.1](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~chris/preprints/beukers_smyth.pdf)

For \(y=\sigma x\), both degrees are \(d\) and both measures are equal.
Independence would give \(M^2\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), impossible past a threshold depending only on \(M\).
In the nontorsion dependent case the equal positive heights give \(b=|a|=1\) by coprimality.
If \(x\) is reciprocal, \(x^{-1}\) is a conjugate generating the same field and defines an automorphism.
No distinct conjugates have torsion quotient under the all-power hypothesis, since two embeddings agreeing on a generating power are equal.
Comparison to \(x\) or \(x^{-1}\) eliminates all the extra twists and gives exactly the two automorphisms.

For the cyclotomic twist, \(\beta^N=\delta^N\) generates \(K\), so \(\beta\) generates \(L=KE\).
Compatibility on \(K\cap E\) gives the common involution \(\tau\), fixing the trace field and negating \(i\).
For any automorphism of \(L\), raise its value \(\xi\beta^{\pm1}\) to an exponent divisible by both \(N\) and \(\operatorname{ord}\xi\).
The resulting \(\delta^{\pm a}\) generates \(K\) and determines identity or inversion on that field.
The restriction kernel is \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\), which identifies with \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/(K\cap E))\) by the fixed-field theorem in the cyclotomic Galois extension.
On \(K\) the kernel acts trivially, and on \(E\) its elements commute with cyclotomic conjugation since the cyclotomic group is abelian.
Thus \(\tau\) centralizes the kernel on their compositum and intersects it trivially because it inverts the nontorsion \(\delta\).
Every automorphism of \(F\) lifts to \(F(i)\) by fixing \(i\), using the irreducible \(X^2+1\) with rational coefficients.
Conversely centrality makes all automorphisms of \(L\) preserve \(L^\tau=F\), and restriction has kernel exactly \(\langle\tau\rangle\).
This proves the asserted direct product and quotient independently of whether the fields generated by the points are normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
