## Mathematical finding

The exact minimum for every omission pattern, its realization, and uniqueness of the unrestricted minimizing field are supported by the argument under the stated sufficiently large block-size hypothesis.
No mathematical gap was found in the inspected scope.

For an arbitrary number field \(K\), the image \(H\) of its absolute Galois group in \(\Gamma\) has fixed field \(P\cap K\).
This gives the required lower bound on \([K:\mathbb Q]\) without normality of \(K\) or containment in \(P\).
If an input root is obtainable by radicals over \(K\), its normal closure over \(K\) lies in a finite solvable extension after adjoining the finitely many required roots of unity.
The induced permutation group on the distinguished label's \(H\)-orbit is therefore solvable.
The passage from roots to labels is equivariant, so this conclusion concerns the actual chosen roots and labels.

The subgroup \(D\) fixes all blocks and acts independently and transitively on the labels in each block.
Consequently the orbit of the distinguished label tuple has size \(k\prod_i b_i\), which equals \([F:\mathbb Q]\).
The orbit of its block tuple under \(HD\) has size \(\ell\).
Translates of this orbit cover the \(k\)-element \(\Gamma\)-orbit, proving \([\Gamma:HD]\ge k/\ell\), and \(\ell\le\prod_i\ell_i\).
For \(J=H\cap D\), the inequality
\[
 [D:J]\ge\prod_a[A_{b(a)}:\operatorname{pr}_a(J)]
\]
follows by embedding \(J\) in the product of its coordinate projections; correlations between coordinates cannot reduce this bound.

In each block reached by the distinguished label orbit, a full alternating projection would give a nonsolvable quotient of the solvable permutation action on that orbit.
Every such projection is therefore proper and has index at least the block size.
If \(\ell_i\ge2\), the label field is omitted and the contribution after allocating the orbit denominator is at least
\[
 \frac{b_i^{\ell_i}}{\ell_i}\ge\frac{b_i^2}{2}
 >\binom{b_i}{2}.
\]
The first inequality holds for every integer \(\ell_i\ge2\), since \(b^t/t\) is increasing for integers \(t\ge2\) and \(b\ge2\).

When \(\ell_i=1\), a fixed label gives the required contribution \(b_i\), and is equivalent to \(E_i\subseteq K\).
For a label orbit of size \(2\le s\le b_i-2\), the alternating projection preserves that \(s\)-set, giving index at least \(\binom{b_i}{s}\ge\binom{b_i}{2}\).
For \(s=b_i-1\) or \(b_i\), restriction to the orbit is faithful on the projection, because at most one label remains outside it.
The projection is thus solvable.
Intersecting it with the product of the disjoint supported \(A_5\) subgroups gives a solvable projection to each simple factor, so its index is at least \(5^{\lfloor b_i/5\rfloor}\).
This exceeds \(\binom{b_i}{2}\) for all sufficiently large \(b_i\), as permitted by the statement.
Multiplying these bounds proves the exact asserted lower bound for every omission pattern, including the empty pattern.

For attainment, the tuple of designated singletons and unordered pairs determines its block tuple.
Independent alternating actions give every permitted label or pair choice over each of the \(k\) block tuples, yielding the asserted stabilizer index.
For each pair, a supported product of two disjoint transpositions swaps its distinguished label while preserving the entire tuple.
Hence precisely the designated label fields are omitted.
Every distinguished label has stabilizer orbit of size at most two, so adjoining its field costs at most a quadratic extension of the constructed base.
The checked [single-label construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v281.md#attainment) then recovers each original root by radicals.
Since every nonempty omission pattern increases the degree when \(b_i>3\), while a base with no omissions contains \(F\), equality at the unrestricted minimum forces \(K=F\).

For a single input, \(k\) is its block count and \(kb=v\), giving the two stated special-case degrees.
For an already compressed reciprocal unit, labels are the inverse pairs, and fixing the distinguished pair is equivalent to fixing \(x+x^{-1}\).
Indeed two nonzero elements have the same sum with their inverses precisely when they are equal or inverse.
This verifies the trace-field identification.
