## Uniformity for every fixed positive integer offset

The argument establishes the bound for both signs at every fixed \(d\), including the positive sign for even \(d\).
Its constants can depend on \(d,s,\eta\).

### Limit and harmonic comparison for the positive sign

C345v9 gives the negative-sign theorem under precisely the hypotheses of C345v10.
For the positive sign put \(A=2+z^d\).
The sparse coefficient bound, \(M(\Pi)=2\) and \(q-1\mid C(2)\le2\cdot4^h\) still give bounded circle supremum and \(h\to\infty\) on a putative sequence with \(q\to\infty\).
The common prefix gives the integral limiting series \(A/K\), all its poles being torsion by the integer recurrence argument.
The polynomial \(z^d+2\) is Eisenstein at two.
The reduced numerator of \(K\) contains \(A\) once, since multiplicity greater than one leaves nontorsion poles in \(A/K\), and a numerator with only torsion roots would make \(K\) twice an integer series, contrary to the unit coefficient of \(z^d\) in \(A\).
The other factors \(U,V\) are coprime squarefree cyclotomic products by the same positivity and simple-pole arguments.

With \(K=AU/V\), the identity \(AUB^\sharp-V\Pi=(AU/2)\Pi(J_q-J)\) and \(|A|\ge1\) give exactly the cutoff estimate needed for \(n/h\to0\).
For fixed \(k\ge1,\ l\in\mathbb Z\), both \(kn+l\) and \(kn+l-d\) therefore belong to the prefix.
The positive sign changes the combination of the two coefficients to their sum with weights \(1,1/2\), leaving atomic phase cancellation and conditional positivity intact.
It follows that \(H_k=(1+z^d/2)\sigma c_k\) is an integer Laurent polynomial, \(H_1\ne0\), and \(|H_k|\le D\).
Since \(M(1+z^d/2)=1\), \(M(K)=2\) and \(\sigma=2\rho/|K|^2\), its geometric mean is \(M(D)=M(\rho)/2\).

### Odd primitive offsets and the even phase grid

Reciprocal reversal gives precisely the bracket \(P(w)=(2+w^a)+\delta w^b(2w^a+1)\).
The substitution \(w=z^g\) preserves zero multiplicities on the circle, and the quotient \(U/V\) can change them only at torsion points.
Away from those points the nonnegative density excludes odd orders of vanishing.
Its Mahler measure equals that of \(P\).
For odd \(a\), replacing \(w\) by \(-w\) produces the negative bracket with sign \(\delta(-1)^{b+1}\).
This comparison uses only bracket zeros and Mahler measure, so it also works when \(g\) is even.
The two large-measure cases in the negative primitive list occur at \(a=1\), hence \(g=d\).
For \(b=-1\) the positive bracket is \(4+w+w^{-1}\), which has no circle roots and thus forces \(U=V=1\).
For \(b=0\) it is \(3(1+w)\).
After lifting, the simple zeros of \(1+z^d\) are exactly the roots of \(UV\), and \(\deg V-\deg U=d\) assigns them all to \(V\).
This determines both exceptional limits without having to prescribe how factors split over \(\mathbb Q\) before substitution.

For even \(a\), coprimality implies \(b\) is odd, so \(w\mapsto-w\) interchanges the bracket signs.
Writing \(w^a=-e^{it}\) gives the zero equation \(w^b(2e^{it}-1)/(2-e^{it})=\delta\).
On a torsion zero the earlier torsion equation restricts \(e^{it}\) to \(1,-1,e^{\pm i\pi/3}\).
In units of \(2\pi/a\), the sector \(r\) adds \(rb+b/2\) to the continuous phase.
The terms \(rb\) exhaust the integer classes modulo \(a\), \(b/2\) is a half-integer, and changing the target sign adds the integer \(a/2\).
Thus the union of target levels is \(\mathbb Z+1/2\) for either sign.
A strict straddling of a level in an open arc forces a sign-changing phase crossing and hence an odd-order bracket zero there.
The prohibited open arcs contain none of the allowed torsion values of \(e^{it}\).

I recomputed the sequence of endpoint levels \(0,(a+l)/6,l/2,(5l-a)/6,l\), \(l=a+b\).
Its increments give \(|l|\le4,\ |a+l|\le6,\ |2l-a|\le6\), whence \(3a=2(a+l)-(2l-a)\le18\), with equality requiring \(l=0\).
Testing the four possible odd \(l\) with exact rational arithmetic leaves only \(l=-1,1\) at \(a=2\) and \(l=-1\) at \(a=4\).
I factored each surviving positive bracket after clearing \(w^b\).
For \((2,-3)\) the quartic factor is \(w^4-w^3+3w^2-w+1\), the signed substitution of the previously checked quartic of measure below \(5/2\).
For \((2,-1)\) the factors \(w+1,\ w^2+w+1\) both have measure one.
For \((4,-5)\) I verified the stated trace identity and the cubic discriminant \(-31\).
The cubic \(X^3-2X^2-X+3\) takes the values \(-11,1\) at \(-2,-1\), and none of \(\pm1,\pm3\) is a root.
Its real root in that interval is simple and has nonreal conjugates, excluding a torsion trace.
The two preimages are simple, away from \(w=\pm1\), and do not meet the other factors, whose trace roots are \(-2,-1\).
They give forbidden simple nontorsion bracket zeros.
Every remaining even primitive case therefore has \(M(P)<2\sqrt2\).

### Exceptional envelopes and final contradiction

For \(M(P)<2\sqrt2\), C345v9's separated-block estimate gives finitely many shapes up to monomial shift.
For \(K=2+z^d\), the squared envelope is \((4+X)^2/(5+2X)\), \(X=z^d+z^{-d}\).
Its mean \(7/2\), bound four, and equality only at \(z^d=\pm1\) are preserved by replacing the circle variable \(w=z^d\) by \(-w\).
The fourth-moment and alignment tests leave only unit monomials and binomials, the latter with gap \(l\le2d\) since all \(l\) alignment points must map to \(\pm1\).
Here \(M(D)=(2+\sqrt3)/2<2\).
For \(K=(2+z^d)/(1+z^d)\), I obtain \(D=3|1+z^d|^2/|2+z^d|\).
All \(d\) simple roots of \(1+z^d\) force at least quadratic vanishing of \(H_k\), giving integral Laurent divisibility by the square.
The quotient \(P_0\) has squared norm at most three.
Equality would force \((2+z^d)P_0\) to be a nonzero constant-modulus Laurent monomial, impossible because it has a nonzero root.
A quotient binomial with gap \(l\ne d\) produces four distinct exponents \(0,l,d,l+d\) after shifting, giving squared norm ten for the product against a bound of nine.
This leaves finitely many shapes and gives \(M(D)=3/2<2\).

For each nonzero shape \(L\), \(M(L)\le M(D)<2=M(2+z^d)\), so \(L\) does not vanish at any root \(\alpha\) of \(2+z^d\) by irreducibility.
The conjugate-phase numerator at \(\alpha\) is nonzero because \(2+\alpha^{-d}=3/2\).
Its phase ratio thus has a pole at \(\alpha\), and the squaring identity would propagate it to all \(\alpha^{2^j}\), of distinct increasing moduli.
This proves the nonidentity needed in C345v9's prime specialization.
At sufficiently large prime order all conjugate points are regular with positive density, and every nonzero conditional moment there is nonreal by the phase ratio test.
The rational density and integer Laurent rows still give integer Fourier–Stieltjes coordinate sequences after clearing one common cyclotomic denominator.
Eventual periodicity and positivity of the progression averages force all sufficiently high rows to vanish.
Pushing through the greatest nonzero harmonic then gives \(1\le M(H_r)\le M(D)/2<1\).
This excludes every positive-sign candidate.

Finally, expanding the two Chebyshev sums gives \(2\pm z^d+z^{2h+2-d}(\mp1-2z^d)\).
For \(h\ge d\) the trailing index exceeds \(h\), so \(s=4,\eta=1\) suffice for these examples.
No parity restriction on \(f\) enters the argument.
The branch derivative identity gives \(\theta-q\ge2/(q+3)\), yielding the stated uniform positive excess from bounded \(q\).
