## Arbitrary valence, both spectral endpoints and effective subdivision bounds

With \(x=2\cosh t\), the Dirichlet matrix of any eliminated finite path stays positive definite for all \(t\ge0\).
For each set of fixed endpoint values \(u\), the full quadratic form at \(t\) exceeds the one at zero by at least \((x-2)\|u\|^2\) before minimizing.
This proves the lower Schur comparison without any bound on the number of internal coordinates.
The difference recurrence on a path of length \(\ell\) gives the diagonal ratio \(-\sinh((\ell-1)t)/\sinh(\ell t)\) and off-diagonal ratio \(-\sinh t/\sinh(\ell t)\).
Their sum and difference reproduce the two stated channels, including the direct edge \(\ell=1\).
At zero, the only length-dependent part is the positive rank-one form with coefficient \(1/\ell\).
For the antisymmetric channel, isolate \(2(\sinh t/t-1)/\ell\); the remaining \(\coth u-1/u\) is between zero and one because \(\tanh u\le u\) and \(e^{2u}-1\ge2u\).
The bound \(t^2+2t+2t^2\le4t\) for \(0<t\le1/2\) controls this channel and also the symmetric channel.
For a free endpoint the appended Dirichlet zero gives \(a_r(t)\).
The finite geometric sums in its expression prove \(a_r(t)\ge e^{-t}r/(r+1)\), and monotonicity in \(r\) gives the independent lower bound \(1/(2\cosh t)\).

For fixed branch count, the suppressed branch-to-branch graph has bounded finite size and all other components are independent pendant paths.
After fixing that graph, a simultaneous subsequence with each of the finitely many connecting lengths nondecreasing exists by successive selection.
A bounded pendant count can be fixed, and its sorted tuple of lengths treated the same way.
An unbounded count can first be made divergent, and then all divergent counts can be made to triple simultaneously at each step because there are finitely many vertices.
At such a vertex the later sum of the \(a_r(t)\)'s exceeds the old count for \(t\le1/2\), since \(2\cosh(1/2)<3\).
This bounds the later diagonal by the old one at zero even if every new arm is shorter than the old arms.
Fixed-count arms increase the reduced diagonal relative to the old one by at most \(t\) each, and the bounded number of connecting edges contributes at most \(4t\) per edge.
Thus the comparison constant in (1) can depend on the chosen subsequence and is uniform in its indices.

Order the Schur eigenvalues increasingly.
Their negative count at zero is nondecreasing by the first comparison and is eventually some \(h\le b\).
The strictly positive shift in the lower comparison at \(t_n>0\) makes all positions above \(h\) positive, including any zero modes at the endpoint.
The second comparison against one fixed later index keeps the first \(h\) positions strictly negative once \(t_n\) is small.
The argument also covers \(h=0\) and \(h\) equal to the full matrix size.
This contradicts singularity for any proposed positive-endpoint sequence, and bipartiteness reflects every tree eigenvalue to its negative.

For a fixed graph the signed recurrence for the negative endpoint results from putting all the signs along a path at its last edge, whose sign then equals \((-1)^\ell\).
The zero block's rank-one part has norm \(2/\ell\) for either sign and its limit as \(\ell\to\infty\) is independent of the sign.
