## Enumeration, positive moment tests and explicit controls

At 2026-11-16T15:45 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin completed inspection of the [six-term certificate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v63.md#exclusion), the [program and reproduction commands](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0163/readme.md#six-term-search) and the full [enumeration source](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0163/search.py).
The reviewer did not participate in the search or its design and received the fixed materials without development dialogue, author explanations or an earlier verdict.
The review covers the finite exclusion for \(2\le h\le26,\ q\ge77,\ 1\le d<e\le h,\ a,b=\pm1\), both endpoint parity choices, the exact rejection tests and the positive controls.

At \(2\) the Chebyshev numerator is \(2m+a(m-d)+b(m-e)>0\), with maximum \(4m-3\).
Its qualified divisors give exactly the positive integers \(u\mid C(2)\) with \(C(2)/u\ge76\).
For \(h\le26\) this leaves \(u=1\), and the maximum for \(h\le18\) is \(73\).
I independently enumerated the sign and offset choices from this single inequality.
There are 726 with \(a=b=1\).
For mixed signs the only possibility is \(a=1,b=-1\), with \(2m+e-d\ge76\) giving one pair at \(h=25\) and ten at \(h=26\).

For every possible Pisot input the corrected measure is positive and retains the two distinct lifted atoms above each of \(h\) interior roots of \(C\).
Its Toeplitz quadratic form is the integral of a squared polynomial of degree at most \(2h-1\), hence strictly positive for nonzero coefficient vectors in the tested range.
The program includes all five nonconstant terms of the lift in the recurrence for \(j_k\), including the reflected negative terms when their indices are reached.
The scale \(2^{2h+1}\operatorname{lcm}(2(q-1),2(q+1))\) clears both endpoint weights and all dyadic coefficients \(j_k/2\) through \(2h-1\).
For the next moment the two by two Schur complement has diagonal \(D_{n+1}/D_n\) and off-diagonal \(X/D_n\), in the [determinant recursion](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v53.md#recursion).
I checked its determinant and bordered prediction-vector update against the code, including the empty predictor with \(D_0=1\).
The coefficient of the proposed integer in \(X\) is \(D_n S/2>0\), and the lower and upper floor divisions implement the two strict inequalities even at equality or a negative endpoint.
There is no independent coefficient cutoff.

The free integer coefficients through \(m=h+1\) parameterize the reciprocal monic lift uniquely, since \(z^k+z^{-k}\) is integral and monic of degree \(k\) in the trace variable.
The two code evaluations at the center equal \(B(2)\) and \(B(-2)\).
The sign of \(C(-2)\) is \((-1)^h\), so the second test is exactly \(2B(-2)/C(-2)\le-2/(q+1)\).
All later integer choices are the forced mirror coefficients.
At the terminal test the negative-remainder chain eliminates any nontrivial common divisor, and the endpoint numerators \(C(\pm2)\) are nonzero.
Variation can drop by at most one at each distinct interior root of \(C\).
Achieving the degree \(h\) as total drop thus entails \(h\) distinct simple roots, each with positive \(-B/C'\).
Conversely those roots and signs give total drop \(h\).
The equality flag selects the appropriate power of \(1+z\) in the [exact inverse](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse), and the code divides by \(1-z^2\) exactly.

I reproduced the two degree-19 cases and the independent rational root-interval benchmark using the recorded commands.

For its displayed reciprocal lift I also constructed the scaled Toeplitz matrices directly from rational moments, independently of the predictor recurrence.

The simple descending zero of \(C\) in \((11/10,23/20)\) and strict negativity of \(B\) there give the same rejection by the residue sign.
At \(q=78\) the 312 free prefixes give nine endpoint survivors and none survives index 27.

At \(h=2,q=10\) the search returns two inputs.

The positive residues at \(-1,1/2\) require \(A+G\le-4\) and \(30A+12G>-71\), and the second endpoint gives \(G\ge-4\).
The integer possibilities are \((A,G,H)=(-1,-3,1),(0,-4,-1)\).
For the first, the two residues are \(4/3,5/12\) and the strict second-endpoint ratio is \(-2\), yielding the sextic printed in the ledger.
