## Independent review of localization and finite-degree interpolation

At 2026-11-18T14:41 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [whole fixed argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v182.md#localization) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed version and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or earlier findings on this argument and had independently reviewed the cited Hardy-space comparison.
The localization inequalities and the interpolation limit hold, including the endpoint normalization and rational-coefficient specialization.

In the positive-shift orientation \(F=A(x^p)+\sigma x^S A^*(x^p)\), reality and coprimality exclude a circle root of either row, since its conjugate is its inverse.
The shift is \(S>0\) after a possible row exchange, and the row integral is \(m(A)\).
At a unit-circle double root \(a\), division by the nonvanishing reversed row makes \(A(x^p)/A^*(x^p)\) agree with \(-\sigma x^S\) in value and first derivative.
The angular derivative of its argument with respect to \(\arg x\) is therefore \(S\), which gives \(S/p\) with respect to the powered argument.
The constant sign contributes zero to this derivative.
For each factor \((X-\gamma)/(1-\bar\gamma X)\) at \(\beta=a^p\) the angular derivative is \((1-|\gamma|^2)/|\beta-\gamma|^2\).
The exterior roots contribute negatively, so the sum restricted to interior roots is at least \(S/p>0\).
For those roots \(1-|\gamma|^2\le -2\log|\gamma|\), and Jensen's formula sums the right sides to \(2(m(A)-\log|A(0)|)=2L\).
The nearest interior root therefore satisfies (1), and strict disjointness of the radius-\(\eta/2\) disks under the negation of (2) gives the counting bound.
This lower bound concerns \(L\) for unnormalized real rows and equals a bound on \(I\) with \(|A(0)|=1\).
For integral rows the nonzero endpoint gives \(I\ge L\).

For the interpolation problem the \(t\) powered nodes are distinct, are closed under conjugation and inversion, and omit zero and all roots of unity.
Their reciprocal pairing makes \(g\) monic reciprocal of degree \(N=2t\) with \(g(0)=1\).
The \(2t\) Hermite data determine \(T\) uniquely below degree \(N\), with real coefficients by conjugation.
Since \(w(a)+a^K w(1/a)=0\), the value of the diagonal vanishes at each mark.
In its Euler derivative the reversed term contributes \(a^K(KA_\epsilon(1/\beta)-p\beta^{-1}A_\epsilon'(1/\beta))\), with the minus sign coming from \(x^{-p}\).
The derivative data reduce the full expression to \(\epsilon K(w(a)+a^K w(1/a))/2=0\).

Reversing to the fixed degree \(N\) gives \(T^*(\beta)=-a^{-r}w(a)\).
Here \(w(a)\ne0\) and \(a^r\ne-1\), so \(T-T^*\) is a nonzero polynomial.
For \(\epsilon\ne0\) a common root of the rows must be among its finitely many roots.
At every root \(\beta\) of \(g\) the row has value \(\epsilon w(a)\ne0\), and each remaining candidate excludes at most one scalar by the affine equation \(g(z)+\epsilon T(z)=0\).
Consequently a sequence tending to zero can avoid every excluded scalar.

The unscaled row \(A_\epsilon\) is monic of degree \(N\), and its constant tends to one.
For all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), this nonzero constant is both the constant of the diagonal and its leading coefficient at \(pN+r\), because it is the top coefficient of the reversed row.
One scalar division therefore gives both diagonal endpoints one, while both row degrees remain \(N\) and the two distinct residue classes remain occupied.
Their moduli agree on the circle and yield \(I=m(A_\epsilon)-\log|A_\epsilon(0)|\).
Continuity of the monic root multiset gives the claimed limit \(m(g)=2p\sum_a\log^+|a|\).
In particular each normalized circle-mark witness has \(I=L>0\) by localization, while the degree-\(2t\) sequence approaches zero.
For fixed separated marks at degrees below \(t\), the root-count bound applies to the normalized cost.
The intermediate degrees remain undecided.

For the conjugates of a reciprocal nontorsion unit, degree preservation makes powering injective on the conjugate set, and every automorphism of its splitting field preserves the interpolation data.
Uniqueness therefore puts \(T\), as well as \(g\), in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
Choosing rational scalars outside the same finite set and normalizing by a nonzero rational constant gives rational diagonals divisible by \(P^2\).
Their row integrals satisfy \(I/p\to2m(P)\) at the fixed input and their degree \(2pd+r\) is strictly below \(p(16d+1)\).
The cited integral comparison uses the indicated prime window and integral coefficients, while the construction meets the numerical degree cap even at other degree-preserving primes.
Clearing denominators can change the fixed endpoints and the measure.
