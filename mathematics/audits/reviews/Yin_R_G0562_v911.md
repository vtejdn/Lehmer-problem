## Independent review of the statement and proof

At 2026-11-21T05:31 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete fixed [C345 v769](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer prepared the Ballaÿ–Sombra and Chambert-Loir–Thuillier source inspections, including the hypotheses of the published global-height theorems, and had no part in constructing the project argument or formulating its claim.
The review checked the applicability of these theorems and the interfaces to the accepted factor bounds, torsion theorem and Boyd–Lawton limit.

For \(P=(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)\), a monomial has torsion value precisely when its exponent vector annihilates \((a,b)\).
Thus a fixed \(P\) with \(z\) nontorsion determines one primitive direction up to sign, irrespective of the translations, their fields and the common dilation.
A sequence with \(N/g\to\infty\) eventually avoids any prescribed finite set, including singularities and intersections with proper support subsums.
For the latter, divisibility of a subsum by \(F\) would force the Newton polytope of its quotient to be a point at the origin, by the additivity of all support functions and containment in the original polygon.
Galois transitivity then excludes shared geometric components.
It also shows that a geometric component with a constant monomial would make every component a binomial with the same exponent direction, contradicting rank two.
Each sum at a projected cut is nonzero and its two product-formula bounds use the original coefficients at all places since every phase has absolute value one.
The resulting \(Nh(z)\ll_F1\) is independent of the field degree.

The coordinate divisor vectors on a fixed smooth component are independent over the reals because an integer matrix has the same rank over \(\mathbf Q\) and \(\mathbf R\), and rational dependence would produce a constant monomial.
The negative part of their nonzero directional combination has positive degree.
Rational directions give scaled pullbacks of the sup metric for a morphism to \(\mathbf P^1\).
For irrational directions the ratio of the two coefficients is irrational, so no nonzero pair of boundary orders has zero pairing.
Fixing the coefficient of modulus one and bracketing the other coefficient by rationals keeps every boundary sign constant.
The weighted average of the two resulting geometric divisors equals the fixed real divisor exactly.

Indeed, near any marked point, the two rational functions are powers of a parameter times analytic units with bounded logarithms.
On sufficiently small neighborhoods the diverging term selects the same branch of the maximum for all sufficiently close directions.
Removing the local divisor singularity leaves a bounded unit expression converging uniformly.
On the compact complement ordinary uniform convergence follows from bounded coordinate logarithms.
This also works on the Berkovich curve.
After passing to a fixed number field and omitting finitely many places, smooth reduction with distinct marked sections and no vertical part in either principal divisor gives unit local factors on their residue domains.
At each such place the weighted approximating metrics and the limiting metric all come from the fixed horizontal real divisor on one model.
At an exceptional finite place, pulling the two standard models back to a common model where the rational maps extend gives two relatively nef real divisors, and their nonnegative weighted sum remains relatively nef.
At infinity the same averaging preserves plurisubharmonicity in local equations.
This proves [semipositivity on the fixed divisor](../literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#adelic-divisors).
The height comparison at a boundary point follows too, since the fixed-divisor differences of Green functions vanish at good places and converge uniformly to zero at the remaining places.
Every rational approximating height is nonnegative there.

For the moving points the Green-function sum is \(|sa+tb|h(z)\), which tends to zero.
Eventual avoidance of finite sets gives genericity on the one chosen component, including after conjugating the points to it and lifting to the normalization.
Nonnegativity everywhere gives equality of the essential and absolute minima at zero.
The degree of the limiting geometric divisor is positive.
Therefore the [global-height theorem](../literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#equidistribution-height) applies to the fixed coordinate pullback; its point height is \(Nh(z)\).
The common limit depends only on the limiting direction, the chosen maximal coordinate and the component.
Conjugate components give the same height for a conjugated point.
A violating selection from arbitrarily translated sections would be another generic small sequence on the same component, so the convergence along this subsequence is uniform in roots and translations.
For rational directions the nonconstant morphism of projective curves pulls back \(\mathcal O(1)\) to an ample bundle, and scaling back gives the hypotheses of [Lemme 6.1](../literature/CLT09/Yin_U001.md#height-convergence).

In each section the map on the parameter has kernel of order \(g\).
The curve has finitely many torsion points and a Vandermonde matrix on the distinct exponents bounds root multiplicity by the number of support points minus one.
Hence the aggregate torsion multiplicity is \(O_F(g)\), with \(g/W\to0\).
In the untwisted primitive integer section, summing primitive factor degrees times their root heights gives the full logarithmic measure, including the contribution of a nonmonic leading coefficient through the absolute height.
The ratio \(W/N\) is a continuous function of the normalized direction.
Multiplying the common \(Nh(z)\) limit by this ratio and using the Boyd–Lawton measure of the untwisted sections determines \(Wh(z)\to m(F)\) for every translated choice on the subsequence.
The positive untwisted lower bound gives strict positivity, and compactness upgrades the conclusion to all long directions.

At each finite place some original coefficient is a unit by primitivity, and support injectivity and multiplication by roots of unity preserve the normalized Gauss norm.
After the monomial shift, the product over the \(k\) embeddings therefore has degree \(kW\), integer coefficients and content one.
Coincident conjugate sections and multiple factors contribute with their full multiplicities.
Only \(O_F(kg)\) of this degree is torsion, and all the individual nontorsion height errors are uniform before summation.
Consequently the total measure divided by \(k\) tends to \(m(F)\) and \(m(S)=(m(F)+o_F(1))\deg S/W\).
The latter gives the stated criterion even with unbounded \(k\).
[Habegger's Theorems 1 and 4](../literature/Hab09/Yin_U001.md#hab09-u001) already establish the limit and a rate for \(X+Y=1\).
The general-curve assertion here supplies a qualitative limit for each fixed rank-two plane curve.
