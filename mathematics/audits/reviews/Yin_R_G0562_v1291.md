### Multiplicities and constants

Since the leading coefficient of \(Q\) survives reduction, the reversal of \(\bar Q=X^tT\) in degree \(r\) is exactly the reversal of \(T\) in degree \(r-t\).
Thus \(T^*=\sigma T\) gives the binomial \(X^N-s\) with \(N=n+t\) and \(s=-\varepsilon\sigma\), including coincident signs in characteristic two.
For each irreducible factor of the binomial its multiplicity minus the multiplicity in the gcd with \(\bar P\) is at most its multiplicity in \(\overline{D/P}\).
The degree-weighted sum of these deficits is therefore at most \(c\).
In the power basis the kernel of \(sB^N-I\) has dimension equal to this gcd degree, and each auxiliary factor \(V(Z)\) used in the proof kills that kernel modulo \(p\).
In Smith form at least \(K\) diagonal entries of each factor are divisible by \(p\).
Every eigenvalue of \(Z\) is a signed \(N\)-th power of a root of \(P\), hence is nontorsion by irreducibility and noncyclotomy.
This ensures the integer determinants are nonzero, so the valuations add for products of the auxiliary factors, even at ramified primes. [Companion and Smith calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0904_v2.md#modular)

For a degree-\(l\) polynomial \(U\), apply the disk maximum bound to \(U\) and \(z^l U(1/z)\) to get \(|U(w)|\le\|U\|_\infty\max(1,|w|)^l\).
Multiplication over the \(d\) conjugates gives \(M(P)^{Nl}\) as stated.
At two, the five factors have total degree six and the squared circle norm is the maximum of \(64a^2(1-a)\) for \(0\le a\le1\).
The maximum \(256/27\) occurs at \(a=2/3\).
At three both factors of \(Z^3-1=(Z-1)(Z^2+Z+1)\) vanish at one, and at larger primes \(Z-1\) contributes once.
The last two circle norms are two.
For \(P=D\), \(K=N\) and \(d/N=1+(r-t)/N<2\), which gives strict lower bounds \((27/8)^{1/6}\), \((9/4)^{1/3}\) and \(p/4\).
The first equals \(\sqrt{3/2}\) and \(5/4>\sqrt{3/2}\).
For \(c=o(N)\), \(K/N\to1\) and \(d/N<2\).
Once \(K>0\), using \(\log p\ge\log5\) in the last bound gives an error uniform over all \(p\ge5\), so taking the minimum of the three limiting lower bounds gives the claimed liminf.

### The record ceiling and the degree-ten calculation

Exact evaluation gives \(16^{10}L(19/16)=330753263801\), \(L(1)=-1\) and \(19^2<3\cdot16^2/2\).
The source identifies the unique exterior root with the measure, so records in the stated degree range have \(M(D)\le M(L)<\sqrt{3/2}\).
At height one the extreme nonzero exponents survive modulo two and all surviving coefficients equal one there, so reflection of the support would force reversal.
In the nonreversal case the cited proof uses an identity in the quotient by the full gcd, retaining repeated factors, and bounds its degree by \(2r+2\sqrt{rj}\) independently of \(p\).
Solving for \(j\) gives the positive-part formula, and division by \(d\) for \(j\le Ad\) gives \(2r/d+2\sqrt{Ar/d}\).
The fixed ceiling \(M(L)<\sqrt{3/2}\) also supplies the strict separation needed in the liminf application to a sequence of record factors. [Nonreversal bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0904_v3.md#finite-field) · [Lehmer polynomial and degree table](../literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree)

The middle exponent lies strictly between \(r\) and \(n\) when \(n+r\) is even and \(n>r\).

### The averaged radial estimate

Removing a genuine initial power of \(X\) preserves \(D\), the exterior-root count and total degree, while increasing \(n\).
If every remaining irreducible factor of \(Q\) were its signed reversal, their product would be so too and \(D\) would have the binomial factor \(X^n\pm1\).
Smyth's bound therefore applies to an irreducible factor with nonzero constant term and gives \(m(Q)\ge\log\theta\), hence \(k\ge1\). [Smyth's theorem and its conventions](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions)

The squared numerator minus squared denominator of each root ratio is \((|z|^2-1)(|\beta|^2-1)\).
For an exterior root the largest ratio occurs at the minimal distance to \(\beta\), on its radial line.
Writing \(x=\log|\beta|\) and \(y=\log|z|\), the logarithmic excess of the hyperbolic-sine ratio over \(\log((x+y)/|x-y|)\) is at most \(\min(x,y)\), by integrating the stated derivative bound.
For \(T\le y\le2T\) and \(x/T\ge4\), the ratio \((x+y)/|x-y|\) is at most three.
Otherwise the positive logarithmic singularity has integral at most two in the rescaled variable \(y/T\), so the mean bound \(\log6+2\) is independent of \(x,T\).
Summing before choosing the radius gives a common \(y\) outside the finite set of poles, with the required bound even for repeated exterior roots.

With \(T=u/k\), any \(C_\lambda>(C_0+2u+\log(1/\delta))/u\) suffices in the contradiction.
For a monic \(F\), the circle mean of \(\log |F|\) is \((\deg F)y+s_y(F)\), so the degree terms cancel exactly when comparing \(D\) with \(z^nQ\).
Each exterior root of \(Q\) loses at most \(y\) when passing from \(m(Q)\) to \(s_y(Q)\), while every other root contributes zero to both.
The two losses \(ky\le2u<\Delta/4\) and \(-\log(1-\delta)<\Delta/4\) leave \(m(D)>\log\lambda\).
Finally \(d=n+r<2n\) gives the asserted \(\sqrt d\) lower bound for \(k\).
This proves the restriction for complete irreducible presentations and leaves open the existence of an unbounded sequence of the specified records.
