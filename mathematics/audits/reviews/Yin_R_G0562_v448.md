The examples establish the claimed sharp individual-height scale at bounded \(\rho\) and the need for collision orders growing with the support.

### Height control of the first harmonic

The discriminant estimate of v114 applies to arbitrary nonzero algebraic numbers with their own degree \(n\).
For a nontorsion unit, Dobrowolski's lower bound, also available in Voutier's formulation, gives \(nt\gg (j(n)/\ell(n))^3\) for large degrees.
In bounded degree there are finitely many candidates of bounded measure, so positivity of the height of each nontorsion unit supplies a common positive lower bound for \(nt\).
For a nonunit, either the leading or the constant coefficient of its primitive minimal polynomial has absolute value at least two, giving \(nt\ge\log 2\).
These bounds supply the same inequality with an absolute constant for all the nontorsion ratios used here.

For \(n\ge 1/t\), the decrease of \(\log(3n)/n\) gives \(\log(3n)/n\le t\log(3/t)\).
For \(n<1/t\), the function \(y\mapsto\log(e+y)/y\) decreases, so the bound on \(nt\) gives \(\log(3n)/n\ll t\ell(1/t)^4/j(1/t)^3\).
The latter scale also dominates the first case, \(t\), and the additive error after taking the square root in (3), for sufficiently small \(t\).
This gives (4).
For an order-\(q\) root of unity, inversion on divisors gives the first harmonic \(\mu(q)/\varphi(q)\), including \(q=1\).
Joint-field averaging uses constant extension multiplicity for each embedding of the ratio field.

### Two ways to control the radial error

After coefficient normalization the diagonal sum in the squared phase expansion is one and the total absolute weight on ordered off-diagonal pairs is \(\rho-1\).
The fixed real normalization scales the rational relation at every embedding by exactly the same constant.
At an embedding, subtract the zero relation divided by the maximum term modulus \(M\).
Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives a squared phase sum at most \(\|c\|_1\sum_i |c_i|(1-r_i)^2\).
For each \(i\), summing \(\log(M/|u_i|)\) over all places with the height weights gives \(A\) by the product formula, and every summand is nonnegative.
The archimedean average is therefore at most \(A\).
Using \((1-r)^2\le-\log r\) proves \(D\le\rho A\).

Dividing all terms by \(u_1\) preserves the squared phase sum.
For \(t_i=\log|\sigma(u_i/u_1)|\), both positive and negative parts have embedding means at most \(\Delta\), including for nonunits.
With \(T=\tfrac12\log(1+s)\), Markov's inequality and the union bound limit the exceptional contribution by \(2\rho s\Delta/T\).
On the remaining embeddings convexity of \((e^x-1)^2\) on \([0,T]\) gives a bound \((e^T-1)^2|t_i|/T\) for \(|e^{t_i}-1|^2\), also for \(t_i<0\).
Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz then gives at most \(2\rho\Delta(e^T-1)^2/T\).
Since \((\sqrt{1+s}-1)^2\le s\), addition yields (6) with its factor eight.

In the absence of a qualifying collision, each torsion correlation is zero or has absolute value below \(1/(2(\rho-1))\), so their weighted sum in absolute value is at most one half.
Each nontorsion correlation is bounded by \(Cg(v)\) for sufficiently small \(v\) since \(g\) is increasing near zero.
This applies with \(v=A\) by projective coordinate projection and with \(v=2\Delta\) by the quotient height inequality.
It proves (7), including the torsion-only case \(v=0\) with no nontorsion error.
For small \(a>0\), putting \(v=a b(x)\) gives \(x\sqrt v=\sqrt a\,j(x)^{3/2}/\ell(x)^2\).
The bounds \(\ell(1/v)\ll(1+|\log a|)\ell(x)\) and \(j(1/v)\gg j(x)\) then give \(xg(v)\ll\sqrt a(1+|\log a|)^2\) uniformly in \(x\ge1\), while \(xb(x)\) stays bounded.
Taking \(a\) smaller also handles \(2\Delta\) in the second use.
Thus (5) or (6), respectively, contradicts (7) below the corresponding threshold in (1).
Here \(\ell(s)/\log(1+s)\) is bounded, and for \(\rho\le s\) both \(b(\rho)\) and \(\ell(s)/(\rho s)\) dominate \(b(s)\) up to absolute factors.
The coordinatewise minimal exponent shift bounds the local maximum by the indicated product at every place, proving (2) for arbitrary Laurent exponents.
Since \(j(s)\) is bounded below and \(\ell(s)\asymp\log(2s)\), the stated cruder width bound follows as well.

### Unit coordinates and varying rank

Each marginal exceptional embedding fraction equals the corresponding fraction in the coordinate's own field, so the union bound gives at most \(p\) in the joint field.
On the complementary embeddings the unit monomials all have modulus one and their phase sum vanishes.
Thus \(D\le\rho p\).
For a nontorsion ratio of degree \(n\), Kronecker supplies at least one off-circle conjugate, and every embedding producing such a conjugate belongs to the exceptional set.
It follows that \(1/n\le p\), independently of intersections among the coordinate fields.
For \(0<p\le1\), (3) and \(h(u_i/u_j)\le H\) give the correlation bound of the proof with \(L=H+p\log(3/p)\).
When \(L\le1\), both \(p\) and \(H\) are at most \(\sqrt L\), so the normalized Gram inequality forces \(L\gg\rho^{-2}\).
For \(L>1\) this holds directly.
When \(p=0\) all ratios are torsion, and \(D=0\) forces a qualifying collision by the same expansion.

For \(0<p\le1\), write \(v=1/(p\rho^2 K)\).
Dividing the second bound of (9) by \(pK\) gives \(v\ll t/p+\log(3/p)/K\).
With \(K\ge1\) and at most \((K+1)^r\) distinct lattice exponents, \(\log(3/p)/K\ll1+r+\log^+v\).
Absorbing the last logarithm yields \(v\ll1+r+t/p\), which is the first bound of (9).
It is immediate for \(p>1\).
For Salem coordinates the exact fraction is \(2/d_j\) and the height is \(\log(\sigma_j)/d_j\).
Substitution gives (8) and its two-coordinate specialization.
At the specified real embedding all monomial values are positive, so a torsion ratio would equal one, precisely an equality of two values.
This verifies the stated extension to dependent tuples with distinct values.

### Examples and comparison

For even \(n\ge4\), \(X^n-nX^{n-1}-2\) is Eisenstein at two and the strict boundary inequality \(n>3\) gives \(n-1\) interior zeros by Rouché's theorem.
The change of sign between \(n\) and \(n+1\) locates the unique exterior root.
A torsion quotient of distinct roots would give equal moduli after an automorphism carrying its numerator to this root, a contradiction, and no individual root is torsion.
The trace relation with \(u_1=1\) has coefficient norms giving \(\rho=(2n)^2/(n^2+n)\).
Its relative height is \(\log\alpha/n\), and the archimedean maximum at every embedding is \(\alpha\), with finite-place maximum one.
Thus the relative height is of order \(\log(2s)/s\) with bounded \(\rho\), exactly the asserted sharpness.
For the sum over a prime-order cyclic group all distinct ratios have order \(p\), with \(\varphi(p)=\rho-1\).
No proper nonempty subsum vanishes, since its indicator polynomial of degree at most \(p-1\) would have to be a scalar multiple of \(\Phi_p\).
The Amoroso–Viada hyperplane threshold is invoked at its recorded statement scope for nondegenerate relations, and the cited equal-coefficient family gives projective height \(O(1/s)\) with every distinct ratio nontorsion.
These comparisons leave the optimal projective bound at growing effective support unresolved.
