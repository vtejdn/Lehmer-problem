# Review of the mixed-index valuation bounds and the uniform factor gaps

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T01:50 (UTC), an agent working for Sheng Yin independently checked the fixed C345 v712 proof and recommends acceptance of all four statements.
The reviewer did not develop v712 or receive its development discussion, an author explanation or a prior verdict on it.
The reviewer had developed other radical estimates, including v702, and had reviewed the earlier J182 exceptional-prime orbit computation.
This review used the submitted claim and its specified mathematical inputs, reconstructing the valuation and height arguments for the exponents and multiplicities in v712.

**Multiplicities at odd primes.**
In the notation of the claim, powering by \(t=q/\ell^{v_\ell(q)}\) sends the full reduced multiset of the radical polynomial to that of \(P_c\).
The source multiplicity is constant on a joint Frobenius and inversion orbit, since the reduced polynomial is both defined over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) and reciprocal.
The surjection from one such orbit of order-\(m\) points to its image orbit of order-\(s\) points has constant fiber size \(L_m/L_s\).
At each fixed target point its multiplicity is the sum of these costs over the contributing source orbits, each multiplied by its integral number of copies.
Consequently a common prime divisor of all the costs must divide the target multiplicity.

With \(b=v_p(N)>0\), remove the \(\ell\)-power from \(N\).
The resulting separable binomial gives multiplicity exactly \(\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\) at each of its roots.
When \(\eta=1\), or when the sign disappears in characteristic two, every primitive \(p^b\)-th root belongs to this binomial.
For \(\eta=-1\) in odd characteristic, a primitive root of order \(2^{v_2(N)+1}p^b\) raised to \(N/\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\) has order two and is therefore \(-1\).
All selected orders exceed two, so adding \(X-c\) where applicable and deleting the exact linear cofactor cannot alter the target multiplicity.
In particular it is prime to \(p\), including when the binomial is inseparable before removing the \(\ell\)-power.

Writing \(f=v_p(q)\), the order identity \(s=m/\gcd(m,t)\) forces \(v_p(m)=b+f\) for every preimage because \(v_p(s)>0\).
Let \(o=\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)\) and \(w=v_p(\ell^o-1)\).
The binomial expansion at odd \(p\) gives \(v_p((\ell^o)^u-1)=w+v_p(u)\), and \(p\nmid o\).
This gives \(v_p(\operatorname{ord}_{p^j}(\ell))=(j-w)_+\).
The additional dyadic component at the target contributes no odd \(p\)-part to the subgroup order, by the Chinese remainder theorem, and adjoining inversion changes that order by at most a factor of two.
It follows that \(v_p(L_s)=(b-w)_+\), while reducing modulo \(p^{b+f}\) gives \(v_p(L_m)\ge(b+f-w)_+\).
If \(b+f>w\) the difference is positive, both for \(b\le w\) and for \(b>w\).
This contradicts the target multiplicity, proving (1).
The example uses \(v_3(2^2-1)=1\) at the appropriate even or odd seed.
For an index \(\ell^e p^f\), the prime-power bound on \(\gamma^{p^f}\) and \(f\le w-b\) give the asserted height consequence with \(\delta=\log(5/2)/6\).

**The complementary prime two.**
At odd characteristic the binomial contains a primitive \(2^k\)-th root for \(k=v_2(N)+[\eta=-1]\).
For \(k\ge2\) neither linear correction touches the root, whose multiplicity is odd.
Exactly one of \(\ell,-\ell\) is \(1\bmod4\), and its difference from one has valuation \(w_2=\max(v_2(\ell-1),v_2(\ell+1))\).
Squaring increases this valuation by one, and odd powers preserve it.
The generated subgroup modulo \(2^j\), \(j\ge2\), has order \(2^{(j-w_2)_+}\) and misses \(-1\), since it is one modulo four.
Thus adjoining inversion gives the claimed extra factor two in both the source projection and the target.
When \(k+v_2(q)>w_2\), reduction from each source group to its dyadic component forces strictly greater two-valuation than at the target.
Every fiber then costs an even number of preimages per point, contradicting the odd multiplicity.
The proof requires \(k\ge2\), precisely the stated range.

**Fixed finite prime supports.**
The index one and prime-power cases give absolute positive lower bounds directly.
For mixed \(q\), each possible reference prime divides \(a\), so the choice \(c=0\) is available without comparing that prime with the delay.
If every prime of \(q\) divides \(N=n+1\) to order at least two, pick any one as reference.
The valuations of all complementary odd primes are bounded by (1), and (2) also applies to a complementary two for either sign.
The finitely many pairs of primes from \(S\) therefore bound \(t\), and the prime-power bound on \(\gamma^t\) applies.

Otherwise choose \(\ell\) with \(v_\ell(N)\le1\).
Each target order divides \(2N\) with multiplicity at most \(\ell\), even after linear deletion.
For complementary primes dividing the binomial exponent in the ranges of (1) or (2), those bounds control their contributions to \(\gcd(t,D_*)\) directly.
At every other complementary \(p\), \(v_p(s)\le2\).
The \(p\)-part of \(\varphi(s)\) is bounded uniformly because all other possible primes \(u\mid s\) lie in \(T\cup\{2\}\) and contribute at most \(p^{v_p(u-1)}\) each, irrespective of \(v_u(s)\).
As \(L_s\mid\varphi(s)\) and every locally admitted order \(m\) satisfies \(L_m/L_s\le\ell\), this bounds \(v_p(L_m)\).
Projection to modulus \(p^{v_p(m)}\) now bounds \(v_p(m)\) via the prime-power order formulas, with valuations zero and one at two already bounded.
The least common multiple over any number of admitted orders inherits the same valuation bound.
All constants can be obtained from the finite sets and their pairwise multiplicative orders and valuations.
Combining the finitely many complementary prime bounds with v711's uniform \(\delta\) proves the effective positive gap even when \(S\cap T\ne\varnothing\).
Under the stated further hypotheses \(R\mid H(a)\) and support of \(H(a)\) in \(S\), this applies to the maximal index.
The factor minimum for \(P_a(X^k)\) is \(\log r/\gcd(k,R)\ge\log r/R\) for every \(k\), while every factor contributed by \(U(X^k)\) is cyclotomic.

**The explicit family and sieve.**
For \(N=5^j,\ n=N-1,\ j\ge2\), the odd exponent \(n-1\) selects exactly one linear cofactor for either sign, giving \(d=N-1\).
If the dyadic exponent is positive, the target modulo two divides the separable \(X^N-1\).
The equal-degree powered multiset then maps bijectively onto those simple target roots.
Writing \(K=2\cdot5^{j-1}\), the binomial valuation at five gives \((-4)^{5^{j-1}}\equiv1\bmod5^j\), hence \(2^K\equiv-1\bmod N\).
The map \(z\mapsto z^{-2^K}\) preserves the source and fixes each target.
Commutation with the bijection forces every source root to be fixed, and \(3\nmid2^K+1\) since \(K\) is even.
Thus \(3^f\)-th powering is invertible on that source group of roots of unity.
The cyclotomic target contains complete primitive orders with constant multiplicities, so the inverse multiset is the target itself, also for \(f=0\).
It follows from divisibility of the reduced minimal polynomial that \(\gamma^N-\epsilon\in2\mathcal O_K\).
The dyadic scalar height transfer gives \(N\log\gamma/d\ge0.427800\) at its recorded published numerical scope.
In the pure ternary case the full prime-power reduction modulo three gives the same scalar congruence at three and the exact larger constant \(\log(5/2)/2\).
For index one \(r>a-1\) suffices.
The factor conclusion therefore has an absolute lower bound, at least \(0.427800\cdot24/25=0.410688\) over the indicated \(j\).

For \(Q=6^j,\ K=256Q\), a prime \(v\ge5\) divides at most one of \(Qb-1,Qb,Qb+1\).
Thus divisibility of their product by \(v^2\) is equivalent to one of the three congruences in \(b\), distinct modulo \(v^2\).
Each covers at most \(K/v^2+1\) candidates and only \(v\le\sqrt{QK+1}\) occur.
The integral bound on \(\sum_{m\ge5}m^{-2}\) is \(1/4\), and \(3\sqrt{256Q^2+1}\le3\sqrt{257}\,Q<64Q=K/4\).
The strict union bound supplies a seed, with an effective finite search and \(a\le256Q^2\).
The central quotient \(a/\operatorname{rad}(a)\) is divisible by \(6^{j-1}\); the sieve excludes every \(v\ge5\) from all three quotients in \(H(a)\).
The delay \(5^j-1\) eventually meets the cited threshold uniformly because \(\log(a+1)=O(j)\) and \(a\ge6^j\).
Hence the maximal field index has only dyadic and ternary support, and (3) and the factor minimum apply at both signs and every dilation.
Finally \(r<a+1\) yields the claimed decay of \(\log r/H(a)\), independently of the size of the actual maximal field index.
