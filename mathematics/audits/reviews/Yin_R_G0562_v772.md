## Spectral factors, coefficient cancellation and the reciprocal application

If \(U\) has largest exponent \(j>0\), reality on the circle implies \(U_{-i}=U_i\), so \(X^jU\) has degree \(2j\) and nonzero constant term.
Its roots are invariant with multiplicities under conjugation and reciprocal conjugation.
At each circle root, \(t\mapsto U(e^{it})\) has the same order of vanishing as \(X^jU\), since \(d(e^{it})/dt\ne0\).
Nonnegativity makes that order even.
Selecting the exterior roots and half of each circle multiplicity therefore gives \(j\) roots closed under conjugation.
The boundary square of their real polynomial has exactly the required zeros and differs from \(U\) by a positive constant, determined at a point where \(U>0\).
Jensen's circle mean, including at integrable boundary zeros, then gives \(B(0)=\sqrt{M(U)}\) after normalization.
The root formula for the integral polynomial \(X^jU\) gives \(M(U)\ge |U_j|\ge1\).
A positive constant summand has the same conclusions directly.
The construction applies to both summands, and strict positivity of \(qu\) gives a denominator \(D\) without zeros on the closed disk and with \(D(0)^2=qM(u)\).

Write \(B,C\) for the two normalized factors.
Padding to degree \(w\) makes \(B^\#=z^w\overline{B}\) and \(C^\#=z^w\overline C\) on the circle, even if a factor has smaller degree.
The boundary columns in the displayed matrix are orthogonal, and both squared lengths before division by \(D\) are \(qu=|D|^2\).
Taking the scalar product against fixed unit vectors on the left and right and applying the maximum principle gives the operator norm bound at zero.
The top Laurent coefficient of \(|B|^2\) in degree \(w\) is \(B_w B(0)\), also when \(B_w=0\).
The corresponding identity holds for \(C\).
Consequently the numerator matrix at zero has squared Frobenius norm \(T\) and determinant \(x(a/x)+y(b/y)=a+b\).
The larger eigenvalue of its real Gram matrix is precisely the right side of (2), giving the bound with the stated factor \(q\).

The degree of \(qu\) ensures \(w\ge h\).
If \(w>h\), cancellation forces \(b=-a\) with \(a\ne0\) since at least one summand reaches \(w\).
The Gram determinant vanishes and two arithmetic-geometric mean bounds give \(T\ge4|a|\).
If \(w=h\) and \(q=1\), then \(a+b=\pm1\).
Two nonzero integral terms have \(|a|+|b|\ge3\), giving \(T\ge6\).
With one term zero, its constant square contributes at least one and the other summand contributes at least two.
At determinant squared one the larger eigenvalue increases with \(T\ge3\), yielding \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
For \(3-X-X^{-1}\) the summands \(1\) and \(2-X-X^{-1}\) are nonzero and nonnegative, \(w=h=1\), and its unique root outside the circle after clearing \(X^{-1}\) is \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
This verifies the asserted sharpness.

For reciprocal \(P\), the real function \(X^{-h}P\) on the circle has constant sign by continuity and the absence of circle roots.
Its positive normalization \(u\) has extreme coefficient of modulus one and agrees with \(|P|\) there.
For a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial \(B\) the top exponent of \(B(X)B(X^{-1})\) is its width, since the extreme coefficient is a product of its two extreme nonzero coefficients.
If \(u-B(X)B(X^{-1})=0\), positive width would factor the irreducible \(P\) into two Laurent nonunits up to a monomial.
Here \(P(0)=1\), so the prime polynomial \(P\) remains prime in the Laurent ring.
Width zero gives a constant boundary square, incompatible with \(h\ge1\).
The remainder is therefore nonzero and nonnegative, proving both certificate bounds without a separate coprimality hypothesis.
The reference to the quadratic estimate is a comparison under that estimate's own hypotheses.
The two-summand proof and its constants follow directly from the fixed argument.
