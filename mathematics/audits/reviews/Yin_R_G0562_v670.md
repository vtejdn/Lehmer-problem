## Support bound and divisibility by the parent

On 2026-11-20 a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v536.md#statement), its two proof sections and the dependency interfaces listed below.
I find inequality (1), the fixed-support divisibility conclusion and the support bound for the retained factor proved under the stated critical hypotheses.

Reciprocity gives exactly \(2k\) off-circle roots of \(P\), so their fraction is \(O_{C,K}(s^{-2})\).
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) has the same number of extensions to \(\mathbb Q(t)\), and \(|t|=1\) is equivalent to \(|t^q|=1\).
Both \(t\) and its inverse are algebraic integers because \(x\) is a unit.
For each nonzero integer \(l\), the field of \(t^l\) contains \(\mathbb Q(x^l)=\mathbb Q(x)\), and its height is \(|l|h_{\rm abs}(x)/q\).
The real and imaginary parts of \(z/|z|\) times the fixed annular cutoff, extended by zero near the origin, are compactly supported Lipschitz functions with uniformly bounded energy and zero circle mean.
The discrepancy theorem at radius \(1/D\) for the minimal polynomial of \(t^l\) contributes \(O(1/D)\) outside the square root and \(O(\log(2D)/D)\) inside it, in addition to the height term.
Equal embedding multiplicities transfer this average back to \(\mathbb Q(t)\), and dropping its off-circle terms costs \(O(s^{-2})\) with the original denominator.
For a relation vanishing on every conjugate, the evaluation matrix on circle embeddings annihilates the original complex coefficient vector, hence so does its Hermitian Gram matrix.
Its largest-coefficient row gives (2) because the diagonal tends to one and each off-diagonal uses a nonzero exponent difference of size at most the span.

The common-root calculation for \(g,B\) excludes simultaneous zeros, and the derivative calculation for \(g\) excludes multiple roots.
Pullback by \(T\mapsto T^{qa}\) preserves their simplicity at every preimage, since those preimages are nonzero.
At any such zero the leading coefficient of \(G\) is a unit and the constant coefficient has valuation one, so Eisenstein applies for every \(q\ge1\).
Primitivity gives divisibility over the Laurent coefficient ring whenever it holds over its fraction field.
Substituting \(Y=T^b\) then gives actual divisibility by \(F(T^q)\).
After clearing monomials, \(F(0)=-1\) allows ordinary polynomial division, and a nonzero remainder of degree below \(\deg F\) would retain degree below \(\deg F(T^q)\) after substitution.
This descends divisibility with the full multiplicity of every factor.

The pairs \((e_i,j_i)\) are distinct because they recover the distinct \(n_i\), so \(H\) is nonzero.
If the parent fails to divide \(A\), irreducibility now gives a nonzero resultant, including \(H^q\) for \(j=0\).
For every conjugate \(t'\), rationality of \(P\) gives \(P((t')^q)=0\), and the fixed polynomial identity \(P\mid A\) gives \(A((t')^q)=0\) with the original coefficients.
Consequently \((t')^b\) is a common zero of the specialized equations, and the resultant vanishes there as a function of \(t'\).
Its determinant products use \(q\) coefficients from \(H\) and \(j\) from \(G\), placing all exponents in \([-qE,qE+jqas]\) with at most \((q+j)!h^q3^j\) terms before collection.
Inserting these bounds into (2) gives (1), with the factor two on \(E\) absorbed in the constant.
For bounded \(h\) and fixed \(q,J\), one can first make the contribution of \(E/b\) sufficiently small and then let \(s\) grow, since \(as/b=O(s^{-2})\) and \(d\to\infty\).
Taking \(n_0\) equal to the least exponent gives the short-span specialization.
For \(A=P\), the span is \(d\), strictly below \(b+as\) for large \(s\), and \(d/b\asymp s^{-1}\) while \(\log(2d)/d=o(s^{-1})\), giving the asserted \(\sqrt{s}\) lower bound.
