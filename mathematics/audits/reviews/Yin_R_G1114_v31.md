### Circle counts and transfer to the Pisot roots

The positive-constant construction puts exactly one simple root \(\sigma\) outside the circle and its simple inverse inside for the monic polynomial \(D\) of degree \(n+2\).
The other \(n\) roots, initially counted with multiplicity, are precisely the fiber of \(-1\) for \(T=zf/g\) on the circle.
The denominator is nonzero there.
The argument principle gives winding number \((n+1)-1=n\), since \(zf\) has the zero at the origin and the \(n\) interior conjugates, while \(g\) has just \(\theta^{-1}\) inside.
On an argument lift the signed count of crossings of all levels \(\pi+2\pi\mathbb Z\) is the change in the associated integer part.
The net contribution at a fiber point is at most one, including higher-order crossings and tangencies.
The signed count reaches the full multiplicity \(n\), so every fiber point has multiplicity one and contributes \(+1\).
On an arc the crossing count is therefore the difference of two integer parts, differing from the argument increment divided by \(2\pi\) by at most one.
Moving both endpoints in the same orientation gives the half-open arc statement, including when both endpoints lie in the fiber.

The interior factors of \(f/g\) pair as \((z-\beta_i)/(1-\bar\beta_i z)\), using invariance of the multiset of roots under conjugation.
The exterior factor is the inverse of the corresponding factor at \(1/\theta\), and \(z\) itself contributes one to the argument derivative.
Hence \(\Psi'=1-P_{1/\theta}+\sum_iP_{\beta_i}\).
Testing a Poisson kernel against \(e^{ikx}\) with normalized arc measure gives \(a^k\), with the required sign of the exponent.
The signed discrepancy between the neutral-root count and the argument-derivative measure has total mass zero and cumulative mass bounded absolutely by one.
Integration by parts over a full turn bounds its \(k\)-th moment by \(2\pi k\).
Finally each radial replacement costs \(1-|\beta_i|^k\le -k\log|\beta_i|\).
Their sum costs at most \(k\log(\theta/q)\) by the constant-coefficient identity.

### Discriminant estimate and the limiting hypotheses

The crossing argument makes the full discriminant nonzero, and monic integrality gives absolute value at least one even with several irreducible factors.

The \(k\)-th nonnegative Fourier energy term is \(t^k|\sum_jw_j^k|^2/k\), with \(t^k\ge(1-1/n)^n\ge1/4\) for \(1\le k\le n\).
The bound on \(\sigma\) from L462v32 gives \(\log\sigma=O(\log(2+\delta))\).
