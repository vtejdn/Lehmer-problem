### Root energy and its normalization

Write \(A_x=\max(1,|x|)\), \(w_x=x/A_x^2\), and \(L=\log(2Y)\).
For one root exterior and the other interior, the two sides of \(|1-w_x\bar w_y|\ge|x-y|/(A_x A_y)\) are equal.
For two interior roots the squared difference of the two sides is \((1-|x|^2)(1-|y|^2)\ge0\).
For two exterior roots, applying the same identity after reflection works since \(|w_x-w_y|=|x-y|/(|x|\,|y|)\).
Also \(|1-tv|^2\ge t|1-v|^2\) for \(|v|\le1\).
In a primitive irreducible degree-\(D\) factor with leading coefficient \(a\), the off-diagonal sum of the negative distance logarithms is \((2D-2)\log|a|-\log|\operatorname{disc}F|\).
Adding the \(A_x A_y\) contributions gives exactly \(2(D-1)m(F)-\log|\operatorname{disc}F|\).
The \(D\) diagonal terms cost at most \(6DL\) and the smoothing loss is \(O(D^2(2Y)^{-6})\).
An irreducible factor is separable over \(\mathbb Q\) and its discriminant is a nonzero integer.
This use of the discriminant includes degree one.

Primitive factors with an absolute leading or nonzero constant coefficient at least two have \(m(F)\ge\log 2\).
The remaining factors, apart from \(z\), define nontorsion units under the root-of-unity exclusion.
The degree-dependent bound applies at their own degrees \(D\le Y\).
For sufficiently large \(D\) it gives \(m(F)\gg(\log\log D/\log D)^3\gg L^{-3}\).
At bounded degrees one can use finiteness of monic polynomials with bounded degree and measure at most two, by the elementary coefficient bound.
The lower bound at unit factors is consistent with Theorem 1 on printed p. 391 and the following sentence on p. 392 in the original Dobrowolski article, which the reviewer checked at statement level.
The theorem uses total multiplicative measure of a nonzero algebraic integer, and taking a logarithm preserves the required lower bound up to an absolute constant. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-34-4-391-401)

Dividing the energy estimate by \(m(F)^2\) costs \(O(dL^7)\), since \(D/m(F)\ll dL^3\) also when the factor measure is large.
Root vectors add with multiplicity, \(z\) has zero vector, and the sum of the factor measures with multiplicity is bounded by \(m(P)\) because integer content contributes \(\log|\operatorname{cont}P|\ge0\).
The triangle inequality before squaring proves the claimed bound for arbitrary repeated roots.
The case \(P=c z^d\) with \(|c|\ge2\) gives zero error and is included.

### Gram bounds and exceptional counts

The scalar products compute the regularized sum with its stated minus sign.
For \(|\zeta|=1\) one has \(|x-\zeta|=A_x|1-w_x\bar\zeta|\), so extracting the Mahler main term also accounts for the leading coefficient in each resultant.
For primitive orbits the same discriminant bound gives \(O(YL)\) on the diagonal.
Between different orbits the reduced-fraction spacing gives a distance \(\gg Y^{-2}\), so moving a node radially by \(1-t\) changes each logarithm by \(O(Y^2(1-t))\).
There are \(O(Y^2)\) node pairs per entry, for total \(O(Y^{-2})\).
The absolute resultant of the two cyclotomic polynomials equals one unless the larger index is a prime-power multiple of the smaller.
In \([Y,2Y]\) the only such quotient is two and requires both endpoints.
Its log resultant is at most \(2Y\log2\), for one entry in either affected row.
This gives absolute row sums \(O(YL)\).

For the full orbits the \(jl\) character factor on multiples of \(\operatorname{lcm}(j,l)\) leaves the prefactor \(\gcd(j,l)\) in the logarithm series.
The sum of the gcds in a row is bounded by \(2Y\sum_{b\mid j}\varphi(b)/b\le2Y\tau(j)\).
Consequently both Gram operator norms are at most \(O(Y^{9/8}L)\).
Multiplying by the root energy and dividing by the square of \(mY^{7/8}/L\) gives \(O(dY^{-5/8}L^{10})=o(Y)\), uniformly for \(d\le Y\).
The first-moment bound for \(j/\varphi(j)\) discards \(O(Y/L)\) further orders to ensure \(\varphi(j)\ge Y/L\).
Outside the union, both smoothed relative errors are \(O(Y^{-1/8})\).

### Removing smoothing and comparing the normalizations

The one-sided inequality costs \(O(d|E_j|(1-t))\), so after division by \(|E_j|m(P)\) the cost is \(O(Y^{-5}L^3)\).
For two-sided primitive control one nearest-node order per distinct nonreal conjugate pair suffices by the same reduced-fraction spacing.
The reflection preserves angle and commutes with conjugation, so the two nearest choices can be paired even in a tie.
For real roots the orders in question eventually exceed two and their primitive nodes have angular distance \(\gg 1/Y\) from both real endpoints.
The zero root needs no deletion.
On the remaining primitive nodes the two-sided cost is \(O(d|E_j|Y^2(1-t))=o(|E_j|m(P))\).

Fix \(0<e<1/2\) with \(d=o(Y)\).
The finitely many dyadic windows covering \([eY,2Y]\) have lower endpoints at least \(eY/2\), which eventually exceed \(d\).
The preceding two-sided primitive estimate then loses \(o_e(Y)\) divisor orders in their union, and each such divisor at least \(eY\) spoils at most \(2/e\) multiples.
All other large divisors contribute their relative totient-weighted main terms with one \(o_e(1)\) error.
The totient weight from divisors below \(eY\) is at most \(2eY^2\) summed over the full window and therefore at most \(\sqrt e\,Y\) outside \(O(\sqrt e\,Y)\) orders.
Each omitted logarithm is nonnegative by integrality and the exclusion of roots of unity.
Using \(\sum_{b\mid j}\varphi(b)=j\), then taking the limsup before sending \(e\) to zero, yields the full lower estimate in density.

In the linear window at least \(Y/2-o(Y)\) orders survive the two-sided primitive bound.
The totient first moment removes at most \(O(c_0Y)\) orders with \(\varphi(j)<c_0Y\), and removing primes costs \(o(Y)\).
Choosing \(c_0\) sufficiently small leaves a fixed positive proportion with \(\log C_j\ge\log D_j\gg Ym(P)\).
Conversely, any fixed positive proportion with \(\log D_j\) or \(\log C_j\ge c_2 d\) intersects its respective density-one upper bound in every late window, which forces \(Ym(P)/d\) to be bounded below there.
The composite-only versions follow by the same intersection on the upper side and the previous prime deletion on the lower side.
At the indicated superlinear scale the full Dobrowolski log-log factor makes \(Ym(P)/d\gg1\) for units of growing degree.
These conclusions use no separate lower bound on number-field discriminants.
