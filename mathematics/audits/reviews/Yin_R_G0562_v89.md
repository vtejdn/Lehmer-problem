## The real first moment, reciprocal divisibility and equality of positive spectra

The reviewer had no development role and received the fixed submission and declared inputs without development dialogue or a previous verdict on this version.

For a totally real algebraic integer \(\beta\notin\{0,1,-1\}\), none of its conjugates is zero or has absolute value one.
Writing \(t\) for the absolute conjugate and taking the arithmetic mean over the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), the geometric mean of \(\sqrt t\) is \(|N(\beta)|^{1/(2\deg\beta)}\ge1\).
Reality gives \(|\beta_i^2-1|=|t_i-1|(t_i+1)\) at every conjugate.
Taking the two factors' geometric means and bounding each by its arithmetic mean gives \(1\le\mathbb E|t-1|(U+1)\) from the nonzero integer norm of \(\beta^2-1\).
Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(|\sqrt t-1|(\sqrt t+1)\) bounds \((\mathbb E|t-1|)^2\) by \((U+1)^2-4(\mathbb E\sqrt t)^2\le (U+1)^2-4\).
For \(X=(U+1)^2\ge4\) these inequalities give \(X^2-4X-1\ge0\), so \(U\ge\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}-1>1\).
The argument uses a strict upper threshold on \(U\) to exclude these integers and makes no sharpness assertion.

For \(\beta=\zeta_n^{-m}P(\zeta_n)\), integrality follows from the cyclotomic unit multiplier and the integral polynomial.
Complex conjugation fixes each centered value by reciprocity of degree \(2m\).
Restriction of the cyclotomic embeddings to \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) covers every conjugate with equal multiplicity and centering preserves its modulus.
Thus the primitive absolute average equals the first absolute moment for this totally real integer, forcing a rational \(v=0,\pm1\) at the stated threshold.
Vanishing of \(P-vX^m\) at one primitive root gives divisibility by its minimal polynomial \(\Phi_n\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
Conversely divisibility gives absolute value \(|v|\) at every primitive root, hence always satisfies the strict threshold.

Since \(0<m<2m\), the centered numerator remains monic of degree \(d\), reciprocal and with constant one.
It is nonzero, giving \(d\ge\varphi(n)\).
Monic division gives an integral monic quotient with constant one, and applying reciprocity to the identity makes this quotient reciprocal with degree \(d-\varphi(n)\).
At degree equality it is one.
Conversely any compatible monic reciprocal quotient of the indicated even degree yields a monic reciprocal \(P\) of the correct degree by adding the indicated center term.
This verifies the classification also for degrees above critical and its impossibility below critical.

For \(s=n/\operatorname{rad}(n)\) and \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)\), the roots of \(\Phi_q(X^s)\) are precisely the primitive \(n\)-th roots.
Indeed if \(p^a\Vert n\), the \(p\)-part of \(s\) is \(p^{a-1}\), so a preimage of a primitive \(q\)-th root must have the full \(p\)-part \(p^a\).
The polynomials are monic of the same degree \(s\varphi(q)=\varphi(n)\).
For \(q\ge3\), \(\varphi(q)\) is even, so the central exponent also scales by \(s\), and the normalized circle integral preserves measure under \(X\mapsto X^s\).
When \(q=2\) and \(n\ge3\), \(\varphi(n)\) is even and the substitution \(Y=X^{\varphi(n)/2}\) gives \(Y^2+vY+1\).
For \(v=0,1,-1\) these are respectively \(\Phi_4,\Phi_3,\Phi_6\) and have measure one.
For even squarefree \(q=2u\) with \(u\ge3\) odd, negation sends primitive \(u\)-th roots bijectively to primitive \(2u\)-th roots.
Thus \(\Phi_{2u}(X)=\Phi_u(-X)\), and substitution of \(-X\) multiplies the central coefficient by \((-1)^{\varphi(u)/2}\).
Both signs are allowed, and rotation preserves measure.
The branch \(v=0\) always has measure one, and each of the remaining odd squarefree examples occurs at its own conductor with primitive average one.
These two inclusions prove equality of the positive spectra even when different conductors yield the same measure.

At squarefree order with \(r\) prime divisors, the Möbius numerator and denominator are each products of \(2^{r-1}\) integral binomials.
Clearing the denominator in a central perturbation produces the sum of the numerator and a signed monomial times the denominator.
It is nonzero, integral, has at most \(2\cdot2^{2^{r-1}}\) terms and has the original multiplicative measure since the clearing factors have measure one.
The Dobrowolski–Smyth theorem therefore gives a uniform positive logarithmic gap for a bounded number of prime divisors, excluding any bounded-count subsequence of positive measures tending to one.
The case of unbounded prime counts remains open under this reduction.
