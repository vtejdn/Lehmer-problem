## Integral harmonic densities and full lower polynomials

The density \(f=H/(2S)\) agrees with its conjugate reciprocal by its real boundary values.
Reflection eliminates poles off the circle away from zero and infinity, and absolute integrability eliminates poles on the circle, so \(L=2f\) is Laurent polynomial.
For constant nonzero \(L\), the constant coefficient of \(SL\) forces \(L=1\) or \(2\), and either integrality of \(S\) or exhaustion by Haar contradicts the hypotheses.
In the nonconstant case I checked that the top coefficient of \(SL\) is \(s_nL_k\), a nonzero integer, and that \(\lvert s_n\rvert\le1\) follows with circle roots allowed.
Averaging the nonnegative \(L\) over the \(k\)-th roots gives \(2\ge L_0\ge2|L_k|\ge2\).
Thus \(L_k=\epsilon=\pm1\), and exhaustion identifies \(\mu\) with \(f\,dm\).
At each of the \(k\) points with \(w^k=-\epsilon\), the vanishing average forces \(L\) to vanish and nonnegativity gives a double zero.
The degree and leading coefficient then give \(w^k L=\epsilon(w^k+\epsilon)^2\), whose associated \(J=1+\epsilon w^k\) has integral formal inverse.

For the arbitrary integral prefactor I checked the [short-interval and mixed tests](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#real-seam) at the actual scale.
The [first fractional index](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) lies inside the common prefix eventually and is at least \(g\).
Division by the integral unit series gives integral coefficients of \(X_i\) on which the short-interval proof uses only the first nonintegral limit coefficient, bounded degree, convergence and the signed moment bounds.
At \(rg\) one obtains integral \(SJ\), and at \(kt+rg\) all active Taylor indices are positive, giving integer transforms for the weighted rows.
Finite interpolation of the joint transform and the [coset decomposition](../literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) apply to the measure weighted by \(2|S|^2\).
Outside its finite base zero set, each combined positive graph component can be divided by this weight, with its projected density dominated by \(\mu\).
Mass over the zero set contributes zero to the rows.
Common multiples of the sheet degrees and torsion orders remove the phase and make every sheet contribute its full projected mass.
The absolutely continuous part of an integer-transform row is integer Laurent by the singleton cosets, so translating the row and letting the other slopes escape gives integrality coefficient by coefficient even in the presence of torsion atoms.
The density argument above excludes every slope, including zero.
The remaining full-torus term has finite positive harmonic range.
At its last nonzero harmonic \(k\), conditional positivity of the \(k\)-th power distribution gives \(|H_k|\le |S| f_0\).
Since \(M(S)=1\) and Jensen equality for \(f_0\) would force the excluded Haar marginal, this contradicts \(M(H_k)\ge1\).
Only torsion atoms of the weighted base marginal can contribute, and one binomial chosen from this fixed marginal works for every further joint limit.
The moving-index contradiction therefore gives simultaneous vanishing of the integer coefficients throughout the stated interval after extraction.

When every root of \(S\) is on the circle, division of the weighted base density has possible nonzero finite poles only there.
Integrability again removes them, so the absolutely continuous density is Laurent polynomial and the remaining measure is supported on the finite exceptional set.
The atoms at roots of \(S\) have their simple Herglotz poles canceled in \(V=SJ\), so \((1-w^T)V\) is polynomial and its Taylor coefficients are integral.
The reciprocity identity then gives the Laurent polynomial \(2Sf_0=V+\epsilon w^n V^*\) with integral Laurent coefficients by division by \(1-w^T\).
The density lemma makes the marginal purely atomic, allowing nontorsion atoms at the zeros of \(S\).
I also checked that the equally weighted conjugate roots of \(1+aw+w^2\) give exactly \(J=(1-w^2)/S\).

For the full lower polynomial, all reflected exponents are absent and all lower Taylor indices are strictly positive on \((K+T)g<l<N-Kg\), including after translation by \(Tg\).
Weak convergence gives uniform smallness of the middle coefficients by integration of the continuous modulus \(|(1-w^T)S(w)|\), which vanishes on the limiting support.
The negative mass and the coefficient perturbation both tend to zero.
Integrality kills the whole middle, and reflection in degree \(N+Tg\) sends \(l=N-Kg\) to \((K+T)g\), so the two boundary indices are included in the bounded ends.
The binomial multiple has boundedly many nonzero integer coefficients, and its measure equals \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one, contradicting the [fixed-support gap](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This proves the neighborhood result in either parity, with variable degree up to \(K\), and a finite cover gives the compact-family assertion.

## A sparse lift with a varying initial profile

I checked the whole theorem with \(K,N,\eta,S\) fixed and with \(j\) unrestricted.
The [complex sparse height inequality](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) applies because the actual lift has unit constant and only circle roots.
Hence its coefficients and the coefficients of its convolution with the [actual inverse series](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) are bounded, and the first fractional index again puts \(g=o(h)\).
The marginal and mixed tests just verified apply to the fractional prefix with integral prefactor one.
The torsion orders are chosen from the single weighted marginal before taking further joint limits, so a fixed \(T,L\) gives an exact zero interval for \(UB\), \(U=1-z^{Tg}\), lying inside the prefix after enlarging \(L\).

The [initial interval estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial) applies at \(g\) because all moments below \(g\) are integral, the positive measure charges the actual interior lifts and the full lift has boundedly many bounded coefficients.
This includes bounded \(g\) and permits a single signed binomial or one for \(P\).
Translation by \(P\) loses only \(O(g)\) indices at the start of the zero interval, where the estimate on translates of \(PB\) bounds the absolute sum for \(C=PUB\).
For \(V=PU S(z^g)\), replacing \(S_i\) by \(S\) costs \(o(1)\) per coefficient of the common prefix uniformly in the test index.
In the [two-sided identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#gram) both required indices are at most \(|r|+\deg V<h/L\) when nonnegative, and the doubled zeroth moment equals two.
The contribution from the polynomial expression to the Gram norm is bounded by its absolute row sums, using the bounded initial coefficient sum of \(C\).
The entrywise replacement error and the passage to the positive part cost \(k\,o(1)\) for \(k\) chosen indices, so choosing \(k\) large and fixed before taking the limit suffices.

Every coefficient of \(VB\) is a bounded-length sum of products drawn from fixed finite sets, even when the exponents in \(P,U,S(z^g)\) coincide.
The nonzero values are therefore bounded away from zero.
Off the union of the transition intervals every active Taylor index is strictly positive, so the partial multiplier is fixed on each block and its circle norm is bounded.
Bessel's inequality bounds the number of nonzero values there with the fixed-\(k\) Gram bound.
On the transition intervals the factorization \(VB=U S(z^g)(PB)\) and the initial estimate bound the absolute sum, which controls the number of terms by the same positive minimum.
The full support has degree \(O(h)\), so boundedly many blocks and transitions suffice.
After extraction the ordered coefficient tuple of \(VB\) is fixed, its constant is one and \(M(VB)=M(B)>1\) tends to one.
In the [phase-translation argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations) this fixed real tuple is sufficient for Lawton specialization, continuity at each translation and the endpoint lower bound after reversal.
Its limiting logarithmic measure zero forces equality at the trivial translation as well, contradicting strict excess.
This establishes the asserted local uniform lower bound for every specified stable nonintegral real center.
