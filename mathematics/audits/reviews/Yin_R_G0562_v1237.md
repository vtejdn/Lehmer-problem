## Fixed grids, integral points and the Salem lift

At 2026-11-25T10:22 UTC, a reviewer outside proof development checked the complete statement and both arguments of C G0345 v891 from the fixed text and its mathematical dependencies.
The initial inspection used no proof-development discussion, author explanation or previous review finding for this claim.

For \(P_0=x^{n-1}(x+4)\) the scaled evaluation is \(1+4M\).
Subtracting \(P_0\) leaves a polynomial of degree at most \(n-1\) divisible over \(\mathbb Q\) by \(Mx-1\), and primitivity gives the integral quotient for every lattice point.
The \(n-1\) multiples \(x^j(Mx-1)\), \(0\le j\le n-2\), form an integral basis of the translation lattice and span the real kernel.

At the ordered nodes the sign of \((t_i-h)Q'(t_i)\) is \((-1)^{n-i}\).
Evaluation at the \(n\) nodes and at \(h\) determines the polynomial of degree at most \(n\), so the cited interpolation formula holds also for real coefficients and for \(n=2,3\).
Monicity cuts the positive weights by their sum \(1-\delta\).
The endpoint factors in the denominator of \(\delta\) multiply to \(K\), while each additional interior factor exceeds one.
This proves emptiness in degree two and gives an affine bijection from the open weight simplex of dimension \(n-1\) in every higher degree.
Even for irrational nodes the sign tests are open in coefficient space.
A fiber carrying an integral point has rational affine coordinates given by that point and a rational basis of the kernel of the pole and observation equations.
Consequently any nonempty relatively open sign region in such a fiber contains a rational polynomial, including when the fiber is a singleton.
Equality of observed values requires the two witnesses to share only those values.

For uniqueness I checked the weighted reconstruction through the first coefficient at which two candidates could differ.
Writing \(c_j=[x^{n-j}]H\), fixed earlier coefficients determine the earlier weighted moments, and the \(j\)th weighted moment equals a known constant plus \(c_j\).
The monic Chebyshev polynomial \(2T_j((x+2)/2)\) bounds this quantity after a known translation in an interval of width \(4(1-\delta)\).
The identity \(K=1+\sum_{j=1}^n c_jM^j\) forces the difference of the two candidate coefficients to be a multiple of \(M\).
The interval width is less than \(4<M\) throughout the nonempty case, including \(q=2\).

The \(n-1\) intervening negative intervals and \((0,h)\) exhaust the degree, giving simple roots and excluding all endpoint roots.
Conversely the \(n-2\) gaps between consecutive negative roots admit rational separators, and the monic factorization over the reals gives the required sign at each node.
For a putative complementary integral factor with negative roots, its positive scaled pole value divides \(4M+1\).
In degree at least two the value exceeds \(M^2\) because the absolute constant term is a positive integer.
In degree one its value is \(1+aM\), \(1\le a\le3\), and \(4(1+aM)-a(4M+1)=4-a\) excludes divisibility.
Thus the norm used by the inverse construction is taken over precisely the conjugates of the positive root.

The norm identity makes \(K/(1-Mw)\) an algebraic integer and \(0<w<h<2/q\) meets the radial hypothesis of the unit criterion.
At a negative trace conjugate the discriminant \(w(w+4)\) is negative, establishing quadratic irreducibility over the totally real trace field.
Only the positive trace place contributes a root outside the unit circle, so the Mahler measure is \(T\).
The identity \(p/q+q/p-2=h\), with \(p=q+1\), gives \(1<T<p/q\) and a positive denominator in the graph coordinate.
The criterion therefore gives the two positive common powers at this place.
Along any unbounded sequence of realized heights the positive logarithmic measures have a subsequence tending to zero.
The proof leaves existence of such a sequence open.
I found no mathematical defect within the stated scope.
