### The boundary and the angular deficit

In logarithmic polar coordinates the identity for each zero gives \(u_t\ge j+d/2=\lambda D>0\), even when \(j<0\).
Monicity and \(D>0\) give \(u(t,\theta)=Dt+o(1)\) at infinity.
The strict inequality on the unit circle therefore gives exactly one level radius \(R>1\) on each ray, including rays through a zero on the circle.
The pole of a possible negative power lies inside \(E\), so the exterior Green function has the indicated normalization at infinity.
Writing \(t=\log R(\theta)\) on the level curve, the outward flux of \(u\) per unit angle is \(u_t-t'u_\theta=u_t+u_\theta^2/u_t\).
This proves the stated lower bound on the equilibrium density.

The retained angles have total length at least \(2\pi-1/2>\pi\).
For angular distance \(\phi\le\pi/2\) the distance to the full ray is \(R\sin\phi\), and for \(\phi\ge\pi/2\) it is \(R\).
Thus every retained boundary point is at least \(R/(8s)\) from \(K\), even for overlapping segments.
Its outward translate by \(R/(16s)\) lies outside \(E\).
There \(G_K\ge G_E\ge\lambda\log(1+1/(16s))\ge\lambda/(32s)\).
Harnack's inequality at half the disk radius loses a factor of at most three, giving \(G_K\ge\lambda/(96s)\) at the center.
The retained angles have equilibrium mass at least \(\lambda/2\).

All points of the finite radial tree are regular, since each belongs to a nondegenerate subsegment with regular exterior, and the complementary domain is connected.
The smooth boundary gives regularity for \(E\).
The logarithmic kernel has bounded positive part on \(E\times K\), so the two equilibrium integrations can be interchanged using Tonelli on a constant minus the kernel.
The potential of \(\mu_E\) equals \(\log\operatorname{cap}E\) everywhere on \(K\subset E\).
Consequently the integral of \(G_K\) gives \(\log\operatorname{cap}E-\log\operatorname{cap}K\) with the sign in (2), and its lower bound is \(\lambda^2/(192s)\).
This includes \(d=0\), \(s=1\), multiple boundary zeros and interior endpoints.

### Powers and the containing region

A factorization of \(A_0\) or a root of unity among its roots would lift under \(X\mapsto X^k\), contradicting the hypotheses on \(A\).
A linear monic unit polynomial has root \(1\) or \(-1\), hence \(a_0>1\).
A pair of opposite roots would force \(A_0(-X)=(-1)^{a_0}A_0(X)\) by irreducibility.
The nonzero constant term then forces even degree and only even powers, contradicting maximality of \(k\).
The squares form a single Galois orbit of size \(a_0\), so their orbit polynomial is monic integral and irreducible.
These are exactly the hypotheses under which the published arithmetic criterion gives capacity at least one for the union of the segments to the squares and fourth powers. [Van Ittersum, Ringeling and Zudilin, §2, p. 237](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972721000654)

For each \(q=2,4\), the \(qk\)-fold product of the rotations of \(B\) is invariant under \(X\mapsto e^{2\pi i/(qk)}X\).
Its leading coefficient has modulus \(|\operatorname{lc}B|^{qk}\ge1\), so passing to the monic polynomial in \(X^{qk}\) preserves the required upper bound on its coefficient norm.
The \(k\) rotations indexed by the \(k\)-th roots of unity preserve \(A_0(X^k)\), so its divisibility into \(f\) gives \(k\) factors bounded by \(Lr^{-g}\).
The elementary bound for the other \(k(q-1)\) factors supplies (3), including \(b=0\).
Each root of \(A_0\) has a lift among the roots of \(A\), and an endpoint outside the disk has a lift with \(r\ge1\).
The exponents obtained at the two endpoint powers are \(k(2j-g+5b)\) and \(2k(2j-g+5b)\).
Both are nonpositive by the floor defining \(j\), and radial monotonicity includes the whole of each exterior segment.

### Uniform constants and all factors

For \(n\ge4/\delta\), \(j+3b/2\ge\delta n/4\) and \(j+3b\le n/2\), giving \(D\ge\delta n/4\) and \(\lambda\ge\delta/2\).
The signs needed for (2) therefore hold before it is applied.
Its use with \(s\le2a/k\) gives \(a\ge k\lambda^2D/(384\log T)\), and \((\log T)/k\le10\log L+\log2\).
Thus \(c=\delta^3/[6144(10\log L+\log2)]\) works in this range and also for \(1\le n<4/\delta\), where \(a/n\ge1/n>\delta/4\ge c\).

The strict gap \(g>0\) separates the two nonempty supports, so \(\deg f=n\) and the number of terms \(t\) satisfies \(2\le t\le\lfloor2L\rfloor\).
Dobrowolski's divisor lemma gives \(m(A)\ge\log(1+0.31c/\lfloor2L\rfloor!)\).
Its hypotheses apply to each individual monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit factor, even if \(f(0)=0\). [Dobrowolski, Lemma 1, p. 203](../literature/Dob06/readme.md#edition)
For an irreducible factor with a leading or nonzero constant coefficient of absolute value at least two, the root product formula for it or its reciprocal gives \(m\ge\log2\).
Up to sign, the remaining irreducible with zero constant term is \(X\), of measure zero, and linear unit factors are cyclotomic.
In the example, the low block has degree \(h+u+v\) and coefficient norm at most eight, and \(h-5u-v\ge2h/5\ge(2h+2u)/6\).
The exponents pair about \(h+u\), and the value at one is \(-2\), which ensures a real root greater than one.
