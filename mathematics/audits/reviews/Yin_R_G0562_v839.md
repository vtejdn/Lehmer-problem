## Independent review of the cofactor bounds and square exclusion

At 2026-11-21T00:54 (UTC), an agent independent of the development of C345 v702 checked its complete fixed proof for Sheng Yin and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had developed the earlier inputs v687 and v698 and checked their application here.
Those inputs received separate independent reviews in [R562 v824](Yin_R_G0562_v824.md#finding) and [R562 v835](Yin_R_G0562_v835.md#finding).

With \(L=n-1\), the root classification removes from \(G\) exactly the simple linear factors of \(X^L-\epsilon\) at \(1,-1\).
Writing \(P=G/U\) and \(A=(X^L-\epsilon)/U\) gives \(d=n+1-\deg U\) and \(D=L-\deg U=d-2\ge4\).
The four possibilities for \(U\), in the order positive sign and even or odd \(L\), then negative sign and odd or even \(L\), are \(X^2-1,X-1,X+1,1\).
At a root \(\zeta\) of \(A\), substitution gives \(P(\zeta)=\epsilon(\zeta^2-1)/U(\zeta)\).
Its absolute product over that grid is one in the first three cases, using respectively \(P(\zeta)=1,\ A(-1)=1,\ A(1)=1\).
For the last case the product is \(A(1)A(-1)=4\).
Since \(p>n\) is coprime to \(2L\), powering by \(p\) permutes the grid after deleting its linear roots.
Thus \(A(X)\mid A(X^p)\) integrally.
For the first positive field radical \(\beta\), the fields generated by \(\beta\) and \(r\) coincide.
Multiplicativity of the integral norm makes \(|N A(\beta)|\) a positive divisor of the corresponding value one or four.
The Salem signature of \(\beta\) provides \(D\) neutral factors in this same norm.

A matched lift falls under v687's bound \(\beta<L\).
Indeed its conjugate supplies a second small factor, and the cited estimate gives \(\beta^{2p-D}<L^{D+2}\) with \(2p-D\ge D+2\).
For each unmatched lift \(\rho\), the nontrivial \(p\)-th root \(\epsilon\rho^L\) has distance at least \(2\sin(\pi/p)\ge4/p\) from one.
The paired angle error in \(w^L\) is at most \(\pi/(ap)\).
Hence \(|w^L-\epsilon|\ge(4-\pi/a)/p>8/(3p)\) for every \(a\ge3\).

For \(\beta\ge2\) put \(x=1/\beta\).
In the three cases with a linear factor removed, the circle modulus of \(U\) is at most two, including \(U=X^2-1\).
The real value \(A(x)\) is at least one for either positive-sign geometric sum, and at least \(1/(1+x)\ge2/3\) for the negative-sign quotient with \(U=X+1\).
Each neutral factor \(|A(w)|\) therefore exceeds \(4/(3p)\).
The two real factors have absolute product \(\beta^D|A(x)|^2\) by reciprocity.
The norm one then gives
\[
       \frac{\beta}{p}<\frac34(3/2)^{2/D}
                   \le \frac34\sqrt{3/2}<1 .
\]
For \(U=1\), \(A(x)=1+x^L>1\), all neutral factors exceed \(8/(3p)\), and the norm at most four instead gives \(\beta/p<(3/8)4^{1/D}\le3\sqrt2/8<1\).
Together with the matched case and \(\beta<2\) this covers every possible first field radical, including \(D=4\).
The strict interval \(a-1<r=\beta^p\) gives the asserted seed bound.

A second positive field radical \(\gamma\) would give \(\beta=\gamma^p\in K\).
The \(e=2\) instance of v698 applies directly to the field of \(r\), with logarithmic Mahler measure \(\log\gamma\) and the original degree \(d\).
Every prime \(p>n\ge7\) is at least eleven, and \(d/(n+1)\ge(n-1)/(n+1)\ge3/4\).
Thus the cited bound gives \(\log\gamma\ge0.320850>1/4\) at its published numerical scope.
The function \(t/4-\log t\) is increasing on \([11,\infty)\) and is positive at eleven by the displayed finite exponential series.
This forces \(\log\beta=p\log\gamma>\log p\), contradicting the first-level result with strict room at the smallest prime.
If \(p^2\mid q\), raising the positive \(q\)-th root to \(q/p^2\) produces the forbidden second radical in the same field.
This proves the asserted valuation bound for every admissible \(q\) and for the largest index \(R\).
