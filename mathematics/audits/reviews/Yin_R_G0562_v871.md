# Frobenius powers and the height over a central quotient of bounded exponent

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T03:12 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete [C345 v731](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer did not develop this proof and inspected the fixed claim and the interfaces below without its development discussion, author explanations or earlier verdicts on v731.

At a fixed prime \(P\) over \(p\), translating the inertia group on the right by any lift of the chosen residue power expresses all lifts as one coset.
Its intersection with each right \(H\)-coset that it meets has size \(|I_P\cap H|\), giving conditional probability \(1/e_v\) by the tower of local ramification indices.
The identity coset is met exactly when the residue power fixes the residue field at \(v\), since the local Galois group for \(L_P/F_v\) maps onto the relative residue Galois group.
Counting all \(P\) over \(v\) using their relative local degrees gives the proportion \(e_vf_v/s\), hence the sum of \(f_v/s\) over \(f_v\mid k\).
This derivation includes the cases of wild inertia and trivial residue action.

The torsion-quotient class product has the same relative degree times absolute height as the given input, since its conjugates correspond bijectively to classes of a common size and it equals a power of the original number times a root of unity.
Thus all its powered relative conjugates are distinct within a given set.
The positive height distinguishes the original and all transformed sets because the different prime powers are pairwise unequal integers greater than one.
For a maximizing coset \(Hg\) at \(p\), the relation \(y\in Hg\) implies \(y^{-1}|_F=g^{-1}|_F\).
The simple roots of \(Z^{p^{f_v}}-Z\) lift the residues uniquely in \(F_v\) and in \(L_P\).
In particular \(y(z)=z^{p^k}\) for the chosen representatives, including residue zero, and \(y^{-1}\) preserves the valuation.
The inequality with error \(1/e_v\) therefore holds for the coefficients of the Gauss-normalized relative polynomial even if its conjugate coefficient field and \(F_v\) have a more ramified compositum.
Modulo \(p\), raising a polynomial to \(p^k\) is the coefficientwise Frobenius followed by \(X\mapsto X^{p^k}\), so integral mixed coefficients have valuation at least one.
Evaluation at each transformed root before powering then gives the cross-difference saving \(p^{-D'/e_v}\), with the normalizing scalar canceled exactly by the product of the original local maxima.

The original multiplicity \(T\) raises each such saving to power \(T\), and all extensions of \(P\) contribute its normalized weight in the product formula.
The conditional saving averaged over \(P\) is consequently \(TD'w_{p,k}\log p\).
For \(S=D'(T+t)\), the divided derivative of order \(j\) has coefficient bounded by \(S^j\) and row-length factor at most \(S^{j+1/2}\) at an archimedean place.
Summation over \(j=0,\ldots,T-1\) and the simple rows gives the exponent \(D'(T^2+t)/2\).
The heights of the transformed nodes are \(p^kh(\beta)\), furnishing the claimed coefficient after division by \(D'\) and \(D'\le D\).
These estimates allow denominators of the original algebraic number.
Taking a single prime, \(D=2\) and \(T=1\) gives \(\log4\) in the first term of (3) and \(2(1+p^k)\log\alpha/s\) in the second.
For the unramified Frobenius order the powered element is the identity at every prime, giving the stated rearrangement at \(p\ge5\).

For the normal subfield the common \(b\)-th power of all unramified Frobenius conjugates is central, so it gives a coset of probability one.
The discriminant of the original power basis bounds the sum of the logarithms of ramified primes by \(2\log d+4m\).
In \([X,2X]\), with \(X=T^2\log(2T)\), this excludes at most \((2\log d+4)/\log X\) among \(\asymp T^2\) candidates.
The stated choice of \(T\) leaves a positive absolute proportion and ensures \(T\log(2T)\gg A\log(2d)\), so the multiplicity absorbs the determinant logarithm.
The cost of powered nodes is \(O_b(T^{4+2b}(\log(2T))^b)\), and division of the remaining \(T^3\log(2T)\) yields \(T^{-(2b+1)}(\log(2T))^{1-b}\).
Using the actual order of \(T\) gives the numerator exponent \(2b+1+1-b=b+2\) in \(g_b(d)\).
The resulting bound on \(s\) at \(m\le1\) is polylogarithmic, implying comparability of \(\log(2D)\) and \(\log(2d)\) and of their adjusted iterated logarithms for fixed \(b\).
This also proves the stated order restriction on shrinking sequences.
The perfect example \(A_5^r\) has exponent \(30\) and trivial abelianization, and the sizes of product conjugacy classes can grow with \(r\).

For a finite Galois base of bounded group exponent and \(p\nmid b\), wild inertia is trivial by Cauchy's theorem and tame inertia is cyclic, hence of order at most \(b\).
The residue degree divides \(b\), so the power lift set is the inertia group and gives the identity probability at least \(1/b\).
Omitting the finite prime divisors of \(b\) and enlarging the constant in \(T\) in terms of \(b\) gives the same exponents at the actual relative degree \(D\).
For an infinite Galois base, the conjugates of the finitely many defining coefficients lie in a finite Galois subextension.
The relative minimal polynomial remains irreducible there and the finite quotient still has exponent dividing \(b\).
The comparison with ADZ14 agrees with its inspected absolute-height theorem for elements in the Galois field and is separate from the relative determinant proof.
