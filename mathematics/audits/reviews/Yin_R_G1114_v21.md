### Irreducibility, conjugates and measure

In characteristic \(\ell\) the cyclotomic polynomial is \((z-1)^d\), while the perturbation is divisible coefficientwise by \(\ell^2\).
The constant of \(P(z+1)\) is \(\ell-10A\), of \(\ell\)-adic valuation one, giving Eisenstein irreducibility.
The perturbation pairs exponents \(1,d-1\) and \(3,d-3\), with zero constant and no leading term once \(d>6\).
The bound \(\ell+10A\) for the supremum on the unit circle also bounds the multiplicative Mahler measure.

On compact subsets outside the unit disk the terms below \(z^d\) in the cyclotomic sum contribute \(O(|z|^{3-b})\) after normalization, with a constant depending on \(\ell\).
The two surviving perturbation terms give the limit \(f(z)=z^2(z-A)-4A\).
For real \(z\le A\) this is strictly negative, and for \(z>A\) it increases through zero before \(2A\).
Thus there is one simple real root \(r\), and the conjugate nonreal roots are simple and have squared modulus \(4A/r\in(2,4)\).
Disk Rouché on disjoint neighborhoods entirely outside the unit circle gives three exterior roots converging to these three.
For \(h=d/2-3\), the centered perturbation is \(2\cos((h+2)\theta)+8\cos(h\theta)\).
At \(j\pi/h\) it has sign \((-1)^j\) and absolute value at least six.
Since \(6A>\ell\), the full centered real polynomial changes sign on each of the \(h\) adjacent intervals, giving \(2h=d-6\) distinct circle roots after conjugation.
The exterior roots and their inverses exhaust the other six degrees.
It follows that \(m(P)\to\log(4A)\).
Convergence separates the real root in modulus from the entire remaining root set.
If distinct roots had equal positive powers, a field automorphism sending the first to that unique largest root would give a different root with the same modulus, proving the asserted injectivity and degree preservation.

### First residues and the dyadic square

At each designated \(p\), the distinct reductions are exactly the roots of order \(N\).
Inertia fixes the reductions and thus every root, proving unramifiedness of the splitting-field completion with \(v_p(p)=1\).
The derivative of \(z^N-1\) is a unit at every reduced root, giving a unique root-of-unity lift \(\zeta\) of order \(N\).
For \(x=\zeta(1+pu)\), reduction of the linear Taylor term gives \(u\equiv (A/p)W_d(\zeta)/(\zeta\Phi_N'(\zeta))\pmod p\).
The derivative denominator is \(N/(\omega-1)\) with \(\omega=\zeta^b\) of order \(\ell\), and \(\zeta^d=\omega^{-1}\).
These give precisely the four terms and the nonzero scalar in (2).
Multiplying the equation \(\bar u=c\) by \(\bar\zeta^3\) puts the powers \(0,2,4,6\) on the perturbation side and \(3\) on the other.
The coefficient at degree four remains nonzero even in characteristic two, so either equality \(c=0,1\) would imply \([\mathbb F_p(\bar\zeta):\mathbb F_p]\le6(\ell-1)\).
The exact degree is the order of \(p\bmod N\), since each Frobenius power fixes \(\bar\zeta\) precisely when its exponent is \(1\bmod N\).
The stated finite threshold excludes that degree bound at every \(p\in S\) and for all roots simultaneously.

For \(1+pv\) with nonzero first residue the prime-to-\(p\) part of an exponent multiplies that residue by a unit.
For odd \(p\), taking a \(p\)-th power at any integral depth \(t\ge1\) has a unique lowest binomial term of depth \(t+1\).
At \(p=2\) an odd exponent preserves the first residue itself.
The first square of \(1+2v\) differs from one by \(4v(1+v)\), which has valuation exactly two because \(\bar v\notin\{0,1\}\).
At depth \(t\ge2\) the linear term in a square has depth \(t+1<2t\).
This proves the exact lift at every exponent, including the odd-exponent and first-squaring boundaries.
For \(N\nmid n\) no cyclic factor reduces to zero, while \(N\mid n\) removes the lifted torsion factor for every root.
Adding the \(d\) equal depths gives the claimed valuation of \(c_n\) and first index \(N=\ell d/(\ell-1)\).

For a compatible pair the unit quotient \(x^p/y\) is \(1-pu_y\bmod p^2\) because its torsion factors cancel and \((1+pu_x)^p\equiv1\bmod p^2\).
At \(p=2\) its first residue is \(\bar u_y\), which already excludes both exceptional values.
Raising this quotient to \(p^a\) therefore gives depth \(a+1\) in its difference from one, and the factor \(y^{p^a}\) is a unit.
The \(p^a\)-power map on the residue field is injective and there is exactly one compatible partner for every first coordinate.
