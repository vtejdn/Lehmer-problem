## Matching classes, surviving simple sections, the sharp tower threshold and the degree filtration

At 2026-11-25T00:07 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) and all four proof sections of C0901 v87, including the final degree-filtration consequence.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v87.
The full subgroup \(D_y=(A_b)^u\) survives under restriction over the indicated infinite base and towers.
The exact threshold for reaching the original \(y\) is \(b\), and the uniform spectral stability and first-entry assertions for \(K_{\le D}\) follow at the stated measure and degree ranges.

The class-product compression is available for every nonzero nontorsion algebraic number, with finite places included in the height when needed.
Its conjugates are distinct modulo torsion and it preserves the original total measure.
If two inputs match, their matched compressed classes are nonzero rational multiples and stabilizing one compressed root therefore stabilizes the class and then the actual value of the other.
The converse stabilizer inclusion follows symmetrically.
This gives equal embedded compressed root fields and equal normal closures after choosing matched embeddings.
Equivariance carries the same scalar relation to every conjugate, so it also identifies their projective multiplicative labels and the normal fields defined by these label actions.
Matching is an equivalence relation at the level of the orbits, since one may transport a shared conjugate in two matches to the same intermediate label and then compose the rational scalings.
In particular all inputs of smaller compressed degree are unmatched with \(y\).
Equality of the original uncompressed splitting fields is unnecessary.

Northcott finiteness, or the coefficient bound for a primitive minimum of bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure, gives finitely many compressed orbits for fixed \(M,d\).
The number of original roots, torsion twists or uncompressed splitting fields above each such orbit need not be finite.
The two kernels in v267 account for them: the compressed-root to label kernel embeds in a product of groups of order at most two, and the original roots satisfy power equations over the compressed splitting field after killing their finite torsion ratios.
For each individual original input, adjoining the relevant roots of unity is abelian and the normal radical extension then has abelian group over that cyclotomic base.
Hence the original splitting field is solvable over its label field, with no common bound on radical exponents required.

In v279 one may take \(N=M\), so \(S=\max(1,M,N)\) and all the requisite degree thresholds depend only on the fixed \(M\), not the number of representatives.
Choose \(U\) at least the threshold and all block counts in the high-degree portion of the finite compressed list, including the target.
The canonical block estimates give \(U=O_M(1+(\log(3d))^{212})\), while \(b\gg_M d/(\log(3d))^{212}\).
For all sufficiently large \(d\) the target therefore has \(b>U\).
Only the large-block representatives with their actual \(b_i>U\) are passed to the independence theorem, together with \(y\).
After removal of matching duplicates these are pairwise unmatched and satisfy \(\min_i b_i>\max_i u_i\), even if their compressed degrees coincide or are arbitrarily close.

For a small compressed degree the compressed group embeds in \(S_U\).
For an input above the threshold but with \(b_i\le U\), the bound on all \(u_i\) embeds its label group in \(S_{b_i}^{\,u_i}\rtimes S_{u_i}\).
A nonabelian simple quotient of any subgroup of an extension either comes from the intersection with its normal kernel or factors through the projection of the subgroup to the quotient.
Iterating along a product and then the wreath product bounds the orders of all such simple sections by \(U!\), independently of the potentially large order of the whole group.
Further solvable kernels preserve the exclusion of \(A_b\), since \(b\) is large and \(b!/2>U!\).
In particular this checks the entire intermediate-size portion left outside the v279 invocation.

Over \(Z\), v279 identifies the actual restriction map on the selected label compositum with the product \(D_y\times\prod_i D_{x_i}\).
Adjoining every \(P_{x_i}\) to \(Z\) kills the other factors precisely, yielding \(D_y\) over \(B_0\), also in the case of an empty selected list.
Over \(B_0\) the uncompressed inputs matching those \(x_i\) add fields solvable over an already present label field.
For every other input its finite Galois extension after base change has group a subgroup of the corresponding original group, so the exclusion of the simple section \(A_b\) persists.
Finite composita have groups embedded in products, and the section exclusion is inherited by subgroups and quotients.
Any finite Galois subextension of the directed compositum is contained in a finite Galois compositum of these contributions.
Thus it too has no such section, even when infinitely many original radical fields occur.
The intersection of \(B_0K\) with \(B_0P_y\) over \(B_0\) is finite Galois, giving a quotient of \(D_y\).
Every nontrivial such quotient has a copy of \(A_b\) as the nontrivial image of at least one factor, which is impossible on the other side.
This proves that \(D_y\) survives unchanged over \(B_0K\).

In the normal closure of a finite permitted tower, after first normalizing the preceding stages, the conjugate new adjunctions over that normal field have groups in subgroups of \(S_{b-1}\), or solvable groups at a solvable stage.
All have no \(A_b\) section, and the group built by their compositum and the previous stages retains this property.
This applies over an infinite starting field in characteristic zero, because each finite tower has a finite normal closure over that starting field.
The directed union of such towers is normal, since the degree and solvability conditions are preserved by conjugation and base change, and the same finite-subextension argument applies after passing to \(B_0K\).
A second trivial-intersection argument leaves \(D_y\) over \(B_0K_*\).
Restriction over the smaller field \(K_*\) then contains the same subgroup in the original action on \(P_y\), which proves (1) and the degree lower bound.

The field \(K_*\) is stable under rational conjugation, so containing \(y\) would imply containing its full splitting field and thus its label field, contradicting (1).
Conversely one can successively fix each block in the faithful label action using indices at most \(u\), then every label within a fixed block using indices at most \(b\).
As \(u<b\), the resulting chain realizes \(P_y\) by individual adjunctions of degree at most \(b\), and a final solvable step reaches \(L_y\).
Base change of that chain reaches \(y\) from \(K\) or \(K'\), while a tower over \(K'\subseteq K\) of smaller bound would also give one over \(K\).
This proves exactness and applies to all earlier records since strict first attainment prevents compression from lowering any record degree.

Finally, for \(d_y>D\), \(K_{\le D}\) is a rationally normal subfield of the unmatched base for this individual \(y\).
Its exact threshold remains \(b_y\).
Uniformity of \(b_y\gg_M d_y/(\log(3d_y))^{212}\) and eventual monotonicity of this lower function give one strictly smaller multiple \(c_M D/(\log(3D))^{212}\) excluding every larger compressed degree simultaneously.
All elements of positive measure at most \(M\) of compressed degree through \(D\) are already present, by their own original splitting fields.
Their measure values coincide with those of degree-at-most-\(D\) nonzero algebraic numbers in that interval by compression and its measure identity.
Below \(\log2\), the ordinary positive integer-polynomial minimum, if under consideration, has a positive irreducible factor of no greater measure or degree, with integer content and monomials removed.
The specified nonempty-range qualifications therefore give the claimed minimum consequence.
If larger compressed degrees occur, the nonempty set of their integer thresholds has a least element, and the upper tower for a target attaining it proves sharpness of the first new entry.
