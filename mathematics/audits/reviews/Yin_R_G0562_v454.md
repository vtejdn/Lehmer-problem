### Independent numbers and linear disjointness

The plane polynomial \(g(U)+VB(U)\) is irreducible because \(g,B\) are coprime and it is linear in \(V\), and its support differences have rank two.
Thus the primitive-direction degree bound tends to infinity uniformly over its nontorsion factors.
The six signed monomials of the section have unit extreme coefficients, so \(\alpha\) is an algebraic integer unit and the measure of its irreducible factor is at most the measure of the section, hence at most \(\log 6\) in logarithmic units.
Kronecker's theorem gives strict positivity for this nontorsion unit.

Let \(M\) be the rational splitting field of its factor and \(N\) the normal closure of a degree-\(l\) field \(L\).
For \(n=d/2\ge7\) the rational group on the \(d\) roots contains the transitive subgroup \(W=V\rtimes A_n\).
If \(l<n\), the common Galois quotient for \(M\cap N\) has order at most \(l!\le(n-1)!<|A_n|\).
Its restriction to the simple natural \(A_n\) must be trivial.
The normal closure of \(A_n\) in \(W\) contains \(V\) because the commutator of a three-cycle with an even flip on a moved and a fixed letter gives a two-letter flip, and such flips generate \(V\).
Thus the kernel contains \(W\) and is transitive on the roots.
Restriction from \(MN/N\) identifies its group with that kernel, proving irreducibility over \(N\) and hence over \(L\).
The two degree towers for \(KL\) then give \([KL:K]=l\).

For a multiplicatively independent \(\beta\), the absolute logarithmic heights are \(m(\alpha)/d\) and \(m(\beta)/l\), and the joint degree in the Amoroso–David product estimate is \(de\), where \(e=[K(\beta):K]\).
Consequently \(l\le C e\,m(\alpha)m(\beta)(\log(3de))^{212}\), with the full logarithmic exponent \(2\kappa(2)=212\).
Under the cap, \(e\le d\) and \(\log(3de)\le2\log(3d)\).
The strict choice of \(c_B\) makes \(l<d/2\) for \(m(\beta)\le B\), allowing the disjointness argument with \(L=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
The height estimate covers nonintegral numbers with the finite-place contribution in \(m(\beta)\).
At sufficiently large \(b/a\), every nontorsion factor stays irreducible over the union of the finite solvable Galois extensions of \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{tr}}\).
If \(\beta\) belongs to that union, irreducibility over its subfield \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) gives \(l=e\) directly and at every absolute degree.

### Dependent numbers and attainment

The absence of torsion quotients of distinct conjugates makes every nonzero integral power of \(\alpha\) generate \(K\).
The abelian-field intersection gives \(\mu(K)=\{\pm1\}\) for \(d>12\).
Divisibility of \([\alpha]\) by a prime in \(\mathcal O_K^\times/\mu(K)\) would thus give a signed prime power in \(K\).
For odd primes the sign can be absorbed, and the power test excludes both signs for squares.
The class is consequently primitive in the free abelian group.

In a dependent pair with \(m(\beta)>0\), both entries are nontorsion, so a reduced relation can be written \(\beta^s=\zeta\alpha^r\) with \((r,s)=1\), \(r\ne0,\ s>0\) and \(\zeta\) a root of unity.
Raising to the order of \(\zeta\) shows \(K\subseteq\mathbb Q(\beta)\), using the power-field equality even for negative \(r\).
Both \(\beta\) and its inverse are integral, and in the unit group modulo torsion the norm gives \(s[N(\beta)]=re[\alpha]\).
A basis coordinate taking \([\alpha]\) to one now gives \(s\mid e\).
The height identity \(s h(\beta)=|r|h(\alpha)\) therefore gives \(m(\beta)=|r|(e/s)m(\alpha)\), a positive integer multiple regardless of the location of \(\zeta\).
Conversely \(\alpha^j\) realizes each such multiple at relative degree one, and any algebraic number of absolute degree at most \(q\) has relative degree at most \(q\).
These arguments give both inclusions after intersection with \((0,B]\).

For \(q\ge1\), the rational number two bounds the candidate for \(\delta(q)\) from above by \(\log2\), and bounded degree and measure bound all coefficients of a primitive minimal polynomial.
The least positive candidate is thus attained.
Both \(\delta(q)\) and \(m(\alpha)\) lie in the \(\log6\) window and are realized with the permitted relative degree.
Their minimum remains the least positive measure without a measure cutoff and when one ranges over all towers of total relative degree at most \(q\), since each element of such a tower individually meets the degree cap and every candidate can be taken in a simple extension.
The external height theorem and the cited large-direction group and arithmetic results were used at their recorded statement scopes.
