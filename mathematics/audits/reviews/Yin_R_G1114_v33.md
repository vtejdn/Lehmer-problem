## Extremal limit and the constant prescribed by degree

Both statements of L462v33 hold, including the quantifier over every integer \(q\) above \(Q_d\) and the relative error at arbitrarily small positive \(T-2\).

The quadratic polynomial \(z^2-(q+2)z+q\) gives a Pisot root with the required constant for every \(q\ge2\).
For the infimum \(b\) over Salem numbers, an example below \(\phi\) gives \(0\le b<1\), since \(\phi+\phi^{-1}-2=\sqrt5-2<1\).
For any admissible positive-constant Pisot polynomial \(f\), \(f(1)<0\), and the monic reciprocal \(zf+f^*\) takes a negative value at one.
The Rouché deformation \(zf+t f^*\), \(0<t<1\), gives at most one exterior root in the limit, with the sign providing one actual real exterior root \(\sigma>1\).
The unit constant of each monic integral factor forces the reciprocal interior root into the same factor.
The factor of degree two yields the integral trace \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\ge3\); higher degree gives a Salem number.
Thus \(b\le \sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\) in both cases.
I checked the use of the recorded inequality in Boyd's Lemma 5 with positive constant, sign plus and delay one.
It gives \(b_q\ge bq(q-1)/(q+1)^2\).
For each fixed Salem polynomial the inverse supplies candidates at every sufficiently large integer \(q\) with excess approaching \(T-2\), so taking the infimum over all Salem numbers after the limsup gives the upper bound even when no Salem number attains \(b\).
This establishes the limit and its restriction to powers of a fixed prime.

For \(d=2m\ge4\) with \(1<\sigma\le2\), I bounded the norm of each row in the reduced Vandermonde matrix by \(\sqrt{d-1}\max(1,|\beta|)^{d-2}\).
Only the row of \(\sigma\) can increase the product, by at most \(2^{d-2}\).
The omitted differences are precisely the \(d-1\) factors of \(P'(\zeta)\); separability and integrality give the lower bound \(1/\Lambda\).
At \(x=2\cos t\), \(0<t<\pi\), the identity \(|P'(\zeta)|=2(T-x)\sin t\,|H'(x)|\) gives the stated lower bound on \(2\sin(t/2)|H'(x)|\), since \(2(T-x)\cos(t/2)\le9\).
With \(L=10d\Lambda\) the inward perturbation term has magnitude at least \(10d/9>m\) before a rounding correction of magnitude at most \(m\).
The real monic \(F_0=p_qV\), the prescribed integer coefficient pairs and the antisymmetric rounding error give \(zf_q+f_q^*=(z+1)P\) exactly.
At a neutral root the signed ratio \(c_j/H'(x_j)<0\) and the negative real part of \(e^{-it/2}p_q(\zeta)\) give strict motion into the open disk.
After the perturbation begins, only a root of \((z+1)P\) could be on the circle, and none of the neutral roots of \(P\) is a zero of \(C\).

Each Laurent factor \(z+z^{-1}-x_j\) has coefficient sum in absolute value at most four.
Differentiating the \(m-1\) factors gives \(\|E\|_1\le2(m-1)4^{m-2}\), also at \(m=2\).
Since \(L=10d\Lambda\), \(\Lambda\le d^{d/2}2^{d-2}\) and \(\|U\|_1\le d\), this gives the asserted \(A_d\).
The nonzero integer \(P(-1)\) gives \(|V(-1)|\ge2/9\).
For \(q>10A_d\) the path has \(|F_0(-1)|>(4/9)q>A_d\), ruling out the remaining crossing for every perturbation parameter.
Consequently \(f_q\) is integral and has just one exterior root, which is positive by \(f_q(1)=P(1)<0\).
Its other roots all have modulus less than one, so a monic factor supported on them would have a nonzero integer constant strictly smaller than one in modulus.
This proves irreducibility and \(\theta>q\).

Since the roots of \(V\) have modulus one, \(|C(\theta)/V(\theta)|\le A_d(\theta/(\theta-1))^{d-2}\le3A_d\) for \(\theta>2d\).
From the quadratic identity the error is \((p_q(\theta)+(T-2))/(\theta-1)\); here \(0<T-2\le1/2\).
Also \(|P(1)|\ge1\) and \(|V(1)|\le2^{d-2}\), giving the required lower bound for dividing by \(T-2\).
In detail \(d\,2^{d-2}(3A_d+1)+1\le 45d^3 8^d d^{d/2}\le 16d^4 32^d d^{d/2}=Q_d\) for \(d\ge4\), and the same \(Q_d\) exceeds \(10A_d\) and \(2d\).
These estimates prove the claimed relative error for every such \(q\).
The stated power of two therefore works at each degree, and the forward delay-one result applies if a sequence of positive excesses tending to zero is supplied at those degree-constant pairs.
Neither direction asserts the existence of a sequence with Salem numbers tending to one.
