# Trace-field degrees on hyperbolic filling slopes

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of the all-slope estimate

At 2026-11-17T07:11 UTC an agent outside proof development independently reviewed the complete [v173 statement and argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v173.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof, its dependencies and the scope of the assignment, with no proof-generation dialogue, exploration notes, author explanation or earlier finding on v173.
The argument establishes both degree bounds for every hyperbolic filling in the stated manifold class and for every nontorsion irreducible factor of every nonzero filling specialization.

For a primitive pair \((p,q)\), choose \(r,s\) with \(ps-qr=1\).
On the subtorus \(m^p\ell^q=1\), the character \(t=m^r\ell^s\) has inverse \(t\mapsto(t^{-q},t^p)\).
Changing \((r,s)\) by a multiple of \((p,q)\) leaves \(t\) fixed, and reversing the slope and the compatible core orientation inverts \(t\), preserving its degree.
The published article uses the dilation components of peripheral holonomy and identifies a geometric point of this intersection for sufficiently long slopes.
Hyperbolic Dehn surgery gives convergence of its peripheral dilations to \((1,1)\), since the limiting peripheral holonomies are parabolic.
Its core is loxodromic and the two possible core dilations are reciprocal with moduli different from one. [Garoufalidis and Jeon, §§2.1–2.2 and Remark 3.5](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/blms.13059)

A polynomial with one support point has no torus zeros.
If all its support lies on a line with primitive integral direction \((a,b)\), the torus equation restricts \(m^a\ell^b\) to the finite root set of a single nonzero Laurent polynomial.
Convergence to one forces \(m^a\ell^b=1\) for all sufficiently long geometric fillings.
The core dilation then satisfies \(t^{-aq+bp}=1\), which requires \(bp=aq\) by loxodromy.
There is only one unoriented primitive slope with this property and there are infinitely many hyperbolic slopes outside it.
This proves that the support used in the specialization has affine dimension two, including when the polynomial is reducible.

In these dilation coordinates Theorem 2.2 gives cyclotomic edge roots for the primitive integral polynomial.
Reversing the primitive edge direction inverts its roots, so either edge orientation satisfies the torsion hypothesis.
Under the substitution the exponent vector is \((-q,p)\), of maximum norm \(N=\max(|p|,|q|)\).
Distinct support exponents can have the same projection only on finitely many rational lines, each containing a single primitive pair up to sign.
All sufficiently long specializations thus survive, and the theorem for torsion edges applies to every nonzero specialization, including those with a collision.
Removing a Laurent monomial leaves the nonzero roots and their degrees unchanged.
I checked the hypotheses and the factor-degree conclusion of v171 here and used its existing independent review for its internal proof. [Torsion-edge degree theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) · [Review of v171](Yin_R_G0562_v171.md#finding)

For the geometric point the core dilation \(t_0\) has infinite order, so its minimal polynomial supplies a factor of degree comparable to \(N\).
If a lift of the core holonomy has eigenvalues \(u,u^{-1}\), then \(t_0=u^{\pm2}\) and \(t_0+t_0^{-1}=(u+u^{-1})^2-2\) belongs to the trace field \(K\) used in the article.
The polynomial \(X^2-(t_0+t_0^{-1})X+1\) therefore gives \([\mathbb Q(t_0):\mathbb Q]\le [K(t_0):\mathbb Q]\le 2[K:\mathbb Q]\).
For the other direction, Theorem 2.4 gives \([K:\mathbb Q]\le C(M)[\mathbb Q(t_0):\mathbb Q]\) for this same geometric point, with no Lehmer hypothesis.
I checked the stated comparison and its applicability in the published source and accepted its character-variety input.
For the finitely many remaining hyperbolic fillings, \([K:\mathbb Q]/N\) is finite and strictly positive, so its extrema can be included in the upper and lower constants even if a small filling specialization vanishes. [Published Theorem 2.4](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/blms.13059)

The published Theorems 1.2 and 2.5 use a Lehmer hypothesis for the global lower bounds, and Theorem 4.1 gives an unconditional lower bound on a restricted sector.
Petersen's lecture abstract reports joint work with Fili and Hoffman giving linear bounds on most slopes and a lower bound of order \(N^{1-\varepsilon}\) on all slopes for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\).
The abstract supplies no proof or precise density convention.
Neither the application nor this review draws a conclusion about priority. [Published article](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/blms.13059) · [Rutgers lecture abstract](https://sites.rutgers.edu/dltg/lectures/)
