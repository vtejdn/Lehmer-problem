# Exact row-resultant valuations for quadratic units

<a id="quadratic-collision-equality-review"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T11:42 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin checked the full [quadratic construction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v155.md#simultaneous).
The reviewer took no part in developing this construction and received the fixed text without development discussion, explanatory notes or previous opinions on it.
The simultaneous equality holds for every \(e\ge1\) at all prime divisors of the power index away from \(p\), with the claimed degree bounds.
The separate formula at \(p=2\) also gives the asserted valuation at the support prime itself.

**The local intersection.**
For \(\ell\ne p\), equal original residues would make the quadratic Vandermonde quotient \((\beta_1-\beta_2)/(\alpha_1-\alpha_2)\) a unit by its geometric-sum expansion.
Hence at a selected \(\ell\) the original residues differ, the powered residues coincide and \(v(\beta_1-\beta_2)=v_\ell(I)\).
The common residue is fixed by residue-field Galois and is nonzero, admitting the chosen unit lift \(\gamma\) in \(\mathbb Z_\ell\).
The inverse branches through the two original residues have the common factor \(t(T)\) with \(t(0)=1\), and \(u_1-u_2\) is a unit even when \(\ell=2\).
Solving the two branch equations therefore preserves their generated ideal.
Exchanging the labels changes the signs of both numerator and denominator in each row formula, giving descent to \(\mathbb Z_\ell\).
In particular \(U\) is monic of degree \(e\) and has reduction \((X-b)^e\).

Division in the completed local ring by \(Q(\gamma+T)^e\) gives a polynomial \(V_\ell\) of degree less than \(2e\), since that monic divisor reduces to \(T^{2e}\).
Its congruence to the series \(V\) preserves the two order-\(e\) jets.
Substitution \(x=\alpha_i+z\) identifies the corresponding branch with \(x\) itself, since its derivative in the defining \(p\)-th power equation is a unit.
Writing \(J=(A,B)\) and \(\mathfrak m=(\pi,T)\), the correction lies in \((AB)\subseteq\mathfrak mJ\).
The new generators lie in \(J\) and still span \(J/\mathfrak mJ\), hence generate \(J\) by Nakayama's lemma.
All roots of the monic \(U\), and likewise of \(A\), lie in this residue class, so division by either distinguished polynomial identifies the local quotient with its ordinary polynomial quotient.
The resultant valuation equals its length divided by the absolute ramification index.
For \(A=(X-\beta_1)^e\) and \(B=(X-\beta_2)^e\) this gives exactly \(e^2v_\ell(I)\), and finiteness gives nonzero resultant for the constructed rows.

**One integral witness.**
The monic division by \(P^e\) defines the same integral linear kernel after tensoring with \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), as follows directly from Smith normal form.
The Chinese remainder theorem can therefore approximate every local solution in a single integral kernel vector, to precision one higher than the prescribed resultant valuation at each \(\ell\).
The determinant at the padded degrees \(e,2e-1\) is polynomial in these coordinates.
Its excess leading-coefficient factor when the second degree drops has valuation zero, since the first row remains monic modulo every selected prime.
The preserved nonzero determinant gives two nonzero coprime rows.
Primitivization preserves \(P^e\) and all prescribed valuations, and \(U(0)\) being a unit at those primes allows removal of a power of \(X\) from \(V\).
If there are no selected primes, taking \(U=1\) and interpolating \(e\) coefficients of the negative reciprocal branch at each of the two distinct nodes gives \(\deg V<2e\).
The interpolation conditions are Galois invariant and the nonzero prescribed values force \(V\ne0\).
Clearing denominators leaves a constant nonzero first row and preserves coprimality over \(\mathbb Q[X]\).

**Explicit cases.**
Quadratic reduction gives \(\alpha^7=377\alpha-144\) for \(x^2-3x+1\) and \(\alpha^3=2\alpha+1\) for \(x^2-x-1\), yielding the stated indices and primes.
For \(p=2,\ P=x^2-ax-1,\ a\ge1\), neither possible rational unit \(1,-1\) is a root, and the basis relation gives \(I=a\).
The even row of \(F=P^e\) is monic of degree \(e\), and the odd part is nonzero since the coefficient of \(x^{2e-1}\) is \(-ea\).
The product of \(|F(-z)|=2|z|\,|V(z^2)|\) over all \(2e\) roots, with multiplicity, contributes exactly \(2^{2e}\) because \(|F(0)|=1\).
At the roots of \(V(x^2)\) the two monic degree-\(2e\) polynomials \(F(x)\) and \(U(x^2)\) agree, so the absolute resultant with \(V(x^2)\) equals \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|^2\), including when \(V\) is constant.
Also \(|\operatorname{Res}(P(x),P(-x))|=4a^2\) by \(P(-z)=2az\) at the two roots of \(P\).
Taking the positive square root gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|=2^{e^2-e}a^{e^2}\) for every \(e\).
In particular the odd-prime valuations have the stated exact exponent and the valuation at two is \(e^2v_2(a)+e^2-e\).
