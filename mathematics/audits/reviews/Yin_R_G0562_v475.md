### Independent computation of each minimum

I regenerated the sample by enumerating odd \(c\) from one, reconstructed the integral traces from the paired exponents of the parent, and removed the trace \(Y^2-Y-1\) in the two \(\Phi_{10}\) cases, as in the earlier review.
All sixteen traces \(H\) are monic of odd degree.
I enumerated primes in increasing order and computed \(\operatorname{Res}(\bar H,\bar H')\) in \(\mathbb F_p\).
Its vanishing is equivalent to a common factor, and this resultant agrees up to sign with \(\operatorname{disc}H\) modulo \(p\) because \(H\) is monic.
This tests the discriminant of the trace polynomial itself, with good reduction defined exactly as in the new section.

At each good prime I worked in the full algebra \(\mathbb F_p[Y]/(\bar H)\), starting with \(Y\) and raising the residue to the \(p\)-th power step by step.
At every odd \(j\) I tested \(\gcd(\bar H,Y^{p^j}-Y)\).
The gcd is nonconstant precisely when some irreducible constituent degree divides \(j\).
For odd \(j\) all such divisors are odd, and every odd constituent of degree \(v\) appears in this test at \(j=v\).
Thus the first nonconstant gcd occurs exactly at \(v_p\), without removing blocks or computing an irreducible factorization.
An odd constituent exists since the degree of \(\bar H\) is odd.
I compared every earlier prime's classification, every earlier good minimum and the first pair satisfying \(av_p\ge b\) with the fixed program's output, and compared all sixteen final pairs with the published table.
I also reran the diagnostic under Python 3.14.3 with python-flint 0.9.0.

The \(92\) direction-prime pairs through the first witnesses consist of \(7\) bad reductions, \(69\) good reductions admitting a short odd degree and \(16\) good witnesses.
The bad reductions occur at \(2\) for \((a,c)=(5,13),(5,17),(5,19),(11,37),(11,41),(11,43)\), and at \(23\) for \((9,37)\).
Six rows already have a witness at \(2\).

At \(157\) the minimum is \(249\).
Each witness is at most \(157\), so the search bound \(199\) includes every smaller prime relevant to any row's leastness.

### What the witnesses exclude

The degree of an odd-degree modular divisor is a sum of irreducible degrees with at least one odd summand.
The least such summand itself gives a divisor, so \(v_p\) is exactly the least odd divisor degree.
Monicity and Gauss's lemma preserve degrees and divisibility under reduction of a rational monic divisor.
Thus each witness alone excludes all rational divisors of odd degree \(j\) with \(aj<b\), including in the thirteen directions that had candidates at three.
In the program the supplied factorization reconstructs the complete reduction and the subset-degree comparison verifies the same minimum.

The finite Salem exclusions use the earlier full intersections of proper degree sets, the real embedding certifying irreducibility of the quadratic lift, and the exact count of \(a\) exterior conjugates in that core.
The single-prime test establishes the short odd-degree exclusion for these inputs without supplying an effective slope threshold for the general Salem equivalence.
