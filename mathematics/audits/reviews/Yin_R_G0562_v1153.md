## The descending chain realizes every constituent and controls the least rank without increasing measure

At 2026-11-25T00:30 UTC an agent outside the development of [C0901 v93](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#statement) independently reviewed all five sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed artifact and requested scope without its development discussion.
The chain, its representation-theoretic description, both uniform asymptotics and the Voutier bound hold with the original measure \(m(w)\) and all of the indicated nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\).

Compression uses actual conjugate products; equal torsion-class sizes preserve both the positive total measure and the entire rational conjugate span.
Bounded-degree coefficient finiteness gives a uniform lower bound on the degree of every nontorsion unit of measure at most the shrinking budget.
One may therefore fix the upper measure bound one in the large-degree block theorems, demand \(b\ge9\), and use Smyth to ensure reciprocity at every stage after making the initial absolute threshold sufficiently small.
The labels in the canonical construction are the inverse pairs here: a rational-power dependence between different conjugates gives equal absolute exponents by equality of their positive heights, so compression and reciprocity identify only an inverse pair.
The polylogarithmic bound on \(u\) thus uses a fixed implied constant throughout the chain.

In the independent case the multiplicatively independent label representatives identify the full signed module with the actual span.
Even flips on a block separate any pair of its coordinate characters and also distinguish coordinates in different blocks.
The character projections, followed by transitivity, give irreducibility even over the complex numbers.
In the oriented case the independent supported alternating factors make the complex block sum-zero modules irreducible and pairwise inequivalent for their product.
Double transitivity gives irreducibility of each sum-zero module by the two-dimensional permutation commutant.
Consequently the transitively transported direct sum \(U\) is absolutely irreducible for the full group.
The map to the actual class space is nonzero on \(U\) because compressed oriented roots over different labels have different classes.
It is injective there; the image of block constants has dimension at most \(u<n-u\) and cannot meet the irreducible image nontrivially.
This step uses only the oriented-module splitting from the earlier argument, allowing its block-constant image \(B\) to be nonzero.

The actual product \(\beta\) on one oriented half generates precisely \(B\) through its conjugate classes: the lists of oriented halves are transitive and each one maps to its product.
Repeated products only reduce the degree, so \(\deg\beta\le2u\), \(h(\beta)\le b h(z)\) and \(m(\beta)\le2ub h(z)=m(z)\).
If the image is nonzero this gives a nontorsion unit, and taking a torsion-class product again preserves it as a rational subspace while preserving the new measure.
Each step takes ordinary products in the original conjugate group and strictly decreases the degree.
Inflating the subrepresentations through restriction of the absolute Galois group to the appropriate splitting field preserves their irreducibility.
At each genuine descent the dimension of the leading constituent exceeds the dimension of the whole remainder, so the resulting summands are pairwise nonisomorphic.
By semisimplicity and multiplicity one, each invariant subspace is the sum of a subset of these *actual* summands; no additional irreducible diagonal subspace can occur.

For \(d=2n\) write \(q,a,s'\) for the neutral inverse pairs, real exterior roots and pairs of nonreal exterior roots.
Ordinary complex conjugation gives, on the formal signed space, negative dimension \(q+s'=d/4+q/2-a/2\).
The full signed case retains it, while removal of the blockwise constants costs at most \(u\) in determining the leading constituent's dimension \(j\).
The real-place bound at the \(2a\) real roots yields \(a\le5\sqrt{m(w)d\log(8d)}\), also trivially at \(a=0\).
Since \(u\ll(\log(3d))^{212}\) and even the smallest stage degree tends to infinity uniformly, both errors are \(o(\sqrt{d\log d})\) across all stages.
It follows that \(j\ge d/4-o(\sqrt{d\log d})\), eventually \(j>u\) and \(d\le5j\).
Together with \(j\le d/2\) and \(d/2-u\le t\le d/2\), this proves both error estimates at the asserted \(j,t\) scales.
In particular \(j_i\) exceeds even the entire dimension of the later summands and a fortiori the sum of their negative dimensions.

For a nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), the power relation up to torsion makes \(V_x\) a nonzero rational submodule regardless of the degree of the radical or of any roots of unity.
It therefore gives a nonempty subset of the chain summands.
Conversely a sum of nonzero rational vectors from chosen summands can be represented after clearing denominators by an ordinary monomial in the original conjugates.
Its orbit spans exactly all chosen summands because the projections are nonzero and the modules are pairwise nonisomorphic.
The negative eigenspace dimensions add, and strict domination over the tail shows all subset sums are distinct, with the unique lowest value \(j_s=k(z_s)\).
For a prescribed \(W_i\subseteq V_x\), \(j_i\le k(x)\) gives the stated degree and rank bounds for \(z_i\).
If \(i\) is the earliest summand in \(V_x\), then \(t_i\le\dim V_x\le t_i+u_i\); applying the uniform estimate at this one index controls the entire \(V_x\) without summing errors over a growing number of indices.
For a least-degree budgeted realization of an actual \(W\), compression cannot lower its degree.
In its own decomposition \(W\) cannot lie in the lower-degree block image, since the block product would realize the prescribed subspace under the same budget.
Projection of \(W\) onto the leading irreducible summand must be an isomorphism and projection to the smaller block image must vanish.
This verifies the bounds for every such minimizing realization, rather than requiring \(V_z=W\).

Finally the unnumbered Voutier theorem gives a strict lower bound on \(d_s h(z_s)=m(z_s)\) with constant \(1/4\); its argument is the actual degree \(d_s\).
For small enough original budget, \(d_s>\exp(\mathrm e)\), where \(\log\log D/\log D\) decreases, and \(d_s\le5j_s\le5k(x)\).
This yields the printed inequality for \(m(w)\), uniformly in all nontorsion \(x\).
When \(m(w)\) exceeds the fixed small-budget threshold, the same right side is below that threshold for all sufficiently large \(k(x)\).
The asymptotic coefficient and the divergence of the least rank follow, while at a strict degree record any nontrivial descent would contradict first attainment.
