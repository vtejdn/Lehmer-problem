### The height and the orbit of the projected root

Once \(b/a\) is larger than the fixed degrees by more than one, the blocks are disjoint and both endpoint gaps are at least \(a\), also when an endpoint block consists of a monomial.
The two unit endpoints give algebraic integer units \(\alpha\) and \(t=\alpha^a\).
Coprimality eliminates zeros of \(A\) and \(B\) at \(t\), and writing \(1=au+bv\) yields \(\alpha=t^u(-A(t)/B(t))^v\).
Thus the \(d\) embeddings used in (2) count each conjugate of \(t\) once.
At each embedding the identity \(\log^+|A/B|=\log\max(|A|,|B|)-\log|B|\) gives (2), since the unit \(\alpha\) has finite-place contribution zero.
The correction is nonnegative before subtraction, and the product of the norms over factors, counted with multiplicity, is \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,A)|^a\).
Indeed evaluation on the roots of \(B(X^a)\) gives \(A(X^a)\), and each root of \(B\) has \(a\) preimages counted with multiplicity, including zero.
The unit leading coefficients give the claimed equality of absolute values, including constant \(B=\pm1\).

Polynomial growth in (2) gives \((b-wa)h(\alpha)\ll_{A,B}1\).
The two gaps bound each conjugate log modulus in absolute value by \((\log L)/a\).
For a unit their absolute sum is \(2dh(\alpha)\), so the all-root square estimate gives \(1/10\le(2\log L/a)d^2 h(\alpha)\) and hence \(d\gg a\sqrt r\).
With \(m(t)=dah(\alpha)\ll d/r\), the estimate for a polynomial maximum gives the displayed error in (1), since \(a/d\ll r^{-1/2}\) also absorbs the norm correction.
Jensen's identity writes \(I=m(B)+\int\log^+|A/B|\).
If both summands vanish, unit leading coefficient and Kronecker force every nonzero root of \(B\) onto the circle.
The continuous inequality \(|A|\le|B|\) there rules out such roots by coprimality, and the remaining monomial denominator has modulus one.
The maximum principle at \(A(0)=\pm1\) then forces a constant numerator, violating the rank hypothesis.

Small-height equidistribution applies to \(t\) since it is nontorsion, \(h(t)\to0\), and \(d\to\infty\).
Composing bounded continuous tests with \(\log|A/B|\) introduces possible discontinuities only on a finite null set.
This proves the weak limit, including for a denominator with circle zeros.
When the circle moduli are not identical, their equality set has measure zero by the Laurent polynomial identity for their squared moduli.
The corresponding exterior fraction and the limiting positive first moment follow, while the finite positive first moment is \(bh(\alpha)\) by the unit condition.

### Counting conjugates near each denominator factor

Each irreducible \(q\mid B\) is monic after choosing its sign, and \(\operatorname{Res}(q,A)\ne0\).
The same evaluation now bounds the mean of \(\log|q(t')|\) between zero and \(a\log|\operatorname{Res}(q,A)|/d\).
In a fixed exterior disk around a root \(\tau\) of \(q\), the equation \(|B(t')|=|t'|^{-r}|A(t')|\) first gives \(|t'-\tau|\ll e^{-c r}\) through the multiplicity \(e_q\).
Taking logarithms then gives \(-\log|q(t')|=(r/e_q)\log|\tau|+O(1)\), since \(r|t'-\tau|\) is bounded and both \(A\) and \(B/q^{e_q}\) stay away from zero.
Every counted conjugate has \(\log^+|t'|\) bounded below by a fixed positive constant, so \(K/d\ll1/r\) by the height bound.

The entire projected orbit lies in the compact annulus \(L^{-1}\le |t'|\le L\).
A neighborhood of an interior zero of \(q\) cannot contain a conjugate for large \(r\), since \(A\) stays away from zero there and \(|t'|^r|B(t')|\) decays uniformly.
For a circle zero \(\xi\), a distance less than \(1/r\) would give \(|t'|^r\le e\) and \(|B(t')|\ll1/r\), also impossible.
The simple zeros of the irreducible \(q\) then give \(|q(t')|\gg1/r\) off its exterior disks, including disks for other divisors.
This proof uses no reciprocity.

For \(n=\lceil r^2\rceil\), the integral of \(\log\max(|q|,1/n)-\log|q|\) on the circle is \(O_q(1/n)\) by integration near the simple circle zeros.
The polynomial-maximum bound for \((nq,1)\) has fixed degree and logarithmic coefficient bound \(O_q(\log(2r))\).
Substitution of \(m(t)/d\ll1/r\) and \(d\gg\sqrt r\) gives error \(O_{A,B}(\log(2r)/r^{1/4})\), uniformly for larger \(d\) by the decay of \(\log(2d)/d\).
Off the disks the truncation at \(1/n\) is inactive, while on a disk its excess is \((r/e_q)\log|\tau|+O(\log(2r))\).
Averaging and using \(K/d\ll1/r\) and \(a/d\ll r^{-1/2}\) proves (3).
If \(m(q)>0\), the left side cannot vanish for sufficiently large \(r\).
Multiplication by \(e_q\) and addition over the finitely many divisors yields \(m(B)\), the difference of positive first moments.

### Exact relations and the quartic

Put \(\epsilon_r=\log(2r)/r^{1/4}\).
For the one-exterior-root divisor \(q_0\), (3) gives \(d/r\ll k_0\) and \(e_0d/r=k_0+O(K\epsilon_r)\), since its log measure is the fixed positive \(\log|\tau_0|\).
Substitution in the remaining formulas (3) gives an ordinary real logarithmic form with coefficients \(e_0 k_{q,\rho}-e_q k_0 n_{q,\rho}\) and absolute value \(O(K\epsilon_r)\).
All its arguments and their inverses are fixed algebraic numbers in a single number field.
Clearing denominators in the product minus one and bounding its other conjugates gives the exponential lower bound for a nonzero form, which suffices in the stated logarithmic range.
For the power range the Baker–Wüstholz theorem gives \((2K)^{-C'}\) for every nonzero form, because the argument heights and field degree are fixed and the modified coefficient height is \(O(\log(2K))\).
Empty sets give the identity immediately.
For the finitely many nonempty sets a common \(C'\) suffices, and \(\eta(C'+1)<1/4\) makes \(K\epsilon_r\) smaller than this lower bound uniformly for \(1\le K\le r^\eta\).
This proves (5), with zero individual exponents in the independent case.
The external logarithmic-form theorem is used at its recorded statement scope.

For the quartic, the cubic factor has one root in each of the three stated intervals and is irreducible by the rational root test.
It is coprime to its reversal and neither \(2\) nor \(1/2\) is a cubic root.
Direct evaluation gives the three absolute resultant contributions \(21,7,128\), whose product is \(18816\).
On the circle the reversal makes \(|A/B|=1\), and on a fixed annulus free of zeros the radial derivative of \(\log|A/B|\) is bounded.
The equation \(r\log|t'|=\log|A/B|\) therefore puts every solution of that annulus exactly on the circle for large \(r\).
Farther outside, exponential smallness of \(|B(t')|\) places the solution in an exterior pole disk.
Thus \(K\) now counts every exterior conjugate and is positive by Kronecker.

The log-modulus vector of the three real cubic roots is nonzero with sum zero since their product is \(-1\).
Squaring a putative relation between the exterior moduli removes signs, so applying a Galois three-cycle gives relations against each cyclic translate of the vector.
These translates span the plane of sum zero because the three-cycle acts there with nonreal eigenvalues.
The relation vector, with zero in the remaining position, must therefore vanish.
In the resulting two-log comparison the coefficient magnitudes are at most \(K\).
The real algebraic arguments generate a field of degree \(3\le D\le6\), have absolute heights less than \((\log6)/3\), and have logarithms of magnitude less than \(\log3\).
The modified argument heights are thus at most one and the modified coefficient height is at most \(\log(2K)\).
Evaluation of \(18\cdot3!\cdot2^3\cdot192^4\log24\) gives less than \(4\cdot10^{12}\), so the claimed exponent \(10^{-14}\) satisfies the strict comparison above.
Equation (5) forces both exterior cubic counts to equal the count at \(2\), proving positivity of all three and divisibility by three.
For even \(a\), no real root can project by \(X^a\) into the negative pole disk, and conjugate pairs make its count even.
This gives divisibility by six.
Finally, for \(a=2\) and odd \(b\), the six exterior square roots of the poles are simple zeros of \(B(X^2)\).
Rouché's theorem gives one simple root per small disk for all sufficiently large \(b\), and the exterior localization exhausts the list.
Eventually \(6\le r^{10^{-14}}\), so every nontorsion factor must take all six and must occur once.
This proves the final irreducibility assertion after removing the cyclotomic factors.
