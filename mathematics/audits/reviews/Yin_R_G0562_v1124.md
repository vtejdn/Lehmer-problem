## The single-coordinate fibers, multi-coordinate bound and finite minimum

At 2026-11-24T22:44 UTC an agent outside the development of v67 independently reviewed its [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v67.md#statement) and both proof sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and scope without development dialogue or prior findings on v67.
Both alternatives, the equality case and the through-degree-55 minimum for groups avoiding \(\mathcal B_2\) hold.

**Coordinates and degree.**
In the rational signed permutation module the even flips distinguish any two basis lines by changing one of them and a third.
The corresponding rational character projections and transitivity on labels prove irreducibility.
The equivariant map sending a coordinate vector to its oriented root class is surjective onto \(V_w\) and is nonzero since \(w\) is nontorsion.
It therefore identifies the formal coordinates with an actual rational basis, so the support size of the class of \(x\) is well-defined after orientation.
All its conjugates span \(V_w\), and the ordinary complex-conjugation fixed dimension is \(a+s=t\).
Indeed a real off-circle pair contributes one fixed line, a circle pair contributes none, and the two inverse labels belonging to a nonreal exterior conjugate pair contribute a signed swap of fixed dimension one.
The radial-rank formula consequently applies to each nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), including radicals whose fields extend beyond the splitting field of \(w\).

For one nonzero coordinate there are exactly \(2n\) distinct conjugate classes \((u/N)[b']\), indexed by the actual conjugates \(b'\) of \(w\), with one consistent nonzero rational scalar.
Each sign on an individual coordinate is possible by using another label to preserve even parity.
In a common normal closure the map from the set of distinct conjugates of \(x\) to its class orbit is equivariant and surjective between transitive sets, so all fibers have the same positive size \(h\).
Equivalently, the fiber size is the index of the actual stabilizer in the class stabilizer.
Two representatives of one class differ by a root of unity, since a number with a torsion power is torsion.
All points of that fiber thus have the same absolute value.
For the fiber corresponding to \(b'\) its log modulus is \((u/N)\log|b'|\).
Exactly \(l\) classes make this positive for either sign of \(u\), as inversion interchanges the exterior and interior conjugates of \(w\).
Counting classes and then their fibers gives \(\deg x=2nh\) and \(l(x)=hl\).

**Other supports.**
For \(2\le j<n\), \(A_n\) is transitive on \(j\)-subsets because a permutation carrying the source subset to the target can have its parity adjusted inside the target subset or its complement.
For \(n\ge6\) at least one of these two sets always has two elements.
After lifting to the signed action, a zero coordinate compensates the parity of any sign choice on the support, giving \(2^j\) distinct vectors for each of the \(\binom nj\) supports.
For \(j=n\), the \(2^{n-1}\) even sign vectors already give distinct images.
The ratios \(2(n-j)/(j+1)\) decrease, so for \(2\le j\le n-1\) the smaller endpoint value \(2n(n-1)\) is a lower bound.
Hence the degree is at least \(\min(2n(n-1),2^{n-1})\) in all multi-coordinate cases, since the number of conjugate classes is at most the number of distinct actual conjugates.

The degree estimate of v64 applies to every reciprocal \(x\), including those with no neutral conjugate.
Its projection count uses all embeddings of \(x\) with their actual multiplicities in the orbit of classes and exactly \(2l(x)\) nonzero projections.
It gives \((\deg x)t\le2n l(x)\) here, since \(r(x)=t\) and \(\dim V_x=n\).
The orbit bounds at \(n=6,7,8\) are respectively \(32,64,112\), giving exterior-count multipliers \(8/3,32/7,7\) on \(t\).
For \(n\ge8\) doubling \(2^{n-1}\) from one degree parameter to the next preserves \(2^{n-1}\ge2n(n-1)\), as \(2(n-1)\ge n+1\).
In every stated degree the multi-coordinate bound strictly exceeds \(2t\), while \(0<l=a+2s\le2t\).
Thus only a single coordinate with \(h=1\) can attain \(l\), and \(w\) itself does attain it.

**Degree eighteen and the cutoff.**
For the degree-eighteen core in v63, the trace signature gives a single real exterior conjugate and two nonreal exterior conjugates exchanged by complex conjugation.
Its proven \(S_9\) action with all even flips meets both group hypotheses.
Thus \(n=9,\ l=3,\ t=2\), so multi-coordinate reciprocal outputs have at least \(16\) exterior conjugates and single-coordinate ones have a positive multiple of three.
This excludes every reciprocal nontorsion output with at most two exterior conjugates, whether it has a neutral conjugate or not.

As a separate check of the quoted approximation, the reviewer isolated the real and one nonreal exterior root directly on the polynomial \(P\) of v63.
For \(D=10^{10}\), take the Gaussian integers \(A=-11442985420\) and \(A=-2590239025+9992890388i\).
In \(D^{18}P((A+3Y)/D)=\sum B_jY^j\), integer binomial expansion gives the floors \(10\) and \(4\), respectively, of
\[
 \frac{\max(|\Re B_1|,|\Im B_1|)}
      {\sum_{j\ne1}(|\Re B_j|+|\Im B_j|)}.
\]
Rouché's theorem gives one root in each disk \(|Dz-A|<3\).
The first disk is invariant under conjugation, so its unique root is real, and the second gives its own separate conjugate disk.
All three disks lie outside the unit circle.
Bounding the negative real root's modulus and the second root's squared modulus by the coordinate rectangles yields \(1.219446874<M(P)<1.219446878\), confirming the quoted value to six decimals.

In the exhaustive lower range, each of the five signed real Salem cores has one exterior root and circle conjugates, and each of the eight complex Salem cores has two exterior roots and circle conjugates.
The previously verified degree-55 comparison fixes all thirteen lower cores strictly below \(1.2193\), with the degree-eighteen candidate alone between \(1.2193\) and \(1.2196\).
Every irreducible sign or power substitution of a lower core has that core's root up to sign among the powers of its conjugates, so its divisible group also contains a member of \(\mathcal B_2\).
This argument considers the full class \(\mathcal B_2\), which also allows two real exterior roots, because the candidate's general rigidity excludes that signature as well.
The candidate is therefore admissible and every smaller unit in the degree range is excluded.
