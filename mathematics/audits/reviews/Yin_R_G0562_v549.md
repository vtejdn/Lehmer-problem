### Necessity, signs and exact comparisons

For a reciprocal irreducible noncyclotomic input with \(M(P)\le T=1089/1000\), each root satisfies \(|\alpha|\le T\).
At \(r=21/25\), \(rT^2=24904341/25000000<1\), so \(|1-r\zeta\alpha|\ge\sqrt r\,|1-\zeta\alpha|\) for every primitive \(m\)-th root \(\zeta\).
The product of the factors \(|1-\zeta\alpha|\) is the absolute nonzero integer resultant.
Expanding the resulting logarithmic bound and using the inverse pairs gives the geometric tail in the program, since \(|s_k|\le D-2+T^k+T^{-k}\) and \(|c_m(k)|\le\varphi(m)\).
For the trace-box tail one can instead truncate at \(h=D/2\), then subtract the omitted terms through \(h\) using their absolute bounds.
The smaller of these upper bounds is valid, and substitution of \(\log(25/21)<7/40\) preserves necessity.
Multiplying all primitive roots by \(-1\) swaps the orders \(1,2\), swaps \(3,6\), and fixes \(4\).
Thus the substitution \(P(X)\mapsto P(-X)\) preserves both tested unions, including the absolute weights in the trace-box bound.

For the native program let \(I\) be the integer score from the floored weights, \(A\) the exact scaled score and \(U=\sum_{k\le N}B_k\).
The coefficient errors give \(|A-I|\le U\).
For a scaled cap \(C\), \(I>\lfloor C\rfloor+U\) implies \(A>C\), whereas \(I\le\lfloor C\rfloor-U\) implies \(A\le C\).
The remaining comparisons use a common denominator and the original strict inequality.
The first fifteen bounds sum to 132 and 135 in the two boxes, so at scale \(10^{10}\) the signed score arithmetic fits in 64 bits.

### Independent enumeration

I generated a prefix in increasing index order by Möbius inversion of \(s_k=\sum_{j\mid k}j e_j\).
If the preceding Euler exponents are integral, the next admissible values obey the single congruence
\[
 s_k\equiv a_k=-\sum_{\substack{j\mid k\\j<k}}\mu(k/j)s_j\pmod k .
\]
Each legal choice through \(h\) gives an integer coefficient prefix by the integral Euler product, and reflection at the middle coefficient gives its unique reciprocal completion.
For \(16\le k\le29\) all proper divisors of \(k\) are at most fourteen.
Thus for each legal length-fifteen prefix the remaining coordinates are independent, with exactly
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{B_k-a_k}{k}\right\rfloor+
 \left\lfloor\frac{B_k+a_k}{k}\right\rfloor+1
\]
choices per coordinate.
Their product gives a direct alternative to the native elimination tables.
I used bounds zero outside multiples of the selected prime divisors of \(D\) for the intersections in inclusion and exclusion, keeping the original caps in every test.
The \(q=2\) exclusion removes every sign-fixed polynomial, so division by two gives the orbit count.

I computed the Ramanujan weights from \(c_m(k)=\sum_{j\mid(m,k)}j\mu(m/j)\), rounded to nearest at the separate scale \(10^{12}\), and used the exact rational comparison whenever the integer error interval met the threshold.
I obtained 5,468,051 and 7,523,731 legal prefixes of length fifteen and recovered 2,712,147,567 and 6,985,779,621 complete vectors before removing powers or signs.
The constant-six prefix and its sign mate account for 262,144 and 524,288 sign orbits.
At length ten the positive prefix exceeds the order-one geometric cap by more than \(2/5\) in both degrees.
