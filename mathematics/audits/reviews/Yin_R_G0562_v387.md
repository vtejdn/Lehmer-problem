## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T12:53 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin checked the entire [degree-ten window calculation](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0168/readme.md#window).
The reviewer had no role in developing the calculation and received or consulted no proof-development discussion, author explanation or earlier verdict on it.
For each of the 24 primes the exact index and exponent agree with the submitted table, the isolated-pair weight equals \(\log I_p\), and the stated lower bound for the cofactor measure holds under the witness hypotheses.

**Exact arithmetic.** I inspected the submitted power-basis elimination and its independent check by ninth-order minors, then ran the program with a separate output destination.
Every field agreed with the [retained CSV](../../computations/outputs/Yin_J_G0168/window.csv).
For a separate computation, let \(A\) be the companion matrix of the displayed monic polynomial \(P\).
I formed the characteristic polynomial \(Q\) of \(A^p\) over the integers for every prime in \((134,268]\).
Each \(Q\) has nonzero discriminant, so its ten roots are the distinct powered conjugates and \(\alpha^p\) has degree ten.
The two power-basis discriminants give \(I_p^2=\operatorname{disc}Q/\operatorname{disc}P\).
The exponent \(s_p\) is the common denominator of \(P'(\alpha)/Q'(\alpha^p)\) in the integral power basis of \(\alpha\), by the conductor formula.
I computed that quotient by polynomial inversion over \(\mathbb Q[x]/(P)\).
The positive square root and least common denominator give \(a^2b\) and \(ab\), respectively, in every submitted row, including the denominator \(457\) at \(p=229\).

**Collision exclusions.** The input polynomial has \(|\operatorname{disc}P|=1332031009\).
For each new \(Q\) I independently computed both integral polynomial resultants against \(Q^{[2]}\) and \(Q^{[2]}+Q'\).
Their common gcd with \(I_p\) is one, and \(\gcd(I_p,p\operatorname{disc}P)=\gcd(p,\operatorname{disc}P)=1\) in all 24 cases.
If a powered residue has multiplicity at least three over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\), the coefficients of \(1,T,T^2\) in \(Q(b+T)\) all vanish there.
In particular \(Q^{[2]}\) is formed over the integers with coefficients \(\binom j2 Q_j\) before reduction, so this test applies also in characteristics two and three.
Such a residue forces divisibility of both resultants by \(\ell\), even if they can individually vanish modulo \(\ell\) for other reasons.
Thus over every divisor of \(I_p\) the original residues are distinct and a colliding powered pair is isolated at every positive depth up to its separation.
The index formula sums exactly these depths, giving \(E_p=\log I_p\), and the double-root resultant consequently satisfies \(\log H\ge4\log I_p\).

**Strict comparisons.** To check the window, the logarithmic data and all strict inequalities independently, I reduced each positive rational argument to \(2^k w\) with \(1\le w<2\).
For \(t=(w-1)/(w+1)\), I enclosed \(2\sum_{j=0}^{67}t^{2j+1}/(2j+1)\) by outward rational rounding, and did the same for \(\log 2\) at \(t=1/3\).
The positive-series tail in each case is at most \(2(1/3)^{137}/(137(1-1/9))<10^{-58}\).
This gives \(133<(\log 30)^4<134\), and an independent prime enumeration gave precisely the 24 tabulated primes.
Disjoint intervals establish the unique extrema of \(\log s_p/p\) at \(139\) and \(263\), and of \(5\log s_p/\log I_p\) at \(193\) and \(179\), respectively in increasing order of value.
Every concentration exceeds \(2.67\).

**Cofactor measure.** The support prime \(p\) contributes at least \(10\log p\) separately from the isolated pairs.
For two distinct occupied classes in an ordinary witness of degree at most \(161p\), a class of nonzero residue has row degree at most \(160\).
At most one class can attain \(161\), so the stripped degree sum is at most \(321\) and the exponent span is at most \(161p\).
Moreover \(C\le\int_0^1[2\min(t,1-t)]^{-1/2}\,dt=2\).
The Salem root lies between \(1\) and \(6/5\) by the exact endpoint evaluations, giving \(m(P)<\log(6/5)\).
Substituting these estimates into the resultant-to-measure inequality for a primitive witness gives exactly the displayed expression in J168.
Its least value is at \(p=139\) and lies between \(1.87586\) and \(1.87587\).
At \(p=263\) it lies between \(4.37180\) and \(4.37181\).
These are lower bounds for every eligible cofactor in this finite window, with no minimization over individual witnesses required.
