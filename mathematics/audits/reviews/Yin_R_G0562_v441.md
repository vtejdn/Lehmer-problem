## Low mass forces the new traces onto one uniform midpoint grid

On 2026-11-18T16:46 a separately assigned agent for Yin reviewed all three sections of [L462 v200](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v200.md#gap) and the stated inputs for the limiting measure, interpolation obstruction and positive constructions.
The reviewer took no part in developing this argument and received the fixed text and scope without development discussion or previous findings about it.
The rigidity below \(1/2+1/(2K)\), the liminf bound for the remaining parity, and the necessary and sufficient conditions for a sequence of total branch masses tending to one half hold as stated.

At a fixed Salem input of degree \(2s\) the full return has one reciprocal pair off the unit circle, is simple and has no roots at \(1,-1\).
Its other rational irreducible factors have all conjugates on the circle and are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
There are \(s-1\) old neutral traces and \(k=d/2-s\) new ones, with \(k>0\) eventually.
The actual branch residues are strictly positive at all of them.
Monic division in the rational branch function gives an integral expansion at infinity, and testing it against \(Q_0W\) kills the exterior pole.
It also kills every old neutral trace exactly, even when its residue varies.
For the symmetric lift of the new traces and the convention \(m_{d,r}=2\int z^r\,d\mu_d\), the test at \(r\ne0\) is \(Q_0 C_{|r|}\) and at \(r=0\) it is twice \(Q_0\).
This proves the integer convolution used at all frequencies.

The weak-limit input in L184 applies because \(\sigma\) is fixed, \(d\to\infty\), \(\log(d^2)=o(d)\), and the sequence of total masses under consideration is bounded.
The angular product estimate implies absolute continuity of arcsine probability with respect to every such limiting branch measure.
The integer Fourier–Stieltjes transform of its symmetric lift weighted by \(2Q_0\) has a finite sum of singleton and arithmetic-progression indicators on \(\mathbb Z\), by the statement of [Sanders, Corollary 12.4 and (12.2)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00041-020-09732-y).
These give an integral Laurent polynomial density and a finite atomic remainder, respectively.
Inversion symmetry puts the density in \(\mathbb Z[z+z^{-1}]\).
At a simple interior Salem trace, positivity forces that polynomial to vanish, since \(Q_0\) changes sign.
Division by \(Q_0\) and domination by the full branch measure as in L184 therefore give a nonzero nonnegative integral polynomial density relative to one half of arcsine probability.
Below total mass one its mean with respect to arcsine probability is the integer one, and each nonconstant coefficient on the circle has modulus strictly below one by positivity on an interval.
Thus the symmetric full lift has Haar part \(h/2\), and the remaining positive atomic measure has mass \(\delta=L-1/2\).
Lifting commutes with weak convergence because the symmetric average of a continuous circle test on the two preimages is continuous on the closed trace interval, including its endpoints.

For any chosen sequence of integers \(r_d\), the full lifts, the old weights on a fixed finite set and the measures \(2z^{r_d}\mu_d\) have simultaneous weakly convergent subsequences.
This uses the uniform variation bound and separability of continuous functions on the compact circle.
Write \(\mu\) for the positive limit of the new lifts and \(\theta\) for the complex limit of the twisted ones.
Testing against an arbitrary continuous complex \(\varphi\) gives \(\left|\int\varphi\,d\theta\right|\le 2\int|\varphi|\,d\mu\), hence \(|\theta|\le2\mu\).
The measure \(\mu\) is dominated by the full lift limit, so the Haar density of \(\theta\) has modulus at most one and the singular part has variation at most \(2\delta\).
This remains true if new nodes approach an old Salem conjugate.

Each fixed coefficient of \(q_*\theta\) is the limit of integers at the shifted frequency \(r_d+n\).
Its singular component has variation at most \(2K\delta<1\), and its absolutely continuous component has both Fourier tails tending to zero.
Consequently every sufficiently high absolute frequency is an integer of modulus below one and vanishes.
Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients gives \(q_*\theta=D h\) with \(D\) an integral Laurent polynomial.
In particular the singular part of \(\theta\) is supported on the neutral Salem conjugates, since \(q_*\) is nonzero at every torsion point.
The density bound gives \(|D|\le |q_*|\) almost everywhere, hence everywhere by continuity.
At any neutral conjugate of \(\sigma\) it follows that \(D\) vanishes.
After clearing Laurent powers, the monic minimal polynomial of that conjugate is \(P_0\), so \(D=q_*H\) with \(H\) integral Laurent.
It satisfies \(|H|\le1\) on the circle, and Parseval gives at most one nonzero coefficient, equal to \(1\) or \(-1\).
Thus the integral of the absolutely continuous component of \(\theta\) is \(0\) or \(\pm1\), and the remaining possible old atoms have total variation at most \(2\delta\).
If the uniform estimate (3) in L200 failed, choosing a witnessing frequency for each member of a violating subsequence would contradict this conclusion on a further convergent subsequence.

Since \(2\delta<1/K\le1/2\), the uniform estimate gives, eventually, a periodic three-valued rounding with maximum error strictly below \(1/K\).
The error convolved with the coefficients of \(q_*\) is an integer of absolute value below one at every residue.
The kernel of this convolution on the actual finite cyclic group is zero because its Fourier eigenvalues are \(q_*\) at roots of unity and the Salem minimal polynomial has no torsion zero.
Thus the rounded moments are the exact ones.
The zeroth moment is strictly positive and hence equals one, making \(2\mu_d\) a probability.
Equality in the triangle inequality for a character of a positive probability on its finite support holds exactly when the character is constant throughout that support.
For a chosen support point \(z_0\), these are precisely the characters trivial on the subgroup \(G\) generated by the ratios to \(z_0\).
They take the value \(z_0^r\) and all remaining Fourier coefficients are zero, giving uniform probability on the entire coset \(z_0G\).
Writing \(n=|G|\), inversion symmetry forces its defining value \(z_0^n\) to equal \(1\) or \(-1\).
The first value includes the forbidden point \(1\), while the second includes \(-1\) if \(n\) is odd.
Thus \(n=2k\) and \(z^{2k}=-1\).
Pairing inverse points gives the \(k\) simple zeros of \(C_k\), each with branch weight \(1/(2k)\) after restoring the mass normalization.

At these nodes the trace polynomial is exactly \(Q_0C_k\), so the integral inverse must satisfy the single-grid condition of L194.
For \(k\ge2\), \(A\) even and \(J_0=(Q_0-A)/x\), reducing \(Q_0V_{k-1}/2\) modulo \(C_k\) gives the integral representative \(J_0V_{k-2}+(A/2)V_{k-1}\).
An integral interpolant after multiplication by \(2-x\) therefore requires its value at two to be even because \(C_k(2)=2\).
In the excluded parity this value is congruent to \((k-1)+k\equiv1\bmod2\), since \(J_0(2)\equiv Q'_0(0)\).
Here \(k\to\infty\), so every convergent subsequence below the stated threshold contradicts this test.
For \(Q_0=x^2-3x-2\), the two real traces are \((3\pm\sqrt{17})/2\), with one exterior and one strictly interior.
The quadratic discriminant \(T^2-4\) is negative at the interior embedding and hence nonsquare in the real quadratic trace field.
This gives a degree-four Salem example, with \(K=8\) and lower bound \(9/16\).

For odd \(A\), the fixed-input integer test of L184 gives the independent positive gap above one half.
The condition \(x^2\mid\overline Q_0\) in L179 is equivalent to \(A\) and \(Q'_0(0)\) both even.
In that case the even construction has an integral inverse for arbitrarily large multiples \(k\) of four, new weights \(1/(2k)\) and old mass tending to zero.
For \(4\mid A\), the central correction \(\Lambda(A)\) of L181 is zero on its odd grids and the old mass again tends to zero.
Both constructions meet the square-constant requirement with \(d=2s+2k\).
Their coefficient lattice permits strictly positive old residues tending to zero along the chosen progressions, and the new stability sum grows only linearly in \(k\) against \(q=d^2\).
The strict inverse criterion therefore gives actual monic irreducible Pisot inputs.
These cases and the two obstructions exhaust the parities.
Attainment concerns a sequence at the given fixed input, and the sharp value of the gap constant remains open.
