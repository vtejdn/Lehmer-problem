### The commutator and its parity

Irreducibility of \(P\) gives irreducibility of \(f\) by reciprocal lifting, and excludes roots at \(X=\pm1\) in the stated degree.
The distinct inverse pairs therefore identify the trace kernel \(K=\operatorname{Gal}(L/T)\) with a subgroup of the coordinate flips.
If \(a,b\) are sign vectors, the multiplication in the signed permutation group is \((a,\sigma)(b,\rho)=(a+\sigma b,\sigma\rho)\).
Writing \(h=(v,\gamma)\), \(c=(u,\pi)\), one obtains \(hch^{-1}c^{-1}=((1+\pi)v+(1+\gamma)u,1)\) when \(\gamma\pi=\pi\gamma\).
Such a lift of any chosen three-cycle exists since the trace action contains \(A_n\).
On three fixed trace labels of both real types, \(\pi\) is the identity, and \(\gamma\) cycles the three signs of \(u\).
The signs on a real inverse pair and on a circle pair differ because complex conjugation respectively fixes and interchanges its roots.
The restriction of \((1+\gamma)u\) to the triple has weight exactly two for either possible mixed sign count.
It gives a nonconstant element \(w\in K\) even if additional coordinates of the commutator are nonzero.

Conjugating \(w\) by any signed lift of a three-cycle acts on \(w\) just by permuting its labels.
Choose three containing both coordinate values of \(w\).
Adding \(w\) to that translate now gives a vector of weight exactly two on the full label set.
The alternating group is transitive on unordered pairs, so \(K\) contains all their vectors, which span \(E_n\).
For \(D=\prod_i(x_i-x_i^{-1})\), each flip multiplies the corresponding nonzero factor by \(-1\), and \(D^2=\prod_i(y_i^2-4)=f(2)f(-2)\).
Consequently \(K\) is contained in \(E_n\) precisely when \(D\in T\), equivalently when the endpoint product is a square in \(T\).
The even space has codimension one, proving the two alternatives in (1).

The Salem case already has the vectors \(\mathbf1+e_i\) from conjugates of complex conjugation, whose differences give the even space using transitivity alone.
Christopoulos and McKee's Proposition 2.3 gives the Salem kernel alternatives and uses the discriminant square criterion, which agrees with the endpoint criterion by their equation (2.4).
The project proof for a particular endpoint product in C345 v272 and the four-fixed-label, prime-degree commutator in C345 v315 are both correctly attributed.
The three-cycle computation above verifies the claimed removal of the fourth fixed label and of the prime-degree condition under the stated alternating action. [Christopoulos and McKee, author manuscript, pp. 4–5](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/ws/files/4543610/SalemGaloispsp.pdf#page=4)

### Normal quadratic base change and the finite places

Assume \(K=E_n\) and \(i\notin T\).
If \(T(i)\subset L\), restriction of \(K\) would give a surjective linear map onto \(\operatorname{Gal}(T(i)/T)\simeq\mathbb F_2\).
Both \(T\) and \(T(i)\) are normal over \(\mathbb Q\), making the kernel of this map invariant under \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
The induced action on the group of order two is trivial, hence the map has a common value on all edge vectors \(e_i+e_j\).
The three edges of a triangle sum to zero, forcing their common value to vanish in characteristic two.
They belong to the orbit generating \(E_n\), contradicting surjectivity.
Thus \(L\cap T(i)=T\), since the intersection is intermediate in the quadratic extension \(T(i)/T\), and restriction gives \(\operatorname{Gal}(LT(i)/T(i))\simeq E_n\).
If \(i\in T\) the base is already \(T\).

Under the absolute unit endpoint condition, \(f(2)\equiv f(-2)\bmod4\) forces the product to be \(+1\), so the even-kernel case applies.
Each \(\delta_i=y_i^2-4\) is an integral unit, including in every larger base field.
At every finite place the integral polynomial \(X^2-y_iX+1\) has separable reduction since its discriminant is a unit.
After passing to the unramified local extension splitting the reduction, Hensel lifting splits the polynomial, including in residue characteristic two.
The compositum is therefore unramified over both \(T\) and \(T(i)\) at all finite places.
Since \(T(i)\) has no real place, Artin reciprocity gives the indicated quotient of its ordinary ideal class group and hence 2-rank at least \(n-1\).
If \(T\) contains a nonreal trace, every embedding of this rational splitting field has image \(T\), so \(T\) too has no real place and its ordinary class group surjects onto \(E_n\).

For \(F_i=\mathbb Q(y_i)\), irreducibility gives real embeddings of both trace types even if the originally chosen \(y_i\) is nonreal.
Thus both \(\delta_i\) and \(-\delta_i\) are negative in some real embedding, so neither is a square in \(F_i\).
The existence of a real embedding implies \(i\notin F_i\).
For \(s,t\in F_i\), the square \((s+it)^2\) lies in \(F_i\) only when \(st=0\), in which case its value is \(s^2\) or \(-t^2\).
Hence adjoining \(\sqrt{\delta_i}\) gives a genuine quadratic extension of \(F_i(i)\), unramified by the same local discriminant argument.
This gives the asserted even ordinary class number.

### Endpoint and root counts on the primitive quartic directions

The high-slope reciprocity and alternating action apply to the irreducible factor carrying the unique simple negative exterior root, with no positive real exterior root.
Inversion partitions the roots off the circle into pairs with one exterior member each.
For \(z=\rho e^{it}\), the imaginary part of \(z+z^{-1}\) equals \((\rho-\rho^{-1})\sin t\).
It follows that the factor has one trace below \(-2\), \(k-1\) nonreal traces, and \(n-k\) distinct traces strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\).
The nonreal ones occur in conjugate pairs, so \(k=1\) or \(k\ge3\) odd.
The uniform comparison \(2n/(rk)\to1\), with \(k\ge1\), gives \(n/k\to\infty\).
In particular \(n\ge5\) and \(n-k\ge2\) eventually, providing the three fixed labels of both types for every possible exterior allocation.

For odd \(a\) and even \(b\), direct substitution gives parent values \(2,-2\) at \(1,-1\).
The primitive cyclotomic table removes precisely one \(\Phi_4\) when \(4\mid b\), and removes nothing when \(b\equiv2\bmod4\).
The degree of the full trace after removal is \(2a+b/2-1_{4\mid b}\), odd in both cases.
The conversion from the reciprocal value at \(-1\) to its trace value at \(-2\) therefore changes sign.
The full trace endpoints are both \(h=1\) or both \(h=2\), respectively.
Monic trace factors have positive values at \(2\) since every real trace is smaller than two and the nonreal conjugate pairs contribute positive products.
Their absolute values at \(-2\) divide \(h\), and their endpoint values are congruent modulo four.
A factor taking the value one at \(2\) consequently takes the value one at \(-2\).
When \(h=2\) a unique factor receives the two at \(2\), and multiplication to \(H(-2)=2\) forces it to receive the two at \(-2\) as well.
The negative factor thus has square endpoint product in both residue classes.

For \(4\mid b\), all factors have unit endpoints, so every asserted rank and even-class-number consequence applies to the negative factor, including the quotient over \(T\) itself when \(k\ge3\).
For \(b\equiv2\bmod4\), equality of the two endpoints still gives the even kernel.
The unit-discriminant argument applies to the negative factor in that residue class when its two values are one, leaving the case where this factor receives both twos without a class group conclusion from that argument.
The independent trace degree comparison separates the Salem and mixed alternatives by \(n<r\) at sufficiently large \(r\).
The class group bounds themselves accommodate either allocation.
