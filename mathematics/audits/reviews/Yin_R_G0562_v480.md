### The two full-degree extrema and the period restriction

The high-slope parity criterion gives odd \(n\) in both families, and \(n\to\infty\).
For an irreducible trace, L220 with modulus one gives a good prime whose minimum odd degree is \(n\), equivalent to a degree-\(n\) irreducible reduction.
This also realizes the largest possible degree of any modular factor.
The factors of degrees at most five exclude such a reduction throughout \(p\le Y\) once \(n>5\).

For the seed of degree \(2j\), reduction of \(X^t=1\) modulo two implies that its monic degree-\(2j\) reduction divides the nonzero polynomial \(X^t-1\), hence \(t\ge2j\).
Writing \(r=2j-4+kM/a\), the nearest-integer condition \(|r/2-j|\le1/2\) gives \(3\le kM/a\le5\), including endpoint ties.
Since \(k\ge1\) and \(t\mid M\), this forces \(2j\le5a\) and \(b\le a(5a+1)\).
It contradicts \(a\le(\log b)^{B_0}\) along an unbounded sequence of \(b\).
The obstruction applies to this prescribed period construction and leaves the general nearest-degree question open.
