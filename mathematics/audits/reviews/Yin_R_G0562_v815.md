## Periodic density, cutoff ranges and exact binary fibers

The arguments establish both periodic density bounds and the exact fiber estimate with their stated quantifiers.
The asymptotics apply along sequences of nontorsion algebraic units with \(m\to0\).

For a minimal signed support, shift the last exponent before a positive gap \(b\) to zero.
The partial sum on the left is a nonzero algebraic integer, including when some of its exponents become negative, since \(\alpha\) is a unit.
Multiplying the two embedding bounds over all conjugates gives \(1\le j^d e^{-mb}\), using the unit norm to identify the product of the contracting moduli.
Summing over adjacent gaps puts the span strictly below the stated period \(q\).
Every translated support then uses distinct residues, so its probability of occurring is \(p^j\), even across the period boundary.
There are at most \(qA_j\) translates at weight \(j\).
Deleting one initially selected residue for every present translate costs at most the number present, including when deletions coincide.
Every forbidden relation contains a minimal support, and nontorsion excludes a two-term relation at distinct positions.
These facts justify (4) and the periodic lift.

For a fixed ordered sign pattern, division by the first term gives a nondegenerate equation on \(j-1\) powers.
Vanishing of a nonempty subsum on the left would contradict minimality, and nontorsion makes the vector of powers injective on normalized exponent lists.
The [inspected statement of Amoroso and Viada, Theorem 6.2](../literature/AV09/Yin_U001.md#retained-primary-statement) applies over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) with both the number of unknowns and the tuple-group rank equal to \(j-1\).
Including all \(2^j\) patterns gives \((\log A_j)/(j-1)\le C_0g^4\log(2g)\) whenever \(A_j>0\).
At \(p=e^{-B}/4\) the expected deletion density is at most \(p/12\).
For \(g\le a_\theta(L/\log(2+L))^{1/4}\), the factor \(\log(2g)\) is at most a constant times \(\log(2+L)\) for large \(L\), giving (2) on decreasing \(a_\theta\).
At \(k=1\), equality of binary residues modulo the minimum is equality of the corresponding sums at \(\alpha\).
Thus v570 supplies \(0<D_1(P)\le m/\log 2\) and the density ratio is at least \((\log 2)m^{\theta-1}\).

Failure of (5) for every positive \(a\) supplies counterexamples with \(m\to0\) and \(jm/L\to0\), by taking \(a=1/n\) and successively smaller measures.
The bound for each fixed weight excludes every bounded range of \(j\) on this sequence.
Some full sign pattern then has more than \((c/m)^{j-2}\) nondegenerate ordered lists up to translation.
For that pattern \(H_2^2=4^{j-2}\), so the count exceeds the threshold \(H_2^2(c/(4m))^{j-2}\) in v672 with its fixed base \(c/4\).
The assumed conjugate supplies the required fixed \(\eta\), and v672 gives \(jm/L\gg_{c,\eta}1\), a contradiction.
For \(p=Km\) and \(c=1/(8K)\), the expected deletion density is at most \(4K^2m^2\sum_{h\ge1}4^{-h}\).
The same estimate works with the period for any smaller cutoff.
If \(g=o(L/m)\), every fixed \(K\) therefore eventually gives density divided by \(m\) at least \(K-O_K(m)\).
A Salem number has \(\log|\alpha|=m\), as required with \(\eta=1\).

For the binary sums the radius \(N\max(1,|\beta|)^N\) bounds each embedded value uniformly over all weights.
Intervals of width \(1/2\) in each real coordinate, including both coordinates of one embedding per complex pair, give at most \((8N)^de^{mN}\) boxes.
Two values sharing a box have difference of modulus less than one at every embedding, hence are equal by integrality.
Pigeonholing all \(2^{n_N}\) words proves (6), and cancellation gives a signed relation on the differing positions of any two distinct words in the fiber.
Their Hamming distance therefore exceeds \(g\).
For each fixed periodic mask of density \(\delta>0\), \(d\log(8N)/n_N\to0\) and \(mN/n_N\to m/\delta\).
The trivial upper rate is \(\log 2\).
Masks of density at least half the supremum exist by its positivity, so the lower rate tends to that upper rate in the two specified regimes after taking the lower limit in \(N\) at each input.

This review uses the fixed density and arity theorems at their stated scopes and the external unit-equation theorem at the cited statement-inspection depth.
