## Independent proof finding

Both norm inequalities and their joint consequence at two hold under the stated reversed-block hypotheses.
The least-degree assumption makes the oriented root product torsion, the real embedding makes it a rational sign, and signing all roots preserves this property.
Thus the two monic polynomials have the same constant term in \(\{1,-1\}\).
For \(q>R\), the real evaluations are positive and nonzero, and the expansion in v809 applies on this entire interval.
The strict coefficient inequality there uses \(b-1\ge2\) positive summands with sum \(\log R\).

At a prime \(P\mid q\), both evaluations reduce to that common sign modulo \(q\mathcal O_{K,P}\), so \(v_P(D)=0\) and \(v_P(w-1)\ge v_P(q)\).
For even \(q\) and \(P\mid2\), this also gives \(v_P(w+1)\ge v_P(2)\), including at ramified dyadic primes.
At odd primes dividing an even \(q\), the first factor alone supplies the required valuation of \(2q\).
At any other prime where \(w\) has negative valuation, \(v_P(w^h-1)=h v_P(w)\), exactly canceled by \(D^h\).
Every remaining valuation is nonnegative, and \(L>0\) ensures a nonzero ideal in each comparison.

The two real factors in the absolute norm have product \((e^{hL}-1)(1-e^{-hL})=4\sinh^2(hL/2)\).
The \(n-2\) remaining embeddings come in conjugate pairs interchanged by \(\tau\), so \(\tau w=w^{-1}\) bounds each factor by two.
Multiplying by \((ND)^h\) and using the local lower bounds gives \(q^n\le 2^n\sinh^2(L/2)ND\) and, for even \(q\), \((2q)^n\le2^n\sinh^2(L)(ND)^2\).
Taking the positive square root in the second inequality gives exactly the stated exponent and constant.

For instance, for \(0<m\le1/10\) one has \(R<1.1\) and \(0<L(2)\le 2mR/(2-R)<3m\).
Using \(\sinh y\le2y\) for \(0\le y\le1\) gives \(ND(2)\ge\max\{1/(9m^2),2^u/(6m)\}\), independently of \(b,u\).
Inversion exchanges opposite valuations, so any denominator prime lies over a split prime of \(E\).
The bound eventually forces nonempty support while allowing higher powers of the same primes.
At \(q=2\), the base \((2-R)/(2b)\) in the earlier resultant estimate is less than one whenever \(1<R<2\), confirming the stated comparison.
