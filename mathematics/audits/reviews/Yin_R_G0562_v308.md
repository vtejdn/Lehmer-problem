The whole argument supports (4)–(8) and the stated prime-family application, including the strict transfer threshold, its equality-of-minima endpoint and the measure cost of the signed product.

**Possible unit constituents.**
After taking a power that clears torsion, each factor contributing to \(w\) comes from a conjugate of a unit fixed by \(G_\alpha\).
The direct sum of the corresponding permutation modules therefore maps onto a space containing the cyclic module of \([w]\).
Semisimplicity allows passage to this submodule, and averaging by \(G\) kills all unit classes by the rational norm.
On the pair sums the augmentation space restricts irreducibly to a \(p\)-cycle.
On the pair differences an order-\(p\) signed lift acts regularly, so a proper rational direct decomposition would contain the unique cyclic invariant line.
Transitivity forces every coordinate of a line generator to have the same nonzero absolute value, whereas \(\tau\) has opposite signs at its real and neutral fixed labels.
This excludes that line and identifies the signed constituent with the nonzero root-class quotient.
The \(\tau\)-fixed dimensions are \(p-C-1\) for the augmentation space and \(R+C\) for the signed space, also when \(C=0\) or \(N=1\).

**Exterior conjugates and signed cost.**
For any rational unit class represented after scaling by a unit \(u\), vanishing of its logarithm at the selected embedding is equivalent to \(u\bar u=1\), since \(u\bar u\) is positive real and its class vanishes exactly when it is torsion.
The logarithm is consequently injective over \(\mathbb Q\) on the fixed space.
The projected orbit spans that space, so it exhibits at least its dimension in distinct nonzero absolute logarithms.
Reciprocity supplies the positive sign in every case, and distinct exterior values of \(w=y^n\) lift to distinct exterior conjugates of \(y\).

When the signed constituent occurs, any surjection to it sends the cyclic generator to a nonzero vector carrying the same stabilizer character \(\lambda\).
An element actually sends \(w\) to \(w^{-1}\), so \(\lambda\) takes both signs.
Each coset outside the forced-neutral set supplies an exterior conjugate of \(w\) in its own antipodal pair of classes, even when two different pairs have equal absolute logarithms.
Conversely, the coordinate vectors span the root space, so one has a nonzero character projection.
Multiplying that projection by the size of its label orbit gives coefficients \(0,\pm1\) with nonzero root class.
The raw exponent vector has character \(\lambda\), so some conjugate of the resulting product is exactly its inverse.
Cosets cover all actual inverse pairs of this product, and only cosets outside the forced-neutral set can contribute an exterior root.
The two counts yield \(b(\beta)\le c(J,\lambda)\le b(w)\le b(y)\).
Every conjugate of \(\beta\) again has signed subset exponents, giving an absolute logarithm at most \(\sum_i|\log|x_i||=\log M(P)\).
Summation over the distinct exterior conjugates gives precisely the factor \(b(\beta)\), and nontorsion of the unit gives strict positivity.
If only the unsigned constituent occurs, its fixed dimension gives \(b(y)\ge\kappa\).
At \(N=C+1\) this gives \(b(y)\ge\kappa=e\ge c_*\), which already suffices for \(c_K=c_*\) at this endpoint.

**One root field.**
A quadratic subfield would be real because \(K\) has a real embedding.
Its compositum with the degree-\(p\) trace field would equal \(K\) and split every real trace place, contradicting the presence of a neutral place.
Thus the only possible even degree of a reciprocal nontorsion element of \(K\) is \(2p\).
The order of \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(K)\) divides both \(2p\) and \(2R\) by free action on the corresponding embeddings, so the only nonidentity automorphism is inversion on \(\alpha\).
For any reciprocal generator \(w\) this automorphism also inverts \(w\), and \(w+w^{-1}\) generates its full fixed field.
Over each real split place one of the two real images is exterior, while each nonsplit place gives two complex conjugate reciprocals of modulus one.
Over a complex place the trace is nonreal, so the four distinct images comprise two exterior roots and their inverses.
The resulting \(e=R+2C\) persists as a lower bound under radical lifting and is attained by \(\alpha\).

**Prime family and external inputs.**
In C345v306 the trace polynomial reduces to \(X^p-X-1\) modulo \(p\), and the Frobenius orbit of any root consists of its \(p\) distinct translates.
The neutral real trace gives a negative discriminant at a real embedding and therefore doubles the degree upon lifting.
The sign changes at consecutive \(k\pi/p\) above \(\pi/3\) and interlacing with the two explicit grids of roots of \(C_p-X\) give \(N_p=2p/3+O(1)\).
Monotonicity outside the trace interval gives \(R_p=1\), so \(R_p+C_p=(e_p+1)/2\) grows unboundedly and \(N_p\ge C_p+1\) eventually.
For the specialization \((1,p)\) the shortest nonzero integral relation has sup norm \(p\), as required by the full Laurent-polynomial specialization theorem. [Deninger, Theorem 14, p. 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.0604#page=12)
Jensen's formula in the second variable gives the displayed integral \(I\), positive on an interval, and concavity bounds \(3I\) above by \(\operatorname{arcosh}(1/2+3\sqrt3/(2\pi))<3\log\theta\).
Thus \(M(P_p)<A\) eventually for every fixed \(A>\exp I\), while the enlarged reciprocal minima tend to infinity.
Integrality follows from the power equation for each radical and its inverse, so Smyth's theorem applies whenever the output itself satisfies \(M(y)<\theta\). [Smyth, §5.1, equation (15)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701397#page=7)
