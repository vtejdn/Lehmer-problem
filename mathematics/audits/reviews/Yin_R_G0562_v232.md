### End blocks and the separating gap

By additivity of logarithmic Mahler measure and its nonnegativity on nonzero integer polynomials, a sequence of positive divisor measures tending to zero gives positive irreducible factor measures tending to zero.
Integer content greater than one costs at least \(\log 2\).
Passing to a subsequence fixes the five nonzero coefficients since their primitive heights are bounded.
For a sufficiently small factor \(S\), the endpoint and Smyth bounds give a reciprocal nontorsion monic unit up to sign.
With \(h=m/d\), the radial inequalities give \(wh=(wt)m/(dt)\ll m^2\).
The two trace comparisons then force both \(nh\gg1\) and \((D-n)h\gg1\), since both trace differences are nonzero outside the two orientations of the norm-product exception.
Hence \(d/\Delta\ll m,\ \Delta t\gg 1/m^2\) and \(w/\Delta\ll m^2\).
At each exterior root both the original and reversed equations hold.
In the identities for \(A(1/x)\) and \(B(1/x)\) the opposite binomial at \(x\) is bounded uniformly by \(O_H(1)\) since \(w\log|x|\le wt\).
This gives (2) with a single constant for all exterior conjugates.

For \(|\mu|\ge1\) the roots of \(B_0 X^b+B_1\) have modulus \(|\mu|^{-1/b}\le1\).
With \(G=D-b-n\ge\Delta-b\), we have \(b=o(G)\) and \(G\to\infty\).
In the dyadic construction \(S=S_0(X^k)\), the at most \(2d/k\) radial segments end at the squares and fourth powers of roots of \(S_0\).
The \(k\) rotations of an exterior root of \(S\) give \(k\) factors bounded by \(L_0|x|^{-G}\) in each rotation product, while the remaining factors cost at most \(L_0|x|^b\) each.
Consequently \(j_0=\lfloor(G-5b)/2\rfloor\) gives exponents \(2j_0-G+5b\le0\) and \(4j_0-2G+10b\le0\) for the two endpoint bounds in the common containing region.
Here \(j_0\ge0\) eventually, \(j_0+3b\asymp G\), and radial monotonicity includes the complete segments and all interior endpoints.
Their joint capacity is at least one by the arithmetic input of v170.
The capacity deficit implies \(j_0+3b\le768s\log T\), with \(s\le 2d/k\) and \(\log T=\log 2+10k\log L_0\).
Thus \(G\ll_H d\), contradicting \(d/\Delta\ll m\).
This uses the containing-region calculation at \(G\) itself and requires no fixed lower bound for \(G/D\).
Reversal gives \(G=n-a\ge\Delta-a\) and the same contradiction for \(|\lambda|\ge1\).

### Counting all roots at the two radial scales

When \(0<|\lambda|,|\mu|<1\), (2) at the largest root gives \(bt\to-\log|\mu|>0\) and \(at\to-\log|\lambda|>0\).
It follows that \(a\asymp b,\ b\to\infty\) and \(\Delta/b\to\infty\).
Equality of the signed rational powers gives a common root of the end binomials by Bézout's identity applied after dividing \(a,b\) by their gcd.
Its modulus differs from one, so the v231 common-root gap applies under exactly the stated exceptions.

For every remaining root with \(\Delta u>C_0\), choosing \(C_0\) large in (2) bounds \(e^{-bu}\) away from both zero and one.
In particular \(c_1\le bu\le c_2\), and inversion gives \(|\mu x^b-1|=O(E)\) with \(E=e^{-c_1\Delta/b}\).
Multiplying \(x\) by \(\exp(-\log(\mu x^b)/b)\) for the branch near one produces a center \(\xi\) with \(\xi^b=\mu^{-1}\).
Since \(a/b=O(1)\), replacing \(x^{-a}\) by \(\xi^{-a}\) costs \(O(E)\).
The disks of radius \(\kappa/b\) are disjoint and have \(\log|X|\ge c_3/b\) when \(\kappa\) is fixed sufficiently small compared with both \(-\log|\mu|\) and the angular spacing constant \(2\pi\).
On each boundary, \(B(X^{-1})=B_0(1-(X/\xi)^{-b})\) is uniformly bounded away from zero by the uniform exponential limit on the stated annulus.
Meanwhile \(|A(X)+cX^n|\ll_H |X|^n\).
The quotient by the upper block tends uniformly to zero using \(D-n\ge\Delta\).
The upper block has exactly one zero in each disk since its other zeros are the other centers and possibly zero.
Rouché's theorem therefore counts at most one root of \(S\) there even when multiplicities in \(P\) are counted.
On the \(b\) centers, raising to power \(-a\) has fibers of size \(f=\gcd(a,b)\) and image of size \(b/f\).
Its image has radius \(|\mu|^{a/b}\gg1\), so an \(O(E)\) chord neighborhood meets \(O(1+(b/f)E)\) image points by their angular spacing.
Counting full fibers gives \(O(f+bE)\) roots above the cutoff.

### Quadratic mass and the remaining families

For \(0<u\le C_0/\Delta\), summing \(u^2\le C_0u/\Delta\) gives at most \(C_0m/\Delta\).
Every root above the cutoff has \(u^2\ll b^{-2}\).
Reciprocity pairs squared radial logarithms exactly, including zero contributions from unit-circle roots, so part A of C855 v2 gives \(d\sum_{u>0}u^2\ge1/20\) in the original degree.
After multiplication by \(d\) the first contribution is \(O(m^2)\), and \(dE/b=O(m(\Delta/b)e^{-c_1\Delta/b})=o(1)\).
The remaining term must give \(df/b^2\gg1\).
When the two rational powers differ, the integer cross-determinant of their reduced fractions is nonzero, hence their separation is at least \(H^{-(a+b)/f}\) for either sign of either rational.
Both powers can be compared with \(x^{-ab/f}\) at the largest root using \(|z^q-y^q|\le q|z-y|\) on the closed unit disk.
Taking logarithms and using \(a\asymp b\) gives \(\Delta t\ll_H b/f\), including the logarithm of the linear prefactor since \(b/f\ge1\).
Thus \(\Delta\ll_H b^2/f\ll_H d\), the required contradiction.

In the v230 product the four-term factor has coefficients \(sz,-s,1,-z\) in increasing order and is taken to \(-s\) times itself under reversal.
The bottom coefficient pair of its expanded multiple is \(-Cz,C\), so the reduced numerator and denominator of \(z\) are bounded by \(H\).
In the v231 product the four-term factor has coefficients \(\sigma,-k,-\sigma k,1\) and reversal multiplier \(\sigma\), with \(k=A_0/B_0\) of rational height at most \(H\).
Each remaining binomial prescribes a positive power of its root equal to a rational number of modulus different from one.
A root of that binomial cannot be an algebraic unit because a rational algebraic unit is \(1\) or \(-1\).
The two products and their reversals therefore give precisely the asserted reduction for unit factors.
For four terms v229 applies with \(K\le4H\), and v181 covers one through three terms.
In the sample multiple the two ends are \(1\), the interior end coefficients are \(2,3\), and the middle coefficient is \(-8\).
This excludes the first product by its absolute endpoint ratio and the second by its middle coefficient in both orientations, as well as \(A=B\).
The value \(-1\) at one and the positive leading coefficient ensure an exterior real root.
