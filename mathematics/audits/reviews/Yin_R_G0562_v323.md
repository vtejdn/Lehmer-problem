### Factor selection and neutral conjugates

Write \(p=840q+k\) with \(0<k<840\), so \(b=420q,\ a=b+k\), \(k\) is odd, and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
The modulus \(420\) torsion exclusion applies with both signs positive.
For \(r>1\), \(H_p(r)>0\), and at \(-r\) the two negative factors \(1-r^a\) and \(1-r^p\) have positive product while the subtracted monomial contributes \(+r^p\).
Reciprocity, \(H_p(0)=1\) and the endpoint values \(H_p(1)=7,\ H_p(-1)=1\) exclude all remaining real roots.
Every monic irreducible factor is positive at both endpoints, since each complex conjugate pair contributes a positive real square modulus there.
Their positive integral values at \(1\), counted with multiplicity, multiply to the prime seven, so a unique factor \(S_p\) takes the value seven and occurs once.
It satisfies \(S_p(-1)=1\) and is a unit polynomial since the monic factors have constant coefficients dividing \(1\).

The balanced specialization result supplies eventual reciprocity, a uniform positive lower bound \(c_0\) for \(\log M(S_p)\), and \(\log M(H_p)\to I_{23}\).
The factorwise upper bound follows by additivity since monic factors have nonnegative logarithmic measure.
Here \(b=(p-k)/2>p/4\), and each exterior root contributes at most \((\log 6)/b\), so \(d_p\ge e_p\ge c_0p/(4\log 6)\).

For the positive signs the seven monomials of \(z^{-p}H_p\) have frequencies \(0,\pm p,\pm a,\pm b\), all with coefficient one.
Thus at a root \(z=e^{i(u+iv)}\), \(y=\cos(p(u+iv)/2)\) satisfies \(4y^2+4ty-1=0\) with \(t=\cos(k(u+iv)/2)\).

### Repeated roots and rational traces at seven

Over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_7\), differentiation at a repeated root \(z\) of \(H_p\) gives \(F_X(x,y)=0\), with \(x=z^a,\ y=z^b\), since \(z\ne0,\ 7\mid b,\ 7\nmid a\).
The case \(y=-1\) gives \(F(x,-1)=x\ne0\).
For all remaining \(y\), the quadratic in \(X\) has nonzero leading coefficient \(y(1+y)\).
Its discriminant is \((1+y+y^2)^2-4y(1+y)^2=y^2(s^2-2s-7)\) with \(s=y+y^{-1}\).
In characteristic seven this gives \(s=2\) or \(s=0\).
The first case forces \(y=1\), then \(F(x,1)=2(x-1)^2\) forces \(x=1\), and Bézout gives \(z=1\).
The second case gives \(y^2=-1\) and double root \(x=-1/(2(1+y))=(y-1)/4\).
Squaring gives \(x^2=-y\) since \(8=1\) in the residue field, so \(x\) has order eight and \(y\) order four.
The order of \(z\) has two-primary part eight because \(a\) is odd.
Since \(4\mid b\), the two-primary part of the order of \(z^b\) is at most two, ruling out this case.

The recurrence \(C_{j+1}=TC_j-C_{j-1}\) gives \(C_j(2)=2,\ C_j'(2)=j^2\).
Thus \(T_p'(2)\equiv2p^2\ne0\pmod7\) and the trace root \(2\) is simple.
Any further repeated trace root would give a repeated root of \(H_p\) above it, whereas \(z=1\) lies above \(2\).
This proves squarefreeness of the full trace polynomial modulo seven.
For a rational trace \(u\), Frobenius preserves the two roots of \(Z^2-uZ+1\), so each corresponding \(z\) has order dividing six or eight, including the coincident-root cases.
Since \(6\mid b\) and \(4\mid b\), \(z^b\in\{1,-1\}\).
The same evaluations of \(F(x,\pm1)\) and coprimality leave \(z=1\) as the only possibility.
The value \(T_p(2)=7\) shows that this rational residue trace does occur.

### From one ramified place to all sign changes

For a root \(\alpha\) of the monic irreducible reciprocal \(S_p\), the conjugates \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) are distinct and give the same trace \(t\).
The equation \(Z^2-tZ+1=0\) then gives \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(t)]=2\).
The minimal polynomial \(Q_p\) of \(t\) divides \(T_p\) over the integers and \(z^{d_p}Q_p(z+z^{-1})=S_p(z)\) by monicity and equality of degrees.
Thus \(Q_p(2)=7,\ Q_p(-2)=(-1)^{d_p}\), and the distinct reductions of its roots include the single rational residue root \(2\).
In \(K=\mathbb Q(t)\), the ideal of \(2-t\) has norm seven, hence is a single prime with residue degree one and valuation one.
The ideal of \(2+t\) has norm one.
Simple-root lifting in a finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_7\) whose residue field splits \(\overline T_p\) splits \(T_p\) and hence \(Q_p\).
In particular the chosen place of \(K\) has absolute ramification index one and local degree one.
The element \(t^2-4\) has odd valuation one there, so its quadratic radical is ramified, tamely since the residue characteristic is seven.
The order discriminant is \(t^2-4\), and its quotient by the field discriminant is locally the square of the integral index.
This gives relative discriminant exponent one there and zero at every other finite place, including the places above two.

Now work in the splitting field of \(Q_p\), completed at any place above seven with \(v(7)=1\).
Exactly one trace \(t_i\) reduces to two, none reduces to minus two, and \(\prod_j(2-t_j)=7\).
Hence the distinguished \(t_i^2-4\) has valuation one and the others are units.
Their unit square roots lie in a common unramified extension, by lifting the distinct roots of the residual unit quadratics after a residue field extension.
Over the subfield generated by these unit square roots, the distinguished radical still has valuation \(1/2\) and generates a totally ramified quadratic extension.
Its involution belongs to inertia in the completed splitting field, since the intermediate field is unramified and the last extension preserves its residue field.
Via the decomposition subgroup this yields a global automorphism of the splitting field of \(S_p\) fixing every trace and every other quadratic radical and interchanging the two roots above \(t_i\).
Every automorphism of the trace splitting field extends to the splitting field of \(S_p\), and the trace group is transitive by irreducibility of \(Q_p\).
Conjugation therefore produces a separate flip at each inverse pair.

Frobenius of the unramified trace splitting field fixes precisely those labels with residue in \(\mathbb F_7\), since reduction is injective on the roots.
It has exactly one fixed label.
In a preserved perfect matching the partner of a fixed label would be a second fixed label, so the trace action admits no such matching.
All hypotheses of the full-flip case of v305 now hold for \(S_p\), making its matching correction zero and the minimum exactly \(e_p\), attained at an original root.
The saturation in v305 allows torsion multipliers, arbitrary positive radical denominators and unrestricted nontorsion outputs.
The argument applies to the selected factor irrespective of the factorization of the rest of \(H_p\) or the primality of \(d_p\).
The eventual cutoff for every \(A>\exp I_{23}\) follows from the factorwise upper limit, and every fixed exterior budget is eventually smaller than \(e_p\).
In the original trace field, the single ramified place for the quadratic root extension contributes local degree one out of the total \(d_p\) above seven, giving fraction \(1/d_p\).
The result leaves open the existence of measures tending to one and makes no assertion about exterior counts for units outside this radical.
