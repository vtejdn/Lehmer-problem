## The dyadic criterion, the square-root field and the fixed-base examples

At 2026-11-25T03:06 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v132 independently reviewed for Yin its fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v132.md#statement) and all four proof sections.
The reviewer received the fixed mathematics and its inputs without development discussion or previous findings on v132.
The attainment and splitting criteria, both finite unit-seed searches, the Lehmer exclusion and the infinite families over every prescribed totally real base hold.

For \(P=X-a\) the reflected coefficient conjugate is \(1-a'X\), giving exactly the displayed \(f_+\) and \(f_-\).
On the circle the difference of the two squared moduli is \((a-a')(a+a'-2\Re z)\).
If \(a'\ge a>1\), strict domination already fails at \(z=1\).
Thus the exact two real conditions are \(a>a'\) and \(c=a+a'>2\), which conversely imply \(a>1\).
The positive exterior roots have traces \(R_++R_+^{-1}=c\) and \(R_-^2+R_-^{-2}=2+(a-a')^2\), by the constant terms and the quadratic equations.
At the nonsplit embeddings these traces respectively become \(2\Re\gamma\) and \(2-4(\Im\gamma)^2\), in \([-2,2]\).
An endpoint as a conjugate would make the trace equal to that rational endpoint, impossible at the marked embedding.
Each trace generates the given base, since the unique marked value would otherwise occur with multiplicity greater than one among all base embeddings.
The reciprocal quadratic attached to either trace is irreducible over that totally real field, having negative discriminant at each of its other places.
This proves the Salem property, the degree \(2u\) and the asserted trace field for either target.

For the minus sign \(a-a'=\sqrt{t-2}=d>0\); the root is nonreal above every other embedding, forcing the quadratic coefficient field to be \(E(d)\).
The relative trace and norm of \((c+d)/2\) are \(c\) and \((c^2-t+2)/4\).
Their integrality is equivalent to integrality of the seed; at another embedding its squared modulus is precisely this relative norm.
Thus the inequalities, congruence, strictness and positivity clauses in (2) are necessary and sufficient.
If the square class lifts modulo four, adding a rational multiple of four makes a chosen representative positive at the marked place.
Dirichlet's logarithmic unit lattice supplies a totally positive unit expanding there and contracting at all other places by taking a lattice point in that open logarithmic cone and squaring the unit.
A positive power is one modulo \(4\mathcal O_E\).
Multiplying the representative by successive powers now preserves the congruence, makes the marked value exceed \(d\) and \(2\), and makes all other values strictly smaller in absolute value than \(\sqrt{2+t_i}\).
This gives infinitely many distinct strict integral seeds, including over fields ramified above two.
For unit seeds, each nonmarked norm value is at least \((2-t_i)/4>0\); its absolute unit norm bounds the absolute marked norm by the reciprocal product of these lower bounds.
Together with the exact formula for \(c^2\) and the bounds on \(c_i\), this is a bounded box in the full lattice of integers of \(E\).

With \(r=\sqrt\alpha\), \(s=r+r^{-1}\), one has \(d=r-r^{-1}\) and \(\mathbb Q(r)=E(s,d)\), as \(t\in\mathbb Q(r^2)\).
The relative norm of \(X^2-dX-1\) is \(X^4-tX^2+1\); its rational norm is therefore \(S_\alpha(X^2)\).
If \(s\notin E\), the totally real quadratic extension \(E(s)\) differs from \(E(d)\) by their signatures.
Their compositum has degree four over \(E\), proving irreducibility of the rational norm and identifying \(r,-r\) as its two exterior roots.
If \(s\in E\), the common field \(\mathbb Q(r)=E(d)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) has the involution \(r\mapsto r^{-1}\).
At the other places squaring gives neutral values, so \(r\) is Salem, and its minimum and the minimum of \(-r\) are distinct because their sole exterior conjugates have opposite signs.
Their degrees exhaust \(S_\alpha(X^2)\); the second minimum is monic \(S_r(-X)\) since the degree is even.
The same field calculation identifies the condition \(s\in E\) with squareness of \(\alpha\) in its own field.
For the target \(\theta^2\) of any Salem number \(\theta\), strict dominance gives full degree on squaring and \(\mathbb Q(\theta^2+\theta^{-2})=\mathbb Q(\theta+\theta^{-1})\).
The trace difference from two is \((\theta+\theta^{-1})^2-4\), which both supplies the congruence and realizes \(\theta\) as \(R_-\).

For the plus sign the relative trace of \(a\) must be \(t\).
The constraints on \(\nu\) in (3) say exactly that the quadratic for \(a\) has distinct real roots at the mark, complex roots at all other places and the required modulus bounds there.
The larger marked root exceeds one.
The other marked root has the sign of \(\nu\), and a nonsplit equality \(\nu_i=1\) forces \(\nu=1\) by injectivity.
A strict integral seed always exists with \(\nu=1-v^j\) for sufficiently large \(j\); the choice \(\nu=1\) instead realizes the specified boundary unit.
The positive parent is already the reciprocal minimum over \(E\), so its rational norm from the coefficient field repeats \(S_\alpha\) twice, whether or not it splits there.
Splitting is equivalent to equality of the two quadratic extensions of \(E\).
The lower bounds \(t_i^2/4>0\) at the other places give the finite box for unit norms \(\nu\).
For strict seeds with positive marked values, the absolute norms of \(\nu\) and \(1-\nu\) give successively \(\nu>1\) and \(\nu-1>1\) at the mark, since all nonmarked factors of both products are strictly between zero and one.
Thus \(t^2/4>2\), equivalently \(\alpha>1+\sqrt2\).

I independently verified \(X^5F(X+X^{-1})\) equals the displayed Lehmer polynomial of v16.
Modulo two \(F\) has neither a linear factor nor the factor \(T^2+T+1\) (the remainder there is \(T\)), so the quintic is irreducible and separable.
Its signs at \(-2,-3/2,-1,-1/2,1\) alternate, and its signs at \(2,9/4\) are negative and positive.
This also verifies the four neutral trace places and one marked place directly.
The odd discriminant makes the index of the power order odd, giving the full ring modulo two and four.
Exact square reduction on the basis \(1,t,t^2,t^3,t^4\) gave the sole root \((1,1,0,1,0)\) modulo two among 32 residues, and none among 1024 residues modulo four.
Equivalently the square of that sole representative differs from \(t-2\) by the coefficient vector \((6,2,-8,2,8)\); adding any multiple of two before squaring leaves that vector unchanged modulo four.

For the infinite unit family take \(A=4(\nu-1)\ne0\) using the contracting totally positive unit.
The nonconstant integral polynomial \(N(Aj^2-1)\) has leading coefficient \(N(A)\ne0\).
A sufficiently large even \(j\) gives a prime ideal dividing \(Aj^2-1\) and avoiding \(2Aj\).
At that prime \(c'=Aj\) satisfies \(c'^2-A\equiv0\), and \(2c'\) acts invertibly on \(\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p^2\).
Consequently an integral translate modulo \(\mathfrak p^2\) has exact valuation one for its square minus \(A\), also after making it positive at the mark and multiplying it by powers of a suitable unit congruent to one modulo \(\mathfrak p^2\).
The marked coefficient \(c\) tends to infinity while the other \(c_i\) tend to zero.
For the roots of \(Y^2-cY+\nu\), the eventual inequalities \(c>1+\nu\) and \(c_i^2<4\nu_i\) give \(a>1>a'>0\), strict nonmarked moduli and integral units.
The negative target traces \(2+c^2-4\nu\) tend to infinity at the mark and have all other values strictly between \(-2,2\).
The valuation one of \(t+2=c^2-A\) rules out its squareness for every such target.
The same varying \(c\)'s produce distinct positive-sign targets.
In the explicit example \(t=15+8\sqrt3\) has quadratic minimum \(T^2-30T+33\) and \(N(t+2)=17^2-3\cdot8^2=97\).
Taking the reciprocal quadratic trace substitution and then replacing \(X\) by \(X^2\) gives the asserted monic octic exactly.
