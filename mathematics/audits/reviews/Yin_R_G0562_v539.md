### Reduction, resultants and discriminants at 101

Exact fourth-power comparisons give maximum allowed one-norm 58 at \(p=101,b=57\), so a nonzero tested coefficient vector remains nonzero on reduction.
Both primitive factors are monic, hence reduction preserves their degrees.
Writing \(F=P_1P_2\), the integral divisibility \(F^2\mid G\) forces every irreducible factor of \(\bar F\) to divide \(\bar G\) and \(\bar G'\).
The two characteristic-\(p\) identities then put each such factor in both \(\bar U\) and \(\bar V\).
Thus the radical of \(\bar F\) has degree at most 57, with inter-factor coincidences included in this joint count.
For a monic polynomial \(S\), multiplication by \(T\) on \(\mathbb F_p[Y]/(\bar S)\) has nullity \(\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar T)\), by cancelling the gcd and counting the resulting ideal.
Applying this to multiplication by \(F'\), and using integer Smith factors of the integral multiplication matrix, gives divisibility of \(\operatorname{disc}F\) by \(101^{55}\) or \(101^{57}\) at total degree 112 or 114 respectively.

If \(P_i\) divided \(A\), its reciprocity would give an integral signed quotient at width \(b-d_i\), and \(P_i(\alpha_j^p)\ne0\) for every designated root because \(A(\alpha_j^p)\ne0\).
Both double roots would survive division, as would the nonzero evaluations of the first row.
When \(d_i\le b\) the quotient has width at most one, contradicting the derivative degree bound.
When \(d_i>b\) the row's degree already rules out divisibility.
Both individual resultants \(\operatorname{Res}(P_i,A)\) are consequently nonzero.
Their integer multiplication matrices have at least \(s_i=\deg\operatorname{rad}(\bar P_i)\) elementary divisors divisible by 101, while the root-product upper bounds are \(58^{d_i}M(P_i)^{57}\).
For their product, \(M(P_1)M(P_2)<2^{1/4}\) suffices once for both factors together.
By independently comparing fourth powers of integers, I found that the largest integer \(s\) with \(101^{4s}\le58^{4d}2^{57}\) is 51 at \(d=56\) and 53 at \(d=58\).
At \(d=112,114\) the corresponding values are 100 and 102.
These give individual losses at least five and joint loss at least twelve.
Multiplication by each \(P_i'\) now yields the individual discriminant divisibilities and the divisibility of their product by \(101^{12}\).
