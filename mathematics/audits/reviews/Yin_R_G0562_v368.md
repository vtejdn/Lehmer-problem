# Independent review of the finite two-class cofactor test

<a id="finite-two-class-test-review"></a>
## Finding on the constants, the coefficient content and the degree window

At 2026-11-18T11:10 UTC an independently assigned reviewer for Sheng Yin checked all three sections of [the fixed ledger analysis](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v153.md#test).
The reviewer did not develop that analysis or receive its development dialogue.
The sufficient condition \(p\,m(P)\ge32\log(12D)\), the interval \([m(P)/3,2m(P)]\) for the minimum divided by \(p\), and the separate degree interval \([pd/2,p(d+1))\) all follow under the stated degree-preservation hypothesis.
Here the cofactor removes exactly two copies of \(P\), and its logarithmic measure omits scalar content.

### Degree costs and the common factor

For two nonempty rows in positions \(0\le r<s<p\), the Euler determinant in the base variable is
\[
      W=(s-r)UV+pX(UV'-U'V).
\]
The coefficient at degree \(\deg U+\deg V\) is \(\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)[s-r+p(\deg V-\deg U)]\), with a nonzero bracket modulo \(p\).
Let \(\beta=\alpha^p\), let \(Q\) be its monic minimal polynomial, and let \(c\) be the common order of the two rows at \(\beta\).
After extracting \((x^p-\beta)^c\) from the two columns, their Wronskian contains its square by the product rule.
Adding one column to the other leaves the residual sum vanishing at \(\alpha\) to order at least \(t-c\) when \(P^t\mid F\), and one differentiation loses at most one order.
Hence the determinant vanishes to order at least \(2c+\max(0,t-c-1)\ge t+c-1\).
The power map has nonzero derivative at \(\alpha\), and every conjugate of \(\beta\) gives the corresponding zero of \(W\).
Thus \(Q^{t+c-1}\mid W\), integrally by monicity.
Taking \(t=2\) gives \(d\le\deg W\le2\deg F/p\).
A single row would require \(Q^2\) in that row, so its original degree is at least \(2pd\).

Conversely both row degrees at most \(d\) give \(2d+2\) rational unknowns.
Evaluation and first differentiation at \(\alpha\) give at most \(2d\) rational linear conditions, since the two values lie in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Distinct residue classes make a nonzero kernel vector a nonzero polynomial, and the one-row degree bound forces both components to occur.
If both vanished at \(\beta\), each would be a nonzero rational multiple of \(Q\).
The remaining binomial would have to vanish at \(\alpha\), since \(Q(x^p)\) vanishes simply there.
This forces \(\alpha^{s-r}\) to be a rational unit, hence \(1\) or \(-1\), contrary to nontorsion.
Clearing denominators and dividing the two integral rows by a primitive integral representative of their gcd therefore preserves the double zero and both rows, and gives degree at most \(pd+s<p(d+1)\).
Gauss's lemma applies even when the row gcd is nonmonic.

### Finite measure bounds

The two Fourier columns are orthogonal with squared norm \(p\).
Their squared two-by-two minors sum to \(p^2\), so there is a minor of modulus at least one.
For its two rotations the determinant after division by \(F_1F_2\) is the difference of their logarithmic Euler derivatives.
Its modulus is at most the sum of \(|x-z|^{-1}\) over the roots of the two polynomials, at most \(2D\) terms with multiplicities.
For \(1/2\le |z|\le2\), \(|e^{it}-|z||^2\ge 2\sin^2(t/2)\).
The average of \((\sin u)^{-1/2}\) on \(0<u<\pi\) is at most \(2\), by symmetry and the chord bound on \([0,\pi/2]\).
For the other radii the inverse square root of the distance is bounded by \(\sqrt2\).
Subadditivity of the square root and Jensen's inequality give the error \(2\log(4D)\) in \(m(W)\le2m(F)+2\log(4D)\).
Circle zeros have integrable logarithms, and undoing a monomial and the base substitution preserves \(m(W)\).

For the divided-derivative construction, three values at each embedding give \(3d\) real coordinates in the box count.
The derivative losses have total exponent \(d(0+1+2)=3d\) on \(1+D\).
For \(b=16d,\ n=32d+2,\ D=pn/2\), the coefficient of \(p h\), where \(h=m(P)\), is \(3n/[2(n-3d)]<48/29\).
Since \(6n\), \(1+D\), and \(2n\) are at most \(12D\), the remaining log coefficient is at most \(1+6d/(n-3d)<35/29\).
Consequently \(m(F_0)/(p h)\le48/29+35/(29\cdot32)=1571/928<7/4\).
One row would cost \(3ph\), since \(Q^3\) would divide it and degree preservation gives \(m(Q)=ph\).
For two nonzero rows the determinant forces \(2+c<7/2+1/16<4\), so \(c\le1\).
Removing a primitive integral row gcd removes precisely \(c\) zeros at \(\alpha\) and preserves at least two copies of \(P\).
Its log measure is nonnegative, as is the log measure of any nonzero integer content subsequently removed.
Both rows survive the divisions, their degrees do not grow, and the resulting \(m(R)/p\) is at most \(2h\).

For the converse estimate, removal of all integer content from a witness leaves its multiset of primitive irreducible factors unchanged.
Its normalized measure is \(2h+m(R)\), also when \(R\) contains further copies of \(P\).
Since \(Q\mid W\), the determinant bound gives \(m(R)/p\ge h/2-(2/p)h-\log(4D)/p\).
The threshold bounds the last term by \(h/32\), so \(p\ge24\) gives at least \(37h/96>h/3\).
The upper construction supplies a nonempty set at bounded measure, and coefficient finiteness for its primitive cofactors of bounded degree then gives a minimum.

### The degree-only threshold

The retained [Voutier author manuscript](../../literature/pdf/Vou96--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=4) defines absolute logarithmic height as \(\log M/d\) on page 1 and gives \(d\,h(\alpha)>2/(\log(3d))^3\) in Corollary 2 on page 4 for nonzero nontorsion algebraic numbers of degree \(d\ge2\).
Comparison with the [source record](../literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) confirms that the ledger's \(h\) is the full \(\log M\).
Multiplication by the proposed prime bound gives \(ph>32\log(12D)\).
For \(d<p-1\), the [power-field criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#power) supplies degree preservation.
In degree ten the endpoint product is \(12\cdot161\cdot32768=63\,307\,776<20^6<e^{18}\), while \(\log30<7/2\).
The sufficient threshold is therefore less than \(16(7/2)^3\cdot18=12\,348<16\,384\).
The source check covers the stated external bound and its normalization. Voutier's proof and numerical parameter optimization lie outside this finding.
