### Recovering the common roots and their multiplicities

Both endpoints of \(H\) are \(a(0)[T^B]a\), since all exponents of \(b(T)b(1/T)\) have absolute value at most \(B-1\).
At a common root \(y\ne0\) put \(t=y^n\) and \(w=y^u\).
If both coefficients \(b(t)\) and \(a(1/t)\) of \(w\) in the two equations vanish, the remaining terms \(a(t)\) and \(b(1/t)\) also vanish, contrary to the hypothesis.
Thus one equation gives \(w\in\mathbb Q(t)\).
For integers \(i,j\) with \(ni+uj=1\), one recovers \(y=t^iw^j\).
This proves both equality of the two number fields and injectivity on the common root set.
Direct expansion cancels the mixed term in the submitted identity for \(H(Y^n)\).
The rational functions multiplying \(A\) and \(A^*\) there are regular at \(y\), so \(H(Y^n)\) vanishes to at least the smaller of the two orders.
The derivative \(ny^{n-1}\ne0\) identifies this order of \(H(Y^n)\) with the order of \(H\) at \(t\).
Summing over distinct \(t\) includes all gcd multiplicities in degree at most \(2B\).
The sufficient seed coprimality excludes a common zero of \(a(T)\) and \(T^{B-1}b(1/T)\), as required.

Let \(v\) be the common field degree.
The primitive integral minimum of \(t\) divides \(H\) integrally, and the remaining integral polynomial has nonnegative logarithmic measure.
The coefficient one-norm of \(H\) is at most \(\|a\|_1^2+\|b\|_1^2\).
The identity \(h_{\rm abs}(y^n)=n h_{\rm abs}(y)\), multiplied by \(v\), now bounds the logarithmic measure of the primitive minimum of \(y\) by \(L/n\).
For \(2\le v\le2B\) the adopted Voutier bound gives logarithmic measure strictly greater than \(2/(\log(3v))^3\ge 2/(\log(6B))^3\), and for \(v=1\) a rational number other than \(0,1,-1\) has logarithmic measure at least \(\log2>1/(\log6)^3\).
Condition (1) excludes both nontorsion cases, including nonintegral common roots.
Reciprocity up to sign of every cyclotomic polynomial ensures identical multiplicities in \(A,A^*\), including at \(1\) and \(-1\).

If \(y\) has order \(m\), the gcd degree gives \(\varphi(m)\le2B\), and \(\varphi(m)\ge\sqrt{m/2}\) gives \(m\le8B^2\).
The prime condition on \(n\) makes \(y\mapsto y^n\) a permutation of primitive \(m\)-th roots for each candidate \(m\).
Choose the nonnegative representative of \(j\equiv un^{-1}\bmod m\), with \(j=0\) at order one.
At these roots the factor \(y^u=t^j\) may be inserted after Euler differentiation.
Before evaluation the chain and product rules give the operators \(n\mathcal E\) and \(u+n\mathcal E\) on the two coefficient polynomials, respectively.
Rational coefficients make vanishing at a primitive \(t\) equivalent to divisibility of the evaluated polynomial by \(\Phi_m\).
At a nonzero root of multiplicity \(k\), the first nonzero Euler derivative is the \(k\)-th, with value \(y^k A^{(k)}(y)\).
Thus the failed test at \(k=0\) excludes an order, and a root that passes the initial test has exact multiplicity at most \(2B\).
Filtering by factors of \(H\) is valid since \(t\) has the same order.
For instance \(B=u=1,\ n=101,\ a(T)=100+T,\ b=-101\) has \(H(T)=100(T-1)^2\) and satisfies (1).
Here \(A(Y)=100+Y^{101}-101Y\) has a double zero at one, so the final test \(k=2B\) really can be needed.

In the representative window write \(X=\log(3d)\).
With bounded \(h\), \(B=4e=O(X)\), \(L=O(X^4)\) and \(n=d/e\ge d/X\), so \(n\) eventually exceeds both \(8B^2\) and \(L(\log(6B))^3\) uniformly.
The cyclotomic gcd is monic with constant term of absolute value one.
Dividing the unpowered row by it preserves nonzero endpoints and drops the degree by exactly its degree.
Its substitution at \(x^p\) still has only roots of unity, and division at the diagonal preserves all nontorsion multiplicities and the measure.
These facts place no restriction on how the original support or jets change.

### The two actual diagonals

For \(|T|=1\), the product over \(x^n=T\) equals \(G(T)^n-(-\sigma)^nT G^*(T)^n\).
Its modulus is at most \(2|G(T)|^n\).
The nonzero endpoint coefficients and disjoint classes of the two summands make \(F\) nonzero, so its circle logarithm is integrable and the finitely many fibers meeting a zero may be omitted in the integral.
Equal-weight averaging over the fibers gives the upper bound of (3).
Take a primitive integral associate \(J\) of the rational gcd of \(G,G^*\).
Reversal preserves this gcd up to sign because the polynomial and its reversal both have nonzero endpoints.
Writing \(G=Jg\) gives \(G^*=\pm Jg^*\) and the coprime integral pair \(g,g^*\).
The resulting diagonal has span \(D=n\deg g+1\), also when \(g\) is constant.
Its circle row integral is \(m(g)\) because \(|g|=|g^*|\) on the circle.
The single-graph bound gives
\[
 m(F)-m(G)\ge -\{4m(g)+2\log(CD)\}/n .
\]
Indeed the factor removed from \(F\) is \(J(x^n)\), of measure \(m(J)\), exactly the measure removed from \(G\).
Since \(m(J)\ge0\) and \(D\le ns+1\), the displayed lower bound implies (3), including nonprimitive \(G\).

For each four-class row, the two distinct classes modulo \(p\) preserve the combined coefficient content of \(a,b\) in \(G\), and the two classes modulo \(n\) preserve primitivity in \(F\).
The seed component has degree \(4ep\), and the fixed construction gives divisibility by \(P_0^2\) and \(P_0(x^n)^2\) in the respective diagonals.
Both monic divisors have logarithmic measure \(2h\).
After their removal, deleting the cyclotomic parts changes neither measure, and leaves every additional nontorsion copy in its respective quotient.
Consequently the difference of the two actual cofactor measures equals \(m(F)-m(G)\).
The uniform representative estimate \(\log(2dp)=o(ph)\) and the four-class upper bound \(m(G)=O(ph)\) give an absolute difference \(O(ph/n)\).
The orbit determinant gives each lifted cofactor separately the lower bound \((1-o(1))ph\), and the upper side of (3) transfers it to its individual seed cofactor at the same window prime.
A fixed positive multiple of \(p\) gained in a lifted lower bound would transfer to the seed with loss at most \((\log2)/n=o(p)\).
Neither comparison produces such a gain.
