# Review of the height cost for cyclotomic perturbations of positive-constant Pisot seeds

## Independent whole-proof finding

The review was performed at 2026-11-17T18:58 UTC on [C345v289](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#statement), including its explicit constants, asymptotic coefficient, nonempty example and selected-inverse consequence.
The reviewer was separately assigned and did not participate in developing this proof.
The inputs were the fixed candidate, necessary mathematical dependencies, review scope and applicable rules; no proof-generation dialogue, author explanation or earlier R conclusion was supplied or consulted before forming this finding.
The accepted source statement of Voutier and the established controlled-inverse theorem are used at their stated interfaces, with the limits below.

The whole stated result is supported.
No mathematical gap was found in the inspected argument.

For the return identity, a monic real polynomial with unit-circle roots and constant coefficient minus one has reversal \(-C\), including repeated cyclotomic factors.
Thus reversal of \((Z-q)C\) in degree \(k+1\) is \((qZ-1)C\), and the cancellation in (5) is exact.
Since \(U(0)=0\) and \(\deg U\le k-1\), both \(ZU\) and \(U^\dagger\) have degree at most \(k\), while their constant coefficients vanish.
The return therefore has degree exactly \(D=k+2\), leading and constant coefficients one, and is reciprocal.

The positive-return input applies because the Pisot minimal polynomial has constant \(q\ge2\).
The norm gives \(\theta>q\), and \(f(1)<0\) gives a real returned root \(\sigma>1\).
The Rouché argument in [L462v32](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) shows that there is at most one exterior root, counted with multiplicity.
Reciprocity gives its interior inverse, and the integer constant of an irreducible factor forces that inverse to belong to the same factor as \(\sigma\).
That factor is reciprocal; it is either quadratic or a Salem minimal polynomial of even degree at least four.
The Schwarz–Pick calculation in [C345v4](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#two-points) has positive denominators and gives exactly
\[
 0<E=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\le(1-1/\theta)(\theta-q)<\theta-q.
\]
In particular the return cannot consist solely of cyclotomic factors.

For the quadratic branch, the integer trace is at least three, so \(E\ge1>b_D\).
For the Salem branch, the [inspected Voutier statement](../literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) applies to the degree \(r\) of \(\sigma\), with \(r h(\sigma)=\log\sigma\) and \(4\le r\le D\).
Writing \(h_0(x)=\log\log x/\log x\), its derivative changes sign only at \(e^e\), its maximum is \(1/e\), and \(h_0(4)>1/(2e)\).
It follows that \(h_0(r)\ge h_0(D)/2\) throughout the required interval.
The elementary inequality \(2\cosh t-2\ge t^2\), applied at \(t=\log\sigma\), therefore gives
\[
 E\ge(\log\sigma)^2
   >\frac1{16}h_0(r)^6
   \ge\frac1{16\cdot64}h_0(D)^6=b_D.
\]
The factor \(2^{-10}\) and its strictness are thus valid at every supported degree, without a large-degree exception.

The root equation and the bounds on every cyclotomic factor give
\[
 (\theta-q)(\theta-1)^k
 \le |U(\theta)|
 \le H\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\theta^j
 <\frac{H\theta^k}{\theta-1}.
\]
The strict last inequality remains valid at \(k=2\).
The function \(t(1-1/t)^{k+1}\) has positive logarithmic derivative on \(t>1\), so replacing \(\theta\) by \(q\) yields exactly the second strict inequality in (2).
No estimate on the individual coefficients of \(C\), factor multiplicities or number of factors is needed.
Also \(U=0\) is impossible under the Pisot-minimal-polynomial hypothesis, so its integral height is indeed at least one.

Taking logarithms and using \(\log(1-1/q)\ge-1/(q-1)\) gives (7), with the omitted negative triple-logarithm term safely absorbed into the stated upper bound for large \(k\).
On the proposed subsequence, setting \(A=(1+\eta)/(1-a)\) gives \(\log q\ge\log k-\log\log k+O(1)\) and \((k+1)/(q-1)\le A^{-1}\log k+o(1)\).
Thus the left side of (7) has leading coefficient \(1-(1-a)/(1+\eta)>a\), while its remaining logarithmic terms are \(o(\log k)\).
This proves the exact limsup coefficient \(1/(1-a)\), including \(a=0\).
For \(q\ge ck\), one may even use \((k+1)/(q-1)\le3/c\), since \(q\ge2\) and \(k\ge2\), to obtain a uniform positive lower bound for \((1-1/q)^{k+1}\).
Consequently (4) has the claimed dependence on \(c\).

The example expands to \(f=Z^4-2Z^3-2Z^2+2\) and satisfies Eisenstein's criterion at two.
Its derivative is the stated \(2Z(2Z+1)(Z-2)\), and the critical values are \(f(-1/2)=29/16\), \(f(0)=2\) and \(f(2)=-6\).
Together with its positive leading coefficient, these values give exactly two real roots.
The values \(f(4/5)=66/625\), \(f(1)=-1\), \(f(5/2)=-43/16\) and \(f(3)=11\) place them in the stated intervals.
Their product is strictly greater than \((4/5)(5/2)=2\), so the nonreal conjugate pair has product, hence squared modulus, strictly less than one.
The larger real root is therefore a Pisot number, with this irreducible quartic as its minimal polynomial.

For the final consequence, the [controlled-inverse statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v31.md#statement) supplies degree-\(d\) seeds with \(q/d\to\infty\) in the selected windows along any hypothetical Salem sequence tending to one.
In a representation of the present form, \(k=d-1\), so the large-norm height estimate applies with a fixed positive \(c\).
Its lower bound diverges and excludes bounded \(H\) for those representations.
This does not exclude general positive-constant Pisot seeds or establish a uniform excess gap.

The review checks the complete local proof and the applicability of its stated dependencies.
It rechecks the positive-return root count and the two-point estimate, while accepting Voutier's established theorem and the controlled-inverse theorem at their inspected statements.
It does not re-audit Voutier's original proof or the full earlier controlled-inverse construction, and it makes no Task-completion or unrestricted Lehmer claim.
