Fix a rational prime \(p\) and the selected unramified extension \(U\).
Completion identifies \(\mathcal O_F\otimes\mathbb Z_p\) with \(\prod_{v\mid p}\mathcal O_{F_v}\).
For each \(v\), the embeddings of the maximal unramified subfield of \(F_v\) into \(U\) are exactly the \(f_v\) residue-field embeddings, since the residue degree of \(U\) is a multiple of \(f_v\).
Its integer ring splits after base change over \(\mathcal O_U\) by the linear factors of a lifted separable residue generator, whose pairwise differences are units.
This provides the integral idempotents separating the \(f_v\) summands, also when the residue of the particular generator \(t\) fails to distinguish them.
Over each summand a uniformizer for the remaining totally ramified extension has an Eisenstein polynomial which stays Eisenstein after the unramified base change.
Thus each summand gives a field of degree \(e_v\), including in the wildly ramified case.
The image of the global generator in the product generates it as a \(U\)-algebra, since \(F=\mathbb Q[t]\).
Its minimal polynomial in each field factor therefore has degree \(e_v\), and polynomials from distinct factors are relatively prime over \(U\) because otherwise their diagonal generator could not generate the product.
Their roots are integral and their monic coefficients belong to the integrally closed ring \(\mathcal O_U\).

The idempotents place the product of the component power-basis orders inside \(\mathcal O_F\otimes\mathcal O_U\), and the original power-basis order embeds diagonally in that product.
The determinant of its basis change into the full integer lattice has \(p\)-valuation \(v_p(I)\) after extension, since \(p\) remains a uniformizer of \(U\).
Comparing the determinant of the trace pairings on the two power-basis orders computes the valuation of the intermediate index as the sum of the valuations of all cross resultants.
All the valuations are nonnegative by integrality.
The sum is consequently at most \(v_p(I)\) even if the original generator fails to give an integral basis within some components.
This argument includes two distinct residue embeddings from a single \(v\).

In the component for \((v,\iota)\), all its \(e_v\) images of \(t\) reduce to the same value of the residue field, because the extension over \(U\) is totally ramified.
Each differs from its unramified residue representative by valuation at least \(1/e_v\).
Two matching residues in a common finite residue field have the same representative by simple-root lifting of \(X^{p^u}-X\) for a common multiple \(u\) of their residue degrees.
This also holds at the zero residue.
A pair of matching components thus has all \(e_v e_w\) differences of valuation at least \(1/\max(e_v,e_w)\), giving a total of at least \(\min(e_v,e_w)\) in their resultant.
When counting components by levels this number is precisely the number of common levels of the pair.
At level \(j\), \(c_{p,i,j}\) counts residue embeddings including multiplicity from the number of places and the individual \(f_v\).
Their values lie in the field of size \(p^{k_{p,i}}\).
The occupations count ordered matching pairs, and balancing any two bins whose occupations differ by at least two yields the exact discrete minimum stated in the theorem.
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(c^2/q-c\), while the pair count is nonnegative.
The sets at a fixed \(p\) are disjoint, so summing charges each unordered pair in at most one set.
The factor two is precisely the passage from unordered to ordered pairs.
The identity \(\sum_p v_p(I)\log p=\log I\) now gives (1); if \(p\nmid I\), every indicated nonnegative local contribution must vanish.

For the Salem specialization, inversion pairs distinct conjugates and gives the degree-two extension of the trace field.
The \(s\) distinct trace conjugates therefore supply all the quadratic factors \(X^2-t_iX+1\).
The resultant of two different quadratics is the square of the trace difference, using the unit product of the roots of either quadratic.
The individual discriminants have product \(\prod_i(t_i^2-4)=Q(2)Q(-2)\), a nonzero integer.
The discriminant of \(P\) thus has absolute value \(|Q(2)Q(-2)|\,|\operatorname{disc}Q|^2\).
The equality \(|\operatorname{disc}Q|=I^2|\operatorname{disc}F|\) puts the endpoint and field terms into (2) with the stated signs.
Hadamard's bound on the Vandermonde matrix at all \(d\) roots gives \(\log|\operatorname{disc}P|\le d\log d+2(d-1)m\), verifying both coefficients after division by two.

For the identity coset the condition on a place \(v\) is \(f_v\mid k\), because this is exactly when the residue \(p^k\)-power fixes its residue subfield.
The proportion of primes of the normal closure lying over \(v\) is \(e_v f_v/s\), and if \(f_v\mid k\) then precisely the fraction \(1/e_v\) of the inertia coset lies in the subgroup there.
The resulting first-level probability is \(c/s\), including at two by the same decomposition and inertia count.
The [source of the identity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#powers) introduces a set of odd test primes for its determinant; the separately stated local calculation justifies its use at two in the current argument.
Conjugation invariance and double transitivity give one orbit for all nonidentity cosets, hence the value \((1-c/s)/(s-1)\) for each.
No such equality or maximum bound from the identity alone is claimed for general actions.
Finally \(q=o(s)\) at bounded \(m\) makes \(y=(q+1)m/(2s)\to0\).
In the unit case of the all-coset height inequality one may take \(n\to\infty\) with \(ny\to0\), giving \(U_n(y)\to0\).
Since \(D=2\) and \(\log p\ge\log2\), the maximum coset probability tends to zero in this comparison, as asserted.
