### Perturbing the rows

The space construction and nonvanishing choices in L206 apply to the original formal rows of degree at most \(n=4d\), before removal of the common factor.
For \(m\ge2\) the choice \(l=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) satisfies \(1\le l<m\) and \(d/l\le6\).
It gives simultaneous logarithmic coefficient bounds \(O(L)\) for both rows and their integral reduced traces, nonzero \(u,D\), irrational \(v/u\), and a nonzero value and derivative determinant.
Since \(Q\) retains all \(d\) conjugates and \(\alpha\) is reciprocal, \(Q\) is monic reciprocal of even degree \(d\) with constant term one.

Inverting \(\alpha\) in the value equation and subtracting gives
\[
 \beta^{-n/2}(A-A^\#)(\beta)=-\sigma(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})U(q).
\]
This is nonzero, so \(A-A^\#\) has no root in common with \(Q\).
The added term \(Q^2(a(1+X^{2d})+bX^d)\) is fixed by reversal about \(4d\), and the fixed difference has at most \(n\) distinct nonzero roots.
At every such \(z\) the coefficient of \(b\) in \(B(z)\) is \(z^dQ(z)^2\ne0\).
Both endpoint coefficients have slope one in \(a\).
The product of the avoidance conditions is therefore nonzero of total degree at most \(n+2\), so the grid of \(n+3\) distinct integers in each variable suffices.
The resulting full-degree row has nonzero constant and is coprime to its reversal over \(\mathbb Q\).

The perturbation vanishes to second order at \(\beta\) and its conjugates.
It preserves both centered jets with the same center \(pn/2\), as well as \(P^2\)-divisibility of the diagonal.
The endpoint hypotheses now permit the integral odd chart after division by the scalar content \(c>0\).
Uniqueness of the reduced representatives makes the new pair \(U/c,V/c\), and linearity gives the new remainder \(D/c\).
The disjoint residue classes \(0,1\bmod p\) give the same content for the diagonal.
Hence primitive normalization preserves all nonvanishing conditions, scaling the two-row determinant by \(1/(c_1c_2)\).
The endpoints give the exact span \(4pd+1\).

The nonzero remainder norm estimate follows from the Chebyshev coefficient bound in L199.
For the lift of the reduced \(U\), the constant Chebyshev term contributes once to the coefficient length and each positive-degree term at most twice.
Thus its logarithmic measure is at most \(\log(2m\|U\|_C)\), also for a constant \(U\).
Each primitive integral factor has nonnegative logarithmic measure, giving the asserted bound for the primitive squarefree part.
If \(t=\log|Q(x^p)|\), the perturbing diagonal on the circle has modulus at most \(6(n+2)e^{2t}\).
The original diagonal has modulus at most \(2(n+1)\|A\|_\infty\), and \(2ph+\log(6(n+2))=O(L)\).
These inequalities give the pointwise logarithmic upper bound \(O(L)+2(t-ph)_+\).
Circle averaging is invariant under \(x\mapsto x^p\), scalar normalization can only lower the measure, and the resulting cofactor has logarithmic measure \(m(F)-2h\).
This proves (1) for the same two rows.

### Circle mean square and regularized discriminant

For each interior root the centered contribution is \(\log|1-z_i e^{-it}|\), and dividing an exterior root's boundary factor by its modulus gives the same formula with \(z_i=1/\bar\gamma_i\).
Regularization multiplies its \(k\)-th coefficients by \(\rho^{|k|}\).
Their squares are bounded by a constant times \(k^{-2}\), giving \(L^2\) convergence for each polynomial, including at unit-circle roots.
The two Fourier coefficients at frequencies \(\pm k\) together give \(|s_k|^2/(2k^2)\) in Parseval.
The centered boundary logarithm has mean zero, so its positive-part integral is half its \(L^1\) norm and \(J(Q)\le\sqrt{V(Q)}/2\).

The regularized energy identity follows by absolute convergence for \(\rho<1\).
Direct subtraction of squares gives
\[
 |1-\rho^2w|^2-\rho^2|1-w|^2=(1-\rho^2)(1-\rho^2|w|^2)\ge0 .
\]
Folding strictly same-side pairs uses the disk inequality, while opposite-side or boundary pairs give the exact normalized distance.
Distinct boundary roots ensure no zero off-diagonal denominator.
The sum of the original log distances over ordered distinct pairs is \(\log|\operatorname{disc}H|\), and the two radial normalizations contribute \(2(d-1)m(H)\).
The \(d\) diagonal terms cost at most \(d\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\).
This proves (2) for the general squarefree monic input in L222.

With \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\) the first \(d\) terms of \(2V\) cost at most \(e^2E_\rho\), and the tail costs at most \(d\).
The discriminant of \(Q\) is a nonzero integer and \(m(Q)=ph\), giving \(V(Q)\ll d\log(2d)+dph\).
The two growth conditions in the text imply \(V(Q)=o(p^2)\) and \(\log(2dp)/p=o(1)\).
The individual discriminant test retains the right side of (2) at this \(\rho\) plus \(d\), which also forces \(d=o(p^2)\) if the test holds.
On the stated shrinking sequences these conclusions imply \(L/p\to0\).

### Prime average and interval size

Degree preservation gives \(s_k(Q_p)=s_{pk}(P)\).
Grouping the nonnegative squared Fourier coefficients by \(n=pk\) counts each prime divisor in \(\Pi\) once.
Distinct quotients \(n/p\) are positive integers less than \(n/Y\), giving \(\omega_\Pi(n)\le n/Y\).
For \(D=d^2\), the terms through \(D\) in the unaveraged sum are bounded by \(e^2 E_{\exp(-1/D)}(P)/Y\).
Here (2) uses \(m(P)=h\), its diagonal term is \(O(d\log(2d))\), and its regularization term \(d(d-1)/D\) is bounded.
For the tail, \(p\le2d\) gives \(D/p\ge1\), and
\[
 \sum_{pk>D}\frac{|s_{pk}(P)|^2}{p^2k^2}
     \le \frac{d^2}{p^2\lfloor D/p\rfloor}
     \le \frac{2d^2}{pD}.
\]
Summation and division by \(|\Pi|\) yield (3) for any nonempty set specified there.

For fixed \(\eta>0\) and \(Y=\sqrt d\,(\log(2d))^{1+\eta}\), eventually \(5\le Y\le d\).
The dyadic count and degree-loss bound give \(|\Pi|\gg Y/\log Y\) for the full set of good primes, with the excluded degree-losing primes a vanishing proportion.
The first term of (3) is then \(O((\log(2d))^{-2\eta})\) on a shrinking sequence.
Markov's inequality at the square root of the vanishing upper bound gives the asserted uniform \(o(p)\) costs outside a vanishing proportion of primes.
In the fourth-logarithmic window, (3) alone leaves the required small mean square open.
In that window \(L/p\to0\), so \(J(Q)=o(p)\) suffices, and a positive lower bound for \(m(R)/p\) on every such short-trace row forces \(J(Q)\gg p\) by (1).
