## Independent review of the counting bound and the half-mass inverse

At 2026-11-18T14:30 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the whole [fixed argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v179.md#genuine) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the version and the review scope without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings on this argument.
The lower bound for the mass carried by the Salem conjugates and the half-integral construction both hold.
The construction gives total neutral branch mass tending to \(1/2\) for each fixed Salem input satisfying the trace congruence.
Its shrinking-excess version requires a sequence of such inputs tending to one.

For a shrinking-excess sequence, the strict inverse formula gives \(0<u<\theta-q\), so the exterior return trace tends to two.
The reciprocal quadratic alternative has integral trace at least three and is eventually excluded.
The degrees must tend to infinity since bounded degree at \(q=d^2\) and bounded excess give bounded conjugates, finitely many monic polynomials and strictly positive excess for each.
Thus \(\log q=o(d)\) and the full-return determinant estimate applies with \(\sigma_*=1\) to both residue products.
Each positive residue contributes to its own class in the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, giving the two factors \((M/p)^p\) and \(((S-M)/(1-p))^{1-p}\).
Replacing \(S-M\) by \(S\) and taking the \(p\)-th root gives the stated lower bound.
For the full set \(M=S\ge G\), and the empty subset has zero mass.
For \(p\) bounded away from zero, bounded \(S\) and a positive lower bound for \(G\) give a positive lower bound for \(M\), including as \(p\to1\).

With \(k=h-h_0\), one has \(1-p=k/(d/2-1)\) and \(\log S\le2\log d+O(1)\) from the endpoint bound on the inverse residues and \(c_i<b_i\).
If \(\limsup k\log d/d\le C\), then \(p\to1\), \(\limsup(1-p)\log S\le4C\), and \((1-p)\log(1-p)\to0\).
Taking logarithms of the mass lower bound gives \(\liminf M\ge e^{-4C}/2\), with the full-set case interpreted directly.
Applying this on any bounded subsequence of \(k\log d/d\) gives the claimed necessary divergence when genuine mass tends to zero.
For subset fractions \(p',s'\), the two-class logarithmic inequality bounds the binary relative entropy by \(\log(S/G)\).
Its derivative in \(s'\) is \((s'-p')/[s'(1-s')]\), and \(s'(1-s')\le1/4\) gives the quadratic lower bound on either side of \(p'\).
If \(S\to1/2\), then \(S/G\to1\), and the positive-deviation subset gives the full absolute-deviation sum \(2S(s'-p')\).
Taking the Salem subset also gives \(M-p/2\to0\) regardless of how its membership varies.

On the grid \(4\mid k\), cancellation at \(x=2\) and division by \(2-x\) give an integral \(E\) of leading coefficient \(k/2\).
The identity \(B'=kV_{k-1}\) gives the residue \(D(y)/B'(y)=1/[2k(2-y)]\) at each simple zero of \(B=C_k\).
Reduction of \((2-x)E=V_{k-1}-(k/2)B\) gives \(x\overline E=\overline V_{k-1}\), while \(C_k=xV_{k-1}-2V_{k-2}\) gives \(\overline B=x^2\overline E\).
Since \(\overline B\) is monic, \(\overline E\ne0\).
For a correction in \(J/2+\mathbb Z[x]\), integrality of \(BR_0+Q_0E/2\) is therefore equivalent to \(\overline Q_0=x^2\overline J\).
One can choose \(J\) of degree at most \(h_0-1\), leaving the top coefficient of \(R_0\) free to be \(1-q-k/4\).
Degree and leading coefficient then give \(1-q\) for the top coefficient of \(R\).

The coefficients of \(Q_0/(x-T)\), in descending order after the leading one, are monic polynomials in \(T\) of successive degrees \(1,\ldots,h_0\).
Thus each nonzero integral combination of this lower coefficient vector is irrational.
Putting \(k=4v\) gives an irrational quadratic coefficient \(64\,n\cdot e\) for each nonzero torus character \(n\), unchanged by the fixed half-integral translation.
The cited equidistribution argument therefore visits the nonempty open set obtained by interpolating sufficiently small positive old residues arbitrarily late.
The extra endpoint sum in (7) is simultaneously small by choosing the individual box widths proportional to \(\min(1,2+x_i)\).

The old and new neutral nodes are disjoint, since the old Salem conjugates are nontorsion and the grid comes from \(z^{2k}+1\).
At old nodes the factor \(B(x_i)\) cancels, and at new nodes \(b_y=(T-y)D(y)/B'(y)\), \(c_y=(2-y)D(y)/B'(y)\).
The identity \(B'(2)/B(2)=k^2/2\), reflection \(y\mapsto-y\) and \(1/(4-y^2)=((2-y)^{-1}+(2+y)^{-1})/4\) give all three sums in the fixed argument.
The endpoint sum grows only linearly in \(k\) at fixed \(T\), so it meets the strict inverse criterion at \(q=(2h_0+2+2k)^2\).
That criterion supplies a monic irreducible Pisot inverse with the specified full return and the exact positive excess formula.
Using \(\theta>q\) in the denominators bounds \(\theta-q-u\) by \(u/(q-1)+(1/2+uk/4+\varepsilon)/(q-2)=O_T(1/k)\).
The old masses vanish with \(\varepsilon\), and the constant grid branch weights \(1/(2k)\) at cosine midpoints converge weakly to half the arcsine probability measure.
At changing inputs one can impose any necessary lower cutoff on \(k\) after fixing each tolerance, also forcing \(uk\to\infty\) when desired.
For \(Q_0=x^2-2x-2\) the second real embedding sends \(T\) into \((-2,2)\), so the discriminant of \(z^2-Tz+1\) is negative there and cannot be a square in \(\mathbb Q(T)\).
The resulting degree-four unit has one exterior real root, one reciprocal interior root and a neutral conjugate pair, giving the required fixed Salem example with \(\overline Q_0=x^2\).
Attainability of inverse total mass \(1/2\) along a shrinking sequence remains open in this argument.
