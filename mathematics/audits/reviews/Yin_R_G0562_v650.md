## Uniform exclusion and preservation under every power

At 2026-11-20T10:19 UTC a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the full [torsion-separation claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v517.md#statement) and its [argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v517.md#argument).
Both separation assertions and the field equality follow under the stated hypotheses.

The [critical estimates](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) give \(m\ll s^{-1}\) and \(d\gg as^2\), so for every root \(x\) of the monic factor, \(h(x^a)=am/d\ll s^{-3}\).
The parent has constant and leading coefficients of absolute value one, so its roots are units, and nontorsion of \(x\) implies nontorsion of \(y=x^a\).
I checked the rank-one specialization against Amoroso and Masser, Theorem 2.1, printed pages 1–2, as recorded in the [relative height unit](../literature/AM16/Yin_U002.md#am16-u002).
For every finite abelian \(L\), the two height inequalities give \([L(y):L]\gg s^{3/(1+\epsilon)}\) with a constant independent of \(L\).
For \(0<\eta<3\), choosing \(0<\epsilon<\eta/(3-\eta)\) yields the claimed exponent, while \(\eta\ge3\) follows from degree at least one.
In particular the comparison allows \(L=\mathbb Q(\xi)\) with unbounded conductor.

At either section root the vanishing of the denominator forces the numerator to vanish by the defining equation.
At a common zero \(z\), substitution of \(z^j=1+z\) gives \(zB_j(z)=-(1+z+z^2)\), so \(z\) is a primitive cube root of unity.
Both \(y\) and \(\xi^a y\) are nontorsion, ruling out even these possible poles.
Writing \(c=\xi^a\) and \(e=\xi^{b'}\), the two values of the rational functions are \(x^b\) and \(e x^{b'}\), whose quotient is \(e y^t\) also when \(t<0\).
Clearing denominators and multiplying by \(T^{\max(0,-t)}\) gives two polynomial terms of degree at most \(s+s'+|t|\) over \(L\).
If their difference is nonzero, its degree is at least \([L(y):L]\).
In the neighboring sections the upper bound is at most \(s+2s^{3-\delta}=o(s^{3-\eta})\) for \(0<\eta<\min(2,\delta)\), including \(\delta>2\) when \(s'\) can be smaller than \(s\) throughout the range.
The comparison within a single parent uses the bound \(2s\) for every \(\xi\), independently of the neighboring range.

At the origin both rational functions have valuation zero, value one and first derivative one before rescaling their argument, including at seed degree three.
Thus a rational identity forces \(t=0\), then \(e=1\), then \(c=1\).
Subtracting \(1+T\) from \(R_j(T)\) gives leading term \(T^{j-1}\), since its numerator is \(T^{j-1}(1+T+T^2)\) and the denominator has constant term one.
Equality determines \(s'=s\), while \(t=0\) gives \(b'=b\) and the two conditions on \(\xi\) force its order to divide \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
This argument used the exterior-root hypothesis only for the original factor.
For each nonzero integer \(n\), equality of two conjugate \(n\)-th powers would give a torsion quotient between the corresponding conjugates, which are roots of the original parent.
The first assertion applies to every conjugate, so the powers are pairwise distinct and the subfield \(\mathbb Q(x^n)\) has the full degree of \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
The threshold for this conclusion is independent of \(n\).
