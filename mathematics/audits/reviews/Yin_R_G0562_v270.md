### Minimal blocks and independent simple factors

The compressed conjugates and labels of v264 form a transitive \(G_0\)-set of size \(v=d/w\) with \(w=1\) or \(2\).
Every block of size greater than one has size at least \(c_M d/\log^{212}(3d)\).
Intersections of blocks with a common point are blocks.
If two systems of minimal nontrivial blocks differ, every intersection has size at most one, since a common block generates the same system under translation.
Counting intersections of a block of size \(b\) with all \(v/b'\) blocks of the other partition gives \(bb'\le v\).
The common lower bound gives \(bb'>v\) for large \(d\).
This proves uniqueness and gives \(u\ll_M\log^{212}(3d)\), \(b>u+2\), and \(b\ge9\), including \(u=1\). [Compression and block bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block)

For this minimal block, v268 gives precisely the natural \(A_b,S_b\) actions or the specified classical subspace actions.
The statement of v269 applies to the same labels, compressed degree, bounded measure and primitive almost simple group, including all three classical cases.
Thus its exclusion gives a group \(H=A_b\) or \(S_b\) on the actual \(b\) letters.
If \(K\) is the block kernel, its image \(N\) in \(H\) is normal with \([H:N]\le u!\), since the block stabilizer modulo \(K\) acts faithfully on the \(u\) blocks.
If \(N\cap A_b=1\), simplicity would give \(|A_b|\le[H:N]\), impossible since \(b!/2>u!\).
Hence each image contains \(A_b\) and its commutator subgroup is \(A_b\).
The group \(K'\le A_b^u\) is consequently subdirect.
In the subdirect induction, forgetting the last coordinate has either the full simple group or the trivial group as kernel in that coordinate.
In the latter case a surjection from the product of the previous simple diagonal factors to the last group has exactly one nontrivial factor image, since the images are commuting normal subgroups in a nonabelian simple group.
This gives disjoint sets of diagonally linked coordinates. [Primitive alternatives](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v268.md#statement) · [Subdirect induction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v268.md#normal) · [Classical exclusion used](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v269.md#statement)

I checked the coordinate matching for \(b\ge9\).
In \(S_b\) an element with precisely \(j\) disjoint three-cycles has centralizer \(3^j j!(b-3j)!\).
That centralizer contains an odd permutation in every case, using two fixed letters for \(j=1\) and the exchange of two three-cycles for \(j\ge2\).
The ratio of its \(A_b\)-centralizer order to the single-cycle case is strictly below one for \(j\ge2\).
For \(j=2\) the ratio is at most \(6/(6\cdot5\cdot4)\), and for \(j\ge3\) the upper bound \(3^{j-1}j!/(3j-3)!\) starts below one and decreases.
Automorphisms preserve the cyclic subgroups with triple support.
Distinct such subgroups commute exactly for disjoint supports, since a three-cycle preserves no nonempty proper part of its support.
The numbers of common disjoint supports \(\binom{b-6+i}{3}\) are distinct for \(i=0,1,2\) even at \(b=9\).
In the graph of triples meeting in two letters, a clique with a common pair has size at most \(b-2\), and any other clique lies in the union of its first two triples and has size at most four.
The largest cliques recover pairs, then intersecting pair stars of size \(b-1>3\) recover letters.
After cancelling the resulting letter permutation, the automorphism fixes each triple subgroup and hence induces the same action for \(g\) and its image on all triples.
That permutation action is faithful, proving that each diagonal coordinate isomorphism identifies natural point stabilizers up to a relabeling.

Equality of stabilizers in the normal subgroup \(K'\) is a \(G_0\)-invariant equivalence relation.
Within a diagonal set of \(s\) linked blocks each equivalence class meets every one of these blocks once, since natural point stabilizers are distinct and correspond under the coordinate relabelings.
In a different diagonal set the original factor is in the entire stabilizer and so cannot give the same subgroup of \(K'\).
Thus \(s\ge2\) would give a block of size \(2\le s\le u<b\), impossible.
Consequently \(K'=D=A_b^u\) acts independently on the blocks.
Every normal subgroup of \(D\) with a nontrivial projection to one factor contains that factor by commuting with it and using simplicity.
Transitivity on factors then makes \(D\) minimal normal in \(G_0\).
An element centralizing \(D\) preserves each factor's support and centralizes the natural \(A_b\) there.
That centralizer is trivial because the natural point stabilizer fixes just its own letter.
Any other minimal normal subgroup would centralize \(D\), which proves uniqueness. [Kernel and matching](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition)

### Relative splitting degree

The intersection of the finite Galois field \(L_\gamma\) with \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is precisely \(L_\gamma^{G_\gamma'}\).
Restriction and the finite Galois compositum formula therefore give relative degree \(|G_\gamma'|\).
The image of this commutator subgroup on the labels contains the perfect subgroup \(D\), so the degree is at least \((b!/2)^u\).
The action on actual roots is faithful and its kernel on labels has order at most \(2^v\), giving the upper bound \(2^v v!\).
Taking logarithms, the lower bound is at least \(v\log d-v\log(wu)-v-u\log2\).
Since \(w\le2\) and \(u\ll_M\log^{212}(3d)\), the loss from \(v\log d\) is \(O_M(d\log\log(3d))\), while the upper loss is at most \(O(d)\).
Furthermore \(d/2\le v\le d\) gives \(\log(v!)\sim v\log d\) uniformly.
Every conjugate class product is in the original splitting field, so that field contains \(L_\gamma\) also after adjoining the abelian field. [Degree bounds](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#degree)

### Signed configurations and global rank

Representatives of labels are nonzero vectors in the rationalized multiplicative group modulo torsion.
Switching a representative negates that vector if there are two choices, and Galois acts by signed permutations on the formal coordinate space.
Two distinct labels are linearly independent by the common positive height argument of v264.
An alternating group on \(b\) letters is transitive on subsets of every size, and signed lifts preserve linear dependence, so if the rank on a block is \(r\), every \(r\) columns form a basis.
For the kernel of the column map, projection onto any \(b-r\) coordinates is an isomorphism, by independence on the complement and equality of dimensions.
The coordinate restrictions on this kernel therefore give a uniform configuration of rank \(b-r\) with the same projective permutation action.
If \(2\le r\le b-2\), choose the smaller of this dual configuration and the original one to obtain rank \(2\le s\le b/2\).
All projective points are distinct since every pair extends to an independent \(s\)-set.
Of any \(s+1\) points, take the first \(s\) as basis.
The last has every coordinate nonzero, so any linear map fixing their \(s+1\) individual lines is scalar.
There are at least three points left and a three-cycle there lifts to a linear map which moves their lines and fixes the chosen frame, a contradiction.
Thus the internal relation space \(I_B\) has dimension at most one. [Uniform configurations and duality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank)

The derived series of the full preimage of the single supported corresponding \(A_b\) stabilizes at a perfect finite subgroup \(P\).
Every term surjects onto \(A_b=A_b'\).
On the other blocks \(P\) fixes actual roots, since the preimage fixes their labels and the permitted fiber permutations form an abelian group.
For the transitive signed action on \(\mathbb Q^B\), an invariant vector is determined by one coordinate and all its coordinates have the same absolute value.
When \(I_B\) is a line, \(P\) acts trivially on it, so this line exhausts the invariant subspace.
Exactness on invariants by averaging then gives \(V_B^P=0\).
When \(I_B=0\), the column map is an equivariant isomorphism onto \(V_B\).
In any global relation the block's contribution is fixed by \(P\), because it is minus the sum of contributions on all other blocks.
In the first case the coefficient restriction thus lies in \(I_B\), and in the second case it is itself invariant.
Any zero coordinate forces the whole block's coefficients to vanish.
This proves independence after the indicated omissions for every choice of representatives, the lower rank \(v-u\), and the stated constant-magnitude condition even for relations involving multiple blocks.
For measures tending to zero, compression preserves measure and bounded compressed degree would give only finitely many compressed numbers by bounded height.
Thus \(d\) tends to infinity as required. [Label compression](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) · [Perfect lifts and relations](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank)
