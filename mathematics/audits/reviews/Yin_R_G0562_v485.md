### The resultant obstruction

The eligible costs form a nonempty subset of the positive integers, since the singleton family \(Q\) is allowed at each stated cap.
Fix any eligible family.
If the rational irreducible \(S\) divided each member, it would divide the monic gcd \(Q\).
Thus one can choose a member \(H=QU\) with \(S\nmid H\), with \(U\) integral by division by the monic \(Q\).
The nonzero integer \(\operatorname{Res}(U,S)\) has modulus at least one.
On taking the product of \(|H(\gamma)|\le\|H\|_1\max(1,|\gamma|)^D\) over the conjugates of \(S\), monicity of \(S\) gives both the absolute resultant and the radial factor \(\exp(Dm(S))\) with no leading-coefficient correction.
This yields (1) for the maximum cost of the family and then for its minimum.
At a cyclotomic test the radial factor is one independently of \(D\), so the same inequality passes to the unrestricted family minimum.

For a degree-preserving prime \(p\), the distinct roots of \(Q\) are all the \(\alpha_i^p\), and the input's nontorsion condition makes every test resultant in (2) nonzero.
A root of \(\Phi_n(X^p)\) of order \(t\) satisfies \(t/\gcd(t,p)=n\).
When \(p\mid n\) this gives just \(t=pn\).
When \(p\nmid n\) it gives both \(n\) and \(pn\), including \(n=1\).
Conversely each primitive root of the listed orders occurs once by separability.
Products over the roots of \(P\), divided in the logarithm by \(\varphi(n)\), give precisely the coefficients \(p\) and \(1,p-1\) in the respective cases.
As a check in both cases, the square of a root of \(X^2-3X+1\) has minimal polynomial \(X^2-7X+1\), giving absolute test resultants \(9\) at \(n=2\) and \(64\) at \(n=3\) by either product.
Each normalized log resultant is nonnegative, and the supremum in (2) is bounded by \(\log C_\infty(Q)\le\log C_{2d}(Q)\le B\) whenever the L227 family exists.
This proves the \(o(p)\) necessity for that construction.

### Sharp norm and the lower bound on degree

For \(m=N/q\), the cyclotomic resultant with \(\Phi_m\) has absolute value \(q^{\varphi(m)}\), including \(m=1\).
Hence (1) gives the norm lower bound \(q\).
For \(j\) running through the prime divisors of \(N\), \(T_j\) contains exactly the factors \(\Phi_t\) with \(t\mid N\) and \(v_j(t)=v_j(N)\).
Taking all \(j\) preserves only \(\Phi_N\), to multiplicity one, and the maximum coefficient norm is \(q\).
This proves (3) even when prime factors repeat in \(N\).

Choose \(H\) avoiding \(\Phi_m\) in a family of cost \(q\).
The product of its \(\varphi(m)\) absolute values at the primitive \(m\)-th roots is at least \(q^{\varphi(m)}\), and each value is at most \(\|H\|_1\le q\).
Consequently every value saturates its triangle inequality.
For any such root \(\zeta\), the ratio of any two nonzero terms is positive real.
Since the coefficients are real, \(\zeta^{k-l}\) must be \(1\) or \(-1\) for any occupied exponents \(k,l\).
This gives \(m\mid2(k-l)\), which remains valid when \(m=1,2\).
Writing \(w=m/\gcd(m,2)\) and choosing the least occupied exponent \(c\) therefore gives \(H=X^c A(X^w)\) with ordinary degree \(c+w\deg A\).
At a primitive \(N\)-th root its \(w\)-th power has order \(N/w\), exactly \(q\) for odd \(N\) and \(2q\) for even \(N\) with odd \(q\).
The minimal polynomial of this power has degree \(q-1\) and divides \(A\).
This gives the claimed lower cap in both cases, independent of any initial monomial or coefficient signs.

### Families attaining the cap

For odd \(N\), \(T_q\) attains the largest degree in the displayed family, namely \(N-N/q\).
For \(N=2^e\), \(e\ge1\), the singleton \(X^{N/2}+1\) has cost two and already has the least possible degree of any nonzero multiple.
For all other even \(N\), put \(M=\prod_{j\mid N,\ j\ {\rm odd\ prime}}j\), \(k=N/(2M)\).
Then \(M>1\) is odd.
The order of \(x^k\) must be \(2M\) to make \(-x^k\) a primitive \(M\)-th root, and this also suffices.
If \(x\) has order \(v\), then \(v/\gcd(v,k)=2M\).
For each prime dividing \(k\) its exponent in \(2M\) is positive, hence its exponent in \(v\) is the sum of its exponents in \(k\) and \(2M\).
Every other prime has the same exponent in \(v\) as in \(2M\).
Thus \(v=N\), and all primitive \(N\)-th roots do occur.
The composed polynomial has simple roots and leading coefficient one since \(k\ge1\), the roots are nonzero and \(\varphi(M)\) is even.
This proves \(\Phi_M(-X^k)=\Phi_N(X)\) with arbitrary exponents of the primes in \(N\).

The Bezout identity for the quotients of the odd-\(M\) family by \(\Phi_M\) still equals one after substitution.
This excludes any extra factor in the composed gcd.
Distinct occupied indices stay distinct and only their signs may change, so the norms are preserved.
The largest composed degree is \(k(M-M/q)\), as in (4).
Independently forming these families and taking exact polynomial gcds for \(N=2,3,9,27,32,6,18,20,36,60,300,1155\) reproduced their norms, maximal degrees and monic gcds.

For \(1155\), the totient is \(2\cdot4\cdot6\cdot10=480\), but its sharp cap is \(10\cdot105=1050\).
Consequently no family at \(D=960\) attains 11.
In the L225 conductor sequence, \(N/\varphi(N)=1+O(s/e^{Cs})\) and all prime factors of \(N\) exceed any fixed power of \(\log(3\varphi(N))\) eventually.
Thus \(D_0(N)<N<2\varphi(N)\) eventually, and each prime in the fourth-logarithmic window permutes the primitive orbit.
The exact logarithmic budget there is \(\log q\) on the same powered cyclotomic input.
The existence of such small families along any general shrinking sequence of irreducible nontorsion units remains open.
