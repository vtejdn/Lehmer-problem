## Limiting measure, second moment and lower limit

On November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the [exterior radius theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v547.md#statement) from the fixed argument and the inputs listed below.
The reviewer took no part in developing it and received the identity of the prior occupation review and the questions to check.
The distribution for every stated test, the all-root second moment, and the exclusion with no exterior-count cap follow.

For an interior seed, writing its radius as \(e^{-w/s}>1/2\) gives \(1-e^{-w/s}\ge w/(2s)\), hence \(we^w\le2s\).
An exterior seed has radius at most \(2^{1/(s-1)}\).
For each fixed positive frequency the corresponding Newton sum vanishes eventually, and the uniform radius bounds make its normalized difference from the angular Fourier coefficient tend to zero.
Complex conjugation deals with the negative frequencies.
Away from the two endpoints of the interior arc and from \(-1\), uniform convergence of \(|1+\zeta|\) to \(|1+\langle\zeta\rangle|\) determines the correct side and permits taking logarithms uniformly.
Angular equidistribution bounds the omitted mass in small arcs for bounded tests.
The resulting limiting measure is the image of normalized circle arclength restricted to \(I\) under \(u\mapsto(u,w(u))\), with mass \(1/3\).
The value at the single point \(-1\) is immaterial.

Jensen's disk count at radius \(e^{-t}\) bounds the normalized seed mass at depths at least \(t\) by \(O(e^{-t})\).
Layer integration bounds its tail of order \(p\ge1\) above \(M\) by a constant times \(M^p e^{-M}+\int_M^\infty p t^{p-1}e^{-t}\,dt\).
The simple zero at \(-1\) gives the same tail control for arclength.
For each fixed integer \(p\ge1\), the occupation error against any test bounded by \(1+w^p\), including its restriction to a tail, is \(O((1+\log^p(2s))s^{-1/32}\sqrt{\log(2s)})=o(1)\).
Here the unweighted estimate applies with \(q=1\), since \(t_s/s\to1/3\) and \(\log(2d)/D\) decays faster than every inverse power of \(s\).
All assigned radii stay above a fixed positive constant, so normalization to a direction and taking log radius are uniformly Lipschitz there.
The actual angular and scaled radial errors are consequently \(O(se^{-cs})\).
On bounded depth intervals one may use uniform continuity, while a sample above \(M\) has assigned seed depth above \(M-1\) and polynomial majorant bounded by a constant times \(1+w_j^p\).
This proves transfer even for continuous tests with arbitrary oscillation at large depth.

The limiting second moment \(J_2\) is finite by the tail estimate and positive on an open subarc.
Dividing the normalized sum of squared scaled radii by \((as)^2/(sD)\) gives the exterior square sum \((J_2+o(1))d/(a^2rs)\).
Reciprocity of this individual factor doubles the sum, since the circle contributes zero.
The roots are algebraic units because the parent has unit leading and constant coefficients.
For a nontorsion unit the squared raw degree norm equals \(d\sum_x(\log|x|)^2\), so its floor \(1/10\) gives \(d/a\ge(1-o(1))\sqrt{rs/(20J_2)}\).
Multiplication by \(k/d\sim s/(3r)\) yields the denominator \(180J_2\) inside the square root.
A subsequence realizing any finite lower limit of \(k/a\) has an eventual fixed cap, allowing all these estimates with that one cap and the original \(C\).
The inequality \(r\le Cs^3\) then proves (2), including the case of zero putative lower limit, while an infinite lower limit already satisfies it.
Arbitrarily large examples with \(k\le Ka\) and \(CK^2<1/(180J_2)\) would contradict (2).
