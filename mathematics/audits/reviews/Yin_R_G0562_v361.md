# Monic multiples supported on two residue classes

## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T10:32 UTC, a separate reviewer for Yin inspected the whole [submitted claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v360.md#decision-monic-two-class-cost-submitted).
The reviewer took no part in developing this argument and received its fixed text without author discussion, development notes or earlier opinions on it.
Both finite estimates, the fixed-input bounds and the conditional varying-input conclusions hold as stated.

**The leading coefficients.**
Any pair at distance \(s\) is a translate of \(\{0,s\}\), so a nonnegative monomial shift suffices for the evaluation without changing coefficient height, term count or measure.
For a leading exponent \(np\), every other term is at least \(p-s\) positions below it.
For a leading exponent \(np+s\), extracting the two possible top positions leaves the factor \(\alpha^s+A\), where the integer \(A\) can be zero.
The remaining exponents are at most \(np-p+s\).
The triangle inequality therefore gives \(kH\ge \rho^{p-s}\delta_s\) in both cases.
Since an algebraic unit in \(\mathbb Z\) is \(1\) or \(-1\), nontorsion excludes \(\alpha^s\in\mathbb Z\).
For a nearest integer when \(\delta_s<1\), bounding the other \(d-1\) factors of the nonzero integral field norm gives the stated lower estimate, even when some embeddings agree on \(\alpha^s\).
For \(\delta_s=1\) the right side is at most one.
The [Dobrowolski–Smyth height bound](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) applies because the two occupied residues ensure \(k\ge2\).
Taking logarithms and subtracting \(m(P)\) gives (1).

**Integral interpolation.**
Distinct powered conjugates give a squarefree monic integral \(Q\), and inversion pairs the roots with no fixed point, so \(Q^*=Q\) and \(Q(0)=1\).
The Galois group permutes the summands defining \(U\), whose coefficients are algebraic integers.
Evaluating at any \(\beta_i\) leaves just \(-\alpha_i^rQ'(\beta_i)\), which is nonzero.
Thus both rows are present and their specialization is divisible by \(P\).
In the coefficient norm estimate the index omitted from the product absorbs \(|\alpha_i|^r\) since \(1\le r<p\).
Summing the \(d\) bounds for each row gives \(d2^d\exp(p\,m(P))\), with \(2^d\exp(p\,m(P))\) sufficient for \(Q\).

**Reciprocity and division.**
The shifts \(t=1,2\) and the degree-\(T\) reversal put all perturbation terms in the open interval of exponents \((0,T)\).
The shifted polynomial and its reversal both retain \(P\), and the initial summand supplies the unit endpoints.
Reversal about \(T\) interchanges the two rows of their reciprocal sum.
At \(\beta\), vanishing of the zero row for both shifts would give \(\beta^2=1\) by taking the ratio of the two nonzero contributions.
After choosing the shift there is still a choice of sign making that row nonzero at \(1\), since \(Q(1)\ne0\).
The common monic divisor \(A\) is integral as a divisor of a monic integral row, and it has unit constant term because the opposite row starts with one.
The reversal pairing preserves every common root multiplicity under inversion, and \(A(1)\ne0\) forces the positive reversal sign.
Thus \(A(0)=1\) and division by \(A(x^p)\) preserves the endpoints, reciprocity and both rows.
Because \(A(\beta)\ne0\), the quotient still contains \(P\).
The coefficient norm before division is at most twice the sum of the bounds for \(Q\) and \(F_0\), and an integral monic divisor has Mahler measure at least one.
These facts give the degree and logarithmic measure caps in (2), with ordinary coprimality before the possible monomial translation and Laurent coprimality afterward.

**The limiting regimes.**
For fixed \(P\), each ratio of distinct roots excludes at most one prime from the construction.
Under the degree cap the term count is at most \(2(b+3)\), so the sparse bound and \(-\log\delta_s=O_P(s+1)\) give the house lower rate.
For a monic multiple, the primitive noncyclotomic cofactor in the [unrestricted minimum](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement) has precisely the logarithmic measure \(m(F/P)\).
Inclusion of the monic class therefore supplies the independent lower rate \(m(P)/2\) for all prescribed pairs.
Translation adds at most \(p-1\) to the constructed degree and preserves measure, so the witnesses eventually meet the cap.
Bounded degree and measure give finitely many monic integral polynomials and hence attainment.
For a single exterior conjugate the logarithm of its modulus equals \(m(P)\), proving equality and the stated factor of two.
On \(0,s\) the same upper bound is attained asymptotically with the ordinary reciprocal and coprimality conditions.

For varying \(P\), the conjugate count bounds the degree of any ratio of two distinct roots by \(d(d-1)\), giving the stated sufficient prime test.
A Salem polynomial has a unique exterior conjugate, so its fiber over its own \(p\)-th power has size one and the power field has full degree.
Under bounded \(m(P)\), \(ds=o(p)\) controls the norm loss and \(d\le b=o(p)\) controls the interpolation error.
For the finer Salem limit, \(s\,m(P)\to0\) puts the exterior root's \(s\)-th power within \(1/2\) of one, and \(\delta_s=e^{s\,m(P)}-1\ge s\,m(P)\).
The assumptions on \(b+|\log(s\,m(P))|\) and \(s/p\) then absorb both the additive errors and the loss from the shorter leading gap at the scale \(p\,m(P)\).
The sparse coefficient theorem was used at its recorded source scope and the unrestricted theorem at its fixed-input scope.
