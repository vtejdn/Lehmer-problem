### Short solutions and the two traces

Reciprocity gives the involution of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) taking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\) and fixing \(q\).
The trace field has degree \(m\), with \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{m-1}\) a basis of the order \(\mathbb Z[q]\) by triangularity with the monic powers.
For \(J=2k+1\), inversion of the first centered equality gives the signed value cancellation.
Inversion of the second gives the centered Euler derivative cancellation, since the multiplier changes from \(1-\sigma\alpha\) to \(1+\sigma\alpha\).
Equivalently these are the vanishing sum and centered derivative for \(A(x^p)+\sigma x^J A(x^{-p})\), up to units at \(\alpha\).
Since \(P\) is separable, these conditions give \(P^2\) as a factor.

In an embedding at \(z\), the powered terms have exponents in \([-k,k]\) after centering, and their derivative multipliers satisfy \(|2pj-J|\le J\) for \(0\le j\le n\).
The trace terms, including the extra possible factor \(z\), have all exponents in \([-m,m]\).
The displayed bound \(5(n+1)(J+1)Te^{k|\log|z||}\) thus applies to each algebraic integer.
In each coordinate box a difference has real absolute value at most \(1/3\), or complex modulus at most \(\sqrt{2}/3\).
A nonzero integral norm precludes two different integers in the same box.
Using two real dimensions per complex embedding pair and both displayed integers gives at most \((T+1)^{2d}\exp(B)\) image points, with the exponent contribution \(2k\sum_z|\log|z||=4kh\).
There are \((T+1)^{n+1+d}\) input tuples, and \(T+1>\exp(B/l)\), so a fiber exceeds \((T+1)^{n+1-d-l}\) points.
Projection to pivot coordinates bounds the number in any affine subspace of dimension \(r\) by \((T+1)^r\) within the original cube.
Differences from a fixed point in that fiber supply the claimed dimension bound on the span of the short integral solutions.

Projection onto \(A\) is injective since the two trace coefficients \(1\pm\sigma\alpha\) are nonzero and reduced trace representatives are unique.
The kernel of the map to trace values has \(A(\beta)=(EA)(\beta)=0\), equivalently \(Q^2\mid A\) since \(Q\) is separable and \(\beta\ne0\).
Hence the trace image has dimension at least \(r-(n+1-2d)>d-l>m\).
This also prevents either entire trace projection from vanishing.

### Fixed factors, resultant and normalization

The homogeneous gcd \(G\) of the basis forms and their reversals is self-reciprocal up to sign because reversal permutes that set.
Removing an integral primitive \(G\) preserves integral coefficients by Gauss's lemma.
If \(G(\beta,1)=0\), the first trace component would vanish on the whole space, contrary to the strict dimension bound.
Since \(n-g+1\ge r>n+1-d-l\) and \(n=4d,\ l<d/2\), every pair of jets at \(\beta\) can be interpolated within degrees \(\le n-g\).
Indeed degrees \(<2d\) already suffice, by the kernel \(Q^2\) and equality of the source and target dimensions.
Multiplying by \(G(X,1)\) induces an invertible triangular map on these two jets.
The conditions on the value and centered Euler derivative require membership in two fixed \(m\)-dimensional rational subspaces of \(K\), after an invertible change of coordinates.
Imposing them on all multiples of \(G\) consequently gives \(r\le n-g+1-d\), whence \(g<l\).

Over the algebraic closure the entire collection of quotient forms and their reversals has no common projective zero.
For binary forms a common geometric linear factor before division would have given a rational common factor together with its conjugates.
In the polynomial ring in the parameters and binary variables, the universal linear combination divided by its fixed binary gcd is primitive, linear in the parameters, and irreducible by Gauss's lemma.
No fixed factor of one family can divide the other generic form, since that would give a joint fixed factor.
Hence a common nonconstant binary divisor over the parameter field would force the two primitive parameter-linear forms to be associated in the polynomial ring.
Comparing their coefficients gives the same quotient by reversal for every basis member.
If this held, each row's values at \(\lceil(n-g+1)/2\rceil\) generic points would determine its values at their reciprocals by the common quotient.
The combined set has more than \(n-g\) distinct points, so this would bound the row-space dimension by \(\lceil(n-g+1)/2\rceil<r\).
This verifies the nonzero homogeneous resultant, including behavior at either projective endpoint.

With \(t_0,t_1\) fixed by two nonzero endpoint coefficients, the row degree after removing its monomial is \(a=t_1-t_0\), the sign is \(\sigma\delta\), and the initial shift is \(S_0=1+p(n-g-t_0-t_1)\).
Both selected rows therefore use the same orientation and \(S=|S_0|\equiv\pm1\bmod p\).
For \(S_0>0\) their common span is \(p(n-g-2t_0)+1\), and for \(S_0<0\) it is \(p(2t_1-(n-g))-1\).
In either case it has the claimed parity and is at most \(4pd+1\).
Neither a monomial nor \(G(x^p,1)\) vanishes at \(\alpha\).
Division, reversal of the row representation and scalar normalization thus preserve the double factor.

### Selecting both witnesses and bounding their determinants

Division by \(G\), a common monomial shift and simultaneous reversal act invertibly on the rational jet image, the last map also using the inversion automorphism of \(K\).
The odd trace coordinates and both even trace charts are invertible coordinates on the two admissible components.
For the positive even sign \(\Delta^2=q^2-4\ne0\), and the congruence identifies the divided remainder with \(v-U_0'(q)\).
For the negative even sign it gives the same expression in its own coordinates.
In the odd chart the expression is \(v+u-2(2\epsilon-q)U_0'(q)\).
In each case the zero-remainder set is a rational linear graph of dimension \(m\).

A linear space of pairs \((u,v)\) with dependent rational coordinate vectors has dimension at most \(m\) for \(m\ge2\).
If two independent first components occur, their sums force a common scalar of proportionality, and adding pairs with first component zero forces their second components to vanish.
If the first components span just one nonzero rational line, all second components belong to it as well, so the dimension is at most \(2\le m\).
If all first components vanish the dimension is at most \(m\).
The larger image therefore permits a nonzero minor in both the original and final charts, together with a nonzero linear remainder component.
Its dimension also exceeds that of a line over \(K^+\), so the original two-row trace determinant is a nonzero polynomial in two independent parameter tuples.
It suffices to choose one nonzero rational coordinate of this determinant.

The two resultants contribute at most \(4n\) to the degree of the simultaneous exclusions.
Four endpoint forms, four linear remainder components, four quadratic minors and the bilinear determinant component add at most \(18\), within the stated allowance of \(24\).
Successive univariate interpolation on the integer grid therefore preserves at least one pair of choices.
Their original rows and reduced trace coefficients have magnitude at most \(H_*=r(4n+24)T\).
The integrated log is bounded by the log of the original diagonal's coefficient length, at most \(\log(2(n+1)H_*)\).
From \(B/l\) and \(k=2pd\) this is \(O(L_l)\) with absolute constants.
Removing any nonzero integral polynomial factor or integer content cannot increase Mahler measure.
This gives the same upper bound for the final noncyclotomic cofactor with multiplicity.

The cross difference of the two original centered equations cancels the \(J\)-terms and gives \(2p\beta^{-n}\widetilde W=(1-\alpha^2)(U_1V_2-U_2V_1)\).
This proves nonvanishing before division.
Each trace value has conjugate absolute bound \(2mH_*e^{(m-1)|\log|z||}\), while \(|N(1\pm\alpha)|\le2^d e^h\).
Multiplying these estimates over the embeddings gives \(\log|N\widetilde W|\ll dL_l\), and the first centered equation gives the same bound for each \(\log|N\widetilde A_i(\beta)|\).
For common division the Euler derivatives of \(G\) cancel in the alternating determinant, leaving the divisor \(G(\beta,1)^2\) of nonzero integral norm.
Common monomial shifts change the determinant by a unit, and reversal of two equal-degree rows sends it to minus its conjugate under inversion times \(\beta^{2a}\).
The individual evaluations transform by the same unit and conjugacy considerations.
Removing each row's scalar content can only decrease the norms.
This proves (1) with nonzero final determinant and evaluations.
For coprime row pairs a common rational graph ratio would force proportionality by a scalar after their common endpoint normalization, contradicting that determinant.
The two-graph inequality consequently applies.

Before division the reduced-coefficient estimate from v199 gives \(\log|N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}D_i|\ll m L_l\).
If \(G=1\), removing \(X^{t_0}\) shifts the odd center by \(pt_0\), preserving the two trace components.
Exchanging the rows multiplies both components by \(-\sigma\), as follows by applying inversion to \(1-\sigma\alpha\) and \(1+\sigma\alpha\) with the corresponding Euler derivative sign.
Dividing by a positive integer content scales the remainder down as well.
For general \(G\) the preserved determinant estimate requires no bound for the individual final remainder norms.
In the specified prime window \(u=h+\log(2dp)/p\to0\), so \(l=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) yields all three normalized costs \(O(u)\).
The adaptive choice \(l=\lceil d\sqrt u\rceil\) lies in the required range eventually, gives \(g<l=o(d)\), and bounds the normalized costs by \(O(\sqrt u)\).
