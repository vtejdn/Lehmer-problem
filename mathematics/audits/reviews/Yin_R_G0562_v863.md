# Simultaneous twist congruences for a cyclotomic compositum

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T02:39 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the fixed [C345 v723](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v723.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had previously independently reviewed the single-prime input v722, took no part in the development of v723 and received the fixed proof and review scope without proof-generation discussion, author explanations or previous findings on v723.
This review checked the full conductor grid, all simultaneous divisibilities, their normalization, the variable-conductor estimates, the examples and the class-product scope.

The cyclotomic extension is abelian, so \(F/\mathbb Q\) is Galois and its group is \(G/H\).
Restriction from the Galois extension \(KE/K\) to \(E\) is injective and the fixed field of its image inside \(E\) is \(E\cap K\), giving exactly \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\) by Galois correspondence.
In particular \([KE:\mathbb Q]=hd\) for arbitrary \(K\).
Each multiplier \(g_p\) is a unit in every conductor coordinate, including the coordinate above \(p\).
For a chosen coset the primitive torsion factor has order \(N\).
Raising a twist to the power \(N\) recovers \(\alpha^{uN}\), a generator of \(K\), after which division by \(\alpha^u\) recovers a primitive root and gives the full compositum.
The action over \(K\) accounts for all choices within a coset.
A conjugacy between two twists at the same level fixes \(\alpha^{uN}\), hence lies over \(K\) and forces the cosets to coincide.
Each embedding of \(K\) occurs \(h\) times and each torsion multiplier has modulus one, so the logarithmic measure is \(hum\).
Unique factorization of \(u\) distinguishes the levels and \(m>0\) distinguishes their measures.
This gives \(bJ\) distinct monic irreducibles of degree \(hd\).

At a fixed conductor prime \(p\), for two labels whose ratio belongs to \(B\cap I_p\) one may choose representatives differing multiplicatively by an element supported in the \(p\)-coordinate, by the definition of the quotient image.
In each conjugate their torsion factors then agree after reduction, since their quotient has \(p\)-power order.
At successive \(j_p\) the new torsion exponent agrees with \(p\) times the old exponent modulo the entire prime-to-\(p\) part of \(N\), because \(g_p\) is \(p\) in all those coordinates.
Its \(p\)-primary discrepancy likewise disappears after reduction.
Holding every other index and the coset fixed, the new reduced root multiset is therefore the Frobenius image of the old one, with every embedding of the common field counted.
Frobenius preserves the old multiset with multiplicities because the polynomial reduces into \(\mathbb F_p[X]\).
This proves common coefficient residues within each block of \(e_pJ_p\) labels.
Overlaps between the partitions for different primes are permitted.

For each other member of a given polynomial's congruence class the resultant at \(p\) is divisible by \(p^{hd}\), by evaluating the coefficient difference at its roots and extracting an integer resultant.
The product discriminant counts each unordered pair twice, so at \(p\) the lower-bound exponent is \(hd\,bJ(e_pJ_p-1)\).
Contributions from all applicable primes multiply even on the same resultant, because it is a nonzero rational integer.
The individual polynomial discriminants dominate \(|D_{KE}|\) by the power-basis index, and the discriminant tower dominates the root discriminant of \(E\).
The cited source gives \(|D_E|^{1/\varphi(N)}=N/\prod_{p\mid N}p^{1/(p-1)}=\varphi(N)/C_N\), including conductors divisible by two.
For \(D=bJhd\), subtracting its logarithm from \(\log D\) gives \(\log((b/s)JdC_N)\).
Summing \(hum\) over all levels and labels multiplies \(bhm\) by the product of the finite geometric sums.
The resulting Vandermonde estimate is exactly (2), including the coefficient \(2-2/D\).

To extract the uniform estimate take \(B=I_p\) with \(|I_p|=e\) and vary only \(j_p\).
Every remaining prime contributes a nonnegative term on the left of (2).
For \(Y=\log(1/(hm))\ge0\), \(J=1+\lfloor Y/\log p\rfloor\) gives \((eJ-1)\log p\ge(e-1)Y\), and \(hm\,p^{J-1}\le1\).
The measure term is at most \(2ep/(p-1)\le4e\), which covers \(p=2\).
For each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the large-prime factors of \(C_N\) have logarithm at most \(\log p/(p-1)\le\varepsilon\log(p-1)\), while the finitely many others contribute a bounded factor regardless of conductor exponents.
This gives \(C_N\ll_\varepsilon\varphi(N)^\varepsilon\).
Each factor is at least one, since \((1+1/(p-1))^{p-1}\le p\) at every prime.
Along \(hm\to0\), Voutier's degree bound gives \(d\to\infty\) and \(h\le1/m<(\log(3d))^3/2\) eventually, while \(s\le d\).
Thus \(\log C_N=o(\log d)\), uniformly over the changing conductor.
If \(e\to\infty\), the terms \(4e+\log J\) are \(o(eY)\), so (4) proves the desired upper limit.
If \(e\) is bounded, Voutier instead gives \(eY=O_e(\log\log(3d))\).
The subsequence argument covers all sequences of conductors and their varying primes.
Replacement of \(Y\) by \(\log(1/m)\) in this conclusion uses the specified smallness of \(\log h\).

For the effective assertion, each factor of \(C_N\) is at most \(p-1\).
Thus \(C_N\le\varphi(N)=sh\).
For \(hm\le1\) and \(d\ge2\) the preceding bound on \(h\) gives \(\log(d C_N)\le5\log(3d)\le10\log(2d)\), and \(d=1\) follows from \(m\ge\log2\).
When \(e\ge2\), the intersection hypothesis and \(J\le1+2Y\le4e^{Y/2}\) give the claimed coefficient \(e-3/2\) on \(Y\).
The ratios \(e/(e-3/2)\le4\) and \(1/(e-3/2)\le2\) give \(16+40/\eta+2\log4\) as an upper bound on \(Y\).
For \(e=1\) the same hypothesis bounds the degree by \(\exp(1/\eta)\), so the degree-one estimate or Voutier yields an effective positive bound.
Together with \(hm>1\) this proves the stated gap for the normalized measure \(hm\).

If every coordinate image equals \(\Gamma\) and \(N\) has at least two distinct prime factors, the complement of each one coordinate still maps onto \(\Gamma\).
Its fixed field in \(E\) is exactly the cyclotomic field of that single prime-power coordinate, and the part of \(F\) fixed by the complement is \(\mathbb Q\).
This proves the asserted individual intersection for \(F=K\cap E\).
With all levels equal to one and \(B=\Gamma\), the left side of (2) is \((s-1)\log\operatorname{rad}(N)\), yielding (3).
For the two distinct odd primes in the example, take the product of the two chosen surjections onto a cyclic group of order \(t\).
Its kernel has index \(t\) and both coordinate images in the quotient have order \(t\).
The compositum of \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{pq})\) with \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^j})\) is \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^j q})\).
Their intersection degree from this formula is \(p-1\), and the intersection contains \(\mathbb Q(\mu_p)\).
This proves the full \(p\)-tower claim for the constructed \(F\), and the same argument applies at \(q\).
For a larger generator field satisfying \(K\cap\mathbb Q(\mu_{pq})=F\), the estimate uses this finite-level intersection, with \(h=(p-1)(q-1)/t\) and \(C_{pq}\le4\).
The assertion about intersections with the two whole primary towers refers to the constructed \(F\) itself.
The existence of a shrinking sequence of such generators is open at this scope.

For the unrestricted input, the equal-size torsion-quotient classes give distinct class products whose conjugates continue to be distinguished by all positive powers.

The compositum and every intersection parameter are then calculated using the field of a class product.
