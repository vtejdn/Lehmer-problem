## Degree and logarithmic measure at an effective delay

At 2026-11-17T10:36 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed v211 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#decision-order-ratio-gap-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received no proof-generation discussion, exploration notes, author explanations or prior review conclusions on v211.
The argument proves (1) for every stated characteristic, both signs and every noncyclotomic irreducible factor in the ninth-power delay range.
It also proves the uniform specializations and the simultaneous small-order example.

The [allocation in v198](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) applies to the same primes, exponents and signs.
Since \(r/g\ge1/g\) and \(g\le D\), its degree error is smaller than \(r/(2g)\), and increasing the fixed delay constant makes its measure error smaller than \((r/(2g))\sum_i\log p_i\).
Consequently \(d\ge n/(2g)\), \(m(S)\asymp d/n\), and \(h(\alpha)\ll n^{-1}\) by monicity.
The constant coefficient of \(P\) is \(\varepsilon\), so its roots are units.
I used the existing [v198 review](Yin_R_G0562_v198.md#prime-block-factorization) for the allocation and the [v201 review](Yin_R_G0562_v201.md#local-effective-delay) for the weighted coefficient count.
I checked the reduction in v201 and the inequalities under which its count is used here.
The sign choice gives \(X^\Delta\overline{A^*}=\prod_{p_i\ne\ell}(-p_i)\overline A\) and \(b^M=b\), including at \(\ell=2\).
Thus the \(m\) distinguished residues have base multiplicity \(M\), with just \(D\) additional roots distributed according to \(\bar A\).

For a local conjugate \(x\), its \(e_x f_x\) conjugates over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) reduce into the Frobenius orbit of its residue, of size at most \(f_x\).
The reduced local minimal polynomial has coefficients in \(\mathbb F_\ell\), so each residue in that orbit occurs equally often and has multiplicity at least \(e_x\) in \(\bar F\).
For \(m\le64g(B+1)\) one has \(m=O((D+1)^2)\), \(B\le2D\) and \(h\) bounded solely by the fixed inputs.
It follows uniformly that \(M\ge2\ell^h(B+1)\) and \(mB+D\le n/(8g)\le d/4\) on enlarging \(C_0\).
The v201 count then bounds \(v(x^m-b)\) above by \(1/(M/\ell^{h-1}-B)\) for all but \(d/4\) of the \(d\) conjugates, since \(S\) divides \(F\).
This valuation is positive in \(e_x^{-1}\mathbb Z\), and \(\bar F\) has multiplicity at most \(M+D\le2M\) at the residue.
These inequalities give both bounds in (3).

For \(m>64g(B+1)\), the two nonzero reductions \(\bar A,\overline{H/\ell^h}\) exclude at most \(D+B\) of the distinguished residues.
Allowing all \(D\) extra roots, the cost is at most \(3(B+1)M<6n/(64g)<d/4\), since \(n+\Delta\le2n\).
At a remaining residue \(e_x\le M\), \(A(x)\) is a unit and \(v(H(x))=h\).
Writing \(x=\zeta(1+y)\) at its unique lift gives \(v(y)=v(x^m-b)>0\), since \(\ell\nmid m\), and \(v((1+y)^N-1)=h\).
If \(v(y)>\ell^{h-1}/M\), the summand of index \(i\) in this last binomial has coefficient valuation at least \(\max(0,k)\) for \(k=v_\ell(M)-v_\ell(i)\), while \(iv(y)>\ell^{h-1-k}\).
For \(k\ge h\) the coefficient has valuation at least \(h\) and \(iv(y)>0\).
For \(0\le k<h\) one has \(\ell^{h-1-k}\ge h-k\), and for \(k<0\) one has \(\ell^{h-1-k}\ge h\).
Every summand would have valuation greater than \(h\), proving the desired upper bound on \(v(y)\) by contradiction.
The positive valuation of \(x^m-b\) in the field of \(x\) now gives \(e_x\ge M/\ell^{h-1}\), also when \(M=1\).

The residue order \(t\) divides both \(2m\) and \(\ell^{f_x}-1\), hence \(t/\gcd(t,m)\mid2\).
Reducing the cyclic group generated by \(\ell\bmod m\) to modulus \(t'=\gcd(t,m)\) gives a kernel of size at most \(m/t'\), by counting representatives in one fiber.
The resulting lower bound \(o t'/m\le f_x\), together with \(f_x\le d/(c_0 M)\), gives \(m/t'\ge Z\) separately for each good conjugate.
If \(Z\le1\), the resulting degree bound already suffices, including for \(m=1\).
Otherwise each such \(m/t'\) is divisible by a power at least \(Z^{1/s}\) of some prime dividing \(m\).
Choosing the least adequate power for each prime makes \(J=2m/p^{a_p}\) independent of \(x\) on a set of at least \(3d/(4s)\) embeddings, and \(t\mid J\) there.

On these embeddings, \(v(x^J-1)\ge1/(2M)\).
Below \(1/(\ell-1)\) the binomial valuation bound multiplies this lower bound by \(\ell\), and from that threshold it adds at least one each time.
Thus the first \(\lceil\log_\ell(2M)\rceil\) raisings suffice even at \(\ell=2\), and the next \(4s\) provide valuation at least \(4s\).
The other embeddings contribute nonnegatively and nontorsion makes the norm nonzero.
Its absolute value is consequently at least \(\ell^{3d}\), while the complex product gives at most \(2^d\exp(JE\,m(S))\).
Taking logarithms and dividing by \(d\) leaves the positive constant \(3\log\ell-\log2\) on the left.
Using \(h(\alpha)\ll1/n\), \(E\le2M\ell^{4s+1}\) and \(mM\le2n\) then gives \(Z^{1/s}\le p^{a_p}\ll\ell^{4s+1}\).
This yields (1), since \(M/n\ge1/m\) and \(m(S)\asymp d/n\).

If \(r\) distinct odd primes divide \(m\), taking the least common multiple of their even totients saves a factor of at least \(2^{r-1}\) for \(r\ge1\).
Including a power of two multiplies the least common multiple by at most its totient, so \(m/o\le K\) bounds \(s\), including the pure dyadic case.
For any fixed set of allowed primes, exponentiation of \(z=\ell^{p-1}\) at odd \(p\), or \(z=\ell^2\) at \(p=2\), gives \(v_p(z^o-1)=b_p+v_p(o)\).
Indeed a geometric sum for an exponent coprime to \(p\) is a unit, and the geometric sum for exponent \(p\) is congruent to \(p\bmod p^2\) in the stated ranges, including \(z\equiv1\bmod4\) at \(2\).
Since \(p^w\Vert m\) implies \(p^w\mid z^o-1\), multiplying the distinct required prime powers in \(o\) bounds \(m/o\) by \(\prod p^{b_p}\).
For \(N=\ell^a u p^b\), bounded positive \(u\) adds only finitely many possible primes, regardless of all the exponents.

For the example with blocks \(2,3\), any other eligible characteristic would divide both \(3\) and \(8\).
For each fixed \(g\ge2\), the integral primitive element gives a nonconstant monic minimal polynomial \(f\) with nonzero constant coefficient.
If finitely many primes with product \(V\) divided its nonzero values, the integers \(f(f(0)Vt)/f(0)\) would be congruent to one modulo every such prime and unbounded in absolute value, a contradiction.
Outside the finite set dividing \(6gT\), a root modulo \(N\) therefore specializes the three required elements simultaneously, since their expressions in \(\theta\) have denominators invertible modulo \(N\).
The cyclotomic factorization of the separable \(X^g-1\) forces the image of \(\zeta_g\) to have exact order \(g\).
Both \(2\) and \(3\) are \(g\)-th powers modulo \(N\), so their orders divide \((N-1)/g\).
As \(N\) can be arbitrarily large, \(n=N-g\) meets the delay bound and \(\gcd(n,g)=1\).
Taking \(g\) arbitrarily large excludes a uniform bound on either order ratio for these primitive examples.
Their individual factor measures remain undetermined by the order upper bounds.
