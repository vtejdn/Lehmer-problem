## Equality would force two forbidden signed periods

At 2026-11-20T21:37 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently checked the full [strict energy bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v659.md#statement) and the projection and integer estimates used in its proof.
The reviewer received the fixed mathematics and the questions to test, without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict on this claim.
The [earlier review of the signed-period obstruction](Yin_R_G0562_v794.md#finding) was available and consulted for that dependency.
The proof establishes absolute \(\eta,m_0>0\) for every integral Laurent numerator indivisible by the specified denominator.

In the balanced division the previous energy floor and its equality conditions give \(mn_\pm\to x=(\log 2)/2\) and squared coefficient excess \(o(1/m)\).
The final scalar optimization in that bound has a unique minimum at \(x\), because the derivative of its support-dependent part is \(2-2\sqrt2 e^{-x}\), with positive derivative in turn.
The excess term has positive coefficient, so \(m\|Q_\pm\|_2^2\to x\) separately.
For the short remainder \(H=R/P\), its squared norm times \(m\) tends to \(1/2\).
Expanding \(\|H+Q\|_2^2\) then forces \(m\operatorname{Re}\langle H,DQ\rangle\to-rw\), where \(r=1/\sqrt2\) and \(w=2(1-e^{-x})\).
The earlier bound \(\limsup\sqrt m\|DQ\|_2\le w\) and Cauchy–Schwarz force convergence to \(w\) and \(\sqrt m\|H+(r/w)DQ\|_2\to0\).

Reflection \(Tf(z)=z^{-1}f(1/z)\) takes index \(h\) to \(-h-1\), including \(0\) to \(-1\), and intertwines \(P_-\) with \(P_+\).
On the reflected input it changes multiplication by \(\bar V\) to multiplication by \(W(z)=\overline{V(\bar z)}\), an analytic inner function.
For either end the translated prefix before impulse \(j\) has strictly negative input frequencies.
Its projected output \(Y\) is orthogonal to \(WH^2\), since one can remove the projection when pairing with \(Wf\) and then cancel \(W\).
The projection of \(1\) to \(H^2\ominus WH^2\) is \(1-\overline{W(0)}W\) and has norm \(s=\sqrt{1-|W(0)|^2}\) for Haar probability measure.
This proves \(|Y(0)|\le s\|Y\|_2\).
Advancing to any next occupied position discards only modes, so \(l=\|Y\|_2\le L_{j-1}\), including when the gap exceeds one.
Adding \(b_j W\) increases the squared norm by \(|b_j|^2\) by orthogonality, and dropping the new constant output subtracts \(|Y(0)+b_j W(0)|^2\) exactly.
Uniformly in \(j\), \(sl\le s\|B\|_2\to\sqrt{2x}<1\) as an upper bound, while \(t|b_j|\ge t\to1\).
Thus one can square the reverse triangle lower bound \(t|b_j|-sl\), leaving the upper bound \((tl+s|b_j|)^2\) on \(L_j^2\).

The corresponding scalar majorant satisfies \(S_j-L_j\ge t(S_{j-1}-L_{j-1})\), and the terminal output projected to nonnegative modes is part of the tail at \(e_n<0\).
Its scaled norm is bounded above by \(\sqrt m S_n\to b=\sqrt2(1-e^{-x})\) at each end.
Before reflection \(DQ_-=Q_--\bar U P_-(UQ_-)\) has only strictly negative modes and \(DQ_+=Q_+-V P_+(\bar V Q_+)\) has only nonnegative modes.
Their norms equal the two terminal projection norms, and their sum has scaled norm tending to \(w=\sqrt2 b\).
Pythagoras forces both scaled norms to tend to \(b\).
Since \(t^n\to e^{-x}>0\), terminal saturation bounds the scaled error at every earlier occupied position uniformly.
At \(k=\lfloor\delta/m\rfloor\), \(0<\delta<x\) fixed, the geometric sum and integer excess consequently give \(\sqrt m L_k\to\sqrt2(1-e^{-\delta})\) and \(m\sum_{i\le k}|b_i|\to\delta\), with all future modes after \(e_k\) included.

Analyticity and the boundary norm of \(W\) give \(\int |W-W(0)|^2\,d\tau=1-|W(0)|^2=s^2\).
The probability obtained by division by \(s^2\) therefore has zeroth Fourier coefficient one.
Projecting above \(e_k\) kills \(W(0)B_k\), so \(L_k\le s\|B_k\|_{L^2(\gamma)}\).
In the expansion of this weighted squared norm, distinct occupied positions give nonzero Fourier lags off the diagonal.
The diagonal weight divided by \((\sum_{i\le k}|b_i|)^2\) tends to zero, since \(\sum_{i\le k}|b_i|^2=O(1/m)\).
As \(s^2/m\to2\), these bounds give \(\liminf\sup_{g\ge1}|\widehat\gamma(g)|\ge((1-e^{-\delta})/\delta)^2\) for each fixed \(\delta\).
Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) after this limit yields one.
Conjugate pairs of roots in the real exterior factor make \(U,V\) real-coefficient functions, so both probabilities are conjugation invariant.
The reflected probability gives the original signed-binomial integrals.
Choosing lags arbitrarily close to the two suprema separately and taking the signs of the real coefficients gives squared distances \(2(1-|\widehat\gamma(g)|)\to0\).

For either independently chosen lag \(g\), the commutator with either Fourier cut uses just the input block \([-g,-1]\).
Partitioning into length-\(g\) blocks, the variation of the squared block norms bounds twice the energy of that block and is at most \(2\|y\|_2\|(X^g-\epsilon)y\|_2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz.
Thus each designated difference makes its own projected term small at scale \(\sqrt m\), uniformly in \(g\), and the other factor has norm at most two.
Applying their product to the reconstructed \(H\) multiplies the error by at most four.
All roots of the product lie on the unit circle, so it is coprime to \(P\) even with multiplicities.
The product times the nonzero short \(R\) remains indivisible by \(P\), and the unrestricted lower limit \(K>0\) contradicts the vanishing scaled squared norm.
Finally, failure of every uniform increment supplies inputs with \(m<1/j\) and scaled energy below \(K+1/j\).
The same lower limit forces that sequence to attain \(K\) in the limit, completing the contradiction.
The argument gives neither an evaluated increment nor a Mahler-measure gap by itself.
