**Mass, zeros and exterior counts.**
The \(2u\) oriented halves form one orbit, giving the same integer multiplicity \(e\) for every actual conjugate of their product.
For reciprocal halves the logarithms of their products are opposite, so their combined exterior contribution at the product level is \(|P_B-N_B|\).
At the original level the contribution is \(P_B+N_B\).
Their difference is \(2\min(P_B,N_B)\), including when the minimum or the whole block vanishes.
If a half contains a neutral root, complex conjugation carries this half to its opposite because the conjugate of that root is its inverse.
Conjugation preserves all absolute values, whereas taking the opposite half negates all logarithms.
Thus \(P_B=N_B\) in that block, and zero loss forces every one of its labels to be neutral.
Without a neutral root, zero loss means that all roots in the half are on the same side of the circle.
These alternatives give exactly the monochromatic blocks and identify the full exterior set at zero loss.
Each exterior half product accounts for \(b\) exterior original roots in a distinct block, yielding \(l(z)\ge be\,l(\beta)\).
Dividing by \(d=2ub\) gives the proportion bound, with equality at zero loss, and fixed-size torsion fibers preserve this proportion upon compression.

**The exact degree after extension of the base.**
The set of exterior roots is strictly better in product modulus than any different subset of the same size, since replacing any exterior root by an unused root decreases modulus.
Its product differs from \(M(z)\) by a rational sign.
Its stabilizer therefore equals the stabilizer of \(M(z)\) itself, also when one compares positive powers.
Over the halves field \(F\) every root permutation acts within each chosen half and applies the same label permutation on its inverse half.
This preserves the three color multiplicities separately in each block and gives the multinomial product as an upper bound.
The supported pure copies of \(A_b\) in the actual splitting group fix all halves.
For \(b\ge9\) some color occurs at least twice, so a transposition preserving the target coloring can correct parity in every block.
The direct product of these supported alternating groups therefore attains the upper bound exactly.
In a nonmonochromatic block the position choices for one color alone give at least \(b\) possibilities.

For finite solvable Galois \(K/F\), the intersection \(K\cap L\) is Galois and solvable over \(F\).
