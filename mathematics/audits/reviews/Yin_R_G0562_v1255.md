## Orders, coefficient words, and polynomial bit cost in the degree ceiling

On 2026-11-25 an agent outside the development of [C0901 v177](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#statement) inspected the fixed proof and its input statements for Yin.
The received material was the final version identified by SHA256 41B1DABD7BA24828E550D0EE87C3C22E3F24912FA25899620E189E258F75850E without development explanations or previous findings on this claim.
The equalities of full composite orders, the necessary binary count, the simultaneous exclusion example and the polynomial-time bounded-degree listing all hold with the scopes stated.

The sign and pole hypotheses of the [realization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) give a Salem unit \(T\) of degree \(d=2D\) and the positive sign in the [common powers](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion).
The Bézout relation between \(u,v\) recovers \(\beta\) from the two coordinates inside \(\mathbb Q(T)\), and \(T=\beta^u\) gives the reverse inclusion.
Thus the only expanding conjugate of \(\beta\) is \(\beta\) itself, and Voutier's estimate applies with degree \(d\).
The defining equation becomes \(q(\beta^{u+v}-1)=p\beta^u(\beta^{v-u}-1)\).
In the full quotients modulo \(p,q\) the opposite consecutive integer and \(\beta\) are invertible where they are canceled.
The [index](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v897.md#index) of \(\mathbb Z[T]\) in \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\) is \(p+q\), coprime to both moduli.
Consequently each full quotient of the first order identifies with the corresponding quotient of the second, and taking the \(u\)-th power preserves the order because it divides \(v\pm u\), which is coprime to \(u\).
This uses the complete reduction of \(F\) and works without squarefreeness.

The norm of \(p-qT\) equals \(L^2\), and its other \(d-1\) factors are bounded in absolute value by \(L\).
This gives \(Y+1\le(T+1)L^{d-3}\) and \(YT<L^d\), since \(T<3/2\) and \(L\ge5\).
If the order of \(\beta\) at \(c\in\{p,q\}\) is a proper divisor of \(v\pm u\), it is at most half that exponent.
The nonzero norm of \(\beta^a-1\) for that order \(a\) is divisible by \(c^d\) since \(\beta^a-1\in c\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Its absolute value is below \(2^d\beta^a\le 2^d(YT)^{1/2}\).
The resulting strict \(c^2<4L\) contradicts \(q^2\ge4(2q+1)\) for \(q\ge9\).
The exact orders differ by \(2u\) and have gcd dividing two.
The bound \(2u<(\log(3d))^3/q\) follows from \(\log T=u\log\beta<1/q\) and [Corollary 2](../literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001).

For small and large \(q\) alike, passage from the even full modulus to two cannot increase the order.
The norm and degree estimates give \(v+u\le d\log L/\log\beta\ll d\log^3(3d)\log\log(9d)\).
For each exponent up to that bound, at most \(O(\sqrt d)\) small binary irreducible factors can occur using the count of all monic polynomials of degree at most \(\lfloor\tfrac12\log_2d\rfloor\).
Each multiplicity has at most \(d+1\) choices.
For the larger factors there are at most \(N\) types and at most \(O(d/\log(3d))\) occurrences in a degree-\(d\) divisor; counting multisets allows repeats even for inseparable reductions.
The logarithm of the resulting binomial bound is \(O(d\log\log(9d)/\log(3d))\) since \(N=O(d\log^3(3d)\log\log(9d))\).
The small-factor cost and the sum over \(N\) are absorbed at this scale.
In the example the coprime lengths \(a,b\) give a product dividing \(Z^{ab}-1\) integrally.
Each geometric factor forces its full length in every modulus by the monic degree bound on a proper divisor of the order, and thus the product forces \(ab\).
The leading two lifted coefficients translate to first trace coefficient \(d+2\equiv4\bmod pq\), sufficient to lift the word in the [pole lattice](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#simplex).
The [one-prime construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v171.md#approximation) supplies the claimed strict realization away from the chosen denominator prime.

For the enumeration, the explicit binary-length upper envelope for \(U\) exceeds \(1/(2/(\log(6X))^3)\), so it bounds \(q\), \(qu\) and \(1/\log\beta\) in the required comparisons.
The looser integer cutoffs obtained by replacing logarithms by binary lengths change only absolute constants.
At fixed \(q\le U\), there are at most \(U/q\) short exponents and \(O(XU\log(2q+1))\) candidate long exponents.
Summing gives \(O(XU^2\log^2(2U))\) triples by elementary harmonic summation.
Each integer input to factorization has degree \(u+v=O(X\log^3(6X)\log\log(9X))\) and coefficient length at most \(2(2q+1)^u\); it is nonzero because the top term of the shifted first summand cannot cancel.
Its bit length is polynomial in the numerical value of \(X\).
Theorem 3.6 of Lenstra, Lenstra and Lovász gives deterministic polynomial bit cost for factoring a primitive integer polynomial, with nonmonic inputs admitted; removing content has polynomial cost. [Author copy](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf)
I inspected that theorem statement for its applicability, without auditing its proof.
The candidate Salem minimum divides the displayed polynomial because \(T^v=Y^u\).
A passing reciprocal factor recovers precisely an integral trace polynomial by the triangular trace conversion and must pass exact pole and signature tests.
In the signature calculation the complex-conjugate summands have opposite signs for each nonzero weight.
After checking all trace roots are real and excluding roots at the three endpoints, weighted signatures count strictly negative, interior and positive root locations exactly.
Entries obtained by Newton recurrences and rational fraction-free inertia calculations have polynomial bit size by the degree and coefficient bounds and Hadamard's inequality.
Thus postprocessing cannot introduce an exponential step in numerical \(X\).
This is an enumeration theorem at a prescribed cutoff and supplies no all-degree termination criterion for the underlying Lehmer question.
No census was executed in this review.
