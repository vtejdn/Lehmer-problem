### Rationality, winding and profile allocation

The negative mass tends to zero in a putative sequence of failures, and the weak limit is a symmetric probability.
The integer coefficients of the tested series stabilize at each fixed index by convergence, even when the tested multiplier varies.
For \(r=m/n\) in lowest terms the normalized reciprocal \(K=n/J_0=(n-mw)/B_0\) has positive real part, integral Taylor coefficients and first coefficient \(-m\bmod n\).
The signed-coset representation of its integer Fourier–Stieltjes sequence gives the squarefree cyclotomic denominator and the nonnegative Laurent density, using singleton cosets for the continuous part.
An analytic rational series integral at zero has only roots of unity as finite poles here by the integral reciprocal recurrence and Kronecker.
A nontorsion zero of \(K\) must therefore be the simple zero of the numerator \(n-mw\) in \(B_0\).
If that zero were absent the normalized numerator of \(K\) would be \(n\) times a product of cyclotomic polynomials, contradicting the first coefficient.
Gauss's lemma for the primitive factor \(n-mw\), and normalization at zero, give the asserted factorization.
At a torsion zero, the lower bound \(\Re K(tv)\ge n(1-t)/(1+t)\) for \(0<t<1\) precludes order two or greater.
The rational identity \(K(w)+K(1/w)=0\) is impossible at \(n/m\), where the reflected term is positive.

Reversal of the normalized cyclotomic factors gives exactly the exponent \(b=\deg V-\deg U-1\) and the product of their leading signs in the bracket.
At a torsion zero \(w\), \(t=(n-mw)/(nw-m)\) is torsion and \(n(tw-1)=m(t-w)\).
The Bézout combination in L462v66 gives \((t-w)/n\) as an algebraic integer all of whose conjugates have modulus at most \(2/n<1\).
Thus \(t=w\) and \(w^2=1\).
The strict bound is available for \(n\ge3\), which accounts for the separate original \(1/2\) classification.
A bracket zero of odd order is a phase crossing, and its odd multiplicity must be changed by a torsion factor for the density to be nonnegative.
In a full turn of the lifted argument of \(w^b(w-r)/(1-rw)\), each upward or downward crossing of the target contributes its sign once, including a stationary crossing of odd order.
Tangencies contribute zero.
The net is \(b+1\); at most two crossings can occur since only the two endpoints are allowed.
This works for ratios where the phase fails to be monotone.
For the \(1/j,\ j\ge3\) argument the Poisson term ranges between \((j-1)/(j+1)\) and \((j+1)/(j-1)\le2\), with the upper endpoint reached only at one angle.
This justifies the stronger monotonicity argument there for \(b\ge0\) or \(b\le-2\).
At \(b=-3,1\) with positive sign neither endpoint solves the phase equation.

I expanded the eight surviving brackets \(w^{\max(0,-b)}P\) and checked the reciprocal identity \(K(w)+K(1/w)=P/B_0\) symbolically in \(j\) for every listed \(B_0\).
Scaling by \(m\) and setting \(j=n/m\) gives the rational table.
For \(b=-3,-2\) with negative sign the respective reciprocal quadratics have trace \(1/r,1/r-1\).
Below trace two their roots are simple circle points away from the endpoints, disjoint from the other bracket zeros and excluded by the torsion test.
This gives \(r\le1/2,\ r\le1/3\), respectively.
At \(r=1/3\) the triple bracket zero at one for \(b=-2\) with negative sign receives one further simple factor in \(U\), giving order four in the density.
At \(b=-1\) with negative sign, exchanging the two linear factors would give \(m(w+w^{-1}-2)\), so the allocation is uniquely determined.
In the other rows the parity of each zero and \(\deg V-\deg U=b+1\) determine the allocation.
The Mahler measures follow from the exterior root \(\lambda(t)\) of a reciprocal quadratic of trace \(t\ge2\), including \(\lambda(2)=1\), and the displayed scalar factors.

### Envelopes and prime specialization

The doubled density of \(J_0\) has \(\sigma=n\rho/|K|^2\) away from atoms.
Since \(M(1-rw)=1\) and \(M(K)=n\), \(M(D)=M(\rho)/n\).
In the row involving \(r\lambda(1/r+1)\), the difference of the squared bounds is precisely \(4(r-2/3)^2\); the proposed comparison is between nonnegative square roots.
Every row outside the two exceptional cases therefore has \(M(D)\le4/3<\sqrt2\).
In the fixed \(1/j\) case the \(1+r\) row also satisfies this bound.
The continuous densities are bounded after their separate atoms are removed, so the separated-block theorem applies.

For \(K=n-mw\), clearing the bound \(D^2\le4\) gives \(r^2(4-(w+w^{-1})^2)\).
Only the two endpoints attain four, and the mean is strictly smaller.
For \(K=(n-mw)/(1-w)\), the double zero of the envelope at one forces \((1-w)^2\mid H\) after clearing any monomial.
Dividing by \(1-w\) gives an integral Laurent polynomial \(L\) whose bound by two, after squaring, leaves exactly \((1-r)^2(2+w+w^{-1})\) in the cleared difference.
Equality is confined to \(-1\).
For either of the resulting norm bounds, Parseval allows at most three unit coefficients.
Three terms outside arithmetic progression have fourth moment \(15>12\).
In progression equal endpoint coefficients give a phase with modulus three, and opposite endpoint coefficients give modulus \(\sqrt5\) at a quarter turn of the progression.
The alignment points of a binomial are as numerous as its exponent difference.
They give gap at most two in the first exception and exactly one in the second, where \(L(1)=0\).
Thus the only possible nonzero \(H\) in the second exception is a shifted signed \((1-w)^2\).
Both exceptional measures, \(1+\sqrt{1-r^2}\) and \(1+r\), are strictly below two at every fixed interior ratio.

For each nonzero shape \(S\) the bound \(M(S)<2\) prevents divisibility by \(n-mw\), of measure \(n\).
At \(w=n/m\) the reflected linear factor is nonzero, so the phase ratio has an actual pole even if \(S(m/n)=0\).
A rational squaring identity would propagate that pole to infinitely many distinct moduli.
The large-prime specialization can therefore avoid all exceptional points from the finitely many shapes, including zeros of the density, and a nonzero specialized moment must be nonreal.
The rational density and integer rows make the conjugate moment systems Galois compatible, with a common denominator in the chosen cyclotomic field.
Their integral power-basis coordinates are integer Fourier–Stieltjes transforms and are periodic outside a finite set.
The positive-tail value on multiples of the period equals the probability mass on the corresponding root group.
Being real, this value must vanish, and every residue-class average then vanishes too.
No nonzero shape vanishes at the chosen node, so there is a greatest nonzero row if any survive.
Taking its power pushforward bounds the corresponding conditional first coefficient by \(1/2\), contradicting \(1\le M(H)\le M(D)/2<1\).
This checks the extinction interface, including every row of L462v65.

### The first discrepancy and the probability at zero

Before the first discrepancy \(n_i\), convolution of \((s_i-r)wJ_0\) with \((1-s_iw)^{-1}\) costs at most \(2|s_i-r|/(1-s_i)\) per coefficient.
This tends uniformly to zero since \(r<1\) is fixed.
The integral discrepancy at \(n_i\) is bounded and nonzero, and retains a nonzero limit after extraction.
Vanishing of all limiting negative-offset differences identifies the first conditional moment at each torsion atom with the extracted phase there.
The atom has its full marginal mass at that phase, so all higher atomic contributions also cancel.
Fixed multiples \(kn_i\) with fixed translated offsets lie in the tested prefix since \(n_i/N_i\to0\).
Changing the multiplier at these indices costs \(o(1)\) by the signed measures' uniform variation bound.
After reflection of the first circle coordinate, the remaining continuous moments have integer coefficients upon multiplication by \(1-rw\).
Their bounded densities make these coefficients square-summable, hence finitely supported, and continuity of the finite conditional Toeplitz matrices supplies positivity at every regular specialization.
This gives \(H_1\ne0\) against extinction.
At \(r=1/2\) the same moving comparison uses the original nine profiles and their fixed-multiplier extinction.
Rotation by \(-1\) handles the positive sign of the linear term.
For L462v65's actual Pisot application the pushforward loses at most \(j/[4(q-1)]\) in negative mass and reads exactly the coefficients \(b_{kd}\) until \(kd<2h+2-d\), including the constant coefficient.

For the degeneration example, at the finitely many zeros of \(1+\cos(l\theta)\) the even multiple \(Ll\) has cosine one.
Compactness gives a fixed \(t>0\) for each chosen pair \(l,L\) making the displayed density strictly positive.
For that \(t,L\), taking \(j\) sufficiently large preserves positivity by uniform convergence of the real part of \(J\).
The analytic transform has constant one and real Taylor coefficients, so the boundary real part represents a symmetric probability with exactly this transform.
Since \(Ll>N\), multiplication by \(1-w/j\) yields the prefix \(1+w^l\).
Every table row except the constant differs by degree two or earlier, and the constant differs at \(l\).
Thus fixed \(l\ge3\) and arbitrarily large \(N\) defeat any common \(c>0,N_0\), even with zero negative mass.
The Dirichlet kernel correction has the same moments as \(\delta_1\) through \(N\), its constant included.
The original continuous density has a positive minimum, so \(a(2N+1)\) below this minimum leaves the new continuous part positive and the only negative part is the specified atom.
Once \(j,N\) are chosen, increasing the integer \(q\) gives the required value \(a=j/[4(q-1)]\).
This counterexample concerns the moment conditions and supplies no Pisot realization.
