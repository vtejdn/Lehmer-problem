## Regularization and exclusion of all parallel-sided limits

For either order of \(d,e\), \(D=e+2d\) is the largest lower offset, and \(b=\max(d,e)\) satisfies \(b\le D\le3b\).
The gcd and the [eight-term rank reduction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight) give \(b/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\), and all the offsets are \(o(h)\).
I checked the normalized prefix and the [signed inverse moments](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) at this scale.

For \(A=2+Y+X(1-Y^2)\), the [circle identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#trapezoid) gives both the two torsion zeros and the bound \(|1-Y^3|^2\le40|S_r|\) uniformly for \(0<r<1\).
Indeed the difference of the squared edge moduli is \((1+2\Re Y)^2\), the sum of the edge moduli is at most five, and shrinking \(X\) can only improve the reverse-triangle lower bound for \(|2+Y+rX(1-Y^2)|\).
The ratio of the two edge polynomials is bounded by one on the disk, so \(W^{10}/S_r\) is holomorphic through the closed bidisk.
For an angular derivative of order \(k\le2\), the estimate at the small denominator has exponent \((10-k)/2-k-1\ge1\) after using the bound for \(|W|^2\).
The uniform second-derivative \(L^2\) bounds therefore control the Fourier coefficients with squared weight \((1+m^2+n^2)^2\).
Since the reciprocal weight is summable on \(\mathbb Z^2\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives a uniform absolute Fourier sum.
Convergence of individual Taylor coefficients and Fatou pass it to \(W^{10}/S\), and signed specialization cannot increase the absolute sum.
For the unweighted inverse the two terms starting at or beyond \(b\) have absolute coefficient sum one, so factoring the first signed half gives the separate local geometric bound through \(Cb\).

The [ordered base and mixed-row tests](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#integer) use fixed shifts of order \(b\) in the common prefix.
I checked the [slope argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) with the actual polynomial.
Its coefficients in \(X\) are coprime and the unequal widths of its two vertical levels preclude association with its reversal.
Thus integrality and reality force divisibility of the slope numerator by \(A\), and any nonzero graph density would give the excluded Haar marginal.
The last continuous harmonic has bound \(M(A)M(f_0)/2<1\), and the weighted rows vanish over the torus zeros.
The [annihilating characters](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#singular) consequently give one fixed product along the extracted sequence, with nonzero shifts after specialization.
For comparable offsets \(D\ge3p\) keeps the third coefficient beyond every first-index test at \(p+o(p)\), and all partial multipliers in the [interval estimates](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) are bounded.
For \(p/b\to0\) the [sixth-root filter](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#separated) uses only the first signed half below \(b\), and its short transition blocks still cover both possible orders.

The local sums and the first-difference moment identity justify all the \(O(b)\) shifts in \(W_i^{10}U_i\).
The regularized inverse gives the other bounded-variation comparison after multiplication by \(Q_0\).
I checked the long common prefix, the polynomial identity and the [two-cutoff estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) with left degree \(O(h)\).
Multiplying by two more powers of \(W_i\) gives a bounded circle \(L^1\) norm without dividing by a torus zero.
The [support bound](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) and the [fixed-support gap](../literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) contradict \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one because each added binomial has measure one.
Combining this result with the [two shapes](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v100.md#actual), the [integral-factor exclusion](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v100.md#integral-factor) and the [equal-side exclusion](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v96.md#reciprocal) covers every parallel-sided convex quadrilateral of an escaping sequence.
I find all five ledger sections valid.

## The full branch-two theorem

The trace construction covers both degree parities and gives paired opposite coefficients with zero midpoint in \(\Pi\).
Thus the bound of nine terms allows at most eight, and the six-term theorem covers every smaller support.
The fixed-branch derivative identity makes bounding \(q\) and bounding \(\theta-q\) from below equivalent for this qualitative assertion.
I checked the extracted rank-two measure-two polynomial and the applicability of the two distinct collinearity exclusions to triples of its support.
Evaluation at any sufficiently late input is positive on all three nonconstant vectors, so their cone is pointed.
Two extreme rays contain one vector each and the third lies strictly between those rays.

The two primitive separating functionals give an injective finite-index change of coordinates and the dual torus map preserves Haar measure.
In the resulting coordinates the cases \(r=p\) and \(s=q_0\) have two parallel opposite sides and are covered by the preceding ledger finding.
In every other case the leading coefficient in each transverse slice has fixed nonzero modulus.
Its log measure is therefore continuous even when the constant tends to zero.
The constant-term Jensen lower bound has the same mean as the log measure, giving equality on every open arc where the constant is nonzero.
A coefficient of modulus two at either extreme would make this lower bound tend to minus infinity near one of its zeros, contradicting the lower bound from the leading coefficient.
Both extreme coefficients consequently have modulus one.

At \(a x^p=-1\), the specialized trinomial has constant one, measure one and leading modulus at least one.
The root product at both endpoints forces unit leading modulus and unit modulus for every root.
Conjugate reciprocity then places its middle exponent exactly halfway between its endpoints.
I checked both ratios \(\{1/2,2\}\) and the coupling between them.
When \(s=2q_0\), the real middle coefficient in \(1+bY+c_0x^rY^2\) forces \(c_0x^r=1\) at every one of the \(p\) roots, hence \(p\mid r\) and \(r=2p\).
Swapping the variables gives the reverse implication.
Two half ratios give the excluded affine midpoint, while two double ratios give \(c_0=1\) because \(x^{2p}=1\) on the slice.

After absorbing the two signs into phases the last polynomial is \(2+X+Y+X^2Y^2\).
The phase average of its diagonal-slice log measure is \(\log2\) under the proposed equality, and the constant two gives that same pointwise lower bound.
Continuity therefore forces the trivial-phase specialization \(2+2z+z^4\) to have no interior root.
For the disk-preserving reduction I checked that outside the closed disk every reciprocal root factor has modulus at most that of its original factor.
The strictly larger leading modulus thus prevents cancellation in \(\operatorname{lc}(P)P-P(0)P^*\).
The numerator vanishes at zero, and the two successive quotients are \(3z^3+4z^2-2\) and \(5z^2+12z+8\).
At both steps the required strict endpoint inequality holds.
The final root product is \(8/5\), which is impossible if both roots are in the closed disk.
I find the proof complete for every positive-constant branch-two input in the stated support range, with qualitative constants independent of the exponents and heights.
