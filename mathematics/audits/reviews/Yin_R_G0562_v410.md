## Admissible approximants, limit of the counts and cost in full energy

At 2026-11-18T14:30 UTC an agent outside proof development independently checked all three sections of [the radial-energy infimum construction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v180.md#bound) against the fixed argument and its cited inputs.
The reviewer received no development dialogue and had independently reviewed the Dirichlet identity and the earlier Salem-pencil example.

Degree preservation makes the roots of \(Q\) the \(d\) distinct powers \(\alpha_i^p\).
The constant-row input applies for each \(1\le r<p\), including \(r=1\) at \(p=2\), with \(b>0\) and \(\deg U_0<2d\).
Because \(U_0(\beta)=b\alpha^r\), \(W=U_0-2b\) takes the nonzero value \(b(\alpha^r-2)\) at \(\beta\), as a power of an algebraic unit cannot equal two.
Irreducibility of \(Q\) gives \(\gcd(W,Q)=1\).
At each of the finitely many complex zeros of \(W\), the coefficient of \(M\) in \(b+M Q^2\) is nonzero.
This excludes a finite set of \(M\) for row coprimality, also allowing constant \(W\).
The coefficient at order two of \((2-x^r)Q(x^p)^2\) at \(\alpha\) is \((2-\alpha^r)(p\alpha^{p-1}Q'(\beta))^2\ne0\).
Thus varying \(M\) leaves at most one further exception to exact multiplicity two.
Both constant coefficients are nonzero for all sufficiently large \(M\), since \(Q(0)^2=1\).
The distinct residues and equal row degrees \(k=2d\) give degree \(pk+j<p(k+1)\) after translation to any \(i<j<p\).
Positive common content divides both rows, and its removal preserves their roots in every pencil and all multiplicities of the diagonal.

For unit \(t\), the normalized monic pencil equals \(Q^2+(U_0-tb)/(M(2-t))\).
The error is of degree below \(k\) and tends uniformly to zero in coefficients since \(|2-t|\ge1\).
For fixed \(P,p,r\) the reciprocal monic polynomial has bounded coefficients too, since the constant terms converge uniformly to one.
All roots therefore lie in a common compact annulus for sufficiently large \(M\).
On any circle avoiding the root radii of \(Q\), its squared polynomial has a strictly positive minimum modulus, so the root counts stabilize uniformly in phase by Rouché's theorem.
For almost every \(u>0\), the limiting radial derivatives at \(u\) and at \(-u\) are respectively \(-2N_+(u/p)\) and \(2N_-(u/p)\), the factor two counting the doubled roots.
The exceptional logarithmic radii, including zero, have measure zero.
The fixed compact support in logarithmic radius and the bound \(k\) on each derivative permit dominated convergence of the squares.
Substituting \(u=ps\) and dividing by \(kp=2dp\) gives exactly the first right-hand side of (1) as the limit along the constructed witnesses.
This bounds the infimum without asserting existence of a minimizer.

Integration of the two count functions gives the total respective positive and absolute negative root logarithms, both \(h\) by the unit hypothesis.
The pointwise bounds \(N_\pm^2\le N_\pm(0)N_\pm\) yield the second inequality.
For a Salem unit both counts equal one on \(0<s<h\) and zero on \(s>h\), so the first bound is \(4h/d\).
The approximation first fixes the input, prime and offset, and may require different \(M\) for each choice.
The resulting bound on the infimum is independent of the prime and offset, giving the stated uniform conclusion even when the prime is chosen from the input.

The same coefficient convergence and the continuity of monic Mahler measure on a compact coefficient set give \(\int m(U-tV)\,d\sigma=\log M+\log2+2ph+o(1)\).
Here the circle average of \(\log|2-t|\) is \(\log2\), and \(m(Q)=ph\).
Writing \(s=\deg W\), the \(k-s\) projective zeros at infinity after the row subtraction contribute \(M^{k-s}\) to the absolute homogeneous resultant.
Homogeneity in the second argument gives \(M^{-s}\operatorname{Res}(W,b+MQ^2)\to\operatorname{Res}(W,Q^2)\ne0\), also when \(s=0\).
Thus \(\log B=k\log M+O(1)\), and the identity \(2kI-\log B=\mathcal D(g)\) gives \(\mathcal D(g)=k\log M+O(1)\).
Scalar division subtracts only a constant from \(g\), and the common content of the rows divides the content of the fixed nonzero \(W\).
Indeed \(F/M\to(2-x^r)Q(x^p)^2\) at fixed input and prime with fixed nonzero limiting leading coefficient, so the cofactor cost can grow after primitive normalization.
The all-prime upper bound concerns radial energy alone, while the earlier prescribed-prime Salem witnesses also bound the cofactor rate.
