## Finding

The convergence \(m(V_j)/j-\log\theta\to0\) holds uniformly over the stated compact range of Pisot units, both signs and all \(k\ge-K\).
The positive-offset estimate and the cyclic resultant conclusion follow with the claimed quantifiers.

The only zero of \(g\) in the closed disk is the simple zero \(a=1/\theta\), where \(f(a)\ne0\) by coprimality.
Multiplication by \(b_a\) removes the pole of \(f/g\), and \(|B|=1\) on the circle bounds \(|B|\) by one inside.
The pole also prevents \(z^{k_+}f/(\sigma z^{k_-}g)\) from being constant, since \(a\ne0\).
Consequently every polynomial difference is nonzero and its boundary logarithm is integrable.
On the circle the two summands in \(F_j\) have the common factor \(b_a^j/g^j\) relative to those in \(V_j\).
Reversal preserves measure and \(m(f)=\log\theta\), giving (2) and the upper error \((\log 2)/j\).

Since \(g(0)=1\) and \(f(0)=\pm1\), the Taylor coefficients \(c_0,c_1\) have the claimed integrality and \(c_0=\pm1\).
If \(c_1=0\), the map \((c_0b_a)^{-1}\circ B\), with the inverse taken as a disk automorphism, fixes zero with derivative one.
Schwarz's lemma would then force \(B=c_0b_a\), contrary to the pole of \(f/g\).
For varying seeds this gives \(\lvert B'(0)-c_0 b_a'(0)\rvert=a\lvert c_1\rvert\ge1/\Theta\).

The extraction in the disk uses boundedness of \(B_s\), compactness of the interval for \(a_s\), the two signs, the two constants \(c_{0,s}\), and the integrality and lower bound of the offsets.
At a fixed nonzero offset the two limit functions have different moduli at zero, since one vanishes there and the other has modulus \(a>0\).
At offsets tending to positive infinity the factor \(z^{k_s}\) tends to zero uniformly on compact subsets, leaving \(C=\sigma b_a\).
At zero offset matching moduli on a disk would imply \(B=c_0b_a\) by constancy of the quotient of modulus one and evaluation at zero.
Locally uniform convergence implies convergence of first derivatives there, contradicting the derivative separation just obtained.
In each case equality of moduli on an open set would imply a constant unit quotient there and hence equality of moduli throughout by analytic continuation.
This establishes a dense set of points with unequal limiting moduli.

Suppose a sequence lost at least \(\epsilon j_s\) in the boundary logarithmic integral.
The measures \(\nu_s\) are positive by \(|F_{j_s}|\le2\), and their boundary logarithmic singularities are integrable even if a zero lies on the circle.
The subharmonic Poisson bound follows by first using \(\log(|F_{j_s}|+\delta)\) and then passing downwards to the integrable boundary logarithm.
At any point of unequal limiting moduli the larger modulus has positive limit, and the quotient of the smaller modulus by the larger one is eventually bounded by \(q<1\).
Factoring out the larger term gives the claimed finite limit for \(j_s^{-1}\log|F_{j_s}|\) because the remaining factor has modulus between \(1-q^{j_s}\) and \(1+q^{j_s}\).
At one such point the strictly positive minimum of the Poisson kernel in (3) bounds the total masses from above.
On the compact circle a weak limit therefore exists along a subsequence, preserves the mass lower bound \(\epsilon\), and gives the limit of each Poisson integral.
Inequality (3) on the dense set bounds \(|C|\) by the exponential of the negative Poisson integral of that limit.
Both sides are continuous, so the inequality extends to the disk.
For \(r\) near one the finite Blaschke product \(C\) is zero-free on \(|z|=r\) and its log modulus tends uniformly to zero as \(r\) increases to one.
The integral of the Poisson kernel in the angular variable equals one, so the circle mean of \(\log|C|\) is at most \(-\nu(\mathbb T)\le-\epsilon\).
The contradiction yields the claimed uniform lower estimate.

For each \(k\ge1\), \(|A(-1/2)|\le1/2\) and \(|C(-1/2)|=b=(2a+1)/(a+2)\).
At this point \(P_z\ge1/3\), so its integral against \(\log|F_j|\) is at most one third of the unweighted log mean plus \((2/3)\log2\).
The reverse triangle inequality bounds \(|F_j(-1/2)|\) below by \(b^j(1-(2b)^{-j})\).
These two inequalities give the displayed lower bound, including its constants.
Multiplying \(b^3-a\) by \((a+2)^3\) gives \((1-a)^3(1+a)>0\).
Compactness inside \(0<a<1\) makes the slope \(\log(b^3/a)\) uniformly positive and keeps \((2b)^{-1}\) bounded strictly below one.

For a fixed seed, Rouché's theorem at \(t<1\) counts one exterior zero with multiplicity.
Two exterior zeros at \(t=1\) would persist near that value, whereas fixed real points on either side of the simple Pisot root give opposite signs for large \(N\).
The reciprocal of \(D_N\) is \(-\sigma D_N\), and the unit constant term forces the irreducible factor of the exterior root to contain its inverse.
Kronecker's theorem then applies to every remaining factor.
For \(G=g/f\) the preceding first-derivative argument gives \(G(0)\ne0\) and \(G'(0)=-c_1/c_0^2\ne0\).
In the three torsion conjugacy cases the rational identities have unequal constant or linear terms as stated, leaving finitely many possible roots for a fixed seed.
At a repeated torsion root \(N=\zeta G'(\zeta)/G(\zeta)\), excluding repetition for large \(N\).
Thus \(H_N\) ranges over a finite set and \(k=\deg H_N-\deg f\) does also.

At each \(w^j=1\), the substitution \(y^d=w\) gives the displayed phase polynomial, including its monomial when \(k<0\).
On this grid its zeros occur exactly at roots of \(H_N\), since \(S_N\) contains an exterior root and is irreducible.
At an exceptional node \(\eta\), the derivative of \(D_N\) equals \(\sigma g(\eta)(N/\eta+f'(\eta)/f(\eta)-g'(\eta)/g(\eta))\).
The finite list of simple factors \(H_N\) has nonzero derivatives there, giving \(|S_N(\eta)|\asymp_f d\).
In the remaining grid product the removed nodes contribute only fixed nonzero differences and powers of \(d\), by differentiating \(z^d-w\) at a removed root.
Exterior phase roots contribute their logarithmic moduli times \(d\), interior roots have bounded error, and torsion roots off the grid take finitely many nonzero distances.
For a fixed nontorsion algebraic phase root \(r\) of modulus one, \(|r^{jd}-1|\le j|r^d-w|\) gives the required polynomial lower bound from the cited logarithmic-form specialization.
These estimates include repeated phase roots with their multiplicities and the actual leading coefficient of each phase polynomial.
Multiplying over \(w^j=1\) gives \(V_j\) up to sign and proves the \(O_{f,j}(\log d)\) error for both signs.
The logarithmic-form theorem was used at the statement and applicability level.

Uniform convergence and \(\log\theta_0>0\) permit one fixed \(j\) with \(m(V_j)>2\log2\) throughout the prescribed range.
For each fixed seed the transfer error divided by \(d\) tends to zero, giving an exponential margin over \(2^d\) along all sufficiently large allowed delays.
The constants in the torsion and phase-root estimates depend on the seed, and the transfer uses this order of quantifiers.
The proof of the lower estimate uses the offset bound to keep \(C\) a finite Blaschke product in the limit.
