## Coefficient growth for all integer exponents and the width-five minimum

At 2026-11-18T22:23 UTC an agent outside the development of C345 v411 independently reviewed the submitted decision and all five mathematical sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer previously checked the two-seed classification and the width-five endpoint argument.
The bound holds for every fixed nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) and fixed positive multiplicity \(m\), uniformly over the supplied integer widths \(b\ge 2\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) and rows satisfying the value hypothesis.
The full width-five exclusion holds at every sufficiently large odd prime.

### Divisibility at a finite place

Nonvanishing of \(V(x_1^p)\) propagates to every conjugate.
The equation \(x_i=-U(z_i)/V(z_i)\) proves field equality and precludes coincident \(z_i\) for distinct \(x_i\).
If the rows are coprime, their simultaneous vanishing at one algebraic point would give a common irreducible divisor, so the value hypothesis follows.
The unit property gives \(z_j^{-1}\) integral, including at the primes above \(p\).

In \(\binom{1/p}{k}\), the denominator before cancellation divides \(p^kk!\), which divides \(A=p^{m-1}(m-1)!\) for \(0\le k<m\).
The binomial series truncated modulo \(T^m\) satisfies \(B(T)^p=1+T\) there.
Substitution \(X\mapsto x_j B((Y-z_j)/z_j)\) sends \(X-x_j\) into the ideal generated by \(Y-z_j\) and sends \(X^p\) to \(Y\) in the truncated ring.
It takes the zero class of the graph modulo \((X-x_j)^m\) to the required zero class modulo \((Y-z_j)^m\).
After multiplying by \(A\), monic division takes place over the algebraic integers.
At \(z_i\) the evaluated numerator equals \(A V(z_i)(x_j B((z_i-z_j)/z_j)-x_i)\), with valuation at least \(mr_{ij}\).
Writing \(a=\nu A\) and \(c=c_{ij}\), each nonconstant term of \(B\) has valuation at least \(k r_{ij}-a\).
When \(r_{ij}>a+c\), the last factor in the numerator consequently has valuation exactly \(c\), giving \(\nu V(z_i)\ge mr_{ij}-a-c\).
When \(r_{ij}\le a+c\), the right side of (2) is nonpositive.
This covers equality at the threshold, \(m=1\) with \(B=1\), and all residue characteristics.

### From one local maximum to the discriminant

At a fixed \(i\), every \(r_{ij}\) except a selected maximum occurs as its minimum with that maximum.
Replacing the maximum by the full sum thus costs at most the sum of pairwise minima.
After summation with weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/n\), the denominator costs \(m\log A=O_m(\log(2p))\).
The cost of the varying maximizing choice of \(c_{ij}\) is bounded by the sum over all \(j\ne i\) of the fixed normalized log norms of the nonzero integral differences \(x_i-x_j\), times \(m\).

For a triple containing a torsion ratio, the valuation of the common collision is bounded by the valuation of the difference of that pair of powers, by the ultrametric inequality.
After removing a unit this is \(\zeta^p-1\), which ranges over a finite set of nonzero algebraic integers on the admissible exponents.
For a triple with all ratios nontorsion, the real vectors of logarithms of absolute values over the embeddings of \(L\) lie on one Euclidean sphere by Galois permutation.
Equality of two vectors would make the corresponding ratio an algebraic unit with all conjugates on the circle, hence torsion.
A line meets a sphere in at most two points, so the two vector differences from the first point are linearly independent.
They exclude every multiplicative dependence of the two ratios, including a relation up to torsion after raising it to a power.

I checked the places and height convention on p. 3 and Proposition 2 on p. 7 of the [authors' preprint](../literature/CZ05/readme.md#arxiv-v2).
For every positive \(\epsilon\), its exceptional independent pairs are finite in the fixed field with \(S\) the archimedean places.
The ordered pairs of positive powers here are independent and distinct as \(p\) varies.
The normalized finite sum of the two valuations' minima is the finite part of the nonnegative logarithmic sum in that proposition, since \(z_i\) is a unit.
It is eventually at most \(\epsilon p\) times the larger fixed height of the ratios.
This proves the required \(o_P(p)\) for all exponent sequences, with only finitely many triples.
In degree two the sum over triples is empty.

Monicity and the distinctness of the powered roots give \(\nu Q'_p(z_i)=\sum_{j\ne i}r_{ij}\).
The product of these derivatives over the \(e\) embeddings is the signed discriminant.
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(z_i)\) repeats \(n/e\) times in \(L\), giving the factor \(1/e\) in (3).
Multiplication by \(-x_i\) preserves the absolute norm.
For unequal moduli the relative error after extracting the larger modulus from \(z_j-z_i\) is exponentially small.
At equal moduli a torsion ratio supplies one of finitely many nonzero distances.
For a nontorsion ratio the nonzero imaginary logarithmic form has modulus at most \(\pi\) and integer coefficients of size \(O(p)\).
I checked the fixed determinations, integer coefficients and height dependence on pp. 5–6 of the [publisher's Wüstholz chapter](../literature/Wus02/readme.md#cambridge-2002).
Its bound gives \(\log|\Lambda|\ge-C_P\log(2p)\), and the chord has length at least \(2|\Lambda|/\pi\).
Finally \(2\max(\ell_i,\ell_j)=\ell_i+\ell_j+|\ell_i-\ell_j|\), whose linear terms sum to zero by the unit property.
This checks the discriminant coefficient \(D(P)\) and the fixed-seed errors in (3).
The two external theorems were checked at statement and application scope.

### The archimedean estimate for a varying width

The top exponents \(p(b-k)+s\), \(k<t,\ s=0,1\), are distinct for \(p\ge2\), including when corresponding coefficients are zero.
An Euler polynomial of degree \(2t-1<m\) applied to the graph vanishes at its root \(x\), since every term involves a derivative of order less than \(m\).
Isolating one exponent gives an integral multiplier of absolute value at least one.
On a lower term at level \(l\ge t\), each difference of exponents has size at most \(p(l+1)+1\), independently of \(b\).
For \(|z|\ge2\), the resulting polynomial in \(l+1\) is uniformly bounded after multiplication by \(|z|^{t-l}\).
The sum over all lower coefficients is at most \(2H\), and the optional factor \(x\) is fixed.
This yields the upper bound on each isolated term and then on either whole row.
The case \(t=0\) uses the ordinary one-norm bound.
On reversing about \(pb+1\), the graph has the order-\(m\) root \(x^{-1}\) and its rows are \(V_b^*,U_b^*\), even with leading or trailing zeros.
Returning from \(|z|^{-1}>1\) multiplies the row bounds by \(|z|^b\), leaving \(|z|^t\).
The argument works for nonreciprocal \(P\) because reversal only requires the reciprocal of the chosen root in the reversed graph.
For fixed \(P\), the exterior moduli raised to \(p\), including those used after reversal, are eventually all at least two.
The archimedean logarithms then sum with exponents \(b-t\) outside and \(t\) inside, and the circle contributes only the bound \(H\).
This gives \((b-2t)ph/e\), with an \(O_{P,m}(\log(2p))\) loss independent of the width.
Combining both norm estimates proves (1), even when \(b\) varies with \(p\) and when the resulting lower bound is nonpositive.

### Salem rates and the prime minimum

Counting the two nonzero Salem log moduli against the \(e-2\) zeros and against each other gives \(D(P)=2(e-1)\log\rho\).
For a reciprocal unit with \(g\) exterior roots, opposite signs contribute \(2gh\) and pairs with a zero contribute \(2(e-2g)h\).
Discarding the remaining nonnegative contributions proves the asserted lower bound.
For a width-five candidate the nonzero reflected determinant has, after multiplication by \(Y^b\), degree at most \(2b\) and contains the square of the degree-\(e\) powered minimum.
Thus \(e\le b\), while the circle bound on that determinant gives \(M(P)\le 2\) in the coefficient box.
The even-degree restriction and the two possible trace quadratics in L462 v242 leave only the two listed quartics up to \(X\mapsto-X\).
For \(m=2,e=4\), (1) gives \((14-b)\log\rho/4\).
At \(b=8\) this exceeds \(\log2\) by \(2^{2/3}<8/5\) and the two exterior traces exceeding \(8/5+5/8\).
For the second root at \(b=9\), \(1+\sqrt2>65/28=7/4+4/7\) gives \(\rho>7/4\) and \(7^5>16\cdot4^5\) gives \((7/4)^{5/4}>2\).
The change of seed sign preserves the log moduli and the coefficient box at odd primes.
Consequently a maximum over the two fixed-seed thresholds suffices at all widths through five, and only the initial integer \(2^p\) can give the minimum.
At larger widths the exclusions concern the indicated quartics individually.
The estimate gives neither a numerical onset nor a uniform positive gap as \(P\) varies.
Indeed \(D(P)\le(e-1)\sum_i|\ell_i|=2(e-1)h\), so a putative sequence with \(h\to0\) would also have \(D(P)/e\to0\).
