## Both projections preserve the multiplicity required for the height bound

The submitted proof establishes the cutoff 390 and the classification of all the specified centers.
For any nonzero direction in characteristic zero, even on an axis, two corner monomials of \(F\) have distinct weights.
Divisibility of \(DF\) by the geometrically irreducible \(F\) would force a scalar quotient by the separate degree bounds, in contradiction to those weights.
The closure of \(F=0\) contains no boundary component, so its intersection with the padded form of \(E\) remains proper even when the latter has boundary factors.
The intersection number is \(4\cdot2+2\cdot4=16\), bounding the number of distinct affine conjugates in a rational direction, including special fibers.
A zero of the Laurent section is multiple exactly when the logarithmic derivative vanishes there, and the nonconstant parametrization cannot lie in this finite intersection.

For either coordinate \(z\), let \(d\) be the degree in the other variable, respectively 2 or 4.
Over the rational function field the leading coefficient of \(F\) is nonzero, so padding \(E\) to degree \(d\) cannot introduce a common projective root at infinity.
The specialized \(F(z,W)\) cannot vanish identically because that would give a line component.
Since \(K=\mathbb Q(z,W)\) is separable over \(\mathbb Q(z)\), its \(e\) embeddings over \(z\) give \(e\le d\) distinct values of \(W\).
Evaluation vectors at these values lie in the right kernel of the specialized Sylvester matrix and contain an invertible \(e\)-row Vandermonde minor.
The left kernel also has dimension at least \(e\), so an invertible constant row transformation gives \(e\) rows vanishing at \(z\).
This proves divisibility of the determinant by \((Z-z)^e\), regardless of coalescence elsewhere or loss of leading degree at \(z\).
Conjugacy and primitivity give the asserted divisibility in \(\mathbb Z[Z]\).
Hadamard's inequality uses exactly \(d\) rows of each type on the unit circle, giving exponent \(d\) on the product of the two coefficient norms, with \(\|F\|_1=53\) and \(\|E\|_1\le159N\).

The two Bézout identities hold for negative and zero exponents as identities in the torus.
Torsion factors preserve the absolute height, so for the coordinate corresponding to the largest absolute exponent one obtains \([K:\mathbb Q]h(z)=N[K:\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\log M(\gamma)\).
The subfield index is at least one and the primitive minimal polynomial has degree at most sixteen.
Kronecker's theorem excludes measure one since \(\gamma\) is nonzero and nontorsion, and the first assertion of the Mossinghoff–Rhin–Wu theorem then gives the endpoint-inclusive lower bound \(M(\gamma)\ge\rho\), also for nonmonic polynomials.
I checked by integer arithmetic the Lehmer polynomial value after scaling at \(7/6\), namely \(-13481789\), the strict reversed inequality at 390, and \(7\cdot390^4>6\cdot391^4\).
Since \((1+1/N)^4\) decreases with \(N\), this last comparison gives strict increase of the ratio from 390 onward.

At every integral root of an ordinary prepared polynomial its restricted-series unit is nonzero, so the actual logarithmic critical value vanishes at the corresponding slope.
The integral inverse series converges there because the slope change has valuation at least one, even in a ramified coefficient field and in the degree-three preparation possible at 3.

In the deep cubic preparation the integral parameter satisfies \(v_p(b-\beta)\ge1/2\), giving convergence of the integral curve and slope expansions and of both logarithms at the resulting principal units.

For a direction algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\), proper intersection makes both coordinates algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\) inside \(\mathbb C_p\).

A zero logarithm vector would force both units, and hence the original point, to be torsion.
The Fuchs–Pham theorem thus applies to a nonzero vector on an algebraic line, and the identity component of its positive-dimensional subgroup has precisely this tangent line.
A one-dimensional subtorus is annihilated by a primitive integral character, so the line is rational, including when one logarithm vanishes.
Exponentiating the integral relation is unnecessary because the character value has zero logarithm and belongs to its torsion kernel.
Consequently the same height cutoff applies.
