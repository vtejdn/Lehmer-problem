Both imply torsion off center, including negative exponents of \(\alpha\), and both apply even if leading terms of \(G\) cancel.
At the degree-one conductors the central equality cannot occur.
In particular \(k=1,a=-1\) gives \(-1\) or \(1\) at \(n=1,2\), and a vanishing constant term can leave the factor \(X\) of measure one.
The shifted polynomial itself is never identically zero because \(\Phi_n\) is nonconstant with nonzero constant term.
Each irreducible reciprocal divisor has nonzero constant, hence its roots are torsion by the inverse-root equations, even for self-inverse linear roots.
For an integer divisor, nonunit content or a primitive irreducible factor with leading coefficient of modulus at least two gives measure at least two.
Otherwise a positive-measure irreducible factor is monic up to sign and gives a nonzero nonreciprocal algebraic integer.

These arguments include the empty root list for \(Q=1\) and the full list for \(F=1\).

If \(eT_a/d\to0\), then eventually \(e<d\log 2/(8T_a)\), ruling out every noncyclotomic factor of \(Q\).
This proves exact equality of the two total measures, including bounded \(|a|\) under condition (2).
