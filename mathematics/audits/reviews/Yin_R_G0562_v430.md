# Review of the cofactor bound for constant trace values

## Adjacent classes, exact symmetry and the integral pencil

On 2026-11-18T16:11 a separately assigned agent for Yin reviewed the [three sections of L462 v192](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v192.md#cost) and their supporting inputs at the scopes used.
The reviewer took no part in developing the proof and received the fixed text and scope without development discussion or earlier findings about this material.
The finite estimates, the symmetry obstruction, the assertions for every admissible pencil member and the two stated asymptotic conclusions hold under their respective hypotheses.

The [odd-span coordinates](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd) give \(v=-b\) when \(u=b\in\mathbb Z\) and \(D=0\), because the reduced trace representative is constant.
If \(b=0\), \(A(\beta)=0\), so the reciprocal minimal factor \(Q\) divides both rows, contrary to coprimality.
The congruence \(J\equiv e\bmod p\) and the parity of \(J-e\) make \(\ell\) a nonnegative integer.
Writing \(W=(2pEA-JA)(\beta)\) gives \(2pEB(\beta)=\beta^{-\ell}W+eB(\beta)\).
For \(e=1\) the value of \(B\) is \(b(1-\sigma\alpha)\) and \(pEB=-\sigma b\alpha\) at \(\beta\).
For \(e=-1\) the value of \(B\) is \(b(\alpha^{-1}-\sigma)\) and \(pEB=-b\alpha^{-1}\) there.
Thus \(T(\beta)\) equals \(b\) or \(-\sigma b\), respectively, and inversion of \(\alpha\) fixes this rational value.

Clearing powers of \(X\) from the nonzero difference \(T-T^\iota\) and dividing by the monic \(Q\) leaves an integral polynomial quotient.
Nonnegativity of its logarithmic measure gives \(ph\le m(T-T^\iota)\).
On the circle inversion preserves the magnitude of the real Laurent polynomial \(T\), so the difference has measure at most \(m(T)+\log2\).
Writing \(EB/B=-\ell+XA'/A\) gives the stated root-sum bound for \(T/B\).
The positive quantity \(1+p(\ell+1)\) bounds both \(1+p\ell\) and the coefficient \(p\) of that sum.
Subadditivity of the square root and Jensen's inequality bound the remaining logarithmic integral by \(2\log(1+C_0 n)\), including the integrable singularities at circle roots.
This yields the stated \(L\).
The [coprime-row diagonal comparison](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) applies since \((S,p)=1\), the span is \(J\) and the row integral is \(m(A)\).
Primitivity of \(F\) and monicity of \(P\) give \(m(R_F)=m(F)-2h\), including any extra copies of \(P\) in the cofactor.
Since \(1-4/p>0\), combining the two inequalities gives (1).

All row exponents retain their coefficients up to the nonzero multiplier \(1-ep(j-\ell)\).
Symmetry of their support forces \(-\ell=-(n-\ell)\), so \(n=2\ell\) and \(S=e=1\).
For this possibility, \(T=T^\iota\) gives \(B-B^\iota=pE(B+B^\iota)\), hence \(B=C+pEC\) and \(T=C-p^2E^2C\).
The two conjugate row values give \(2C(\beta)=2b-\sigma bq\), whereas \(T(\beta)=b\), proving (2) with the stated sign.
The coefficients of \(2C\) are integral and \(\beta\) is a unit, so \(bq\in p^2\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
The trace field has degree \(d/2\), and \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]=\mathbb Z[q]\oplus\alpha\mathbb Z[q]\) by the quadratic equation and inverse pairing.
For \(d\ge4\), \(q\) occurs in the power basis of the first summand, so the \(q\)-coefficient \(b\) of \(bq\) must be divisible by \(p^2\).

For the [integral pencil](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#vanishing), \(b\) is the additive order of \(x\) modulo the powered suborder on two multiplicity layers.
The local result for a good prime applies at multiplicity two and exponent one, so its \(p\)-part is \(p^{1+v_p(1!)}=p\).
One can also see the exact valuation from the first-order power index \(I\).
Squarefreeness of \(\bar P\) makes Frobenius onto \(\mathbb F_p[x]/(\bar P)\), giving \(p\nmid I\).
The [integral representation modulo \(P^2\)](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#denominator) then gives \(b\mid p s^3\), with the additive exponent \(s\) of the index quotient prime to \(p\).
Differentiating a representation of \(bx\) modulo \(P^2\) and reducing modulo \(p\) gives \(\bar P\mid b\), so \(p\mid b\).
This confirms \(v_p(b)=1\) and hence excludes symmetry for all admissible members at good primes when \(d\ge4\).

At any degree-preserving prime the two extreme coefficients of \(T\) in the pencil agree only if \(2pd M=-(1+pd)T_0(0)\), a condition with at most one integer solution.
For the large-\(|M|\) assertion the constant and leading coefficients of \(F_M/M\) tend to \(1\) and \(\sigma\), respectively, and the limit has the same degree \(2pd+1\).
Its logarithmic measure is \(2ph\), since substitution in \(Q\) preserves \(m(Q)\), \(Q\) is squared and \(1+\sigma x\) has logarithmic measure zero.
Continuity in this fixed degree and the exact scalar \(|M|\) give \(m(F_M)=\log|M|+2ph+o(1)\) for either sign of \(M\).
The minimal choice of \(b\) in the pencil guarantees primitivity, so subtracting \(2h\) proves the precise claimed constant.

Finally \(n\le J/p\) and \(p\ell=(J-e)/2\), giving \(L=O(\log(2+J)+\log p)\) uniformly.
In the fourth-logarithmic prime window with the indicated cap, these logarithms are \(O(\log(3d))=o(ph)\) and \(p\to\infty\).
Dividing (1) by \(ph\) gives the uniform \(1-o(1)\) lower bound in all its stated cases.
The pencil limit fixes \(P,p,\sigma,T_0\), and (1) leaves the potentially symmetric exceptional member at a bad prime outside its coverage.
