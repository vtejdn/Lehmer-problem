### Partial fractions and the strict pair-pole inequality

For every permitted factorization Q=FZ, the previously proved strict signs give Z(b_j)>0 at all original poles, including -1.
The rational function F/B is proper, with degree difference one and simple denominator poles.
Its residue at b_j is F(b_j)/B'(b_j)=r_j/Z(b_j), which proves the stated partial-fraction formula.
This remains true if F or Z has repeated roots, since B's simple roots are not roots of Q.

Expanding the monic division at infinity gives the first three coefficients
\[
 1,\qquad 2+2s,\qquad 3+4s+3s^2+d.
\]
For example, the reciprocal of 1-2s/t+(s^2-d)/t^2 begins with
1+2s/t+(3s^2+d)/t^2, which gives those coefficients when multiplied by the expansion of Q/B.
No higher residue moment enters this step.

The sign of alpha_j=r_j/Z(b_j) is the original residue sign, so beta_j=(b_j-y)alpha_j>0.
The positive weight 2-b_j then gives
\[
 \sum_j(2-b_j)\beta_j
 =-\sum_j b_j^2\alpha_j+(2+y)\sum_j b_j\alpha_j-2y\sum_j\alpha_j
 =1-3s^2-d+2ys.
\]
At -1, the residue is r_j=3h(-1)/p.
Its contribution is exactly 9(-1-y)h(-1)/(pZ(-1)).
Every other contribution is strictly positive.
Substituting h(-1)=32 and multiplying by Z(-1)=(s+1)^2-d>0 gives the exact inequality (2), including its factor nine and strict direction.

This argument proves (2) for both real and nonreal residual quadratics and every allowed root selection.
The later cover uses only its restriction to the nonreal positive-shell rectangle.
Points with Z(-1)=0 are already impossible for an original input; no division at such a parameter is used to justify an original configuration.
