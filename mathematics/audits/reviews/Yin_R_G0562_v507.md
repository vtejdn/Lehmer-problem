## Outcome of the proof review

The algorithm returns the exact \(M_p(b)\) in \(f(b)(p+1)^4\) classical bit operations for a computable \(f\).
The same bound covers recovery of a genuine witness when one attains the terminal cost.

**Threshold problem.**
The reflected determinant has Laurent span at most \(2b\) and contains \(Q^2\) after a monomial shift, giving \(e\le b\).
The individual lower bound gives \(m(P)\le\log 2\), hence each coefficient bound used in enumeration.
The monic proper-factor bound applies to all enumerated candidates since a monic factor has Mahler measure at most that of its monic product.
The prime-power formula for \(\varphi\) gives \(\varphi(n)^2\ge n/2\), so the finite order check detects all cyclotomic candidates of degree at most \(b\).
Seed preprocessing thus has a computable cost depending only on \(b\).
Divisibility modulo the monic \(P^2\) yields the requisite integer linear system in the row coefficients.
The sign inequalities enforce the two separate one-norm bounds, and the leading terms, fixed-degree resultant and sum of squares enforce exactly the remaining conditions, including the constant-row cases.
The polynomial used for avoidance has degree at most \(u+v+6\) regardless of \(p\).

**Search in the lattice.**
Active inequalities span the ambient dual at each vertex of a nonempty bounded polytope, also in lower dimension.
Consequently enumerating the indicated intersections detects emptiness and recovers the affine hull.
The unimodular gcd steps parametrize every integral point in that hull or demonstrate inconsistency.
For a full-dimensional polytope, replacement of one vertex in a maximal simplex shows \(|y_i|\le1\) at every transformed vertex.
The transformed standard simplex contains the cube used in the argument because the sum of the upper coordinate bounds is less than one and the lower bounds are positive.
In the small-\(G\) branch the displacement in sup norm of every grid point from the center is at most \(k(\delta+1/2)2^kG\le\rho\).
The invertible affine substitution preserves degree, so vanishing on the full \((\delta+1)^k\) grid forces identical vanishing on this slice.
In the other branch, projection onto \(w_k^*\) divides the diameter bound by \(G\), giving a parameter-bounded number of integer layers.
The translation cancels because \(v_0+B\tau=0\), and \(Bw_1,\ldots,Bw_k\) generate exactly \(\mathbb Z^k\).
Thus fixing the last integer coordinate enumerates all admissible slices and taking their preimages recovers an original witness.

**Bit operations.**
In bounded dimension, clearing denominators, determinants, inverses, unimodular two-column changes and polynomial substitutions multiply bit lengths only by parameter-dependent constants.
The number of these transformations on any branch is parameter-bounded, with Euclidean iterations charged separately.
I inspected inequality (1.7) on PDF page 3 and Proposition (1.26) and its following remark on PDF page 8 of LLL82.
For rank \(k\) the latter gives \(O(k^4\log B_0)\) integer operations on \(O(k\log B_0)\)-bit integers and the stated classical \(O(k^6(\log B_0)^3)\) bit cost, where \(B_0\ge2\) bounds the squared lengths of the input basis.
After scaling \(B^{-1}\), this input is a full-rank integer basis with \(\log B_0=O_{k,m,\delta}(L+1)\).
Rescaling preserves inequality (1.7), which bounds each \(\|w_i\|\) by \(2^{(k-1)/2}G\), sufficient for the grid estimate.
Classical extended Euclid also costs at most cubic time in the linear operand size.
Branch count and depth depend only on \(k,m,\delta\), so their cost multiplies the cubic bound by a computable parameter function and does not enter its exponent. [LLL82, basis reduction and complexity](../literature/LLL82/Yin_U001.md#complexity)

For each seed, the matrix defining multiplication by \(X\bmod P^2\) depends only on \(b\) and the preprocessed candidate.
At most \(pb+1\) products suffice and their coefficients have \(O_b(p+1)\) bits.
Thus forming the threshold data costs at most \(O_b((p+1)^3)\), and each full threshold search has the same dependence on \(p\).
Binary search needs at most \(p+1\) queries, followed by at most one search for an actual witness at the answer.
The resulting exponent four is independent of \(b\) and concerns the numerical prime \(p\).
No implementation or practical running-time estimate was examined.

For historical attribution, Lenstra's 1983 treatment identifies the maximal-simplex normalization and dimension-bounded hyperplane recursion.
