# A bounded progression of exponents and one outlier on reciprocal conjugates

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:00 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the submitted [C345 v745](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v745.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing its argument, received the fixed text without its development discussion or a prior verdict, and had already reviewed its earlier three-term input.
The inspection covered all support degeneracies, the graph incidence estimate for complex coefficients and the deduction for small values.

A common translation of all the exponents multiplies every monomial row by a nonzero scalar, preserving the hyperplane incidences among the original embeddings.
On the true support the hyperplane functional takes the form \(A(z^a)+cz^b\), with \(\deg A\le T\), after absorbing any outlier that is itself in the displayed progression.
A proper hyperplane gives a nonzero functional in the distinct monomial coordinates.
When \(c=0\), at most \(T\) values of \(z^a\) vanish, of mass at most \(1/e_*\) each.
When \(c\ne0\) there are no zeros if \(A=0\), and a single term of \(A\) imposes one value of a nonzero power of \(z\).
If its exponent difference is negative, taking reciprocals gives the same mass bound.

For at least two terms of \(A\), the projective incidence question reduces to graph membership of \((z^a,z^b)\).
The coordinate field is generated by \(z^{\gcd(a,|b|)}\) even when \(b<0\), so the orbit has size \(D\ge e_*\) and is invariant under inversion.
In the splitting field of \(\alpha\), interpolation of \(P\) at the selected points and imposition of the zero coefficients give a consistent affine linear system, because its rank is unchanged after extending scalars to \(\mathbb C\).
The complex solution has every specified nonzero coefficient, so its affine solution space over the splitting field lies in none of the forbidden coordinate hyperplanes.
An infinite field has rational points outside any finite union of such proper affine subspaces.
Thus an algebraic graph can retain every selected orbit point and every prescribed nonzero coefficient.
Writing \(M'\) for the actual occupancy of that graph allows additional points.
Each Galois conjugate graph intersects its inverse graph only above the zeros of \(x^T(P^\sigma(x)P(1/x)-1)\), a polynomial of degree at most \(2T\).
The polynomial is nonzero because a Laurent polynomial with at least two terms has no Laurent polynomial inverse.
For each such nonzero \(x\) the image graph specifies exactly one \(y\).
The inverse graph contains exactly \(M'\) orbit points, so Galois transitivity gives mean intersection \((M')^2/D\), bounded by \(2T\).
This proves the occupancy estimate after combining with the single-power cases and the absolute bound one.

After translation to minimum zero the row coordinates are algebraic integers, the norms are at least one and at most \(\sqrt r\max(1,|z|)^W\).
Every ordered tuple has algebraic-integer determinant, and the Galois-invariant product over the nonzero determinants is a nonzero rational integer.
Thus summing the normalized logarithmic determinant costs charges each row norm at most \(r d^{r-1}\) times.
The total is at most \(r d^r(\log\sqrt r+Wh)\), also when there are no such tuples.
A partial independent row set of size less than \(r\) lies in a proper complex hyperplane, so upon drawing the next small-value row the probability lost to linear dependence is at most \(L\).
The probability of a full independent tuple is at least \(\delta_t(\delta_t-L)^{r-1}\) for \(\delta_t>2L\).
After unitary change of coordinates the small normalized coefficient functional is its last coordinate and complementary minors have modulus at most one by Hadamard's bound.
Every selected nonzero tuple costs at least \(\log(1/(rt))\), giving the coefficient and exponent in (1).
The exact-zero set itself lies on one hyperplane.
For the asymptotic, \(r\le T+2\) is bounded, the [norm descent](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v739.md#volume) forces \(e_*\to\infty\), and [Smyth's bound](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) supplies eventual reciprocity under the stated integral hypothesis.
