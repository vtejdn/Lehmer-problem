# Residual periods and exterior counts for individual factors of primitive pole sections

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:37 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the submitted [C345 v755](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v755.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing this proof and received its fixed text without its development discussion or a prior verdict.
The review covered the period estimates, the exact thresholds for rational prime poles, the simultaneous construction and the claimed count for every individual noncyclotomic factor.

The projected polynomial over the specified pole-field prime, with all multiplicities retained, divides \(X^M-1\) after removal of the available short factor in the unit-residue case.
Its least positive period \(L\) divides \(M\), since \(X\) is a unit in the residue quotient ring and its order divides every available exponent.
The constant divisor has \(L=1\) by convention.
A product of \(X^L-1\) and the one short binomial when needed therefore pays the [fixed local cost](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v753.md#norm) \(\log q/[E:\mathbb Q]\).
The [cyclic norm envelope](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) makes the short cost vanish and bounds the other cost uniformly by a function tending to zero with \(2C L/M\).
Hence \(M/L\le A\) for sufficiently large indices with the claimed dependence, without any restriction on \(\omega(M)\).
The period is the least common multiple of the full periods \(r p^{\lceil\log_p k\rceil}\) of the relative irreducible powers.
A prime \(\ell>A\) different from \(p\) therefore occurs to its entire exponent from \(M\) in some \(r\).
The Frobenius orbit gives \(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell^b}(q)\mid \operatorname{ord}_r(q)\le D\), with \(D\) the relative degree over the pole field.
The largest characteristic part is at most \(pD\) in \(L\), since the multiplicity of each irreducible is at most \(D\).
Multiplication by \(M/L\) gives the claimed bound for that prime in \(M\).

At \(B=p\) the monic integral polynomial contains the minimal polynomial as an integer factor, and its normalized mod-\(p\) equation is \(X^N-\epsilon\).
If the minimal polynomial divides \(X^n-\delta\) after reduction, the nonzero norm of \(\beta^n-\delta\) is divisible by \(p^{[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]}\).
At infinity the cost of the triangle inequality is at most \(\log2+n h(\beta)\) after normalization.
The [long-power height limit](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement) gives \(Nh(\beta)\to\log p\).
For sign plus, \(\log2+(\log p)/2<\log p\) exactly when \(p>4\), while \(\log2+(\log3)/3<\log3\) excludes a period index of at least three at three.
For sign minus in odd characteristic, \(-1\) has order two in the nonzero residue quotient ring, including in the presence of nilpotents.
It is the unique order-two element of the cyclic group generated by \(X\), so the period \(2n\) has \(X^n=-1\) and \(N/n\) odd.
The same norm inequality excludes every such quotient greater than one when \(p\ge3\).

The two specified congruences for \(u_j\) are compatible by the disjoint prime supports and allow arbitrarily large positive representatives.
They give coprimality with \(N_j=S_j\ell_j^{b_j}\), while \(b_j\) being a multiple of \(\operatorname{ord}_{T_j}(\ell_j)\) implies \(N_j\equiv S_j\equiv u_j\bmod T_j\).
Thus \(T_j\mid v_j\), \(S_j\mid u_j+v_j\), and \(\gcd(u_j,v_j)=1\).
The lower bound on \(u_j\) gives \(\ell_j^{b_j-w_j}\ge jN_j/u_j\).
The [odd prime-power order calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v753.md#periods) gives this lower bound on the multiplicative order in (4) even when the initial valuation \(w_j\) exceeds one.
The freely large \(b_j\) gives the stated slope growth, and the two long indices each contain at least \(j\) distinct primes.

For any root \(z\) of the constructed polynomial, monicity and the unit constant term give an algebraic unit.
Its \(u_j\)-th power cannot equal two, because the numerator \(2X-1\) is nonzero at two.
The fractional linear height upper bound follows by taking the maximum for \(2X-1,X-2\) and using the product formula for the denominator, including the sign.
Hence \((v_j-u_j)h(z)\le\log3\) for all the roots of either polynomial, so \((u_j+v_j)h(z)=O(1)\) uniformly.
Every minimal polynomial divides \(X^{N_j}-1\) after reduction at two, since it is a monic integral factor.
If its degree were smaller than \(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell_j^{b_j}}(2)\), every residue root order would divide \(N_j/\ell_j\), and removing one factor \(\ell_j\) would preserve all characteristic multiplicities.
Thus \(z^{N_j/\ell_j}-1\) would lie in \(2\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(z)}\).
For a nontorsion \(z\) its normalized finite-place log cost is then at least \(\log2\), whereas the uniform height of \(z^{N_j/\ell_j}\) tends to zero because \(\ell_j>j\).
The cyclic norm envelope contradicts that cost simultaneously for all nontorsion roots.

The [fixed plane-curve lower height bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) applies to each root, since the bilinear graph of the selected sign is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), its four support points have difference rank two, and the exponent direction is primitive.
Taking the smaller constant over the two graphs gives \(h(z)\gg1/v_j\) independently of the factor.
Multiplication by the residue degree lower bound in (4) gives \(m(z)\gg j N_j/(u_j v_j)\).
The root equation bounds every exterior root \(y\) by \(|y|^{u_j}\le5\), so its contribution to a monic factor's log measure is at most \(\log5/u_j\).
Their number in that factor is consequently \(\gg j N_j/v_j\ge c j\).
The [winding and real-root argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#winding) gives a positive exterior root of each full section for slope greater than three, which the construction permits at every index.
The result excludes Salem factors for large \(j\) on actual primitive sections with growing prime supports.
