## Short quotients, angular spacing and unrestricted division

At 2026-11-20T21:55 UTC a reviewer outside proof development independently checked the [two lower bounds](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v663.md#statement) and the arguments supporting them.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and the assignment without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict on this claim.
The earlier review of the unrestricted division estimate was consulted for its input scope.
Both stated lower limits hold for the indicated monic integral polynomials with unit constant term and no root on the unit circle, including reducible polynomials and repeated roots.

For the short estimate, reflection gives an exterior polynomial \(W\) with \(|W|=|P|\), \(W(0)=e^m\) and \(|(W^*/W)(0)|=e^{-2m}\).
In the weighted space of numerators of degree below \(d\), the resulting constant-one minimum is \(C=e^{-2m}/(1-e^{-4m})\).
Varying the minimizing numerator by any complex multiple of a constant-zero polynomial gives the required orthogonality.
The bound by the ordinary coefficient norm divided by \(\max|P|\) gives attainment of the integral minimum \(L\).
Translation preserves both the boundary modulus and the width.

For a translated nonzero short \(T\), each stripping step subtracts a nonzero integral multiple \(q_iP\) and removes the resulting initial power of \(X\).
The leading coefficient of \(P\) ensures that every step produces another nonzero polynomial of degree below \(d\).
Coincident sign-normalized states would give \(P\mid (X^h\pm1)T_i\) with \(h>0\), impossible by coprimality and the degree of \(T_i\).
This coprimality holds with multiplicities.
For \(N=\lfloor C^{1/3}\rfloor\), put \(\delta=\sqrt{(N-1)/C}\) and \(b=\max_{i<N}\|H_{S_i}\|_2\).
The distinct stripped monomials are orthogonal and \(|q_i|\le b\) by evaluation, so \(b\le\sqrt{e_T}+\delta b\) for this arbitrary starting vector.
For a starting minimizer, centering at \(s\) gives squared distances at most \(b^2-1\), since the constants of the normalized states are positive integers.
The sum of squared pair distances then gives \((N-1)l/(2N)\le b^2-1\), proving the cited \(\liminf l\ge2\).

On a subsequence with \(l\to\ell\le2.05\), if \(e_T<7/2\) then \(b^2\le (7/2)(1-\delta)^{-2}<4\) eventually.
All digits are therefore units, and the sign-normalized states have constant one.
The prefix error in the normalized stripping identity is at most \(\delta\), independently of the numerator, degree, gaps and pair of indices.
Each \(H_{S_i}\) has squared norm \(e_T+O(\delta)\).
For their mean \(B\), subtracting the mean squared pairwise dispersion from the mean squared distance to \(s\) gives
\[
 \|B-s\|_2^2\le e_T-1-\frac{N-1}{2N}l+O(\delta).
\]
The mean of the phased copies of \(H_T\) is within \(\delta\) of \(B\) and has modulus at most \(|H_T|\) everywhere.
The positive part of \(|s|-|H_T|\) is bounded by the distance from \(s\) to that phased mean pointwise.
All norms used to square this estimate are uniformly bounded.
For \(e_T\ge7/2\), the squared positive-part norm is at most one, which is already at most \(e_T-1-l/2\) eventually.
This verifies the envelope uniformly over the full short lattice, as required when applying it to pair differences.

Take a minimizing starting vector, so that \(e_T=l\) is eventually below \(7/2\).
The mean \(B\) has norm at least one by constant-one orthogonality.
The ordered distinct pairs thus have mean squared distance at most \(2N(l-1+O(\delta))/(N-1)\).
Their differences are nonzero short integral polynomials and satisfy the envelope after any necessary translation.
Each differs at the level of its normalized quotient from \(H_T(x_i-x_j)\) by at most \(2\delta\) in norm.
The positive-part modulus map is a contraction and both output norms are at most one, so the mean squared positive-part integral is at most \(3(l-2)/2+o(1)\).

At each circle point, the \(N\) cyclic arcs spanning \(k\) consecutive gaps have mean \(2\pi k/N\), since each gap is counted exactly \(k\) times, including zero-length gaps.
Summing over \(1\le k<N\) accounts for every ordered distinct pair once, even where several phases agree.
For \(p,q\ge0\), \(t\mapsto(p-2q\sin(t/2))_+^2\) is convex on \([0,2\pi]\) by composition with the convex nondecreasing squared positive part.
Jensen's inequality may therefore replace the arcs at each \(k\) by their mean.
With \(a=2\sin(\pi k/N)\), the triangle inequality gives an integrated lower bound \((1-a\sqrt l)_+^2\).
Uniform convergence of the resulting continuous integrands as \(l\to\ell\) and \(N\to\infty\) gives the stated Riemann integral.
On the symmetric end intervals of length \(1/\sqrt\ell<\pi\), the inequality \(2\sin(t/2)\le t\) on the first interval and symmetry give the lower bound \(1/(3\pi\sqrt\ell)\).
Consequently \((\ell-2)\sqrt\ell\ge2/(9\pi)\).
The left side is strictly increasing for \(\ell\ge2\).
The root lies between \(2.049\) and \(2.05\), so the subsequence restriction loses no possible smaller lower limit, and \(mC\to1/4\) yields the first constant.

For unrestricted width, the two endpoint expansions in the balanced division give an integral quotient \(Q\) and a nonzero remainder \(R\) of degree below \(d\).
Moving the cut changes the difference of the two occupied counts by at most two, so the counts can differ by at most one.
A short input admits \(Q=0\).
The remainder is orthogonal, after the exterior normalization of the division estimate, to both projection spaces.
At each end the selected quotient outputs are given by chronological triangular contractions with constant diagonal of modulus \(t=e^{-m}\).
A prefix of length \(h\) has least singular value at least \(t^h\), since its determinant has modulus \(t^h\) and all singular values are at most one.

For \(a=\tfrac12\log(7/3)\), the inequality \(t^{\lfloor a/m\rfloor-1}>1/(1+t)\) holds eventually because \(e^{-a}>1/2\).
It permits nonnegative backward coefficients in the weighted row estimate through that prefix.
When \(n>\lfloor a/m\rfloor\), each end contains the whole prefix, and its squared output norm is at least the sum of the squares of \((1+t)t^{i-1}-1\) through the prefix.
The two orthogonal output ranges then give a scaled lower limit of \(2\int_0^a(2e^{-u}-1)^2\,du=2a-4(1-e^{-a})^2>0.370\).
The logarithm series and geometric remainder specified in the claim give \(r_0^2+2\log(2r_0)-4r_0+2<0.366801\) by using \(r_0<0.716\), and a lower bound \(0.37015\) on the prefix expression by using \(\sqrt{7/3}<1.5276\).
The former substitution uses the nonnegative derivative \(2(u-1)^2/u\).
Thus a hypothetical limiting value \(F<B_0\) forces eventual localization to \(mn\le a\).

For \(E=\|A/P\|_2^2\), the determinant bounds give \(\|Q\|_2^2\le t^{-2n}E\) and bound every cumulative squared input-output defect by \((t^{-2n}-1)E\).
They bound the squared norm outside both projection spaces by this same quantity.
The scaled quotient and remainder norms are bounded, the latter by the triangle inequality.
Writing \(s_t=\sqrt{1-t^2}\), the limsup of \(s_t^2\) times the largest squared row defect is at most \(2(e^{2a}-1)F=(8/3)F<1\).
This strict inequality justifies squaring the chronological row lower bound even for the smallest nonzero input.
The recursion for the nonnegative defect norm has multiplier \(t\) and additive term \(s_t|q_i|\).
Iteration, together with \(|q_i|-1\le(|q_i|^2-1)/3\), bounds the two-end limiting complementary norm by \(w\le2(1-e^{-x})+(\sqrt2/3)(B_Q-2x)\), where balance and integrality give \(B_Q\ge2x\).
Indeed the two geometric terms each tend after scaling to \(\sqrt2(1-e^{-x})\), and the sum of the scaled surplus terms tends to \((\sqrt2/3)(B_Q-2x)\).
This includes an empty end.

The other complementary bound gives \(w^2\le(4/3)F<1/2\), whereas the short result gives \(r\ge r_0>1/\sqrt2\).
Orthogonality yields \(F\ge r^2+B_Q-2rw\), whose right side increases with \(r\) throughout this range.
Substitution of \(r_0\) and the row estimate leaves the nonnegative surplus \((1-2\sqrt2\,r_0/3)(B_Q-2x)\) in addition to \(r_0^2+2x-4r_0(1-e^{-x})\).
The latter has positive second derivative and attains its minimum at \(\log(2r_0)\), giving the second stated constant.
The decimal values are \(0.5123527342\ldots\) and \(0.3667523746\ldots\).
The estimates impose necessary conditions along shrinking-measure sequences, whose existence remains open.
