## Joint periods, empty slots and uniformity in the weight

The finite bounds, the uniform estimate (1) and its conditional terminal-witness consequence hold as stated.

At each selected root write \(x=\beta^i,\ y=\beta^j\).
Eliminating \(y\) from \(cx+ey=U,\ c/x+e/y=V\) gives
\[
       Vc x^2+(e^2-c^2-VU)x+cU=0.
\]
Since \(c,e\) are invertible, an ordinary test has at most two choices of \(x\), each determining \(y\).
The polynomial is identically zero precisely when \(U=V=0,\ e^2=c^2\), including in characteristic two.
Then \(y/x=-c/e\) determines at most one difference class and \(\beta^t=-D/a\) determines at most one third-position class.
If either prescribed value fails to belong to the required cyclic group, there are no completions for that branch.

For a prescribed exceptional subset, compatibility gives a single class of \(t\) modulo \(E\).
At each actual \(t\) there are at most \(2^b\) ordinary branches.
Compatible branches fix \(i\) modulo \(O\) and \(u\) modulo \(\operatorname{lcm}(E,O)=M\), also when the orders have common divisors.
Thus the quadratic contribution from the two position intervals has denominator \(EO\ge M\).
An interval of \(X\) sites contributes at most \(\mathbf1_{X>0}+X/E\), which vanishes for an empty slot.
Counting the positive \(u\le W\) separately gives (3), even when some independently counted placements violate the full order or span.

For \(h\) positive base positions the \(h+1\) vacancies have sum \(N=W-h\).
These weak compositions are equiprobable, and the possible sites on each exterior side have cardinality equal to the last part.
Writing \(q=h+1\), a part has mean \(N/q\) and second factorial moment \(2N(N-1)/(q(q+1))\), by extracting the coefficient of \(x^N\) from \(2x^2/(1-x)^{q+2}\) and dividing by the number of compositions.
This includes the single deterministic part when \(h=0\).
Even for coincident slots, Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\mathbb E[XY]\le 2\mu^2+\mu\).
The product of indicators is at most \(X\), and the two mixed terms have expectations at most \(\mu/E\) and \(\mu/O\).
Summation over subsets gives \(3^k\), and \(\binom Wh\mu=\binom W{h+1}\) gives (2).
For \(r=5\), fixing \(t=0\) by translation leaves \(U=V=-a\ne0\).
Every branch fixes both \(i\) and \(u\) modulo \(M\), with \(i\) confined by its prescribed side of zero to at most \(W\) sites and \(1\le u\le W\).

If a single order exceeds \(2W\), exceptionality would require that order to divide \(2u\).
Fixing the other \(r-4\) survivors gives at most two ordinary pairs and there are \(\binom W{r-5}\) such normalized placements.
Otherwise a minimal selection attaining the threshold \(\min(M,W)>1\) has lcm \(M'\le 2W^2\) by deleting one member.
Each retained order uniquely attains the full power of some prime dividing \(M'\), giving distinct primes for different members.
The factorial lower bound for their product makes \(3^k\) for this reduced selection \(W^{o(1)}\) uniformly.
When \(M\le W\) the selection preserves \(M\), and when \(M>W\) it gives \(1+W/M'\le2\).
The single test for \(M=1\) and the inequality \(\mu\le W\) complete the uniform estimate in all cases.

A zero sum of the two erased terms would force a positive power of \(\alpha\) to be rational, so inversion would fix that power and make \(\alpha\) torsion.
For their nonzero sum \(z\), the trace product \(z\iota(z)\) fixes the positive gap, and \(z\) then fixes the common shift.
This verifies recovery on translation classes with the prescribed orientation.
At a neutral witness the marked position gives at most \(r\) orders, and the fixed span bound permits \(W\ge d\) in the stated asymptotic regime.
The rank inequality at the joint order gives \(Mm/d\ge B(\delta)>0\) even with residue multiplicities, since each selected distinct root contributes at least one degree to the gcd at its prime.
Hence the remaining factor \(O_\delta((1+\log(2d))^2)\) is subpower in \(W\).
Existence of coefficients and roots supplying this rank at an actual witness is an additional hypothesis.
