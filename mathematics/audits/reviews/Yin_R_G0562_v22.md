## Varying real coefficients and bounded reduced lower exponents

On 2026-11-16T17:50 a separately assigned agent for Yin independently reviewed every section of the [real exterior-prefix claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v22.md#statement) and of [its ledger derivation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#exterior), including the use of the exact rational result for actual lifts.
The reviewer previously examined the six-term argument, took no part in developing the present claim or ledger, and assessed the fixed arguments independently of earlier review outcomes.
The review found the coefficient-neighborhood and compact-set conclusions valid for the stated maximum degree, norm bound and prefix fraction, and the fixed-branch gap and necessary conditions on every escaping bounded-offset sequence valid for both input parities.

The first nonintegral coefficient occurs at \(o(h)\) and lies within the prescribed prefix, where every coefficient below \(g\) is integral, so \(g=o(h)\).
The inverse series has constant one, bounded doubled positive-index moments and negative mass tending to zero as the branch index varies.
Dividing the integer \(B^\sharp\) by the integral formal unit \(G\) gives an integral series whose coefficients agree with those of \(S_i(z^g)J_i\) wherever tested.

The product of the exterior root moduli makes the leading coefficient of \(S\) nonzero of modulus less than one.
Through the first nonintegral coefficient \(\lambda=s_l\), division by the polynomial of the preceding integral coefficients makes the first \(l-1\) limiting multipliers zero and leaves \(\lambda\) at \(l\).
For \(t=o(g)\) the bounded raw moments justify discarding the tending-to-zero multipliers on both sides of \(lg\).
At \(lg\) itself the Taylor constant one supplies precisely \(\lambda\).
The mixed Fourier sequence is bounded with finite range, since the first marginal has bounded integral coefficients at positive indices and both the shifted positive tests and the negative tests are integral.
Polynomial interpolation of its finitely many levels preserves the Fourier–Stieltjes algebra, and the integer-transform theorem gives a common periodic part on both tails.
Consequently the negative tail's integrality forces \(\lambda D(r,0)\) integral eventually on the positive side.
A purely atomic integral marginal has finite torsion support, which would make the mixed moment at zero equal to a value on the integral negative tail, contradicting the fractional condition at zero.
The eventual integrality rules out the reciprocal-binomial profile.
For the binomial \(B=1+\delta w^u\), \(H=F+\lambda B\) is integral Laurent and \(H-\lambda B\) vanishes to order at least two at all \(u\) simple roots of \(B\).
Thus \(H/B\) is integral Laurent and equals \(\lambda\) at each such root.
Its residue representative of degree less than \(u\) has integer coefficients because \(w^u=-\delta\) is a unit there.
A polynomial of degree less than \(u\) taking the same value at all \(u\) roots must be constant, contrary to \(\lambda\notin\mathbb Z\).
This argument also covers irrational \(\lambda\).
The resulting Haar marginal gives the short zero interval, and testing finitely many characters against the positive part gives a bounded count of nonzero integer coefficients on each fixed initial multiple of \(g\).
When \(g\) is bounded the coefficient-size estimate suffices.

I checked the distinction between the finite-valued transform of \(2|S|^2\Omega\) and the integer transforms of the individual rows.
The bounded integer coefficient sets are uniform over all mixed frequencies, so convolution with the finitely many real coefficients of \(S^*\) gives one finite set of possible values on all nonzero layers.
The identity \(S^*B_0+SB_0^*\) does the same on the zero layer.
The finite-range decomposition permits complex weights in the joint measure, while positivity gives nonnegative projected slope densities and each continuous row still has integer Laurent coefficients.
The separated translates on common multiples of the fiber orders yield an integer Laurent polynomial \(H=2Sf\) for each slope with density \(f\).

If \(f\ne0\), then \(M(H)<2\) by \(M(S)=1\), the mass bound and exclusion of a Haar marginal.
Because \(f\) is real, \(H/H^*=S/S^*\) as rational functions.
For \(n=\deg S\) the denominator \(w^nS(1/w)\) has only interior roots and has nonzero constant term, whereas \(w^nS(w)\) has only the root zero and strictly exterior roots.
Reducing the rational expression over \(\mathbb Q\) therefore gives \(w^nS\) up to scalar, and the coefficient one at index \(n\) fixes that scalar over \(\mathbb Q\).
Moreover a nonzero finite pole of \(H/S\) would have a reciprocal conjugate pole in the disk, so \(H/S\) is a rational-coefficient Laurent polynomial.
For the least denominator \(m\ge2\), the polynomial \(mS\) is primitive by minimality.
Gauss's lemma in the Laurent ring now makes \(H\) an integral multiple of \(mS\), giving \(M(H)\ge m\) and excluding the slope.
If the leading coefficient has modulus at most \(1/2\), its exterior roots already have product of moduli at least two, giving the additional direct measure bound for an integral multiple.

The remaining full-torus term has finite support in the fiber frequency, and at a last nonzero row the pushforward conditional measure gives first moment of modulus at most \(1/2\).
The marginal density has geometric mean strictly below one, so an integer row cannot survive.
The fixed marginal has only finitely many singular atoms, at torsion points, since \(|S|\) is bounded below on the circle.
A common binomial annihilates the mixed rows over that marginal and forces a zero window for the integer coefficients of the filtered \(X_i\) by compactness.
Coefficient convergence at a fixed degree cap gives uniform convergence on a closed disk of radius \(R>1\) free of zeros of the center.
This proves the geometric bound for \(1/S_i\) even when the degrees vary, and it dominates the linear correction cost of a shifted residue class.
The two cutoff estimates and the positive circle minimum of \(S_i\) bound the integral norm of the cyclotomic multiple of the actual denominator.
Its bounded support contradicts approach of its Mahler measure to one from above.
A finite cover of a compact set gives one neighborhood and a common positive minimum of the local bounds with the degree cap, circle norm bound and prefix fraction fixed.

For the actual anti-reciprocal lifts, the interior trace roots and endpoint factors give multiplicative measure one after normalization.
The bounded-support height inequality applies to complex coefficients and hence to these rational ones.
The lower and reflected supports lie on opposite sides of the midpoint, giving the exact interior coefficient sum \(2\sum|s_{i,r}|\).
The lower bound on that sum excludes a constant limit.
If a limit had an interior root, a fixed surrounding circle \(|w|=r_0<1\) free of its zeros gives a lower bound for \(|S_i|\) there.
At \(|z|=r_0^{1/g}\) the reflected contribution tends to zero by \(h/g\to\infty\), while \(S_i(z^g)\) has an enclosed zero.
Rouché would contradict the circle-root property of the lift.
The neighborhood theorem excludes strictly exterior limits, so at least one boundary root is necessary.
Finally a subsequence of bounded branch indices has a further subsequence with a single \(j\) and a single rational polynomial by discreteness of the bounded coefficient grid.
An integral polynomial contradicts the first nondivisible-index theorem, and a nonintegral one contradicts the fixed rational prefix theorem, which permits boundary roots.
This proves divergence of \(j\) and the uniform gap at bounded \(K,j\).
The derivative identity \(q=j/(\theta-q)+H_q'(0)-1/\theta\), with \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\), gives \(q\le J/c(K,J)+2\).
