## Surviving spans and common rational powers

At 2026-11-20T20:45 UTC a reviewer for Sheng Yin independently checked the whole proof of the [surviving-span theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v642.md#statement).

No development dialogue, author explanations or previous finding on this theorem entered the review.
The finite bounds and the conditional conclusions for the growing terminal witnesses hold for every allowed choice among tied coefficients.

Multiplying the original relation by its image under reciprocal conjugation after moving the deleted pair to the other side gives the stated identity for the surviving sum.
Translation contributes reciprocal monomials whose product is one.
The largest positive degree of the surviving autocorrelation is exactly \(w\), since its coefficient comes from a unique pair of nonzero endpoints.
A Laurent identity with the deleted autocorrelation would require \(u=w\) and \(AB\le B^2\).
Then \(A=B\), and both endpoints would already contribute \(2B^2\) to the constant coefficient.
Since \(r\ge5\), at least one more surviving square is strictly positive.
Clearing negative powers therefore gives a nonzero annihilating polynomial of degree at most \(2\max(u,w)\).
For any common positive divisor \(h\) of the indicated differences and \(u\), the same clearing in the variable \(Y=X^h\) gives degree at most \(2\max(u,w)/h\) at \(\alpha^h\), including when powering lowers the field degree.

The neutral embedding gives \(A/B\le r-1\) by the triangle inequality.
At either deleted outermost exponent, evaluation at the expanding real conjugate in the corresponding direction bounds \(Be^{mv}\) by \(A+(r-2)B\).
Thus each such outermost gap is strictly less than \(G\).
Unless both deleted exponents lie on the same side, each lost endpoint is followed immediately by a surviving exponent, so the full span loses at most two such gaps.
The relation with its smallest exponent translated to zero has degree at least \(d\).
If both terms instead lie on one side, their own gap is outermost, and \(u<G\le d/4\) in the half-degree assertion.
The autocorrelation degree bound gives \(w\ge d/2\) in that case also.

In the short range \(d-w\ge8G^2>2G\), so the one-sided placement is compulsory.
Putting the pair on the right by reciprocity gives \(l\ge d-w-u\).
Every surviving coefficient has magnitude at most that of the outer term.
After normalization the absolute surviving sum is at most \((r-2)e^{-ml}\), which is at most \(2T^2e^{-m(d-w)}\) because \(e^{mu}<2T\).
For the error \(E\) of the submitted proof, set \(L=\log(2T)>2\), so \(G=L/m\ge L\).
The inequalities \(\log(E/m)\le2L+G-8GL<0\) and
\(\log(2GE T^{2G})\le G+2L+2GL-8GL<0\) hold throughout \(0<m\le1\).
Since \(e^{mu}\ge1+m\), the signed target ratio is strictly greater than one.
This forces opposite signs and puts the smaller magnitude at the outer exponent, ruling out a tie between the two deleted magnitudes in this range.

For two admissible pairs, the logarithm is \(1\)-Lipschitz on the interval joining either ratio to its exponential, since both values exceed one.
The absolute logarithm of \(q_1^{u_2}/q_2^{u_1}\) is consequently less than \(T^{-2G}\).
Its numerator and denominator before reduction are positive integers at most \(T^{u_1+u_2}\).
If unequal, integration of \(1/x\) between them would give absolute logarithm at least \(T^{-(u_1+u_2)}\ge T^{-2G}\).
Equality of the rational powers thus holds simultaneously for each pair of deletions.
The finite set of distinct positive gaps has a gcd \(g\), and Bézout's identity writes \(\exp(cg)\) as a rational product since \(c=\log q/u\) is common to the entire set.
In lowest terms the numerator of this rational base exceeds one, so its \(k\)-th power as the numerator of \(q\) gives \(k\le\lfloor\log_2 T\rfloor\).
At a fixed tuple the largest two magnitudes, counted with multiplicity, are determined, and their ratio determines its gap because \(c>0\).

For the cited terminal witnesses \(r\le j\le \lceil64d\log(2d)\rceil+1\), the coefficients have the same upper bound, and \(r\to\infty\).
Coincident exponents would give a proper zero subsum or a two-term sum at a rational carrier, both excluded at the eventual weights.
For Salem inputs \(m\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure.
Bounded-degree finiteness gives \(d\to\infty\), and the external degree-dependent lower bound gives \(G^2=O((\log d)^8/(\log\log d)^6)=o(d)\).
Thus \(d\ge4G\) eventually and the simultaneous gap count is \(O(\log d)\) for this coefficient and weight window.
