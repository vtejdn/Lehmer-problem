## Three roots in a closed disk, residue separation and rational controls

The reviewer received the claim and the requested scope without development discussion, an author explanation or an earlier finding on this claim.
The review used the fixed dependencies and consulted the prior findings on the [power signs](Yin_R_G0562_v1034.md#sign), the [integral realization](Yin_R_G0562_v1237.md#finding) and the [simultaneous approximation](Yin_R_G0562_v1250.md#finding).
The disk bounds and the rational controls hold with the stated quantifiers.

For the exterior root \(T\), the trace-pole hypothesis gives \(1<T<p/q\), so \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)>0\) and the common-power criterion gives \(T^{v_0}=Y^u\).
For even \(q\) the graph quotient is \(T V(q/p,T)\), and for odd \(q\) it is \((T V(p/q,T))^{-1}\).
Thus \(T^N=V(x,T)^m\) in both cases and the identity passes to every conjugate.
Monicity and unit constant make each conjugate a dyadic unit.
After recentering at any such unit \(z_0\), the three factors in the logarithm of the normalized relation are principal units for \(v(w)>0\).
The logarithm is additive there, which gives the required implication from roots of the polynomial to zeros of \(G\).
The rational function \(z^N/V(x,z)^m\) has order \(-m\ne0\) at \(z=x\), so its logarithmic derivative is nonzero.

The denominator \((1+w)(1+Aw)(1-Bw)\) has integral coefficients and constant term one.
Its product with \(G'\) has degree at most two, so its recurrence gives \(v(h_j)\ge t=\min_{0\le i\le2}v(h_i)\) for all \(j\ge3\), and nonvanishing of \(G'\) gives \(t<\infty\).
In \(G(8y)\) the coefficient of degree \(j\ge1\) is \(8^jh_{j-1}/j\).
Some coefficient with \(1\le j\le3\) has valuation at most \(t+9\).
At \(j=4\) the lower bound is \(t+10\), and for \(j\ge5\) it is at least \(t+2j+1\) since \(v_2(j)\le j-1\).
Thus the last coefficient of maximal absolute value has index \(h\le3\).
For an integral zero \(a\), the coefficient of degree \(h-1\) of the quotient by \(y-a\) has the same absolute value as the old coefficient of degree \(h\), since the remaining tail is strictly smaller.
All later coefficients of the quotient have smaller absolute value, and all earlier ones have at most this value.
For \(h=0\) the constant term already dominates on the whole disk.
Repeated division therefore excludes four distinct zeros over any finite dyadic extension.
Any four putative roots and the chosen root center belong to such an extension, even for a disk originally specified with another center.
Reciprocity pairs rational dyadic roots within each of the four odd classes modulo eight, as inversion fixes these classes and the Salem minimum has no root at either fixed point of inversion.
The bound of three in each disk consequently gives at most two rational roots per class and eight in all.

For a root with residue \(r\), the linear coefficient is \(N-mxU\), where \(U=(A-1+B)/x\) has residue \(r+r^{-1}\).
Outside orders one and three this residue is neither zero nor one.
Dividing by \(2^c\), with \(c=\min(v_2(N),v_2(mx))\), leaves a unit even when the two valuations coincide, because the unit residues of the rational scalars are one.
For each \(j\ge2\), \(j\) times the coefficient of \(w^j\) is, up to signs, a sum of terms \(N,m(A^j-1),mB^j\), all of valuation at least \(c\).
For \(v(w)>1\) the difference between the lower bound for its term valuation and the linear term valuation is \((j-1)v(w)-v_2(j)>0\).
The argument holds for fractional \(v(w)\) in ramified extensions and requires the stated strict inequality.

For each sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four, the approximation theorem supplies unbounded admissible \(D\), so \(D-J-2\ge k\) for all sufficiently large such degrees at any prescribed \(k\).
The \(z_j\) are odd and strictly increasing above one, where \(z\mapsto z+z^{-1}-2\) is strictly increasing, so their traces are distinct in \(\mathbb Z_2\).
In the free rank-\(k\) algebra modulo the monic \(C\), multiplication by \(MX-1\), \(M=q(q+1)\), reduces to the identity modulo two and hence has unit determinant.
This proves solvability by a polynomial \(G_0\) of degree \(<k\) even though the traces coincide modulo two.
Adding \(cC\) to \(G_0\) changes the derivative of the resulting trace polynomial at each \(a_j\) by \(c(Ma_j-1)C'(a_j)\) with nonzero slope, so all \(k\) derivative tests exclude only finitely many \(c\in\mathbb Z_2\).
The degree bound allows this perturbation, and the derivative of the reciprocal lift \(P\) at \(z_j\) is \(z_j^DH'(a_j)(1-z_j^{-2})\ne0\).

Choose an integer \(s\ge3\) larger than every \(v_2(z_i-z_j)\) for \(i\ne j\), and write \(d_j=v_2(P'(z_j))\).
One integer \(b\) with \(b>2d_j\) and \(b-d_j\ge s\) for all \(j\) suffices.
For any integral dyadic coefficient perturbation \(\widetilde P\equiv P\bmod 2^b\), the residual valuation at \(z_j\) is at least \(b\) and the derivative retains valuation \(d_j\).
Hensel's inequality yields a root of \(\widetilde P\) in \(z_j+2^{b-d_j}\mathbb Z_2\), with distinct roots in the chosen disjoint disks inside \(3+8\mathbb Z_2\).
Rounding the free trace coefficients of \(G_0+cC\) modulo \(2^b\) gives an ordinary integral representative, since multiplication by \(MX-1\) and reciprocal lifting at fixed degree preserve coefficient congruence.
The modulus \(2^b\) is coprime to every permitted odd denominator prime \(\ell\), so the joint approximation realizes this residue on the same irreducible polynomial with all the required real, pole, moment and capacity conditions.
The precision can depend on the chosen degree and points, and the denominator exponent is chosen afterwards with \(\gcd(a-v_\ell(M),D-1)=1\), giving primitive measure \(\ell^a\lambda\).
This proves the separation from the rational controls for each fixed odd prime and every prescribed \(k\ge4\).
