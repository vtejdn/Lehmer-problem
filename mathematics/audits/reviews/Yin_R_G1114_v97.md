# Positive real part and width one in the delay-two construction

## Review of the limiting functions and their reciprocal completions

At 2026-11-16T19:29 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Yin checked all four sections of L462v114 and all three sections of L462v116.
The reviewer took no part in developing either proof and received no development dialogue, author explanation or earlier verdict on these arguments.

The seed bounds allow a fixed norm and a pole parameter separated from one.
Finitely many repeated seeds contribute finitely many measures at this delay, while reciprocal seeds give only their own Pisot measure.
For a nonreciprocal seed the product of the interior conjugate moduli is \(q/\theta\), so a real conjugate in \([0,1/\theta]\) could occur only at \(1/\theta\), where irreducibility would imply reciprocity.
Thus \(e f^*(\theta)>0\), and the sign opposite Boyd's gives a root beyond \(\theta\).
For Boyd's sign, circle Rouché with the reciprocal summand damped counts precisely one exterior root with multiplicity.
At full strength strict measure excess ensures this root survives and is simple and real.
Reciprocity pairs it with an interior root, whose sign must be positive since \(b_r(-u)=(r+u)/(1+ru)>u\) for \(0<u<1\), whereas \(|(-u)^2 B(-u)|\le u^2\).

In \(J\) the original pole of \(F\) is removable, and the sole interior pole of \(J\) is the simple root \(\tau\) of the associate.
Multiplication by \(T-z-z^{-1}\) removes that pole, and \(J=1+O(z^2)\) gives \(K(0)=T/2\) after cancellation at zero.
The finite Blaschke product \(h\) fixes \(\tau\) and has degree at least two.
Conjugation taking \(\tau\) to zero preserves the derivative at corresponding boundary fixed points, and the factor \(z\) of the conjugated product contributes one to its phase derivative.
The remaining factors contribute strictly positive Poisson kernels.
Consequently at \(w(\xi)=1\) the phase derivative of \(w\) is \(D b_r(\xi)(Dh(\xi)-1)>0\).
The weight of \(2K\) at \(\xi\) is \((T-2\operatorname{Re}\xi)/Dw(\xi)>0\), as one also obtains by taking the ratio of the residues of \(J\) and \((\xi+z)/(\xi-z)\).
After subtraction of these kernels the real part vanishes by the maximum principle.
The positive constant at the origin then gives positive total mass.

Distinct seed poles give distinct functions in the compact Pisot family.
Boyd and Mauldin's derived-set criterion gives \(|F|<1\) away from finitely many boundary points.
Bounded masses give a compact family of Herglotz transforms.
In the limit the Taylor coefficients of \(J_i=1+2z^2 U_i\) stabilize, with \(U_i\) integral, and substitution of \(T=2\) gives \(K(0)=1\), \(K'(0)=q-1\) and the integral higher coefficients.
Direct inversion gives the displayed \(F\), with the factor \(z\) in its denominator canceled in the germ.
On an arc where \(|F|<1\) and \(z\ne1\), the boundary real part of \(2K\) is \(|1-z|^2(1-|F|^2)/|1+z^2 F|^2>0\).
Thus the continuous-part hypothesis in L462v59 applies.
The classification there uses eventual periodicity for the coefficients of \(K\) and \(1/K\), followed by parity of zeros of the continuous density.
It yields the stated three types before the additional seam constraints of that ledger.
The derivative selects precisely the two indicated linear or reciprocal-linear models at norm two and excludes the \(m=1\) models at norm one.

For the constant model, \(J_*=1-2z^2/(1-z)^2\).
A discrepancy of \(F_i\) at \(s\) first affects \(J_i\) at \(s+2\) and \(K_i^0-1\) at \(s+1\).
Thus the coefficients of \(K_i\) at indices \(1\le n\le s\) are \(-t(n-1)\).
Testing its measure with the Fejér kernel counts the coefficient at each such index with weight \(1-n/(s+1)\), since that coefficient equals twice the real Fourier moment.
The weighted sum of \(n-1\) is \(s(s-1)/6\).
This gives \(t(s(s-1)-3)\le6\) and, using \(t=(\alpha-1)^2/\alpha\), the asserted bounded product.

For L462v116, Jensen's formula with \(Q(0)=1\) counts the single disk zero to give \(M(Q)=\theta_*\).
Multiplying Boyd's geometric mean of \(1-|F|^2\) by \(M(Q)^2\) therefore gives the width \(M(\Delta)\), and a common cyclotomic factor contributes measure one.
At width one the extremal Laurent coefficients of \(\Delta\) are units.
Expanding \(A_p Q-AQ_p=s z^p\Delta\) shows that the two signs have their first departures at \(p-b\) with opposite unit values.
Given any sufficiently late departure index \(j\), taking \(p=j+b\) realizes that index.
After any common cyclotomic factor is divided out, the damped high term is strictly dominated on the circle.
Passage to the undamped denominator admits at most one disk zero, and on a fixed small neighborhood of the limiting pole, uniform convergence preserves exactly one zero of \(Q_p\) and none of \(A_p\).
All circle zeros cancel with their full multiplicity by reversal and real coefficients.
The integral Taylor series, positive constant, surviving simple positive pole and unit boundary modulus verify membership in Boyd's class.
His Lemma 1 gives uniqueness after either late signed departure since the successor coefficient intervals have lengths below one.
The reciprocal of the unique disk zero of \(Q_p\) is the sole exterior zero of the monic \(sA_p\).
Equality of the rational functions identifies that exterior zero with the actual seed root, and Kronecker's theorem makes the residual monic factor a product of cyclotomic polynomials since its constant is nonzero.
The identity \(C^*=\pm C\) then gives a bounded-term multiple of the actual associate with exactly the same measure.

For \(K=k/l\), putting \(X=(1-z)l\) gives \(|X-zk|^2-|X-k|^2=2|1-z|^2\operatorname{Re}(k\bar l)\) on the circle.
For each nonconstant model this yields the stated binomial defect and width one.
Any common zero must satisfy \(k=(1-z)l\) and \((1-z)^2l=0\), forcing \(z=1\) by coprimality.
At a remaining circle zero of \(Q\), the boundary inequality would force \(A\) to vanish to at least the same order.
In the norm-two cases the numerator and denominator together have at most five terms, and in the norm-one cases they have at most seven even before cancellation.
Doubling gives ten and fourteen terms respectively for the associate multiple, so the fixed-support theorem rules out escape along a subsequence tending to any fixed nonconstant profile.
The local coefficient threshold may depend on the profile.
For \(K=1\) the defect is \(2|1-z|^2\), of width two.

Both ledger arguments pass this review with the constant profile remaining possible.
The reviewer checked the statements attributed to Boyd and Mauldin on pp. 1–2 of their typescript and Boyd's membership, width and uniqueness passages on printed pp. 1246 and 1249–1250.
The underlying compactness, width formula, integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem and fixed-support isolation theorem were used at their stated scopes.
