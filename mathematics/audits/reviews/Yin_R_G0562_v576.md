They remain distinct after every positive power.
With the incoming constant pair fixed, the \(2m\) block equations at formal parameter zero require both coordinate polynomials to vanish at all \(m\) phases raised to the selected exponent.
The zero constant pair gives zero as the only solution, since neither polynomial of degree at most \(m\) can have these \(m\) distinct nonzero zeros and also vanish at zero.
Thus the determinant at this specialization is nonzero.
Residue-class grouping on the phase bases gives a nonzero polynomial coefficient on each progression, and the nondegenerate recurrence estimate bounds the actual inverse by \(\exp(o(n))\) because the cofactors grow at most polynomially.
Complex conjugation preserves the node set and the incoming real pair, giving reality by uniqueness.

At zero parameter each output polynomial equals its own incoming constant multiplied by \(D_R(Y)/D_R(0)\).
For any fixed subset size a real linear functional on the torsion quotient of the finitely generated group of phases may be chosen with distinct weights on these phases.
The subset of the largest weights uniquely maximizes their sum.
Its product is therefore alone even modulo torsion among the maximizing terms of the elementary symmetric function.
It survives on each class after grouping by powers, and Xiao's lemma gives eventual nonvanishing on each class.
This includes conjugate pairs of phases, since inverse elements can be separated by the linear functional when their ratio is nontorsion.
The constant and leading coefficients need no recurrence estimate.
The endpoint transfer at zero parameter multiplies both incoming entries by the same nonzero scalar \(1/D_R(0)\).
Both components of \((1,-\varepsilon)\) are nonzero, so the preceding blocks and the current elementary symmetric coefficient give a nonzero specialization for each individual output.
This holds for every sufficiently large phase exponent.
On any class of the actual Cramer numerator one may therefore choose a sufficiently large representative, so at least one grouped polynomial is nonzero there.
At parameter \(n\) Xiao's bound gives a numerator of modulus at least \(\exp(-o(n))\).
The denominator is a finite product of determinants with polynomial upper bound, and the successive inverse estimates give the matching upper bound on the outputs.
Restoring \(\exp(n\gamma_{k_0})R^{n\ell}\) gives each coordinate rate \(\gamma_{k_0+\ell}\) separately.

The interval \(L+1,\ldots,L+2d\) ends below \(b-L\) even at the stated minimum width.
Rounding keeps both coordinates at index zero and costs at most a fixed amount at the finitely many other prescribed indices.
All \(\gamma_j>0\) for \(j>0\), so their logarithmic limits persist.
With \(\log b=o(n)\) and \(q\log2\le nS+o(n)\) the completed middle costs at most \(\exp(nS+o(n))\), while either prescribed coefficient at index \(L\) supplies the lower bound.

Its derivative acquires the extra \(nk_0\) times the zero block value, hence both block residuals vanish before rounding.

Rounding contributes \(O(n R^{-n})\) since index zero is fixed.
For \(j>L\), monotonicity of \(j R^{-nj}\) and \(S-L\log R\le\Lambda_R\) give a further normalized exponential deficit of at least \(\log R-o(1)\).
The two coordinates each obey the same tail bound, uniformly in the allowed widths.
