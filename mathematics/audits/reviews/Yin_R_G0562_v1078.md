# Review of Fourier regularity with forbidden cyclotomic tests

## The mixed differences recover every cyclotomic moment of the regular part

At 2026-11-24T20:27 UTC an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed all four sections for Yin.
The reviewer had contributed to the earlier unrestricted moment theorem and checked its application here as a fixed input.
The review received no development dialogue, proof explanations or previous findings on the new argument.
The stated conclusions, including the restriction to a Fourier-summable residual measure in the classification, follow at the given scope.

An atom at an allowed primitive root would make the corresponding bounded-above logarithm have integral \(-\infty\).
Choose a prime \(p\) dividing none of the forbidden orders.
For every \(j\ge1\), a zero \(z\) of \(\Phi_p(X^j)\) has order \(t\) satisfying \(t/\gcd(t,j)=p\), so none of its cyclotomic factors is forbidden.
The logarithm of its modulus has nonnegative integral and pointwise upper bound \(\log p\), by the geometric sum.
If a nontorsion point \(z_0\) had mass \(w>0\), a sequence of its powers tending to a primitive \(p\)-th root would force
\(\int\log|\Phi_p(z^j)|\,d\mu\le w\log|\Phi_p(z_0^j)|+(1-w)\log p\to-\infty\).
Thus removing precisely the masses at the finite forbidden orbits leaves a positive atomless measure, even before imposing (1).

For a fixed \(k\), the series \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}\tau(\ell)|\widehat\nu(k\ell)|/\ell\) is bounded by \(k\) times (1), by comparison on the subsequence \(k\ell\).
The radial logarithms integrate to absolutely convergent series, whose finite limit is \(-\sum_\ell\operatorname{Re}\widehat\nu(j\ell)/\ell\).
Applying Fatou's lemma to the nonnegative functions \(\log2-\log|1-rz^j|\) gives a finite integral of \(\log2-\log|1-z^j|\).
The displayed lower bound by \(\log|1-z^j|+\tfrac12\log r\), together with the common upper bound, now gives domination for \(r\ge1/2\).
This identifies the radial limit with \(A(j)\).
The absolute double sum over \(n,\ell\) bounding \(\sum_n |A(kn)|/n\) is \(\sum_m\tau(m)|\widehat\nu(km)|/m\).
Each \(\log|\Phi_s(z^j)|\) is integrable, since \(\log|1-z^{sj}|\) is a finite sum of bounded-above factor logarithms with finite integral.
Finite divisor inversion expresses \(I_s(j)\) in terms of \(A(dj)\) for \(d\mid s\), proving the asserted harmonic summability at each fixed multiple.

At an induction step let \(Q\) be \(L\) times the product of the integers already chosen, and include the empty subproduct \(k=1\) among the previous subproducts.
For each of the finitely many \(k\), the integers \(n\) with \(|I_s(kn)|\ge\epsilon\) have reciprocal sum at most \(\epsilon^{-1}\sum_n |I_s(kn)|/n\).
Their union has finite reciprocal sum, whereas \(\sum_{n\equiv1\bmod Q}1/n\) diverges.
This gives a new integer greater than one, coprime to all previous choices and to \(L\), imposing exactly the new subset-product bounds and retaining the previous ones.

For \(\gcd(J,s)=1\), the possible orders of roots of \(\Phi_s(X^J)\) are exactly \(s d\) with \(d\mid J\), by the order equation.
Each root is simple, because powering has nonzero derivative there.
In the alternating product, pairwise coprimality of the \(j_i\) assigns each divisor of their product uniquely to a tuple \(d_i\mid j_i\).
The sum of signs of the subproducts containing all indices with \(d_i>1\) is one when all \(h\) indices are present and zero otherwise.
Every factor remaining in \(G_h\) therefore has an order divisible by a prime outside \(L\), so its logarithm has nonnegative integral against \(\mu\).
For a primitive forbidden \(\zeta\) with order different from \(s\), \(\zeta^J=\zeta\) makes all numerator and denominator evaluations equal and nonzero.
For order \(s\), expanding at \(\zeta\) gives \(\Phi_s(X^J)=J\Phi_s'(\zeta)(X-\zeta)+O((X-\zeta)^2)\), since \(\zeta^{J-1}=1\).
The total exponent of \(X-\zeta\) and of \(\Phi_s'(\zeta)\) is zero.
For each \(i\), the exponent of \(j_i\) is the sum of signs over subsets containing \(i\), which vanishes for \(h=2,3\).
Hence \(G_h(\zeta)=1\) in both cases, justifying subtraction of arbitrary individual forbidden atom masses without assuming they are equal on an orbit.
The alternating log identity holds away from finitely many roots, hence almost everywhere for \(\nu\), with every term integrable.
At \(h=2\) the constant-subproduct term has sign \(+1\) and the other three contribute at most \(3\epsilon\) in absolute value.
At \(h=3\) its sign is \(-1\) and the remaining seven contribute at most \(7\epsilon\).
The two lower tests imply \(-3\epsilon\le I_s(1)\le7\epsilon\), giving \(I_s(1)=0\) for every \(s\).
Normalization of a nonzero \(\nu\) now gives precisely the hypotheses of the unrestricted moment theorem.
One can also check its conclusion here by summing the zero moments over the divisors of \(j\) to get \(A(j)=0\), then applying arithmetic Möbius inversion to the absolutely summable double series above to obtain \(\operatorname{Re}\widehat\nu(j)=0\) for every \(j>0\).
For zero residual mass the symmetrization assertion holds directly.

The divisor square-sum bound \(\sum_{n\le X}\tau(n)^2\ll X(1+\log X)^3\) gives \(\sum_n\tau(n)^2/n^2<\infty\) by dyadic summation.
Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz give (1) for a square-integrable density.
For the arc bound one may reduce the exponent to \(0<c<1\).
Integrating the Fejér kernel on the central arc and successive doubled annuli bounds its convolution by \(O_\nu(N^{1-c})\), uniformly in the center, since the annular bounds have factor \(2^{-(2-c)l}\).
A second integration bounds the sum of the first \(N\) squared Fourier coefficients by the same order.
Dyadic Cauchy–Schwarz with the divisor square-sum estimate then gives \(O_\nu(N^{-c/2}(1+\log(2N))^{3/2})\) per block, which suffices.

At each of the orders \(1,2,3,4,6\), the primitive roots form one conjugation orbit.
Evenness therefore makes the residual mass removal uniform within each such orbit, and the preceding theorem makes the even residual a multiple of arc length under (1).
Conversely arc length has all cyclotomic moments zero, and a uniform forbidden-orbit measure gives each allowed logarithm its normalized log absolute resultant, which is nonnegative.
All the stated mixtures have residuals satisfying (1).
The finite set of orders and evenness enter exactly where the claimed classification uses uniformity on a whole primitive orbit.
The objective and constraint logarithms are bounded above and upper semicontinuous, so their integrals are upper semicontinuous on probabilities by truncation from above by continuous functions \(\max(\log|P|,-T)\) and the analogous cyclotomic truncations.
Thus the even feasible set is compact and contains arc length with finite objective.
Any maximum strictly above the orbit-and-arc-length value in (2) requires a residual failing (1), by the mixture classification and the fixed forbidden-order dual formula.
This argument supplies no assertion that all atomless feasible residuals satisfy (1).
