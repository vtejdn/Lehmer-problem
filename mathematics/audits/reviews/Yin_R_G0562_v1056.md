## The first relation and the finite sequence of quotient lattices

At 2026-11-24T18:58 an agent outside the development of this proof independently checked the five fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without development discussion, author explanations or prior findings on this proof.

The two row minima give the exponent \(t=1+p(j_1-j_0)\) after division of the graph by its first pivot monomial.
The remaining within-row differences divided by their gcd \(s\) give the primitive vector \(m\), including when one row has only one term.
At \(\gamma=\alpha^{ps}\) the coordinate monomials \(\gamma^{m_i}\) generate \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) by the gcd condition.
The nonzero row values recover \(\alpha^t\) in this field.
Since \(p\) is coprime to \(t\), Bézout yields the exact field \(\mathbb Q(y)=\mathbb Q(\alpha^g)\) with \(g=\gcd(ps,|t|)=\gcd(s,|t|)\).
The inclusion in each direction uses respectively the recovered powers and the divisibility of their exponents by \(g\).
The binomial over this field gives \(d\le ge\), and \(g\mid s\) then gives \(m_i\le b/s\le Ke\) for every horizontal exponent.
This proves (1) without a hypothesis on degree preservation by powers.

The local value equation has nonzero denominator at both \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\), by coprimality of the original rows.
Multiplying the two order-two contacts gives vanishing of the horizontal determinant and its directional derivative at \(y\).
Indeed the parameter Euler derivative is \(ps\) times \(\partial_m\), a nonzero scalar multiple.
The entire specialization of the determinant is nonzero, since substituting the \(s\)-th power for the parameter variable reproduces the original nonzero determinant and both pivot monomials cancel.

For the section estimate consider a geometric common irreducible Laurent factor \(J\) through \(x\).
If its multiplicity in \(D\) is one, the product rule would give \(J\mid\partial_a J\).
The support containment \(N(\partial_aJ)\subseteq N(J)\) and additivity of Newton polytopes imply that a nonzero quotient \(\partial_a J/J\) must be a scalar.
The zero derivative gives the same eigenvector identity with scalar zero.
Thus all exponents in \(J\) have the same \(a\)-weight and \(J(Z^a)\) is a scalar times one monomial.
Its vanishing at \(x\) would make \(D(Z^a)\) identically zero.
This proves that the common factors and their Galois conjugates avoid \(x\), even if distinct simple factors of \(D\) meet there.
A zero \(\partial_a D\) would itself make \(D\) homogeneous of weight zero and could not give a nonzero specialization vanishing at \(x\).
The rational gcd therefore removes all common geometric factors, and clearing coordinate denominators loses no torus point.
The residual coprime equations have total component degree at most \(L^2\) and dimension at most \(N-2\).

The integer kernel of a primitive weight \(a\) is saturated of rank \(q=N-1\).
Adjoining an integral vector with pairing one to a kernel basis makes a unimodular basis of \(\mathbb Z^N\).
The perpendicular height of that vector above the kernel is \(1/\|a\|_2\), so the Euclidean covolume of the kernel is \(\|a\|_2\).
The vectors realizing its successive minima \(\lambda_i\) span a subgroup of finite index in the kernel.
Their binomials therefore cut out finitely many translates of the connected one-dimensional torus parametrized by \(Z^a\).
The point and all its rational conjugates belong to this identity component, where the nonzero polynomial \(D(Z^a)\) has only finitely many zeros.
Positive-dimensional components on other translates do not contain these conjugates.

Each cleared binomial has degree at most the one-norm of its exponent difference, hence at most \(\sqrt N\,\lambda_i\).
For a component of dimension \(j\), a containing section retains its degree, while a proper section gives children of dimension at most \(j-1\) whose degrees sum to at most the current multiplier times the parent's degree.
Induction with budget \(j\) bounds its final isolated descendants by its degree times the product of the largest \(j\) remaining degree bounds, or all the bounds when fewer remain.
At a proper section the current multiplier times the largest \(j-1\) later bounds is at most the largest-\(j\) product before the section.
This handles different branches retaining different binomials and permits discarding components wholly at the boundary.
Starting with \(j\le N-2\) leaves the product over \(i=2,\ldots,N-1\), empty when \(N=2\).
Minkowski's upper product theorem in the Euclidean kernel span gives \(\prod_i\lambda_i\le q^{q/2}\|a\|_2\), using the cube of side \(2/\sqrt q\) inside its unit ball.
Combining these bounds gives (2) with the stated powers of \(N\) and \(N-1\).

At least one row has two distinct nonpivot positions because \(k\ge5\).
In that row the coordinate derivatives are, up to the common sign for the second row, \(c_i(P^\iota-P/z_i^2)\), with \(P\) denoting the chosen row.
Nonvanishing of the row at \(y,y^{-1}\) would force the same value of \(z_i^2\) at both positions if all these derivatives vanished.
Their distinct exponents and nontorsion \(\gamma\) exclude that equality.
Thus the initial determinant is smooth at the selected point, ruling out a repeated geometric factor there.
Its monomials are constant, linear, inverse-linear or of the form \(z_i/z_j\).
Multiplying by the product of the \(n\) variables gives degree at most \(n+1\) for both equations.
Substituting \(L=n+1,\ N=n,\ \|m\|_2/e\le\sqrt n\,K\) in (2) yields the stated
\[
 \lambda_1\le K(n+1)^2 n^{(n-1)/2}(n-1)^{(n-1)/2}.
\]
The shortest nonzero relation is primitive since dividing a common integer divisor would shorten it.

For each such relation a unimodular completion makes its character one coordinate.
The corresponding coordinate of the point is one, so dropping it preserves the point field and the remaining monomials give the same parametrization in the quotient torus.
The new weights remain primitive because the induced pairing has the same image \(\mathbb Z\) as before.
Their norms are at most a fixed multiple of \(e\) for each of the finitely many completions chosen at the current depth.
The transformed exponents and cleared degrees of the determinant are bounded on the same finite list, and cancellations only reduce the necessary degree bounds.
The original nonzero one-parameter specialization and both zero jets persist under this character substitution.
If there is no repeated factor through the point and dimension is at least two, (2) bounds the next relation independently of \(e,p\) and of the coefficients.
Rank decreases at each step, so taking the finite union of choices at each depth yields the claimed finite list depending only on \(k,K\).
In rank one the primitive weight is \(1\) or \(-1\), and a nonzero polynomial of bounded cleared degree contains the powered point as a root.
The bounded-degree branch gives a positive measure bound by norm descent.
The alternative branch is specifically a repeated geometric Laurent factor passing through the point after a listed quotient and supplies no measure gap there.
