# Independent review of angular distribution weighted by the two logarithmic masses

## Conditioning within blocks and the limits of both mass measures

At 2026-11-25T04:24 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v149 independently reviewed for Yin its fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v149.md#statement) and all four proof sections, including the explicit source-position cosets \(gH\).
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs without development discussion or previous findings on v149.
The arc estimate and bounded densities hold on the noncoordinate branch; when \(e\to\infty\), both normalized strict-sign measures converge to arclength.

For either invariant partition, full blocks or all oriented halves, the factor discriminants have nonzero integral product and the sum of their log measures is \(m_0\).
The size of a part is \(n=2b\) or \(b\), respectively, and there are at most \(2u\) parts.
The reflected energy summed over them is \(O(b(m_0+u\log(3b)))\) at \(\rho=e^{-1/n}\).
Its \(h\)-th term gives the stated square-root bound after division by \(n\), since \(\rho^{-h}\) is bounded for \(h\le b\).
Replacing radial powers by phase powers costs at most \(h\sum|\log|y||\le 2hm_0\) before division.
For a Fejér kernel comparable to \(H\) on a short arc, the resulting proportion is \(O(1/H+\sqrt{H(m_0+u\log(3b))/b}+Hm_0/b)\).
With \(H\) of order \(\min(1/|I|,1/s)\) this is \(O(|I|+s)\), and for each fixed nonzero Fourier order the normalized single-part sum tends to zero.

Uniform group averaging counts each actual conjugate of \(A\) with the same multiplicity.
The unit norm gives \(\mathbb E L_+=\mathbb E L_-=\mathbb E|L|/2\), so these are the correct denominators for the two sign measures.
Transitivity gives \(\mathbb E|\ell_i|=2m_0/d\).
In the independent signed model each supported even-flip vector has uniform independent signs on any four distinct positions in its block, since \(b\ge5\), and the blockwise groups act independently.
Conditionally the second and fourth moments of the signed log sum are therefore \(\sum\ell_i^2\) and at most \(3(\sum\ell_i^2)^2\), over its nonzero positions.
Interpolation of its first, second and fourth norms, then Cauchy–Schwarz on these \(e\) positions, gives \(\mathbb E|L|\gg\sqrt e\,h_1\).

In an occupied oriented block with majority size \(m_B\ge b/3\) and \(e_B=b-m_B\) exceptions one can form \(\min(e_B,m_B-2)\gg e_B\) disjoint unlike-color pairs after two equal labels are reserved.
Compensating an odd number of swaps by the transposition of the reserved pair preserves the actual monomial on that pair.
Consequently independent fair swap choices yield a symmetric signed sum plus a constant; its expected absolute value is minimized at constant zero.
For a chosen pair, after its first image is specified, the second ranges uniformly over the other members of the same transported half by the point stabilizer in the supported alternating group.
The half-product is torsion, so the conditional mean of the second log is \(-\ell_i/(b-1)\).
Jensen gives \(\mathbb E|\ell_i-\ell_j|\ge h_1\).
Applying the moment estimate to the swap differences with \(|c_i-c_j|\ge1\) and summing over the disjoint pairs gives the same lower bound as in the independent case.
In that case supported three-cycle lifts can also be made pure in a prescribed orientation: their fourth powers have order three with sign product one on the three moved coordinates, and an even flip corrects these signs.
This permits balancing the two signs of the nonzero coefficients in each block.

For the arc estimates, the source-position subgroup \(H\) permutes a chosen exceptional label \(j\) and baseline positions, omitting a distinct prescribed position when needed.
Right multiplication of \(g\) by \(H\) keeps the actual baseline monomial and all other differences fixed, while the image of \(j\) is uniform on \(\gg b\) positions in the transported full block.
This works for a nonzero baseline as its complete product over the transported selection is invariant under these permutations.
The available angles need not themselves be uniform: comparison to the \(2b\)-point full block and the preimage under \(t_j=c_j-c_{\rm base}\in\{\pm1,\pm2\}\) bound the conditional arc probability by \(p=\min(1,C(|I|+s))\).
In the independent case the balanced orientation leaves at least two exceptions whenever \(e\ge2\): a sparse block uses zero baseline and a dense block has a positive fraction of its nonzeros or zeros outside a largest color.
For each absolute-log term one may therefore hold its own source position fixed and move a different exception.
Using the actual nonzero terms in the independent model and the differences from baseline in the oriented one costs at most \(O(peh_1)\) in total.
Alternatively \(L_0=L-t_j\ell_j\) is fixed under this averaging, so the cost is at most \(p\,\mathbb E|L_0|+2h_1\), with \(\mathbb E|L_0|\le\mathbb E|L|+2h_1\).
Division by the lower first-moment estimate yields the minimum of \(O(\sqrt e\,p)\) and \(O(p+e^{-1/2})\), hence \(O(\sqrt p)\).
The factor two from either strict sign denominator preserves this bound.
Along a weakly convergent subsequence, extract either bounded \(e\) or \(e\to\infty\).
The corresponding first or second bound on each fixed open arc gives a finite constant times its length for the limit by the open-set inequality.
Covering open sets by arcs gives bounded density with respect to normalized arclength.

When \(e\to\infty\) but \(e\le\sqrt b\), the oriented exception can visit all but \(O(\sqrt b)\) positions of a whole half.
At fixed frequency \(h t_j\ne0\) the omitted positions contribute \(o(1)\) to its normalized phase sum.
In the independent model zero baselines allow a flip on \(j\) paired with a spare zero coefficient; combined with permutations this makes both orientations equally available and excludes at most \(O(\sqrt b)\) full-block roots.
In both cases the phase of the remaining monomial is fixed during the average.
Replacing either strict-sign weight by the corresponding 1-Lipschitz function of \(L_0\) costs \(O(h_1)=o(\mathbb E|L|)\).
The resulting conditional phase mean tends uniformly to zero and \(\mathbb E|L_0|=O(\mathbb E|L|)\).

For \(e>\sqrt b\), unlike-color pair counts are \(\gg e\) also in the independent model by its balanced orientation.
Thus one may use \(K=\lceil C_h\log(3d)\rceil\) pairs with compensation, since \(b\) grows faster than every fixed power of \(\log d\).
Replacing a strict-sign weight by the corresponding function of the swap-average log costs \(O(Kh_1)=o(\mathbb E|L|)\).
Conditional on the original images, averaging the phases over the swaps gives exactly the stated product of absolute cosines in modulus, since the compensating equal-color pair does not affect the phase.
To bound the expectation of that product, right-average over the supported pure permutations and expose the pair positions successively.
A prescribed partial assignment has equally many even completions while at least two positions per block remain unused.
For the second member of each pair the remaining \(b-O(K)\) roots are distinct members of a full \(2b\)-point block, even if the transported orientations in the independent model differ across labels.
For fixed nonzero \(h\) and \(|c_i-c_j|=1\) or \(2\), the conditional near-maximal absolute cosine set is covered by a bounded number of arcs of arbitrarily small total length, uniformly in the first angle.
The full block estimate therefore bounds its conditional probability by \(1/2\) after choosing the level sufficiently near one and the degree large.
This gives the uniform product expectation bound \(\gamma_h^K\) by successive conditioning, without asserting independence of the exposed pairs.
The swap-average log has maximum at most \(ub\,\mathbb E|L|\) by the transitive projection estimate applied to the actual value of \(A\).
Taking \(C_h\) large makes this last Fourier contribution negligible as well.
Thus each normalized sign measure has all fixed nonzero Fourier coefficients tending to zero, proving the arclength limit.
