# Power quotients and the signed binomial ideal for consecutive coefficients

<a id="finding"></a>
## Both power quotients divide the coefficient sum, and the long norm has the claimed divisor

At 2026-11-21T17:55 UTC a separate reviewing agent acting for Sheng Yin independently checked the full statement and all three proof sections of [the submitted claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v878.md#decision-consecutive-coefficient-power-divisors-submitted).
The reviewer took no part in developing this proof and received no development discussion, author explanation or earlier finding about it.
The argument establishes every stated divisibility and both signed ideal formulas.

Dividing the section by the sign of \(a\) gives \(q+(q+1)T-qTZ-(q+1)Z=0\) for either coefficient sign and either reciprocal sign.
Thus \(EZ=q+(q+1)T\), \(E(1-Z)=1-T\), and \(E(1+Z)=L(1+T)\).
The difference \((q+1)E-qEZ=L\) gives the inverse identity in \(A\).
Multiplication of the binomial identities and cancellation of \(1-\beta^2\) gives \(E^2U_v=LU_u\).
Multiplying by \((E')^2\) and cancelling \(L\) gives the other identity.
Nontorsion excludes zero in each cancelled power difference, and \(EE'=L>0\).
If a nontorsion algebraic unit is a root at dilation \(g\), its \(g\)-th power retains both properties and satisfies the section just checked.

Successive subtractions \(G_n-X^{n-m}G_m=G_{n-m}\) preserve the two-generator ideal and reduce the indices to their gcd, which is one.
Consequently \(U_u,U_v\) are comaximal, including when \(u=1\).
Modulo \(U_v\), the identity \(E^2U_v=LU_u\) kills \(L\) because \(U_u\) is invertible there.
The other identity gives the same conclusion modulo \(U_u\).
The intersection of the two principal ideals equals their product, giving \(U_uU_v\mid L\) in the order itself.
Extension to the full ring of integers preserves both comaximality and the divisibility, so the factorization \(\beta^{2n}-1=(\beta^2-1)U_n\) gives the claimed support and valuations.

At a zero of \(E(X)\), the value of the parent is \(-q+(q+1)^2/q=L/q\).
Its degree is \(u+v\) and the leading coefficient of \(E(X)\) has magnitude \(q\), giving absolute resultant \(q^vL^u\).
Since \(S\) is monic, its quotient in the integral parent is integral, and the resultant with \(S\) has magnitude \(|N(E)|\).
Combining the resulting divisor with \(|N(E)|\mid L^D\) removes the full \(q^v\) factor.
For \(D\ge 2u\), the norm identity therefore expresses \(|N(U_v)|\) as \(L^{D-2u}|N(U_u)|(L^u/|N(E)|)^2\).
The lower divisor is one at \(D=2u\), and taking the norm of the product divisor gives the upper bound at every degree.
For \(D>2u\), every \(p\mid L\) must divide \(N(U_v)\) and thus has a prime ideal above it dividing \(U_v\).
When \(v\) is prime and \(p\ne v\), the residue of \(\beta^2\) is a \(v\)-th root of unity different from one, since \(G_v(1)=v\).
Thus \(v\mid p^f-1\) at its residue degree \(f\), whence \(\operatorname{ord}_v(p)\mid f\le D\).
The cited [primitive degree estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement) gives \(D/u\ge q\log(2)/12\) once \(H=q+1\ge8\), uniformly in the dilation.
This is the only earlier estimate required for the eventual strict inequality.
After forming the findings on v878, the reviewer checked the applicable scope of the [earlier degree review](Yin_R_G0562_v1017.md#finding).

Finally \(E(1-Z)=1-T\) makes \(I\) principal.
The constant coefficient of \(S\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Z\), so \(\beta,T,Z\) are units in \(A\) and negative Bézout exponents are valid.
When \(s^v=t^u\), the identity \(hu+jv=1\) gives \((s^ht^j)^u=s\) and \((s^ht^j)^v=t\).
Hence \(T^h Z^j=\eta\beta\) equals one modulo \(I\), while \(T=(\eta\beta)^u\) and \(Z=(\eta\beta)^v\) give the reverse inclusion.
The generators are nonzero in the domain \(A\), so their ratio is a unit and the absolute norm of \(1-Z\) equals \(|S(\eta)|\).
For incompatible signs \(T^v=-Z^u\) puts \(2\) in \(I\), after which the same Bézout relation forces \(\beta=1\) in \(A/I\).
Conversely \(T=Z=1\) in \(A/(2,1-\beta)\).
This proves the ideal equality, and the monic presentation identifies the additive index with \(\gcd(2,|S(1)|)\), including index one.
The determinant of multiplication by \(1-Z\) computes this index on the full lattice \(A\), without a maximal-order assumption.
The result supplies necessary conditions for a nontorsion unit root and leaves the existence question for unbounded \(q\) open.
