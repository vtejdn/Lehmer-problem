## Salem degrees, actual fixed subgroups and the logarithmic window

For the second seed the listed sign changes locate all three roots, including its unique positive exterior root in \((2,5/2)\).
The eight nonzero rationally weighted formal terms are eligible for the minimal-relative-order bound, even when some values at a root of unity coincide.
Within a rank-one partition, a vertical line leaves the columns separately vanishing, contradicting the absence of unit zeros of \(g\).
A nonvertical line gives at most one point per column in a block, hence exactly one cross-column pair.

The coefficients at height one then have unequal magnitudes.

The relative-order bound yields \(t\mid210D\), so \(t\mid12600\mid J\).
Now \(g-g^*=-z(z-1)\) leaves only \(1\), with numerator derivative \(-J-1\).

The Rouché comparison before removing the endpoint has fixed degree and exactly one exterior zero for each contraction parameter below one.
The limit can have at most one, with multiplicity, and the opposite signs on two fixed real points on either side of the exterior seed root give its existence for every sufficiently large \(J\) and its convergence.

This proves the claimed minimal degree \(J+2\), Salem property and convergence.

An embedding fixing a positive power must send the exterior root to a conjugate of the same modulus, so separability and uniqueness prove equality of the power fields.
I also checked the bottom coefficients \(1,-1,0\) and \(1,-1,-2\), the top reversals and the interior alternating or constant coefficients from the two geometric sums.

## Four Laurent terms at a specified vertex

I checked the full C0900v2 statement, both arguments, both directions of the table and every direct dependency declared in its submission.
An integral exposing functional can be chosen strictly positive on all three other exponent differences.
If their affine span omits zero, rotation in the common character and the strict supremum excess above two give \(M(P)>2\).
This covers rank three and three collinear nonconstant vectors in rank two.
If two nonconstant vectors lie on a line through zero they are positive multiples of a common direction, and the third is independent in the remaining case.
Jensen in that independent character gives \(M(T)=2\) and \(\min|T|\ge1\) if equality is assumed, since a strict pointwise shortfall persists on an open arc.
The marked constant remains an endpoint of \(T\), so minimum-modulus rigidity excludes the resulting genuine trinomial.

For rank two with no collinear triple the two integral extreme-ray functionals embed the lattice with finite index into \(\mathbb Z^2\).
Pullback on the dual tori preserves Haar measure, and the interior vector has strictly positive coordinates.
With neither coordinate of this vector matching the corresponding extreme exponent, continuity and the nonzero leading coefficient of the transverse slice force each extreme coefficient to be a sign.
Indeed the mean of the slice log measure equals the mean of its constant-coefficient lower bound, whereas a zero of that constant would contradict the fixed lower bound from the leading coefficient.
At each phase with extreme term \(-1\) the slice has constant one and all roots on the circle.
Conjugate reversal reflects the single middle exponent to itself, giving the two possible ratios on each axis.
For a doubled ratio, self-inversiveness of \(1+bY+c_0x^rY^2\) with \(b=\pm1\) forces \(c_0x^r=1\) at every \(x^p=-1/a\).
Taking ratios forces \(p\mid r\), so the other ratio is also two, and then \(c_0=1\).
Simultaneous halves give a collinear triple.
Simultaneous doubles give \(2+X+Y+X^2Y^2\), since the two sign rotations disappear from the squared term.
If its measure were two, continuity and equality of the phase average would force that value for \(2+2z+z^4\) too.
The two disk-preserving reductions of its reversal give \(3z^3+4z^2-2\) and \(5z^2+12z+8\).
Their explicit coefficients and the last root product \(8/5\) verify the contradiction.
The matching-coordinate cases each form a convex quadrilateral with a parallel pair, completing the exhaustive geometry.

For such a quadrilateral Jensen in the transverse character reduces equality to pointwise domination of the opposite binomial.
The cited calculation yields \(n/m=1,2\), with all sign and magnitude entries in the claim, sufficiency included.
For example with \(|a|=2\) the common zeros force \(m\mid n\), equal opposite magnitudes \(B\) and \(B(n/m)\le2\).
With \(|a|=1\) the squared mean permits only \(\{1,2\}\) or \(\{1,1\}\).
The former forces identical squared moduli and \(n=m\), while the unit pair forces \(m\mid n\) and excludes \(n/m\ge3\) by the phase \(\pi/(n/m)\).
Since \(m\mid n\), \(x=z^m\) and \(y=u\) generate precisely the support lattice and are independent.
Its embedding into any larger lattice induces a surjection on the dual tori preserving Haar probability.
The four-term sparse vertex gap applies to each case outside the equality list at the same fixed vertex modulus two, in every stated ambient dimension.
The classification holds without a trace hypothesis.
