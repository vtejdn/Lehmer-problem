## The trace degree, the phase count and the span lower bound

At 2026-11-17T01:17 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the complete C345v111 submission fixed at 2026-11-17T01:16 UTC.
The reviewer had no part in its development and received the fixed claim without its proof-generation discussion or a previous verdict on it.

The coefficient of \(z^{\deg U}\) in \(U(z)U(z^{-1})\) is the product of the leading and constant coefficients for positive degree.
Since \(z^j+z^{-j}\) is monic of degree \(j\) in \(z+z^{-1}\) for \(j>0\), the trace polynomial has exactly that degree, including degree zero for a nonzero constant \(U\).
Factoring each paired linear term gives one trace root per root of \(U\), counted with multiplicity.
Divisibility over \(\mathbb R\) supplies the equations at every distinct conjugate and its inverse, and multiplication removes the two opposite offsets.
The common values are nonzero since a torsion root of the Salem minimal polynomial would make the polynomial cyclotomic.
If the two trace polynomials coincide, each root of \(B\) solves \(z^2-tz+1=0\) for a real \(t\in[-2,2]\).
At an interior trace the two solutions are complex conjugates with equal multiplicities in each real polynomial, hence each multiplicity is half the trace multiplicity.
At an endpoint the entire trace multiplicity belongs to the one root.
It follows that \(B=cA\) and the identical trace polynomials force \(c=\pm1\).
With \(k>0\), neither \(1+\lambda^k\) nor \(1-\lambda^k\) vanishes, and with \(k=0\) the only possible cancellation is the excluded zero polynomial.
This proves \(\max(\deg A,e)\ge d/2\) from the distinct trace roots without rationality of any coefficient of \(B\).

In the reflected energy estimate of C345v108 the smoothing factor \(e^{-2\ell/d}\) is at least \(e^{-2}\) through frequency \(d\).
The two positive real conjugates both reflect to radius \(e^{-m}\) at angle zero, so the change from their reflected powers to their projected powers is exactly \(2(1-e^{-\ell m})\).
On this frequency range the unweighted squared sum for the projected phases is therefore \(O_M(d^2\log(2d))\), by bounding \(\ell\) by \(d\) in the weighted norm and using the elementary squared triangle bound.
The Fejér kernel sum over all ordered pairs equals \(d^2+2\sum_{1\le\ell<d}(1-\ell/d)|S_\ell|^2\).
Its kernel values are nonnegative everywhere and at least a fixed multiple of \(d\) within circular distance \(1/d\), by the sine quotient including its value at zero.
This bounds the count of close ordered pairs by \(O_M(d\log(2d))\), including the coincident real phases and the diagonal.
Half-open bins have widths comparable to \(1/d\) and at most \(1/d\), so their sum of squared occupancies is at most this count.
Cauchy–Schwarz now applies to any selected bins without an assumption about the spacing of the perturbation roots.

Write \(s=2\cosh m\) and \(u=\min(s-2,1)\).
On the prescribed trace interval, \(2-t\) lies between \(u/4\) and \(u/2\), and \(|dt/d\theta|=\sqrt{(2-t)(2+t)}\asymp\sqrt u\).
The angular interval thus has length comparable to \(\sqrt u\).
At \(m\le M\), one has \(u\asymp_M\min(m^2,1)\), and the stated external Dobrowolski bound gives \(\sqrt u/\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\longrightarrow\infty\) uniformly.
The half-width meets the short-arc hypothesis from C345v108 for large \(d\), yielding \(\gg_M d\sqrt u\) phases, all neutral since the interval has positive distance from angle zero at each \(d\).
Each real projection of a possibly complex perturbation trace root excludes at most \(O(1)\) bins through an angular interval of length \(O(1/d)\).
Thus \(e\) roots can exclude at most \(O_M(\sqrt{ed\log(2d)})\) phases, with zero excluded when \(e=0\).
Choosing a sufficiently small constant in \(e<c_Mdu/\log(2d)\) leaves a neutral trace \(t\) with \(|t-\gamma|\ge\sqrt u/d\) simultaneously for all perturbation roots and ensures \(D=\deg A\ge d/2\).

For each parent trace root \(\xi<t\), adding \((s-t)/(t-\xi)\ge u/4\) to one gives its distance ratio.
For \(t<\xi\le2\), the numerator is at least \(s-2\) and the denominator at most \(u/2\), so the ratio is at least two.
The common nonzero values exclude \(\xi=t\).
For a perturbation trace root \(\gamma\), the distance ratio is at most \(1+(s-t)d/\sqrt u\le 1+O_M(d)\), since \(s-2\ll_M u\) and \(u\le1\).
The leading coefficients cancel in the ratios at \(s,t\), even if the real coefficients of \(B\) are arbitrarily large or small.
Taking logarithms of the factorizations gives \(du\ll_M e\log(2d)\), contradicting the small choice of \(c_M\).
Squaring Dobrowolski's lower bound in the range \(m\le1\) gives the numerator \((\log\log d)^6\) and, with the packing loss, the denominator \((\log d)^7\).
In the range \(m>1\), \(du/\log(2d)\gg_M d/\log(2d)\) already suffices.
Each fixed degree and measure bound gives finitely many monic integer polynomials by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds, so the final bounded-span exclusion follows for any sequence of distinct Salem numbers.
