### Logarithmic derivative and the three slopes

For each distinct primitive factor \(f\) of \(T\), vanishing at all its roots gives \(f\mid B\) over \(\mathbb Q\) and hence an integral quotient by primitivity and Gauss's lemma.
Its contribution \(k_f X f'(B/f)\) is integral even when \(f\) is nonmonic.
Over \(\mathbb C\) the same sum counts each root \(z\) with multiplicity and uses \(XB/(X-z)\).
For \(|z|\le1\), descending synthetic division expresses every quotient coefficient as a sum of at most \(n\) coefficients of \(B\) times nonnegative powers of \(z\).
For \(|z|>1\) the recurrence from the constant term uses strictly negative powers instead, giving the same \(n\|B\|_\infty\) bound.
Thus \(C=B(ET/T)\) has degree at most \(n\) and coefficient norm at most \(en\|B\|_\infty\).
At each finite place the integral coordinates \([C(x):B(x)]\) have maximum at most \(\max(1,|x|)^n\), and at archimedean places the extra factor is at most \((n+1)\max(1,en)\|B\|_\infty\).
Taking the projective height gives (2).

The original short row of L206 or its formal reversal is a multiple of the final row after the fixed form, endpoint monomial and content are removed.
Its coefficient bound survives reversal, and the nonzero evaluation is preserved by inversion of \(\beta\) and the unit monomial.
With \(n\le4d\) and \(h_{\rm W}(\beta)=ph/d\), (2) and the elementary height inequalities give \(h_{\rm W}(w)\ll L_l\), including the extra factors \(2p\) and \(J\le4pd+1\).
Dividing the derivative jet by the value jet gives \(v/u=w(1-\epsilon\alpha)/(1+\epsilon\alpha)\) for odd span.
For even span the negative chart gives \(w/(2\Delta)\), and the positive chart gives \((\Delta w/2-q)/(q^2-4)\).
All denominators are nonzero by nontorsion and \(u\ne0\), and their heights are bounded by an absolute constant plus \(O(h/d)\).
This verifies the slope estimate for each chart, including the normalization by \(q^2-4\).

For the norm bound on \(u\), the first evaluation has a unit power of \(\alpha\) and the corresponding extra factor \(1-\epsilon\alpha,1\), or \(\Delta/2\).
The nonzero algebraic integers \(1-\epsilon\alpha\) and \(\Delta\) have absolute norms at least one.
The last chart therefore costs at most \(\log2\) on dividing the logarithmic norm by \(d\).
Since \(u\) lies in the trace field, \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}u=(N_+u)^2\) gives \(\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}u|/d=\log|N_+u|/m\).
For every nonzero trace-field element \(z\), summing the positive parts of its archimedean logarithms gives \(\log|N_+z|/m\le h_{\rm W}(z)\).
Applying this to \(v/u\) and using multiplicativity gives the bound for \(v\) without requiring a height bound for \(u\).

### Boxes for nonmonic factors

For a factor of degree \(s_f\) and positive leading coefficient \(a_f\), the side length \(a_f^{-N/s_f}/3\) bounds the modulus of a difference by that length at each real root, and by \(\sqrt2\) times that length at either member of a complex pair.
If \(V\ne0\) is a difference in the same box, then
\[
 a_f^{\deg V}\prod_{f(z)=0}|V(z)|
 \le (\sqrt2/3)^{s_f}a_f^{\deg V-N}<1 .
\]
Unless \(f\mid V\) this is the absolute value of a nonzero integer resultant.
Counting both real coordinates per complex pair gives at most \(12(N+1)(K+1)\max(1,|z|)^N a_f^{N/s_f}\) intervals per real coordinate.
Multiplying the \(s_f\) bounds gives \(a_f^N\prod_{f(z)=0}\max(1,|z|)^N\) times the common prefactor \((12(N+1)(K+1))^{s_f}\).
Multiplication over the distinct factors gives the stated bound with \(N m(S)\), as \(S\) is primitive and squarefree.

With \(N=2d\), the choice of \(K\) bounds \(\log K\) by \((s/d)\log(12(N+1))+(N/d)m(S)\) for \(s>0\).
It also makes some fiber strictly larger than \((K+1)^{N+1-s-d}\).
If all its differences vanished at \(\alpha\), coprimality and the minimal polynomial would make them multiples of \(SP\).
The dimension of that rational coefficient space up to degree \(N\) is \(N+1-s-d\), which is positive even at \(s=d\).
Projection of the original fiber to an injective set of that many coordinate positions in its affine span bounds its size by the same power of \(K+1\), a contradiction.
An admissible nonvanishing multiple thus satisfies (3).
For constant primitive \(S\) the choice \(B=1\) suffices.

For \(T=X^rU_0(X+X^{-1})\), \(T(\alpha)=\alpha^r u\ne0\) and \(\deg T=2r<d\).
At \(\alpha\) its Euler logarithmic derivative is \(r+\Delta U'_0(q)/u\).
Combining (2) and (3) with \(h_{\rm W}(\alpha)=h/d\) bounds the height of \(U'_0(q)/u\) by \(O(\log(2d)+h+m(S_u))\).
For constant \(U_0\) that derivative is zero.
The applicable formula for \(D/u\), the slope estimate and multiplicativity of norms give the final inequality in (1).
In the specified fourth-logarithmic window, \(L_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}/p\to0\) on the stated shrinking sequences.
This gives the sufficient condition including \(m(S_u)=0\), and a positive lower bound on the normalized remainder norm along such a sequence forces \(m(S_u)\gg p\).

### Fixed-input example

The two trace embeddings of \(q=1+\sqrt2\) have quadratic polynomial \(t^2-2t-1\).
The negative discriminant at \(q'=1-\sqrt2\in(-2,2)\) gives the irreducible quartic lift displayed in L215.
Taking fifth powers preserves the two distinct real moduli and cannot identify the two neutral conjugates since they are a nontorsion inverse pair.
An integer annihilating \(R^2/\Gamma\) puts \((bq^j,bq^{j+1})\) in the jet lattice for every \(j\).
The lift in L199 has nonzero row value there, so a parameter avoiding its finitely many coprimality and endpoint exceptions and coprime to the reduced row content gives a primitive coprime lift of degree \(2d\).
The two absolute trace norms equal \(b^2\) because \(N_+q=-1\), and their slope is \(q\).

Substitution of \(U_0(t)=b(a_j+c_jt)\) gives the two displayed remainder limits upon division by \(bq^j\), since \(c_j/q^j\to1/(q-q')\).
The first limit is positive as \(q>2\), and the second is strictly negative as \(q'<2\).
Their product thus gives \(\log|N_+D_j|/2=j\log q+O(1)\).
A common prime divisor of \(a_j,c_j\) would make its square divide \(N_+(q^j)=\pm1\).
Also \(a_j+c_jq'=(q')^j\), so \(-a_j/c_j\to q'\) and \(c_j>0\).
Eventually \(c_jX^2+a_jX+c_j\) is primitive with two distinct circle roots and gives precisely \(S_u\), with logarithmic measure \(\log c_j=j\log q+O(1)\).
This example allows unrestricted cofactor cost and leaves the uniform squarefree bound for the two low-cofactor rows open.
