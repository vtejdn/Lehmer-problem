### The resultant and the leading coefficients

A common nonzero root of the normalized rows would be algebraic and its minimal polynomial would give a common nonunit Laurent factor.
At zero at least one row has a nonzero constant coefficient.
Thus each \(F(\alpha,Z)\) is a nonzero polynomial for every complex \(\alpha\).
When \(s>0\), the leading polynomial \(c(Y)\) has finitely many zeros.
Choose \(y\) outside them, and then choose \(z\) outside them and outside the zero sets of \(F(\alpha,Z)\) for the finitely many roots of \(P_y\).
The specialized Sylvester determinant is nonzero, so the integral parameter resultant is nonzero.
This argument includes rows for which a generic \(P_y\) itself has multiple roots.

For a nonzero integral polynomial on a product torus the positive part of its logarithm is bounded.
The leading coefficient in one variable is nonzero almost everywhere in the other variables, by inducting on the number of variables.
The inner Jensen lower bound by its logarithm bounds the integral of the negative part by a finite quantity, using the inductive integrability of the coefficient logarithm.
The same induction gives nonnegativity of the total logarithmic measure, starting with a nonzero integer.
This verifies joint absolute integrability for \(F,R\) and integrability of the leading logarithm for \(c\).
In particular the zero set of \(R\), which contains the parameter diagonal, is negligible for the independent product measure.
Fubini identifies \(\mathbb E_y m_X(P_y)\) with \(I\).

For almost every pair the resultant has absolute value
\[
 |c(y)|^s|c(z)|^s\prod_{i,j}|\alpha_i(y)-\alpha_j(z)|.
\]
Reflecting the roots into the closed disk bounds each normalized distance from above by \(|1-w_i(y)\overline{w_j(z)}|\).
For roots lying on opposite sides this is equality, and on the same side it follows from the disk identity in the cited ledger.
Shrinking by \(q\) leaves a lower bound of \(\sqrt q\) times the normalized distance.
Taking negative logarithms over the \(s^2\) pairs gives the term \(-s^2\log q/2\).
Each \(\log h_i(y)\) occurs \(s\) times, and the leading coefficient contributes \(s\log|c(y)|\), giving precisely \(s\,m_X(P_y)\).
The \(z\)-terms give \(s\,m_X(P_z)\) by the same count.
This recovers (3) also when the slices have nonunit leading coefficients of varying magnitude.

As an equality check, take \(F(X,Y)=c(Y)(X-Y)^s\) with \(c(Y)=(2Y+3)(Y-1)\).
The rows have no common base zero, and the entire slice vanishes at the exceptional parameter \(1\).
All roots of a generic slice coincide, yet \(R(y,z)=c(y)^s c(z)^s(y-z)^{s^2}\).
Here \(V(t)=I=\log3\) and \(m_2(R)=2s\log3\), so both sides of the first inequality of (1) vanish.

### Averaged kernel and boundary Fourier series

On the set of generic parameters the root multiset, counted with multiplicity, depends continuously on the coefficients after division by \(c(y)\).
Reflection gives a measurable family of counting measures of mass \(s\) with mean \(\sigma\).
For fixed \(0<q<1\), the kernel \(-\log|1-qw\bar v|\) has a uniformly absolutely convergent series of real parts on the two closed disks.
Independence of \(y,z\) factors the integrated term of order \(\ell\) into \(\left|\int w^\ell\,d\sigma\right|^2\).
Averaging (3) and applying monotone convergence to the resulting nonnegative series gives \(\mathcal E(\sigma)\le2sI-m_2(R)\).
Only the regularized kernels are integrated in this step.

On the circle the factorization of \(P_y\) subtracts the constant \(m_X(P_y)\) when each root is replaced by its reflected logarithm.
Each reflected kernel has zero mean and positive part at most \(\log2\), hence \(L^1\) norm at most \(2\log2\), including for a root of modulus one.
This uniform bound times the mass \(s\) justifies Fubini in the formula for \(V(t)-I\).
For \(0<\rho<1\) the difference of the kernels at \(w,\rho w\) has zero mean and positive part at most \(\log(2/(1+\rho))\), uniformly for \(|w|\le1\).
The \(L^1\) loss is consequently at most twice this quantity.
Passing each Fourier coefficient to the boundary gives magnitude \(\left|\int w^\ell\,d\sigma\right|/(2\ell)\) for each of the two signs of \(\ell\).
The function \(V\) is bounded by the coefficient estimates since the continuous \(B\) has a positive minimum.
Parseval now gives the weight \(1/(2\ell^2)\) after adding both signs.
Comparison with the energy weight \(1/\ell\) proves (1), including the sign and coefficient of \(m_2(R)\).

### Coefficient bounds and scope

The triangle inequality gives \(\log B\ge V-\log(M+1)\), and the elementary symmetric formulas give \(\log B\le V+M\log2\) for every nonzero fiber in \(Y\), including constant fibers.
Since \(I\ge0\), one has \(V^-\le(V-I)^-\), and the latter integrates to \(\|V-I\|_1/2\) by centering.
Cauchy–Schwarz in normalized measure and (1) therefore give \(L_-\le\log(M+1)+\sqrt{sI}/2\).
Using \(L_+-L_-=\int\log B\) gives the second inequality of (2).
When \(s=0\), \(V\) is constant and \(B\) is the maximum absolute value of nonzero integer coefficients.
Base Laurent monomial shifts preserve the moduli, and removing a common factor \(H(X)\) subtracts \(m(H)\) from both measures when the diagonal specialization is nonzero.

For width \(s\le2d\) the resulting estimate removes the logarithmic term under the square root in v328.
The two coprime cyclotomic products there have \(I=O(\sqrt k)\) and \(L_-\ge\binom{2k-2}{k-1}/4-O(\sqrt k)\), so they still exclude width-independent linear control by \(1+I\).
Neither that example nor the new estimate decides divergence at fixed positive \(I\) or gives a comparison of diagonal and independent means for arbitrary moving coefficients.
