## Classification and discriminant contribution of each repeated root

For odd \(p\), a multiple section root \(u\) gives \(y=u^{p^a}\), \(D(y)=0\), and \(u^{p^b+\epsilon}=-y^2/(2A(y))\).
Conversely these equations force both the section and its derivative to vanish.
The constant and leading coefficients of \(D\) are units at every odd prime, and its reciprocal symmetry and rational coefficients put \(u^\epsilon\) and \(u^{p^b}\) among its roots.
Their product is therefore a root of the first resultant even if roots of \(D\) coalesce after reduction.
The content \(2^{16}\) is invertible and the remaining squarefree groups have degrees \(8,8,2\), multiplicities \(2,4,8\), and leading coefficients \(16,4,1\).
Thus every such product annuls \(H\), including at the odd primes of bad reduction of \(D\).
The defining equation gives \(4A(y)A^*(y)=y^4\ne0\), so clearing the denominator retains every collision.
I recomputed the positive second resultant using the expanded homogeneous sum and recovered the stated factorization, including \(6644192153^4\).
The program checks the product as an integer identity and tests every possible divisor from two through the integer square root of each candidate prime.

I ran the submitted program with SymPy 1.14.0 and independently enumerated the section coordinate \(u\) itself over each distinct irreducible factor of \(D\) at all ten odd candidate primes.
For a factor of degree \(d\), coefficient-vector multiplication modulo its monic lift and \(p^2\) gave the Teichmüller representative \(U=Y^{p^d}\).
Indeed \(Y^{p^d-1}\) reduces to one, so its \(p^d\)-th power equals one modulo \(p^2\).
For \(1\le a,b\le d\) and both signs, I used the positive integer exponents \(p^a\) and \(p^b+\epsilon\) directly, testing both \(F\) and its vertical derivative modulo \(p\).
These representatives exhaust the exponents since \(U^{p^d}=U\).
This calculation returned all displayed trace factors with their listed signs and residue classes, the two endpoints at 5, and no other collisions.
It also returned exact valuation one for all 12 nonendpoint evaluations at 3, all 12 at 7 and all four at 79.
The signed exponent reductions in the program agree with these direct powers because \(p^d\equiv1\pmod{p^d-1}\).
Substituting \(b=2a\) in the positive rows leaves precisely the indicated linear factor at 79.

At an odd collision the quadratic in the vertical coordinate has a unit quadratic term after translation to its double root.
The vertical coordinate changes to first order in a section parameter, whereas the horizontal coordinate changes only in order \(p^a\ge3\), giving exact multiplicity two.
The trace map has local degree two at either endpoint and degree one elsewhere, identifying inverse orientations in the same trace disk.
Thus the endpoints at 5 are simple in the trace polynomial.
In characteristic two the section derivative is nonzero at every root since its vertical derivative is \(y^2\), \(y\ne0\), and the vertical exponent is odd.
A repeated trace root would pull back to a multiple section root there as well.

Extend the unramified base until all trace residues split, normalizing \(v_p(p)=1\).
Hensel factorization separates each double residue from all other disks, whose factors evaluate to units at the selected trace lift.
Since the monomial relating the section and trace is a unit there, the lifted monic quadratic translated to that lift has constant term of valuation one and linear coefficient divisible by \(p\).
Its discriminant has valuation one because \(p\) is odd.
The resultants between distinct disks are units, so the total discriminant valuations are two at the indicated rows for 3 and 7 and one at the indicated rows for 79.
Every other trace reduction is squarefree and contributes valuation zero at its base prime.
A common residue of two monic integral factors would belong to one of the double disks and force the product at its lift to have valuation at least two.
This establishes the coprimality conclusion for every factorization in the stated family.
