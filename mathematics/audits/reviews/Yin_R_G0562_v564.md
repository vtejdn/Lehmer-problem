I received the fixed claim and scope without its development discussion and took no part in its construction.
I checked the new rank and recurrence arguments directly and consulted the [earlier exterior-root review](Yin_R_G0562_v562.md#finding), the [review of the real and conjugate cases](Yin_R_G0562_v562.md#conjugates), and the reviews of the [jet estimate](Yin_R_G0562_v543.md#finding) and [signed projections](Yin_R_G0562_v552.md#finding) for their respective inputs.
The proof gives the claimed slope \(Th\) on a set having bounded gaps in \(n\equiv-1\bmod q\), for every fixed reciprocal nontorsion unit satisfying the power-degree hypothesis, every finite nonempty set of positive powers and every fixed modulus \(q\).

### Rank at a repeated radius

Let \(s=\log R>0\) and \(w_i\) be the integer coefficient pairs after reversing both rows and removing a common monomial.
The nonzero pair \(w_0\) ensures that the upper envelope \(L(s)\) of the limiting quantities \(n^{-1}\log |w_i|-is\) is nonnegative.
On a subsequence with normalized log norm bounded by \(C\), choose \(J\) with \((J+1)\min s>C+2\).
The row norm bounds even the differentiated tail at every selected radius by \(O(n(J+1)e^{n(C-(J+1)s)})\), regardless of the formal width.
If the extreme maximizing indices are \(k,k+j\), the normalizing factor in the original value equation has magnitude
\[
 R^{-kn}\max_{0\le\ell\le j}\bigl(|w_{k+\ell}|R^{-\ell n}\bigr)=\exp(nL(s)+o(n)).
\]
All discarded terms are exponentially small relative to it, including after subtracting \(nk\) times the value equation.
After passage to a subsequence the normalized coefficients give a limiting pair \(U,V\) of degree at most \(j\) with maximum coefficient magnitude one.
At a double node \(c\), the limiting equations at \(z=\lim(R/c)^n\) are \(U(z)=cV(z)\) and \(U'(z)=cV'(z)\), since \(z\ne0\) and the undifferentiated \(cV\) term is divided by \(n\).
For a single double node with \(j=0\), the unscaled equations instead bound \(v_k\) and then \(u_k\) by exponentially small multiples of \(|w_k|\).

At alignment the \(m\) double nodes have distinct \(z_i=c_i/R\).
If \(m\ge2\) and \(j\le m-1\), write \(L_0(Y)=\prod_i(Y-z_i)\).
The value conditions give \(U-RYV=uL_0\), and its derivative at the nodes gives \(RV(z_i)=-uL_0'(z_i)\).
Interpolation gives \(RV=-uL_0'\) and \(U=u(L_0-YL_0')\).
The leading coefficient of the latter is \(u(1-m)\), so the degree bound forces \(u=0\).
With \(m\) double nodes and two additional simple nodes, \(j\le m\) makes \(U-RYV\) a polynomial of degree at most \(m+1\) with \(m+2\) zeros.
Then \(U=RYV\), and \(V\) has degree at most \(m-1\) and all \(m\) double nodes as zeros.
For \(m=0\), \(U=RYV\) already excludes a nonzero constant pair.
Thus both interpolation maps just considered are injective at alignment.
A nonzero full-column minor stays nonzero on a closed neighborhood of this configuration, where a sequence of normalized coefficient pairs has a nonzero limit after extraction.
Intersecting the finitely many neighborhoods works for the full list and every one-factor reduction.

### The progression and the cost of a common factor

With \(g=(|c|/c)_c\) and \(K=\overline{\langle g^q\rangle}\), take the neighborhood \(O\) of \(g^{-1}\) in the claim and put \(V_0=K\cap gO\).
The nonnegative powers of \(g^q\) are dense in \(K\), including when \(K\) is finite.
The translates \((g^q)^{-j}V_0\), \(j\ge0\), cover \(K\).
Taking the largest \(j\) in a finite subcover bounds the forward waiting time from every position in the progression.
This proves the claimed bounded gaps for \(n=qj-1\) with \(n\ge2\).

The breakpoint identity \(L(0)-\gamma_0=\sum_{s>0}s\,\Delta k(s)\) now counts at least one copy of \(\log|c|\) for every exterior double node, including all collisions of radii.
Each \(P_a\) has measure \(ah\), and the \(P_a\) are pairwise distinct because \(h>0\).
Thus on the return set any bounded-slope subsequence of graphs with the full factors has limiting lower slope at least \(Th\).
Let \(Q_a\) be the minimum of \(\delta^{an}\).
The full power degrees give \(m(Q_a)=nah\) and distinguish the \(Q_a\) for different \(a\).
Consequently dividing out one common \(Q_a\) removes exactly one order at each root of \(P_a\) and preserves multiplicity at the other factors, by separability of \(Q_a(X^n)\).
If there is a nonreal exterior conjugate \(x\), the two powers \(x^a,\bar x^a\) stay distinct, by the degree hypothesis.
The rank calculation with these two simple nodes adds a jump at their shared radius beyond the number of remaining double nodes there, yielding the strictly positive gain \(a\log|x|\) over \((T-a)h\).
If all exterior conjugates are real, \(|x|^a=|y|^b\) implies \(x^{2a}=y^{2b}\).
Positive absolute height forces \(a=b\) and the power-degree condition then gives \(x=y\).
All retained double nodes and any chosen extra simple node have distinct radii.
The [algebraic norm argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#simple) gives the required strict gain in this case, including an empty list of double nodes.

### Attainment and the count

At width \(w=b-1\), the two-row box of \(l=2(w+1)\) coefficients has maximum exponent at most \(n(w+1)\).
Counting values and first divided derivatives in all the fixed fields gives leading term \(nTh\,l/(l-J)\) in the log norm, with \(J=2\sum_a\deg P_a\).
This count uses integrality and the fixed measures and applies before imposing a test budget.
For \(w\to\infty,\ w=o(n)\), the remaining logarithmic terms are \(o(n)\).
At a sufficiently large fixed \(w\), the bound gives any chosen positive excess for every sufficiently large \(n\).
For a hypothetical common \(Q_a\), restoring it raises the log Mahler measure of a largest quotient row by \(nah\), and that row's log one-norm exceeds its log measure by at most \(w\log2\).
The strict lower gain therefore contradicts the interpolated bound, choosing the fixed-width excess below every gain when needed.
The value equation then gives nonzero values for both rows at each powered node.

The two reciprocal projections preserve all \(P_a^2\).
Multiplying by \(Y+1\) or \(Y-1\) gives the desired sign at width \(w+1\), and these multipliers do not vanish at nontorsion nodes.
At each powered node the two pre-multiplier projections sum to twice the nonzero first row value.
Consequently at most \(|I|\) parameters are forbidden in combining the projected rows, and some integer between zero and \(|I|\) works at bounded multiplicative cost.
This proves existence and the required upper slope for either sign on the same set of exponents.
Nonempty sets of positive integer one-norms have a minimum.

If \(\lambda<B/(2T)\), factorization and the small-measure reduction give a reciprocal unit minimum with \(0<h<B/(2T)\).
Here I used [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) at statement scope.
For the [norm replacement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm), raising the relative norm to the chosen common torsion exponent gives a power of the field generator, so every positive power of the norm generates that field.
Multiplication of absolute height by the relative degree exactly cancels the reduction of degree and preserves \(h\), while the inversion automorphism preserves reciprocity.
Choose the fixed interpolation excess inside \(2Th<B\) and the gains just proved.
The multiplier \(Y^k(Y+1)^e\), \(e\in\{0,1\}\), pads to any larger prescribed width at a norm cost at most two and is nonzero at each powered node.
Since \(\log(2nb+1)=o(n)\), the strict test budget holds eventually on the progression return set.
One row there realizes the \(r\) distinct values \(h,\ldots,rh\), so bounded gaps preclude arbitrarily long consecutive blocks in that progression with counts below \(r\).
For \(r=3\), \(2T=12\) and the resulting conditional lower bound is \((\log2)/48\).
The argument leaves the block exclusion to be proved and its waiting bound depends on the fixed input.
