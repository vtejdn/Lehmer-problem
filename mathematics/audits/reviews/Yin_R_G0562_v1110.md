The algebraic equivalence, the splitting-field inclusion and the identification of the detected records hold with the stated quantifiers.

Write \(H_w\) for the group of products of integer powers of all conjugates of \(w\).
Its saturation after adjoining all roots of unity consists precisely of the \(x\) for which \(x^b\in H_w\) for some positive integer \(b\), since one further power removes any torsion multiplier.
For each oriented Salem root \(\gamma\), the conditional norm formula gives a coefficient \(a/(2e)\), where \(e=[\mathbb Q(w,\gamma):\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\) and \(\rho/\tau\rho=\pm\gamma^a\).
Both \(\rho\) and \(\tau\rho\) belong to \(H_w\), as each embedding of the compositum defining the norm extends to the common normal field and the group of products is Galois stable.
A nonzero coefficient gives \(\gamma^{2|a|}\in H_w\), also for the inverse orientation.
Conjugating yields \(\alpha^{2|a|}\in H_w\).
Every element of \(H_w\) belongs to the splitting field of \(w\).
For \(j>0\), the inclusion \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\subset\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) is equality since the only conjugate with \(j\)-th power \(\alpha^j\) is the unique dominant root \(\alpha\), so the chosen embedding of the smaller field has just one extension.
Normality of the splitting field of \(w\) now places every conjugate of \(\alpha\) in that field.

Conversely a product relation \(\alpha^b=\prod_\sigma \sigma(w)^{c_\sigma}\) gives the same linear relation among the real functions \(f\) with integer coefficients and \(b>0\).
Simultaneous invariance gives \(\langle f_{\sigma(w)},f_\alpha\rangle=\langle f_w,f_{\sigma^{-1}(\alpha)}\rangle\).
Vanishing for every tested conjugate would make \(b\langle f_\alpha,f_\alpha\rangle=0\), contradicted by the positive value of \(\log\alpha\) at the real Salem embedding.
For torsion \(w\) all these products remain torsion and all its logarithms vanish.
Every nonprimitive Salem number is a positive power of the positive primitive relative unit in the same Salem field, including at degree four by the same rank-one kernel and embedding argument.
Membership in the saturation is equivalent for that number and its primitive generator.

At a detected strict record with \(d\ge6\) and \(m(w)<c_{\mathbb R}\), the comparison gives \(\deg\alpha\le d\) and \(\log\alpha\le m(w)\).
Minimality among positive measures through degree \(d\) forces equality of measures, and first attainment then forces \(\deg\alpha=d=2n\).
The signed-vector orbit lies below this same bound \(2n\).
Full support is impossible for \(n\ge5\) because \(2^{n-1}>2n\).
At proper support size \(k\), independent choices of signs on that support are realized using an unused label to correct parity, and transitivity gives at least \(n/k\) support sets.
Thus \(2^k\le2k\), leaving \(k=1,2\).

For \(k=2\), every support set contributes at least four distinct signed vectors, giving at most \(n/2\) sets in the orbit.
Their union contains all \(n\) labels by transitivity, so equality is necessary, \(n\) is even and the sets partition the labels into pairs.
The label action is the action on the real trace embeddings, and its partition is invariant in the Galois group of the trace splitting field because the common normal field maps onto this group.
The point stabilizer \(J\) at the chosen trace preserves its unique pair.
The setwise pair stabilizer \(I\) acts transitively on that pair since any group element carrying the point to its partner preserves the unique pair containing the partner.
Consequently \([I:J]=2\), and the corresponding fixed fields are \(C\subset B\) with \([B:C]=2\).
This separable quadratic extension supplies a nonidentity rational automorphism of \(B\).
The trace \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) meets the whole-base theorem's cutoff \(H=c_{\mathbb R}\) because its exceptional-place parameter is exactly \(\log\alpha=m(w)\).
Consequently \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(B)=1\) at all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly for the records in question.

The vector stabilizer therefore equals the point stabilizer of the oriented root.
The stabilizer of \(w\) is contained in it and both have index \(2n\) in the common Galois group.
Equality of stabilizers gives \(\mathbb Q(w)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) in the chosen algebraic closure.
Transporting both to the embedding carrying \(\gamma\) to \(\alpha\) preserves measure and degree.
The small-element classification in this Salem field applies since \(0<m(w)<c_{\mathbb R}<\log 2\) and \(d\) is large.
The quadratic exceptions have smaller degree, so the transported \(w\) is \(\pm\alpha^j\) for a nonzero integer \(j\), using primitivity of \(\alpha\).
Its measure is \(|j|\log\alpha\), by preservation of degree under powers and the single dominant conjugate.
Thus \(|j|=1\), and the inverse is itself a conjugate, as required after returning to the original embedding.
