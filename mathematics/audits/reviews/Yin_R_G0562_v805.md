# Independent review of the Archimedean count for terminal exponents

## The unrestricted count and both witness estimates hold

At 2026-11-20T22:15 UTC, an agent outside proof development independently examined the complete [Archimedean count and its consequences](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v668.md#statement) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its review scope, with no proof-development dialogue, author explanation or earlier verdict on this claim.
All three conclusions follow under the stated hypotheses.

At the chosen embedding, conjugation preserves both the equation with rational coefficients and every test for a vanishing subsum.
If successive moduli all increased by factors greater than two, their geometric tail below the largest would have total modulus strictly less than the largest.
Stopping with at least two roots makes every merged component a proper subset of the original labels, so its sum is nonzero.
Any proper vanishing collection of surviving components would expand to a forbidden subsum.
Completing a forest to a full labeled tree and marking its component roots recovers every forest among at most \(2^{2r}(2r-3)!!\) pairs of trees and subsets.
After the shifts inside the children are known, comparability restricts their representative difference to a closed interval of length \(2\log(2)/H\), independently of the chronology of operations in disjoint components.
For \(k\) roots, division by one root yields a nondegenerate inhomogeneous equation with \(k-1\) variables and fixed nonzero coefficients in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
The tuple group has rank \(k-1\), and its powers identify their integer exponents because \(|\gamma|\ne1\).
These are precisely the dimension, rank and nondegeneracy hypotheses of the retained Amoroso–Viada statement, giving the stated \(B_k\), while two roots allow at most one difference.

For a given increasing list, equal signed coefficients can permute the distinct exponents freely.
Translation cannot identify two of these assignments because it would preserve their finite set of exponents.
Sorting recovers the underlying list class, so distinct classes have disjoint orbits.
Removing two largest coefficient magnitudes leaves exactly the weights in \(H_2^2\) in the multinomial bound.
The remaining factor from tree counts and \(r(r-1)\) is bounded by an absolute exponential after division by the factorials.
This proves the upper count uniformly in the span, including both endpoints \(k=2,r\).

Terminality gives distinct positions for \(r\ge4\), and for each of the at most \(r\) possible places of the mark, translating it to zero uniquely recovers a witness.
Cancelling the same \(H_2^2\) in both counts and using \(1+2\log(2)/H=O_\eta(1/m)\) leaves \((k-2)L\le Ar+Dk^5\log(2k)\).
With \(k=r<L\), division by \(r\) first gives \(L\ll 1+r^4\log(2+L)\), while \(r\ge L\) already suffices for the preliminary lower bound.
A sufficiently small constant multiple of \((L/\log(2+L))^{1/4}\), rounded down, is then an admissible \(k\) and absorbs the last term into \(kL/4\).
As \(k\) tends to infinity, the remaining inequality yields the claimed \(L^{5/4}/(\log(2+L))^{1/4}\) bound.

For a fixed union of \(v\) residues, a residue vector and a translation class of quotient arrays determine at most one original labeled translation class, since changing a quotient representative translates all original entries by a common multiple of \(q\).
A labeled solution can admit several such representations, which only enlarges the upper count.
Permuting equal original coefficients preserves membership in the fixed union when all \(v^r\) residue vectors are counted together.
The node interval for \(\gamma^q\) has length at most one, giving at most two choices even at integer endpoints.
The fixed-union bound and \(\binom qv\le(eq/v)^v\) therefore give the claimed upper bound on the exceptional proportion after accounting for the mark.
For a unit, the sum of all positive logarithmic conjugate radii and the sum of the absolute values of the negative ones are both \(m\), whence \(\eta m\le H\le m\) and \(\log q=L+O_\eta(1)\).
The weight estimate gives \(L=o(r)\).
Choosing \(v=\lfloor\delta\min(r,q)\rfloor\) leaves a negative term of magnitude at least \((1-\delta)r\log(1/\delta)\), which dominates the uniform \(O_{c,\eta}(r)\) error by the margin needed for \(e^{-r}\).
For \(r=o(q)\) and \(v=\lfloor(1-\epsilon)r\rfloor\), the same magnitude divided by \(r\) diverges for every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1\).
The conclusions apply to the original marked witnesses, including their variable occupied classes.

The review accepted Amoroso–Viada's theorem at the level of its inspected statement and did not re-audit its source proof.
The weighted witness lower bound was reused at its existing scope for neutral or near-neutral inputs.
Neither conclusion on shrinking sequences asserts the existence or impossibility of such a sequence.
