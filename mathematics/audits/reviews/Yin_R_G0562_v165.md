## Collinearity, the two thresholds and the total fiber height

At 2026-11-17T05:04 UTC, an agent outside proof development checked the complete [v165 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v165.md#decision-modular-lift-doubling-submitted) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and its dependencies and consulted no development discussion, author explanations or earlier conclusions on v165.

For two pairs with the same projected sum, their difference \((\Delta x,\Delta y)\) satisfies \(H\Delta x=-N\Delta y\).
Coprimality gives \(H\mid\Delta y\), and \(|\Delta y|\le2R<H\) forces both differences to vanish.
Projection therefore gives a bijection of the two sumsets.
This uses just the coordinate representation in v163 and works for composite \(H\) as well.

In the noncollinear case let \(e,t,b\) count edges, triangles and boundary vertices of the triangulation.
Including all points along the boundary in \(b\), one has \(k-e+t=1\) and \(3t=2e-b\), hence \(e=3k-3-b\).
Every edge midpoint lies in precisely one open edge and no vertex lies in its interior.
Doubling the \(e\) midpoints and \(k\) vertices thus exhibits \(4k-3-b\ge3k-3\) distinct sums.
The independent count agrees with the planar specialization of Freiman's Lemma 1.14 as stated in [Matolcsi and Ruzsa, Theorem 1.1](https://math.bme.hu/~matolcsi/hullarxiv.pdf).

On the resulting line the projection has an affine inverse whose direction \(w\) satisfies \(s w\in\mathbb Z^2\) for every \(s\in S\).
An integral linear combination of the positive \(s\) equals one, so \(w=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2\).
The equation \(Hu+Nv=1\) implies \(v\ne0\) because \(H\ge2\).
For \(k=2\) the normalized support is exactly \(\{0,1\}\), giving the same direction by subtraction.
For an interval of diameter \(d\), the strict inequality \(k>(2d+4)/3\) gives \(2d+1<3k-3\) under the same width assumption.
The two-row example contains 0 and 1 and has three noncollinear lift points already at \(h=2\).
For every \(h\ge2\), the pair sums occupy the three adjacent integer intervals of length \(H=2h-1\) starting at \(0,H,2H\).
Their total is \(3H=3k-3\), and \(d=3h-2\) gives \(3k=2d+4\), while \(2R=H-1\) still satisfies the strict width assumption.
The same example establishes sharpness of both thresholds.

At \(X=\xi\) the Laurent monomial multiplying \(P(\xi^uY^v)\) is a unit on the multiplicative group.
For each nonzero root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), the power equation gives \(|v|\) distinct preimages, including when \(v<0\).
Roots of unity have absolute value one at every place, and the product formula gives \(h(z^{-1})=h(z)\).
Thus every preimage has absolute height \(h(\alpha)/|v|\).
Characteristic zero and irreducibility give \(d\) distinct conjugate roots of equal height, and the primitive minimal-polynomial normalization gives \(d h(\alpha)=m(P)\), including for nonmonic \(P\).
Partitioning all preimages into relative Galois orbits leaves this exact total at every torsion order.
A proper subcollection contributes at most that amount.
For the archimedean logarithmic measure the monomial has modulus one on the circle and \(Y\mapsto\xi^uY^v\) preserves normalized circle measure for either sign of \(v\).
These arguments verify the full statement, with height equality for the complete fiber in the multiplicative group.
