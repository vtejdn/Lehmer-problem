## Independent whole-proof finding

At 2026-11-21T09:11 UTC an independent reviewer for Sheng Yin recommends acceptance of [the fixed v811 theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#statement), including its conditional irreducibility conclusion.
The reviewer had no part in developing the proof and checked both pole choices, the finite-ring argument, the losses and the use of the four asymptotic inputs below.

The unit relation at the pole is \(V=-c\), since \(F/U^2=V+c\) there.
The equality \(U^l=V^k\) gives precisely the ideal \((\gamma)\) in \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\), and conversely \(X=U^aV^b\) has the prescribed powers for \(ak+bl=1\).
Indeed \(c^k=(-1)^k\beta^l\) makes \(c\) invertible in that quotient, and \(|\beta'|<1\) ensures \(\gamma\ne0\).
At \(j=4\), \(\beta=2+\sqrt5\) generates the order of index two, and \(c=56\) makes two invertible in the quotient, so inverting two identifies it with the full-order quotient.
At \(j=3\) the discriminant is the fundamental discriminant 13.
The logarithmic derivative of \(F/U^2\) at the pole has contributions \(kw((2j-7)c^2+2j+7)\) from \(t\) and \(-lc\) from \(V\).
The coefficient of \(kw\) is respectively \(-1751\) and \(3151\), so \(D\ne0\) by irrationality.
Thus the common derivative quotient has size at most \(|N D|\), and imposing both complementary factors gives a quotient of this common derivative quotient.
As a numerical check at \((k,l)=(3,41)\), the full polynomial resultants in absolute value at the two poles are respectively \(139248280684889952977211047\) and \(8913408933958968438970672773119\), equal to the independently expanded quadratic norms.

Writing \(n_{\mathfrak p}=N\mathfrak p\), the local quotient by an individual factor has order \(n_{\mathfrak p}^{e_i}\).
The sum of the nonnegative excesses \((\sum_i e_i-e)\log n_{\mathfrak p}\) vanishes by multiplicativity of the resultant, hence every excess vanishes separately, even for factors listed with repetition.
Each summand of the product derivative is then divisible in the local fiber by the uniformizer to at least \(e-\max_i e_i\), an exponent at most \(e\).
This bounds the loss on the retained branch by the valuation of \(D\).
At each nonsplit prime conjugation fixes the ideal, so its entire contribution occurs in the ideal gcd with the conjugate.
At a split prime of exponents \(h,g\), the gcd contributes \(p^{2\min(h,g)}\) in norm and the discarded branch costs only \(p^{\min(h,g)}\).
The rational integer \(c^{2k}-(-1)^l\) belongs to that gcd, proving the first loss bound, including ramification.
At a retained split prime the completion is \(\mathbb Z_p\), and reducing the pole point to exponent \(\max_i e_i\) enforces all three congruences at that prime power.
The CRT then gives a single unit for each assigned product, and assignment to one index per rational prime gives pairwise coprimality and the individual divisibilities.

Factoring \(q_j(z^k)\) over its two roots identifies \(b_i\) with the absolute norm of the projected factor evaluated at \(\beta\).
The uniform pole estimate therefore gives \(\log b_i/d_i\to(\log\beta)/2\), and the degree estimate with one root gives \(\log l=o(d_i)\).
Each loss is nonnegative and at most the entire \(O(k+\log(2+k+l))\) loss, proving the individual asymptotic whenever \(k=o(d_i)\).
The uniform exterior count and its lower bound give \(d_i\gg(l/k)k(\log\log l/\log l)^3=B_l\) since \(2l+4k\sim2l\).
Condition (3) yields \(k=o(B_l)\) and \(\log(N/p^a)=o(B_l)\), so every modulus has logarithm at least a fixed positive multiple of \(B_l\) eventually.
If two factors were present, one modulus would be prime to \(p\) and would divide \(N/p^a\), even if \(p^a\) were a proper divisor of the full \(p\)-part.
This contradiction covers repeated factors as well.
The proof leaves open the existence of infinitely many qualifying directions.
