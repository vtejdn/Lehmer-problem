### What the local certificates supply

The arithmetic alternative used in v335 is the local statement in v334 about the gcd quotient of any occupied subset of the model rows.
Its proof applies with two or more occupied rows and allows the original polynomial to be reducible.
In particular, it does not require that any prescribed pair of rows in the full model be occupied.
In the finite comparison of v334, the separated-frequency Vandermonde estimate uses the full list with zero coefficients allowed, and its normalization uses the nonzero coefficient vector.
The swept-zero packing, real-coordinate threshold count and local disk bounds apply after the gcd is removed.
The frequency tests on the far larger Fourier box then compare the clipped means.
I checked that the parameter choices in v334 (13)–(14) make the two clipping and Fourier approximation terms at most \(g_R/128+2g_R/128\), the additive \(d/N\) error at most \(g_R/128\), and its coefficient of \(I\) at most \(1/16\).
Thus (17) gives \(\mu\ge (15/16)I-g_R/32\).
The displayed \(g_R\) is a positive rational lower bound for \(c_R\).

The primitive integer separating direction distinguishes every occupied fast exponent.
After the unimodular coordinate change the highest coefficient in that variable is one slow row times a Laurent monomial in the other fast variables.
An irreducible factor independent of the separating variable must thus be a factor of the slow row up to a Laurent monomial.
It would divide every slow row, which the primitive gcd excludes, also over the algebraic closure.
Hence any nontorsion component has positive width in the distinguished variable and yields \(I\ge c_R\), contradicting \(\mu<g_R/2\).
Otherwise the primitive lift factors, with multiplicities, into integral cyclotomic polynomials in characters and a signed Laurent monomial.
Their nonzero fast vectors \(v\) lie in the coordinate-width box because \(\varphi(e)|v_j|\le w_j\) and \(\varphi(e)|c|\le2d\) by width additivity.
The Fourier cutoff contains this box, so rounding gives \(|v\cdot q|\ge\rho N/2>Jd\) since \(d/N\le e\le\rho/(4J)\).
The cutoff is chosen before \(\rho\), and the prescribed margin \(J\) enters only in the final width allowance.
These checks give the stated torsion alternative with \(J=2L+4\). [Local certificate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#certificate)

For each point of the compact ordered center polytope, a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of the additive group generated by its entries gives a full-column-rank integer matrix with first row zero and last row nonzero.
The basis coordinates are rationally independent.
After choosing a separation \(\gamma\) for the distinct rows, the finitely many Fourier tests at the computed cutoff therefore all admit a common positive dyadic lower bound \(\rho\).
The projection error at the chosen point is zero, so this certificate contains it whatever positive tolerance remains after \(J\) is prescribed.
All certificate regions are open relative to the polytope and have rational linear membership tests.
Compactness gives a finite subcover, and rational feasibility of the complement detects it in the enumerated finite stages.
Consequently \(\epsilon,D,g,B\) in v335 (2) are computable from \(k,L\), without requiring numerical execution of the search. [Finite cover](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#cover)

### Initial cases and support assignment

Replacing each interval by the span of its occupied integer exponents makes its width at most \(\lfloor W\rfloor\), and discarding empty intervals preserves the covering condition.
With one interval, \(0\) and \(N\) both belong to it, so \(Q=P\) works even for a nonzero constant.
The same choice handles \(N\le W/\epsilon\) or \(N<D\) with integer \(W\ge1\) under the stated choice of \(C\).
At \(N<D,\ W=0\), the elementary bound (20) of v334 applies to reducible inputs as well.
Indeed for \(1<M(P)<2\) an exterior root \(\alpha\) is an algebraic integer and \(\alpha\bar\alpha\) has degree at most \(D^2\).
Its conjugates are products of roots, all of modulus below four, so the nonzero integer norm of \(1-\alpha\bar\alpha\) gives \(|\alpha|^2-1\ge5^{1-D^2}\).
This implies \(m(P)\ge 5^{1-D^2}/8\), while \(M(P)\ge2\) already exceeds the threshold.
Thus \(m(P)<\eta\) forces measure zero here, and Kronecker's theorem gives ordinary cyclotomic factors with total totient degree \(N<D\le C\).
Because \(P(0)\ne0\), there is no zero root and \(Q=P(0)=\pm1\) has both required end-block properties. [Measure-one characterization](../literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001)

In the remaining case the endpoint representatives \(0,N\) are in different original intervals since \(W\le\epsilon N<N\).
Rounding the rational model coordinates assigns an exponent in the interval of its representative to within \((\epsilon+\tau)N+A_1\) of its scalar fast frequency.
The ceiling gives \(d/N<e\) by (14) and (16) of v334.
The endpoint rows are different by the projection tolerance, and distinct scalar fast frequencies differ by at least \(\gamma N>2d\).
The specialized bands are therefore ordered and disjoint.
Assigning each exponent once to an original interval and merging representatives at the same model row preserves all coefficients.
Duplicating a representative for the length-\(k\) list requires no additional occupied exponents.
The band containing \(0\) is the first occupied one, and the band containing \(N\) is a different one.
The entire first band \(U\) has nonzero constant, degree at most \(2d\), and support covered by at most \(k-1\) original intervals, as the interval assigned the endpoint \(N\) contributes only to its own row. [Integer support assignment](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#application)

### Extreme coefficients and induction

For the integral row gcd \(h\), both \(m(h)\) and the specialized primitive quotient measure are nonnegative.
Additivity gives the latter as \(m(P)-m(h)<\eta\le g\), placing it in the torsion alternative of its own certificate.
Dividing through by \(h\) and shifting all rows by a common Laurent monomial preserves fast support and cannot increase the total slow width.
For each specialized character one has \(|c+v\cdot q|>(J-2)d>L\deg U\).
Each factor has a unique monomial of smallest scalar fast frequency, because \(v\cdot q\ne0\) and its cyclotomic polynomial has nonzero constant and leading coefficients of modulus one.
Every other monomial in the factor has strictly larger scalar frequency, so the product's smallest fast term can only arise by taking the individual minima.
Its coefficient is a signed Laurent monomial in \(X\).
Restoring \(h\) gives \(h\) times a signed monomial for the first actual specialized row \(U\), including integer content and every repeated factor of \(h\).
Reciprocity up to sign and monomial converts negative specialized character exponents to positive \(b\).
Both \(P\) and \(U\prod_\nu\Phi_{e_\nu}(X^{b_\nu})\) have valuation zero, forcing the remaining monomial shift to vanish.
This gives (4) with \(m(U)=m(P)\).

Additivity of every fast coordinate width gives \(\sum_\nu\varphi(e_\nu)|v_{\nu j}|=w'_j\) exactly for the occupied primitive lift.
Every nonzero integral \(v_\nu\) has \(\|v_\nu\|_1\ge1\), hence the newly extracted factors cost at most \(\sum_jw'_j\le B\) in total totient degree, counting repetitions.
The induction hypothesis on the at most \(k-1\) intervals covering \(U\) applies because \(m(U)<\eta(k-1,L)\).
Its initial block \(Q\) is an initial block of \(P\), has \(s\le C(k-1,L)W\), and contributes at most \(C(k-1,L)\) further totient degree.
The character exponents already removed exceed \(L\deg U\ge Ls\), and the inductive ones exceed \(Ls\) as well.
These bounds explain the additive term \(B+C(k-1,L)\) in (2).

In the positively oriented cyclotomic product the gap after its constant term and the gap before its leading term both exceed \(s\), since every nonconstant factor has step \(b_\nu>Ls\ge s\).
This remains true after multiplying factors because all their exponents are nonnegative, and the same argument applies to exponents measured backwards from the leading term.
Convolution with \(Q\) gives copies of \(Q\) in increasing exponent order at both ends, multiplied by the respective unit endpoint coefficients.
The global sign can therefore be chosen so that the initial block is \(Q\), and the final block equals it up to sign.
The empty product obeys the same assertion directly.
Every root of \(\Phi_e(X^b)\) is torsion, so all noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(P\) occur in \(Q\) with their original multiplicities, and \(m(Q)=m(P)\).

### Measure spectrum and necessary width

For fixed \(k,W\) each value below the threshold is attained by its actual initial block of degree at most \(C(k,1)W\), whose support is still covered by the same intervals.
The elementary symmetric bound gives \(|[X^j]Q|\le2^{\deg Q}e^\eta\), hence a finite set of integer candidates.
For a rational cutoff \(r\) strictly between \(1\) and \(e^\eta\), support coverage is decidable from the finite ordered support and \(M(Q)\) is real algebraic by the product formula over its roots.
Exact comparison with \(r\) therefore gives the stated enumeration below the smaller threshold.
If \(S\mid P\) is irreducible and noncyclotomic, the multiplicity conclusion gives \(\deg S\le\deg Q\le C(k,1)W\).
This controls the absolute width in terms of the degree of the retained factor, including after cyclotomic padding, whenever the total measure remains below the threshold.
For \(P=S\) it gives a relative-width lower bound against the degree of \(P\) itself.
