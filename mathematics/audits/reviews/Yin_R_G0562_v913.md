# Finite-place laws for every factor and the resulting all-section measure gap

At 2026-11-21T05:46 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the full [C345 v772](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v772.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and the requested topics of inspection, without participating in the development of v772 or consulting a previous verdict on it.
This reviewer previously reviewed the directional metric and generic sequence of v769.

On a fixed signed face of the sup-norm sphere a coordinate has exponent \(sN\) with \(s=\pm1\), so its discrete valuation times \(s\) gives \(Nv_p(z)\) at every embedding.
This identity survives extension to a field containing all the phases because a root of unity has valuation zero.
The normalized function \(-\log|\cdot|_v/\log p\) extends continuously from the multiplicative group to the analytic projective line with the extended real line as target.
Composing with the coordinate morphism and a continuous function on that compact interval gives a bounded invariant Green function for the zero divisor.
It can be prescribed at all places above \(p\) of the fixed field of definition and taken zero elsewhere.
For a field of definition \(E\) of a point and the data, the embeddings inducing a particular place over \(p\) have multiplicity \([E_v:\mathbb Q_p]\).
They have the same test value, and restriction from \(E\) to \(\mathbb Q(z)\) has constant multiplicity.
Hence the global height of the test divisor equals the full empirical mean, including when the chosen component or the torsion orders vary before conjugation.
Conjugating to a fixed component preserves this complete mean and preserves genericity since the conjugates of a fixed finite algebraic set form a finite set.
The common-height limit for the directional small sequence from v769 applies to any such continuous test divisor by [Ballaÿ–Sombra's global theorem](../literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#equidistribution-height).
Along the same directions its convergence is uniform in the selected root by taking a violating subsequence.
For the whole untwisted section, summing each rational root orbit with its factor multiplicity consequently gives the same limit, since its \(O_F(g)\) torsion roots have negligible total weight against a bounded test.

For an unshifted section the minimum of \(v_p(c_e)+et\) has one-sided derivatives equal to extremal horizontal coordinates of the minimizing face.
Weighted Gauss multiplicativity follows by scaling the variable at rational \(t\) and by continuity at real \(t\).
Applying it to the linear factors shows that the right derivative, after subtracting the smallest exponent, counts the root valuations strictly above \(t\).
Each root therefore contributes its valuation at the negative slope, with horizontal edge length giving multiplicity.
With horizontal coordinates divided by \(N\) this computation multiplies root valuations by \(N\) and divides the horizontal masses by \(N\).
For \(t\) away from the limiting breaks every possible limiting minimizing coordinate is the same.
The finite support then gives convergence of both derivatives there and of the widths to \(w_A(u)>0\), even when different support points coalesce horizontally.
The limiting survival function has full mass, since outside the finitely many limiting breaks it is eventually one at the left and zero at the right.
This also excludes mass escaping to the infinities of the compact test interval.

In the long directions the support projection is injective, so each translated coefficient is one integer times a root of unity in every extension.
This description also applies to every conjugate section that contains a conjugate of the selected root.
The ordinate difference on any nonhorizontal edge is a nonzero integer, while its normalized horizontal length is at most \(C_F\).
There are therefore no nonzero valuations of absolute value below \(1/C_F\) in either the individual empirical laws or the limit.
A continuous function supported inside that gap and equal to one at zero extracts the exact limiting zero proportion.
The horizontal zero-ordinate face has width \(w_{A_p}(u)\), possibly zero if the unit coefficients project to the same coordinate.

For any non-torsion dependent unit pair, a primitive generator of its subgroup modulo torsion is an integral product of powers of its coordinates and remains a unit.
In directions of growing primitive norm all valuations of this generator vanish, forcing the limit to be the point mass at zero.
In a fixed primitive direction infinitely many distinct pairs are generic on the normalization after conjugation, and the character \(x^b y^{-a}\) has height zero on those pairs.
The nearby primitive directions from [v770](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#fixed) give a second generic sequence small for the same nonconstant character.
At a corner one can choose the approximating directions on the side keeping the same maximal coordinate, by choosing the sign of the determinant-one perturbation.
The common continuous test means then identify their limiting polygon law with the unit point mass.
Compactness covers mixed sequences by the two cases.
For each prime equality of the two widths is exactly the presence of a coefficient prime to \(p\) on each extreme face, equivalent over all primes to a separate gcd of one on each face.
Only the finite normal fan and primes dividing some coefficient need to be examined.
Rank two and rational irreducibility exclude a translated subtorus component, and hence an identically vanishing section.

Failure of the finite test in every direction gives a finite set of dependent unit pairs.
For each recurring rank-one pair, choosing a fixed Bézout generator \(\beta\) expresses any section parameter up to inversion by \(z^g=\omega\beta\) with \(\omega\) torsion.
The fixed-seed bound \(m(z)\ge m(\beta)/(\kappa k_\beta)>0\) in [v767](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v767.md#external) is uniform over these phases and dilations.
A nonunit already has measure at least \(\log2\), as follows from the leading or constant coefficient of its primitive minimal polynomial.
The minimum over the finitely many seeds and this threshold gives the asserted positive constant.

For the example direct expansion yields \(B_0=3X^5-19X^4+36X^3-19X^2+3X\), reciprocal with respect to degree six.
The even outer rows have corner coefficients \(18,94\) in opposite order and the middle row has an odd coefficient, giving content one and the full rectangle.
The odd support lies strictly inside, and its evaluation at \(X=1\) vanishes modulo two, so the face failure is compatible with an actual torus zero in the reduction.
The two indicated modulus sets of roots of \(A_0\) and \(A_0^*\) are disjoint, hence the polynomial is primitive in \(Y\) over \(\mathbb C[X]\).
In \(A_0^*=2(3-X)(3-47X^5)\) the zero at three is simple, \(B_0(3)=0\), and \(A_0(3)\ne0\).
Eisenstein at \(X-3\) gives irreducibility over \(\mathbb C(X)\), then over the polynomial and Laurent rings by primitivity and the absence of coordinate factors.
For \(\beta+\beta^{-1}=3\) the next two power sums are \(7\) and \(18\), giving the claimed cancellation, and both \(\beta\)'s are nontorsion units.
Thus the example satisfies the all-section gap despite the explicit repeating unit point, with a qualitative lower bound.
