# Exterior radii, sign kernels and products on blocks

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T16:14 UTC I independently reviewed for Yin the [C345 v271 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#decision-exterior-radii-and-signs-submitted).
I used the fixed claim, its cited mathematical inputs and my earlier independent review of v270.
I did not participate in developing v271 or receive its proof-development discussion.
I found its conclusions valid under the stated compression, reciprocal-unit and bounded-measure hypotheses.

### Real and complex exterior radii

Inversion commutes with the Galois action and takes the torsion-quotient classes of any reciprocal unit to the classes of the inverse conjugates.
Class products are consequently reciprocal units, and two distinct products have nontorsion quotient by v264.
For a nontorsion reciprocal input no root is self-inverse, so the labels are exactly the \(v=d/2\) inverse pairs.
Each exterior label has exactly one representative of modulus greater than one.
Complex conjugation permutes these representatives and acts on their blocks because it lies in the splitting group. [Class products](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) · [Block partition](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition)

Let \(a_j\) be the rational coefficients in a relation on the logarithms of the \(c\)-orbit radii.
After multiplying by a common denominator, squaring the product converts a negative or positive real representative \(x\) to \(x^2\), and a nonreal one to \(x\bar{x}\).
In the latter case \(x,\bar{x}\) are distinct exterior roots and cannot be inverses, so they represent two different labels.
Distinct orbits have disjoint supports.
The map to formal rational coefficients on inverse labels sends \(a_j\) to \(2a_j\) on a singleton and \(a_j\) on each member of a doubleton.
It is injective, and its image on log relations lies in the relation space of v270 and is \(c\)-invariant as an array on the exterior labels.
Any nonzero member of that image has full support in each block it meets.
At most \(\lfloor(b-1)/2\rfloor\) radii occupy at most \(b-1\) labels, proving the independence assertion.
All blocks containing a neutral label have zero coefficients.
From each \(c\)-orbit of the remaining blocks, evaluate at one label in one representative block.
A zero evaluation on that block forces every coefficient there to vanish, and conjugation then gives zero also on its conjugate block.
Thus these \(f\) evaluations inject the rational relation space on \(s\) radii into \(\mathbb Q^f\).
The log rank is at least \(s-f\), even when two different exterior orbits could a priori have had the same real radius. [Exterior orbit argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#radii) · [Full-support restrictions](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank)

### The relative kernel and the twists of lifted permutations

An inverse pair fixed as a label by \(c\) has sign zero precisely for a real root and sign one precisely when \(\bar{x}=x^{-1}\), or \(|x|=1\).
These cases are disjoint for nontorsion roots.
On three fixed labels of mixed sign, use the alternating factor to lift a three-cycle to \(h\).
Every other label is fixed by \(h\), and its action on the two roots there squares to the identity.
On the three labels \(h^2\) is still a three-cycle.
Writing the signed group as \(\mathbb F_2^v\rtimes S_v\), conjugation of a pure sign vector by \((a,\pi)\) sends it to \(\pi\) applied to that vector, independently of \(a\).
On the chosen triple \(c\) is such a pure sign vector, while its stable complement is fixed pointwise by \(h^2\).
The commutator therefore has sign equal to the sum of a nonconstant binary triple and its cyclic rotation, which has weight exactly two.
It fixes all other roots, including any other pairs permuted by \(c\).
Conjugating this element gives all two-coordinate flips within each block.
They generate \(u\) commuting, independently supported even-sign spaces of total dimension \(u(b-1)=v-u\).
These lie in \(G'\); applying a flip involving a nonzero relation coefficient and subtracting creates a nonzero relation on at most two labels, impossible since \(b\ge9\).
This proves full multiplicative rank under the mixed-sign hypothesis.
When all traces are real all labels are fixed by \(c\), and a mixed block has a third fixed label since \(b\ge3\). [Mixed signs](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#inversions)

Two traces \(x+x^{-1}\) and \(y+y^{-1}\) coincide precisely when \(y=x\) or \(x^{-1}\), by the quadratic equation for the corresponding pair.
Thus \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\) identifies with the label group, and \(N=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\le\mathbb F_2^v\).
The compositum with \(F=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\) identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(FL/F)\) with \(G'\) and \(\operatorname{Gal}(FK/F)\) with \(H'\).
Restriction is onto \(H'\) and has kernel \(N\cap G'\).
This proves \([L:K]\ge[FL:FK]\ge2^{v-u}\).
Since \(H'\) contains the perfect product of the independent alternating factors, the other bound in (1) follows. [Relative degree input](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#degree)

For a Salem number, a torsion multiple of its unique exterior conjugate has the same modulus, so its class is a singleton.
Transitivity then gives the compression hypothesis for all conjugates.
With \(\mathbf1\) the all-one sign vector, the conjugates of complex conjugation are \(\mathbf1+e_i\) for every \(i\).
These involutions have the same image in the abelianization, so products with different exceptions give each double flip \(e_i+e_j\) inside \(N\cap G'\).
Conversely, the total sign, given by the sum in \(\mathbb F_2\) of the sign coordinates of a signed permutation, is a homomorphism on the entire wreath product since permutation of the coordinates preserves that sum.
Consequently \(N\cap G'\) is exactly the even-sign subgroup, irrespective of the choice of inverse-pair representatives.
This review observation sharpens the submitted Salem lower bound to \([FL:FK]=2^{v-1}\) and gives \([FL:F]=2^{v-1}|H'|\).
Combining it with \(|H'|\ge(b!/2)^u\) gives the submitted bound \([FL:F]\ge2^{v-1}(b!/2)^u\).
For comparison, over the absolute trace splitting field \(K\) the cited Salem theorem permits the full sign kernel or the even one for odd \(v\) and forces the full one for even \(v\).
That absolute result and the project’s previously established independence of Salem labels agree with the conclusions used here. [Absolute Salem kernel and attribution](../../claims/Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population) · [Salem label independence](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v116.md#records)

The comparison with the earlier one-dimensional rank estimate for exterior logarithms and the signed group calculation for a single exterior nonreal pair introduces no additional hypothesis into v271. [Rank-one estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v65.md#statement) · [Earlier signed calculation](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#flips)

### Canonical block orientations and the height estimate

Let \(\mathcal R\) be the rational relation space modulo torsion, and suppose it is nonzero.
Its projection to at least one block is nonzero, and signed Galois transitivity gives the same on every block.
On each projection every nonzero vector has no zero coordinate and has constant absolute coordinate value by v270.
Evaluation at one coordinate has trivial kernel, so the projection is a rational line with primitive integral generators opposite sign vectors.
Changing initial inverse-pair representatives changes the coordinates of this line by exactly those signs, leaving the unordered pair of opposite orientations of actual roots intrinsic.
The unordered pair of products of the chosen \(b\) roots is thus Galois-equivariant over varying blocks.
It gives at most \(2u\) possible conjugates of any one block product \(\beta_B\), even if its inverse occurs only as a possible orientation.
Its height is at most \(b h(\gamma)=bm/d\), since heights are invariant under conjugacy and inversion and subadditive on products.
Multiplication by the degree bound gives \(m(\beta_B)\le 2ubm/d=m\).
Each product is an algebraic unit, and \(2u<d\) follows from \(b>1\). [Block products](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#descent)

The blocks are conjugate, so the two orientations of each product and its images all have the same torsion status.
In the torsion case, the internal sign vectors of the \(u\) blocks are relations with disjoint supports.
Their span is all of \(\mathcal R\), since that space embeds in the direct sum of its \(u\) one-dimensional projections.
This gives rank \(v-u\).
In the nontorsion case the product has positive height, hence gives a lower-degree unit of positive measure at most \(m\).
A reciprocal unit of least degree among all algebraic numbers in the given measure interval cannot admit this latter case.
Together with \(\mathcal R=0\) this proves the stated least-degree dichotomy.
