## Tree reconstruction, grouped translations and the two exceptional proportions

The labeled tree bound (3), the simultaneous gap count (4), both distribution bounds and their stated consequences hold with the given hypotheses.

For a partial group \(A\) let \(i\) be its least label and write its contribution as \(X^{u_i}b_A(X)\).
Every proper group formed in a nondegenerate identity has \(b_A\ne0\).
Among at least two current groups, a unique maximal degree at infinity would give a nonzero leading coefficient of the total sum.
Merging two groups tied at the maximum therefore remains possible until the root.
For children with representatives \(i,j\), equality of degrees fixes \(u_j-u_i\).
It determines the normalized parent coefficient and all shifts between the two children, using only their already reconstructed data.
The root sum may vanish because its coefficient is never used in a further degree comparison.
Thus any one labeled tree yields at most one array with \(u_n=0\), even if several interleavings or several trees occur for an array.
A rooted binary tree on \(n-1\) leaves has \(2n-4\) edges and one additional place above the root to insert leaf \(n\), giving \((2n-3)!!\) trees starting at two leaves.
In the lower example, a sum of any nonempty collection of the positive monomials is nonzero, and the negative polynomial cancels them only when every monomial is present.
The \((n-1)!\) permutations therefore give distinct nondegenerate normalized arrays.

For any finite family in a single residue class modulo \(Q\), the threshold in (3) keeps every nonzero Laurent minor of its monomial matrix nonzero at \(\alpha^Q\).
Indeed a minor of size \(s\le n\) has circle maximum at most \(s^{s/2}\le n^{n/2}\), and after clearing Laurent powers its vanishing would force divisibility by the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^Q\).
Solving on a maximal minor gives a common kernel vector over \(\mathbb C(X)\) regular at the specialization and equal there to the original coefficient vector with its residue multipliers.

The tree count bounds each finite family, which also bounds the whole class, and there are \(Q^{n-1}\) residue classes because one exponent is zero.

For a fixed full order every solution with at most \(k\) exceptional gaps admits some \(k\) cuts covering them.

When \(k\ge1\) each group is proper and has nonzero normalized coefficient, and every proper subsum of translated groups expands to a proper subsum of the original terms.
Fixing one group at zero, the other \(k\) translations give an injective tuple in \(\langle\alpha\rangle^k\), whose rank is \(k\) since each coordinate is independently an infinite cyclic group.
Dividing the equation by the negative of the fixed group's coefficient makes its right side one.

Applying (3) to \(k+1\) groups gives the alternative \(Q^k(2k-1)!!\).

The witness family fixes the order of the unmarked coefficients.

Degree preservation gives \(\log M(\alpha^Q)=Qm\) by the absolute height identity.
The floor plus one in the chosen \(Q\) ensures the strict threshold even at an integral quotient.
Its logarithm and \((2k-1)!!\le(2k)^k\) together cost at most \(k[L+2\log(k+1)+\log\log(2k+2)+O(1)]\).
After including the internal gaps, the order choices and \(\binom{r-1}k\le2^{r-1}\), the logarithm of the exceptional proportion is bounded by
\[
 -(1-\theta)rL+k[(1-\theta)L+2\log r]
        +O(r+L+k\log\log(3r)).
\]
For \(k\) in (1), the ratio of its last error term \(k\log\log(3r)\) to \(rL\) is \(O_\theta(\log\log(3r)/(L+\log r))=o(1)\).
Also \(r+L=o(rL)\), and \(k\to\infty\) with \(k<r-1\) eventually.
Thus the logarithm is at most \(-\epsilon(1-\theta)rL/2\) eventually.
Initial intervals select precisely the gaps exceeding \(m^{-\theta}\).
Dividing the threshold by \(r-1\) and choosing a small fixed \(\epsilon\) gives both stated fraction consequences.
