### Height, rotation and limiting profiles

The leading coefficient of \(C\) is two and every trace root lies in \((-2,2)\).
Thus \(0<C(2)\le2\cdot4^h\), and the nonzero integral divisor \(q-1\mid C(2)\) forces \(h\to\infty\) on a sequence with \(q\to\infty\).
The reciprocal lift \(z^hC(z+z^{-1})\) and the extra factor \(1-z^2\) have only circle roots.
Their product has leading coefficient of absolute value two, hence \(M(\Pi)=2\).
It has at least two terms by its endpoints, so the sparse height bound gives \(\|\Pi\|_\infty\le s2^{s-2}M(\Pi)\) on the circle.
Here both the bound and the Mahler measure apply to the full polynomial, independently of the degree and the unprescribed coefficients.

For the \(2+z\) prefix, replacing \(z\) by \(-z\) in all four functions gives the same \(2-z\) prefix as in the other case.
This rotation preserves every Taylor product and preserves Mahler measure by invariance of arc length.
The polynomial \(B^\sharp\) has even reciprocal degree \(2h+2\), so this substitution also preserves its reciprocity and its constant one.
The representing measures are pushed forward by \(v\mapsto-v\).
In particular the negative atom moves to \(-1\) without changing its mass or the total variation.
The real part of the limiting \(K\) is positive, its positive continuous Herglotz component stays nonzero, and multiplication of each original moment by \((-1)^l\) preserves integrality and eventual periodicity.
The limiting series for \((2-z)/K\) has integer coefficients by stabilization on each fixed prefix of \(B^\sharp=\Pi/K_q\), and \(K'_q(0)=-1-2[z]B^\sharp\) is odd eventually.
These data give the cyclotomic and phase calculation of L462v44, including the nonzero continuous density needed to assign the numerator and denominator factors.
They establish the same nine candidates after either sign choice.

### The two cutoff polynomials

Suppose that finite discrepancies \(n\ge c h\) occur along a subsequence with \(c>0\), or that equality \(n=\infty\) occurs infinitely often.
Choose \(0<\eta<\min(c,\epsilon,1)\) in the finite case, and \(0<\eta<\min(\epsilon,1)\) in the equality case.
For \(A=\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\), both \(B^\sharp-B_\infty\) and \(\Pi-(2-z)\) then vanish through degree \(A\).
The common constant of the two denominators in \(J_q=2B^\sharp/\Pi\) and \(J=2B_\infty/(2-z)\) is two, so \(\Delta_q=J_q-J\) also vanishes through that degree.
For every positive index its coefficient is twice the corresponding moment of the difference of two representing measures of uniformly bounded variation.

The first cutoff has support in \([A-2b,3L+2b]\) and equals one on \([A,3L]\), with \(b=\lfloor A/3\rfloor\).
The lower endpoint eventually exceeds zero, and all indices in the lower transition have zero discrepancy.
The kernel has \(L^1\) norm at most \(\sqrt{(3L-A+2b+1)/(2b+1)}\) by orthogonality of the two interval sums and Cauchy–Schwarz.
This is uniformly bounded on the subsequence since \(L=2h+O(1)\) and \(b\) grows proportionally to \(h\).
Convolution with the representing measure, using reflection for the moment convention, gives an analytic polynomial \(T_q\) of bounded \(L^1\) norm agreeing with \(\Delta_q\) at every index from 0 through \(3L\).

I checked the exact polynomial identity
\[
S_q=(2-z)U_0B^\sharp-V\Pi=\tfrac12(2-z)U_0\Pi\Delta_q
\]
using \(J=2V/((2-z)U_0)\).
The height bound on \(\Pi\) allows replacement of \(\Delta_q\) by \(T_q\) on the right to produce a bounded-\(L^1\) analytic polynomial that agrees with \(S_q\) through \(3L\).
The degree choice gives \(\deg S_q<L\) even if neither leading term cancels.
The second cutoff is supported on \([-2L,3L]\), with plateau \([0,L]\).
The polynomial being filtered has no negative frequencies and has zero coefficients at every integer in \((L,3L]\).
Thus convolution returns precisely \(S_q\), and the ratio of the two interval lengths gives the stated kernel norm bound.
As a finite arithmetic check I used rational coefficients to reproduce these two coefficient filters at \((h,A)=(15,8),(19,9),(22,10),(24,11)\) on series with a varying middle term.

On the circle, \(|2-z|\ge1\) gives \(\|U_0B^\sharp\|_1\le\|S_q\|_1+\|V\Pi\|_1\).
This integral polynomial therefore has uniformly bounded support by L462v50's prime-grid argument, which permits arbitrary degree and coefficient heights.
Its measure is that of \(B^\sharp\), since all factors of \(U_0\) are cyclotomic.
That measure is greater than one and tends to one by the delay-one return on the fixed second branch.
This contradicts the fixed-support Mahler gap and rules out both proposed cases, proving eventual finiteness and \(n/h\to0\).
The fixed-prefix stabilization separately gives \(n\to\infty\).

### Transfer at moving frequencies
