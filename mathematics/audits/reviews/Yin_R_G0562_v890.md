# Primitive specializations of a four-term pole graph

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:17 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the full [C345 v750](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing the result and received its fixed text without its development discussion or a previous verdict.

The primitive minimal polynomial homogenized at \(XY-\epsilon,Y-\epsilon X\) gives the asserted integer norm even for a nonintegral pole.
For a conjugate \(D\) one has \(D\ne0,\pm1\), so a zero of \(X^n-1/D\) is simple and the leading coefficient \(X^n-D\) is nonzero there.
The constant coefficient is thus divisible exactly once by this linear prime, all intermediate coefficients vanish, and the leading and constant coefficients have no common root.
Eisenstein and primitivity prove absolute irreducibility for the diagonal pullback in the polynomial ring and after Laurent localization.
The signed adjugate turns every nonsingular integral monomial substitution into such a positive diagonal power, while the second substitution takes nonunits to nonunits by injectivity on the exponent lattice.
Distinct pole components retain distinct coefficient vectors on the identical transformed support, and a finite support admits no nonzero translation preserving it.
The coefficient at the image of \(XY\) excludes any residual scalar association.
Thus transitivity of the absolute Galois group on the conjugate poles prevents any proper rational factor of the pullback.
Each of the four original corner terms survives in the norm at the corresponding extreme of its square, giving rank two and excluding a multivariate cyclotomic parent.

For a primitive direction the subtorus has a prime binomial defining ideal, so its containment in one absolutely irreducible rank-two graph is impossible.
Consequently the univariate section is nonzero.
The [rational specialization conjecture](../literature/AS18/Yin_U001.md#as18-u001) uses rational irreducible factors, finite hyperplanes and matrices, and includes multiplicity after cyclotomic parts are removed.
Only two primitive directions lie on any one exceptional line.
Every allowed isogeny pullback has exactly one rational irreducible factor, so the conjecture indeed predicts at most one noncyclotomic irreducible factor outside a finite set, including multiplicity.
The reciprocal identity holds with sign \(-\epsilon\) for each graph; the proven non-self-inversive case and the nonconstant function-field theorem do not establish this rational case.

For real \(D\), the circle map has no zero or pole on the circle, has winding \(1\) for \(|D|>1\) and \(-1\) for \(|D|<1\), and its argument derivative is \((D^2-1)/|D-e^{it}|^2\).
Substitution of \(Z^u\) multiplies the derivative by \(u\), and the imposed strict slope bound makes the difference from \(v\) positive everywhere, also for the negative winding case.
The exact number of simple circle roots over one turn is consequently \(v-u\) or \(v+u\), respectively.
Reciprocity and degree \(u+v\) give precisely \(u\) exterior roots with multiplicity for every outer conjugate \(D\) and none for the others.
Adding the contributions across all pole conjugates gives \(uh_B\) without any requirement that roots of different components on the circle be distinct.
For the positive exterior root over \(B\), the negative-sign equation has a unique transverse intersection on \(t>B\) by strict opposite monotonicities.
For the positive sign the derivative with respect to \(\log t\) of the indicated logarithmic ratio starts strictly below \(v/u\), increases strictly on \(1<t<B\), and the logarithm diverges at \(B\).
This yields a unique transverse intersection after the endpoint \(t=1\).
No other conjugate component shares this positive root since \(Y-\epsilon X\ne0\) uniquely determines its pole.
Thus its multiplicity in the entire norm is one, and a Salem minimal polynomial containing it cannot supply a second exterior root.

In a shrinking sequence the exact four-term classification permits a subsequence with fixed totally real \(B\) and sign.
Dividing the two exponents by their gcd preserves the pole relation at the positive Salem power, which has the same degree by uniqueness of the exterior conjugate.
The [common-exponent bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v747.md#statement) gives \(u\to\infty\) while \(v/u\to\infty\), so infinitely many distinct primitive directions have two distinct noncyclotomic factors regardless of the finite exceptional lines.
This would contradict the one rational conjectural instance and gives the asserted conditional exclusion for a finite list of poles.

Multiplication verifies the explicit three-factor polynomial identity, and division by the middle Laurent powers gives the stated cubic and quartic trace polynomials.
The cubic takes values \(-1,2,-1\) at \(-2,-1,0\), and \(-1,14\) at \(2,3\), and has neither possible rational root.
The quartic takes values \(27,-1,1,3,-1,7\) at \(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2\).
Its reduction has no linear root, and modulo the unique irreducible quadratic over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(T^4+T^3+1\) reduces to \(T\).
These checks give both trace degrees and all the real root intervals.
In each totally real trace field a trace conjugate in \((-2,2)\) makes the quadratic discriminant negative at an embedding, proving the corresponding reciprocal factor irreducible.
The cubic supplies the unique positive exterior conjugate and the quartic a negative exterior conjugate whose negative is Salem.
Together they account for both exterior roots, consistently with a single exceptional primitive direction.
