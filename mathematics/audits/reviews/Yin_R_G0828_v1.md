### Complete return conditioning and the actual midpoint

The integer columns representing beta^j are legitimate for every exponent because the fixed polynomial is monic and O has its integral power basis.
The constraint on all 2n columns is exactly the original algebraic return, rather than a neutral projection of that constraint.
The real and imaginary midpoint coefficients are deterministic, use the first n positions with the stated phase -zeta^-n, and vanish on the remaining positions.
Each squared coefficient sum is at most n.
Thus C419 applies directly to each Cartesian coordinate under the same complete return law.
If a coordinate has zero variance proxy, it is identically zero and requires no Chernoff division.
Otherwise the C419 two-sided bound at r/sqrt(2), followed by the two-coordinate union bound, gives precisely

    pi_n{|Z_n|>r} <= 4 exp(-r^2/(4n)).

The coefficient proxy is sum r_j^2, not the smaller unconditional proxy.
The C419 proof was read at this exact finite-lemma scope: Fourier orthogonality gives the product of complex squared cosines; its absolute value expands into nonnegative terms p_(J^c) times products of sinh squared; the actual event with the J digits zero gives p_N>=2^(-|J|)p_(J^c).
Summing yields p_N times the product of cosh, so division is by the actual complete p_N throughout.
There is no conditional-independence assumption, martingale assertion or exponential rough return denominator in this application.

For E_n the first half contains A and two zero middle digits, so the algebraic midpoint equals -beta^-n A(beta)=beta^(k+4-n)c=beta^2c.
Hence the neutral modulus is exactly rho(c), independently of which complete tails represent the fiber.

Subtracting the full-law midpoint tail from pi_n(E_n)>=1/16 proves the claimed lower bound for delta_n(r).

### Neutral inversion and every affine constant

    sqrt(13)(|ell|+|p|) <= C_neu*rho(c)+5.
