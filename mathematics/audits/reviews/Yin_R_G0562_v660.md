## Order multiplicities, contraction and the quadratic case

On 2026-11-20 at 10:49 UTC a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin checked the [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v526.md#statement) independently of its development.
At a prime ideal, the valuation of the intersection is the maximum over the products indexed by \(p\).
It is bounded above by the sum of the orderwise maxima times the valuations of the cyclotomic factors.
For the reverse bound, if the residue characteristic is prime to \(d\), a prime ideal dividing \(\Phi_d(\alpha)\) determines \(d\) uniquely as the order of the residue of \(\alpha\).
Each such prime ideal can therefore use its own maximizing \(p\) without any double counting between retained layers.
The [full norm estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#aggregate) and [prime-to-order estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#layers) apply to the same field and give the claimed errors since \(2^{\omega(d)}\le\tau(d)\).
All norms for the finitely many small orders are positive because \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
Each congruence class in the interval contributes at most \(1+\lfloor L/d\rfloor\) terms, including the diagonal in the sum factors and excluding it from the difference factors.
Thus \(a_d\le10L/d\) throughout the range, and summing the two norm errors costs at most a fixed-seed constant times \(L\log(2L)(1+\log(4L))^2\).

With \(M_\ell=\max_{\mathfrak p\mid\ell} v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak a)/e_{\mathfrak p}\), the contraction requires precisely \(v_\ell(D_{\pm})=\lceil M_\ell\rceil\).
The norm exponent is at most \(eM_\ell\) by the local degree sum.
Inversion sends every factor \(\alpha^m-1\) to a unit multiple of itself and preserves the normalized valuations within a prime orbit.
If the maximizing prime splits over the involution's fixed field, both primes contribute at the maximum.
Otherwise its local degree over the fixed field is two, so its absolute local degree is at least two.
In either case the norm exponent is at least \(2M_\ell\), and the rounding loss in the logarithm is bounded by the sum of \(\log\ell\) over the rational primes ramified in the fixed seed field \(K\).

For any distinct powered nodes, the derivative quotient at \(\alpha^p\) gives the identity \(D/T_p=(D/Q'(\alpha^p))(Q'(\alpha^p)/T_p)\).
Both factors on the right are integral in the splitting field by the [definition of the denominator](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v521.md#statement) and the factorization of the monic derivative into node differences at that root.
The left side belongs to \(K\), hence to \(\mathcal O_K\).
In degree two, nontorsion and positivity of the exponents make all nodes \(\alpha^{\pm p}\) distinct for every allowed set.
At \(\alpha^p\) the second quotient is then a unit, and conjugation gives the same integrality condition at \(\alpha^{-p}\).
This proves equality of the two denominators and the claimed logarithmic asymptotic by squeezing the norm between the contraction bounds.

Finally \(a_d\varphi(d)\le10L\) at every index, whereas every distinct sum of two different primes supplies an index of weight at least \(\varphi(d)\).
The [dyadic totient estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v494.md#statement) supplies the absolute quadratic lower bound for the full prime set.
Since \(h>0\) and \(L\log^3(2L)=o(L^2)\), the two contraction bounds transfer this order to the undeleted denominator for a fixed seed.
I find all stated conclusions supported in their specified ranges, with no uniformity in the seed required of the error terms.
