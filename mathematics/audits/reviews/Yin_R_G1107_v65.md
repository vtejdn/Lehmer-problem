## All conductors, the quotient order and the cyclotomic sign restriction

For \(p\ge7\), discrete orthogonality turns \((a_0,a_1,a_2)=(2,0,4)\) into residue moments \(2,0,6\), since \(E_2=t^2-1\).
Summing \((2-b)(b-u)\rho\) puts every separator below \(-3/2\), so the rational separator satisfies \(3A/2<\beta<2A\).
The identity for the positive quantities \(F_j\) follows by summing \(p(2-b)(Ab+\beta)\rho\).
Integrality gives \(\beta\le2A-1\), and \((A-4)^2\ge0\) bounds \((4\beta-6A)/A^2\) by \(1/4\).

The covering box follows from the arithmetic midpoint of the sign gap, where \(\sum_j|b_j-u||\rho_j|=-2u<4\).
The full-grid gap bound \(32/p^2\) and \(|E_i|\le i+1\) give \(|a_i|\le p^2(i+1)/4\) on all conductors.
I checked the coefficient norm bound \(\|h^*\|_1\le p^5\) by summing the \(i+1\) monomials in each \(E_i\).
For an actual polynomial factor, the lifted quadratic factors as \(A(z-w)(z-\bar w)\) with both roots on the unit circle.
Two synthetic divisions each increase the coefficient norm by a factor at most \(2d\), giving \(v(b_j)\le\|v^*\|_1\le p^7\).
The leading coefficient of the integral cofactor is nonzero, giving the separate upper bound on \(A\) from \(|a_d|\).

Deleting the closest full-circle point leaves a product at least \(2p/\pi\) by the sine quotient for \(z^p-1\).
Deleting the index zero and the conjugate index when still present costs at most a further factor of four.
Upon folding, each paired product is exactly \(|b_j-\alpha|\), so the surviving product exceeds \(p/8\).
Both full weight products equal \(p\), and the deleted node costs at most \(16\) in their combined product.
The cofactor norm over all grid nodes is a positive integral resultant, making its surviving product at least \(p^{-7}\).
These estimates give \(A^{2N}/(1024p^3)\) for the product lower bound and hence the stated arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
Replacing \(N\) by \(m=n-1\) weakens the lower bound because both \(N/p\) and \((1024p^3)^{-1/N}\) increase with \(N\).
I checked the integer base comparisons at \(65\) and \(37\) and their two-step growth inequalities.
They exclude \(p\ge65\) and reduce \(37\le p\le63\) to \(A\le15\).

On the complete grid the product lower bound is \(p^2R_n^2\) by the resultant and \(R_n=A^nB_n(-\beta/A)\).
For an independent arithmetic check I evaluated this integer from
\[
 U_i=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor i/2\rfloor}
 (-1)^j\binom{i-j}{j}(-\beta)^{i-2j}A^{2j},\qquad
 R_n=U_n+A U_{n-1},
\]
using the coefficient formula for \(E_i\).
I checked all odd \(p=7,\ldots,63\), taking the leading-coefficient cap below \(37\), \(A\le15\) thereafter, and the necessary bound \((mA)^m\le(2p)^m1024p^3\).
Exactly 7,864 primitive triples in the strict separator range reached the complete-grid inequality.
Only \((7,5,9)\) passed it, with \(R_3=1\).
At \(p=7\) the three fixed coordinates give \(h=4t^2-2\), whose roots are irrational.
At \(p=5\) reciprocity equates coefficients two and four of the degree-six lift, whereas the profile prescribes \(4\) and \(8\).
This proves the polynomial-factor exclusion for every allowed conductor.

In the quotient order, evaluation at all nodes still gives the positive masses and complete-grid product, even for a cofactor of arbitrary degree.
The rational number \(\alpha\in(-2,-3/2)\) cannot be a torsion trace since it would be a rational algebraic integer.
The polynomial \(Az^2+\beta z+A\) is primitive and irreducible, with two conjugate roots of modulus one, giving \(h_{\rm Weil}(w)=\tfrac12\log A\).
For \(y^k=w\), the field-degree bounds \(2\le[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\le2k\) follow from \(\mathbb Q(w)\subseteq\mathbb Q(y)\) and the polynomial \(Az^{2k}+\beta z^k+A\).
The height identity gives \(h_{\rm Weil}(y)\le1\) for the specified \(k\), and \(y\) is nontorsion.
I checked the estimate as reported on printed page 29, including its absolute constant, the factor \(D^4\log D\max(h_{\rm Weil}(y),1/D)\), and its dependence on the exponent \(kp\ge2\). [Dimitrov, equation (9.1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.04664#page=29)
The estimate's transcendence proof remains an accepted external input.
Under (6), \(k^4=o(p/(\log p)^2)\) and \(\log(2k)\log(kp)=O((\log p)^2)\), giving \(-\log|1-w^p|\le o(p)\).
Together with \(1\le|1-w|\le2\) and \(|1-w^p|\le2\), this makes the right side of (7) tend to \(1/2\), contradicting the required upper bound \(1/4\).

For every odd composite \(p\), including a prime power, there are at least two nonempty layers of distinct exact orders above one.
A layer omitting a nearest trace consists entirely of nodes at distance at least half the minimum grid spacing from \(\alpha\), also when there is a tie.
The product of the integral cofactor values on this whole layer is the norm over the real cyclotomic field and is positive by the nodewise signs.
Taking the geometric mean against \(|h(b_j)|\le p^5\) gives \(16A/p^2\le p^5\).
Thus all such composite sequences satisfy (6), whereas the prime-conductor conclusion retains (6) as a hypothesis.

Finally, at the smallest normalized primitive angle \(1/Q\) of the largest surviving order, an odd exponent changes the sign of the cyclotomic product or quotient.
Distinct primitive angles with orders at most \(Q\) are separated by at least \(1/Q^2\).
When \(p>\max(24,Q^2)\), the flanking grid nodes enclose just that change and have angles at most \(1/3+1/p<3/8\).
Their traces both exceed \(-3/2\), where the strict cut and moment test require positive signs.
This proves the stated restriction, with even multiplicities and grid-positive multipliers allowed as in the ledger.
