## Finding

L0462 v78 proves the uniform comparison for \(0<m(P)\le1\) and its equivalence with a positive Lehmer gap for nontorsion units.
The upper error is \(o(d^2m(P))\) for the sum over the entire window.

For each \(\ell>d^{1/5}\) with \(\ell\nmid j\), separability of \(X^j-1\) makes the reductions of its cyclotomic factors pairwise coprime.
Since \(\alpha\) is a unit, its residue at each \(\mathfrak p\mid\ell\) has nonzero finite-field order.
Thus a prime ideal dividing \(\Phi_j(\alpha)\) in this characteristic contributes precisely when \(o_{\mathfrak p}=j\), and the other factors of \(\alpha^j-1\) are local units.
Conversely a prime ideal of order \(j\) has \(\ell\nmid j\), since \(j\mid N\mathfrak p-1\).
With \(f_{\mathfrak p}=[\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p:\mathbb F_\ell]\), factoring the integral principal ideal gives
\[
       v_\ell(D_j)\log\ell
          =\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid\ell}
                   v_{\mathfrak p}(\Phi_j(\alpha)) f_{\mathfrak p}\log\ell .
\]
Here \(D_j=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\Phi_j(\alpha)|\) because \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The norm of each prime ideal is \(\ell^{f_{\mathfrak p}}\), so the local-uniformizer normalization already accounts for ramification through the valuation of the element.
No assumption on the index of \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) is needed.
Summing over \(\ell,j\) counts each included prime ideal once with its initial full valuation, and every term lies above one of finitely many nonzero \(\alpha^j-1\).
This proves \(\log N\mathfrak I=\sum T_j(P)\).
The fixed choices \(A=1,\delta=1/5\) in C0899 v2 give \(\gg d\) orders each contributing \(\gg d m(P)\), while all other contributions are nonnegative.

At \(Y=d\), the root energy \(O(dL^7m^2)\) and the primitive Gram norm \(O(dL)\) in L0462 v70 bound the summed squared smoothed errors by \(O(d^2L^8m^2)\), where \(L=\log(2d)\).
All \(d+1\) orders occur in this estimate, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives an absolute error sum \(O(d^{3/2}L^4m)=o(d^2m)\).
For \(1-t=(2d)^{-6}\) the one-sided radial removal costs at most \(O(d(1-t)\sum_j\varphi(j))=O(d^{-3})\) on the full window, using \(\varphi(j)\le2d\).
This is \(o(d^2m)\) uniformly by \(m\gg L^{-3}\).
Summing \(T_j\le\log D_j\) now retains the stated leading coefficient \(\sum_j\varphi(j)\).
In particular \(\log N\mathfrak I\ll d^2m\), so a uniform quadratic lower bound forces \(m\gg1\) for all sufficiently large degrees in the tested range.
In bounded degrees the integer coefficient bounds at \(m\le1\) leave only finitely many eligible polynomials, all of positive measure.
The remaining units have \(m>1\), and the converse follows directly from the lower comparison just checked.
