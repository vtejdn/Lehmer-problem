# Comparing label fields inside a solvable extension

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review

At 2026-11-17T17:26 UTC a reviewer outside proof development inspected the complete [C345 v278 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v278.md#decision-comparison-label-fields-submitted) for Yin using the fixed proof and its mathematical dependencies.
The restriction map gives either descent to the normal closure of the block field or equality with a canonical matching of labels.
In the equality case the matched compressed roots are multiplicatively dependent.

### The proper quotient and its size

The kernel of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_x/\mathbb Q)\to\operatorname{Gal}(P_x/\mathbb Q)\) is solvable by the original-class and signed-label kernel calculation in C345 v267.
The extension \(J/L_x\) in the hypothesis is solvable, so \(\operatorname{Gal}(J/P_x)\) is solvable and normal in \(\operatorname{Gal}(J/\mathbb Q)\).
Its image in \(\operatorname{Gal}(P_y/\mathbb Q)\) is solvable and normal.
The trivial radical there forces the image to be trivial, proving \(P_y\subseteq P_x\) inside the specified normal extension.
For a tower of finitely many radical adjunctions above \(L_x\), at each stage one can adjoin the requisite roots of unity and roots of all conjugate radicands over the preceding normal closure.
The two resulting steps at each stage have abelian relative groups, so the normal closure obtained is solvable over \(L_x\).

For the surjection \(G_x\to G_y\), any nontrivial kernel contains the unique minimal normal subgroup \(D_x=(A_{b_x})^{u_x}\).
The kernel \(K_x\) of block permutation embeds in \((S_{b_x})^{u_x}\).
The kernel of coordinatewise parity on \(K_x\) is exactly \(D_x\), since each alternating factor acts independently.
The image of \(K_x\) in \(G_y\) is then abelian and normal, hence trivial.
The core of a block stabilizer is \(K_x\), so its fixed field is the compositum of all conjugates of \(B_x\), namely \(\widetilde B_x\).
This field contains \(P_y\) and is strictly smaller than \(P_x\) since \(D_x\subseteq K_x\) is nontrivial.
The group of \(\widetilde B_x\) is a subgroup of \(S_{u_x}\), and its quotient \(G_y\) contains a copy of \(A_{b_y}\).
Thus \(b_y!/2\le u_x!\), forcing \(b_y\le u_x\) since \(b_y\ge9\).

The two-height block bound gives \(d_y\ll_N u_x\log^{212}(3d_y)\).
For sufficiently large \(d_y\), \(\log^{212}(3d_y)\le\sqrt{d_y}\), giving \(\sqrt{d_y}\ll_N u_x\).
Substitution in the original logarithm gives \(d_y\ll_N u_x\log^{212}(3u_x)\).
The bound \(u_x\ll_M\log^{212}(3d_x)\) then gives exactly the second estimate in (1), including its iterated logarithm.
For \(u_x=1\), \(\widetilde B_x=\mathbb Q\) cannot contain the nontrivial label field \(P_y\).

### Recovering labels and their actual roots

Injectivity of restriction gives equality of the fields.
In the resulting common group the unique minimal normal subgroup has the same simple direct factors in both actions, since those factors are precisely its minimal normal subgroups.
Their number gives \(u_x=u_y\), and their orders \(b!/2\) give \(b_x=b_y=b\).
For \(b\ge9\) every automorphism of \(A_b\) is induced by permuting its natural letters, so the family of its point stabilizers is independent of how its two natural actions are identified.
The full \(D\)-stabilizer of a label intersects its moving factor in this point stabilizer and contains all the other factors.
It specifies a unique label in each action because the moving factor and its natural point stabilizer are both distinguished.
Equality of these \(D\)-stabilizers therefore matches labels, and conjugation makes the matching \(G\)-equivariant.
Matched point stabilizers in \(G\) are equal, giving the same fixed subfield \(E\) of degree \(v=bu\).
For arbitrary small-degree inputs it is the embeddings of the stabilizer field that are indexed by labels.
For example \(2+\sqrt2\) has two distinct labels and a regular action of degree two, so the two corresponding embeddings have the same image field.

Within the common normal extension, the stabilizer of an actual compressed root is a subgroup of its label stabilizer.
The latter is transitive on all \(w_z\) roots above its label, by transitivity on all roots and equivariance of their projection to labels.
Consequently \(E\) lies in the field of each selected root, of relative degree exactly \(w_z\le2\).
The compositum of the two selected root fields therefore has absolute degree \(J\le w_xw_y v\le4v\), regardless of whether those fields are disjoint over \(E\).
Their heights are bounded by \(M/(w_xv)\) and \(N/(w_yv)\).
Amoroso–David's independent-pair inequality at this absolute joint degree would give \(MN\gg v/\log^{212}(3v)\).
The right-hand side tends to infinity.
Separate thresholds in \(M\) and \(N\) suffice since \(MN\le\max(M^2,N^2)\) and \(v\ge\max(d_x,d_y)/2\) in the equality case.
The qualitative degree hypothesis requires no numerical threshold.
Since neither selected root is torsion, dependence uses a nonzero exponent at each coordinate.
Each class product is the appropriate power of any original representative times torsion, giving the claimed relation on the original conjugates.
Also \(d_y/d_x=w_y/w_x\in\{1/2,1,2\}\).

The trace-field corollary concerns compressed reciprocal units.
To see the need for this scope, start with such an \(x\) and choose an odd prime \(p\) for which \(x\) is not a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
Such primes exist by finite generation of the unit group.
For \(y^p=x\), a relative degree \(e<p\) would give \(N(y)^p=x^e\), making \(x\) a \(p\)-th power by \(\gcd(e,p)=1\).
Thus the reciprocal minimal polynomial of \(y\) has degree \(p d_x\), while the class over \(x\) has product \(x\), its \(p\) members being all \(p\)-th roots of \(x\).
The measure is unchanged and the splitting extension above \(L_x\) is solvable, but the uncompressed trace \(y+y^{-1}\) has degree \(p d_x/2\).
