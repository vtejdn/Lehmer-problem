# Signed lifts of parity words and the largest common line-record fiber

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T07:01 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed [C345 v788](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v788.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of (1)–(4), their conditional rate consequences and the example of a parity without a signed lift.
The reviewer took no part in proof development and received the fixed claim without development discussion, author explanations or prior findings on this claim.
The reviewer had independently checked the integral splitting for C345 v787 and read R562 v927 on the earlier marginal estimate.
The mod-two kernels, collision formula and lifting estimates were checked directly from the fixed proof.

In a basis splitting the one-line kernel from the first \(d\) power-basis positions, a coordinate-direction map vanishes on tensors with a kernel basis vector in its slot and maps the remaining basis tensors bijectively to an output basis.
Reduction modulo two preserves the splitting, hence intersection over the \(J\) directions gives the full tensor kernel over either ring.
In particular every binary full-grid kernel vector lifts to an integer kernel vector by lifting its coefficients in the tensor basis.
The full binary kernel has codimension \(q=n-K^J\), so shortening to a mask imposes at most \(q\) independent equations there, including when \(L=d\).
A signed integer kernel vector reduces to its support, and any lift of norm at most one has values \(\pm1\) precisely on that support.
Each prescribed signed difference has probability \(2^{-s-w}\) for two independent bit arrays, since a fixed nonzero sign has probability \(1/4\) and equality has probability \(1/2\).
Summing over the \(O(z)\) signs for each parity gives (1), using \(\sum_y\Pr(Y=y)^2\le\max_y\Pr(Y=y)\).
Each of the \(p2^{D_S}\) liftable words counted in (2) contributes at least \(2^{-t}\) to its right-hand sum in (1).

Multiplication by any power of \(\alpha\) is invertible modulo two, so every consecutive \(d\)-column block of the one-line matrix is invertible there.
Each nonzero row-space word therefore has support of size at least \(\lfloor L/d\rfloor\).
An obstruction to prescribing values on at most \(\lfloor L/d\rfloor-1\) one-line coordinates would be a nonzero dual word supported on those coordinates.
For any grid site among this many distinct sites, one can accordingly choose one factor per axis taking value one at its distinguished coordinate and zero at all other coordinate values in the set on that axis.
Their tensor product gives the chosen unit vector on the set of sites, even when several sites have repeated values on some axes.
Surjectivity of the coordinate projection thus gives uniform independent bits on that set.
For \(L\ge3d\) this gives variance \(n/4\) for the weight, so the upper tail at \(n/2+n^{3/4}\) has probability at most \(1/(4\sqrt n)\).
A liftable fraction bounded away from zero still leaves a fraction bounded away from zero below that threshold.

For \(2r\le\lfloor L/d\rfloor-1\), each term of the \(2r\)-th moment of the sum of centered signs agrees with its independent counterpart.
Every surviving index sequence admits a pairing of its \(2r\) positions, giving at most \((2r-1)!!n^r\) such sequences.
Dividing by \(2^{2r}\) for centered bits and using \((2r-1)!!\le(2r)^r\) gives the stated tail bound.
For fixed integer \(r>A\) and \((1+A/r)/2<\beta<1\), \(n^{r(1-2\beta)}=o(n^{-A})\) and \(n^\beta=o(n)\).
The assumption \(L/d\to\infty\) permits this choice of moment eventually, leaving at least \(p/2\) of all words liftable below \(n/2+n^\beta\).
With \(\log_2(p/2)=O(\log n)\) this gives (3).
The limit of \(K^J/n=(1-d/L)^J\) is \(e^{-1/c}\) when \(L/(Jd)\to c>0\) and \(J\to\infty\), giving the claimed strict threshold.

For a uniform shortened-code word every coordinate projection is zero or fair, so its mean weight is at most \(s/2\).
When all words lift, convexity of \(2^{-w}\) in (1) yields the asserted \(D_S-s/2\) bound.
For partial lifting a set of \(D_S\) independent columns of a generator matrix gives \(D_S\) independent fair bits.
Their sum \(T\) satisfies \(\mathbb E\exp(u(T-D_S/2))\le\exp(D_Su^2/8)\) because \(\log\cosh v\le v^2/2\).
Optimizing Markov's inequality at \(u=4x/D_S\) gives a tail of at most \(p/2\) at the stated \(x\).
The remaining \(s-D_S\) bits add at most \(s-D_S\) to \(T\), so (2) yields exactly (4).
For \(D_S\ge s-q\) its main term is at least \(s/2-3q/2\), and both losses are \(o(n)\) under the stated uniform hypothesis.
In the all-words bound the main term is at least \(s/2-q\), giving the other mask threshold.

The parity supported at \(0,M\) has zero reduced line record by the order of \(\alpha\bmod 2\), but either choice of relative sign in a signed lift would require \(\alpha^M=\pm1\).
Tensoring that parity repeats the same two nonzero positions on each occupied line, ruling out a signed lift in every dimension.
For \(X^2-3X+1\), reduction gives \(X^2+X+1\), whose root is a nontrivial cube root of unity over \(\mathbb F_2\), whereas the larger real root of the integer polynomial exceeds one.
The example disproves universal signed lifting and leaves the typical fraction and its weight distribution undetermined.
Accordingly the positive fiber rates in (3) and (4) have the respective lifting hypotheses stated in the claim.
