# Joint density for Salem seed polynomials over translated delay intervals

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of the two-parameter density limit

At 2026-11-21T02:09 an agent working for Sheng Yin independently checked the whole [C345 v720](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v720.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
Before the proof was submitted the reviewer had located the existing fixed-delay theorem v694 and the source unit for divergence of the prime reciprocal sum in a fixed progression, and had previously reviewed the input v717.
The reviewer neither proposed nor developed the new two-parameter proof and received no development discussion or previous verdict on v720.
This review inspected the fixed argument and the actual scopes of the stated inputs.

For a fixed prime \(p\) and \(b\ge2\), let \(m=p^b\).
At an auxiliary prime in the class \(1+m\bmod mp\), the residue \(1+m\) has order \(p\) modulo \(mp\), as follows from \((1+m)^j\equiv1+jm\bmod mp\).
The residue field has a primitive order-\(m\) element \(z\), and \(z\ne\pm1\) since \(m\ge4\).
For a simple occurrence of \(z\) in the reduced Salem polynomial the two orders in v717 have ratio \(p\), forcing irreducibility of \(P(X^p)\) and thereby excluding \(p\mid R\).
This argument includes \(p=2\).

Conditional on \(n\equiv u\bmod m\), an equality \(z^{u-1}=\epsilon\) would give \(p^b\mid2(u-1)\), which is impossible outside the omitted class modulo \(p^{b-1}\).
Solving the resulting nondegenerate linear equation in the seed gives the claimed single residue \(a_\lambda\), and \(G_z(z)=\epsilon(z^2-1)\ne0\) shows \(a_\lambda\ne z\).
The derivative at \(z\) is affine in \(n\bmod\lambda\), with nonzero slope \(z^{u-1}(z-a_\lambda)\), so it vanishes in exactly one residue.
In each of the remaining \(\lambda-1\) delay residues the one selected seed residue gives a simple root.
The deleted cofactor has only the linear roots \(1,-1\), and thus preserves this obstruction.
The resulting excluded proportion is \((\lambda-1)/\lambda^2\) among pairs of residues modulo \(\lambda\).

For a finite set of the designated primes put \(Q=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\lambda\).
The sets of allowed seed and delay pairs are periodic modulo \(Q\) in the seed and modulo \(mQ\) in the delay.
Conditional on one retained \(u\), the Chinese remainder theorem identifies their joint residue counts with the independent counts for the different primes.
Every delay interval of length \(N\) gives counts within one of \(N/(mQ)\) in each residue, uniformly in its starting point.
The analogous error for each seed residue is at most one around \((A-2)/Q\).
At these fixed periods this proves convergence to the uniform residue fractions as \(\min(A,N)\to\infty\), independently of the side ratio and uniformly over delay translates.
The omitted \(p\) classes modulo \(p^b\) account for fraction \(p^{1-b}\).
For each retained \(u\) the complementary fraction is bounded by the product in (3).
At fixed \(p,b\) its logarithm is at most \(-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(1/\lambda-1/\lambda^2)\).
The source unit supplies reciprocal divergence for this particular reduced progression, so the product can be made arbitrarily small before taking \(b\to\infty\).
This proves uniform density zero for the fixed prime and sign, without a varying-modulus prime-distribution estimate.

The all-delay estimate in v703 bounds the number of *seeds* exceptional for the divisibility \(R\mid H(a)\) at even one delay or sign by \(\exp(C\sqrt{\log(A+1)})=o(A)\).
Each such seed costs at most \(N\) pairs in any translated interval.
For the others, \(p\mid R\) and \(p>Y\) imply that one of the three adjacent integers is divisible by \(p^2\), so \(p\le\sqrt{A+1}\).
For each such prime and each offset there are at most \(A/p^2+1\) eligible seeds.
After division by \(A-2\) the limsup of the total cost is at most \(3\sum_{j>Y}j^{-2}\), uniformly in the delay interval.
The finitely many smaller primes and both signs contribute zero upper limiting fraction by the first sieve, and this remaining tail tends to zero.
By v205 a pair is exceptional exactly when the maximal field index exceeds one for at least one sign.
When both indices equal one, the dilated Salem minimal polynomial is the single noncyclotomic factor and has logarithmic measure \(\log r\), since only torsion roots come from the dilated linear factors.

For initial intervals of uniformly bounded length there are only finitely many delays.
At every one, v694 gives a finite exceptional seed set covering both signs and every exponent.
Their finite union contributes \(O(1/(A-2))\) to the exceptional fraction for all the specified lengths.
An unbounded sequence of lengths has a subsequence tending to infinity, to which the translated growing-interval result applies.
This rules out any sequence violating the second limit.
The first limit concerns arbitrary translations in the delay variable when both sides grow, and the bounded-length conclusion uses the fixed starting delay \(M=6\).
