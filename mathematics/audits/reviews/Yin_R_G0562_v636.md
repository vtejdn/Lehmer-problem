## The contact divisor in the Laurent quotient

Both local equality criteria hold, and each prescribed contact divisor divides the order \(D_Y\) of the normalized Laurent quotient with the stated multiplicity.
The proof supports every stated lower bound at the coefficient cost of the original row pair.

**Affine and Laurent orders.**
Joint primitivity makes at least one reduction nonzero at every prime.
The ordinary resultant kills the affine quotient, and the finite-dimensional reductions control all successive layers of the primary groups, which proves finiteness before using the Sylvester comparison.
The polynomials of degree below \(m+n\) span each reduction, giving the integral surjection from the Sylvester cokernel onto the affine quotient.
If a leading coefficient is a local unit, division by that row reduces a relation to the two Sylvester degree bounds, including when the row is constant.
If both leading coefficients vanish at the prime, the reduced Sylvester kernel has dimension \(\deg g+\min(m-\deg\bar u,n-\deg\bar v)\), for \(g=\gcd(\bar u,\bar v)\).
With two nonzero reductions this counts the allowed multiples of the primitive syzygy, while if only \(\bar v\) survives the free coefficient has dimension \(n\).
The excess over \(\deg g\) forces strict loss in the order at that prime.
For two constant rows both quotients have order one by their normalized Bézout identity.

On each finite primary group localization retains the stable image of multiplication by \(Y\).
The stable image is the whole group precisely when \(Y\) is onto after reduction, or equivalently when \(Y\nmid g\).
This proves the criterion at zero, also when that primary group is trivial.
The two examples in the claim respectively have orders \((|S|,D,D_Y)=(t,1,1)\) and \((t,t,1)\), where \(S\) is the Sylvester cokernel.
The joint content is intrinsic because containment of the original ideal in \((k)\) is exactly divisibility of all coefficients by \(k\), also for Laurent generators.
A prime dividing the content of the original pair gives an infinite residue-ring quotient before normalization.
Any new pair generating the same normalized Laurent ideal has joint content one.
Its rational common factors can only be powers of \(Y\), since the Laurent ideal contains a nonzero integer.
Removing the powers preserves the norms and the Laurent quotient and weakly decreases the degrees, so Hadamard's upper bound also covers generators whose original ordinary resultant is zero.

**Integral contact divisors.**
Division of each graph by a monic squared minimum commutes with scaling the two rows, so the contact equations apply directly to the normalized pair.
For different seeds the two squared powered minima give an \(\ell\)-adic quotient of length \(4v_\ell(C)\), in which the row ideal has at most \(d=2\min(e,f)\) generators and is annihilated by \(k=\Delta_p\Delta_q\rho^3\).
The further quotient by the normalized rows therefore has length at least \(4v_\ell(C)-d\,v_\ell(k)\).
The unit constant term makes that quotient an image of \(A_Y\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\), so clipping this lower bound at zero gives exactly the asserted divisor.
For a repeated seed use the simultaneous image for the normalized rows over the splitting valuation ring \(O\).
Its cokernel in the sum of the labeled row quotients has at most \(2e^2\) generators and is killed by \(\tau_m\tau_n\).
The off-diagonal quotients contribute \(4v(z_i-w_j)-4v(mn(a_i-a_j))\) as lower bounds, and the diagonal quotients contribute \(2v(z_i-w_i)\).
The simultaneous image is a quotient of \(A_Y\otimes O\), whose length is \(v_O(D_Y)\) by cyclic decomposition.
Summation gives the correction \((\tau_m\tau_n)^{2e^2}(mn)^{4e(e-1)}|\operatorname{disc}P|^4\), and descent divides every valuation of a rational integer by the same ramification index.
This establishes the corrected divisors at all primes, hence their least common multiple for every qualifying choice.
The established fixed-seed asymptotics estimate the logarithms of these same integers, including the diagonal subtraction and the Salem coefficient \([(4e-6)p+(4e-2)q]\log M(P)\).

**Quantitative bounds.**
At the specified width every qualifying choice satisfies \(\log D_Y\ge\log W\ge(1-1/b)\Gamma\).
The upper bound uses the normalized row norm \(H/c\), while the denominator in \(K_Y\) and the weighted contact bound \(p h_1+q h_2\le\log H\) use the original \(H\).
For \(0<\eta<\min(\log Q,2/81)\), each \(h_i\le3\eta<2/27\), where \(t\exp((2/t)^{1/3})\) decreases.
Voutier's strict corollary bounds each \(d_i h_i\) below by \(\eta\exp((2/(3\eta))^{1/3})\).
The mixed estimate has coefficient \(4\sqrt{pq}/(p+q)\ge4\sqrt2/3\) and the repeated estimate has coefficient at least \(2\).
Multiplying the resulting bound for \(K_Y\) by \(\eta\) still gives divergence as \(\eta\to0\) and \(b(p)\to\infty\).

For the smaller logarithmic measure \(h\le\eta\) the crossed products give \(\Gamma/(p+q)\ge4dh/3\), also valid after the repeated-seed diagonal subtraction.
For \(0<\eta\le10^{-3}\), Voutier's two bounds give \(\log d>T_h>3\) on the increasing branch of \(T/\log T\).
The derivative of \(\log(s\exp T_s)\) with respect to \(T_s\) is positive there, so \(h\exp T_h\ge\eta\exp T_\eta\).
This proves \(K_Y>c_b\exp T_\eta\), and strict decrease of \(\log L/L\) above \(\mathrm e\) gives the stated inverse inequality.
At \(\eta=10^{-3}\), \(18/\log18<25/4<250^{1/3}\), so \(T_\eta>18\) throughout the indicated interval.
For \(b\ge4\), \(c_b\ge1\) and \(\mathrm e^{18}>20^6=64{,}000{,}000\).
Finally \(T_\eta\sim (4\eta)^{-1/3}\log((4\eta)^{-1/3})\), which gives the claimed lower limiting coefficient \(4^{-1/3}/3\).
The equivalence with \(\lambda=0\) concerns the original passing pairs at the same width and supplies no unconditional existence of such a sequence.
