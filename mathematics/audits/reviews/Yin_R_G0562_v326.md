### Normalization, derivative tests and quadrature

Subtracting \(a_0\) from both inputs leaves each difference unchanged, and division by the positive \(\lambda\) subtracts exactly \(\log\lambda\) from both means.
After this normalization a coefficient of \(z^m(A-c)\) at an exponent other than \(m\) has modulus one for every real \(c\).
For degree \(h\le2m\), the elementary symmetric expansion bounds every coefficient by \(2^h\) times the multiplicative Mahler measure, also when extreme coefficients vanish.
This gives \(j(c)\ge-\beta_m\), hence \(J\ge0\).
For \(|c|\ge4m\) the bound \(\|A\|_\infty\le2m\) gives \(j(c)\ge\log|c|-\log2\), and for smaller \(|c|\) the fiber lower bound suffices for (5).
The distinct frequencies \(\pm jn\) of a nonzero fast coefficient lie outside the slow band since \(d<n\), ensuring a nonzero diagonal polynomial with integrable logarithm.
For the double integral the positive logarithmic part is bounded for each given pair of inputs, and (4) then bounds the integral of the negative part on every fast fiber.

The matrix \(((ij)^k)_{1\le k\le 2m,\ 1\le |j|\le m}\) has determinant equal, up to the order of columns, to a Vandermonde determinant times \(\prod_j ij\).
Thus the normalized maximum coefficient gives a lower bound depending only on \(m\) for at least one derivative at every point.
Since all derivatives are real on the line and their next derivatives are uniformly bounded through order \(2m+1\), a mesh independent of the fast shape preserves that lower bound up to a factor two.
On each mesh interval the selected derivative of \(A-h\) stays bounded away from zero under (7).
From a sublevel set of length \(L>0\) one can select \(k+1\) points separated by \(L/(4k)\), since removing \(k\) intervals of radius \(L/(4k)\) covers at most \(L/2\).
The divided-difference formula yields \(L^k\ll_m u\) and summing the finitely many intervals gives (8), because \(u^{1/k}\le u^{1/(2m)}\) for \(u\le1\).
The supremum of \(|A-c|\) on the full period dominates its unit Fourier coefficient, and \(h\) differs from a selected constant value by at most \(2\pi\delta_m\), giving the anchor \(3/4\).

For \(|x|\le2B\) the clipped ratio in the quadrature estimate lies between \(u/(3B)\) and one.
For \(|x|>2B\) it is at least \(1/3\) by \(|a|\le2m<B\).
Between consecutive breakpoints the ratio is a positive quotient of affine functions, so its logarithm is monotonic there.
Summing its variation over the at most \(2d\) monotonic arcs of \(b\) bounds the variation of the composition by \(O_m(d\log(C_m/u))\).
Conditioning on the fast phase produces the uniform shifted \(n\)-point mesh, with error at most the variation divided by \(n\).
The subtracted \(\log(B+|b|)\) cancels after averaging, and integrating (8) with constant \(h\) gives the double-integral clipping loss \(O_m(u^{1/(2m)})\).

### Global and local estimates for the diagonal logarithm

There are two amplitude thresholds and \(4m\) derivative thresholds, each with at most \(2d\) solutions on the circle for nonconstant \(b\).
On a partition interval where \(b^{(k)}\) is the first derivative exceeding its threshold, this derivative has fixed sign and magnitude at least \(\delta_m n^k\).
Derivatives within their thresholds may change sign there.
The range of \(b^{(k-1)}\) has length at most \(2\delta_m n^{k-1}\) if \(k>1\), and at most \(2B\) for \(k=1\).
Monotonicity of this preceding quantity bounds the length of the interval by \(O_m(1/n)\).
This restricted sign observation is sufficient for the global and local flag counts.
Boundary intersections add \(O_m(d)\) periods in the global partition.
The remaining full periods have either \(|F|>1\) or both the finite-order estimate and the anchor.
Choosing \(D\) large leaves at least one such period, and adding one neighbor to each flagged run costs total length \(O_m(d/n)\).

On each enlarged interval the \(K\) terms of \(F\) have distinct imaginary frequencies, with \(2\le K\le 2d+2m+1\).
For relative sublevel length \(p\), the measurable-set Turán–Nazarov theorem and the anchor give \(3/4\le (C/p)^{K-1}u\).
Hence \(p\le (4/3)C u^{1/(K-1)}\) and the layer integral is at most \(C'(K-1)\) times the interval length.
The good periods contribute \(O_m(u^{1/(2m)})\).
At \(u=D^{-2m}\) this proves (12) and (13), since \(K\ll_{m,\gamma}E\) whenever \(E\ge\gamma d\) and \(E\ge1\).
This use of the external theorem concerns its recorded statement and hypotheses.

For \(E<\gamma d\), the input \(f=1+\log^+|b|\) meets the hypotheses of the adaptive cover in v325.
At a critical radius \(P_r*f=dr\ge1\), and the disjoint radius-\(r/5\) cores imply \(\sum r_i\ll1\).
At each selected enlargement \(2^kr_i\), the disjoint representative arcs in v325 bound the sum of the assigned \(r_i^2\) by \(O(2^{-k/2}E/d)\), giving \(\sum r_i^2\ll E/d\).
The periodic strip majorant depends only on \(b\) and \(d\), giving (15) on the larger disk.
Cauchy's inequalities there cost \(k!/(nr)^k\) for each scaled derivative up to \(k=2m\), bounded in terms of \(m\) because \(nr=DM\) and \(M\ge1\).
The farther endpoint of each signed threshold interval leaves distance at least \(\min(1/2,\delta_m/4)>0\) at its center.
Jensen therefore counts \(O_m(M)\) zeros of all the level functions in the disk of radius \(4r\).
The first-excessive-derivative argument now uses a lower bound \(\delta_m n^k/2\), giving \(O_m(M)\) locally flagged periods.
Since \(nr=DM\), the fixed lower threshold on \(D\) puts each enlarged run well inside the larger counting interval, with total length \(O_m(M/n)\) and each length \(l\le r/100\).

The slow disk bound and \(|A(nz)|\le2m e^{mn|\operatorname{Im}z|}\) give \(\sup\log^+|F|=O_m(M)\) on the radius-\(8l\) disk around the anchor.
Jensen counts \(O_m(M)\) zeros within radius \(5l\), since \(\log|F(\xi)|\ge\log(3/4)\).
Removing their Blaschke factors inside a zero-free boundary of radius between \(4l\) and \(5l\) gives a zero-free quotient whose logarithm is bounded below by \(-O_m(M)\) on the run by Harnack.
Each removed factor costs \(O(l)\) by integrating \(\log^+(6l/|t-\xi-a|)\) along the real interval, including when the zero \(a+\xi\) is real.
Summing the exceptional losses gives \(O_m(\sum_i M_i^2/n)=O_m(E/D)\).
The good periods contribute \(O_m(u^{1/(2m)}\sum_i(r_i+1/n))=O_m(u^{1/(2m)})\).
The covering arcs may overlap because the losses being summed are nonnegative.
These are exactly the slow-polynomial and disk estimates needed from the [v325 argument and review](Yin_R_G0562_v325.md#relative-trace-gap-review).

Taking \(u=D^{-2m}\) and restoring the scale gives (1).
If \(\mu-\log\lambda\le H\), then \(J\le H+\beta_m+|\mu-I|\), so absorbing its coefficient \(C_m/D\) bounds the discrepancy by \(O_{m,H}(\log(2D)/D)\).
For the necessity of scale control, multiply both inputs of the [v314 square-root-width example](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v314.md#statement) by \(e^{-\mu_{n,d}}\).
Its new diagonal mean is zero and \(d/n\to0\), while its discrepancy keeps the positive lower limit in v314 (4).
An unnormalized upper bound on the real diagonal mean thus allows failure of uniform convergence.

### The integral specialization

The \(Y\)-width is \(2s\), and \(Y^s\mathcal F\) has leading and constant \(Y\)-coefficient one, including when the slow term is constant.
The torus integral is its logarithmic Mahler measure.
The v156 monic partial-degree result gives \(I=0\) or \(I\ge c_{2s}\ge c_{2m}\), with the quoted denominator \(2e^{16384}(\log(6m))^{48}\).
In the zero case a nontorsion \(Y\)-dependent component would contradict the nonmonic component bound of v156.
The remaining horizontal factors are cyclotomic in primitive characters \(X^aY^b\), and the base factor divides the leading \(Y\)-coefficient in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\), so is a Laurent unit.
After specialization, a nonconstant character gives only roots of unity and unit extreme coefficients, including when its new exponent is negative.
A constant character contributes the integer \(\Phi_h(1)\), whose vanishing would contradict the surviving extreme coefficients at \(\pm sn\).
The absolute value of the leading coefficient of \(P\) is the product of the absolute values of these integers and equals one.
Consequently every such integer is a unit and the specialized measure is zero.
The [review of v156](Yin_R_G0562_v156.md#monic-fiber-mahler-gap) covers the partial-degree theorem and its component inputs.

For a positive-measure specialization, \(I\ge c_{2m}\) and \(\lambda\ge1\), so (1) gives \(\mu\ge(1-C_m/D)I-C_m(1+\beta_m+\log(2D))/D\).
Taking \(D\) as in the submission gives \(\mu\ge c_{2m}/2\).
When \(d=0\) the equality \(\mu=I\) follows from invariance of circle measure under the \(n\)-th power map.
The integral conclusion allows multiple roots and reducible products, and concerns the full measure of (2) at fixed fast degree.
