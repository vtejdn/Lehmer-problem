## Local degrees, exceptional roots and positive measure

At 2026-11-17T12:55 UTC an agent for Yin independently checked the [fixed submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v235.md#decision-dyadic-reciprocal-five-submitted), including the explicit positive-measure qualification in its example.
The reviewer took no part in developing the proof and received its fixed text, scope and named dependencies without the development discussion or a previous finding on this input.
The degree alternative, the exact local factorization and both Mahler measure bounds hold with the stated hypotheses.

Write \(N=2^u\) and \(H=3^v h\).
The roots of \(X^{3H}-1\) outside \(X^H-1\) are simple and have orders \(s=3^{v+1}t\) for \(t\mid h\).
Each appears exactly \(N\) times after the Frobenius power in the reduction of \(P/a\).
The order of two modulo \(s\) is a multiple of \(2\cdot3^v\), because \(v_3(4^k-1)=1+v_3(k)\) for positive \(k\).
This follows by successively cubing, with a final power prime to three preserving the valuation.
The Teichmüller lift \(\xi\) has \(\xi^n\) of order three, so the outer terms in the normalized value contribute exactly \(-a\).
If \(3^v\nmid l\), the three-part of the order of \(\bar\xi^l\) is at least nine.
Its trace with its inverse lies outside \(\mathbb F_2\), since the only nonzero elements with trace in that field have order one or three.
Thus the normalized value has valuation at most \(w\), including when \(c=a\), and exactly one for \(w=1\).

In the latter case the translated monic degree-\(N\) Hensel factor in each root class is Eisenstein over the unramified splitting field.
Over \(\mathbb Q_2\) the lift attached to a full orbit is irreducible, since any divisor meeting that orbit contains the degree-\(N\) factor at one root and all its Frobenius conjugates.
Each such lift occurs once, and summing their degrees gives \(N\sum_{t\mid h}\varphi(3^{v+1}t)=2n\).
For \(h=1\) the orbit length equals \(\varphi(3^{v+1})\), so there is just one lift, including when \(N=1\).

For \(N<2^{w+4}\), the degree of any rational factor containing a given root is at least the residue degree \(f_s\ge2\cdot3^v\), which suffices since \(w\ge1\).
In the complementary range \(J=N/2^{w+1}\ge8\), the identity for \(\binom{\pm n}{i}\) gives \(v_2\binom{\pm n}{i}\ge u-v_2(i)\ge w+1\) through degree \(J\).
This applies also to the multiplier \((1+T)^n\) through those degrees.
If the constant has valuation greater than \(w\), the first coefficient detects \(y+y^{-1}\) for \(y=\bar\xi^l\ne1\) because \(l\) is odd.
For \(y=1\) the second coefficient divided by \(2^w\) reduces to the odd scalar \((b/2^w)l^2\).
The witness \(j\le2\) therefore also bounds the multiplicity of an exact zero at the coordinate origin.

Let \(V\) be the lower polygon after omitting an initial zero of the polynomial with its full multiplicity, keeping the original abscissas.
Then \(V(j)\le w\), \(V(N)=0\), and the slopes up to \(N\) are strictly negative, since the first unit coefficient is at \(N\).
Every negative segment extending to the right of \(j\) ends at \(q>J\).
An endpoint with \(j<q\le J\) would have to be a coefficient point strictly below \(w\).
If this segment has absolute slope \(s_0\), monotonicity of slopes gives \((q-j)s_0\le V(j)-V(q)\le w\), also for a segment straddling \(j\).
Hence the positive valuations exceeding \(w/(J-2)\), together with the exact zeros at the origin, account for at most \(j\) roots with multiplicity in the class.
A remaining root has ramification index at least \((J-2)/w\) over the unramified splitting field.
Its conjugates over that field stay in its class, and the \(f_s\) distinct Frobenius classes contribute disjointly to the rational degree.
Since \(J-2\ge3J/4\), this gives the asserted lower bound with an absolute constant.
Under coprimality and \(v\ge1\), one can take \(j=0\) throughout.
When \(v=0\), even a divisor supported entirely on exceptions has degree at most \(2\cdot 2h\).

The width bound applies to the actual five nonzero coefficients at width \(2n\), with effective support at most five.
For a nontorsion unit of degree \(d\le4h\), the radial floor gives \(1/10\le d\sum_i\log^2|\alpha_i|\le4d\,m(\alpha)^2\) by the product formula.
This yields \(m(\alpha)\gg h^{-1/2}\), sufficient for all \(h,w\ge1\).
A nonunit endpoint of a primitive factor or a nontrivial integer content costs at least \(\log2\).
For an irreducible nontorsion factor after dilation, \(\beta=\alpha^g\) remains nontorsion and satisfies the reduced polynomial with the same coefficient length, so \(m(\alpha)\ge m(\beta)/R_L\).
Additivity extends both bounds to positive-measure integral divisors.
In the example the leading term is positive and \(P(1)=-1\), so a real root exceeds one.
The qualification on the factor measures matters even at \(h=1\), since at \(n=4,l=3\) a primitive twelfth root has traces \(-1\) and \(0\) at the two exponents and is a zero of \(P\).
