## The least norm and its two-value consequences

At 2026-11-19T01:37 UTC I reviewed the complete [arbitrary-exponent argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v272.md#rate) for Sheng Yin as a reviewer outside its development.
The assignment supplied the fixed proof and its scope, without the development discussion, author explanations or a prior finding on this argument.
I checked the cited mathematical inputs at the points described below.

### The weight of primes dividing the exponent

Put \(m=2n\).
For a fixed prime \(\mathfrak l\mid\ell\), the reduction order \(a\) is prime to \(\ell\).
If \(a\mid m\), successively raising \(x^a\) to \(\ell\)-th powers eventually puts its difference from one at normalized valuation strictly above \(1/(\ell-1)\).
Indeed the new valuation is at least \(\min(v+1,\ell v)\), strictly increasing by a positive lower bound while \(v\) stays above its initial positive value.
The binomial term linear in the difference then has unique smallest valuation, giving an increment of one at each further lift.
The prime-to-\(\ell\) part of \(m/a\) leaves the valuation unchanged and nontorsion excludes an exact zero.
This gives the required \(O(\log m)\) at each prime of a fixed exceptional set.

At an unramified odd \(\ell\mid m\), every positive valuation is already above the threshold when starting at \(x^a\).
Its initial contribution is bounded by \(\log |N(x^a-1)|=O(1+a)\), and the later increments together contribute at most \([L:\mathbb Q]\log m\) over all \(\ell\mid m\).
Writing \(d=[L:\mathbb Q]\), I obtain the respective upper bounds \(O_{L,x}(\sqrt m\log m)\) and \(O_{L,x}(m^{1-1/(2d)})\) on the initial contributions below and above \(m^{1/(2d)}\).
These follow from \(a\mid\ell^f-1\) at a prime of residue degree \(f\le d\), and \(a\mid m/\ell^{v_\ell(m)}\) at every contributing prime.
There are at most \(2d\) distinct prime divisors in the upper range.
Both \(x^n-1\) and \(x^n+1\) divide \(x^{2n}-1\), so the discarded part of the signed valuation sum is \(o(n)\) also when the sign varies.

### Contraction and the lower norm bound

The [signed resultant estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) applies to each irreducible factor, since no root is torsion, giving \(\log R_n=nW+O_F(\log(2n))\) by multiplicativity.
At a retained prime, the signed nodes have distinct residues and lie in the unramified extension, so the maximum valuation \(v_\ell(B_n)\) is an integer.
Any positive maximum occurs at two distinct inverse nodes of equal valuation, because the residues \(1,-1\) cannot be zeros of \(F\) there.
This proves \(2\log B_n\le\log R_n\).

For the converse, take one representative from every inverse pair.
The logarithmic norm sum of the \(t_xw_x\) for these representatives is exactly \(\frac12\log R_n\), with prime weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/d\).
Unique lifting of the residue node at each contributing retained prime bounds every \(t_xw_x\) by \(e_{\mathfrak l}v_\ell(B_n)\).
Summing the latter with these weights costs exactly \(\log B_n\).
The loss in taking the maximum over \(x\) is at most the sum of pairwise minima, with a bounded factor for the multiplicities.
For an independent pair, any relation between \(\sigma_n x^n,\sigma_n y^n\) would give a dependence modulo torsion between \(x,y\).
The pairs are distinct as \(n\) varies and have heights growing at most linearly.
Thus the [finite-place consequence of Proposition 2](../literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) gives \(o(n)\) for the overlap, by using each fixed positive height coefficient in turn.
For a dependent pair, reduction of the primitive relation modulo the common ideal is valid also for negative exponents because \(x,y\) are units.
The ideal then contains the nonzero \(\eta^n-\sigma_n^{u+v}\), whose norm ranges over a bounded finite set.
The primitive slope is unique up to sign for a pair of nontorsion elements dependent modulo torsion, so the phase condition permits a fixed relation for the pair.
Together with the preceding prime estimate this proves \(\log B_n=nW/2+o(n)\) for the list and for every nonempty fixed subproduct.

Removing the maximal common \((Y-\sigma_n)^j\) from the rows preserves divisibility by \(F\) after substitution.
Evaluation of the quotient graph at the inverse maximizing nodes gives a two-by-two integral system of unit determinant in the local ring.
Hence both evaluated row integers are divisible by \(B_n\), and at least one is nonzero by maximality.
It is a divided derivative bounded by \(\binom bj\) times the maximum original row one-norm.
Since \(\log\binom bj\le b\log2=o(n)\), the bound covers fixed widths as well as growing sublinear widths.

### Attaining the rate at either graph sign

I checked the [jet count](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) against the embedding bound on each divided derivative.
The box uses \(2(b_0+1)\) distinct coefficient positions once \(n\ge2\).
Its maximum monomial exponent is at most \(n(b_0+1)\), and the number of rational jet coordinates is \(J=\sum_i t_i\deg P_i\), including the single value for \(t_i=1\).
The leading slope at fixed \(b_0\) is \(W(b_0+1)/(2(b_0+1)-J)\), tending to \(W/2\) as the fixed width increases.
At \(b_0=b(n)-1\) the error terms in the count are \(o(n)\).

Injectivity on the full root set gives \(\deg Q_i=\deg P_i\), \(\log M(Q_i)=nh_i\), and disjoint powered orbits.
If one row vanishes at a powered root, the graph equation forces both to contain \(Q_i\).
The substitution \(Q_i(X^n)\) removes exactly one order at each original root of \(P_i\).
The subproduct bound therefore gives a nonzero quotient row of log norm at least \(n(W-h_i)/2-o(n)\), with the bound zero by integrality for an empty subproduct.
Recovering its measure loses at most \(b_0\log2\), and multiplication by \(Q_i\) then adds \(nh_i\).
The resulting lower slope \((W+h_i)/2\) exceeds the achievable upper slope, choosing the fixed-width excess strictly below \(\min_i h_i/2\).
Thus all prescribed evaluations survive.
The [two signed projections](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention) preserve each reciprocal power by formal reversal.
At each powered node the two candidate numerators cannot both vanish, and multiplying by \(Y+1\) or \(Y-1\) does not introduce a zero.
One integer slope avoiding the finitely many forbidden values supplies the chosen graph sign and increases the width by one.
The positive integer one-norms therefore have a least member throughout the asserted tail.

### Dyadic orbits and arbitrary pairs

The [norm representative](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) preserves the measure and gives full degree at every power.
Below the stated budget the monic unit reduction and [Smyth's bound](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) ensure reciprocity.
Within one orbit, equal positive absolute heights force every dependence to have primitive exponents of equal absolute value.
The all-power property excludes a torsion quotient between distinct conjugates, and reciprocity then identifies the only possible torsion product partner as the inverse.
For distinct dyadic levels any dependent roots come from equal or inverse underlying conjugates, so after orientation the higher-level root is an exact even power of the lower-level root, with phase \(1\) and odd primitive exponent sum.
Hence the auxiliary sign \(-1\) works simultaneously at every \(n\).
The heights distinguish the dyadic levels and no power loses degree, giving the required injectivity.
At order two the sum \(W=2(2^r-1)h<B\) leaves exactly the needed strict slack in the row budget.
The factor \(Y^k(Y+1)^e\) with \(e\in\{0,1\}\) preserves reversal and nonvanishing at the selected roots and costs at most two in norm.
Thus even after padding to \(\log b=o(n)\) the budget holds.
For \(r=2\), any positive irreducible \(h<B/6\) realizes both \(h\) and \(2h\), which proves the conditional lower bound on \(\lambda\).

For two arbitrary unequal small irreducible measures, a torsion quotient across orbits would make some powered representatives conjugate, equating both their full degrees and their absolute heights and hence their measures.
For a dependent pair the [matching argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#two) gives a fixed positive primitive slope, a unique positively oriented match for each conjugate and conjugate phases.
If their common order is odd, negating the orbit with odd exponent changes each phase to its negative, of even order, preserving all field-degree and measure conditions.
An even-order phase has nontrivial \(n\)-th power for each odd \(n\), so auxiliary sign \(+1\) works for the entire pair.
For opposite exponent parities, the sign \(-1\) also resolves every simultaneous trivial phase power at even \(n\), and \(+1\) handles the other powers.
For independent orbits the condition is empty.
Taking both multiplicities equal to two gives strict slack precisely under \(h_1+h_2<B/2\).
I find the whole fixed proof valid, including its uniformity in the exponent with the input list fixed.
The gap consequence assumes an infinite two-value exclusion.
The cited resultant, height, nonreciprocity and unit-gcd results were used at their stated scopes, without independent reproof of their external source theorems.

## The formal Claim and its applications

At 2026-11-19T01:41 UTC, after completing the preceding finding, I independently inspected the [formal Claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#statement), its complete proof and both applications for Sheng Yin.
I received its fixed text without participating in its preparation.
The hypothesis permits a different auxiliary sign at each exponent and requires a common sign for all the dependent pairs at that exponent.
The constructed row may independently have either prescribed graph sign.
The [discarded-prime estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#bad) treats the primes above \(2n\) and the fixed exceptional primes using the residue-order bounds and logarithmic lifting cost checked above.
In the [norm proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#argument), taking pairwise minima costs at most a fixed multiplicity factor, and the lower bound for a subproduct applies to the remaining contacts after division by \(Q_i\).
This checks the two width quantifiers separately, including a fixed width for each requested positive slope excess, and eventual simultaneous nonvanishing.
The [two applications](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests) use full degree at all powers for the chosen small-measure representatives, conjugate phases for a dependent pair, and the fixed double-factor count.
Indeed a row of fixed-width slope at most \(W/2+\delta\) has normalized logarithmic budget at most \(4W+8\delta+o(1)\), strictly below \(\log 2\) for the specified slack.
The padding contributes \(o(1)\) also when the growing width exceeds \(n\), since its cost enters through the logarithm and the norm multiplier is bounded.
With \(r=1\) a single value is realized.
With \(r=2\) the contrapositive gives the asserted conditional gap on an arbitrary infinite sequence, including powers of two.
The general pair is asserted on odd exponents, where a phase of even order suffices.

At 2026-11-19T01:42 UTC I checked the revised statement and application with the exclusion chosen for each input.
Assuming \(\lambda<B/6\), first choose a small positive irreducible measure \(h<B/6\) and fix its reciprocal all-power representative \(\delta\).
The exclusion hypothesis now supplies a graph sign, widths and an unbounded set of exponents for this same \(\delta\).
The realization at \(r=2\) uses the actual minima \(P,Q\) of \(\delta,\delta^2\) on every sufficiently large exponent of that set, since padding preserves both double factors and both numerator values at the chosen sign.
Thus the per-input exclusion contradicts \(\lambda<B/6\) without a common width sequence or a uniform threshold over \(\delta\).
The inverse pairs of conjugates have no fixed points, so \(P\) has even degree.
Since squaring preserves its degree, the squared conjugates are distinct and give every root of \(Q\) exactly once.
Multiplying \(X-\delta_j\) and \(X+\delta_j\) over all conjugates yields \(Q(X^2)=P(X)P(-X)\), with positive leading coefficient by even degree.
Modulo two this gives \(Q(X^2)=P(X)^2=P(X^2)\), and injectivity of polynomial substitution gives \(Q\equiv P\bmod 2\).
I find the revised formal statement, its argument and both applications supported, including the input-dependent exclusion.
