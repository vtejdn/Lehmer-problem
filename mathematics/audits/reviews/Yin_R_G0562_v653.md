## The scaled rows retain the full multiplicity of each ordered representation

On 2026-11-20 at 10:26 UTC a separate Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the complete [C345 v520 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v520.md#decision-all-sums-local-multiplicity-submission) without taking part in its development.
The Hasse coefficient bounds, the order of the local target and both global invariant-factor divisibilities follow under the stated hypotheses.
This finding uses the fixed prime-field theorem and the finiteness theorem at their stated scopes.

The certified characteristic exceeds every exponent prime, the reduced roots are simple, and the prescribed choice of \(g\) gives \(t>2\) for sufficiently large \(L\).
Write \(\alpha=\xi_z(1+c)\), with \(c\ne0\) since a torsion lift would make the irreducible minimum cyclotomic.
For every nonzero integer \(h\) prime to \(\ell\), the quotient \(((1+c)^h-1)/c\) reduces to \(h\), using inversion when \(h<0\).
Taking \(h=n\) gives \(v_\ell(c)=w\).
Inside a fixed residue, distinct first exponents have differences divisible by \(t\) and of absolute value between one and \(L<\ell\).
Taking \(h\) equal to such a difference gives valuation exactly \(w\) for the difference of the lifted nodes, since their root-of-unity parts agree.
The exponents \(-q=p-n\) have exactly the same pairwise differences, so the second list also gives \(m_y\) distinct points after scaling and reduction.
An intersection of the two lists after reduction causes no loss because they test different row combinations.

For either pair \((s,\beta)=(p,\alpha),(q,\alpha^{-1})\), set \(x=\beta^s\), \(a=(x-\xi_y)/\ell^w\) and \(D=O[T]/(T-a)^k\).
The binomial expansion of \(\beta(1+\ell^w(T-a)/x)^{1/s}\) through degree \(k-1\) defines an element \(f\in D\).
Its coefficients are integral even if \(k\ge\ell\), by continuity of each binomial polynomial at \(1/s\) along nonnegative integer approximations.
The formal binomial identity gives \(f^s=\xi_y+\ell^wT\), and \(P(f)\in(T-a)D\) since \(P(\beta)=0\).
Monic division of the normalized integral graph by \(P^k\) has zero remainder, as the remainder vanishes over the rationals.
Substitution yields \(\ell^W(u_*+fv_*)=0\) in \(D\), where \(W\) is the common minimum over the two scaled rows before division.
The powers of \(T-a\) below degree \(k\) are an \(O\)-basis of \(D\), so cancellation is valid, and reduction gives multiplicity \(k\) at the reduced center for the combination labeled by \(\bar\beta\).

At least one normalized row survives reduction, while every term of degree greater than \(W/w\) disappears in each row.
If \(W<wkm_y\), both reduced combinations vanish as polynomials because their respective \(m_y\) distinct roots account for more multiplicity than their degrees permit.
Their coefficient matrix has determinant \(z^{-1}-z\ne0\), contradicting survival of a row.
Before division the coefficient at degree \(a\) is \(\ell^{wa}\) times the original Hasse derivative at \(\xi_y\), so \(W\ge wd_y\) gives depth \(w(d_y-a)\) through degree \(d_y-1\).
These depths are exactly the coefficient conditions for the stated ideal power.

At each center the truncated powers of \(Y-\xi_y\) give independent cyclic factors of orders \(\ell^{w(d_y-a)}\) for \(0\le a<d_y\), directly from the monomial generators of the ideal power.
The constant term \(\xi_y\) is a unit and the displacement is nilpotent, allowing Laurent representatives.
Different residual centers give comaximal powers, and coefficientwise reduction modulo \(\ell^{w\max d_y}\) realizes every resulting polynomial representative by an integer polynomial.
Thus the finite group maps onto the local product, and its primary subgroup alone maps onto this target.
Summing the exponents gives the displayed logarithmic order, while \(\sum m_y=R_n\) and the at most \(\varphi(t)\) elements of order \(t\) give both the quadratic and largest-multiplicity estimates.

In each increasing block of \(k\) cyclic exponents the \(i\)-th exponent is at least \(i\), including in the first block when \(w=1\).
There are \(R_n\ge R_*\) blocks at each prime, and the primary decomposition of the finite source makes the selected local surjections simultaneous.
At each of the \(R_*\) copies and each layer \(i\), the Chinese remainder theorem combines the cyclic groups into one of order \(Q^i\).
A surjection induces a surjection on successive primary layers \(\ell^{i-1}A_\ell/\ell^i A_\ell\), whose dimension counts the factors with exponent at least \(i\).
Applying this at \(i=1,k\) at every chosen prime proves the two divisibilities in the common decreasing invariant-factor ordering.
Finally the chosen prime count gives \(\log Q\ge\lceil L/m^2\rceil\log L\gg L/\log L\).
