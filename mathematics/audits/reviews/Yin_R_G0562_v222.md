## Independent finding

At 2026-11-17T11:50 UTC, a separate agent acting for Yin independently checked the [submitted theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v222.md#statement) and all five parts of its proof.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and the assigned scope and had taken no part in its development.
No proof-generation discussion, exploration notes or earlier verdicts were supplied or used to form the finding.
The reviewer checked the revised source citation for progression densities against the lecture statement on printed p. 8 and rechecked its use in the endpoint count.
The argument establishes both assertions, for both signs, with absolute constants in the disjoint-pair construction and uniformity in the measure threshold for each fixed gap constant.

For the two-form sieve, removing primes dividing \(2mj\) leaves two distinct roots modulo each sieve prime.
Counting residue classes by the Chinese remainder theorem gives error at most \(2^{\omega([d,e])}\) in each term of the quadratic form.
The divisor identity follows prime by prime from \(1+1/b(p)=1/w(p)\).
Möbius inversion gives \(w(d)\lambda_d=\mu(d)b(d)\sum_{(a,d)=1,\,da\in\mathcal D}b(a)/G\), so \(\lambda_1=1\), the quadratic main coefficient is \(1/G\), and \(|\lambda_d|\le\prod_{p\mid d}p/(p-2)\le d\).
There are at most \(z^2\) terms with weight product at most \(z^2\) and residue error at most \(z^2\), giving absolute total error \(O(z^6)\).
In the small-prime product the inclusion probability is \(2/p\).
The factorial estimate bounds the expected logarithm by \(4\log y=(\log z)/4\), so a fixed positive fraction of the product contributes to \(G\).
The reciprocal Euler product dominates the harmonic sum up to \(y=x^{1/320}\), and omitting primes dividing \(2mj\) costs at most \(4F(m)F(j)\).
Thus the leading constant is absolute, and \(x^{3/10}=o(x/(\log x)^3)\) absorbs the remainder uniformly for \(m\le C\log x\), with the onset allowed to depend on \(C\).

The nonnegative divisor expansion for \(F\) has convergent mean product because \(a(p)/p=O(p^{-2})\).
For a common divisor \(m\ge D\) the gap is \(mj\le H\), so the bounded mean over \(j\) leaves \(xH/(\log x)^2\) times \(\sum_{m\ge D}F(m)/m^2\ll1/D\).
This includes composite common divisors and gives zero eligible pairs when \(D>H\).
For fixed \(B\), inclusion-exclusion over \(4\) and the odd primes through \(B\) uses pairwise coprime moduli and the natural prime density \(1/\varphi(a)\) in each complementary intersection.
Subtracting the prime counts at \(x\) and \(2x\) and removing the three exceptional order sets gives the claimed lower bound.
The bound on \(\sum_{p\le B}1/p\) implies \(B\Delta_B^2\to\infty\).
Pairing adjacent entries wastes at most one prime, and at most \(x/H=\Delta_B x/(16\log x)\) pairs have gap exceeding \(H\).
The upper bound for gcd failures costs \(O(1/(B\Delta_B^2))\) of the retained lower bound, with an absolute constant.
Fixing \(B\) first and then removing gaps below \(c_2\log x\) loses only \(O(c_2 x/\log x)\) further pairs and preserves disjointness.

For the degree second moment, sorting the three distinct primes counted by \(D(p)(D(p)-1)\) gives two shifts in \([1,2H]\) and at most six orderings per triple.
Omitting primes dividing \(6ij(j-i)\) leaves three distinct root classes modulo primes \(p\ge5\).
The same diagonalization has \(b(p)/w(p)=p/(p-3)\le p\) and residue error \(3^{\omega([d,e])}\le de\).
The mean logarithm of the small-prime product is at most \(6\log y<(\log z)/2\), yielding the asserted three-power Euler-product bound with the same \(O(z^6)\) error.
Each of \(i,j,j-i\) has at most \(2H\) occurrences at a fixed value.
The bounded mean of \((k/\varphi(k))^9\) therefore bounds each cubic sum for Hölder's inequality by \(O(H^2)\).
The resulting second moment is \(O_C(x/\log x)\), so a set of \(o(x/\log x)\) exceptional endpoints meets at most \(o_C(x/\log x)\) pairs by Cauchy–Schwarz, including when one endpoint belongs to many pairs.

For any pair of good endpoints, \(q=r+g\) is an odd prime coprime to the even \(g\), and \(r/(2g+1)^9\) tends to infinity uniformly for \(g\le C\log x\).
The cyclotomic classification therefore gives precisely the simple factor \(X+\varepsilon\).
The positive exterior counts of the remaining constituents sum to \(g\).
On each simple grid of size \(N=q-1,q-1,r-1\), reciprocity and Frobenius invariance make \(g(d-\delta_\ell)-Ns\) a multiple of its orbit size \(t_\ell\).
The pre-rounding degree estimate bounds this integer by \(O_C(\sqrt{x}(\log x)^{7/2})\), less than the required order at the corresponding endpoint, for every constituent.
Consequently the denominator of \(s/g\) divides all three \(N/t_\ell\), hence divides \(\gcd(r-1,q-1)\).
On the disjoint pairs that gcd equals two, while \((q-1)/t_2\) is odd because \(q\equiv3\bmod4\) and \(t_2\) is even.
Thus each positive share equals one, proving irreducibility and giving the asserted error in the measure formula with \(s=g\).
On all good pairs the error is uniformly \(o_C(1/g)\).
Writing \(s/g=u/v\) in lowest terms, a factor of measure at most \(\eta\) forces \(\gcd(r-1,q-1)\ge v\ge(\log6)/(2\eta)\).
Using \(D=1\) when this threshold is less than one, and zero qualifying good pairs when it exceeds \(H\), gives the stated bound uniformly over \(\eta>0\).
The exceptional-endpoint term is independent of \(\eta\), which also proves the asserted proportion for every subcollection of the stated size.
