## Independent finite computation and application

At 2026-11-18T06:08 UTC a reviewer outside development inspected the [finite exclusion](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0167/readme.md#p53) and its [native verifier](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0167/verify.py) for Sheng Yin.
The inputs included the polynomial, the specified finite domain, the submitted calculation and the cited mathematical dependencies.
The reviewer received no development dialogue and consulted the existing review of the degree estimate as background for its application.

For \(1\le a<b\le52\), the residues \(0,a,b\) contribute respectively three, two and two exponents in \([0,106]\).
Thus the coefficient vector of a multiple on that support would lie in the kernel of the ten-by-seven matrix of monomial remainders modulo \(L\).
For the [separate verifier](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0167/checks/maximal_minors.py), the reviewer divided each of \(1,x,\ldots,x^{106}\) individually by the monic integer polynomial \(L\), then computed all \(\binom{10}{7}=120\) maximal minors of each matrix by Laplace expansion along successive columns.
For each of the 1,326 pairs at least one integer minor was nonzero modulo 1009, which gives a trivial rational kernel.
By Cauchy–Binet the sum of the squares of the 120 integer minors equals the Gram determinant.
Its least value was \(27\,346\,309\,442\), uniquely at \((a,b)=(15,34)\).
Running the submitted verifier and the separate verifier from the repository root reproduced the same rank and minimum.

The [degree-ten benchmark](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#minus-delay-progression) identifies \(L\) as monic and irreducible with \(1<M(L)<\theta\), where \(\theta\) is the plastic number.
The constant and linear coefficients of \(L\) are both one, so its support differences have gcd one.
If a cyclotomic multiple occupies two or three classes at 53, the [centered lift](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#lift) sends its one opposite pair to \(\pm1\), giving fast width two.
Since \(48<53\), the [surviving factor theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#statement) yields a factor of fast width \(s\le2\) whose monomial-normalized diagonal is divisible by \(L\) and has degree at most \(10/(1-48/53)=106\).
That diagonal occupies at most \(s+1\le3\) residues because its terms in each fast row share a residue.
Normalization places an occupied exponent at zero, so its support fits one of the tested triples even if fewer than three residues occur.
The rank result excludes every such diagonal.

For \(p>58\), the [reciprocal three-term bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v348.md#primes) excludes two and three classes.
In fact the surviving diagonal would have degree at most \(10p/(p-48)<p\), hence at most three terms.
Its unit endpoints make a binomial cyclotomic, while reciprocity forces a trinomial to have the form \(x^{2e}+u x^e+1\) with \(u\in\mathbb Z\).
The quadratic formula gives measure one for \(|u|\le2\) and measure at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2>\theta\) otherwise, contradicting \(1<M(L)<\theta\).
A single occupied residue would give a polynomial in \(x^p\) because the constant coefficient is nonzero.
The orbit of an expanding root under multiplication by the \(p\)-th roots of unity would supply \(p\) distinct expanding roots, all of which would have to belong to \(L\).
Since the next prime after 53 is 59, every cyclotomic multiple of \(L\) therefore occupies at least four residues at every prime \(p\ge53\).
The degree bound and the benchmark are accepted at their cited scopes, and the finite computation examines only the modulus 53.
