## Torsion extraction, irreducibility under dilation and the four minima

The torsion factor, the remaining irreducible factor and the four exact minima follow under the stated assumptions \(n>m\ge1\), with both signs allowed and no restriction on their common divisor.

### The torsion test

At successive test angles \((k+1/2)\pi/n\), \(0\le k<n\), the term \(2\sin(nt)\) has modulus two and alternating signs while the second term has modulus at most one.
The \(n-1\) intervening zeros therefore account for the full degree after the strictly monotone substitution \(x=2\cos t\).
Both endpoints are nonzeros.
The identity \(U_{gk-1}(\cos t)=U_{g-1}(\cos t)\,U_{k-1}(\cos(gt))\) extracts the common factor as a polynomial identity, including where a sine denominator vanishes.

For torsion \(z\) over a trace root, \((z^m-z^{-m})/2=-\varepsilon(z^n-z^{-n})\) is an algebraic integer.
All conjugates have modulus at most one since they are obtained by conjugating a root of unity.
Kronecker gives zero or a root of unity.
In the zero case the common equation is \(z^{2g}=1\) with the two excluded endpoints, exactly the roots above the common trace factor.
Otherwise \(|\sin(mt)|=1\) and \(|\sin(nt)|=1/2\), hence \(z^m=\pm i\) and \(z^n\) is a primitive twelfth root.
For \(w=z^g\) its order is then a multiple of twelve dividing \(\gcd(4M,12N)=4\gcd(M,3)\).
Thus it is exactly twelve, \(N\) is coprime to twelve, and \(M\) is \(3\) or \(9\bmod12\).
The signed sine test at the primitive root of angle \(\pi/6\) checks the full Galois orbit, because the equation with \(w^N-w^{-N}\) and \(w^M-w^{-M}\) is rational.
Taking all preimages explains exactly \(X_g^2-3\), and these roots cannot be shared with \(U_{g-1}\) since \(w\) has order twelve.

The quotient by the two monic factors is integral.
The original polynomial has leading coefficient two and reduces modulo two to the monic smaller Chebyshev polynomial, hence the quotient is primitive with leading coefficient two.
Every monic irreducible factor with all conjugates in the trace interval would lift to a monic polynomial all of whose zeros have modulus one, bringing back torsion roots already removed.
Factoring the remaining primitive polynomial into two nonmonic primitive factors would require leading coefficient at least four in absolute value.
It cannot be constant because a constant of leading coefficient two is not primitive.
This proves irreducibility for the actual dilation, and substitution at \(2\) gives \(A(2)=2N+\varepsilon M\) even in the optional-factor case.

### The extremal multipliers

For every admissible \(G=AR\), \(R(2)\) is a nonzero integer.
The endpoint cost is at least \(2N+\varepsilon M\).
In the positive-sign case the numerator before optional division attains this bound by \(|U_{k-1}|\le k\).
For two odd reduced frequencies with negative sign, the smaller Laurent support is contained in the larger one and all coefficients of the weighted difference are nonnegative.
Evaluating at \(x=2\) gives its norm and realizes \(2N-M\).

For the negative sign and opposite parities the optional factor is absent, and \(|A|=2N+M\) at every trace with \(X_g=-2\).
If the product norm is strictly smaller, \(R(y)\) has modulus below one on that entire fiber, which contains all conjugates of each of its traces.
Its norm as an algebraic integer forces \(R(y)=0\).
For odd \(g\) this includes \(-2\), so \(4\mid R(2)\), and \(4(2N-M)>2N+M\).
For even \(g\) the simple roots of \(X_{g/2}\) are exactly that fiber since \(X_g+2=X_{g/2}^2\).
Monic division in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) gives \(X_{g/2}\mid R\) and \(2\mid R(2)\).
Thus every improvement costs at least \(2(2N-M)\).
The constant multiplier gives the original \(2N+M\).
Taking \(R=X_{g/2}\) uses the identity \(x U_{k-1}((x^2-2)/2)=U_{2k-1}(x/2)\).
The resulting two even frequencies again have nested Laurent support, so their norm equals \(2(2N-M)\) and the smaller candidate is attained, including equality of the candidates.

As an independent exact arithmetic check I tested both signs for all \(1\le m<n\le25\) in \(\mathbb Z[x]\), generating \(U_{j-1}(x/2)\) and \(X_j\) by the second-order recurrences.
For all 600 cases I checked squarefreeness of \(C\), exact divisibility by the predicted torsion factor, irreducibility and leading coefficient of the quotient, and its endpoint value.
The optional factor occurred in 27 cases.
For all 47 negative-sign cases of opposite parity and even dilation I additionally checked the polynomial identity for \(R=X_{g/2}\), including four ties at reduced frequencies \((3,2)\).
These checks supplement the all-degree arguments.

Finally \(N<2N-M\le L\le2N+M<3N\), and the multiplier theorem uses \(c=2\), so \(L^2/2\) diverges exactly when \(N\to\infty\).
As \(\gcd(h+1,d)=\gcd(n,n-m)=g\), this is exactly denominator divergence for the reduced ratio \(d/(h+1)\).
At bounded denominator an unbounded \(q\) would have a subsequence tending to infinity and hence \(\theta-q\to0\) by the fixed-branch derivative, contradicting the multiplier theorem.
The same derivative bounds the excess below on bounded \(q\).
The argument treats the existence of successful Pisot inputs conditionally and leaves rational limits through increasing denominators possible.
