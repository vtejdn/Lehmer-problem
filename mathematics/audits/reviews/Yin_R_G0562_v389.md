## Both signs and every branch

At 2026-11-18T13:11 UTC an agent outside proof development independently reviewed the whole [fixed argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v164.md#all-towers) for every positive odd \(n\) and every nontorsion root of \(A_n\).
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its references without development dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or earlier findings on this argument.
The two nonsquare conclusions, the degree and measure identities, and the stated consequence for varying factors all hold.

The Bézout identity gives an integral inverse for \(b(\alpha^n)\) after substituting \(g(\alpha^n)=-\alpha b(\alpha^n)\).
The leading and constant coefficients of \(-A_n\) give the unit property of \(\alpha\).
For even \(P(1)\), divisibility at both endpoints gives \(|P(\pm1)|=2\), so each relevant principal ideal is a prime with residue field \(\mathbb F_2\).
If \(\epsilon\alpha=u^2\), the algebraic integer unit \(u\) reduces to \(1\) at the prime generated by \(1-\epsilon\alpha\), forcing this generator into the square of its own ideal.

For odd \(P(1)\), differentiating at a nonzero reduced root gives the repeated-root test, whose numerator reduces to \((t+1)^6\).
The only possible repeated root of \(\overline A_n\) is \(1\), which \(\overline P\) avoids.
The identity \(a=f(a^n)\) then distinguishes all \(d\) images \(a^n\).
The odd discriminant gives an odd order index and hence the stated presentation of the integer ring modulo four.
In this finite free \(\mathbb Z/4\)-algebra the derivative \(P'(x^2)\) is invertible modulo two at every component, so the first-order correction lifts \(x^2\) to a root of \(P\).
Squaring the congruence \(\sigma(u)\equiv u^2\bmod 2\) gives equality modulo four, and odd \(n\) preserves the sign on passing to \(\beta=\alpha^n\).
The image of the denominator under \(\sigma\) remains a unit.
I expanded both cleared numerators over the integers and checked their displayed factorizations.
Freeness justifies canceling two and passing to all \(d\) residues.

For the negative sign, reducing the two cyclotomic factors leaves orders three and fifteen.
I verified the \(t^9\) identity over the integers.
An order-fifteen \(t\) would be a power of the order-five \(a=t^9\), leaving at most two possible residues.
The strict real sign inequalities include \(x=\pm1\), and reciprocity extends them through \(0<|x|<1\).
This excludes degree one, and a quadratic unit with complex conjugate roots has both moduli one, hence is torsion.

For the positive sign I checked the quintic product and the two zero remainders for each quintic modulo two.
Both quintics are prime to \(t(t+1)\) and divide \(t^{32}-t\), so their roots have degree five and order \(31\).
It follows from \(a=t^{23}\) and \(a^n=t\) that \(n\equiv27\bmod31\), with \(d\le10\).
The Bézout bound on the unit disk, reversal, and the zero archimedean mean of \(\log|b(\beta)|\) give \(h(\beta)\le\log3/(3-1/n)\).
I checked the strict bound for \(d h(\alpha)\) and its \(d\ge2\) and nontorsion hypotheses against Voutier's Corollary 2 on page 4 of the retained author edition.
For \(n\ge3\) even the rounded constants give \(n<81.782\), so oddness leaves \(27\) alone in the permitted residue class.
At \(n=1\) the polynomial is \(-\Phi_8\).

For \(n=27\) I regenerated \(-A_{27}\) from its two cubic rows and used a separate coefficient-array computation over \(\mathbb F_3\).
I cubed residues by tripling monomial exponents and used polynomial long division for both reduction and Euclid's algorithm.
For \(j=6,7,8,9,10\) the degrees were respectively \(80,81,81,79,80\), with monic gcd \(1\) in every case.
Every degree from one through ten divides one of these \(j\), so no modular irreducible factor has such a degree and no monic integer factor can have one either.

For each choice of root in a tower the first quadratic is irreducible.
At every later step the norm down to \(K\) of a root of the current irreducible binomial is its constant coefficient \(-\alpha\), because its degree is even.
A square at that step would have square norm, so the next degree doubles on every branch.
Since \(K\subseteq\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), the resulting absolute degree equals the degree of the monic annihilator \(P(x^{2^k})\), including \(k=0\).
The power formula for height then gives the claimed equality of logarithmic measures.
The cited two-sided factor bound has constants uniform in the selected root and \(n\), so it yields precisely the equivalence with \(d/n\to0\) on the selected lifts.
This review uses the cited height results at their stated scope and leaves the existence questions in the final section open.
