## Gap bounds at a real exterior conjugate and the odd-prime obstruction

For integer exponent \(p\ge2\) and width \(b\ge0\), the two lists of exponents occupy the classes \(0,1\bmod p\).
Each pair therefore has a unique upper member, and folding preserves all support positions.
Conjugacy and the reversal of \(F\) give double zeros at the four distinct positive numbers \(a^{\pm1},a^{\pm2}\).
The multiplicity form of Rolle's theorem gives \(2|J|-1\ge8\), hence \(|J|=5\).

In the alternant recursion, each new column contributes \((z_i-t)\sum_{m=l_j}^{l_{j+1}-1}z_i^m t^{l_{j+1}-1-m}\) in row \(i\).
Expansion along the last row cancels the signs from these factors in the Vandermonde quotient, and the exponent ranges are strictly ordered.
Selecting their left endpoints gives the stated monomial, which survives coalescence by nonnegativity.
Confluence cancels one vanishing Vandermonde factor for each ordinary derivative row.
There are at most four cross pairs, and \(\prod_i z_i^{i-1}\le a^{12}\), including for every row prefix.
In the Cramer numerator the smaller replacement exponent \(s_i\) occupies the first position in the maximal sorted product.
The node there is \(a\), so division by the lower minor bound contributes the factor \(a^{s_i-s_{i+1}}\).
The \(r!\) summands, coefficient norm at most \(2H\) on the right, and at most \(r\le4\) factors \(N+1\) give the claimed bound for each successive gap because the recovered coefficient is a nonzero integer.

After shifting by the lowest exponent, the upper block starts at \(T=s_1+s_5-N>0\) and both blocks have width \(\Delta\).
A zero of \(U\) at either \(a\) or \(a^2\) implies \(d\le2\Delta\) by the square-field degree bound.
With both denominators nonzero the two contacts imply a double zero at \(a\) of the cleared squaring identity.
For a nonzero rational function commuting with squaring the leading Laurent coefficient is one.
If the first nonconstant term after division by the leading monomial has coefficient \(c\), squaring gives coefficient \(2c\) at its index and doubling the variable gives zero.
In characteristic zero the resulting monomial must have exponent \(T\) by its value at \(a>1\), contradicting the disjoint block supports.
The nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(4\Delta\) therefore contains the square of the separable degree-\(d\) minimum.

At an odd prime, the unit minimum has no zero residue, and its divisibility into \(F(X),F'(X),F(X^2),F'(X^2)\) passes to reduction.
At each residue \(\xi\) the derivative singles out the reversed row, and the value equation and Frobenius then give \(\bar A(\xi)=\bar A(\xi^2)=0\).
Content normalization leaves at least one group nonzero.
A single surviving group bounds the number of residues by \(l\).
For two surviving groups one may substitute the two row equations directly into the cleared defect, including at common zeros of the groups.
More than \(4l\) distinct roots force the defect to vanish identically.
The Laurent coefficient comparison applies in every odd characteristic, so the reductions are opposite monomial translates and have equal support sizes.
If all five coefficients survived, the sum of those sizes would be odd.

For a Salem number \(h=\log a=\log M(a)>2/\log^3(3d)\) by Voutier's Corollary 2.

The two norm tests bound each row separately, since the other row in the product test has norm at least one.
For arbitrary odd primes, division of the gap bound by \(ph\) and \(a-1\ge h\) give \(\Delta/p=O_K(1+\log^4(3d))\) uniformly.
At every squarefree prime \(s_p=d\) eventually exceeds \(4l\).
