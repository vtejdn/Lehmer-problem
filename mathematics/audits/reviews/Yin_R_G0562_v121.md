## Integral Chebyshev moments, bounded pole count and isolation at zero

The reviewer received the fixed version without development dialogue or a prior verdict and took no part in its development.

For a real inverse trace outside \([-2,2]\) the exterior member of its inverse pair costs \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|x|/2)\) in the full logarithmic measure.
Thus all inverse traces lie in \([-2\cosh L,2\cosh L]\), and each pole is between two successive roots of any real fiber.
For \(t>2\cosh L\), the branch ordering puts every source trace strictly between \(g(-t)\) and \(g(t)\).
The width is \(2t/k-\sum_i2tw_i/(t^2-b_i^2)\).
C345v120 forces \(k=1\) and total residue one at sufficiently small positive measure for nonlinear maps.
Its span lemma applies since the Salem trace is a totally real algebraic integer of degree at least two, so its span \(D\) exceeds two.
At \(L=0\), letting \(t\) decrease to two in the width bound gives \(D\le4/k\), excluding \(k\ge2\).
With \(k=1\) the negative residue sum is the integral Laurent coefficient at \(X^{-1}\).
At a nonlinear map this makes \(W\) a positive integer, and \(D\le4-W\) excludes \(W\ge2\) also at zero.
The affine case already has pole count zero.

Under \(k=W=1\), the positive pole measure has mass one and integral moments of every order by Laurent division of the monic polynomials.
The squared monic integer polynomial \(V_j^2\) has an integral average.
If \(j<p\), the polynomial \(V_j\) cannot vanish at all \(p\) distinct support points, hence this average is a positive integer.
This uses positivity of the actual residues and remains valid at poles outside \([-2,2]\).

The weights \(a_j=(2/N)(1-j/(N+1))\) for \(1\le j\le N\) sum to one.
Fejér positivity gives \(\sum a_j\cos(2j\theta)\ge-1/N\), so the weighted sine-square sum is at most \((N+1)/(2N)\).
For each pole \(X\), taking \(\cos\theta=X/t\) is legitimate since \(|X|<t\).
The Chebyshev identity therefore gives the stated upper bound at \(2X/t\).
The recurrence bounds the coefficient norm \(\sum_l|v_{il}|3^l\) by \(4^i\), since \(3\cdot4^i+4^{i-1}\le4^{i+1}\) and the two initial bounds hold.
Both arguments and the segment between them lie in \([-3,3]\) when \(t\le3\).
The derivative bound for \(V_i^2\) is at most \(2i16^i\) there, and \(|X-2X/t|\le t-2\).
The uniform replacement error is thus at most \(2N16^N(t-2)\) for \(i<N\), including poles outside \([-2,2]\).
When \(p\ge N\), the weighted integral at the original arguments is at least one by the positive integer bound at all degrees \(0,\ldots,N-1\).
The remaining integral in the upper bound is exactly \(t/2\) times \(\sum_i2tw_i/(t^2-b_i^2)\).
The endpoint width bounds this by \((t/2)(2t-D)\), proving (3).

The right side of (3) at \(t=2\) is \((N+1)(4-D)/(2N)\).
It is strictly less than one precisely when \(N>(4-D)/(D-2)=2/(D-2)-1\).
The given maximum of one and the integer part of \(2/(D-2)\) satisfies the strict inequality, including when the quotient is an integer or \(D\ge4\).
With \(N\) now fixed at this value, continuity supplies an interval immediately above two where the upper bound remains below one.
For all sufficiently small \(L\ge0\) one can choose \(t\) in this interval with \(2\cosh L<t\le3\), contradicting \(p\ge N\).
Consequently \(p<N_D\).

C345v117 gives a finite list of Salem sources at the fixed full-measure bound one, uniformly over real poles and all nonidentity maps in the specified class.
Taking the minimum of the finitely many source thresholds, together with the monicity threshold, yields one neighborhood of zero on which \(s\) and \(m\) are bounded.
The monic integer full inverse then has bounded degree \(2sm\) and bounded measure, so its coefficients range over a finite set by elementary symmetric function bounds.
The finite set of measures in this neighborhood gives the absolute gap, also when it contains no positive value.
For the presentations, the monic \(B\) has bounded degree and bounded real roots by interlacing, including the constant denominator in the affine case.
For each source fix one real root \(y\).
The monic \(A-yB\) has bounded degree and roots in the same bounded trace interval, and \(yB\) has bounded coefficients as well.
Their sum is the given integer numerator, hence there are only finitely many triples, in particular at zero.
The reviewer finds the uniform positive gap for full inverse polynomials, the fixed-source pole bound, and finiteness of zero-measure presentations proved.
The isolation concerns the full measure of the inverse polynomial with all its cofactors.
