## A dual bound uniform over all conductors and the rounding at fixed degree

At 2026-11-17T03:23 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the complete C345v140 submission fixed at 2026-11-17T03:21 UTC.
The reviewer had no role in developing this version and reconstructed the divisor bound, its two conductor ranges and its use at the integer cutoff.

For a nonzero weight attached to \(l\), the anchor multiple lies in \(J\) and \(l\ge(k-H)/R>H\) for all large \(k\), so each \(l\) has at most one anchor.
At a fixed conductor \(n\le kR\), each active divisor \(l\) represents \(n/j\) by a fraction with denominator \(j/l\le R\).
Two distinct such numbers differ by at least \(R^{-2}\).
Their total spread is at most \(kRH/(k-H)^2=O(k^{-1/10})=o(R^{-2})\), so a single \(j\) serves all contributing divisors of \(n\).
With that \(j\), summing over all divisors of \((n,j)\) preserves the sum because \(f\) vanishes for \(j/l\ge R\).
The function \(f\) is \(1/\log R\)-Lipschitz in \(\log s\), including after capping at zero and one.
Thus \(f(j/l)\) can be replaced by \(f(j/n)\) with additive error at most \(n(\log R)^{-1}\sum_{v\mid n}|\mu(v)|\log(v)/v\).
The residual conductor sum is \(f(j/n)c_n(j)\) by including/excluding divisors in the primitive residue exponential sum.
It has modulus at most \(\varphi(n)\), also when \(n=1\); conductors without an active divisor cost zero.

The relative error is uniform in this entire range.
Expanding the squarefree divisor sum and selecting each contributing prime once yields the product \(\prod_{p\mid n}(1+1/p)\) times \(\sum_{p\mid n}\log p/(p+1)\).
For \(p\le x\) the power \(p^{-(s-1)}\) in the submission is at least \(1/2\), whence \((1-p^{-s})^{-1}\ge1+1/(2p)\ge(1+1/p)^{1/2}\).
The Euler product and the integral bound for \(\zeta(s)\) thus bound the small-prime product by \(O((1+\log x)^2)\).
For the remaining prime divisors, \(\sum\log p\le\log n\) controls both the large-prime reciprocal sum and its logarithmically weighted counterpart with \(x\ge(\log(3n))^2\).
The inequality \(n/\varphi(n)\le\zeta(2)\prod_{p\mid n}(1+1/p)\) follows by extracting the factors \((1-p^{-2})^{-1}\).
The stated sixth logarithmic power therefore bounds the relative error, which is \(o(1)\) uniformly for \(n\le kR\).
For \(n>kR\) the trivial \(k\tau(n)\) bound divided by \(\varphi(n)\) is at most \(k\tau(n)^2/n\).
The prime-power proof of \(\tau(n)^2=O_\epsilon(n^\epsilon)\) is uniform across all such \(n\), so \(k(k^{41/40})^{-1+\epsilon}=o(1)\) on choosing \(0<\epsilon<1/41\).
This last step permits arbitrary conductors and positive multiplicities in the eventual minimization.

For a reciprocal-normalized factor of order \(n\), the factorization into \(1-w^l\) for \(l\mid n\) has exponent \(\mu(n/l)\), including exponent \(+1\) at order one.
Multiplication with nonnegative multiplicities and the uniform conductor bound charge at most \((1+o(1))\sum_n b_n\varphi(n)\), proving the lower dual bound independently of the candidate completion's degree.
The matched prefix uniquely determines its formal exponents by integral coefficient recursion.
On the source, \(p_j-e^{aj}\) is the reflected power sum minus \(e^{-aj}\) and has magnitude \(O_A(1+\sqrt{jd\log(2d)})\).
All proper divisor terms in the Möbius formula for \(lc_l\) involve \(e\le l/2\).
Dobrowolski, including the bounded-degree case, gives \(k=O_{A,B}((\log(2d))^4)\).
The aggregate error from replacing \(lc_l\) by \(e^{al}\), for the \(k\) weighted terms, is therefore \(o(e^{ak})\) uniformly since \(e^{ak}\ge d^{1/2+\delta}\).
The weights are nonnegative everywhere and equal \(j\) on \(J\).
Also \(aH\gg\log d/k^{1/8}\gg\sqrt{\log d}\) in the full window after absorbing the floor.
Consequently the retained terminal geometric sum is \((1-o(1))e^{ak}/(1-e^{-a})\).
Together with the dual bound this gives the required lower bound on every cyclotomic comparator of any degree.

For the upper bound the polynomial construction of C345v138 permits every signed integral formal exponent and supplies the degree inequality with proper-divisor contributions.
In its top sum the reflected moment error is \(O_A(k(1+\sqrt{kd\log(2d)}))\), and all remaining terms involve exponential size at most \(e^{ak/2}\) times a polylogarithmic factor, in addition to the reflected error.
These are \(o(e^{ak})\) throughout the same window.
If its polynomial happens to have degree below \(k\), multiplication by \(z^{k+1}-1\) leaves its reversal unchanged through degree \(k\) and adds negligible degree.
This proves the asymptotic for the actual minimum.

At the original degree C345v138 already yields \(aK(P)/\log d\to1\) uniformly for bounded \(a\).
The logarithms of \(1-e^{-a}\) and \(1-e^{-2a}\) have size at most \(O_A(1+\log\log(2d))\), and a floor costs at most \(A\) after multiplication by \(a\).
One may therefore substitute \(k=K(P)\) in the newly proved window with \(\delta=1/4,\ B=2\).
There is a degree-\(d\) completion by definition, so \(D(P,K(P))\le d\); the uniform relative error becomes the asserted additive \(o_A(1)\) after taking the logarithm.
The opposite floor comes from the previously constructed completion of exactly degree \(d\), without assuming the minimum-degree optimizer can be padded at negligible *integer* excess.
For shrinking \(a\), \(\log((1-e^{-a})/a)=o(1)\) and either floor costs at most \(a=o(1)\), giving the limit zero.
At \(a\) bounded away from zero the two floors instead agree under the stated strict avoidance of integers.
