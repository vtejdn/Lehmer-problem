The inverse pairing identifies exactly \(D\) distinct trace coordinates, since a given sum \(z+z^{-1}\) determines the unordered pair of roots of the corresponding quadratic.
Thus \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q(t)\) is quadratic, with involution taking \(\alpha\) to its inverse.
Integrality of \(\alpha^{-1}\) in the monogenic order and reduction by \(\alpha^2=t\alpha-1\) give \(R=\mathbb Z[t]+\alpha\mathbb Z[t]\).
The full scalar congruence is exactly what places \(\eta=G(\alpha)/q\) in that order, regardless of primality or multiplicative order.
Its relative norm \(y\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z[t]\) and all \(n\) nondistinguished real evaluations are \(|G(z)/q|^2\le A\).
If the distinguished value lies outside \([0,A]\), it has multiplicity one among the \(D\) embeddings.
Separability then implies \(\mathbb Q(y)=\mathbb Q(t)\).
The inclusion \(\mathbb Z[y]\subseteq\mathbb Z[t]\) is now of finite index, so the absolute Vandermonde determinant of all the values of \(y\) is \([\mathbb Z[t]:\mathbb Z[y]]\sqrt{\Delta}\).

The nonzero input polynomial has \(H>0\), so the interval Chebyshev normalization is valid.
For each \(j\ge1\), \(2(A/4)^jT_j(2x/A-1)\) has leading coefficient one and norm at most \(2(A/4)^j\) on \([0,A]\).
Changing to this monic basis preserves the determinant, and bounding each of the \(n\) normalized column norms by \(\sqrt n\) yields \(n^{n/2}2^{n-1}(A/4)^{n(n-1)/2}\) for the part among the neutral values.
The other \(n\) factors are bounded by \(|y|+A\).
Taking the \(n\)-th root yields exactly (4), since \((4/A)^{(n-1)/2}=(2q/H)^{n-1}\).
At \(n=1\) the neutral determinant and the exponent-dependent factor are both one, and the same argument gives \(|y|+A\ge\sqrt\Delta\).

If the distinguished value is in the interval and \(A<4\), every conjugate of \(y-2\) is in \([-2,2]\).
The roots of its quadratic lift are nonzero algebraic integers of house one, so Kronecker's theorem gives the asserted torsion trace.
For order \(k\ge2\), a conjugate corresponding to the argument \(2\pi/k\) gives \(4\cos^2(\pi/k)\le A\).
This proves the direction of the upper bound on \(k\), excluding \(k=1\) by \(A<4\).
Under \(q>H\) the strict inequality \(\arccos(H/(2q))>\pi/3\) leaves only \(k=2\), hence \(y=0\).
The feature that a field norm vanishes only at zero then gives \(G(\alpha)=0\).
On the other branch, the product of the absolute values at the two real places is at most \(S^2 e^{Wm}\), so the strict numerical test (6) contradicts (4).
Exact vanishing always implies the scalar congruence.
Increasing the supplied circle bound preserves the argument with its enlarged interval and its corresponding strict thresholds.

For the specified even alternating words the two real evaluations have opposite strict signs.
Summing disjoint intervals of positive endpoint powers gives the two factors \(\alpha^W-1\) and \(1-\alpha^{-W}\) as bounds.
Their product is \(4\sinh^2(Wm/2)\), and \(H\le r\) gives (1).
The lower bound \((2q/r)^{n-1}/(2\sqrt n)\) exceeds \(2(r/q)^2\) uniformly eventually at any fixed \(q/r\ge c>1/2\).
Subtracting the circle term and paying this factor of two gives the stated constant \(-\log4\) in (2).
The ordinary full norm instead gives \(q^d\le e^{Wm}r^{d-2}\), exactly (3).
For unrestricted patterns under \(q/S\to c\in(1,2)\), the torsion case forces zero eventually, and a nonzero value must obey the separated-embedding bound.
Here \(n-1=d/2-2\), so the degree-normalized limit is at least \(\tfrac12\log(2c)\), strictly above \(\log c\) on the claimed open interval.
These are necessity and detection results for words in the full scalar quotient.
They imply neither a count of surviving supports nor a Lehmer gap.
