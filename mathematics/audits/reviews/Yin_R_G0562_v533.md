## Outcome of the full review

C416's statements hold for either one prescribed sign at every odd prime.
Every divergent subexponential width gives the stated interval between half the Lehmer infimum and the full infimum for the lower and upper normalized limits.
The computable width selected using contraction divisors gives the exact limit, with no uniform fixed-seed convergence assumption.

**Derivative and finite test.**
For \(U=A,\ V=\epsilon A_b^*\), a passing value \(z=\alpha^p\) has \(U(z)V(z)\ne0\) and \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\).
Thus the two number fields agree, so \(m(\min z)=p\,m(\min\alpha)\) holds also with nonmonic primitive minima, by the finite-place formula for absolute height.
Multiplying the Euler derivative by \(V(z)\) and substituting the graph value gives \(p z(U'V-UV')(z)-U(z)V(z)\).
Its defining polynomial \(W\) is nonzero because \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0 U-\operatorname{ord}_0 V)-1\ne0\) at the lowest product monomial.
It has degree at most \(2b\), and its one-norm is at most \((2pb+1)H^2\).
Primitive divisibility of \(W\) and nonnegativity of integral cofactor measure give the claimed individual inequality.
Roots at zero or of finite multiplicative order can be discarded on the powered side exactly as on the original side.
For each remaining irreducible factor of \(W\), nonvanishing of \(V(z)\) and the power equation give nonzero \(U(z)\), the desired \(\alpha\), vanishing of the graph and vanishing of its derivative.
In particular a repeated root caused entirely by a factor shared by the rows never passes through a zero denominator in this test.

As a check on nonintegral roots, for \(p=3,b=2\) the plus polynomial \(A=-64-173Y+188Y^2\) and the minus polynomial \(A=32+391Y+116Y^2\) each give double graph roots at \(2\) and \(1/2\).
For the displayed order of the signs, \(A(1/8)\) is \(-1323/16\) and \(1323/16\).
Direct expansion puts both \(Y-8\) and \(8Y-1\) in the corresponding \(W\)'s.
These examples test the predicate before imposing the coefficient cutoff.

**Upper bound and gap test.**
With \(t=b(p)-1\), C408 applies since \(t\) eventually grows through positive integers, without any monotonicity requirement.
The degree-ten benchmark puts its limit strictly below the normalized fallback, so an actual minimizing pair with a repeated reciprocal unit minimum exists at each sufficiently large prime.
Reversal at formal graph degree \(pt+1\) preserves that repeated factor, including when the displayed coefficient width exceeds the actual degrees.
At its powered root \(z\), at least one of \(U_0\pm V_{0,t}^*\) is nonzero since \(U_0(z)\ne0\).
Choosing \(Y+1\) or \(Y-1\) for its reversal sign makes the output sign exactly the originally prescribed \(\epsilon\) at width \(b\).
Neither multiplier vanishes at \(z\) and the norm increases by at most a factor four over the two-row cost.
The normalized loss vanishes.
For the lower bound, the logarithmic width assumption makes \(\log(2pb+1)/p\) tend to zero, and the fallback also satisfies the bound.
When \(\lambda>0\) an integer \(k\) with \((\log2)/k<\lambda/2\) leaves a strict margin, and when \(\lambda=0\) the upper bound leaves a strict margin below each fixed \((\log2)/k\).
The floors and the weaker infinitely-many-primes requirement in the equivalence follow.

**Calibrated width.**
The lists at fixed \(j\) are finite, effectively enumerable and exclude every cyclotomic polynomial.
Their cyclotomic evaluations are therefore nonzero, so the inverse denominator, resultant and removal of powers of \(p\) define \(D_+,D_-\) at every tested odd prime.
The algebraic comparisons are decidable, including equality.
For a fixed \(j\) the logarithm of (3), divided by \(jp^2\), asks for a lower approximation to \(m(P)\) within \((\log2)/j\).
The fixed-input limits with denominator \((p-1)^2\) also hold with \(p^2\), and the list is finite.
Thus each fixed \(j\) eventually passes at every prime large enough that \(j\le\sqrt p\).
The largest passing \(j\) diverges regardless of whether the tests are nested, and the initial default cannot affect the limit.

For any passing row at the selected width, the degree bound on its powered minimum is \(2b\).
The derivative estimate gives \(m(P)\le2\log2+o(1)<\log8\) uniformly.
A leading or constant coefficient of absolute value at least two gives \(m(P)\ge\log2\) by the two root products and therefore already gives a normalized cost above \(\lambda\).
For a nonreciprocal monic unit \(P\), reciprocity of \(Q\) would induce an automorphism \(z\mapsto z^{-1}\) of \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Signed reversal sends the recovered rational expression \(-U(z)/V(z)\) to its inverse under this automorphism, contradicting nonreciprocity of \(P\).
Reversal of the Euler commutator in \(W\) and exchange of the signed rows leave it reciprocal at formal degree \(2b\), so both relatively prime powered factors \(Q,Q^*\) divide it, even if its true degree is smaller.
Their equal measures supply the doubled bound.

In the remaining monic reciprocal unit case the coefficient bound \(|P_i|\le\binom{\deg P}{i}\exp m(P)\) puts \(P\) in the list actually tested at this prime.
The graph is nonzero, has at most two residue classes and contains \(P^2\), even if it has other common, cyclotomic or repeated factors.
The finite contraction inequalities for \(P^2\) involve \(D_+^2,D_-^2\), since all non-\(p\) valuations and their maxima double.
The two resultant factors multiply at \(X^{2p}-1\).
Deleting the specified copies, the cyclotomic factors, a possible monomial and scalar content to form the cofactor can only reduce degree and measure.
Combining the finite inequality with \(b\)'s defining comparison gives the three explicit losses \((\log2)/b,\ (pb+1)\log2/(2p^2),\ (\log2)/p\).
All vanish uniformly by \(b\to\infty,\ b\le\sqrt p\).
This last step uses no fixed-input asymptotic with a varying polynomial.
