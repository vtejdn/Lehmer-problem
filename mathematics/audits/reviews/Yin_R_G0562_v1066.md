## The rank-two area bound and the limit on division of \(T\)

At 2026-11-24T19:35 an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin all four sections of the [directional estimates](../../claims/Yin_C_G0902_v4.md#statement).
The reviewer received the fixed claim and scope without development dialogue or prior findings on this version.
The earlier independent reviews of the [generators](Yin_R_G0562_v1040.md#finding) and the [radical minimum](Yin_R_G0562_v1062.md#finding) apply at their indicated inputs.
The proposed inequalities, uniformity, division index at most \(109\), and necessary growth in the remaining direction all follow.

The height-product theorem of Amoroso and David uses the product of absolute logarithmic heights, the joint field degree, and exponent \(r((r+1)((r+1)!)^r-r)\) at rank \(r\).
For two independent units this is \(212\), and \(c(2)\) is an absolute positive constant of unspecified numerical value.
I checked this specialization against the registered published statement and the authors' statement of Theorem 1.6, without auditing its proof. [Published edition](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/1944852) · [Authors' text, Theorems 1.5–1.6](https://www.academia.edu/116312250/Le_probl%C3%A8me_de_Lehmer_en_dimension_sup%C3%A9rieure)
The lifts of two independent vectors attaining the successive minima are multiplicatively independent.
Their joint degree is at most \(2n\), and for every lift \(u\), summing the logarithms over the embeddings of \(K\) gives \(2nh(u)=\ell(\lambda(u))\), including repetitions over a proper subfield.
Consequently \(l_1l_2\ge2nc(2)/(\log(6n))^{212}\).
The unit diamond has diagonals two and one, so Minkowski's second theorem gives \(2D_U\le l_1l_2\le4D_U\).
Dividing the uniform asymptotic for \(D_H\) by the resulting lower bound on \(D_U\) gives (1).
The quotient \((\log(6n))^{212}/n\) is bounded for \(n\ge4\) and tends to zero with \(n\).
The degree index in \(\ell(\lambda(u))=[K:\mathbb Q(u)]m(u)\) is one or three, so \(\mu\le l_1\le3\mu\), giving the stated lower bound on \(l_2\).

For \(u^r=\pm T^a\), absolute logarithms scale by \(a/r\).
The index bound three and \(m(T)\sim\log M\) make \(|a/r|<1\) uniformly under the proposed small-measure assumption.
The interior member \(\rho\) of \(u,u^{-1}\) is real and has distance at most \(|a/r|\log T=O(M^{-1/2})\) from its sign.
The real units \(u,u^{-1}\) are conjugate, and the nonzero integer \(P(\operatorname{sgn}\rho)\) has modulus at least one.
Each coefficient of \(P\) is bounded by its circle supremum, giving \(\sup_{|z|\le1}|P'(z)|\le D(D+1)\|P\|_{\mathbb T}/2\).
Integration along the real segment gives the required lower limit \(1/2\), since \(D\le2n\le(\log M)/\log2+3\).

The finite circle inequality applies to the monic separable minimum \(P\) with \(1\le j=\lceil D/24\rceil\le D\).
With \(N=D+j\), one has \(N/j\le25\), and monotonicity of \(s\log(\mathrm e N/s)\) on \([1,N]\) bounds the averaged binomial sum by \(j\log(25\mathrm e)\).
The resulting upper limit is at most \(25/110+\log(25\mathrm e)/(24\log2)=0.480879\ldots<0.49\), strictly below \(1/2\).
The contradiction is uniform even if the degree stays bounded.
For an external radical, killing its torsion factor gives \(\gamma^r=T^a\) with \(a\ne0\).
Putting \(F=\mathbb Q(T^a)\) and \(s=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):F]\), the norm \(v\) to \(F\) satisfies \(v^r=T^{as}\), lies in \(U\) by positivity at a neutral place, and has \(m(\gamma)=[F:\mathbb Q(v)]m(v)\).
Thus the same lower bound holds outside \(K\) with all proper-subfield factors accounted for.
For a generator of the lattice on the \(T\) line, \(T=\pm u^{j_T}\) implies \(\mathbb Q(u)=K\) and \(m(T)=j_Tm(u)\).
The bound \(j_T<111\) eventually and the nonsquare class of \(T\) make \(j_T\le109\).

For independent \(T,u\), their joint field is \(K\), and multiplying the height-product inequality by the two individual degrees \(2n\) and at least \(2n/3\) gives the first inequality in (3).
In \(u^I=\pm T^aY^b\) the exponent \(b\) is nonzero, so the absolute determinant of \(\lambda(T)\) and \(I\lambda(u)\) is at least \(D_H\).
Expanding the two coordinate products shows \(2|\det(v,w)|\le\ell(v)\ell(w)\), giving \(I\,m(u)\ge2D_H/(3m(T))\).
The right side is asymptotic to \((n-1)\log M/3\).
Every sequence of positive measures shrinking in the radical closures descends to such a sequence of relative units, since the \(T\) direction is excluded and bounded \(q\) allows only finitely many fields with a positive minimum.
It follows that \(I/(n\log M)\) must diverge.
The two-direction product estimate still permits the length of one independent direction to shrink.
