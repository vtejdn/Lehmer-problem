# Review of the first denominator index for six-term lifts with independent frequencies

## The limiting measure and the obstruction on the two coordinate circles

At 2026-11-16T16:00 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin inspected the entire [first-index claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v16.md#statement), all three argument sections and their dependency interfaces.
The reviewer worked separately on six-term lifts whose two offsets have fixed reduced ratio \(1:2\), took no part in the argument for diverging reduced frequencies and received this version without development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict on it.
The claimed bound passes for branch two as stated, with absolute \(C,Q,R\).

Failure for every triple of constants supplies a sequence with all three quantities \(q,\ e/\gcd(d,e)\) and \(t/e\) tending to infinity, where \(t\) denotes the first positive index with \(b_t\ne0\).
The [six-term restrictions](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) apply since \(q\to\infty\) on this fixed branch.
They give \(e=o(N)\) and unit interior coefficients after extraction, without a separation assumption on \(d/e\).
The [actual denominator](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) is monic and reciprocal of degree \(N\) in the lifted variable.
Its measure is strictly above one and tends to one by the [delay-one estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return), so it cannot equal \(1+z^N\).
Thus some nonendpoint coefficient is nonzero, and \(t\le N/2\).
For \(e<n<N-e\), each of \(n,n-d,n-e\) is positive, so all three Taylor coefficients of \(J\) used at \(b_n\) are doubled moments of the [signed measure](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure).
On a subsequence with \(t\ge\eta N\), the cutoff with \(s=\lfloor\eta N/8\rfloor\) has transition regions wholly within the initial and reflected zero intervals.
Its support stays inside the moment window, and it is one at all nonendpoint nonzeros.
The product of the two interval sums has normalized \(L^1\) norm bounded by the square root of the ratio of their lengths after the indicated normalization.
Convolution therefore bounds \(\|B^\sharp\|_1\), contradicting the [integer support estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) and the fixed-support gap.
This establishes the two strict scale separations \(e=o(t)\), \(t=o(N)\).

Through \(t-1\) the identity \(B^\sharp=F_iJ_i\) gives exact Taylor agreement with \(1/F_i\).
For a fixed nonzero \((l,r)\), the relation \(ld+re=0\) is impossible eventually because a nonzero integer relation has sup norm at least \(e/\gcd(d,e)\).
When \(m=ld+re>0\), each contributing \(p,s\ge0\) with \(pd+se=m\) has \(s\le|l|+|r|\).
Thus only boundedly many pairs can contribute, and for each bounded \(s\) the weight \(\binom{p+s}{s}2^{-p-s}\) tends to zero as \(p\to\infty\).
Any bounded pair with repeated contribution must coincide with \((l,r)\) by the same relation bound.
Conjugation covers \(m<0\), including subsequences on which the sign changes for a mixed exponent pair.
The signs from \(x=-av^d,\ y=-bv^e\) cancel those in the coefficients of \(1/F_i\).
The contributing limit is half the binomial weight for each nonzero pair in the same closed quadrant, and is zero for strict mixed signs.
The moment at the origin is one.

For \(F=1-(x+y)/2\), the damped real parts in the proof are nonnegative since their analytic denominators have positive real part.
For fixed \(x\ne1\), \(|2-x|>1\), so integration in \(y\) of the undamped reciprocal gives \((1-x/2)^{-1}\).
Its real part has integral one in \(x\).
Tonelli's theorem therefore gives integral one for the undamped nonnegative density \(\Re(1/F)\) on the two-torus.
Equal masses and almost-everywhere convergence of the damped densities give \(L^1\) convergence, identifying all limiting moments and ruling out a singular component even at \((1,1)\).

After reflection in the first two coordinates the character at \((l,r)\) in \(F\sigma c_k\) tests the three original indices \(kt+ld+re\) and its shifts by \(-d,-e\).
All are positive and below \(N-e\) eventually for fixed \(k\ge1,l,r\), and the removed negative mass costs \(o(1)\) uniformly for each test.
The resulting coefficient is the limit of the signed integer \(b_{kt+ld+re}\) with the stated coordinate signs.
The constant coefficient of \(H_1\) is nonzero because \(b_t\) is an integer of modulus at least one and has a limit in this extraction.
The envelope \(D=2\Re F/|F|\) has squared mean strictly less than four and tends to zero along the diagonal approaching \((1,1)\).
Parseval leaves at most three nonzero unit coefficients per row, and vanishing at \((1,1)\) requires equal sign counts, leaving two opposite coefficients in each nonzero row.

For the resulting \(1-x^p y^s\) factor, the whole alignment level \(x^p y^s=-1\) avoids \((1,1)\) and forces \(D=2\), hence \(\Im(x+y)=0\).
The identity for the sum of two sines expresses this last locus as \(xy=1\) or \(x/y=-1\).
Components of the alignment level have the primitive direction of \((p,s)\), so each must coincide with one of those two circles by finiteness of intersections in distinct directions.
In direction \((1,1)\) its component constants never equal \(1\).
In direction \((1,-1)\) the equation \((x/y)^p=-1\) has \(|p|\) distinct level constants, all required to equal \(-1\), leaving \(|p|=1\).
This proves the asserted signed-monomial form times \(x-y\).

The quotient \(H_k/(F\sigma)\) is continuous wherever \((x,y)\ne(1,1)\), since \(\Re F>0\) there.
The conditional Toeplitz matrices therefore remain positive semidefinite on the full punctured torus by continuity and positivity almost everywhere.
On either punctured coordinate axis \(\Re(1/F)=1\).
For a nonzero row its moment restricts to \(\epsilon y^B\) on \(x=1\) and \(-\epsilon x^A\) on \(y=1\), both of unit modulus.
Equality for a probability moment forces the doubled harmonic to be its square on each axis.
The required restrictions of that new row both have positive sign, whereas every allowed nonzero row has opposite signs on the two axes.
Monomial exponents cannot alter that discrepancy on a full punctured circle.
This contradicts the nonzero first row and proves the first-index bound.
The conclusion is qualitative and leaves existence of six-term inputs with an earlier nonzero coefficient open.
