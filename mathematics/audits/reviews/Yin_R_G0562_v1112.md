The set of commutators of conjugates of complex conjugation is invariant under conjugation by \(G\).
Its generated subgroup \(D\) is normal and lies in \(C'\), where \(C\) is the normal closure of complex conjugation.
After quotienting by \(D\), the generators of \(C\) commute pairwise, giving \(C'=D\).
The trace action \(H\) is \(A_n\) or \(S_n\), and the nonreal trace pair makes \(\pi(C)\) nontrivial.
If this normal group had trivial intersection with \(A_n\), its commutator with \(A_n\) would vanish.
A permutation centralizing \(A_n\) preserves every three-element support, hence fixes each point by intersecting the supports that contain it.
Simplicity and perfection of \(A_n\) now give \(\pi(D)=A_n\).

For the sign kernel \(V\), every lift in \(D\) of a three-cycle acts on \(V\) by ordinary coordinate permutation, irrespective of the lift's own signs.
In the nonconstant case choose a triple on which \(v\in V\) is nonconstant.
Its difference from a cyclic shift has weight exactly two and belongs to \(V\cap D\) by the normality of both groups.
Conjugating supplies all weight-two vectors, since \(A_n\) is transitive on unordered pairs.
Their span is the even-sum subspace, so \([V:V\cap D]\le2\).
In the constant case this index bound follows from \(|V|\le2\) directly.
The kernel of \(G/D\to H/A_n\) is \(VD/D\), since \(\pi(D)=A_n\), so \(|G/D|\le4\).
All groups of these orders are abelian, proving \(G'=D\).
When \(H=S_n\), a transposition lift \(g=(u,s)\) has square \((u+su,1)\).
In the nonconstant case this is even and in \(D\), while in the constant case it must be zero as it vanishes at every fixed point of \(s\).
Together with \(VD/D\) the image of this lift generates the quotient, proving exponent two whenever the quotient is nontrivial.
This argument accommodates both possibilities for \(V\cap D\) within a constant sign kernel.

It follows that \(E\subset T(i)\), in particular \(L^D\subset T(i)\).
The compositum \(M\) is normal and restriction onto \(G\) is surjective.
Conjugating elements of \(G\) lift to \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)\), so all defining commutators for \(D\) lift to commutators fixing \(T(i)\).
Conversely an automorphism fixing \(T(i)\) restricts to \(D\), and restriction to \(L\) is injective on this subgroup because \(L\) and \(T(i)\) generate \(M\).
Thus the commutators generate all of \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/T(i))\), a finite group, and \(L\cap T(i)=L^D\).
The fixed field \(T(i)\) is consequently the largest Galois subfield of \(M\) with the stated commuting property, including among infinite Galois subfields.

For a Salem splitting field, complex conjugation fixes every trace and acts by an element of its elementary abelian normal sign kernel.

If a Salem number of degree at least four were in \(M\), its normal closure would therefore be in \(T(i)\).
Its real value would belong to \(T(i)\cap\mathbb R=T\), contradicting its nonreal conjugates.
