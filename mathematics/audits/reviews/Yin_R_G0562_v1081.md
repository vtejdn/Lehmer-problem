## Positive normalized multiplicity and divergent normalized degree

At 2026-11-24T20:40 an agent outside the development of this result independently checked the four fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without proof-development dialogue, author explanations or prior findings on this proof.

Let \(f=a^{-1}\log|P^a C|-m(P)\) with normalized Haar measure on the circle.
Since all cyclotomic factors have logarithmic measure zero, \(f\) has integral zero, positive part bounded by \(\Delta\), and \(L^1\)-norm at most \(2\Delta\).
Zeros on the circle cause only integrable logarithmic singularities.
For the positive moment convention \(\int z^j f(z)\,d\eta(z)\), one reflected root contributes \(-w^j/(2j)\), including boundary roots by the radial \(L^1\) limit and zero roots with contribution zero.
The exterior scalar and leading coefficient are constants and have zero nonconstant moments.
Consequently
\[
 \left|S_j+a^{-1}\sum_n c_n r_n(j)\right|\le4j\Delta ,
\]
where \(r_n(j)\) counts primitive roots with their \(j\)-th powers, without division by \(\varphi(n)\).
The Möbius divisor sum of \(r_n(d)\) at \(d\mid k\) retains just the term with divisor \(k\), since \(\sum_{h\mid d\mid k}\mu_{\rm ar}(k/d)\) is one for \(h=k\) and zero otherwise.
It thus gives \(k\mu_{\rm ar}(n/k)\) when \(k\mid n\) and zero otherwise.
Dividing the summed error by \(k\) gives \(4\Delta\sum_{d\mid k}|\mu_{\rm ar}(d)|/d=4\Delta\prod_{p\mid k}(1+1/p)\).
This proves (2) with the stated sign and normalization, also for \(k=1\).

The pushforward \(U_h\) takes a positive moment \(j\) to \(hj\).
If \(v_p(n)<e\), then \(U_{p^{e-1}}\sigma_n\) is a constant multiple of the counting measure on a primitive orbit of order coprime to \(p\), by the transitive action of the units and equal fibers under powering.
It is fixed by \(U_p\).
All the pushforwards commute, giving \(D_N\sigma_n=0\) off the multiples of \(N\).
For a fixed \(j,N\), each surviving signed summand has modulus at most \(\sum_{d\mid hj}d\) uniformly in \(n\), by the Ramanujan formula.
The empty product for \(N=1\) is the identity in both the selection and the bound.

The reflected nonzero off-circle root in the hypothesis gives at least one nonzero interior Poisson center.
Powering sends each interior Poisson probability to the probability at the powered center, since all positive moments agree, and sends each boundary atom to an atom.
The smallest index in the expansion of \(D_N\) is \(h_0=N/\operatorname{rad}(N)\), with coefficient \((-1)^{\omega(N)}\).
For \(N=1\) this index is one with positive sign.
If \(0<r<1\) is the maximal modulus among nonzero interior centers, every term with \(h>h_0\) has center of modulus strictly below \(r^{h_0}\).
At modulus \(r^{h_0}\) all terms have the same nonzero sign, even if distinct centers become equal after powering.
After combining coincident centers, the signed list of distinct nonzero centers therefore has a nonzero weight.
Its first \(q\) positive moments cannot all vanish for a list of length \(q\), because the matrix of powers \(1,\ldots,q\) has determinant a Vandermonde product times the product of the nonzero centers.
Any zero center contributes only Haar measure.
Thus the absolutely continuous part of \(D_N\nu_P\) is not a constant multiple of Haar.
Its remaining part is finite atomic, so uniqueness of Lebesgue decomposition precludes \(D_N\nu_P\) itself from being a multiple of Haar.
It is a real signed measure, hence it has some nonzero positive moment \(b_j\) by Fourier uniqueness.

For that \(j\), adding the finitely many moment inequalities at indices \(hj\) shows that the normalized sum over \(N\mid n\) tends to \(-b_j\) when \(\Delta\to0\).
Nonnegative \(c_{n,a}\) and the uniform bound \(B_{j,N}\) therefore give a liminf at least \(|b_j|/B_{j,N}>0\).
If all multiples are forbidden, writing \(D_N=\sum_h d_hU_h\) in the same inequalities gives
\[
                   |b_j|\le4j\Delta\sum_h |d_h|h .
\]
This verifies the strict input-dependent increase even for circle norm, and the coefficient one-norm dominates it.

For the degree conclusion a bounded subsequence of the normalized selected masses would give a finite positive weak limit on the compact circle.
The uniform probability on the primitive \(n\)-th roots tends to Haar as \(n\to\infty\), since every fixed nonzero moment is \(r_n(j)/\varphi(n)\to0\).
Taking a diagonal subsequence for the weights at each fixed orbit, uniform convergence against each fixed continuous test on the tail leaves only a nonnegative multiple of Haar in addition to those countably many atomic orbits.
Each \(U_h\) preserves Haar and takes an atomic finite measure to an atomic measure, so the absolutely continuous part after \(D_N\) is still a multiple of Haar.
Weak convergence commutes with these finitely many continuous pushforwards.
The moment estimates, now for every fixed \(j\ge1\), would make \(D_N(\nu+\nu_P)\) a multiple of Haar.
Its absolutely continuous part would contain the nonconstant contribution proved above, a contradiction.
Thus the selected masses \(\sum_{N\mid n}\varphi(n)c_{n,a}/a\) tend to infinity even if the unselected masses are unbounded from the outset.

If the normalized coefficient log norm tends to \(m(P)\), then the circle maximum has the same limit by the measure lower bound and the norm upper bound.
A fixed bound on the number of distinct prime divisors per order would rule out all multiples of a product of more distinct primes than that bound.
Kronecker's theorem supplies the required nonzero off-circle root for every nontorsion algebraic unit minimum.
The proof concerns all multiples of each fixed conductor in aggregate.
The positive multiplicity constant can depend on both the input and the conductor.
