## Sharp upper ratio and rational realization

The upper bound holds for every allowed real trace with the strict cut, including without an endpoint hypothesis.
The prefix identity applies through degree \(L-2\) on every odd grid.
At the odd \(D\) midpoint quadrature nodes, the cardinal polynomial \(R\) for the leftmost node \(v\) vanishes at the distinct node zero.
Thus \(U=R/t\) has degree \(D-2<n\), and \((t-a)U^2\) has degree \(2D-3\le L-2\).
Its discrete residue sum is strictly positive since \((b_j-a)\rho_j>0\) and \(U\) cannot vanish at all grid points.
The arcsine integral of \((t-a)R^2\) equals \((v-a)/D\) by midpoint exactness through degree \(2D-1\).
Letting \(a\) approach the upper endpoint of the separator interval gives \(b_{n-\kappa}\le v\).
Both angles are in \((0,\pi)\), so the cosine comparison gives \(2D(2\kappa+1)\le p\).
The choice of the largest odd \(D\) gives \(L-2\le2D\), hence the asserted upper limiting ratio for fixed \(\kappa\).

For the lower bound, I checked the periodic indicator identity on all \(dq\)-th roots of unity.
In the full-grid average the two equal half-grid contributions and the zero weight at \(j=0\) give precisely the prescribed affine moments.
The Fourier frequencies of \(\phi-1\) have absolute representatives between \(q-1\) and \(\kappa q+1<p/2\).
The extreme coefficients of \(t^2E_i(t)\) are one at both ends, and its degrees through \(i=q-3\) stay below \(p/2\).
Consequently the first change from the zero-profile coordinates occurs at \(i=q-3\), where the two coefficients \(-B/2\) change the positive even coordinate from \(4\) to \(4-B\).
The affine lift identity places its changed coefficient at index \(q-1\), leaving exact matching length \(q-2\).
The integer \(4(d-1)p^2\) clears all Laurent coefficients of \(\phi\), including the factors \(1/2\) from \(\cos\theta\).
A full-grid mean adds the coefficients at multiples of \(p\), so the same integer clears every coordinate, including those above the matching range.
The monic integral trace basis transfers this denominator bound to \(Q_*\) and its lift.

Writing \(w=p\tan((\pi-\theta)/2)\) gives the factor \(w^2-\tau^2\) for the nonmultiples of \(d\).
The last \(\kappa\) indices are all nonmultiples of \(d\).
For fixed \(d\), the largest \(w\) there is at most \(\tau-1/2\) eventually, while the preceding \(w\)'s are at least \(d\pi/2>\tau+1\).
I checked the uniform sign margin \(|b_j^2\phi_j|\ge1/(2p^2)\) on the central interval, at multiples of \(d\) near \(b=2\), and at the remaining nodes using \(w\)'s separation from \(\tau\).
Thus the absolute residue margin is at least \(1/p^3\), whereas a trace constant of magnitude less than \(1/(4p^2)\) changes each residue by less than \(1/p^3\).
At \(-2\) only the negative block can raise the ratio above its polynomial part \(-2\).
Its total contribution is at most \(4\kappa\tau^2/p\), so the endpoint ratio is below \(-1\) eventually and the prescribed trace constant preserves its strict sign.

The vanishing first moment and unit mass of \(\omega\) give \(Q_*=t^2 B_nD_p\) by polynomial division.
The full-grid inverse-trace recurrence gives \(F_1=1/2\) and \(F_v=F_0=(-1)^{(v-1)/2}/2\) for the relevant odd \(v>1\).
The indicator of the multiples of \(d\) reduces its average to the \(q\)-grid.
The remaining cosine contribution to \(-D_p(0)\) vanishes because \(\cos\theta/b=1/2\) and \(T\) has mean zero.
Both sign choices for \(F_0(p),F_0(q)\) therefore give \(|D_p(0)|\ge1/(4(d-1))\) eventually.

On the complex disk of radius \(R=1/(100d^2p)\), the node distance bound \(|t-b_j|\ge |b_j|/2\) holds throughout.
Summing \(|b_j|^{-2}\) by the distances of the integers \(j\) from \(p/4\) gives at most \(p^2\), hence \(|D_p(t)-D_p(0)|\le8dpR\).
Telescoping the Chebyshev transfer matrix powers in the complex operator norm gives \(|B_n(t)-B_n(0)|\le2pR\exp(pR)<1/10\).
With \(|B_n(0)|=1\), the two estimates ensure \(|S|=|B_nD_p|>1/(16d)\) on the entire disk.
The given \(\epsilon\) has the sign of \(S(0)\) and magnitude smaller than both \(1/(4p^2)\) and \(R^2/(16d)\).
It changes only the last affine coordinate, and its denominator \(10^6(d-1)d^5p^2\) also clears all coefficients of \(Q_*\).

The two comparison functions in Rouché's theorem are polynomials and the boundary inequality is strict.
The zero-free factor \(S\) leaves exactly two zeros of \(t^2S+\epsilon\) in the disk, counted with multiplicity.
On the real diameter \(S\) has constant sign and the choice of \(\epsilon\) excludes real zeros, so the two zeros form a nonreal conjugate pair.
The \(n-2\) same-sign pole gaps and the endpoint interval account for \(n-1\) distinct real roots inside \((-2,2)\), exhausting the remaining degree.
They lift to the unit circle, while each nonreal trace lifts to one exterior and one interior root.
The quadratic formula bounds each exterior modulus by \(\exp(\operatorname{arsinh}(R/2))\le e^{R/2}\).
The complete monic lift thus has \(1<M(P)\le e^R\), and the exact ratio \((q-2)/(dq)\) tends to \(1/d\).
The construction and sharpness apply to rational coefficients for every fixed \(\kappa\) and all sufficiently large odd \(q\).
