# Review of cyclotomic descent for radicals of primitive units

## Independent whole-proof finding

At 2026-11-18T03:13 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed v336 statement and proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v336.md#statement) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer had previously seen the fixed claim during unrelated work and received no development discussion, author explanation or previous finding on v336.
The reviewer reconstructed its argument and then compared the existing findings on the v61 and v78 inputs.
The field and degree identities, the uniform finite list of reduced equations under both stated bounds, the classification at equal measure and both quartic realizations hold under their specified hypotheses.

### Fields, norm and exact power substitution

If two embeddings give the same value on \(\alpha^v\) for a nonzero integer \(v\), their values on \(\alpha\) have torsion quotient.
The assumed exclusion gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^v)=K\), and the identity \(\beta^{bt}=\alpha^{at}\) for \(t=\operatorname{ord}\zeta\) puts \(K\) in \(H\).
Taking the norm to \(K\) and passing to the unit group modulo torsion gives \(b[N_{H/K}(\beta)]=a[H:K][\alpha]\).
Indeed \(\beta\) and its inverse are integral because powers of both are algebraic integers.
Applying an integral unit-lattice coordinate taking \([\alpha]\) to \(1\), and using \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), proves \(b\mid[H:K]\) also for negative \(a\).
The identity for heights then gives the exact ratio \(|a|j\).
When the twist lies in \(K\), the original binomial bounds this degree above by \(b\), so its norm lower bound is attained.
The \(t\)-th power of \(\zeta\alpha^a\) generates \(K\), which proves the stated absolute minimal polynomial by degree.

For a least-degree record near zero the integer endpoint bounds give unit roots and v78 supplies eventual reciprocity.
The product of the roots in any torsion-quotient class has logarithmic modulus equal to the class size times that of any member.
The class products form a single orbit of smaller degree at unchanged positive measure if a class contains two roots, as required by v61.
Divisibility of the seed's unit class by \(k>1\) would instead give a unit of degree at most \(d\) with total positive measure at most \(L/k\) in logarithmic units.
Both possibilities contradict the chosen record.

Over \(K_c\) the binomial splits after adjoining \(\beta\), and its automorphisms multiply \(\beta\) by elements of \(\mu_b\) fixed by the full relative group.
The resulting injection has image exactly \(\mu_s\) for the group order \(s\).
Thus \(\gamma=\beta^s\in H\cap K_c\), and \(\alpha^{at}=\gamma^{ht}\) gives \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\supset K\).
The degree of \(\beta\) over \(K_c\) is \(s\), whereas its degree over \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) is at most \(s\) by its binomial.
The intermediate base \(H\cap K_c\) forces equality throughout, including \(H\cap K_c=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
Consequently \([\mathbb Q(\gamma):K]=hj\), \(P_\gamma(X^s)\) is a monic annihilator of the full absolute degree and the height factor \(s\) cancels the degree loss.
The exponents and the degree of the smaller polynomial scale by the same integer \(s\).

### The denominator retained over the abelian field

Taking an \(h\)-th root \(\xi\) of \(\zeta\) in \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) gives \((\gamma/\xi)^h=\alpha^a\).
For integers \(u,v\) with \(au+hv=1\), the element \(w=(\gamma/\xi)^u\alpha^v\in K_c\) is a unit with \(w^h=\alpha\).
Write \(z_\sigma=\sigma(w)/w\in\mu_h\) and let \(c_\sigma\) be the cyclotomic exponent on \(\mu_h\).
The restriction of \(\operatorname{Gal}(K_c/K)\) to the abelian field is injective, so commutation gives \(z_\tau^{c_\sigma-1}=z_\sigma^{c_\tau-1}\).
It follows that \(\tau(w^{c_\sigma-1}/z_\sigma)=w^{c_\sigma-1}/z_\sigma\) for every \(\tau\).
This element is a \(K\)-unit with \(h\)-th power \(\alpha^{c_\sigma-1}\).
The same unit-lattice coordinate now forces \(h\mid c_\sigma-1\).
Thus the group fixes every element of \(\mu_h\), proving \(h\mid w_K\) even when the original degree \(b\) is arbitrarily large.

The field \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) contains \(K(\zeta)\), and restriction to the finite Galois cyclotomic field gives \(\varphi(\operatorname{ord}\zeta)\le hj[E:\mathbb Q]\).
Also \(\mathbb Q(\mu_h)\subset E\), so \(\varphi(h)\le[E:\mathbb Q]\).
Together with \(|a|j=m(\beta)/L\) and \(\varphi(v)^2\ge v/2\), these give the finite list of triples with the stated uniformity in the seed.
In the real cyclotomic example the trace \(x=\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}\) generates the maximal real subfield and is a unit by the prime cyclotomic norm at \(-1\).
Distinct real conjugates cannot be negatives since negating the two inverse roots corresponding to a trace changes their order from \(p\) to \(2p\).
The positive primitive representative on the ray satisfies \(x=\alpha^k\) and generates the same field.
It inherits the absence of torsion collisions because the power \(x\) still generates that field.
Taking \(\beta=\zeta_p\alpha\) recovers the quadratic cyclotomic extension, so the twist order is unbounded at measure ratio two.

### Equal-measure external twists and the two examples

For \(\alpha>0\) in a real embedding and \(E=\mathbb Q\), equality of measures gives \(|a|=j=1\).
An external twist lies in \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), hence \(h=2\) and \(K(\zeta)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) is quadratic over \(K\).
The trivial cyclotomic intersection gives twist orders \(3,4,6\).
Complex conjugation is the nonidentity automorphism of this quadratic extension.
For \(\gamma^2=\zeta\alpha^a\) one has \(\gamma\bar\gamma=\alpha^a\), \(\bar\gamma=\zeta^{-1}\gamma\), and the square of the relative trace is \((2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1})\alpha^a\).
The three coefficients are \(1,2,3\), respectively.
The first is excluded by primitivity, and coexistence of the latter two would put a real quadratic field in \(E\).
The tests are unchanged under \(a\mapsto-a\), and a prime of odd ramification index over \(p_0\) rules out \(p_0\alpha^a\) being a square by valuation.

Conversely each square root \(T\) in the indicated field gives \(\gamma=T/(1+\zeta^{-1})\) with the required square, trace and norm.
Its powers recover the seed field, and \(\gamma^2/\alpha^a\) recovers the twist, giving absolute degree \(2d\).
Taking the product of its relative quadratics over the embeddings of \(K\) gives the absolute minimal polynomial by this degree.
For any \(\beta^s=\gamma\), the original norm calculation applied with denominator \(2s\) gives relative degree at least \(2s\), while the tower through \(\gamma\) gives the reverse bound.
This proves irreducibility and exact measure retention for every further substitution.

For \(p=2,3\), Eisenstein gives transitivity on the quartic roots, and the resolvent \(Y^3+4pY-p^2\) is irreducible since its only real root is strictly between zero and one.
The group order is divisible by twelve, and the single complex pair gives a transposition, hence the group is \(S_4\).
An exact polynomial computation also gave group orders \(24,24\) and discriminants \(-2480,-9099\), respectively.
The maximal nonnormal point stabilizer makes \(K\) a nonnormal quartic with no nontrivial proper subfield, so \(E=\mathbb Q\).
For its positive root \(\pi\) of the defining quartic, \(x=1-\pi=\pi^4/p\) generates \(K\), and \(f_p(1)=1\) gives its unit norm.
Writing \(x=\alpha^k\) on the positive primitive unit ray, even \(k\) would put \(\sqrt p\) in \(K\).
Thus \(k\) is odd and division of \(px=\pi^4\) by the square \(\alpha^{k-1}\) gives the required \(p\alpha\) square.
A torsion quotient for one ordered pair of conjugates of \(x\) would transfer to all ordered pairs by double transitivity.
The unit norm would then put every conjugate on the unit circle, contrary to \(0<x<1\).
Since \(x=\alpha^k\) generates the same field, this excludes torsion collisions for \(\alpha\) as well.
The two fields therefore realize the claimed exceptional equations for primitive seeds.
The examples do not establish that either seed attains a Mahler measure record.
