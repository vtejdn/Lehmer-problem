## Fixed-count densities and the cost of a positive bound

On 2026-11-18T18:14 UTC a separately assigned agent for Yin independently reviewed the whole of [L462 v209](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v209.md#density), its three arguments and their inputs at the indicated scopes.
The reviewer received the fixed text without proof-development discussion or a prior finding on it and took no part in its development.
The relative density bound, the sharp logarithmic rate on identity progressions and the intrinsic threshold for a prescribed gain all hold.

The assumptions on \(\gamma\) make its own torsion-quotient class a singleton.
Reciprocity then places each inverse among its conjugates, and any conjugate differing by torsion from that inverse must equal it.
The \(d/2\) pairs are in equivariant bijection with the distinct traces, since the trace determines the roots of \(Z^2-tZ+1\).
Thus C270 applies to the faithful Galois action on those traces with \(n=d/2=bu\), \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\), and \(b\to\infty\).
At an unramified prime the finite decomposition group is generated by Frobenius, so its fixed letters are exactly the traces in the chosen \(\mathbb Q_p\).

Fix a coset of \(D=(A_b)^u\).
Its block permutation is constant, and multiplication by independent uniform elements in the \(u\) alternating factors makes the internal permutations on its stationary blocks independent, each with its prescribed sign.
For a stationary block of sign \(\varepsilon=\pm1\) the probability generating polynomial for the binomial moments of its number \(Y\) of fixed letters is
\[
 \mathbb E(1+z)^Y
 =\sum_{j=0}^{b-2}\frac{z^j}{j!}
  +(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{z^{b-1}}{(b-1)!}+\frac{z^b}{b!}\right).
\]
Indeed, prescribing \(j\le b-2\) fixed letters leaves half of the \((b-j)!\) completions of each sign, whereas prescribing \(b-1\) or \(b\) forces the identity.
The coefficient of \(z^k\) in the product for the \(s\) stationary blocks is \(\mathbb E\binom Xk\).
Through degree \(b-2\) this product agrees with \(\exp(sz)\), including for \(s=0\).
In higher degree each monomial of weight \(k\) receives at most \(\min(s,\lfloor k/(b-1)\rfloor)\) factors of two relative to the corresponding product of reciprocal factorials.
Extending the sum over block occupancies to all nonnegative parts gives the stated upper bound, since \(\binom Xk\ge1\) whenever \(X\ge k\).

For \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{m_0})\), reduction of the primes to a reduced residue class fixes the Frobenius restriction to \(E\), after omission of finitely many ramified primes.
Restriction to \(L\) identifies this fiber bijectively with the compatible coset of \(H={\rm Gal}(L/(L\cap E))\).
Indeed the compatible pairs of automorphisms of the two Galois fields give exactly the automorphisms of their compositum.
The abelian quotient \(G/H\) kills the perfect subgroup \(D\).
Conjugation preserves both the fiber, by abelianity over \(\mathbb Q\) of \(E\), and the fixed-count event.
I checked the counting form of the classical Chebotarev theorem in the first display on page 1 of the identified author preprint of Kadiri and Wong, at the statement scope needed here.
Summing over the appropriate conjugacy classes and dividing by the positive density of the fiber gives its uniform counting proportion.
In particular the bound for its \(D\)-cosets applies to every reduced class, including a fiber having no elements that meet the threshold.

For \(k=(\lambda+o(1))d/\log d\) one has \(k\log k=(\lambda+o(1))d\) and \(k(1+\log u)+u\log2=o_M(d)\) for fixed \(\lambda>0\).
This gives the upper exponential rate uniformly in the progression.
In the identity fiber the proportion assigned to \(D\) is \([H:D]^{-1}\ge(2^u u!)^{-1}\), whose negative logarithm is \(O(u\log(2u))=o_M(d)\).
Since \(u(b-2)=d/2-2u\), each such \(k\) eventually fits among the blocks with at most \(b-2\) prescribed fixed letters per block.
In \(D\) the probability of fixing that particular choice is \(\prod_i (b)_{j_i}^{-1}\ge b^{-k}\ge d^{-k}\).
This proves the matching lower rate on each identity progression, with errors independent of its modulus.
For \(p^j\le d^C\), positivity in C396 requires \(k>d\log2/(C\log d)\), accounting for the strict integer cutoff and the exponent \(-\log2/C\) there.
All these density comparisons first take the prime-counting limit with the individual field, progression and \(k\) fixed.

Substitution of \(\rho_p=2k/d\) into C396 gives precisely \(f(q)=(k\log q-d\log2)/q\) for the bound obtainable at \(q=p^j\).
For \(k>0\), the derivative has the sign of \(k+d\log2-k\log q\), so the unique maximizer over \(q>1\) gives \(k\exp(-1-d\log2/k)\).
Demanding a value at least \(\eta>0\) implies (3).
For \(k<e\eta\) the left side of (3) is negative, and for \(k\ge e\eta\) it is strictly increasing.
At \(k\sim c d/\log d\) its ratio to \(d\) tends to \(c\), giving the stated first integer asymptotic and the upper density rate at \(c=\log2\).
Finally \(k\le d/2\) implies \(2\eta/d\le\log(q/4)/q\).
The right side decreases for \(q>4e\), and at \(q=(1+\varepsilon)d\log d/(2\eta)\) its ratio to \(2\eta/d\) tends to \(1/(1+\varepsilon)\).
This proves the upper bound on \(q\) for every fixed \(\eta\).
Sharpness concerns the density of primes meeting the necessary fixed-count condition in identity progressions.
