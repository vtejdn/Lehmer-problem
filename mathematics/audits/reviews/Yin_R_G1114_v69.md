## Finding

C0899 v2 proves its two simultaneous estimates for a fixed positive proportion of composite orders, and the stated equivalence for one order in the degree window.
The multiplicity calculation works for inseparable reductions, and the error terms relative to \(d\,m(P)\) are uniform over the specified monic minimal polynomials with \(0<m(P)\le1\).

### Valuation of the product over primitive roots

For \(j=u\ell^a\) with \(\ell\nmid u,\ a\ge1\), choose a finite residue extension containing all \(u\)-th roots and its unramified lift of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\).
Each primitive \(u\)-th residue root \(\xi\) lifts uniquely to a \(u\)-th root in that field by simplicity.
The lift has exact order \(u\), since a power equal to one reduces to a power equal to one.
Products with primitive \(\ell^a\)-th roots range bijectively over primitive \(j\)-th roots, including \(u=1\).
The shifted cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{\ell^a}(1+X)\) is monic, reduces to \(X^{\varphi(\ell^a)}\) modulo \(\ell\), and has constant term \(\ell\).
It is Eisenstein also over the unramified ring, so every primitive \(\lambda\) has \(v_\ell(\lambda-1)=1/\varphi(\ell^a)\) in the normalization \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).

Taylor coefficients at the lifted \(\tilde\xi\) are integral by the polynomial binomial expansion.
Their reductions are exactly the coefficients of \(\bar P(\xi+X)\).
If \(h_\xi<\varphi(\ell^a)\) is the root multiplicity, the term of index \(h_\xi\) at \(X=\tilde\xi(\lambda-1)\) has valuation \(h_\xi/\varphi(\ell^a)<1\).
Each lower coefficient lies in the maximal ideal generated by \(\ell\) before adjoining \(\lambda\), so every lower term has valuation at least one.
Every higher term has larger valuation.
The unique minimum gives equality even for \(h_\xi=0\).
Summing over all primitive \(j\)-th roots gives \(\sum_\xi h_\xi\), with all \(\varphi(\ell^a)\) evaluations above each residue root and all residue conjugates counted once.
The resultant is nonzero since the original unit is nontorsion.

### Charging the multiplicities

Over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), the kernel dimension of multiplication by \(\bar P'\) on \(\mathbb F_\ell[z]/(\bar P)\) is \(\deg\gcd(\bar P,\bar P')\).
Indeed a kernel representative must be divisible by \(\bar P/\gcd(\bar P,\bar P')\).
Extending the residue field preserves this dimension, and at a root of multiplicity \(h\) the gcd has multiplicity at least \(h-1\), including when the characteristic divides \(h\).
The same multiplication before reduction has determinant \(\operatorname{Res}(P,P')\) in the monic power basis, a nonzero integer because \(P\) is separable in characteristic zero.
Its reduction defect counts the elementary divisors divisible by \(\ell\), hence is bounded by their total valuation.
The square of the complex Vandermonde determinant is the discriminant up to sign, with leading coefficient one.
Hadamard's inequality gives \(\log|\operatorname{disc}P|\le d\log d+2(d-1)m(P)\).
Thus \(\sum_{\ell>d^\delta} E_\ell=O_\delta(d)\).

For a fixed \(\ell\) and fixed nonzero residue root, the order \(u\) is fixed, so the indices it can affect are in the geometric sequence \(u\ell^a\).
At most two occur in a closed dyadic window, even when considering endpoints.
A failure of the simple valuation formula has \(h_\xi-1\ge\varphi(\ell^a)-1\ge\ell-2\gg d^\delta\) for large \(d\).
It therefore costs that many copies in \(E_\ell\) per root and at most twice as many lost orders as charged roots.
This gives the first \(O_\delta(d^{1-\delta})\) exceptional count.

On all remaining orders, the first copy of each contributing residue root costs at most \(\sum_{\ell\mid j,\ \ell>d^\delta}(j/\ell)\log\ell\le j d^{-\delta}\log j\).
The extra copies cost at most \(2\log|\operatorname{disc}P|\) after summing across the window, by the same two-index bound.
The number of orders where the extra-copy cost exceeds \(d^{1-\delta/2}\) is consequently \(O_\delta(d^{\delta/2}\log d)\).
Both exceptional sets have size \(o(d)\).
At fixed \(A,\delta\) the bound off those sets is \(O_{A,\delta}(d^{1-\delta}\log d+d^{1-\delta/2})\).
The total logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies \(m(P)\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) for large \(d\), so the ratio of this error to \(d\,m(P)\) tends to zero uniformly.

### Intersection with the sieve and the single witness

The C0899 v1 sieve applies to these monic primitive polynomials with no root of unity.
Its choices \(1/2<\beta<\alpha<1,\ 2\gamma+\delta<\beta,\ \gamma>\delta\) allow \(A\log(\alpha/\beta)>1/2\) under exactly the displayed upper bound for \(\delta\).
The distinguished-prime multiples retain this margin above the \(d/2+o(d)\) possible losses in L0462 v70.
The count of small-characteristic incidences and removal of a sufficiently small totient fraction preserve a positive proportion of composite orders.
On those orders **every** prime factor of \(D_j\) exceeds \(d^\delta\), so the two new exceptional sets and the uniform estimate account for all logarithms omitted by \(T_j\).
The sieve lower bound on \(\log D_j\) allows division by that quantity.
For each retained prime with \(\ell\nmid j\), the reductions of the factors of \(z^j-1\) indexed by divisors of \(j\) are disjoint because this polynomial is separable.
The common root detected by the resultant in \(\Phi_j\bmod\ell\) therefore has exact order \(j\).
This concerns the contributing roots, without a uniform order requirement on the other roots modulo \(\ell\).

The choice \(\delta=1/5,\ A=1\) satisfies the strict parameter restriction.
A uniform positive lower bound on \(m(P)\) immediately yields composite witnesses at size \(cd\).
Conversely fix the minimal polynomial \(Q\) of any nontorsion unit of degree \(D\) with \(m(Q)\le1\).
The irreducible substitutions \(Q(z^p)\) at all sufficiently large primes have degree \(d=Dp\) and the same measure.
Irreducibility follows from the factor-product criterion for \(X^p-\alpha\) over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and the finiteness of bounded-degree algebraic integers with bounded conjugates, as proved in L0462 v60.
If \(p\mid j\), the primitive resultant is a power of \(D_{j/p}(Q)\), where \(D\le j/p\le2D\).
Only finitely many fixed rational primes occur in these values, so the moving cutoff eventually forces \(T_j=0\) on every such order.
Any witness has \(p\nmid j\), where powering permutes the primitive roots and the resultant equals \(D_j(Q)\).
For fixed \(Q\), \(\log D_j(Q)/\varphi(j)\to m(Q)\) as \(j\to\infty\), with the retained lower bound for a fixed algebraic circle root's distance to a root of unity used at its stated scope.
Since \(T_j\le\log D_j(Q)\) and \(\varphi(j)\le2d\), these witnesses imply \(m(Q)\ge c/2\) for the fixed unit of any original degree.
Units outside the test range satisfy \(m(Q)>1\).
This proves the converse for either choice on compositeness and does not require a prior lower bound for unrestricted \(m(Q)\).
