At 2026-11-17T09:12 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [fixed submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#decision-moving-block-allocation-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations, exploration notes or prior findings about this submission.
The estimates (1)–(3), the exterior-root threshold and the prime-binomial gap follow with the stated dependence on the parameters.

For the polynomial maximum test, the integer resultant has modulus at least one, and every cofactor of the Sylvester matrix has modulus at most \(H^{2w}\) by the column norm bound.
The resulting Bézout identity bounds the maximum from below on the closed disk; if one polynomial is constant its modulus is already at least one.
Common nonzero roots of the reversed pair would give common roots of the original pair, and the two reversed polynomials cannot both vanish at zero.
Thus \(g\) is bounded by \(O(wh)\) on both closed coordinate disks of the sphere, and the derivative bounds for the polynomials give its Lipschitz bound \(\exp(O(wh))\) there.
Inversion is distance decreasing between exterior points, and splitting a segment at its crossings with the unit circle supplies the plane estimate.

The positive Riesz measure of \(f\) has total mass \(D\) by the circular mean formula and the growth \(D\log |z|+O(1)\).
Subtracting \(D\log^+|z|\) therefore gives a Laplacian of total mass zero and total variation at most \(4\pi D\).
Integration by parts against \(g\), which is also Lipschitz in the coordinate at infinity, bounds its Dirichlet integral by \(O(w^2h)\).
The signed-energy identity then gives the stated Cauchy–Schwarz estimate for the compactly supported smoothed discrepancy used here.
The diagonal terms of the positive-log interaction contribute \(d^{-1}\log\epsilon\); the two submean inequalities and the nonzero integral discriminant make the sum of the off-diagonal terms nonnegative.
The interaction with circle measure is the average of \(\log^+|z|\), which changes by at most \(\epsilon\) on smoothing and has original average \(m/d\).
These signs give the asserted upper energy bound, including \(d=1\).
The chosen radius costs at most \(1/d\) on undoing the smoothing, contributes \(O((wh+\log(2d))/d)\) to the energy, and restoring the radial logarithm costs \(Dm/d\).
This proves (3).

The degree assumption makes the leading coefficient of \(P\) one, independently of any overlap of the blocks.
Its coefficient norm bounds \(m(S)\le\ell\), and both the constant term and coprimality exclude vanishing in the root identity.
The integer \(\operatorname{Res}(A,S)\) divides the nonzero integer \(\operatorname{Res}(A,P)=\operatorname{Res}(A,B)\), using monicity of \(A\); the Sylvester bound gives logarithm \(O(W\ell)\), also valid for \(A=1\).
After division by \(d\) the resultant contributes \(O(W\ell/d)\), which, like the first term of (3), fits the right side of (1).
For bounded \(L\) the squared error is \(O((W^3+W^2\log(2d))/d)\), which vanishes for \(W=o(d^{1/3})\).

In the reciprocal case \(A\) has no unit-circle zero, and \(cX^nA\) strictly dominates \(A^*\) there for \(c>1\).
The resulting upper bound \(\deg A\) on the number of exterior roots survives the limit \(c\downarrow1\) for the strictly exterior roots, with multiplicity.
Reversal supplies the same bound on strictly interior roots without requiring that the chosen factor be reciprocal.
On the fixed root disk, each monic divisor of \(A\) has coefficient norm at most \(2^W L\), so the Bézout cost for a pair of distinct irreducible divisors is \(\exp(O_L(W^2))\), and the cost for \(A,A^*\) is only \(\exp(O_L(W))\).
At an exterior root every unassigned divisor has modulus at least the pairwise lower bound by comparison with the minimizer, also in a tie.
Here \(|A^*|\ge|A|\), so the root equation bounds \(e_q\log|q(z)|+n\log|z|\) by \(O_L(W^3)\) for the assigned \(q\).
At an interior root \(|A|\ge|A^*|\); division by the upper bound for \(|A/q|\) gives \(|\log|q(z)||=O_L(W)\).

On the circle \(\log\max(|q|,|q^*|)=\log|q|\), of mean \(m(q)\).
For this coprime pair, (3) costs \(O_L(W^{3/2}\sqrt{(W^2+\log(2d))/d})\), and the test has size \(O_L(W^2)\) on the entire root disk.
Consequently omitting the at most \(2W\) noncircle terms costs \(O_L(W^3/d)\) in the normalized sum.
The sum of \(\log|q|\) over all conjugates is between zero and \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(q,A^*)|=O_L(W^2)\).
Subtracting the assigned terms leaves at most \(W\) errors of size \(O_L(W^3)\), and the other noncircle terms cost no more.
This yields (2) with its coefficient \(n/(e_qd)\), including when no root is assigned to \(q\).

Each irreducible divisor \(q\) of the nonconstant \(A\) has nonzero constant term and is nonreciprocal, so Smyth's lower bound gives \(m(q)\ge\log\theta>0\).
At \(d=C_L W^5\) the squared first term in (2) is bounded, up to the fixed implied constant, by \((1+W^{-2}\log(2C_LW^5))/C_L\); this tends uniformly to zero as \(C_L\to\infty\).
The \(W^4/d\) term does likewise, and both bounds decrease for larger \(d\).
Thus every \(q\) receives a root; for constant \(A\) the count is empty.
Eisenstein's criterion and the distinct primes give exactly \(k\) different factors in the final family, with all their roots exterior and all reversed roots interior.
For \(b=\min u_i\), either \(|z|^b<2\max p_i\) or the product of the \(k\) bounded quotients gives a constant bound for \(|z|^n\), hence for \(|z|^b\).
Fewer than \(k\) exterior roots implies \(m(S)=O(1/b)\), and comparability of the block exponents gives \(W=O(1/m(S))\).
The preceding threshold then gives \(d=O(m(S)^{-5})\).
If the positive measures approach zero, coefficient bounds force \(d\to\infty\).
For a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer of sufficiently large degree, [Dobrowolski, Theorem 1, p. 391](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-34-4-391-401) gives \(1/m(S)=O((\log(2d))^3)\) by taking the logarithm of the multiplicative bound.
This would force \(d=O((\log(2d))^{15})\), a contradiction; a Salem minimal polynomial has one exterior root and is included for \(k=2\).
The review checked Dobrowolski's published statement and the retained Smyth statement and their hypotheses, without re-auditing the original proofs.
