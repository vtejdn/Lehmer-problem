## Shortest lattice vectors, actual local degrees and uniformity over the divisible group

At 2026-11-24T23:36 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v76.md#statement) and both proof sections of C0901 v76.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v76.
The shortest-vector inequality (3), the archimedean bound (1) and both finite-place conclusions hold for the stated unit inputs, including arbitrary growing constituent ranks and ramification.

The rational span of the conjugate classes has a full finitely generated free lattice, stable under \(G\).
Its intersection with the rational subspace \(W\) has full rank there, by clearing denominators in a rational basis, so the chosen positive definite metric has a shortest nonzero lattice vector \(v\).
Every vector \(u=gv\) is again shortest, and every nonzero difference \(u-su\) lies in the same lattice and has length at least \(\|v\|\).
For orthogonal \(H\)-actions, averaging the squared lengths of these differences gives \(2\|u\|^2-2(u,P_Hu)=2\|(1-P_H)u\|^2\).
Exactly the fraction \(1/|H\cdot u|\) of \(H\) fixes \(u\), giving the desired lower bound for that average.

The conjugation average of \(P_H\) lies in the center of the rational group algebra acting on \(W\).
On each irreducible complex summand its scalar is the dimension of the \(H\)-fixed subspace divided by the dimension of that summand, since each conjugate of \(P_H\) has the same trace there.
These scalars are rational, and rational irreducibility then gives a single eigenvalue on all of \(W\), including when complexification splits.
Taking traces identifies it as \(\dim W^H/r\).
Averaging the difference estimate and rearranging proves \(\mathbb E_g(1/|H\cdot gv|)\ge2\dim W^H/r-1\).

Reversing \(v\) preserves every orbit size, and permits choosing the product of at most half the original conjugates with strictly positive pairing, as in v68.
Both signs occur because the roots span the space and their class sum is zero.
The product pairs positively with the selected sign of \(v\), hence is nontorsion and has \(2h(z)\le m(w)\).
The stabilizer of \(v\) permutes the actual selected roots and fixes \(z\), so \(|H\cdot gz|\le|H\cdot gv|\) for every \(g\).
For \(H=\langle c\rangle\), the mean reciprocal orbit size of \(gv\) is \((1+\Pr(cgv=gv))/2\), also when \(c\) acts trivially.
Inserting \(\dim W^H=r-j\) gives the lower bound \(1-4j/r\) on this fixed probability.
Each such \(g\) gives \(gz\) real, and uniform restriction counts transport the fraction to the conjugates of \(z\).
Garza's bound and its monotonicity yield (1) whenever the asserted \(p\) is positive.
For any fixed downward margin from \(1/4\), this gives one positive lower bound on \(m(w)\), independent of the constituent dimension.
Every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) has a nonzero rational conjugate span \(V_x\subseteq V\), since a positive power has a monomial expression up to torsion.
Complete reducibility and the negative-eigenspace formula for the arguments identify its rank fraction as the dimension-weighted average of the constituent fractions, giving the same uniform lower restriction.

After fixing an \(\ell\)-adic embedding of the splitting field, \(D\) is the full group of the local Galois extension and \(|D\cdot u|=[\mathbb Q_\ell(u):\mathbb Q_\ell]\) for each \(u\in L\).
A left-coset decomposition of \(D\) gives \(|D\cdot u|\le[D:H]|H\cdot u|\), with no normality requirement on \(H\).
When \(2\dim W^H/r-1\ge\lambda>0\), bounding the reciprocal orbit size by one on orbits of size at most \(C\) and by \(1/(C+1)\) on the others proves \(\Pr(|H\cdot gv|\le C)\ge\lambda/2\) at \(C\ge2/\lambda\).
Taking \(B_0=AC\), it follows for the same \(g\)'s that \(\mathbb Q_\ell(gz)\) has degree at most \(B_0\).

In a local extension of degree at most \(B_0\), both the ramification index and residue degree are at most \(B_0\).
The positive residue order of the unit image divides \(\ell^f-1\), hence divides the displayed common exponent \(T\).
In the normalization \(\nu(\ell)=1\) one has \(\nu((gz)^T-1)\ge1/B_0\) at every such embedding.
For any \(\nu(y)\ge1/B_0\), the two possible minimal term bounds for the binomial expansion are \(1+\nu(y)\) and \(\ell\nu(y)\), each at least \(\nu(y)+1/B_0\).
This includes the dyadic case and permits cancellations only in the improving direction.
Iteration gives valuation at least \((a+1)/B_0\) at exponent \(N=T\ell^a\).
The algebraic integer \(z^N-1\) is nonzero by nontorsion.
All other finite places contribute nonpositive logarithms, while the archimedean mean log modulus is at most \(\log2+Nh(z)\).
Uniform local embeddings count the local degree weights above \(\ell\), hence the product formula gives \(Nh(z)\ge\delta(a+1)\log(\ell)/B_0-\log2\).
Choosing \(C\) and then \(a\) as indicated gives an effective positive bound on \(m(w)\) for every fixed violation margin, depending only on \(\ell,A,\lambda\), uniformly in the constituent, place and subgroup.
This proves (2).
Finally at an unramified prime every eigenvalue of Frobenius of order at most \(B\) is fixed by its \(B!\)-th power.
That power generates a subgroup of index at most \(B!\), so diagonalizability and (2) give the stated upper proportion, including multiplicities.
