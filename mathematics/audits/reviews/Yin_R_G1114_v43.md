## Least interpolation order and its necessary divergence

The second-branch conclusion of C345v7 gives content \(t=1\) and leading coefficient \(c=2\) for \(A\) eventually.
Simplicity of the trace zeros implies that \(P=AV\) and \(W=U/V\) are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\).
For nonconstant monic \(W\), reduction into the basis \(1,\ldots,x^{\deg W-1}\) is integral.
Multiplication by \(P\) has eigenvalues \(P(y)\) at the distinct roots \(y\) of \(W\), so its determinant is \(\operatorname{Res}(W,P)\ne0\).
Its cokernel has order the absolute value of this determinant.
The additive order of \([Q_P]\) therefore divides the determinant's absolute value and equals the least denominator of \(P^{-1}Q_P\bmod W\) in the integral basis.

For an integral multiple \(G=PH\) put \(u=H(2)\).
Then \(H=u+(x-2)J\) for some integral \(J\), and
\[
G-uP(2)=(x-2)(uQ_P+PJ).
\]
Since every root of \(W\) is simple and different from two, equality \(G(y)=G(2)\) at all these roots is equivalent to \(W\mid uQ_P+PJ\) in \(\mathbb Z[x]\).
An integer \(u\) is thus attainable exactly when \(u[Q_P]=0\) in the cokernel.
This proves optimality for the specified interpolation at every choice of \(V\), also when \(W=1\) makes the condition empty and the least positive order one.

For \(u=b(P,W)\) the common value is \(e=uP(2)\ne0\), since the zeros of \(P\) lie in \((-2,2)\).
The measure \(G^2\lambda/2\) vanishes at all roots of \(P\) and equals \(e^2\lambda/2\) at each retained root of \(W\).
The endpoint integral is at most \(e^2s/2\), independently of the size of the multiplier elsewhere.
I checked the integral division in C345v7 with the parameters \(t=1,c=2\), which gives the congruence \(s'\in\mathbb Z+e^2/(q-1)\).
The lift in that claim has integral Fourier coefficients, total mass \(\lceil2s'\rceil\), and no contribution at 1 from the trace poles.
If the minimum in (1) remained bounded along a subsequence, the masses would be uniformly bounded by the bound on \(s\).
Since \(q\to\infty\), eventually \(0<2e^2/(q-1)<1\), and the atom \(\lceil2s'\rceil-2s'=1-2e^2/(q-1)\) violates C345v6 at the left of one.
Taking \(V=1\) proves both assertions for bounded \(A(2)\).
The separate endpoint formula follows directly from \(C(2)/B(2)=1-q\) and \(C=UA\).

## The irreducible family and the odd-prime Fourier conditions

Multiplication by \(w-1\) verifies the coefficients \(2,3,\ldots,3,2\) of \(S_N\), giving primitivity, reciprocity and leading coefficient two.
The symmetric Laurent expansion of \(z^{-m}S_N(-z)\) consequently gives an integral degree-\(m\) trace polynomial with leading coefficient two.
On the circle the argument derivative of the finite disk map in the root equation is
\[
N-1+\frac{1-1/4}{|w+1/2|^2}>0.
\]
Its degree is \(N\), hence its \(N\) preimages of 1 are distinct and exhaust the numerator roots.

For a torsion point of odd order, applying the squaring automorphism forces \(T(w^2)=T(w)^2\).
Clearing the nonzero denominators gives \(2(w-1)^3(w+1)\), admitting only \(w=1\) at odd order.
For order \(2h\) with \(h\) odd, \(h+2\) is coprime to \(2h\) and \(w^{h+2}=-w^2\).
The even power \(N-1\) gives \(T(-w^2)=T(w)^2\), whose numerator I verified up to sign as \(2(w^4-2w^3-7w^2-2w+1)\).
Division by \(w^2\) gives the equation \(x^2-2x-9=0\), whose smaller root \(1-\sqrt{10}\) is below \(-2\) and larger root is above \(2\).
When the order \(h\) is divisible by four, \(1+h/2\) is coprime to \(h\) and sends \(w\) to \(-w\).
Thus \(T(-w)=T(w)\), impossible because this Möbius map is injective and \(w\ne0\).
These cases cover order two and remove every torsion root from \(S_N\) after division by \(w-1\).

Any monic factor would have all roots on the circle and hence torsion by Kronecker's theorem.
Gauss's lemma and the prime leading coefficient then force irreducibility, with multiplicity one.
Substitution of \(-z\) preserves irreducibility, and each positive-degree factor of \(A_m\) would lift to a positive-degree integral factor of \(S_N(-z)\).
All \(2m\) roots occur in distinct inverse pairs away from \(\pm1\), giving \(m\) simple trace roots in the open interval.

The coefficient sum gives \(S_N(1)=3N-2\), and evaluation of the defining quotient gives \(S_N(-1)=1\).
At each nontrivial \(m\)-th root \(\zeta\) for \(m\) odd, \(w=-\zeta\ne1\) and \(w^{2m}=1\), so the numerator reduces to \(w-1\) and \(A_m(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})=1\).
The symmetric full sum of the \(m\)-th roots' powers gives the claimed monic \(U\), with \(U(2)=m\).
Hence the multiplication matrix for \(A_m\bmod U\) is the identity, giving signed resultant one and additive order one.
At the other endpoint \(|A_m(-2)|=3N-2\) diverges.
On the circle, \(|x+2|=|1+z|^2\) cancels the denominator from \(S_N(-z)\), leaving the bound \(6|1+z|\le12\) on \(|(x+2)A_m(x)|\), also at \(z=-1\) by continuity.
The equality \(B(2)=-1\) realizes only the stated endpoint divisibility in these example data.
As an arithmetic check I expanded the traces for \(1\le m\le11\), verified the endpoints and irreducibility, and obtained determinant one in the torsion trace quotient for odd \(3\le m\le11\).

For \(m=2^k\) both the roots and their inverses are integral over \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) by the reciprocal polynomial divided by two.
The sums of powers are rational by invariance under conjugates and hence belong to this integrally closed ring.
The normalization \(2m\) and both mixture weights preserve the same ring.
The nontrivial support is one irreducible orbit of degree \(2m\), disjoint from 1, so the identity mass is exactly \(1-2^{-k}\).
For example, the first moment of \(\rho_k\) is \(3/(4m)\) by the leading two coefficients of \(S_N(-z)\), which displays the dyadic denominator directly.
This verifies the failure of isolation with the stated local moment conditions and nontorsion support.
