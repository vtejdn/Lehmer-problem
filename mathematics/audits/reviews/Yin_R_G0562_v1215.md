## The fixed-first-factor argument permits an arbitrary locally bounded growth target

The equivalence holds for unrestricted positive integer-polynomial measures, with squarefree products of exactly two different irreducible strict records, freely prescribed second-iterate growth and both weighted angular limits.

For strict positive Perron units \(A,B_i\), any automorphism preserving the product must attain the maximal modulus in each factor separately.
Uniqueness there fixes both actual values; conversely their common stabilizer fixes the product.
This proves the embedded compositum equality and the strict Perron property of the product.
The relative degree over the fixed first field tends to infinity because its absolute compositum degree is at least \(\deg B_i\).
The height inequalities applied to \(AB_i\) and to \((AB_i)/B_i\) give the two-sided error \(D_i h(B_i)\).
Dividing by \(D_i\) shows convergence to the fixed positive height \(h(A)\), even if the unnormalized additive error is itself large.
No degree-product or linear-disjointness assertion is needed.

In a fixed embedding of \(K=\mathbb Q(A)\), the values of the second factor are the roots of its monic relative minimum, all distinct and numbering \(q_i=[K(B_i):K]\).
Because every coefficient conjugate of an algebraic integer is integral, the product of the absolute discriminants over embeddings of \(K\) is the absolute norm of a nonzero algebraic integer in \(K\), hence at least one.
In the sum across all these fibers the sum of positive logs is \([K:\mathbb Q]q_i h(B_i)\), with both positive and absolute negative sums equal since the number is a unit.
The reflected Fourier estimate from v148 bounds each single fiber by retaining its nonnegative energy in the total and using this product of discriminants.
With \(K\) fixed, division by the fiber size gives an error tending to zero for every fixed nonzero frequency as \(h(B_i)\to0\) and \(q_i\to\infty\).
The radial correction to the pure phases in any one normalized fiber is at most \(2|k|[K:\mathbb Q]h(B_i)\).
These calculations justify the cited estimate for the actual relative minimum, even when its conjugate fibers for different base embeddings overlap as rational conjugate sets.
Rotation by the corresponding image of \(A\) preserves the Haar limit.

Under uniform full embeddings, the average absolute difference between the radial logs of the product and those of the fixed first factor is exactly \(2h(B_i)\).
The positive-part and negative-part weight functions are both 1-Lipschitz, so against a bounded phase test either weight may be replaced with the corresponding weight from the first factor at vanishing cost.
These replacement weights are constant in each fiber and each has mean \(h(A)>0\).
The conditional Fourier limits therefore give vanishing normalized nonconstant coefficients after dividing by the respective total positive or negative mean weights.
The unweighted product law follows as well: the radial log differs in probability from its fixed-factor fiber value by a vanishing amount, and conditional angular convergence holds on every one of the finitely many fixed fibers.

If the unrestricted gap fails, successive strict decreases of the positive minimum through bounded degrees exist by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness and tend to one in multiplicative measure.
At a strict first attainment, a decomposition into positive-degree factors would give a lower-degree positive-measure factor at or below the new minimum, a contradiction.
This argument also discards extraneous cyclotomic factors and powers of zero, while sufficiently small positive measure excludes nonunit content and nonunit extreme coefficients.
The resulting monic irreducible record factors have strictly increasing degrees.
Their actual measures are distinct positive strict Perron units, with maximal modulus equal to the original positive value and absolute height at most its logarithm.
Their degrees as *values* diverge by bounded-house bounded-degree coefficient finiteness.
Thus the entire tail of later record values can play the role of the second factors for any one temporarily fixed first record.

At stage \(j\) keep the first log measure fixed and positive until the second index is selected.
The interval from this fixed log measure to twice itself is compact in the positive half-line, so local boundedness suffices to bound the arbitrary target function there.
The fixed-factor asymptotic diverges along all sufficiently late second indices, while their first log measures approach zero and the first \(j\) Fourier tests on both normalized weights converge at this fixed choice.
All the required inequalities can therefore be imposed simultaneously, including strict domination of the target and of \(j+2\log(AB_i)\).
Choosing the next first index beyond previous choices gives disjoint record factors and squarefree two-factor products.
The first log of the second record is negligible relative to the first record's, and the diagonal Fourier conditions imply both angular limits by trigonometric approximation.
The reverse direction needs only that these particular integer polynomials have positive logarithmic measures tending to zero.
The result never assumes the original examples witnessing failure already had large second iterates.

In the concrete example, v152 gives the vanishing absolute height of the five-term family’s actual measure by dividing its second total logarithmic measure by its increasing degree.
The fixed Salem number is a strict positive Perron unit whose own measure equals that number.
The joint height asymptotic and both angular limits therefore apply exactly as in the fixed-factor argument.
The multiplicative first measures tend to \(AL>1\), so this example verifies the mechanism without satisfying the first-measure condition for Lehmer failure.
