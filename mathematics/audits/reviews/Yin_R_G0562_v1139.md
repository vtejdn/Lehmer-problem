## The projected fiber products control both the field and the two-point degree

At 2026-11-24T23:41 UTC an agent outside the development of [C0901 v80](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v80.md#statement) independently reviewed the whole fixed argument for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and requested scope without the development discussion.
The statements on field kernels, intersection and both asymptotic rank constants hold with their stated hypotheses.

The conjugates constitute one transitive \(G\)-set. Their images span the nonzero quotient, so none of these images is zero.
All \(t\) fibers have exactly \(e=d/t\) actual conjugates, including any distinct roots differing by torsion.
The product over the fiber above \(u\) projects to \(eu\), so products over different fibers are distinct even in multiplicative classes and every product is nontorsion.
The action of \(G\) takes the product above \(u\) to the product above \(gu\), without a torsion discrepancy.
The stabilizer is exactly that of \(u\), giving degree \(t\) and total measure at most \(te\,h(w)=m\).
For a strict record, a lower degree with lower or equal measure contradicts respectively minimality or first attainment.
Thus every quotient separates the \(d\) conjugates, and an element fixing the quotient fixes each actual conjugate and hence the splitting field pointwise.
By complete reducibility this reasoning applies to each nonzero rational submodule even when equivalent constituents occur with multiplicity.

Multiplicative classes in the algebraic closure form a rational vector space after passage modulo all roots of unity.
For \(x\in D(w)\), a power relation up to torsion places its class and all its Galois translates in \(V_w\).
Its nonzero span \(V_x\) is invariant, and the absolute Galois action on it has kernel exactly the subgroup fixing \(L\): it already factors through \(G\), whose action there is faithful.
The subgroup fixing the actual conjugates of \(x\) lies in this kernel, giving containment of \(L\) in the splitting field of \(x\).
Taking the fixed field of the class-action kernel recovers \(L\) from \(x\) alone, even for radicals and torsion multiples whose actual splitting fields could be larger.
Applying this intrinsic description twice rules out every nontorsion intersection element for strict records of different splitting fields.

The projected orbit lies on a sphere of positive radius.
With more than two points an antipodal pair cannot be closest, since a third point on that sphere has smaller distance to either endpoint.
Nonproportionality of a closest pair gives multiplicative independence of the associated fiber products by projecting any putative relation.
Their ordered-pair orbit has size \([\mathbb Q(z_u,z_v):\mathbb Q]\); after the first point is chosen the possible second points lie in its set of nearest neighbors.
For distinct neighbors the difference of their length-\(\lambda\) displacement vectors has norm at least \(\lambda\).
After normalization this bounds all pairwise inner products by \(1/2\).

In a cap of opening \(\pi/4\) the axial components are at least \(1/\sqrt2\); subtracting their products leaves all perpendicular inner products nonpositive.
An axial unit vector itself precludes any second point in the cap.
Otherwise normalize the projections, and for their Gram matrix use \(b_{ij}^2\le-b_{ij}\) off the diagonal and \(\sum_{ij}b_{ij}\ge0\).
This gives \(\operatorname{tr}(B^2)\le2s\) and \(s\le2(r-1)\) by the trace-rank inequality.
The cap probability is at least \(2^{-(r-1)/2}/(\pi(r-1))\), since \(d\theta=dx/\sqrt{1-x^2}\ge dx\) for \(x=\sin\theta\) on this interval and the full polar denominator is at most \(\pi\).
This also works for \(r=2\).
Averaging over axes gives the printed upper bound on the number of neighbors.

The Amoroso–David height-product theorem at fixed rank two has exponent \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\) and uses the degree of the joint field of the two independent numbers.
Thus \(m^2/t^2\) is at least \(c/[D(\log(3D))^{212}]\).
Use the packing bound for \(D/t\) and \(D\le t^2\) separately, so \(\log(3D)\le2\log(3t)\).
This proves (1) with an absolute constant, without requiring an effective numerical value from the source.

For strict records \(t=d\); taking logs yields the coefficient \(2/\log2\) once \(2\log r\) is bounded by \(O(\log\log(3d))\) in the stated small-\(r\) branch.
In the other branch \(4\log(3d)\) already dominates this leading bound.
The error in this estimate is uniform over all constituents for a common upper measure bound.
For \(m\to0\), the uniform lower bound on the negative eigenspace fraction from v76 gives \(1/4-o(1)\) in every constituent.
Its product with the lower bound on \(r\) gives the constant \(1/(2\log2)\), uniformly for every nontorsion \(x\) because \(V_x\) contains such a constituent and its negative eigenspace counts its own conjugate argument rank.
Bounded-degree coefficient finiteness and factorization show that a zero unrestricted infimum would supply infinitely many strict drops, realized below \(\log2\) by unit minima.
