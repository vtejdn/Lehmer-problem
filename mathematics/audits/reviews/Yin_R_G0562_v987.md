## Verification of the translation point and Frobenius periods

The affine gradient has a common zero only for nonzero \(t\), and substitution there gives \(t^{-4}(1+t^3+t^4)\).
Bihomogenization excludes \((\infty,\infty)\) and gives just the points \(O,S\) at infinity, with the local linear term \((1+t)u\) at \(O\) and its symmetric counterpart at \(S\).
For each smooth parameter the divisor sequence gives geometric connectedness and genus one, since the only nonzero cohomology of \(\mathcal O(-2,-2)\) is a one-dimensional second cohomology group.
The rational origin consequently defines an elliptic curve over the stated field.

The relations in \(w\) recover both elementary symmetric functions of \(x,y\), and the nonidentity swap exchanges the two generic ordered lifts.
The projection to \(x\) is likewise a separable double cover, with pole divisor \(2[O]\).
The two points over \(w(O)\) are \(O,S\) because the swap moves \(O\) to the distinct point \(S\).
Linear equivalence of the respective fibers thus gives the two involutions \(-P\) and \(S-P\) by the Abel–Jacobi isomorphism.
Taking their composition in the displayed order gives translation by \(-S\), whose affine coordinates follow from the finite root of \((1+t)y+1=0\) over \(x=1\).
The second coordinate formula also follows from the quadratic root product, including at a double root whenever the displayed denominator is nonzero.

On the Frobenius graph \(y=x^q\), the only candidates outside the domain of that formula are the boundary points or points with \(x=0,1\).
Neither boundary point satisfies the first-coordinate equality, and \((0,0)\) is off the curve.
At \(x=1\) the unique candidate is \((1,1)\) on \(t=0\), which the translation sends to \(S\).
Clearing the remaining nonzero denominator gives exactly \(G(x)=0\), and each such root on this fiber recovers its unique point \((x,x^q)\).
The endomorphism \(F-1\) has nonzero differential and its geometric kernel is the rational group, so every geometric fiber has exactly \(N_t\) distinct points.
Rationality of \(A_t\) implies that Frobenius returns each of these points first at the order of \(A_t\).
This proves the relative degree and the factor count using squarefreeness of \(G\).
The external inputs apply to the elliptic curve just obtained.
Sutherland's Fall 2023 MIT 18.783 notes give the divisor-class isomorphism in Lecture 23, Theorem 23.17 (p. 7), the separability test in Lecture 7, Lemma 7.1, and the stated point-count bound in Lecture 7, Theorem 7.3 (p. 1).

A nonzero root and its inverse exhaust the possible preimages of its trace, and the reciprocal graphs are disjoint, so squaring preserves the root-to-trace bijection and all its periods.
For a root of binary period \(d\), uniqueness of its label forces the label period to divide \(d\).
The period under raising to \(2^a\) is \(d/\gcd(d,a)\).
For a full-degree label this gives \(a\mid d\) and hence \(d=ah_t\).
Squaring bijects the \(a\) conjugate fibers, so their \(aN_t\) points form exactly \(N_t/h_t\) binary irreducible factors.
For a proper-subfield label only the stated relation and upper bound follow from this argument.
These checks establish the submitted claim throughout the stated smooth locus.
