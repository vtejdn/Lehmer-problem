## Outcome of the module and arithmetic checks

The rational module formulas and the integral index identities hold with the stated width and power hypotheses.
All 24 sample indices agree with an independent computation by maximal minors.

At a fixed sufficiently large \(p\), the width-three kernel vanishes and the homomorphism from the ten rational width-four coefficients into \(\mathbb Q[X]/P^2\) has rank at most eight.
Projection onto the two top coefficients is therefore injective on a space of dimension at least two, hence is an isomorphism.
Clearing denominators shows that the integral kernel is a rank-two lattice spanning that space.
The powered degree is four because the map on the four conjugates has fibers of equal size and only one conjugate maps to the exterior \(\rho^p\).
For any two kernel pairs, multiplying the first graph by the second \(V(X^p)\) and subtracting the reverse product proves that \(P^2\) divides the determinant after substitution.
At each conjugate \(\alpha\) of the seed the derivative of \(X^p\) is \(p\alpha^{p-1}\ne0\).
Thus the determinant has a double zero at each of the four powered conjugates.
For a lattice basis its leading coefficient \(d\) is the determinant of its top vectors, so the determinant is exactly \(dQ^2\).
Cancellation of successive top coefficients over \(\mathbb Q\) yields the claimed expressions with multiplier degrees at most \(b-4\), and independence over \(\mathbb Q(Y)\) follows from this determinant.

Swapping the two rows and reversing both at formal width four preserves the kernel because the seed is reciprocal.
On determinants this operation gives the negative formal reversal at degree eight.
Two independent vectors with the same eigenvalue would therefore have an antireciprocal determinant and a nonzero determinant proportional to the reciprocal \(Q^2\), an impossibility.
The involution splits over \(\mathbb Q\), giving one line of each sign and nonzero determinant constant since \(Q(0)=1\).
Mixing two integral line generators gives a pair independent of its reversal and hence with unequal row moduli.
A common factor disjoint from \(Q\) would cancel to give a nonzero lower-width solution, while a common \(Q\) would leave constant rows and force a nonzero rational linear polynomial to vanish at \(\rho\), since \(Q(X^p)\) is simple there.
This verifies the uncapped existence assertion even when the required coefficient cost exceeds the cap.

The projection onto the top coefficient vector fits the integral exact sequence with kernel \(K_{b-1}(\mathbb Z)\) and image \(\Lambda_b\).
Multiplication by \(Y\) embeds each leading lattice in the next.
The width-four lattice has index \(|d|\), and the coordinate vectors occur at width eight from \(Q^2\) in either component and persist at larger widths by shifting.
At width five the inverse image of \(\Lambda_4\) under top projection is exactly \(K_4(\mathbb Z)+YK_4(\mathbb Z)\), giving the ratio \(I_4/I_5\).
Reversal of a width-five pair has top vector \((v_0,u_0)\), so its determinant with \((u_5,v_5)\) is \(c\), divisible by the index \(I_5\) of their common lattice.
When \(c\ne0\), division of the reflected determinant by \(Q^2\) and the bound on the leading coefficient by the multiplicative measure give \(I_5\le |c|\le H^2/\rho^{2p}\).
C410 guarantees \(c\ne0\) for a genuine capped pair at sufficiently large odd primes on the two classified seeds.
For a given smaller prime the same implication applies to any pair with \(c\ne0\).

Sending a vector of newest coefficients to its combination of the last two columns modulo \(A_{b-1}\) is surjective onto \(A_b/A_{b-1}\).
Its kernel is precisely the set of coefficients that can be completed integrally by earlier columns, which proves the formula for \(I_b\).
At the eventual powers the absence of a rational width-three relation proves rank eight already for \(A_3\).
For the sample powers the computation only requires rank eight at widths four and five.

For each of the two specified \(P\) and twelve \(p\), the reviewer computed \(X^{pi+j}\bmod P^2\) by binary exponentiation, using ordinary integral polynomial convolution and monic reduction.
For each column matrix the gcd of its maximal minors is its index in \(\mathbb Z^8\) when this gcd is positive, by unimodular reduction to Smith form.
All \(45\) minors for \(A_4\) and all \(495\) for \(A_5\) were evaluated by fraction-free integer elimination with pivot swaps.
Every gcd was positive, and dividing the width-four gcd by the width-five gcd in the listed prime order gave
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_A &: 1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 6,\ 3,\ 1,\ 28,\ 3,\ 2,\ 2,\ 9,\ 8,\\
 P_B &: 1,\ 1,\ 3,\ 2,\ 2,\ 1,\ 1,\ 36,\ 3,\ 3,\ 6,\ 1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(P_B\) the two exceptional ratios before cancellation were \(147/49\) at \(p=7\) and \(144/4\) at \(p=23\).

Rational brackets \([17/10,7/4]\) for \(\rho_A\) and \([15/8,19/10]\) for \(\rho_B\) suffice for all comparisons.
The polynomial values at the respective endpoints are \((-1509/10000,53/256)\) and \((-239/4096,1241/10000)\), and each polynomial has a unique exterior real zero greater than one.
With \(l,u\) the corresponding bracket endpoints, exact exponentiation gives \(I_5l^{2p}>4^p\) precisely for \(P_B\) at \(7,23\), and \(I_5u^{2p}<4^p\) for the other 22 cases.
The first two comparisons exclude a nonzero \(c\) in the box.
The other 22 admit a nonzero integer multiple of \(I_5\) below that ceiling, leaving the reported finite exclusions to the other tests in the census.
