## Finding on the full two-term family and its sparse perturbations

At 2026-11-16T15:17 UTC, a separate reviewer for Yin inspected all five mathematical sections of [C345v12](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#statement) and all five sections of [L462v59](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#interior).
The reviewer had no part in developing either argument and received their fixed texts and the review assignment, without proof-development dialogue, author explanations or a requested verdict.
The reviewer checked the cited dependency interfaces and reached the following finding before consulting any earlier review conclusion.
Both arguments pass at their stated scopes.

On the second branch, the signed measure has total mass one and its negative mass at \(1\) tends to zero.
The coefficient at \(d\) uses the Taylor constant \(j_0=1\), whereas \(2\int 1\,d\omega_q=2\).
This distinction produces the odd value of \(2M(0,1)\) in the ledger's mixed moments.
For \(t=o(\min(d,h-d))\), each \(d+rt\) with fixed \(r\) is eventually positive and precedes the reflected terms, while \(0<rt<d\) eventually for every fixed \(r>0\).
The integer transform of \(4y\,d\Omega\) has the same periodic part on both tails, which transfers the evenness of the negative coefficients to the positive tail.
The half-integral value at zero also rules out a purely torsion-atomic first marginal by testing a negative multiple of a common period.

The Herglotz transform \(J\) and its reciprocal are both normalized at zero, have integer coefficients, and have positive real part.
Their integer moment sequences give reduced squarefree cyclotomic numerator and denominator for \(J\).
In its continuous density the bracket \(1+\epsilon x^{\deg V-\deg U}\) must cancel each simple denominator root and bring each simple numerator zero to even order.
Conversely an unpaired simple bracket zero would have odd order in the density.
For unequal degrees this gives \(\deg U+\deg V=|\deg V-\deg U|\), and for equal degrees the nonzero density forces both products to be constant.
The reciprocal binomials have infinitely many odd coefficients.
For a polynomial binomial the density bound on the mixed transform forces an integer Laurent polynomial divisible by \((1+\delta x^\ell)^2\), while its reduction modulo two is \(1+x^\ell\).
This contradiction works also for even \(\ell\), since \(1+x^\ell\) is a nonunit in the Laurent ring over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Thus every first marginal is Haar, and any failure of uniform vanishing through \(c\min(d,h-d)\) would supply nonzero integer coefficients tending to zero.
These steps use the [signed measure](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure), [integer transform decomposition](../literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) and [cyclotomic classification](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v55.md#moving) with their stated normalizations.

The vanishing of all positive difference moments gives Gram matrix \(I+\gamma{\bf1}{\bf1}^{\mathsf T}\) against the positive part.
For five tested nonzero coefficients below \(d\) the limiting lower energy is five and the upper energy is four.
For nine above \(d\) the corresponding bounds are nine and eight, since \(j_d\) is half-odd-integral and \(|j_d|<5/2\) for sufficiently small \(\gamma\).
The reflected lift starts above \(h+1\), so the interval estimates and reciprocity bound the entire support when \(\min(d,h-d)\ge\eta h\).
The [trace construction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) makes \(q-1\) divide \(C_h(2)\), which is positive and at most \(3(h+1)\) in this family.
The resulting bounded-support contradiction uses the actual denominator in both degree parities.

For the ledger's final section, fixed \(k\ge2\) and \(r\) put both prefix indices \(kd+r,(k-1)d+r\) strictly before reflection when \(d,h/d\to\infty\).
The limit of integers stabilizes separately at each such pair.
Conditional expectation over the Haar marginal is an \(L^2\) contraction, so the squared row norm is at most \(5+2\tau M(0,1)\le8\).
On \(k=0\) the formula for \(D\) uses the integral \(M(r,0)\) and the integers \(2M(r,1),2M(-r,1)\).
On \(k=1\) the expression involving \(A(r,2)\) is integral, and on the upper half-plane the recurrence is \(A_k=D_k/4-\tau A_{k+1}/2\).
Three substitutions have error at most \(3/8\), so rounding recovers every integer \(A_k\) there.
Bounded variation at most 18 permits the coset decomposition on discrete \(\mathbb Z^2\).
On each row the sum from subgroups containing a nonzero horizontal vector is periodic, and the other cosets contribute at finitely many points.
The periodic contribution therefore vanishes identically on each tested row.
For the proposed positive-density example, the integral of \(x^r y^k\) vanishes eventually for fixed nonzero \(r\) by \(d\)-th-root invariance, and at \(r=0\) it is the \(k\)-th moment of the indicated \(y\)-density.
This verifies the product limit at the stated moment-only scope.

For the sparse extension in C345v12, \(C(2)\le2\cdot4^h\) forces \(h\to\infty\) as \(q\to\infty\).
An offset bounded below by a positive fraction of \(h\) leaves the entire lift even through a positive fraction strictly below \(d\), contradicting the [first odd index theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#statement).
The [both-sign fixed-offset theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v10.md#statement) handles every bounded subsequence of offsets.
For \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(h)\), the common prefix includes the entire mixed-moment and Gram-matrix calculation just checked on every interval \([0,Ld]\) with fixed \(L\).
The signed measure bounds each positive Taylor coefficient of \(J_q\) by \(2(1+2\gamma)\), giving the stated uniform coefficient bound in that interval.

For \(d=o(t)\) and \(t=o(h)\), the [projected prefix theorem](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix) gives a single [table profile](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#profiles) after extraction because the number of tested multiples tends to infinity.
The poles of its limiting \(J_0\) on the circle come from \(U_0\), whose zeros are sixth roots in all nine rows and remain sixth roots under the sign rotation.
After reflection the \(l\)-th coefficient of the \(u^k\)-weighted projection multiplied by \(1+\tau w/2\) is the limit of the integers \(b_{kt+ld}\).
Both indices needed for this equality are positive and in the common prefix for every fixed \(k\ge1\) and \(l\in\mathbb Z\).
The singleton terms of its integer-transform decomposition invert to an integer Laurent density and the arithmetic progressions invert to atomic measures.
Uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition therefore gives integer continuous rows even with arbitrary conditional moments at the finitely many atoms.
At offset one the [bracket bounds](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#brackets) put seven profiles within the [finite-shape criterion](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes), and the [exceptional envelopes](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#exceptions) give finiteness for the other two.
The integral rows have the required conditional positivity at regular points and \(M(D_*)<2\) in every case.
In the [prime specialization](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) a prime avoiding the finitely many shapes' zeros forces all sufficiently late rows to vanish.
If any row survived, the greatest one would have conditional moment bounded by \(1/2\), giving \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(D_*)/2<1\).
This argument works for any first nonzero harmonic.
The remaining \(k=1\) measure is dominated in variation by a bounded multiple of the marginal and is atomic, hence is supported on sixth roots.
The equality of its coefficients at 0 and 6, together with integrality and extraction from any violating subsequence, proves the claimed eventual equality at distance \(6d\).

Failure of a common window \(Ld\le t\le h/L\) for every fixed large \(L\) would select a sequence contradicting this equality with \(t/d\to\infty\) and \(t/h\to0\).
For \(p=6d\), every coefficient of \((1-z^p)B^\sharp\) between \((L+6)d\) and \(H=\lfloor h/L\rfloor\), exclusive of the lower endpoint, therefore vanishes.
The preceding local bounds give a uniform sum of absolute coefficients of its truncation \(Q\).
For a term shifted by \(a=e+md\), the full congruence indicator costs one unit of measure variation, and its unwanted nonnegative terms below \(a\) cost at most \(1+a/p\) more.
Since \(e/p\le(L+6)/6\), the sum over \(m\) converges uniformly after weighting by \(2^{-m}\).
This constructs a bounded complex measure for the required positive coefficients of \(J_*\) and justifies its use with varying \(d\).

In the cutoff identity all left-hand degrees are \(O(h)\) and the difference \(J_q-J_*\) vanishes through a fixed positive fraction of \(h\).
The [sparse height inequality](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) applies to \(\Pi\) because the [trace roots](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) place all its zeros on the circle and its leading coefficient has modulus two.
The [first cutoff](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) uses only the positive-index bounded measure, and the second recovers the exact polynomial from agreement through three times the degree bound.
The bounds on \(Q\Pi\) and \(|2+\tau z^d|\ge1\) yield the asserted circle \(L^1\) bound for \((1-z^{6d})B^\sharp\).
The [prime-grid argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) bounds its integer support uniformly.
Its Mahler measure is strictly greater than one and tends to one by the [delay-one estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) and the [branch derivative identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second), since both \(1-z^{6d}\) and the possible extra delay-one factor \(z+1\) have measure one.
The [fixed-support gap](../literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) precludes this sequence.
The derivative bound \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\) then gives \(\theta-q\ge2/(q+3)\), and the four-term Chebyshev lift has the required prefix through \(h\) even at \(d=h\).
The conclusion is qualitative for the specified sparse family.
The external theorems enter at their cited statement and application scopes, and this review gives no numerical bound or finding on the unrestricted Lehmer problem.
