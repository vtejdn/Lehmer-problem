The review treats the cited Baker–Wüstholz theorem as an established input and checks its application to the algebraic angles.

Write \(g=\operatorname{ord}_{(R/4R)^\times}(\alpha)\) and \(n=2^a b\) with \(b\) odd.
Since \(\alpha^g\equiv1\bmod4R\), the geometric sum for the \(b\)-th power is one modulo \(2R\) and its multiplication matrix has odd determinant.
At each of the \(a\) squarings the additional factor has the form \(2w\) with \(w\equiv1\bmod2R\), giving norm valuation exactly \(d\).
The surjection \(G_{gn}\to G_n\) therefore bounds the two-primary order of \(G_n\) by \(C_0 2^{da}\).
For a full-conductor character \(\chi_*\), the conductor modulo \(I_n\) of \(2^h\chi_*\) is exactly the kernel of multiplication by \(2^h\) on \(G_n\), since the defining character identity tests against all multipliers in \(R\).
For \(h\ge h_0\) this kernel contains the whole two-primary subgroup, and multiplication is invertible on the odd subgroup.
The order of the odd quotient tends to infinity by the exponential lower bound on \(|G_n|\) and the polynomial upper bound on the omitted subgroup.

The endpoint multiplier \(K\ll_P N^{d+1}\) is fixed for the whole dyadic interval, including every block and progression inside it.
Fix a denominator exponent \(L\) with \(|z_i^n-1|^{-1}\ll_P N^L\) before enlarging the unit-contraction exponent \(A\).
Then the scaled neutral terms are \(O_P(N^{d+L-A})\), which can be made \(O_P(N^{-2})\), and \(C_1\) can subsequently absorb the polynomial size of the scaled real coefficients in both geometric tails.
This bounds the scaled middle square sum by \(O_P(1)\) and its maximum by \(1/4\), simultaneously for all the smaller multipliers.
The two sets of affine endpoint forms retain coefficients independent of the period, with the index \(l=n-j\) fixed at the terminal end.
Multiplication by \(K\) preserves a polynomial lower bound on each maximal slope, permits an upper bound \(O_P(N^{-2})\) under the chosen contraction, and preserves an exponentially small real approximation error.

On the complement of strips of width \(v=M^{-\nu}\) about the coordinate poles, a form with maximal slope \(a'\) varies about its constant by \(O_P(a'/v)=o(1)\).
Only boundedly many integer targets can meet its forbidden region.
Mesh cubes of side at most \(v^4\) meeting that region enlarge its width by \(O_P(a'v^2)\), and the absolute derivative in a maximal-slope direction is at least \(\pi a'\).
Integration in that direction bounds the volume of its enlarged forbidden region by \(O_P(v)\).
Applying the uniform box estimate to the union of cubes for all endpoints gives exceptional proportion \(O_P(Jv+v^{-4s}M^{-\xi})\), including the pole strips.

The middle contributes \(O_P(1)\) for this same \(h\), proving the polynomial amplitude, and the centered endpoint squares contribute at most \(2J/4\) to the energy.
