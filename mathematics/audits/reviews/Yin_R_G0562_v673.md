## Uniform occupancy and simultaneous prescriptions

On November 20, 2026, a separate reviewing agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the [finite bound and simultaneous target theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v539.md#statement).
I received the fixed candidate and the review scope, had no role in developing the proof, and examined its argument and cited mathematical inputs without a development discussion or a prior finding on this candidate.
Both conclusions follow with the stated constants and quantifiers.

Each block discriminant is a symmetric product of squared root differences, so it is an algebraic integer and Galois automorphisms permute the discriminants with the blocks.
Separability therefore makes their product a nonzero rational integer even when the partition has several orbits.
Consequently the sum of their logarithmic absolute values is nonnegative, while the sum of the block log measures equals the total log measure by the monic product formula.
For each block, formula (4.16) in the accepted Pritsker unit applies to its complex monic polynomial with simple roots.
At smoothing radius \(1/n\) the terms involving this radius sum to \((\log n+4)/n\).
The source bounds a nonnegative regularized energy by the displayed radicand, so every block radicand is bounded by their sum.
This step controls a block even when its own discriminant has absolute value less than one.

The cone has gradient of length \(1/r\) almost everywhere inside the disk and zero almost everywhere outside, giving Dirichlet integral \(\pi\) and discrepancy prefactor \(1/\sqrt 2\).
On the arc parametrized by \(-r/2\le t\le r/2\), the chord has length at most \(|t|\), so normalized arclength contributes at least \(r/(4\pi)\) to the test mean.
The boundary has test value zero, as required for open-disk counts.
Combining this mean with the modulus bound \(1/(nr)\) yields (1).
If the disk is empty, taking the positive part before squaring yields the asserted lower bound on \(m\).

Write \(S=2M+u(\log n+4)\).
The scale condition gives \(nr^2/S\to\infty\) and \(S\ge4\), hence both \(1/(nr)\) and \(\sqrt{S/(2n)}\) are \(o(r)\) independently of the center and block.
For \(r=d^{-1/2+\epsilon}\), the ratio \(nr^2/S=d^{2\epsilon}/(uS)\) tends to infinity by the cited polylogarithmic bound on \(u\) and \(n=d/u\).
Thus each disk eventually contains at least \(nr/(8\pi)\) roots in its block.
At most two of those roots share an inverse-pair label, giving at least \(br/(8\pi)\) eligible destinations for each prescription.
If there are \(k\le\lfloor br/(8\pi)\rfloor\) prescriptions, fewer than \(k\) destinations have been used at every greedy choice, including when all centers coincide.
Since \(r\le1\) and \(b=d/(2u)\to\infty\), there are at least two unassigned images after matching, so an extension can have its parity corrected while keeping the assignments.
The simultaneous product of these even permutations belongs to the Galois image on the labels by the fixed alternating-block theorem and hence admits a lift realizing all prescriptions in the splitting field.
For each chosen destination there exists a root \(w\) within the target disk, as the theorem requires of the unordered pair.
The trace estimate follows from \(|w|>1-r\) and \(|w^{-1}-z^{-1}|=|w-z|/|w|<2r\) for \(r\le1/2\).

The review uses Pritsker's restricted discrepancy inequality and the previous alternating-block theorem at their recorded statement scopes.
