# Independent review of the quadratic sparse-divisor bound

## Phase averages, simultaneous annihilators and factor degrees

At 2026-11-17T08:29 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the [complete C345 v186 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#decision-quadratic-sparse-divisor-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed submission and the requested scope without proof-generation dialogue, exploration notes, author explanations or previous findings on this version.
Both bounds and the degree-sum consequence hold with absolute constants at the stated widths.

For each \(1\le r\le T\), the powered unit has degree \(\delta\) and height \(s=r m/d\).
Each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^r)\) extends to exactly \(d/\delta\) embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), so normalizing either phase sum gives the same mean.
The proof of equation (3) in [C345 v108](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) uses distinct conjugates of any unit for its integer discriminant estimate.
It requires neither reciprocity nor a condition on a multiple of the minimal polynomial.
Writing \(\beta_w=\exp(-|\log |w||)\,w/|w|\) and \(q=e^{-1/\delta}\), the first nonnegative term is \(q^2\big|\sum_w\beta_w\big|^2\).
Dividing the energy bound by \(q^2\delta^2\) costs at most the absolute factor \(e^2\) and gives \(O(s+\log(2\delta)/\delta)\) for the squared reflected mean.
Passing to phases costs at most \(2s\) in the unsquared mean, since the powered conjugates have sum of absolute logarithmic moduli \(2\delta s\).

Taking the norm to \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^r)\) gives \(\gamma^r=(\alpha^r)^{d/\delta}\), including \(\gamma=\alpha\) when the fields coincide.
This identity gives nontorsion and \(h(\gamma)=(d/\delta)h(\alpha)\), and the norm preserves units.
For \(b=\deg\gamma\le\delta\) it follows that \(m(\gamma)=b m/\delta\le m\), consistent with [C231, Part D](../../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#statement).
The [adopted quadratic floor, Part A](../../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) then gives
\[
 \tfrac1{10}\le b\sum_\sigma\log^2|\sigma\gamma|
 \le b\left(\sum_\sigma |\log|\sigma\gamma||\right)^2
 =4b\,m(\gamma)^2\le 4\delta m^2.
\]
At least one nonzero polynomial in the family has all \(d\) distinct original conjugates as roots, so \(T\ge d\) even for complex coefficients.
Thus \(\log(2\delta)/\delta\ll\delta^{-1/2}\ll m\le H=Tm/d\) for every permitted \(\delta\).
With \(s\le H\le1\), both terms in the phase bound are \(O(\sqrt H)\).
Complex conjugation gives the same estimate for negative harmonics.

For the radial estimate, every scalar unit-vector projection of \(F\) has supremum at most \(S\) on the circle and grows by at most the factor \((1+1/T)^T\) on the disk of radius \(1+1/T\).
Cauchy's formula on radius \(1/T\), followed by Euclidean duality, gives \(\|F'\|\le eTS\) on the unit disk, independently of the number of components.
For an exterior root \(r_i z_i\), the reversed vector has a zero at \(r_i^{-1}\overline{z_i}\) and norm \(\|F(z_i)\|\) at \(\overline{z_i}\).
In both orientations the radial segment has length \(1-e^{-|\log r_i|}\le|\log r_i|\).
Combining this estimate with \(\|F(z_i)\|\le S\) before squaring gives the average bound \(2eHS^2\), including all circle roots.

Parseval gives \(b_0>0\), and \(S^2\le\sum |b_n|=A b_0\).
The lower bound on the same average takes absolute values only after the coefficients from equal differences and all components have been added.
For \(H\le1\) the resulting inequality implies \(1\le(C+2e)A\sqrt H\), also when \(A=1\).
For \(H>1\), \(A\ge1\) suffices.
A monomial shift preserves the scalar relation at each nonzero conjugate and gives its actual width as the degree.
For one component the triangle inequality on the autocorrelations gives \(A\le\rho\), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\rho\le k\).

Each nontorsion unit factor of the same integer input therefore satisfies its degree bound with the original \(T,\rho\).
A primitive irreducible factor with a nonzero nonunit root has a leading or constant coefficient of absolute value at least two, hence logarithmic measure at least \(\log 2\) by invariance under reversal.
Its degree is at most \(T\) after monomial removal.
Adding the individual bounds with their actual multiplicities gives the claimed sum, since logarithmic measure is additive and integer content has nonnegative logarithmic measure.
Taking the polynomial to be a single primitive irreducible of positive measure gives \(T=d\).

The \(k!\) and degree ratio in the comparison agree with Lemma 1 on p. 203 of [Dobrowolski's published article](../literature/Dob06/readme.md#edition), where \(k\) counts the terms of the multiple.
The earlier [effective-support estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0092_v1.md#angular-short-annihilator-effective-support) has a phase error of order \(H^{1/4}\sqrt{1+\log(1/H)}\) in its small-\(H\) range.
These comparisons use the stated bounds only.
The review checked the project dependencies at the indicated interfaces and did not re-audit the proof of the quadratic floor or Dobrowolski's original proof.
The reducible conclusion still depends on the fraction of the width accounted for by nontorsion factors.
