### From a radical output to an unoriented cut

The circuit has a single primitive relation on each parent.
After orientation, the rational logarithmic kernel on the exterior parent \(E\) consists of constants, and every integral monomial relation modulo torsion is constant on each parent.
Consequently all representations of a given conjugate of \(w=y^N\in\Gamma\), with \(N\) chosen to clear torsion as well as the radical, have the same centered restriction to \(E\).
A nonconstant restriction on some parent exists because \(w\) is nontorsion.
Transporting that parent to \(E\) gives a nonzero projection.
Its logarithm uses only \(E\), and distinct projections give distinct logarithms by injectivity on the zero-sum space.

Complex conjugation inverts each root on a neutral parent, so transport from that parent to \(E\) realizes pure inversion on \(E\).
This makes the set of nonzero projections symmetric even for a nonreciprocal \(y\).
Of its \(2j\) distinct vectors precisely \(j\) have positive logarithm.
Each positive logarithm gives a different conjugate of \(w\), and embeddings extend to \(y\), whose corresponding conjugates are still exterior.
The common sign on each parent then gives an action of the unsigned \(H\) on these \(j\) vector pairs.
Its signed permutation kernel is exactly the kernel on their span, which is intransitive since the span contains a nonconstant fixed vector for that kernel.
A normal intransitive subgroup of a primitive faithful permutation group is trivial, since its orbits would otherwise form a nontrivial block system.

For a nonzero centered vector, let \(J\) stabilize its pair with its negative.
If \(J\) is intransitive, each \(J\)-orbit is a proper nonempty subset whose cut stabilizer contains \(J\).
If \(J\) is transitive, all absolute coordinates agree and the zero sum forces equal numbers of each sign, so the positive coordinates give a balanced cut with stabilizer \(J\).
Conversely centering the indicator of any cut side gives a vector pair with exactly that cut stabilizer, including the possible interchange of equal sides.
Thus minimizing the vector-pair orbit is exactly minimizing the cut orbit, and \(b(y)\ge\kappa(H)\).

### Attainment, degree and measure

For an orbit of \(r\) cuts, pure inversion supplies both signs above each transported side in the exterior parent.
Two same-sign squared products in one parent can coincide only for the same side, by subtracting their exponent vectors.
For opposite signs the sum of the indicators must be constant, which for proper nonempty sides is equivalent to complementarity.
The relation has value \(\omega^2=1\) after squaring, so the coincidence in that case is actual.
Different parents give a nonconstant difference on each support, excluding every cross-parent coincidence.
The squared products therefore have \(2r\) values per parent in a single inverse-closed orbit of algebraic units, giving the reciprocal irreducible polynomial of degree \(2nr\).
The exterior parent gives \(r\) positive real exterior roots and contributes \(2\sum |s_A|\) to logarithmic measure, taking one side per cut.
The input logs sum to zero and have absolute mass \(\mu\), so each \(|s_A|\le\mu/2\).
For \(\omega=1\) complementarity already gives exact inverse unsquared products, yielding the same count and half the logarithmic measure.
Choosing \(x_i\) on \(A\) and \(x_i^{-1}\) on its complement gives the product \(\omega p_A^2\) of a subset of actual roots.
The rational sign preserves the exterior count.

### Small orbits and primitive actions

If \(\kappa(H)\le2\), the action on the signed minimizing vectors has a \(2\)-group quotient with intransitive normal kernel \(K\).
The stabilizer of a \(K\)-orbit is proper by transitivity of \(H\), and contains both \(K\) and a point stabilizer.
Its image in the quotient lies in a maximal subgroup, which has index two by the normalizer argument in the claim.
The resulting equivariant two-point quotient of \(E\) gives a fixed cut, proving \(\kappa(H)=1\).
For such a cut the reciprocal degree is \(2n\), with one positive exterior root and unit-circle conjugates for \(n\ge2\), so the asserted Salem witness and costs follow.
For \(n=1\), powering a reciprocal orbit preserves its inverse closure, and the bipartition construction gives a reciprocal quadratic witness.
The unrestricted optimization uses \(n\ge2\).

In the primitive case the membership strings distinguish the labels, since equal strings give an invariant equivalence relation and some cut separates labels.

For \(e>2\) it too must be discrete, giving \(e\le 2^{r-1}\).
Singleton cuts realize the three-label bound.
On four primitive labels the balanced cuts form one orbit of size three, and the four-label product formula gives the sharper costs claimed.
For the sharpness examples, translation blocks through zero are subspaces invariant under \(S_r\).
When \(r\) is odd, any such subspace containing a nonzero even word contains the difference with a transposition of unequal bits, hence contains all weight-two words and their span.
This proves primitivity, and the \(r\) coordinate cuts are distinct since two chosen coordinates can agree or differ on even words when \(r\ge3\).
Their orbit attains equality for the abstract action.
