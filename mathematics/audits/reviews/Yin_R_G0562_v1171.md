## Unit norms, the ramification estimate and realization over a prescribed trace field

At 2026-11-25T01:36 UTC an agent outside the development of v111 independently reviewed the [full statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v111.md#statement), its three proof sections and the cited inputs for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed text without its development discussion.
The rank identity, quantitative consequences and realization of every stated rank over each specified totally real base hold.

**Ranks of the three norm groups.**
By Dirichlet's theorem the totally real field \(F\) has \(\dim_{\mathbb F_2} U_F/U_F^2=n\), including the torsion sign.
Imposing positivity only at the \(n-1\) nonsplit real places leaves the subspace \(W\) of dimension \(n-s\).
At those places positivity is exactly the local norm condition, and at the split real place there is no condition.
Unramified finite local norm maps are onto on units, so only the \(r_f\) ramified finite primes are needed on \(W\), including any ramified dyadic primes.
Hilbert reciprocity gives one even-sum constraint if there are any such primes.
The cyclic Hasse norm theorem identifies the kernel with \((U_F\cap N K^\times)/U_F^2\), yielding \(v\le\max(0,r_f-1)\) with the zero-prime case included. [Milne, Remark III.4.8](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=124) · [Milne, Theorem VIII.3.1](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=243)

For an ordinary ambiguous ideal class the norm of any generator of \(\sigma I/I\) is a unit of \(F\).
Changing the ideal by a principal ideal leaves the image unchanged modulo unit norms.
For the converse map, if \(Nx\) is a unit the valuations of \(x\) at conjugate primes exchanged by \(\sigma\) are opposite, and at a fixed prime its valuation is zero since the residue degree there is positive.
Thus \((x)=\sigma I/I\) for a fractional ideal \(I\), establishing surjectivity onto the quotient in question.
If \(Nx\) is a unit norm, division by that unit preimage makes \(x\) an element of norm one without changing \((x)\).
Hilbert 90 then supplies an invariant representative for the original class, and the converse kernel inclusion follows by taking \(I\) invariant.
This checks the exact quotient with ordinary fractional ideals and with the full unit norm in the denominator. [Lemmermeyer, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.1071#page=2)

The norm image of \(U_K\) contains \(U_F^2\), and its quotient by the squares has exponent two.
The isomorphism with \(U_K/(U_F U^1)\) follows by taking out the norm kernel and the image of \(U_F\).
For \(u\in U_K\), the class of \(u/\sigma u\) in \(U^1/(U^1)^2\) is well-defined on this quotient.
If the quotient is \(e^2\) with \(e\in U^1\), then \(\sigma(u/e)=u/e\), so \(u/e\in U_F\).
Dirichlet ranks are \(n\) for \(U_K\) and \(n-1\) for \(U_F\), and \(K\) has only the torsion \(\{\pm1\}\) since it has a real embedding.
Hence \(U^1\) has rank one and its quotient by squares has order four.
It follows that \(q\le2\).
The two successive quotients from the kernel on \(W\) down to \(U_F^2\) have dimensions \(a\) and \(q\), giving the exact equality \(n=s+v+a+q\).

**From finite characters to measure.**
Every ramified finite prime divides the quadratic field discriminant ideal, which divides the order discriminant \((t^2-4)\), including at dyadic primes.
Also \(N_{K/F}(\alpha^2-1)=2-\alpha^2-\alpha^{-2}=-(t^2-4)\).
In the consecutive-power estimate for \(z=\alpha^2\), \(h(z)=m/n\), and the containing-field degree is \(2n\).
Its \(L_1\) term is the full absolute cyclic norm divided by \(2n\).
Multiplication yields exactly \(2n\log T/(T-1)+2(T+1)m/3\).
For \(v>0\) at least \(v+1\) distinct primes ramify.
Ordering all finite prime ideals of \(F\) by norm gives the lower bounds in (1) even with ties.
On a shrinking sequence the choice of \(T\) gives \(O(\sqrt{nm\log(2n/m)})\), whose ratio to \(\sqrt{n\log(2n)}\) tends to zero since \(m\log(1/m)\to0\).
When \(v=0\), \(n-s-a=q\le2\) already.
This gives (2) along the full sequence and excludes such a sequence under the square-root-scale deficit hypothesis, since bounded-degree finiteness forces the degrees to tend to infinity.
For a linear deficit one can take fixed \(T\) with \(2\log T/(T-1)<\delta\log2/2\) and absorb the bounded \(q\) error for large \(n\).
Finally \(s=o(n)\) along a shrinking sequence implies \(a=n-o(n)\) and hence the asserted order of the class quotient.

**All norm ranks over the chosen base.**
Unit squareclasses of \(W\) inject into \(F^\times/(F^\times)^2\), since a square root in \(F\) of a unit has zero valuation at every finite place.
The elementary abelian Kummer extension \(F(\sqrt W)/F\) is therefore dual to this entire space.
For any \(j\) between zero and \(n-s\), Chebotarev realizes a prescribed linearly independent list of \(j\) functionals by distinct unramified odd primes avoiding the prescribed places.
The corresponding Frobenius signs are exactly the residue quadratic characters on these units.
For \(j=0\) this choice imposes no prime.

Include every dyadic prime and every prescribed finite place in the finite ray modulus, with exponent greater than \(2v_{\mathfrak p}(2)\).
Weak approximation supplies \(x_0\equiv1\) there with the required signs at all real places.
This involves no restriction on its valuations outside the modulus.
In the narrow ray class of \((x_0)J^{-1}\), where \(J\) is the product of the selected \(j\) primes, there are infinitely many primes \(\mathfrak q\) away from both \(J\) and the modulus by ray class reciprocity and Chebotarev.
The ideal equality \(J\mathfrak q=(x_0 b)\) then holds with \(b\) totally positive and congruent to one at the modulus.
Thus \(D=x_0b\) has precisely the requested real signs, is integral with its only nonzero finite valuations equal to one at the \(j+1\) indicated primes, and is locally square at every modulus prime by Hensel's lemma. [Milne, ray class existence and Artin isomorphism](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=167)

The quadratic extension obtained by adjoining \(\sqrt D\) ramifies at precisely those \(j+1\) finite primes.
At each ramified odd prime the norm of a unit has square residue and every local unit with square residue is itself a square, hence a norm.
Conversely a norm which is a unit comes from a local unit by the valuation formula.
Thus the first \(j\) characters on \(W\) are independent, and the last one equals their sum by reciprocity.
This gives rank \(j\), also when \(j=0\) and the single ramified prime has trivial character on \(W\).
For \(j=n-s\) the rank identity forces both \(a=q=0\).
Varying \(\mathfrak q\) changes the ramified prime set and gives distinct extensions of the given \(F\).

The norm-one group of any field so constructed has rank one by its real signature.
A nontorsion unit cannot take value \(1\) or \(-1\) in a real embedding, so squaring and inverting can put it above one at the chosen split place.
The other split value is its reciprocal and the complex pairs above the remaining real places lie on the unit circle.
This produces exactly one exterior value counted among all embeddings of \(K\).
If the unit lay in a proper subfield its exterior embedding would extend at least twice, a contradiction.
It therefore generates \(K\), and the quadratic equation over its trace field implies that its trace generates the original \(F\).
As \(n\ge2\) there is a neutral conjugate, so these are genuine Salem realizations.
The construction prescribes signatures and ramification and supplies no bound tending to zero on their measures.
