# Integral Fourier moments of branch measures at a fixed Salem input

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent review of the half-mass obstruction and the atomic correction

At 2026-11-18T15:10 UTC an agent outside proof development independently reviewed the [full L184 argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#fixed) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed input and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or earlier findings on this argument and had independently checked the two grid constructions used for attainment.
The structural decomposition, the parity-dependent lower bound, the single-atom classification and the finite test inequalities hold under their stated hypotheses.

In the branch rational function the leading coefficient of \((x-2)R\) is \(1-q\), so the polynomial part cancels exactly.
Formal division by monic \(Q\) gives an integral series in \(1/x\), and simplicity of all the traces identifies its coefficient at \(x^{-j-1}\) with \(c_T T^j+\int x^j\,d\nu_d\).
Multiplication of the test by \(Q_0\) annihilates the exterior contribution, irrespective of the sign of \(c_T\).
This gives the integral tests for every finite return and, by continuity on the compact interval, for each weak limit with the same fixed \(Q_0\).
In the monic integral basis \(1,C_1,C_2,\ldots\), the squared norms for \(\rho\) are \(1,2,2,\ldots\), and every nonconstant basis element has even value at zero.
Thus \(\int Q_0W\,d\rho\equiv Q_0(0)W(0)\pmod2\), giving both directions of the moment criterion for \(\rho/2\).

For the symmetric lift \(\mu\) of \(2Q_0\nu\), averaging a circle character over the two preimages gives \(\cos(jt)\), also at the endpoints with their single preimage.
Using \(\widehat\mu(j)=\int z^j\,d\mu(z)\) gives the integral coefficients \(\int Q_0 C_{|j|}\,d\nu\) for \(j\ne0\) and \(2\int Q_0\,d\nu\) for \(j=0\).
This is an integer-valued member of \(B(\mathbb Z)\) because \(\mu\) has finite total variation.
The reviewer checked the locally compact abelian statement of Corollary 12.4 and its representation (12.2) in the [published article by Sanders](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00041-020-09732-y).
The coefficient lists are integral and absolutely summable, hence finitely supported, and every subgroup of the discrete group \(\mathbb Z\) is open.
Only the qualitative finiteness conclusion is used.

For the subgroup \(\{0\}\), the singleton indicator at \(r\) is the transform of \(z^{-r}\,dm(z)\) with \(m\) circle Haar probability.
For \(m_0\ge1\), the indicator of \(r+m_0\mathbb Z\) is the transform of the measure assigning \(\zeta^{-r}/m_0\) to each \(\zeta^{m_0}=1\), by the finite character sum.
These individual atomic contributions can have complex weights.
Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients identifies their sum together with the Laurent density as \(\mu\).
Its absolutely continuous component inherits reality and inversion symmetry from the full measure.
Its Laurent polynomial has integral coefficients, and symmetry pairs the coefficients at \(j,-j\), so its density is \(D(z+z^{-1})\) for \(D\in\mathbb Z[x]\).
The pushforward of Haar probability is \(\rho\), yielding \(2Q_0\nu=D\rho\) plus finitely many atoms at torsion traces.

Off the zeros of \(Q_0\), division gives the density \(D/(2Q_0)\) and at most those finitely many atoms.
The only additional possible mass lies on the finitely many neutral conjugates of the Salem trace.
At any such conjugate \(Q_0\) has a simple sign change inside the interval.
If \(D\) were nonzero there, the density would be negative on a neighboring open subinterval, contrary to positivity.
Thus \(Q_0\mid D\) in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) by rational irreducibility and monicity.
It follows that \(H_1=D/Q_0\) is nonnegative throughout \([-2,2]\) by continuity and full support of \(\rho\), and the singular part of the positive measure \(\nu\) has nonnegative atom weights.
For fixed \(\sigma\) and \(q=d^2\), one has \(\log q=o(d)\), so the angular estimate gives \(\rho\ll\nu\) for each bounded-mass limit.
It rules out the purely atomic case \(H_1=0\).

The \(\rho\)-mean of this nonzero nonnegative integral polynomial is its positive integer constant coefficient in the Chebyshev basis.
If this integer is one, each nonzero-frequency Fourier coefficient has modulus strictly below one, since the corresponding character has nonconstant phase on an open arc of positive density.
Those coefficients are integers, forcing \(H_1=1\).
Every limit of mass below one therefore has absolutely continuous part \(\rho/2\).
If \(A\) is odd, the constant test requires the remaining atomic measure \(\eta\) to have \(\int Q_0\,d\eta\) a half-integer of modulus at least \(1/2\).
Its mass is at least \(1/(2\max|Q_0|)\).
The target lower bound is at most one since \(\max|Q_0|\ge|A|\ge1\), so the larger-mass cases are covered as well.
Compactness handles finite liminf subsequences and gives the unique possible limit \(\rho/2\) at mass \(1/2\).
For \(Q_0=x^2-3x+1\) the maximum absolute value is \(11\) at \(-2\), giving \(1/2+1/22=6/11\).
The second real embedding sends the trace into \((-2,2)\), so the negative discriminant there supplies the claimed degree-four Salem input.

For a single correcting atom at \(s\), the constant test gives odd \(N=2\lambda Q_0(s)\), and the tests at \(x^j\) give \(Ns^j\in2\mathbb Z\) for all \(j\ge1\).
The first power gives rational \(s\), and any prime in its reduced denominator would appear to arbitrarily high order in \(s^j\), which fixed nonzero \(N\) cannot clear.
The remaining integer must be even by the first power test, giving exactly \(-2,0,2\) as candidate locations.
At any of them \(W(s)\equiv W(0)\pmod2\), so odd \(N\) cancels the parity defect for every \(W\).
The minimum central mass follows by taking \(|N|=1\) with the sign of \(A\) and is realized by the odd grid.
The endpoint statement proves moment compatibility only.

For odd \(A,W(0)\), a finite return and the half-arcsine measure differ on \(Q_0W\) by at least \(1/2\), and the same assertion holds for fixed-input weak limits.
Dividing the test by its uniform norm gives the stated total variation bound with the absolute-measure convention.
Dividing by the sum of that norm and the derivative supremum gives an admissible bounded-Lipschitz test.
On a compact interval these tests form a precompact family in the uniform norm, so weak convergence with bounded mass would force the bounded-Lipschitz distance to zero even when the actual tests vary.
This requires control of the relevant test norms to obtain a uniform obstruction.
Neither the norm estimates nor the fixed-polynomial decomposition prove a uniform positive gap under a bound on the odd central integers alone.
The half-measure constructions apply in their respective congruence classes, leaving the stated even-constant, odd-derivative case open.
