## Split prime orbits and the Salem involution

At 2026-11-27T12:33 UTC an agent outside proof development independently checked both sections of [the splitting claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v204.md#splitting) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its scope and consulted the cited mathematical inputs without development discussion, author explanations or a previous verdict on this claim.
Both the subgroup bound and its Salem specialization hold under the stated adjacent-record hypotheses.

For the integer target \(2\), the second closest exponent of the [adjacent-record norm theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#rows) gives \(A^aB<2\) and \(\log |P_{A^aB}(2)|/D\to\log 2\).
Both factors are strictly dominant positive units, so an embedding fixing their product must preserve each of their maximal moduli and hence both factors.
This identifies \(\mathbb Q(A^aB)=F\), makes \(P_{A^aB}(2)=N_{F/\mathbb Q}z\), and verifies that the same nonzero integral \(z\) and field degree occur in both sections.

For a nonidentity \(\tau\in G\), \(\tau B\ne B\) and \(|\tau B|<B\) in the distinguished embedding, so \(\gamma=\tau B/B\) is a unit of infinite order.
The absence of finite height contributions and the archimedean bound \(B\) give \(h(B)\le b\), whence \(h(\gamma)\le 2b\).
Since \(z-\tau z=A^aB(\gamma-1)\), at each finite \(Q\) the smaller of the two translate valuations is bounded by \(v_Q(\gamma-1)\).
For this unit the latter valuations sum, with weights \(\log NQ/D\), to the \(t=1\) quantity in the [cyclic norm bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement).
The estimate there is uniform because \(x\log(1/x)\) is increasing for sufficiently small positive \(x\).

In \(F/F^G\) the primes above a fixed prime form one orbit, and its stabilizer has order \(ef\) for the relative ramification index and residue degree.
At a prime \(Q\) with nonidentity stabilizer, a nonidentity element fixing \(Q\) gives \(v_Q(\tau z)=v_Q(z)\).
On a free orbit, choose a maximal-valuation prime \(P\) and charge each discarded \(Q\) to the distinct-from-identity element with \(\tau P=Q\), since \(v_Q(\tau z)=v_P(z)\ge v_Q(z)\).
For a fixed \(\tau\) all these charges come from distinct primes and fit inside its single ideal-gcd sum.
Thus the loss is at most \(C(|G|-1)\sqrt{b\log(1/b)}\), and each retained prime lies over one with \(e=f=1\).
The trivial subgroup has zero loss.

The [relative orbit bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits) supplies a lower bound for \([K(B):K]\) growing with the second source degree even after adjoining the preceding splitting field.
Once this degree exceeds two, the Salem polynomial's only two real roots cannot exhaust the relative images.
Extend a \(K\)-embedding sending \(B\) to a nonreal image \(B'\) to \(s\) in a normal closure.
Since \(s\) and complex conjugation \(c\) both fix \(K\), \(s^{-1}cs\) fixes \(K\) and takes \(B\) to \(B^{-1}\) by \(\overline{B'}=1/B'\).
Its restriction to \(F\) is nonidentity and fixes \(B+B^{-1}\), while \(B\) satisfies \(X^2-(B+B^{-1})X+1=0\).
This proves the exact degree two without requiring the original Salem involution to extend across an arbitrary compositum.

To intersect with [the deepest embeddings at each rational prime](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v202.md#depth), lift each retained \(Q\mid p\) to its whole set of \(p\)-adic embeddings.
Its \(e_Q f_Q\) embeddings, using absolute local indices here, each have depth \(v_Q(z)/e_Q\), so their total weighted mass is precisely \(w_Q\).
Intersecting the two selections therefore loses at most \(C\sqrt{b\log(1/b)}+\sqrt u/4\) in the quadratic case, where \(u\) is the depth theorem's parameter.
Inert and ramified primes can carry some of the discarded mass, and the argument permits several split pairs above a single rational prime.
