# Saturation of a unit modulo arbitrary torsion at bounded coefficient length

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T05:14 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin completed an independent review of [C345 v767](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v767.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of the complete claim for the stated algebraic units.
The reviewer took no part in developing its proof.

Choosing a relation with least absolute coefficient sum rules out a vanishing proper subsum, since that subsum would be a shorter integer relation.
The consecutive-gap bound gives \(T h(\beta)\le (L-1)\log L\) after the monomial shift.
For \(p^a\Vert r\), removing the part of the phase whose order is prime to \(p\) uses an automorphism of that component's cyclic group.
Each intermediate power \(z_i\) generates \(F\) over the \(p\)-primary cyclotomic field \(E\), because it recovers \(\beta\) after powering and dividing by \(\zeta\).
The shifted element \(x_i=z_i/\zeta\) has height exactly \(h(\beta)/p^i\).
The [sparse width inequality](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) at the last consecutive zero bounds \(p^j\) by \(A\).
This covers all exponents \(a\le j+c\), including the case where every evaluation vanishes.

In the remaining case \(a-j>c\), so the [ramified coefficient congruence](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v766.md#congruence) applies at the required depth.
The consecutive matrix powers have characteristic polynomials equal to the respective minimal polynomials by the equality of relative fields.
Monic division works over the cyclotomic integer ring with translated coefficients, and evaluation at \(z_{j+1}\) gives a nonzero multiple of \(p^{a-j-c}\).
Taking the absolute norm to \(\mathbb Q\) contributes the original degree \(d\) to the divisibility exponent, and the upper bound is \(L^d\exp(d T h(x_{j+1}))\).
After multiplication by \(p^j\) this proves (4).
When \(j=0\) the sharper inequality excludes \(p>\max(2L,2H)\), whereas \(j\ge1\) already gives \(p\le A\).
The rational-prime and dyadic components are both covered.
Summing the logarithms of the individual maximal prime powers up to \(Q\), using the recorded elementary prime count, gives the asserted logarithmic size for \(H_L\).

In the free abelian unit group modulo torsion the admissible exponents are exactly the divisors of the gcd of the basis coordinates of \(\beta\).
The maximum has an actual in-field witness with phase of order \(w\), and \(\theta\) and this phase generate \(F\).
The relative height argument therefore uses degree \(d/\varphi(w)\) over \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\).
The exceptional odd primes lie among the prime divisors of \(w\), and \(w\le2\varphi(w)^2\le2d^2\) bounds their count.
The cubic inequality gives \(k\varphi(w)\ll m/G(d)\) as in the [reviewed relative estimate](Yin_R_G0562_v906.md#finding).
The bounded-length parent gives \(m\le\log L\), whence \(w\ll_L\log^6(2d)\), and the totient inequality gives \(w/\varphi(w)\ll_L J(d)\).
Since \(k\le H_Lw\), these estimates prove (2).

In an external equation \(\eta=\alpha^g/\beta\) belongs to \(F(\alpha)\) even when it does not belong to either original field separately.
The relative norm of \(\eta\) is torsion, and the norm of \(\alpha\) is a unit.
Taking gcds of the free coordinates in the norm identity gives \(g\mid tk\).
An embedding of the compositum fixing \(\alpha\) is determined by the image of \(\beta\), which has torsion quotient with \(\beta\).
Thus \([F(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\le\kappa\).
Combining this inequality with \(h(\alpha)=h(\beta)/g\) gives (3).
For nontorsion unit solutions on the primitive graph sections, the earlier [comparison of conjugate components](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#twists) uniformly bounds \(\kappa\), and the norm of the graph equation supplies the bounded-length relation.

In the example the real unit is \(1+\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}\).
The field generated by \(\beta\) is a normal subfield of the abelian fifth cyclotomic field, and \(\overline\beta/\beta=\zeta_5^2\) generates that entire field.
Each ordinary \(5^a\)-th root of \(\beta\) is the real unit times a primitive \(5^{a+1}\)-th root of unity, so every such branch has relative degree \(5^a\).
This establishes the stated difference from the [ordinary radical denominator](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement), with the coefficient length varying in the example.
The denominator for arbitrary phases in (1) and the quantitative saturation-index bound concern a different question from the already bounded ordinary denominator.
