The single denominator two gives all the claimed canonical square endpoints and the Salem subclass with no bound on coefficients, degrees or relative discriminants.
The resulting gap equivalence is with the bounded-second-iterate problem.

For a fixed relatively reciprocal \(f\) with constant \(\epsilon\), the leading and constant equations for \(XP+\epsilon(P^\tau)^*=qf\) require the rational leading coefficient \(q\) and match the constant automatically.
The remaining two equations at reflected distinct indices are images of one another; one coefficient may be chosen as \(qf_i\) and its partner zero.
At a middle index the coefficient must lie in the image of \(1+\epsilon\tau\), with target \(qf_{b/2}\).
Its unscaled class lies in the indicated eigenspace quotient and is killed by two.
Thus an obstruction excludes exactly the odd multipliers and none of the even ones.
As a direct check at two, the integral polynomial
\[
            P_2=X^{b-1}+(f-\epsilon)/X
\]
has leading coefficient two and satisfies \(XP_2+\epsilon(P_2^\tau)^*=2f\) by relative reciprocity.
It is an algebraic particular solution before imposing root conditions.
Conversely any integral solution at odd \(q\) minus \(((q-1)/2)P_2\) gives an integral particular solution at one.

After dividing an available particular solution by its fixed \(q\), the other monic solutions subject to the same denominator bound are the translate by \(q^{-1}\) times the full integral homogeneous lattice.
Its Minkowski image still has a bounded fundamental domain, with constants allowed to depend on the fixed \(q,f\).

The critical-point seed target at complex embeddings and the strict split real direction apply to \(f\) itself with the original normalization.
Thus the approximation yields the strict disk and boundary conditions, a nonzero constant, and seed exterior roots tending to infinity for every fixed allowable multiplier.
Multiplication back by positive rational \(q\) changes no roots and scales both boundary absolute values equally.

For the general input at two the congruence makes \(F\) integral, and the division makes its leading coefficient one and its constant precisely the chosen sign.
The marked-place root argument of v128 uses only the analytic conditions and therefore applies to \(P/2\), without requiring this monic seed to be integral.
The exterior root is simple, positive and real, and all other marked roots are strictly interior.
Their product of moduli is \(1/R\) by the unit constant term.
The opposite split place reverses the root multiset, while the nonsplit places contribute only unit-circle roots.
Taking the rational norm of \(F\) therefore yields a monic integer polynomial of measure \(R^2\), even when factors repeat.
Taking the norm of the numerator before division would introduce the additional scalar \(2^{[K:\mathbb Q]}\).
The monic minimum of \(R\) divides the normalized norm over the integers, so \(M(R)\le R^2\).
Squaring its conjugates can only reduce their number by identifications, and each positive exterior log doubles before such identifications; this gives \(M(R^2)\le M(R)^2\le R^4\).

A shrinking sequence of these norm measures thus gives a shrinking sequence of integer-polynomial measures with bounded second iterates.
