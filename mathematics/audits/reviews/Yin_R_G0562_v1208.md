## The maximal leading space, the nested case and angular distribution in every fiber

At 2026-11-25T04:09 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [fixed statement of C0901 v148](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v148.md#statement) and all four argument sections.
The reviewer had previously reviewed some supporting materials in this family and received the fixed v148 without development discussion.
The real-mass estimate and its two strict-side consequences hold for the stated arbitrary integer-polynomial inputs.

For small \(m>0\) the positive-measure factors are monic units up to the harmless rational sign, with individual log measures summing to \(m\).
The product \(A\) of their measures is a positive Perron unit, so the orbit of its actual values and the orbit of its classes have the same size \(D\).
The sum of all positive logs equals the absolute sum of all negative logs, giving \(\mathbb E|L|=2\mu/D\).
The joint decomposition identifies repeated constituents as actual subspaces rather than counting them twice.
One can choose any maximal degree \(d\) among the distinct active spaces, and all compressed representatives therein have unbounded degrees uniformly as \(m\to0\), by bounded-degree finiteness.

Over the other active splitting fields, the relative theorem for the largest term applies to every other unmatched representative, including ties.
Their common stabilizer is normal and fixes the complementary component at every translate.
Its supported alternating permutations on an oriented sum-zero block or its even flips on a signed block leave no invariant vectors on \(W\).
Conditional averaging therefore kills \(L_1\) while preserving \(L'\), so Jensen and the triangle inequality give both bounds in (2).
The actual reality of \(gA\) implies \(cgv=gv\).
On each coset a signed coordinate can be fixed only at a real label: its projected orbit separates the two signs and all labels, including on an oriented sum-zero space.
There are \(o(b)\) real labels even in the whole term.
For two exceptions the single-coordinate conditioning allows at most \(a+1\) possible second positions among at least \(b-1\), where \(a=o(b)\) is the number of real labels.
For three or more, the sparse and dense estimates of v144 require only the supported permutations, and in the signed case the supported even flips also available to this relative stabilizer.
Those estimates are uniform after a choice of block destinations.
This makes the conditional probability \(o(1)\) and proves that the contribution weighted by \(|L'|\) is \(o(\mathbb E|L|)\).

For the unconditional \(|L_1|\) contribution when \(v\) is noncoordinate, the proof on the leading \(W\) uses its actual signed or sum-zero coordinates, the uniform real-label count, and the bound on the maximum of an orbit functional by its dimension times the mean absolute value.
For exactly two exceptions the absolute first-moment comparison uses zero conditional mean on unrelated signed coordinates or different oriented blocks, and the factor \(-1/(b-1)\) in a common oriented block.
These properties apply on \(W\) even if the chain has a lower summand, since the projection already subtracts each block mean.
The result follows for an arbitrary log functional on \(W\), including when its restriction vanishes.
Alternatively, if a second term's label field is not contained in \(L_z\), the image of the subgroup fixing \(L_z\) on that label group is nontrivial and normal.
It then contains the unique minimal normal product of alternating groups.
The same supported lift argument, now at this second term, gives the conditional \(o(1)\) probability while \(|L_1|\) is fixed.
This handles the nonnested case without any unproved independence of the fields.

When all \(P_j\subseteq L_z\), the abelian normal sign subgroup over \(P_z\) maps to an abelian normal subgroup in each label group and so maps trivially.
Thus \(P_j\subseteq P_z\).
Equality would contradict survival of the selected alternating group after adjoining \(L_j\); in a proper inclusion the nontrivial restriction kernel contains \((A_b)^u\).
It follows that the compositum of all such \(P_j\) lies in the *same* fixed field of that product.
Its ambient group embeds in \((\mathbb Z/2)^u\rtimes S_u\) and has degree at most \(2^u u!\).
Each \(A_{b_j}\) is a composition factor of the quotient acting on \(P_j\).
The abelian kernel and the subgroup of \(S_u\) bound its order by \(u!\), forcing \(b_j\le u\) at the growing block sizes.
Together with the independent polylogarithmic bound on \(u_j\) at \(d_j\le d\), this bounds \(d_j\) by a fixed power of \(\log(3d)\).

An input factor assigned any such space has log measure at least the measure of its chain term and thus at least \(2/(\log(3d))^3\), by Voutier at the degree of that term.
The total budget \(m\) bounds the number of assigned factors; after the first chain degree at most \(d\), each subsequent degree drops by at least a factor of two.
This gives a polylogarithmic bound on the total number of remaining spaces without assuming such a bound on all original factors.
Adjoining both roots of each inverse pair needs at most \(d_j/2\) square roots over its label field.
The action on all these splitting fields together with the image of the single projected coordinate (at most \(d\) choices) determines the actual class of \(A\).
Taking logarithms gives (3), including with an empty list of other fields.

The \(d\) projected signed coordinates biject with the \(d\) actual conjugates of the selected compressed root.
Since \(A\) is real, this gives a real choice of root, and every automorphism fixing \(A\) fixes the selected actual root.
The saturated unit \(r>1\) on this real line generates the same embedded field \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\subseteq\mathbb Q(A)\); its positive powers have full degree.
For an input measure \(A_h\) projecting onto \(W\), an earlier active term of degree \(d'\) would have \(d'\le\deg A_h\le D\) by its nonzero leading orbit and the Perron field inclusion.
The next chain degree after it is at most a constant times \((\log(3d'))^{212}\), and the later degree \(d\) is no larger.
By (3) this gives \(d\ll(\log(3d))^{212 C_0}\), impossible for large \(d\).
Hence the earlier projections vanish, and the lossless descent expresses \(A_h\) as a positive integral power of the measure of the matched term.
This term is a signed conjugate of an integral power of \(r\) by saturation in the common embedded root field, so its measure is a positive integral power of \(M(r)\).
Summation with multiplicity proves \(e\ge1\) integral and \(e\log M(r)\le m\).

After orienting the actual real root by sign and inversion, its exterior coefficient is one.
For the signed case a projection along this coordinate then has coefficient one.
For the oriented case the other coefficients in its block are all equal after subtracting the selected multiple, giving the nonzero difference \(1-0\) or \(1-(-1)\).
Thus the projected scalar is exactly \(je\) with \(j=1,2\) as applicable.
This makes \(C=A/r^{je}\) an actual algebraic unit in \(\mathbb Q(A)\) with zero projection to \(W\).
In an oriented block the subtraction of its mean costs at most another \(\mathbb E|\lambda(gr)|=2\log M(r)/d\) by signed-coordinate transitivity.
With \(je\le2e\), the resulting bound on \(\mathbb E|L_1|\) is \(8m/d\).
On a hypothetical sequence carrying a fixed positive fraction of real absolute mass this and the negligible real \(|L'|\) contribution imply \(\mu=O(mq)\), \(q=D/d\).
On \(M(A)>A^2\) a match with the selected term is impossible by the interval at a match.
The quantitative unmatched bound gives \(\mu\gg d/(m(\log(3d))^{212})\), so \(q\gg d/(m^2(\log(3d))^{212})\ge d^{1-o(1)}\) and \(h(C)\le\mu/D+jeh(r)=O(m/d)\).

For an oriented block let \(T\) be the field of its selected half and in the independent model take the field of the block itself.
Their stabilizers show \(T\subset K\), with \([K:T]=b\) or \(2b\) respectively.
After subtracting \(je[r]\) the remaining components lie in other active terms or in lower terms of the selected chain; each is unmatched to \(z\) and has degree at most \(d\).
Thus a positive power of \(C\), up to torsion, is generated by roots in the base of v87.
Its radical and the necessary roots of unity belong to the enlarged normal base, as does \(T\) since its degree is at most \(2u<b\).
Over that base supported alternating label actions give all \(b\) distinct positions in the selected block, preserving the chosen half when its field is fixed.
In the independent model normality and the full sign group give supported double flips in the fixing subgroup, so both actual signs of each label occur.
Consequently the orbit of \(r\) even there has at least \([K:T]\) points, and its degree over the smaller \(T(C)\) is exactly \([K:T]\).
Since \(K(C)=\mathbb Q(A)\), the tower law gives \([T(C):T]=[K(C):K]=q\).
The minimum of \(C\) over \(T\) remains irreducible over \(K\), so every fixed embedding of \(K\) sees all \(q\) distinct values in the corresponding \(T\)-fiber.

The minimum of \(C\) over \(T\) is monic integral and separable, so the absolute product of its discriminants over all \(t_0=[T:\mathbb Q]\) embeddings is at least one.
The total of its conjugate log measures is \(t_0 q h(C)\), counting embeddings of \(T(C)\); this identity also holds if \(C\) generates a proper subfield thereof.
Summing the reflected energies of v628 before dropping the discriminant gives the stated \(O(q(t_0 qh(C)+t_0\log(3q)))\) with \(\rho=e^{-1/q}\).
For fixed frequency \(h\), the energy is nonnegative in each fiber, giving the square-root bound on its normalized reflected sum.
The cost of replacing those powers by unit phases is at most \(h/q\) times the sum of absolute logs in the fiber, bounded using the all-fiber total \(2t_0 q h(C)\) since \(C\) is a unit.
Both errors vanish uniformly: \(t_0\) is polylogarithmic in \(d\), \(h(C)=O(m/d)\), and \(q\ge d^{1-o(1)}\).
Two nonnegative Fejér kernels centered at \(0,\pi\) consequently bound the real fraction by \(2/H+o_H(1)\) for each fixed order \(H\); letting \(H\) increase after the sequence limit is legitimate uniformly in the fibers.
In particular the conditional real fraction for \(A\) at each conjugate of \(r\) is \(o(1)\), because real \(A\) implies real \(C\).
The weight \(L_1\) depends only on that conjugate, contradicting the assumed real mass via (2).

Finally the positive and absolute negative total log masses separately equal \(\mu\).
Removing a real absolute mass \(o(\mu)\) leaves the stated fractions on both nonreal strict sides.
Using an intermediate exponent in the known large-iterate bound absorbs this vanishing loss.
Either bounded side along a shrinking sequence forces the low interval eventually; on that interval both sides are bounded by \(2m\to0\).
The asserted gap equivalence follows from the earlier bounded-second-iterate reduction, leaving the existence of a shrinking sequence open.
