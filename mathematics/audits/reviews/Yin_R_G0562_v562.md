## Exact cost for powers of a Salem number or a quadratic reciprocal unit

At 2026-11-19T02:05 UTC I independently reviewed Sheng Yin's [fixed statement and proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#statement) as a reviewer outside its development.
The lower bound holds for arbitrary row widths at the given distinct exterior radii, and an additional simple zero at a distinct exterior unit costs a positive excess depending on the fixed nodes.
For the stated Salem and quadratic inputs, the normalized logarithm of the least norm of a signed row with every numerator nonzero tends to \(\sum_i a_i\log\delta\) as \(n\) runs through all positive integers.

### The two contact equations and the extra radius

After reversing and removing a common monomial, the original graph equals \(U(X^{-n})-XV(X^{-n})\) times a monomial which is invertible at every selected node.
Differentiating this expression at \(c\), multiplying by \(c\), and putting \(t=c^{-n}\) gives \(n\sum_j j(u_j-cv_j)t^j=-c\sum_j v_jt^j\).
This uses no quotient of row values.
For \(j>J\), the row one-norm bounds the derivative tail by a constant times \(nH(J+1)|t|^{J+1}\) once \(j|t|^j\) decreases there.
Hence even exponentially many available coefficient positions introduce no width factor.
The constant coefficient vector is nonzero and integral, so the upper envelope at a node has value at least zero.
At a unique maximizer \(k\), the value equation makes \((u_k-cv_k)t^k\) exponentially small relative to \(|w_k t^k|\).
Subtracting \(nk\) times the value equation from the derivative equation does the same for \(v_k t^k\), contradicting maximality even if one entire row is zero.

The sum of the positive breakpoints weighted by their index jumps equals \(L(0)-\gamma_0\), by integrating the slope on each interval.
An upper rate \(S\) leaves room for precisely one unit jump at each required node, since \(\gamma_0\ge0\) and \(L(0)\le S\).
In particular no nonconsecutive or third maximizing index can occur at such a jump.
In decreasing order write \(d=T_{i-1}'w_{i-1}\) and \(e=T_i'w_i\).
After normalization the leading equations give \(L_{c_i}e=-L_{c_i}d\) and \(e_2=-d_2+(n/c_i)L_{c_i}d\), up to exponentially decaying errors.
These give exactly the stated transfer matrix, with the remainder still decaying exponentially after finitely many multiplications by matrices polynomial in \(n\).
The extra simple node lies between jumps or outside the extreme jumps and therefore has a unique leading coefficient.
For an empty double list this coefficient is \(w_0\) and the matrix product is empty.

The resulting scalar \(p(n)w_0\) is an algebraic integer because the fixed nodes are units.
All its conjugates grow at most subexponentially since \(p\) is fixed and \(\gamma_0=0\).
An exponentially small absolute value forces this scalar to vanish by its integer norm.
For every embedding \(\tau\), the rational vector \(w_0\ne0\) then lies in the common kernel of \(p(n)\) and \(\tau p(n)\) on infinitely many integers.
Their determinant at zero is \(c_*-\tau c_*\), so its identically vanishing forces the exterior unit \(c_*\) to be rational, which is impossible.
If the uniform positive excess failed, choosing graphs at arbitrarily large exponents with excess tending to zero would give exactly the excluded subsequence.

### Attainment and the passing test

The unique conjugate of \(\delta\) outside the unit disk gives only one possible image of \(\delta\) under an embedding fixing \(\delta^k\), for every \(k\ge1\).
Separability gives equal fields, and the unique exterior value gives \(m(Q_i)=na_i\log\delta\).
These measures distinguish the powered minima, including at even \(n\).
The integral box count bounds pairs of value and divided-derivative jets at each original root by multiplying the separate embedding counts, even though the roots belong to one field.
Its exponent has leading term \(nTh\,l/(l-J)\) for \(l=2(w+1)\) and fixed \(J=2\sum_i\deg P_i\).
The count uses distinct exponents \(0,1\bmod n\) for \(n\ge2\).
Its proof works for any fixed measure budget, and taking \(w=b-1\to\infty\) with \(b=o(n)\) makes the normalized logarithmic overhead vanish.

If \(Q_i\) divides both rows, dividing the graph by \(Q_i(X^n)\) lowers its order at \(c_i\) by one and preserves its order at each other selected exterior node.
The quotient graph retains a simple zero at \(c_i\) and all remaining double zeros.
For a quotient row of largest one-norm, the loss from passing to its log Mahler measure is at most \(w\log 2\).
Adding \(m(Q_i)\) on restoration gives the original graph a normalized log norm at least \(Th+\eta_i-o(1)\).
The same argument works for a single input using the empty-double-list case.
For fixed width choose the interpolation excess below every \(\eta_i\) and the requested tolerance before taking \(n\) large.

At a powered node the two projected rows before their linear multipliers sum to twice the nonzero first row value.
The multipliers \(Y+1\) and \(Y-1\) are nonzero there, change the two projections to the same chosen sign at width \(w+1\), and preserve the reciprocal double factors.
Each node rules out at most one integer linear-combination parameter, so \(r+1\) parameters suffice.
Integral positive one-norms then have a least element.
Finally the test requires \(2\log\|A\|_1/n+\log(2nb+1)/n<(\log 2)/4\).
With \(2Th<(\log2)/4\), the reciprocal padding multiplier of norm at most two preserves this inequality for every prescribed \(b\to\infty\) with \(\log b=o(n)\).
The argument gives the three-input slope \(6h\) for \(a=1,2,3\).
The attainment and passing conclusions concern a fixed Salem or quadratic input and supply no uniform estimate as that input varies.

## The extension to real exterior roots and nonreal conjugate pairs

At 2026-11-19T02:11 UTC I independently reviewed Sheng Yin's [extended statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#statement) and its [equal-modulus argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#conjugates).
The extension gives the same exact cost with \(h=\log M(\delta)\) whenever the stated condition on collisions holds for the selected list of powers.
In particular, all lists satisfy the condition in each of the two specified classes of exterior conjugates.

Suppose two different double nodes \(c,d\) lie at one radius and the envelope has jump one there.
The only possible leading vectors are \(v=w_k\) and \(v'=r_n w_{k+1}\).
Divide both by the larger of their norms.
After dividing each pair of equations by its own \(k\)-th power of the node parameter, the discarded terms relative to this normalization decay exponentially, since the larger of the unscaled leading magnitudes has exponent \(L(s)\ge0\).
The fixed-index and infinite tails obey the same bound even with the factors \(nj\).
Writing \(N_c=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-c\\c^{-1}&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), the two pairs give \(Zv'=(-I+nN_c)v\) and \(Wv'=(-I+nN_d)v\), up to exponentially small errors.
Eliminating \(v'\) gives a matrix \(W M_c(n)-Z M_d(n)\) on \(v\).
Both \(N_c\) and \(N_d\) have trace and determinant zero, and \(\operatorname{tr}(N_cN_d)=-(c-d)^2/(cd)\).
The determinant of the eliminated matrix is consequently \((W-Z)^2-n^2WZ(c-d)^2/(cd)\), which agrees with the four-row determinant after multiplication by \(cd\).
Since \(|WZ|=1\), the quadratic term dominates uniformly even if \(W=Z\) or the phases approach each other arbitrarily fast.
Polynomial bounds on the inverse make both normalized vectors exponentially small, contradicting the normalization.
Thus two distinct double nodes require a jump of at least two.
At a simple nonreal node, the invertible real-linear map \((u,v)\mapsto u-cv\) likewise excludes a unique leading vector using the value equation alone.

Full power degrees make powering injective on each conjugacy orbit and give \(m(P_i)=a_i h\), \(m(Q_i)=na_i h\).
Different \(a_i\) give disjoint orbits since \(h>0\), so two members of a conjugate pair always belong to the same \(P_i\).
Under the modulus hypothesis the weighted jumps count each exterior root, yielding the full cost \(Th\).
After division by \(Q_i(X^n)\) the double nodes in every other orbit still supply \((T-a_i)h\).
If any exterior conjugate \(x\) of \(\delta\) is nonreal, its power \(x^{a_i}\) remains nonreal by injectivity, and its conjugate also lies in \(P_i\).
Hence no other orbit uses this radius, so its surviving simple zero adds the positive gain \(a_i\log|x|\).
With entirely real exterior roots every selected radius is distinct, and the earlier strict bound for one further simple zero applies directly.
These arguments include one selected power and allow the chosen representative \(\delta\) itself to lie on the unit circle.

For real exterior conjugates \(x,y\), equality of powered moduli gives \(x^a=\pm y^b\).
The absolute heights of conjugates agree and are positive, so the power identity gives \(a=b\).
Raising to the second power and using the full degree for \(2a\) then gives \(x=y\).
For one nonreal exterior pair the distinct levels already have distinct radii and the two conjugates at each level stay distinct.
The unchanged integral-jet estimate, exclusion of common factors by the gain, sign selection and padding apply to each eligible list.
In particular the \(6h\) specialization uses the modulus condition for the list \(1,2,3\).
The geometric terminology for the upper envelope agrees with the [recorded scalar convention](../literature/AKNR18/Yin_U001.md#tropical) after changing \(s\) to \(-s\).
The argument for the vector coefficients follows from the two contact equations.
