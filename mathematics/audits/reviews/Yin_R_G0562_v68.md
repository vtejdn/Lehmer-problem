## Finding on the inverse-pair action and the height bound

At 2026-11-16T22:42 UTC an independent reviewer for Yin checked the complete [C345v68 theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#statement) submitted at 22:41 UTC, both argument sections and their three cited dependencies at the interfaces below.
The reviewer took no part in developing this argument and received the fixed text and scope without proof-development dialogue, an author explanation or a previous verdict on the theorem.
The group assertion, the estimate for every stated invariant partition, and the asymptotic consequences pass.

The irreducible polynomial has distinct roots and none is \(1\) or \(-1\), so inversion pairs them into \(m\) two-element labels.
The exterior conjugates \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\) have different labels because they are nonreal and both have modulus greater than one.
Ordinary conjugation interchanges those labels and fixes each neutral label, since \(\bar u=u^{-1}\) on the circle.
It also fixes every real root individually.
The induced permutation is consequently a single transposition, even though the same automorphism may interchange the two roots on many fixed labels.
Conjugating in the image of the Galois group supplies the single transposition on each translated edge.
Along a path the identity \((u\,v)(v\,w)(u\,v)=(u\,w)\) gives all transpositions of its component, each fixing the complement pointwise.
These subgroups on disjoint supports generate their direct product in the label action.
They carry any one edge of a component to every two-element subset there, proving completeness.
Transitivity of the label action makes every vertex incident with an orbit edge and makes the components equipotent, so \(b\ge2\).
For one component the image contains \(S_m\) and hence equals it.
This proof requires no choice of commuting lifts to the action on individual roots.

The [compression argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) uses one common multiple \(n\) of the within-class torsion orders.
The product \(v_A\) of a class of size \(f\) satisfies \(v_A^n=x^{fn}\) for every \(x\in A\).
Thus a torsion quotient of two class products would already identify their original classes.
The orbit of products is integral and inversion invariant, and each class of exterior roots contributes exactly its original product of moduli to the new measure.
This verifies the reduction at the stated scope where two exterior roots remain.

For two representatives \(x,y\) of different labels the common absolute logarithmic height is \(H=\log M(P)/d>0\).
A multiplicative dependence \(x^u y^v=1\) would have both exponents nonzero, and height of powers would give \(|u|H=|v|H\).
Consequently \(x/y\) or \(x/(y^{-1})\) would be torsion, violating the compressed hypothesis since \(x\) differs from both \(y\) and \(y^{-1}\).
This argument applies to every compressed reciprocal input of measure greater than one used in the partition estimate.
The [earlier Salem specialization](../literature/AM16/Yin_U004.md#am16-u004) gives the joint-field convention in its original setting.

The joint field degree counts images of the ordered pair of actual roots \((x,y)\), since every embedding of that field extends to its normal closure inside the splitting field.
Once the image of \(x\) has been chosen, its label determines one block of the invariant partition.
The image of \(y\) has at most \(b-1\) other possible labels in this block and at most two choices of root per label, irrespective of the kernel of the label action.
Hence \(D\le 2d(b-1)\le d^2/t\).
For the two independent coordinates, [Amoroso and David, Theorem 1.6](../literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) has \(\kappa(2)=3(3!)^2-2=106\) and product exponent \(212\).
Using \(\log(3D)\le2\log(3d)\) in its lower bound on \(H^2\) gives
\[
       \log M(P)\ge 2^{-106}\sqrt{c_{\rm prod}(2)}\,
               \frac{\sqrt{t}}{(\log(3d))^{106}} .
\]
The external theorem is used at its accepted statement and application scope.
Its original proof was outside this review.
On a sequence with \(M(P)>1\) tending to one, a bounded-degree subsequence would have bounded integer coefficients by the elementary symmetric coefficient bound and hence only finitely many measures.
Thus \(d\to\infty\), and squaring the displayed inequality gives \(t/(\log(3d))^{212}\to0\).
Since every fixed power of \(\log(3d)\) is \(o(d)\), this implies \(b=d/(2t)\to\infty\).
