Its divided differences are integral since the sum of two such square roots is a unit.
Consequently the divided difference of \(yq(y)\) is a unit, by writing the difference as \((y-z)q(y)+z(q(y)-q(z))\).
The two transformed roots are distinct square roots of the same element of valuation \(t\), so their difference has valuation \(t/2\), which also forces \(t\) even in this split case.
The exponential section coordinate adds one to this distance valuation and inversion preserves it.
