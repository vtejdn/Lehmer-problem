## Finding on the finite cyclotomic search and the rank conditions

**The factor carrying the prescribed polynomial.**
Widths add on Laurent products by multiplication of the two extreme layers, and \(n>w\) makes specialization injective on each factor's occupied exponent vectors.
Since \(P\) is irreducible with nonzero constant coefficient, it divides some specialized rational irreducible factor after monomial shifts.
A base-only factor of width at most \(w<d\) cannot supply it.
Any torsion-translate component in the remaining factor \(H\) would give a rational cyclotomic polynomial in a primitive monomial, as the v156 factorization shows.
The monomial specializes nontrivially by injectivity, so all its nonzero specialized roots would be torsion, contradicting the presence of \(\alpha\).
Thus the Beukers–Smyth finite torsion-point bound applies to \(H\), with Euclidean Newton polygon area at most \(sr\).
The map \(\zeta\mapsto(\zeta,\zeta^n)\) is injective, giving at most \(22sr\) distinct cyclotomic roots of \(h=H(X,X^n)\).

Over characteristic zero the irreducible rational \(H\) has no repeated geometric factor.
If a geometric factor \(G\) divided \(D_nH\), reduction of the product rule modulo \(G\) would give \(G\mid D_nG\).
Total degree forces \(D_nG=cG\), and all its monomials would have the same \(i+nj\).
The \(X\)-width bound \(s<n\) then gives only one monomial, impossible after the normalization removes variable factors.
This proves the nonzero resultant assertion, including \(s=0\).
The Sylvester bound is \((\deg_Y H)(\deg_X D_nH)+(\deg_Y D_nH)(\deg_X H)\le2sr\).
Specializing its polynomial Bézout identity makes \(\gcd(h,Xh')\) divide that nonzero resultant in \(\mathbb C[X]\).
Each nonzero root of multiplicity \(\ell\) contributes at least \(\ell-1\) to this gcd, so adding repetitions to the distinct cyclotomic roots costs at most \(2sr\) in total.
This gives \(24sr\) for their total degree.

The exponent span of \(h\) counts every nonzero root with multiplicity and is between \(rn-s\) and \(rn+s\) by the occupied extreme \(Y\)-layers.
Subtracting at most \(24sr\) cyclotomic degree and one copy of \(P\) gives (2) for the cofactor in the original multiple.
For cyclotomic cofactor, the monic product supplies integral monic factors up to signs and monomials, hence \(h\) itself supplies a witness with cyclotomic cofactor degree at most \(24sr\le24Rw\).
The inequalities \(d\le rn+s\) and \(rn-s\le d+24rs\) imply \(n\ge(d-w)/R\) and \(n\le d/r+24s+s/r\le d+25w\).
The bound \(\varphi(q)\ge\sqrt{q/2}\) holds componentwise on prime powers, with a loss of \(1/\sqrt2\) possible only at the single prime power \(2^1\).
It makes the order and multiplicity search finite.
The reduced witness has a nonzero bottom layer, so its least specialized exponent is in \([0,s]\).
Thus testing shifts \(0\le a\le w\) suffices for existence at this same spacing, and a successful interval test constructs an admissible witness.

**Dimension and measure.**
The \(K\) columns in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) are dependent exactly when a nonzero rational combination vanishes at \(\alpha\).
Clearing denominators gives an integral lift and specialization, which remains nonzero because the exponents \(i+jn\) are distinct.
This applies at every \(n>w\) when \(K>d\), without any restriction on the cofactor.
Every lift of an admissible witness has the nontorsion horizontal component found above, so v156 gives at least \(c_R\) for its two-variable measure even at smaller actual vertical width.
For positive horizontal width the v159 error increases upon using the larger support, the larger vertical width, and \(w/n\) in place of the actual width ratio.
In particular \(\frac{d}{dv}[v\log(2RK/v)]=\log(2RK/v)-1>0\) for the displayed \(R,K\) and \(0<v\le1\).
When the actual horizontal width is zero, invariance of circle measure under the \(n\)-th power gives exact equality of the two measures after monomial normalization.
Additivity of logarithmic Mahler measure now gives (3) for every cofactor, including arbitrary integer content.

**Residue classes and effectiveness.**
For \(K\le d\) the embedding matrix on the powers \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}\) is invertible, so rational column rank equals rank over the splitting field after embedding.
The determinant of every \(K\)-row minor expands into at most \(K!\) terms whose bases are nonzero elements of the conjugate-generated group.
Its torsion subgroup is finite because the group is finitely generated.
After passing to \(n=a+et\), equal \(e\)-th powers combine and distinct remaining bases have nontorsion quotients.
For each nonempty sum, taking an odd \(p\)-adic place where its bases are units and then a common power puts them in \(1+p^2\mathcal O\).
The exponential interpolants on each subprogression converge on a disk of radius greater than one, also at a ramified place with \(|p|_p=p^{-1}\).
Infinite nonnegative integer zeros would accumulate in \(\mathbb Z_p\), forcing the interpolant to vanish identically on the disk.
Its values at consecutive integers then contradict invertibility of the Vandermonde matrix on the distinct powered bases.
This is the simple-base case of the characteristic-zero recurrence theorem and the number-field proof on pp. 417–418 of [Lech's paper](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/116/6892-11512_2007_Article_BF02590997.pdf).

Thus an identically vanishing collection of minors gives the full class, while any nonempty minor permits only finitely many exceptional \(n\) there.
Checking \(K!\) consecutive ranks on a class tests identity because each minor has at most \(K!\) distinct bases and the first consecutive values determine all combined coefficients.
This does not bound the last zero of a nonempty minor.
For \(w+1\le n\le\lfloor(d-1-w)/R\rfloor\) the distinct powers lie among the \(d\) basis powers, so the interval has full rank at every integer.
Its length is exactly \(1+\lfloor(d-K)/R\rfloor\), at least one when \(K\le d\).
The consecutive integers therefore rule out the stated minimum number of full classes modulo \(e\), which gives (4) and the density after discarding finite exceptions.
For Salem inputs the inverse pairs generate a group free of torsion by the fixed independence result in C165, so this interval excludes the sole possible full class.

Modulo two, \(X\) is invertible in the finite quotient even if \(\bar P\) has repeated factors, because its constant coefficient is one.
At every \(n\equiv w+1\bmod b_2\) with \(n>w\), the column images are the consecutive powers with exponents \(0,\ldots,K-1\).
For \(K\le d\) those powers are independent in the quotient by the monic degree-\(d\) polynomial \(\bar P\).
A rational dependence yields a primitive integral dependence before reduction and hence a nonzero coefficient vector after reduction, which is impossible.
Finally, norm \(-1\) excludes a square root of the golden ratio \(\beta\) in its quadratic field.
The real embedding taking \(\sqrt\beta\) to \(-\sqrt\beta\) excludes a further square root in the resulting quartic field.
This verifies the degree-eight minimal polynomial, \(K=6\), and the relations of degree at most two in \(\alpha^n\) for all \(n\) divisible by four.

The inspection supports the finite decision procedure for cyclotomic cofactors and the stated quantifiers for arbitrary cofactors.
The external Beukers–Smyth bound was used at the scope of the cited source unit, and the relevant number-field argument of Lech was checked directly.
