# Torsion translates, totally real and CM units, and relative degree

<a id="finding"></a>
## The exclusions hold for all signs and common dilations

At 2026-11-23T08:08 UTC an agent acting for Yin independently reviewed the full statement and three proof sections of C0345v882 submitted at 2026-11-23T08:03 UTC.
The reviewer had no part in developing this proof and received no exploration notes, development dialogue, author explanations or previous conclusions about v882.
The review supports the whole submitted claim.

Write \(B=b/a\).
The constant, linear and quadratic coefficients of \((1+BtT)(T+B)-w(1+BT)(tT+B)\) are \(B(1-w)\), \(1-wt+B^2(t-w)\) and \(Bt(1-w)\).
Dividing the resulting zero by the unit \(T\) and multiplying by \(a^2\) therefore gives the claimed integral identity.
Since \(a,b\) are coprime rational integers, \(a^2\) is invertible modulo \(b\mathcal O_K\).
Thus \((1-wt)/b\) is integral and every conjugate has modulus at most \(2/(q+1)<1\).
Its norm gives \(wt=1\).
For \(t\ne1\), substitution and cancellation give \(a(T+t^{-1}T^{-1})=-b(1+t^{-1})\).
Now \((1+t^{-1})/a\) is integral and has every conjugate of modulus strictly below one, even at \(q=2\), since an embedding cannot send \(t\ne1\) to one.
The norm forces \(t=-1\), and the remaining equation gives \(T^2=1\), contrary to nontorsion.
All rational-function denominators used here are nonzero because \(-B\) is a rational nonunit and \(T,tT\) are units.
For the two polynomial roots the twists are \(\zeta^u,\zeta^v\), so Bézout's identity gives \(\zeta=1\) without a conjugacy assumption.
For any nonzero integer \(n\), equality of two conjugate \(n\)-th powers would make their quotient torsion.
Powering is consequently injective on the conjugates of \(\beta\), and every conjugate of \(\beta^n\) occurs by extension of embeddings.
The two nested number fields have the same degree.

For \(u\ge2\) the strict radial estimate and reciprocity give \(\lvert\log|x|\rvert<\frac12\operatorname{arcosh}(3/2)=\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\) at each real conjugate off the circle.
Real nontorsion conjugates are all off the circle, so the integral nonzero element \(\beta-\beta^{-1}\) would have all conjugates of modulus less than one.
For \(u=1\) the unit norm requires conjugates on both sides, while the parent has at most one root counted with multiplicity on either open side.
Reciprocity forces the two conjugates to be \(x,x^{-1}\), including when \(x<-1\).
Their trace has integral absolute value strictly between \(2\) and \(2(1+1/q)\le3\).
This excludes the remaining totally real case.
In a finite \(E(i)\) with \(E\) totally real, embeddings commute with complex conjugation on both generators \(E\) and \(i\).
Hence the unit \(\beta/\bar\beta\) has conjugates of modulus one, and Kronecker's theorem and the graph result identify \(\beta=\bar\beta\).
The fixed field is \(E\), contradicting the totally real exclusion.
For an embedded CM field, the real parts of elements are totally real by commutation of conjugation with embeddings, and the imaginary parts are totally real by the same argument in the compositum with \(\mathbb Q(i)\).
Roots of unity likewise belong to \(\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}(i)\).
If \(z^n\) lies in that field, so does \(\beta^n=(z^n)^g\), which generates the field of \(\beta\).
This verifies the exclusion at every \(g\), with either sign of \(n\).

The precise inputs to the degree estimate have \(H=q+1\), \(\delta=1/q\), and the primitive unit \(\beta=z^g\).
The individual height estimate gives \(h(\beta)\le 2\log(q+1)/v\) eventually, and the primitive slope estimate gives \(v/u\gg q^2\log(q+1)\).
In particular \(v\to\infty\) and \(\log(q+1)\ll\log v\).
The rational-base specialization of Amoroso and Delsinne applies to this nontorsion \(\beta\) over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
To obtain its absolute constant, one can apply the theorem to a finite abelian number field containing \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) and the coefficients of the minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), retaining its exact relative degree \(d\).
Using \(1\le d\le u+v<2v\) in the height inequalities, one first obtains \(d\gg v/(\log v)^5\) since \(\log\log(5d)\ge\log\log5>0\).
This makes \(\log d\) comparable to \(\log v\), recovering the asserted numerator \((\log\log v)^3\) in the second application.
The resulting lower bound tends to infinity.
Taking the compositum of the power-field equality with \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) gives \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\beta)=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\beta^n)=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\zeta\beta^n)\), since \(\zeta\in\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
If a torsion multiple of \(z^n\) lies in \(K\), then \(\beta^n\in K\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), which gives the claimed lower bound for the degree of that compositum.
The field generated by finitely many multiplicative generators is fixed, so its compositum has bounded relative degree, including when the constructed elements use arbitrary radicals and torsion.

The radial, counting, slope and height inputs were checked at their specified uses against v869 and v875 and their existing reviews R1017 and R1023.
The Amoroso–Delsinne theorem was used at the statement and specialization scope of the source unit, whose inspection excludes a full independent proof of the determinant and ramification estimates.
The existence of solutions at unbounded \(q\) remains open.
