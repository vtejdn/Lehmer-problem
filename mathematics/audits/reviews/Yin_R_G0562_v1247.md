# Independent review of the single-prime examples for all powered capacity tests

<a id="finding"></a>
## Linked arms, reciprocal perturbation and the primitive leading coefficient

An agent outside the development of C0901 v169 independently checked its statement and three proof sections for Yin against the fixed text and its direct inputs.
The reviewer received no development discussion, author explanation or previous finding on this argument.
The examples, with all positive powers tested for the same irreducible polynomial and all finite denominators confined to any prescribed prime, have the claimed properties.
The comparison with integral inputs uses the monic and degree assumptions in the published capacity criterion.

For the \(n\)-arm star the polynomial pullback of the Green function has boundary value zero and logarithmic growth with coefficient one.
Since the interval has capacity \(1/4\), the capacity of the star is \(4^{-1/n}\).
At \(R>1\) its Green function is \(2n^{-1}\operatorname{arcosh}(R^{n/2})\).
The exterior conformal map normalized with positive derivative at infinity has modulus \(e^{g_n(R)}\) there and maps the real interval outward by symmetry and injectivity.
Adding the slit in this exterior coordinate multiplies the capacity by \((2+e^{g_n(R)}+e^{-g_n(R)})/4\), from the Joukowski map and interval capacity.
At \(R=e^{4/\sqrt n}\), the expansion \(g_n(R)=4/\sqrt n+O(n^{-1})\) and \(2\log\cosh(g/2)=g^2/4+O(g^4)\) give the positive coefficient \(4-\log4\) in the \(1/n\) term.

For the auxiliary polynomial, the positive radial equation has one solution \(u>0\) because both positive hyperbolic factors strictly increase there.
At \(u=a/\sqrt n\) its right side times \(\sqrt n\) tends to \(2a\sinh(a/4)\), which straddles \(1\) for \(a=1,2\).
The midpoint evaluations of the circle equation have alternating sign, since the minimum relevant sine factor is \(\sin(\pi/(4J+2))\) and \(4\sin(\pi/(4J+2))>t\) eventually.
The \(J\) open intervals below \(\pi\) and their conjugates together with the real inverse pair account for degree \(2J+2\), forcing simplicity.
A root of unity in \(Q_t\) would express the strictly intermediate rational number \(t\) as an algebraic integer.
The auxiliary factors are consequently coprime and the degree of their product is \(n+2J+1\).
The first \(J\) coefficients below the top degree of the unperturbed integral product vanish, and the perturbation starts exactly at depth \(J+1\).
The constant remains one.
Newton's identities therefore give zero through order \(J\).

In the trace polynomial before the central perturbation, the conversion of reciprocal Laurent pairs into polynomials in \(Z+Z^{-1}\) is integral and linear in the coefficients.
The central addition changes only the trace constant, to valuation \(-hD-1\).
At position \(D-1\) the line joining the extreme valuation points already has ordinate \(-h-1/D\), strictly below \(-h\).
It lies lower still at every other interior position.
For a root each interior summand then has valuation strictly above the smaller endpoint valuation, proving the stated minimal-denominator slope directly by the ultrametric inequality.
In a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) such a denominator divides the ramification degree.
Its value here is \(D\), so the trace polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_p\) and over \(\mathbb Q\).
Disjoint sign-changing real intervals keep one trace above two and all other traces strictly between minus two and two.
Having all \(D\) real embeddings and a negative image of the discriminant at an interior trace makes the quadratic lift irreducible over the trace field.
Thus its reciprocal rational minimum has exactly one strictly exterior root, which excludes torsion quotients by Galois transitivity and preserves degree under every positive power.

The all-powers assertion uses two different uniformities.
For the auxiliary star, \(2k\) permutes the nonzero residues modulo \(n\) when \(n\nmid k\); the exterior root supplies precisely the missing positive arm and extends it past the required radius.
For \(k\) divisible by \(n\) the long positive segment alone suffices for all sufficiently large \(n\).
After fixing \(n\), each sufficiently large perturbation has \(\log\lambda_h>u/2\), giving segment capacity strictly above one for all \(k\ge\lceil\log 4/(2u)\rceil\).
Below this cutoff only finitely many \(k\) remain and all are coprime to \(n\).
For each, all old neutral directions and the fixed positive direction are distinct.
The slope of the angular matching on each of the finitely many intervening arcs approaches one.
Both arc lengths between any two directions are thus bounded below by \(1-o(1)\) times their original lengths, giving the same lower bound for the shorter arc and its chord by sine concavity.
The polar distance formula gives a common lower Lipschitz bound on the whole star after the radial scaling.
It applies to all products of pair distances in the definition of the transfinite diameter and preserves strict capacity excess for large \(h\).
One choice of \(h\) works for all the finitely many smaller \(k\), as well as for the remaining \(k\) by the segment bound.

The central coefficient has exact denominator \(p^{hD+1}\), whereas all other coefficients have denominator dividing \(p^h\).
Primitivity after clearing follows immediately at \(p\) from the central coefficient and at the other primes from the leading coefficient.
At each prime different from \(p\) both the polynomial and its reciprocal are monic integral, making all roots local units.
Hence the primitive total log measure includes precisely the stated leading coefficient and the one exterior log.
The coefficientwise error tends to \(t\) with \(h\) at fixed \(n\), and the first \(J\asymp\sqrt d\) zero coefficients persist.
These distinctions permit small Archimedean cost and prevent a claim of small total Mahler measure.

The [published comparison, Section 2 and Theorems 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972721000654) was checked directly for its monic irreducibility and degree hypotheses, its necessary capacity threshold, and its asymptotic for unrestricted geometric configurations.
The proof of the external criterion was not audited.
The local square-energy theorem contributes its degree-normalized lower bound under the reciprocal hypotheses and the conformal disk-slit calculation at their stated scopes.
