## All first measure values lie on one primitive unit line in the same embedded field

At 2026-11-25T00:37 UTC an agent outside the construction of [C0901 v95](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#statement) independently reviewed its four sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and scope without its development discussion.
The reviewer previously developed the separate norm-cost input v94; this finding checks its applicability and the iteration substitution without serving as an independent review of that earlier result.
The common-field, integral-index, multiple-closure and iteration conclusions hold for the stated nontorsion elements, with no hypothesis that the original unit is reciprocal or has large degree.

Taking a radical of a torsion-twisted conjugate monomial preserves zero valuations at every finite place, so every element of \(D(w)\) is a unit.
After conjugating the power relation, each individual log modulus is a rational linear combination of the original conjugate log moduli.
The positive log modulus terms occurring in each \(\log M(x)\) therefore give an element of the same one-dimensional rational space as \(\log M(w)\).
Both are positive for nontorsion units, so the exponents in the common-power equality can indeed be taken positive.
This argument counts the distinct conjugates of each actual radical and uses no degree identification.

For a nontorsion unit \(y\), the entire strictly exterior set \(S\) is nonempty and stable under complex conjugation.
Its product is real and nonzero; the fixed rational sign makes it exactly \(A=M(y)>1\).
All Galois translates of \(S\) have the same cardinality.
If one of them differs from \(S\), some strictly exterior members have been replaced by entries of modulus at most one, and the product of the moduli decreases strictly even in the presence of neutral roots.
Therefore \(\lvert g A\rvert=A\) implies that \(gS=S\) and \(gA=A\), with no residual root-of-unity factor.
Fixing \(A^n\) consequently forces fixing \(A\) for every \(n>0\), so the embedded fields generated by these powers coincide.
Applying this separately to \(M(x)\) and \(M(w)\) identifies their fields by their common power.
The cited introduction attributes the Perron property to Adler and Marcus, and the argument here proves the required unit case directly. [Fili–Pottmeyer–Zhang, Introduction](https://math.okstate.edu/people/fili/iterated-mahler.pdf)

For the separate comparison, Samuels's Lemma 3.1 states that the set of multiplicative measure values at elements of \(\operatorname{Rad}(K)\) bounded by a fixed \(B\ge1\) is finite for a fixed Galois number field \(K\).
A torsion twist in a power relation can be killed by raising that relation to the order of the twist, so \(D(w)\) lies in the radical of its fixed splitting field.
This verifies the scope of the finiteness comparison; the common field and integer generator in the present claim follow from the rank-one and stabilizer arguments. [Samuels, Lemma 3.1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A2178587175246490D9E398C9842C192/S0008439500019330a.pdf/infimum_in_the_metric_mahler_measure.pdf)

The field \(F\) is embedded in the reals, so its roots of unity are just \(\pm1\), even if some other embedding is nonreal.
In each torsion class exactly one of the two unit representatives is positive in this embedding.
Dirichlet's unit lattice on a rational line through the nonzero class of \(M(w)\) has a primitive generator unique up to inversion.
Its positive representative \(\eta>1\) in the expanding direction gives \(M(w)=\eta^h\), \(h\ge1\), and every positive measure value in this line is \(\eta^e\) with \(e\ge1\).
The powers \((\eta^n)^h=M(w)^n\) generate \(F\), while \(\eta^n\) belongs to \(F\), giving the equality of embedded power fields.
Moreover \(\eta\in D(w)\) because its \(h\)th power is an actual signed product of conjugates of \(w\).

For any realized \(M(x)\), compressing the distinct conjugates of \(x\) by torsion classes preserves its total measure and stays in the same Galois-invariant divisible group.
The resulting conjugates remain distinct under all positive powers, giving \(M(y^n)=M(y)^n\) for this compressed representative \(y\) and proving attainment of every positive multiple of the original index.
No such identity is needed on \(x\) before compression.
For \(\eta\) itself, membership in \(D(w)\) gives the integer \(t\) by the already established power-line assertion.
Its powers preserve their degree by the field equality and therefore satisfy \(M(\eta^n)=\eta^{tn}\).
Starting at \(M(x)=\eta^{e(x)}\) proves all iterates, including \(j=1\).
At \(t=1\) the unit \(\eta\) realizes index one, which realizes the whole positive spectrum by multiple closure.
For \(t>1\) no assertion that index one is attained was needed.

Finally a reciprocal unit with exactly one nonreal pair of exterior roots has log moduli in the rational line spanned by \(\log|\alpha|\), including its inverse roots and neutral roots.
Under the full v94 hypotheses the quotient \(m(M(\alpha))/m(\alpha)\) is \(2b-3\) or \(b-1\) in the respective block actions.
Formula (2) in v95 at \(x=\alpha,\ j=2\) identifies this quotient with \(t\), which was already proved common to all nontorsion members of \(D(\alpha)\).
The large-degree, compression and canonical-action assumptions apply only to those two evaluated values of \(t\).
