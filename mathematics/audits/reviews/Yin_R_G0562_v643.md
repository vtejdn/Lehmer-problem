## The circle mean and the reciprocal pairs give the same factor constant

The proof establishes both limiting means and the claimed Haar distribution for every sequence satisfying the hypotheses.
In particular, the normalization by the degree \(d\) of the individual factor is consistent in the discrepancy estimate, the logarithmic tail and the resultant.
The existence of such a sequence remains open.

For a character \((i,j)\ne(0,0)\), the integer \(n=i+js\) is eventually nonzero.
If \(D=[\mathbb Q(y^n):\mathbb Q]\), adjoining \(y\) has degree at most \(|n|\), also for negative \(n\) since \(\mathbb Q(y^n)=\mathbb Q(y^{|n|})\).
Thus \(D\ge d/|n|\to\infty\), and each of the \(D\) conjugates has precisely \(d/D\) preimages among the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(y)\).
The powered element is a unit of height \(O_{i,j}(s^{-2})\), so its monic minimal polynomial has simple roots and absolute discriminant at least one.
With smoothing radius \(1/D\), Pritsker's bound for either compact angular test tends to zero, since its Dirichlet integral is bounded and \((\log D+4)/D\) tends to zero.
Discarding both members of the \(k\) reciprocal pairs costs \(O(k/d)\).
All remaining points lie on the circle, where the test equals the required real or imaginary part.
The total mass tends to one, giving the Haar limit by trigonometric approximation.

The real function \(|1+e^{it}|-1\) has simple zeros at the angles of the two primitive cube roots.
Thus \(v=|g(y)|\) small forces the distance from \(y\) to one such root \(\omega\) to be \(O(v)\).
Along the chord, \(|g(\omega)|\le v+(s+1)|y-\omega|\), so the lower bound \(|g(\omega)|\ge1\) gives \(v\gg1/s\).
The nonnegative distance cutoff for the two small arcs has gradient bounded by \(\sqrt{s}\) on a set of uniformly bounded area and circle integral \(O(u+s^{-1/2})\).
In Pritsker's estimate at the actual degree \(d\), the height term is \(O(s^{-3})\) and \((\log d+4)/d\) decays faster than any fixed inverse power of \(s\) by v507.
The discrepancy is therefore \(O(s^{-1})\), uniformly in \(u\).
Integrating the resulting sublevel count against \(du/u\) down to \(c/s\) costs \(O(\delta+s^{-1/2}\log(2s))\).
The same angular restriction gives a Haar sublevel mass \(O(u)\) without a lower cutoff, so the Haar truncation costs \(O(\delta)\).
Taking the limits in that order permits Jensen's circle integral in \(w\) and gives \(\mu\), which is positive since \(|1+e^{it}|>1\) on an arc.

At a root \(\zeta\) of \(g\), one has \(B(\zeta)=-(\zeta^2+\zeta+1)/\zeta\).
The product of the roots of \(g\) has modulus one, so \(|\operatorname{Res}(g,B)|=|g(\omega)g(\bar\omega)|\), equal to \(4\) if \(s\equiv2\bmod 3\) and \(1\) otherwise.
Evaluation over the \(a\) lifts of every \(\zeta\) shows that the parent resultant has this absolute value raised to the power \(a\).
Gauss's lemma and multiplicativity bound the log resultant of the individual monic factor between \(0\) and \(a\log4\).
For the inside member \(x\) of a pair, direct substitution of \(z=x^a\) gives
\[
 \log|g(z)g(1/z)|=-(r-s)(-\log|z|)+2\log|B(z)|.
\]
The derivative bound for \(g\) gives \(|\zeta-\omega|,|\zeta-\bar\omega|\ge1/(s+1)\), and at least one distance is at least \(\sqrt3/2\).
It follows that \(|B(\zeta)|\gg1/s\) at every interior zero.
Full localization to distance \(O(s^{-4})\) and the disk bound \(|B'|\le 2s\) preserve this lower bound at \(z\).
Hence the \(B\)-terms contribute \(O(k\log(2s))=o(d)\).
Summing \(-\log|z|\) once per pair gives \(am=h\), so the norm identity yields \((r-s)h/d\to\mu\).
Since \(s/r\to0\), both \(bm/d\) and \((sa+b)m/d\) have this limit.
