## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T12:39 UTC an independent agent reviewer for Yin checked the three sections of the [powered-order calculation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v162.md#conductor).
The reviewer had no role in developing this calculation and consulted neither development discussions nor previous findings on it.
The conductor formula, the two collision inequalities and the sharp powering-prime denominators hold for the stated orders.

The embedding by \(X=x^p\) identifies the two rational algebras since the powered roots remain distinct and both quotients have dimension \(ed\).
For a monic \(H\), reversing one power basis puts the coefficient pairing for \(\lambda_H\) in triangular form with diagonal entries one, even for repeated roots.
The sum of finite residues of \(f\,dz/H\) equals the coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) in the expansion at infinity, namely \(\lambda_H(f)\).
At \(\alpha_i\) the map \(x\mapsto x^p\) is a local analytic coordinate, taking the pole at \(\beta_i\) to one pole above it.
Using precisely these \(d\) local branches gives the pullback \(p x^{p-1} f(x^p)\,dx/(P(x)^e L(x)^e)\).
Since \(L\) is invertible modulo \(P^e\), replacing the numerator divided by \(L^e\) with its polynomial representative changes this differential by a holomorphic term at every \(\alpha_i\).
It follows that \(\lambda_{Q^e}\) corresponds to \(a\mapsto\lambda_{P^e}(M_e a)\).
Testing \(ta\) against every element of \(B_e\), and using \(1\in B_e\subset A_e\), now identifies the conductor with \(M_e^{-1}A_e\) by the two perfect integral pairings.
An integer annihilates \(A_e/B_e\) exactly when it belongs to this conductor, giving the asserted least common denominator in the integral power basis.

At \(e=1\) the derivative identity yields \(M_1=P'(\alpha)/Q'(\beta)\).
For each conjugate the integrality of \(sM_1\) bounds \(v_\ell(s)\) below by the sum of the valuations of its powered differences minus those of its original differences.
Summing over conjugates counts every unordered pair twice, giving the average \(2v_\ell(I)/d\) by the Vandermonde determinant.
Taking the maximum and then multiplying the prime-power inequalities proves \(I^{2/d}\le s\), and the exponent of a finite group divides its order.
For the cited Salem decomposition the largest cyclic summand has order \(3^{p-1}\), and summing the exponents with their multiplicities gives \(v_3(I)=(d-2)(p-1)/2\), as required by the comparison.

Modulo \(p\) the image of \(B_1\) in \(A_1/pA_1\) is exactly the set of \(p\)-th powers, since the coefficient field is \(\mathbb F_p\).
Surjectivity is equivalent both to \(p\nmid I\) by the basis determinant and to reducedness by finiteness and the nilpotent kernel of a sufficiently high Frobenius iterate.
For a monogenic quotient this is squarefreeness of \(\bar P\), hence \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(P)\).
Under this hypothesis the localized equality of the two simple orders supplies \(G(\beta)=\alpha\).
The unit \(\alpha\) lifts to a unit \(G\) across the nilpotent ideal \((Q)\), and differentiation of \(X-G^p=QC\) gives \(Q'C=1\bmod(p,Q)\).
Consequently \(V=C/G^p\) is a unit after reduction modulo \((p,Q^e)\).
In the common rational algebra, the root of \(1+QV\) congruent to one modulo \(P\) is unique since the factor between the difference of two \(p\)-th powers and the difference of their roots is \(p\) modulo this nilpotent ideal.
The resulting binomial expansion for \(x^i\) terminates after \(j=e-1\).

With \(k=e-1\), the integer \(p^{k+v_p(k!)}\) clears the \(p\)-denominators of the expansion for every \(i\), hence also for a power basis of \(A_e\).
For \(p\nmid i\), each factor \(i-hp\) in the numerator is a \(p\)-unit and \(j+v_p(j!)\) increases strictly with \(j\).
After clearing that denominator, reduction in \(B_e/pB_e\) leaves a unit times \(\bar Q^k\), nonzero by degree in \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(\bar Q^{k+1})\).
For \(k>0\) this excludes every smaller \(p\)-power denominator for the individual class, and for \(k=0\) the localized orders already coincide.
Thus every \(1\le r<p\) attains the full \(p\)-part of the group exponent.
When \(I=1\) the layer index excludes all other primes, giving the full order of each of these classes at every multiplicity.
