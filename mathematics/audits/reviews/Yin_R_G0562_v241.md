### The contour counts and both real rays

Removing the product of all unit-circle roots with multiplicities is valid over the reals.
Its multiset is stable under conjugation and inversion, so reversal is a scalar multiple of that monic product with multiplier of modulus one.
No integral factorization of this intermediate polynomial is required.
The remaining \(B\) has no root on the circle and there \(|B|=|B^*|\).
For \(0\le t<1\), the strict Rouché inequality gives \(N+\deg B-E\) interior zeros and no circle zeros.
The leading term of \(z^NB\) keeps the degree constant, even if \(B(0)=0\).
Disjoint exterior disks chosen at \(t=1\) preserve the multiplicity count by another use of Rouché for \(t\) close to one.
This gives the asserted upper bound \(E\) without a coprimality condition on the two blocks.

For an individual root \(\beta\), the squared difference giving the exterior-contour bound is exactly \((r^2-1)(1-|\beta|^2)\) when the numerator squared is subtracted from the denominator squared.
Thus the ratio is at most one for all interior and circle roots, including zero roots.
For \(T=|\beta|>r\) the maximum occurs at \(|z-\beta|=T-r\), and the derivative in \(T\) of \((rT-1)/(T-r)\) is \((1-r^2)/(T-r)^2<0\).
After multiplying these bounds, (2) yields a strict contour inequality and exactly \(E\) zeros outside \(r\), leaving no zero in \(1<|z|\le r\).
Differentiating the two logarithms at \(r=1\) gives \((R_0+1)/(R_0-1)\); a strict \(N_0\) above \(E\) times this value therefore works at one \(r\) for all \(N\ge N_0\).
The [previous contour](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v1.md#height-free-exterior-contour) uses the same individual root ratio in the coprime unit-seed setting.
The passage to \(t=1\) and the annular count here do not require the previous simple-circle-root conclusion or any removal of off-circle common factors.

On a real exterior ray the pair ratio follows by multiplying the two denominators and numerators and dividing both by \(2e^{u+t}\).
Since \(1-c^2=\sin^2\eta>0\), the displayed square identity bounds the absolute logarithmic derivative of \(\cosh v-c\).
Equality occurs at most at isolated points of the integration interval, and cannot occur identically, so the integral bound remains strict when \(N\sin\eta=2\).
For the other criterion the second factor \(x^2+1-2ax\) is at least \((|x|-1)^2>0\).
This excludes both real exterior rays for \(N=2,\ |a|=1\) as well.
The ratios at all remaining roots are at most one and no exterior real point is a pole, so neither sign in the return can produce a real exterior zero.

The at most two remaining exterior roots consequently form a simple complex conjugate pair if any exist.
Complex conjugation places them in the same rational irreducible factor.
The leading and constant coefficients of \(P\) are \(1,\sigma\), so its monic integral factors are unit polynomials.
Any such factor without an exterior root is cyclotomic up to products by Kronecker's theorem.
The normalized reversal of a positive-measure factor also has positive measure, and the reversal of \(P\) is \(\sigma P\), forcing that unique factor to be reciprocal.
The pair gives both its multiplicity one and its measure greater than \(2\log r\) when (2) holds.
For the Smyth supplement, a positive-measure nonreciprocal irreducible factor of the monic \(A\) satisfies \(M\ge\theta\) at the [precise threshold](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
All other logarithmic factor measures are nonnegative and \(m(A)=2\log R\), so \(R\ge\sqrt\theta\).

### The examples and the delay-one comparison

For the dense example the degree is \(k+2\), and the displayed lower bound \(2k+3\) on the circle is strict relative to the perturbation of length \(k\).
The first summand has exactly \(k\) interior zeros there.
The inequality at an exterior zero follows by dividing the sum of the smaller powers by \(\alpha^k\); each ratio then has modulus less than one.
Thus \(\Re(\alpha^2)\le-k-4\), giving the claimed modulus and sector, with no possible real root among the two exterior ones.
At \(r=5/4,\ R_0=11/5\) the squared contour threshold is \(1225/361<15625/4096=(5/4)^6\).

For \(M\ge2\) the polynomials \(X^h+M\) and \(MX^h+1\) are coprime over \(\mathbb C\), and each root of the second is simple and nonzero.
Eisenstein at such a linear prime, followed by primitivity, proves irreducibility in \(\mathbb C[X,Y]\) of \(Y^h(X^h+M)+\sigma(MX^h+1)\).
This remains a nonunit irreducible in the Laurent ring.
An integral matrix of nonzero determinant induces an injection on the support lattice, preserving nonunits as any Laurent nonunit has at least two terms.
Applying its adjugate to a purported factorization would contradict irreducibility for the power \(|\det|\); a negative determinant adds only the inversion automorphism.
The specified directions substitute \(A=z^2+M,\ A^*=1+Mz^2\) and satisfy the strip condition for every \(N\ge2\).
The relation in a primitive odd direction is an integer multiple of \((N,-2)\).
At \(N=2,\sigma=-1\) the two middle terms cancel and the return is \(z^4-1\).
For the other cases there are at most four terms, so the [whole-polynomial support gap](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#statement) bounds the unique positive irreducible factor whenever one exists.

The cited comparisons have the indicated scopes: one real Pisot root in v30, fixed polynomials in v167 and v193, coefficient-length bounds in v170 and v172, and total measure in v3.
For a monic irreducible reciprocal target with two nonreal exterior roots the two exterior traces are conjugate nonreal numbers, since \(\Im(z+z^{-1})\ne0\) for a nonreal \(z\) with \(|z|>1\).
Their quadratic \(G\) satisfies \(G(2)>0\); the negative parameters in the [delay-one construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v35.md#statement) produce irreducible seeds whose exterior roots divided by \(\sqrt{|C|}\) tend to \(i,-i\).

A literal extension to all reducible reciprocal targets would fail: \(S=(z-1)^2(z^4+4z^2+1)\) has just one exterior nonreal pair, but \((z+1)S=zA+A^*\) forces \(A(1)=0\), inconsistent with irreducibility at the required degree.
