## The positive factors, the two endpoints and strict descent

At 2026-11-25T02:20 UTC an agent outside the development of C0901 v120 independently checked its fixed [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) and all three proof sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its inputs without development discussion or earlier findings on v120.
The reviewer developed earlier constituent-chain and Perron inputs and checked the interfaces used here.
The uniform dichotomy, the characterization at the lower endpoint, even powers above it, the smaller Salem witness from one factor at every strict interior value, and both directions of the bounded-second-iterate reduction follow.

For \(1<A<2\), removing content, powers of \(X\) and cyclotomic factors leaves a finite list of nontorsion monic unit factors counted with multiplicity.
Their individual log measures sum to \(m\), so the same smallness cutoff applies to all chain terms regardless of the length of the list.
For each positive \(A_h=M(w_h)\) its only conjugate attaining its maximal modulus is \(A_h\) itself.
Equality of the modulus of a conjugate of the product with \(A\) therefore fixes each \(A_h\), giving \(\mathbb Q(A_h)\subseteq\mathbb Q(A)\) and full degree for powers of these Perron units.
The direct sum of actual inequivalent chain constituents in v118 allows projection onto the chosen maximal-degree active \(W\) simultaneously for the product and all factors.
The unmatched case is exactly the estimate of v118, including its range of \(\eta\).
In the matched case the common actual field, primitive reciprocal Perron generator \(\rho>1\), and equality \(D=\deg A=\deg\rho=\deg z\) meet the saturation hypotheses.

If the projection of \([A_h]\) onto \(W\) is nonzero, its chain has a matching term \(z_h\) giving \(W\), of degree \(D\).
An earlier active term of degree \(d'>D\) would yield a projection with orbit at least \(d'\), impossible for a conjugate orbit of size at most \(\deg A_h\le D\).
At each preceding step the blockwise exterior vector therefore has constant coefficients within every oriented block.
Induction with the zero-loss half-product formula of v109 gives \(A_h=M(z_h)^{E_h}\) with a positive integral exponent, independently of the second measure of \(w_h\).
A suitable conjugate of \(z_h\) lies on the matched real class line and its actual field agrees with \(\mathbb Q(\rho)\) by compression.
The torsion there consists of rational signs, so it equals a signed nonzero integral power of \(\rho\).
Full degree of powers and invariance under inversion for units give \(M(z_h)=M(\rho)^{|j_h|}\).
This proves that all contributing factors give positive integer exponents, including repeats.
The remaining factors give \(B\ge1\) with zero projection onto \(W\) even when their other components survive.
The result is \(\rho^k=M(\rho)^eB\) with \(e\ge1\).

The class of \(\rho\) is the selected coordinate in its own root coordinates, obtained from those of \(z\) by a common nonzero scaling.
In the independent model the exterior entry there is one, giving \(k/e=1\).
In the oriented model the kernel of projection of the coordinate space onto the embedded sum-zero space consists of block constants.
On the selected half this requires every other coefficient to be \(1-k/e\); the admissible entries are \(-1,0,1\).
It gives \(k/e=1\) or \(2\), with all coefficients constant on each other block.
In the first case \(M(\rho)\ge\rho\) and \(B\ge1\) force \(M(\rho)=\rho\) and \(B=1\).
In the second case \(B=(\rho^2/M(\rho))^e\), giving \(A\le M(A)=M(\rho)^k\le A^2\).
A reciprocal Perron unit of degree greater than two at the lower endpoint has a single exterior conjugate, its inverse and otherwise neutral conjugates.
Large degree here follows uniformly from bounded-degree finiteness.
Above the lower endpoint \(k=2e\), and strictly below the upper endpoint in that case \(B>1\), so at least one positive factor occurs outside the contributing set.

In the strict interior the other \(b-1\) members of the selected half are all interior and \(b>1\).
Writing \(R,I,J\) as in the claim gives \(0<I\) and \(J<R-I<R\).
Complex conjugation fixes the half through its unique member \(\rho\) at that coordinate, hence its product \(\beta\) is real.
The opposite product is \(\beta^{-1}\) and the products of the other halves have absolute log modulus at most \(J\).
The unique maximum on these \(2u\) halves has multiplicity one.
Transitivity on the halves makes every fiber of the map to their actual products have the same size, hence each size is one.
The positive \(r_1=|\beta|\) differs from \(\beta\) by a rational sign, has degree \(2u<2ub=D\), and is reciprocal and strictly Perron.
The constant exterior signs on every other block give \(\log M(r_1)=R-I+J\).
Thus \(\rho^2/M(\rho)=r_1^2/M(r_1)\) and \(1\le\log M(r_1)/\log r_1<2\).

Using \(e\) copies of the minimal polynomial of \(r_1\) and the old factors supplying \(B\) gives the actual value \(A_1=M(r_1)^eB=r_1^{2e}\).
It is strictly between one and \(A\), has degree \(2u\) by the Perron property, and satisfies \(A_1\le M(A_1)<A_1^2\).
The same cutoff applies at each repetition, and the strict upper bound excludes the growing branch for all sufficiently small \(m\).
The initial \(\rho\) lies in the divisible conjugate group of one original factor by its matching chain term.
Every subsequent primitive line unit has a positive power equal to its current target, and every new half product is a signed product of its conjugates.
This keeps all descendants in that first divisible group, including when the new representing polynomial uses other factors.
Strict decrease of positive integer degrees forces arrival at the lower endpoint, since the construction always preserves the strict inequality at the upper endpoint.
The terminal positive Salem value has degree less than \(\deg A\) and lies below \(A\).

At the original upper endpoint \(M(\rho)=\rho^2\) and full degree gives \(\log M(M(\rho))=2\log M(\rho)\).
Thus the single-input minimal-degree theorem applies to any shrinking sequence there with no increase in the first log measure.
Lower endpoints supply Salem numbers directly and strict interiors supply smaller ones.
Conversely, Salem numbers and the second class in v109 give minimal polynomials with bounded second log measures along any shrinking sequence, using \(M(z)=z^2\) and full degree in the second class.
No boundedness of higher iterates is required.
