## Shared zeros, unoccupied forbidden orders and the uniform estimate

At 2026-11-24T20:15 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v19.md#statement) and both proof sections of C0901 v19, including the unrestricted infimum convention for real inputs and the fixed-\(P\) quantifier in the final limit.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v19.
The simultaneous resultant formula (1), exact multiplicity conclusion, duality (3) and both bounds in (2) follow.

For any allowed \(C\), the supremum of \(\log|G^a C|\) bounds its integral against each dual probability.
Every allowed cyclotomic integral is finite and nonnegative, and an objective of \(-\infty\) imposes no lower bound.
Haar has finite objective \(m(G)\).
For the upper bound, truncate the objective and finitely many allowed logarithms from below.
Strict separation gives nonnegative weights with positive coefficient on the objective by the truncated Haar moments.
If weights giving a target strictly above the dual supremum were unavailable, the corresponding truncated constraints would admit measures for every finite stage.
Taking a weak limit, first testing at each fixed truncation and then decreasing the truncations, gives a dual measure with objective at least that target, a contradiction.
All truncated functions are even, so symmetrized point masses give the same pointwise bounds.
Rounding the finitely many weights upwards at exponent \(a\) adds bounded log supremum and produces degree \(O(a)\) for the fixed data and tolerance.
The resulting coefficient-norm loss is \(O(\log a)\) by Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz.
These bounds give the unrestricted real-input infimum rate and the same rate under any degree budget with \(W(a)/a\to\infty\).
Finite degree allows only finitely many cyclotomic products, and for integral \(Q\) the unrestricted coefficient one-norms are positive integers, so the minimum in (1) is attained.

For \(G=Q\), finite objective excludes all atoms at roots of \(Q\), including shared forbidden roots, because \(\log|Q|\) is bounded above.
The allowed constraints exclude atoms at every other torsion point outside the forbidden set.
Choose a common modulus divisible by every order represented in \(Q\) or \(B\).
Raising to an exponent \(j\) coprime to it permutes the primitive roots at each of those orders and is unramified there.
Therefore \(Q(X^j)/Q(X)\) is an integral monic cyclotomic product with no forbidden factor, also at shared orders where the multiplicities cancel exactly.
The log of that quotient has a finite nonnegative integral, so the power-image integral of \(\log|Q|\) is finite and at least the original objective.

In the weak limit of the averaged power images, each nontorsion point contributes Haar mass by equidistribution on every coprime residue progression.
An atom at a primitive forbidden root contributes its mass uniformly on that primitive orbit by surjectivity of reduction of the unit classes.
Only forbidden orbits disjoint from \(Q\) can have positive weight.
Testing first with continuous upper truncations of \(\log|Q|\) and then decreasing them bounds the original objective by the Haar and disjoint-orbit averages.
The Haar integral is zero and each remaining orbit average is \(\varphi(b)^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_b,Q)|\).
This argument assigns no logarithmic resultant to an orbit on which \(Q\) vanishes.
The uniform measure on each disjoint forbidden orbit is feasible since its logarithmic averages for allowed factors are normalized logs of nonzero absolute integer resultants.
Together with Haar these tests attain the maximum in (1).
If \(Q=\prod_{b\in B}\Phi_b^{t_b}\), all prescribed orbits are occupied, the maximum is zero and the additional multiplier gives them no further multiplicity.

For \(G=P\prod_{b\in B}\Phi_b^{t_b}\), a finite dual objective makes \(\int\log|P|\) and each forbidden logarithmic integral finite individually because all the summands are bounded above and every \(t_b>0\).
There are now no torsion atoms anywhere, so the coprime power averages converge weakly to Haar.
For each \(b\in B\), apply the quotient and upper-truncation argument to \(\Phi_b\) to obtain \(I_b\le0\).
For any allowed \(n\) the same argument can use a modulus also divisible by \(n\), giving \(I_n=0\) together with the dual lower bound.
If every one of the first \(M\) integrals is at least \(-M^{-2}\), the finite stability estimate bounds the objective by \(m(P)+CdM^{-1/64}\), since the forbidden terms are nonpositive.
If the test fails at some \(b\le M\), that order is forbidden and \(t_b I_b<-d\).
This offsets the entire possible excess \(d\log2\) in the pointwise bound on \(\log|P|\).
Haar gives the matching lower bound \(m(P)\).
The compulsory degree \(a\sum_{b\in B}t_b\varphi(b)\) leaves a divergent budget per copy for the allowed cofactor at fixed \(B,t\).
For fixed \(P\), choosing \(M=\lfloor\sqrt{\min_b t_b/d}\rfloor\) once it is at least two proves convergence of \(\Gamma_B(P;t)\) to \(m(P)\), uniformly over finite nonempty \(B\) avoiding the roots and all the stated profiles.
The defining limit in \(a\) is taken for fixed input data.
