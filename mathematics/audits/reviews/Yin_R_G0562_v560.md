## Aggregate bounds, exact recovery and exterior coefficient estimates

At 2026-11-19T02:00 UTC I independently reviewed the [fixed Claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v428.md#statement) for Sheng Yin, including its complete argument, [implementation](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/versions/v3/square_pairs.py) and three documented controls.
I received the fixed texts and no development discussion, author explanation or previous conclusion about this argument.
I had reviewed adjacent work on prescribed contacts and had read the signed-row theorem used in the Salem specialization.

For \(V=Y^bA(1/Y)\), the square of each row modulo two is its evaluation at \(Y^2\).
The two terms defining \(2K\) therefore agree modulo two, have degree at most \(4b\), and have one-norm at most \(H^3\) separately.
When \(K=0\), a zero or pole of the rational function \(f=-\varepsilon A/V\) in \(\mathbb C^\times\) would have \(2^j\) distinct preimages under repeated squaring, all zeros or all poles of \(f\).
This forces \(f=Y^e\) for an integer \(e\), since its nonzero constant multiplier must equal its square.
The equation \(\delta=\delta^{ne}\) excludes a nontorsion contact for every \(n\ge2\).

For \(K\ne0\), expansion of the [square identity](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v428.md#square) cancels its two terms containing \(X^2V(X^{2n})V(X^n)^2\).
On its factored right-hand side, both \(G_n(X)\) and \(G_n(X^2)\) vanish to order at least \(t\) at \(\delta\), where squaring and raising to the \(n\)-th power have nonzero derivatives.
Thus \(K\) vanishes to order at least \(t\) at \(\beta=\delta^n\).
Both row values at each contact are nonzero, giving \(\delta=f(\beta)\), equality of the fields, and equivariant recovery of \(\delta\) from any conjugate of \(\beta\).
If two pairs used the same irreducible factor they could therefore be conjugated to the same \(\beta,\delta\), and equality of the powers of this nontorsion \(\delta\) would force the exponents to agree.
Consequently the total multiplicity-weighted degree is at most \(4b\).
The height power law in the common field gives \(\log M(\min(\beta))=n m_\delta\).
Each remaining nonzero integral polynomial factor has measure at least one, so multiplicativity and \(M(K)\le\|K\|_1\le H^3\) give the simultaneous measure bound.

The local unit denominator makes the graph equation equivalent, with all orders preserved, to \(X=f(X^n)\).
At the first double zero differentiation gives \(n\beta f'(\beta)=u\) for \(u=f(\beta)\).
Dividing by the nonzero derivative yields the stated unique field element for \(n\).
It must be an integer at least two, and the [three final tests](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v428.md#inverse) then impose the correct power and the value and derivative at \(u^2\).
They suffice for both double zeros, with \(u\) and its inverse integral because their \(n\)-th powers are units.

In the [exterior estimate](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v428.md#house), reversing the independent variable introduces the minus sign in equation (3), and removing a common monomial has no effect on that equation at the nonzero nodes.
The remaining constant coefficients have positive integral maximum \(C_0\).
To check the constants, divide the derivative equation by \(s\) at \(T=z^2\).
Its linear coefficient costs at most \((2n+1)C_1x^2\) when solving for \(b_0\).
The rest costs at most \((4n+1)Hx^4\), since \(j|T|^{j-2}\le2\) for \(j\ge2\) and each row has one-norm at most \(H\).
The value equation and \(|s|\le4\) then bound \(C_0\) by \(24n(C_1x^2+Hx^4)\).
At \(T=z\), subtracting \(n\) times the value equation in the derivative sum leaves the constant term and a tail weighted by \(j-1\).
Since \((j-1)x^{j-2}\le1\) for \(x\le1/2\), solving for \(b_1z\) bounds its magnitude by \((2n+1)(C_0+Hx^2)\).
Substitution into the value equation gives \(C_1x\le24n(C_0+Hx^2)\).
The combined coefficient of \(C_0\) is \(576n^2x\), at most \(1/2\) when \(x\le1/(1152n^2)\).
After absorption the coefficient of \(Hx^3\) is at most \(1152n^2+48n\le1200n^2\).
In the complementary range \(H\ge1\) proves the second line of (2), and the first line also implies that weaker bound throughout its own range.
Combining it with \(H<2^{n/8}\) gives the stated strict inequality for \(\log\rho\).
For a Salem number every power has full degree because the fiber of its unique exterior positive power has only one conjugate and all fibers of the orbit map have the same size.
The inverse pairs in the two levels then meet the negative-endpoint phase condition of the [signed-row theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests).
Their two multiplicities are two and their measures are \(m_\delta,2m_\delta\), giving the matching least-norm limit \(3m_\delta\) at either sign for \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(n)\).
