## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T13:11 a separate reviewer for Yin checked the complete [submitted proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v377.md#decision-good-index-submitted) and its direct mathematical interfaces.
The reviewer took no part in developing the claim and received the fixed text without exploration notes, proof-generation dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict.
After deriving the findings below, the reviewer compared the earlier reviews of the finite window, the nested-disk estimate and the mixed-characteristic derivative at their cited interfaces.

The assumption \(\ell\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\) puts all the conjugates in an unramified extension with distinct residues, so the collision depths of the powered conjugates are integers.
In one positive-depth collision disk, the residues of the original conjugates lie in a single fiber of the \(p\)-th power map on units.
Their \(k\)-th powers are distinct because \(p\nmid k\), and the fiber has at most \(p\) elements.
The two rows are coprime, so their ratio is defined at every marked point and realizes these distinct labels up to sign.

For a path between two points with different labels, the difference of Gauss valuations for \(U-\lambda V\) and a generic pencil becomes positive at one end and stays zero at the other.
At a boundary where it is zero, proportional reduced rows would give the same nonzero reduced polynomial up to a scalar for both combinations.
They would then have equal slopes on the two incident path segments, given by the degree on the exterior segment and the vanishing orders in the relevant residue directions on interior segments.
This also handles the joining disk, where both segments can run inward.
Thus every path has a nonconstant disk.
The maximal disks chosen from all pairs cover at least \(m-1\) marks, since a path disk contains at least one endpoint as a marked center.
If all these disks are separable, summing \(2n_B\ge m_B+2\) gives \(2n_D\ge m+1\).
An inseparable disk has \(n_B\ge\ell\), which suffices under \(2\ell\ge\min(d,p)+1\).

On the unit segment ending at depth \(s\), the first normalization makes the two rows integral at depth \(s-1\).
After the final scaling their degree-\(j\) coefficients therefore have valuation at least \(j-a_D\).
The derivative terms in the critical polynomial use row indices summing to \(j+1\), and the terms with index sum \(j\) acquire the factor of valuation \(s\ge1\).
Consequently its degree-\(j\) coefficient has valuation at least \(j+1-2a_D\).
For a nonconstant reduction with inseparable degree \(\ell^t\), the unit coefficient of the twisted quotient at degree \(\ell^t\) bounds the content valuation of the critical polynomial by \(t\).
The \(m\) different characteristic-zero double jets thus give \(m+1\le2a_D+t\).
Here \(t\le\log_\ell n_D\le n_D\le a_D\), including the separable case \(t=0\).
The count applies to a single marked descendant as well.

For a constant disk, write \(b\) for the number of maximal nonconstant integer-depth descendants and \(R_1\) for the pencil roots in their enlarged open disks.
The marks have integral mutual valuations, so those open disks are disjoint, remain inside the starting disk and contain exactly the same marks as their respective descendants.
The pencil-root count throughout each incoming unit segment is bounded by the count in its enlarged open disk.
Hence \(\sum_B(m_B+1)\le3R_1\).
If \(u\ge2\) marks remain, every nonconstant disk on their connecting tree has fractional depth and splits at most one edge when deleted.
The path result puts distinct ends in different components, giving at least \(u-1\) deleted disks.
At each such depth every mark in the disk is in the zero direction around its center.
A generic pencil has a root in a finite nonzero direction there, including for an inseparable quotient, by surjectivity over the algebraic closure and lifting a reduced linear factor with multiplicity.
These open directions are disjoint from each other and from the enlarged descendant disks.
Indeed, intersection gives containment, and a larger off-tree direction would then contain a mark or the smaller disk's center mark.
In the other possible containment by an enlarged descendant disk, its integral boundary depth is strictly smaller than the fractional depth, forcing the remaining center mark into that descendant.
The exclusions on pencil residues are finite because the nonconstant disks occur only at Taylor-minimum breakpoints on finitely many marked rays.
This gives \(u-1\le n_D-R_1\), also for \(u\le1\).
Adding the counts yields \(m\le n_D+2R_1+1-b\le3n_D\) if \(b\ge1\).
For \(b=0\), \(m\le1+n_D\) and \(m\ge2\) give the same bound.

All marking groups at integer depth \(s\) stay disjoint on the open incoming segments of depths \(s-1<t<s\), again by integrality of the mutual valuations.
Summing the squared pencil-root counts over these segments charges each root pair by at most its mutual depth.
The local intersection identity bounds the resulting sum by \(v_\ell H\), including when the rows have content or their degrees change in the pencil.
Cauchy's inequality on a segment of length one gives at least \(a_D^2\ge m_D^2/9\), whereas its index contribution is \(\binom{m_D}{2}\).
This proves the coefficient \(2/9\).
Under the large-characteristic condition the segment contributes at least \(n_D^2\ge (m_D+1)^2/4\), sufficient for the coefficient \(1/2\).

For classes \(0,1\) the degree cap gives row degrees at most \(16d+1,16d\), respectively.
The finite-window witness has extra logarithmic measure at most \(2p\,m(P)\), so substituting the good-prime lower bound for \(\log H\) into the resultant-to-measure estimate gives the displayed finite inequality.
Division by \(2p+2\) and \(p\asymp(\log(3d))^4\) make its error tend to zero, while the coefficient on \(\log I_p^{\rm good}/(pd)\) tends to \(1/288\).
The complete proof supports both local bounds and the stated conditional lower bound.
Its good-prime hypothesis excludes the entire index in the cited Salem example, where three divides the original discriminant.
An application to a uniform gap would still need to establish the required index growth for a suitable choice of prime on small-measure inputs.
