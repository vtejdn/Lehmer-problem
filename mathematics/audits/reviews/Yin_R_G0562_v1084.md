## The logarithmic rotation estimate suffices for exact positive profiles

At 2026-11-24T20:46 an agent outside the development of this proof independently checked all five fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on this argument.

For an interval of \(k\) consecutive positive integers, the contribution of \(p^i\) to the valuation of \(\binom{m+k}{k}\) is its count of multiples on the interval minus \(\lfloor k/p^i\rfloor\), which is always nonnegative.
If the largest \(p\)-valuation of an interval member is \(h\), each level \(\lfloor\log_p k\rfloor<i\le h\) contributes one.
The lower levels are covered by \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)\), proving the divisibility.
For a dyadic interval from \(1\) to \(2^j\), its lower half divides the lower-half lcm and its upper half divides that lcm times the middle binomial.
Iteration gives a logarithmic bound \(O(2^j)\), hence \(O(k)\) for arbitrary \(k\).
On a subinterval of length \(k\le\delta N\) in \([N,2N)\), monotonicity of \(k\log(2\mathrm e N/k)\) gives \(\log D=O(\delta\log(2/\delta)N)\) also for the shorter last interval.

For \(q\mid D\), \(\|Dx\|\le(D/q)\|qx\|\) gives \(H(qx)\le\log(D/q)+H(Dx)\).
There are \(O(1/\delta)\) interval lcms per dyadic block for \(N\) sufficiently large at fixed \(\delta\).
Since \(q\ge N\) and \(H\ge0\), after subtraction of \(u=C\delta\log(2/\delta)\) the positive excess in that block has integral \(O(K/(\delta N))\).
Summing the positive excesses over \(N=2^jT\) pays \(u\) just once and gives \(u+O(K/(\delta T))\) for the integral of the tail supremum.
Taking \(T\to\infty\) first proves (2).
A finite uniform integral bound excludes every rational point with positive mass.
The full supremum is integrable by adding finitely many individual \(H(qx)/q\) to one integrable tail, and the nonincreasing tails converge to zero almost everywhere.

At irrational \(x\), the minimum of \(\|qx\|\) over \(1\le q\le N\) tends to zero, and no fixed finite set of denominators has zero error, so any chosen minimizer tends to infinity.
The nearest integer numerator is coprime to this denominator because reducing a common divisor would provide a smaller error at an admissible denominator.
For \(1\le m<q\), with \(b\) nearest to \(mx\), this coprimality excludes \(qb=mp\).
The integer determinant then gives \(1\le q\|mx\|+m\|qx\|\le(q+m)\|mx\|\).
Differences in any block of at most \(q\) successive points therefore give separation \(1/(2q)\).
After removing one point nearest to zero, the number of remaining points in an arc \(\|nx\|<y<1/2\) is at most \(4qy\), since a separated list inside the full arc of length \(2y\) contains at most \(1+4qy\) points.
If the arc contains any points the removed point is among them.
Integrating from \(J\ge\log2\) bounds the retained tail by \(4qe^{-J}\) per block.
Each discarded point costs at most \(H(qx)\) by the original minimizing property, including in the final incomplete block.
The block count divided by \(N\) is at most \(2/q\), giving both constants in (3).
This reasoning includes singleton blocks when \(q=1\).
The minimizer is measurable and \(H(q_Nx)/q_N\) tends to zero almost everywhere and in \(L^1(\rho)\) by the integrable supremum just obtained.

For an atomless probability, the exceptional pairs \(k(x-y)\in\mathbb Z\) have zero product mass for each fixed nonzero integer \(k\).
Integrating the bounded exponential Cesàro average in the difference variable gives convergence to zero of the mean squared Fourier moments.
Cauchy–Schwarz gives convergence of the mean absolute values, and uniform trigonometric approximation gives the stated continuous-test conclusion.
For \(g(x)=\log|1-e^{2\pi i x}|\) the lower chord bound \(4\|x\|\le|1-e^{2\pi i x}|\) makes the truncation error at most \((H-J)_+\).
By (3) its limiting upper mean after integration is at most \(8e^{-J}\).
The fixed continuous truncation has mean absolute deviation tending to zero, and its Haar mean tends to the Haar mean zero of \(g\).
Taking \(J\to\infty\) establishes the absolute Cesàro assertion (4).

For the feasible measure in the first assertion, boundedness above and the given lower bounds make every cyclotomic logarithm integrable.
In particular there are no torsion atoms, and the existing allowed-test argument rules out the nontorsion atoms.
The power-average bound for cyclotomic inputs applied to one allowed test now gives zero for its integral, since every exceptional orbit has zero mass.
All power logarithm integrals \(A(n)\) are therefore the finite sums over the forbidden divisors of \(n\), periodic with the claimed period and uniformly bounded below.
The upper chord estimate implies \(H(nx)\le\log(2\pi)-g(nx)\), giving the uniform integral bound required in (2) and (4).
The Cesàro mean of the absolute values of a finite periodic sequence is its average over one period, so (4) forces all \(A(n)=0\).
Möbius inversion and the unrestricted moment theorem yield the stated conjugation symmetrization.
The proof uses no Fourier decay, density or separate regularity assumption in this finite-integral case.

For the prescribed profile, writing \(a=uj\) with the least common denominator \(u\) gives the exact bijection between the specified cofactors and the compulsory product \(\prod_{b\in B}\Phi_b^{j u t_b}\) times an additional cofactor avoiding \(B\).
Zeros of \(P\) at forbidden roots do not alter this cofactor specification.
Every factor logarithm in \(\log|G|\) is bounded above.
Since each \(ut_b>0\), finiteness of the objective forces all the forbidden logarithmic integrals to be finite individually.
The only even feasible probability with finite objective is consequently Haar, whose objective is \(m(G)=u m(P)\).
The dual formula applied at exponent \(j\), then divided by \(u\), proves (1).
Subtracting the compulsory degree \(a\sum_b t_b\varphi(b)\) leaves a budget whose ratio to \(j\) tends to infinity.
The empty set uses \(u=1\), and for a nonempty profile the argument uses the stated positivity and rationality of all its entries.

For the finite-entropy residual, simple zeros ensure a positive exponential moment of \(|v_s|\) in Haar measure, also after every positive power map.
For fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the integrated Young bound on \(\{f>M\}\) has one term controlled by the tail of \(f\log(1+f/\epsilon)\), which is integrable, and another uniformly bounded by a constant times \(\epsilon\).
Taking \(M\to\infty\) and then \(\epsilon\to0\) proves the stated uniform tail estimate, as well as integrability for each fixed power.
For the truncated density, \(L^2\) approximation tests against functions \(v_s(z^j)\) of fixed \(L^2\) norm with mean zero and Fourier support on the multiples of \(j\).
Every fixed trigonometric polynomial therefore gives zero integral for all sufficiently large \(j\).
The integrals against \(f\) tend to zero along all positive integers.
One may choose pairwise coprime integers \(1\bmod L\) successively so that all finitely many new subproducts are large.
The two- and three-difference polynomials are allowed and have modulus one at all removed atoms, including at their canceled zeros.
The resulting opposite bounds force every residual cyclotomic integral to vanish.
Normalizing the positive-mass residual gives its symmetrization as Haar, and a zero residual gives the result directly.
The claim leaves open the general classification of a diffuse residual with a nonintegrable forbidden logarithm.
