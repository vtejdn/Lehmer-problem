# Review of the Lehmer infimum indexed by normal subfields

<a id="finding"></a>
## Mixed radicals, record units and the rank of the normal-field contribution

At 2026-11-24T23:46 UTC an agent outside the development of v81 independently reviewed its [final statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v81.md#statement) and both proof sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed text and scope without development dialogue or previous findings on v81.
The infimum formula allows arbitrary intersections and inclusions of fields, the product selection works for every nontorsion algebraic unit, and the faithful-constituent criterion and rank recursion hold.
The strict-degree-record assertion in the final text explicitly concerns nontorsion algebraic units.

**Normal subfield averages.**
For a finite expression one may take a common finite normal field \(N\) containing its terms, their fields and the chosen twists and radicals.
Every resulting number is a unit because a positive power and its inverse are integral.
On multiplicative classes modulo torsion, averaging over \(\operatorname{Gal}(N/E)\) is the norm divided by the group order.
For normal \(E,F\subset N\), restriction to \(F\) has image \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/(E\cap F))\), with equal restriction fibers.
The intersection \(E\cap F\) is itself normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
Hence the average of a class from \(B(F)\) is that class when \(F\subseteq E\) and is zero when \(F\not\subseteq E\).
This calculation uses no disjointness.
It also applies to the rational spans, since taking a root in the group modulo torsion divides the class by its exponent.
Grouping the finitely many summands by distinct fields and projecting to an inclusion-minimal nonzero field term isolates that term.
This proves the claimed directness, including for nested fields.
For the log functions at a fixed complex embedding, conditioning on the restriction to \(E\) averages over the same cosets.
The conditional log mean for \(B(F)\) is zero when \(F\not\subseteq E\), and the function for \(B(E)\) is constant on those cosets.
Interchanging the fields handles \(F\subsetneq E\) and gives the stated orthogonality.

**Degree and height in the product selection.**
For a nontorsion mixed element \(y\), take an inclusion-minimal field \(F_0\) in the support of its class.
For \(J=\operatorname{Gal}(N/F_0)\) let \(e\) be the number of distinct conjugates in \(Jy\).
The product \(z\) of this orbit belongs to \(F_0\), and its class is \(eP_{F_0}[y]\), because every orbit point is counted \(|J|/e\) times in the group average.
The projected term is nonzero and its proper normal projections vanish, so \(z\) is a nontorsion member of \(B(F_0)\).
Normality makes all \(J\)-orbits on the full set of conjugates of \(y\) have the same cardinality \(e\).
Every conjugate of \(z\) is a product over one of these orbits, and coincidences among products can only reduce the degree.
Thus \(\deg z\le(\deg y)/e\), while multiplication gives \(h(z)\le e h(y)\).
This proves \(m(z)\le m(y)\) with all radical degrees and torsion twists included.
The reverse bound on the infimum follows by inclusion of each original group.
For a fixed \(F\) a bounded measure bounds the coefficients of its units' minimal polynomials at degree at most \([F:\mathbb Q]\), giving the claimed minimum when nonempty.
The empty nontorsion case has infimum \(+\infty\), and a finite family with at least one nontorsion unit attains the least of its finite minima.

For a chosen Galois-stable divisible subgroup, its classes form a rational subspace, and the orbit product has class in the selected subgroup at \(F_0\).
The stated torsion convention therefore puts the product itself there.
The same conclusion follows even if such subgroups are initially taken as literal divisible subgroups of algebraic numbers stable under the absolute Galois action.
Indeed let \(u\) be a nontorsion member of one of them, fix a prime \(\ell\), and choose members \(v_j\) with \(v_j^{\ell^j}=u\).
Since \(h(v_j)=h(u)/\ell^j>0\), bounded-degree finiteness gives \([\mathbb Q(u,v_j):\mathbb Q(u)]\to\infty\).
The distinct relative conjugates divided by \(v_j\) then give unboundedly many distinct elements of that subgroup in \(\mu_{\ell^j}\).
Nestedness of the cyclic groups of \(\ell\)-power roots of unity implies that the subgroup contains all of them.
This holds at every prime, while a purely torsion subgroup contributes no positive measure.

**Arbitrary inputs and strict records.**
Starting with the actual splitting field \(L\) of \(w\), the identity projection at \(L\) guarantees some inclusion-minimal normal \(F\) with \(P_F[w]\ne0\).
For every proper normal \(E\subsetneq F\), nested averaging gives \(P_EP_F=P_E\), hence \(P_EP_F[w]=0\).
The same orbit product now realizes a nonempty selection of the distinct conjugates of \(w\) in \(B(F)\), with no increase of total measure.
For a nonidentity normal subgroup of the splitting group, faithfulness on the roots implies it moves at least one root and transitivity makes its orbits have size at least two everywhere.
A nonzero projection at a proper normal subfield would therefore produce a positive measure at strictly smaller degree, no greater than the measure of \(w\).
Either a smaller value or equality there contradicts the two conditions defining a strict degree record.
Thus all the required norms of such a record unit in \(L\) are torsion.
For the unrestricted infimum every integer polynomial of positive logarithmic measure below \(\log2\) has a nontorsion irreducible unit factor of no greater measure after discarding monomial factors.
This uses additivity and the integer leading and constant coefficients of the nonzero factors.
The known degree-ten Salem example lies below that threshold and identifies the unrestricted infimum with the one over units.

**Ranks and complex conjugation.**
In a finite Galois field all infinite places have the same local degree.
Under a fixed embedding their permutation representation is induced from the trivial character of \(\langle c\rangle\): the left and right coset conventions for embeddings are interchanged by inversion.
Dirichlet's logarithmic embedding identifies the real span of unit classes with its sum-zero hyperplane, and the transitive permutation representation contains precisely one trivial copy.
On complexifying, Frobenius reciprocity gives the multiplicity of each nontrivial irreducible complex representation \(U\) as \(\dim U^{\langle c\rangle}\).
For each normal subgroup \(J\), its invariant subspace in \(U\) is a \(G_F\)-submodule, so averaging over \(J\) is identity when \(J\subseteq\ker U\) and zero otherwise.
Intersecting the kernels of all nonidentity normal subgroup averages thus leaves exactly the faithful complex constituents that occur.
All these averaging operators are rational, so the intersection commutes with extension of scalars.
A nonzero complex dimension supplies a nonzero rational vector and, upon clearing denominators, an actual unit satisfying all the norm conditions.
For a faithful complex irreducible, a noncentral involution cannot act as a scalar and therefore has a positive-dimensional fixed space.
A nonidentity central involution must act as \(-I\) by Schur's lemma and faithfulness.
The center acts faithfully by roots of unity and hence is cyclic.
For \(F=\mathbb Q\) the removed trivial copy is the whole permutation representation and the unit rank is zero.

Over \(\mathbb Q\), decompose the unit module for a normal \(E\) into rational irreducibles and group all copies by their exact kernels.
If \(J=\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\), its invariant subspace is exactly the rational span of the \(K\)-units: every invariant rational class equals its norm average, and the converse follows by inclusion.
Inside this subspace, zero averages to every proper normal subfield of \(K\) select exactly the irreducible summands with kernel \(J\).
This identifies their aggregate dimension with \(\operatorname{rank}B(K)\), without identifying individual complex constituents with units or treating their dimensions as rational multiplicities.
Summing over kernels gives the displayed Dirichlet-rank recursion and permits subtraction over all proper normal subfields.
