## Outcome of the proof review

For a fixed quartic Salem seed, vanishing of the outer determinant coefficient gives the asserted \(\tfrac32p\log\rho-o_P(p)\) lower bound.
The proof excludes nonzero rational width-three double-root graphs for every sufficiently large positive integer \(p\).

**Two-node identity.**
Reversal of a width-three graph interchanges its two reversed rows, so one of the sum and difference projections has a nonzero first row \(A\).
Pairing opposite coefficients expresses \(A\pm A^*\) in the stated forms with \(E,O\) of degree at most one.
The local factor \((1+\varepsilon x)x^p(x^p+1)/2\) is nonzero at both nodes.
Also \(dS/d\log x=p(x^p-x^{-p})\ne0\), so division by that factor and differentiation with respect to \(S\) preserve the double-zero condition.
The powered traces are conjugate in the original quadratic trace field and take distinct values \(S_1>2\) and \(-2<S_2<2\).
A nonzero rational affine-linear \(O\) therefore has no zero at either trace, while \(O=0\) would force \(E=0\) from its two value equations and then \(A=0\).
For \(R(S)=(aS+b)/(cS+d)\), its derivative is \((ad-bc)/(cS+d)^2\) and its secant slope is \((ad-bc)/((cS_1+d)(cS_2+d))\).
Their product identity also holds when \(ad-bc=0\), proving exactly the necessary condition (2) for the two local branches of \(B\).

**Uniformity on the circle.**
The identities \(dj/dS=(1-j^2)^2/(8j)\) and \(d k/d\log x=(1-k^2)/2\) give the displayed derivative of \(kj\).
At the exterior root its two terms have sizes \(O_P(y^{-2})\) and \(\asymp_P(py)^{-1}\), respectively, since \(k^2\ne1\) and \(1-j^2=4y/(y+1)^2\).
At the circle root the terms have ratio in absolute value, second over first, equal to \((1+d^2)|t|/(p|d|(1+t^2))\).
This is at most \((1+d^2)/(2p|d|)\) for all real nonzero \(t\).
It gives the stated lower bound \(|B'_2|\gg_P1+t^2\) uniformly, including \(|t|\to0\) and \(|t|\to\infty\).
At the same time \(B_2=-dt\) and \(B_1\) stays bounded, so (2) bounds \((1+t^2)/(py)\) above by a fixed multiple of \((1+t^2)/y^2\).
This contradicts \(y/p\to\infty\).
The finitely many signs can share one fixed-seed onset, and nontorsion of the unit-circle conjugate ensures \(x^p\ne\pm1\) for all \(p\).

**Cancellation and the height bound.**
The value equation divided by \(y^5\) bounds \(u_5+\rho v_5\) by \(O_P(H/y)\).
Subtracting \(5p\) times that same equation from the Euler derivative before division leaves \(\rho v_5\) as the only top term, bounding it by \(O_P(pH/y)\).
Reversal gives the same bounds at the other end.
If both top vectors vanish, the original nonzero determinant already permits the width-four theorem.
Otherwise their integral \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix has rank one, and a primitive annihilating pair obtained from one nonzero column has coefficients \(O_P(pH/y)\).
The determinant of the polynomial rows \(R,J\) is \(Y^5D\ne0\), so this combination is nonzero.
If \(a=\pm b\), reversal interchanges \(R,J\) and the combination is an eigenvector.
Its vanished top entries then force its bottom entries to vanish as well.
After dividing the rows by \(Y\), their graph has lost only the factor \(X^p\), coprime to \(P\), contradicting width three.
Hence \(a^2-b^2\ne0\), and direct expansion cancels the mixed Laurent terms and gives determinant \((a^2-b^2)D\ne0\).

The new integral rows have degree at most four, their graph retains \(P^2\), and their norm is at most \(O_P(pH^2/y)\).
The width-four estimate has no coprimality requirement and is uniform over these rows for a fixed seed.
Its vanishing error after division by \(p\) uses the fixed-seed bound for common differences of the multiplicatively independent units \(\rho,\eta\), valid over all integer powers. [Collision estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v246.md#collisions) · [Corvaja–Zannier, Proposition 2](../literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd)
Taking logarithms absorbs \(\log p\) into \(o_P(p)\) and gives the claimed coefficient rate.
For the two classified exterior traces, \(8/5+5/8\) lies strictly below both, so \(\rho>8/5>2^{2/3}\).
The sign change on rows at odd \(p\) preserves the height and preserves vanishing of the outer coefficient.
Thus the height cutoff excludes this case eventually for all eligible seeds, while the case of a nonzero outer coefficient remains open.
