## The abstract prefix theorem and its Pisot consequence

The proof establishes the three prefix alternatives with absolute constants, the eventual existence of an odd coefficient on the stated Pisot branch, and the asserted bounds on its position.

### Moving indices and the half-integral test

After diagonal extraction, the relation subgroup in \(\mathbb Z^t\) is saturated because eventual vanishing of \(k(a\cdot n)\) for a fixed nonzero integer \(k\) is equivalent to eventual vanishing of \(a\cdot n\).
Its rank is less than \(t\), since a positive tested index never vanishes.
One may therefore use the dual of the free quotient, a connected torus, for the pushforward limit.
Each relation takes value one throughout the eventual supports, and their countable intersection still has full limit measure.
For a nonzero quotient character, symmetry handles either sign of \(a\cdot n\), and \(0<|a\cdot n|<D\) eventually.
Its integer coefficient against \(2\omega\) differs from that against \(2\omega^+\) by at most twice the negative mass, so weak convergence gives eventual equality with the integer limit.

To test an annihilating Laurent polynomial one may divide its coefficients by their common divisor, so at least one coefficient is odd.
Distinct quotient characters give eventually distinct exponents after substitution.
Translation of their span to \([0,o(D)]\) allows the last odd coefficient to be shifted precisely to index \(D\), regardless of the ordering of the lifted characters.
Every resulting index is in \((0,2D)\).
Terms below \(D\) contribute integers to the integral against \(2\omega\), the term at \(D\) contributes a half-odd-integer, and terms above \(D\) contribute integers by evenness of their coefficients.
The total has modulus at least \(1/2\) because all moments are real.
The error from discarding the negative part is at most twice its mass times the fixed coefficient absolute sum.
Squaring and dividing by the mass of \(2\omega^+\), which tends to two, yields the stated lower bound \(1/8\) in the limit.
The index shift may vary since its modulus on the circle is one.

### Fourier classification and uniform prefix length

The recorded Sanders corollary treats general locally compact abelian groups, so it applies also when the generated discrete group has rank two.
Every nonsingleton subgroup coset in its representation transforms back to a character multiple of Haar measure on the subgroup annihilator.
Choosing one nonzero exponent in the subgroup puts that annihilator in the zero set of \(1-X^a\), a Haar-null set even when the annihilator is disconnected.
The sum of singleton terms is an integer Laurent density.
Uniqueness of Lebesgue decomposition transfers positivity and reflection symmetry to its two parts.
If the density vanished, the product of the characters' binomials would annihilate the entire remaining measure.
This product is nonzero in the integral Laurent domain, contradicting the parity test.

The mean of the density is thus one or two.
At mean two, an integer-vector specialization separating all supported exponents and zero preserves their coefficients, symmetry and nonnegativity, so the one-variable mean-two theorem leaves at most one opposite pair of nonconstant terms.
At mean one the other integer coefficients vanish, because a nonconstant character cannot have one phase on the open set where the nonnegative density is positive.
The remaining positive probability has integer moments of modulus at most one.
Every character with nonzero moment is almost surely a constant sign, so these characters form a subgroup and their signs multiply.
These arguments permit rank one or two and do not require the tested indices to have a fixed ratio.

A failed uniform local assertion would give \(D\ge i\), negative mass at most \(1/i\), and a violation at positive indices bounded by \(D/i\) after decreasing the candidate fraction.
Pass to a fixed violation type.
Distinct positive \(r<s\) give nonzero, distinct, nonopposite quotient characters, including when \(s=2r\).
If both coefficients are nonzero, the subgroup case forces the sign product at \(s-r,s+r\) and \(+1\) at \(2r\).
All three indices are positive \(o(D)\), hence their integral values agree eventually with the limiting coefficients.
The single-index test excludes coefficients with modulus greater than one.
With the least nonzero \(r\), repeatedly subtracting \(r\) from each larger nonzero index forces divisibility by \(r\).
If a second such index exists, \(2r\le L\) and its coefficient is one.
The sum rule from \(r,kr\), \(k\ge2\), now fills every multiple up to \(L\) with the required sign power.
Zero or one supported index instead gives the constant or binomial model exactly through \(L\).

### Transfer to the Pisot branch

The denominator lift has constant one and \(2B^\sharp=\Pi J_q\) by the trace identity.
The endpoint equation \(C(2)=(1-q)B(2)\) gives \(q-1\mid C(2)\), with \(0<C(2)\le2\cdot4^h\), and the branch derivative gives \(\theta-q\to0\) for fixed \(j=2\) and \(q\to\infty\).
If \(\Pi\) vanished modulo two, cancellation of \(1-z^2\) and injectivity of \(C\mapsto C(z+z^{-1})\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) would imply \(C=0\bmod2\).
Then \(C/2\) is monic, so the primitive nonmonic factor in the earlier multiplier theorem is one, and the multiplier \(G=1\) contradicts its divergence.
This justifies eventual existence independently of the position of preceding even coefficients.

The inverse series of \(E+z^D O/2\) agrees with \(E^{-1}-z^D O/(2E^2)\) through \(2D-1\).
Multiplication by \(B^\sharp\) with constant one keeps the coefficients integral before \(D\), half-odd-integral at \(D\), and half-integral afterwards in the required range.
The symmetric signed measure for \(J_q\) has mass one and negative mass \(1/[2(q-1)]\).
If \(D\ge\eta h\) along a subsequence, the abstract theorem gives a common prefix of length comparable to \(h\).
For the polynomial model \(1+\epsilon z^r\) the nonnegative real part gives a positive mass-one representing measure.
For the inverse model the average of \(1\) and the positive kernel \((1+\epsilon z^r)/(1-\epsilon z^r)\) has the same property.
Thus every coefficient at a strictly positive *index* of \(J_q-J_*\) is a moment of a uniformly bounded measure, including negative-valued coefficients.

One may choose the cutoff degree \(L\) larger than \(2h+2+\max(\deg U,\deg V)\) and still \(O(h)\), as \(D\le2h+2\).
For prefix length \(A\asymp h\), the first cutoff with \(b=\lfloor A/3\rfloor\) has bounded norm and agrees through \(3L\) because all discrepancy coefficients in the lower transition vanish.
The factors \(U,\Pi\) have bounded circle suprema uniformly in the moving binomial and the trace degree, using \(M(\Pi)=2\) and fixed support size for \(\Pi\).
Multiplication uses only nonnegative Fourier indices, so it preserves agreement through \(3L\).
The second cutoff returns \(2UB^\sharp-V\Pi\) exactly, since its coefficients are zero on \((L,3L]\).
Adding \(V\Pi\) bounds \(\|UB^\sharp\|_1\) without division by a binomial at a circle root.
The integer coefficient support bound transfers to arbitrarily fine prime grids for each polynomial separately.
The variable sign-binomial \(U\) has measure one, and \(B^\sharp\) differs from the delay-one associate by at most the factor \(z+1\).
Thus \(M(UB^\sharp)=M(B^\sharp)>1\) tends to one, contradicting the fixed-term Mahler gap.

Finally the two-term Chebyshev lift has its first odd coefficient at \(d\) since the first trailing degree \(2h+2-d\) exceeds \(h\).
Every bounded subsequence of offsets contains a fixed offset and sign, excluded by the earlier fixed-offset theorem.
This proves both conclusions \(d\to\infty\) and \(d/h\to0\).
