### Actual trace field, regular quotient and all small degrees

A genuine degree-2t Salem polynomial has t distinct inverse pairs and distinct trace values: equality of two traces makes the corresponding reciprocal quadratics equal, hence gives the same pair.
The actual trace field has degree t.
Because F is Galois, it contains all those conjugate trace values and is their splitting field.
Its group T acts transitively on the trace values, and each value generates F, so its stabilizer is trivial.
Thus the pair action is the regular action of T, with |T|=t.
This uses normality of the actual trace field, not an arbitrary enlarged normal field or an assumption of abelianity.

The accepted L162 argument identifies V as the kernel of this pair action.
Conjugates of complex conjugation flip all but one pair.
Their span is all of F2^t for even t and contains the even-weight subspace for odd t, exactly as used here.
The CM10 source attribution covers that kernel assertion; neither its extension-splitting assertion nor a chosen group complement is used.

For a root beta in pair i, its stabilizer H has trivial image in the regular quotient and is exactly {v in V:v_i=0}.
For every other pair j, H contains a flip at j: use e_j when t is even, or e_j+e_l with a third pair l when t is odd.
The latter is available at t=3 and all larger odd t.
The smallest permitted t=2 is the even case and has the required single other-pair flip.
There is no t=1 case because the statement requires genuine Salem degree at least four.
These elements give an orbit of size exactly two on each different pair and fix both roots of the starting pair separately.

### Quotient identity and majority selection

When c is the identity, sigma belongs to V and either fixes both roots of a pair or swaps them.
