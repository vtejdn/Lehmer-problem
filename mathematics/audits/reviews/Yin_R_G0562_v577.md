### The small polynomial and the precision of its remainder

The equalities of degrees follow from the hypothesis on every power field.
The orbits of \(\delta\) and \(\delta^2\) have different positive absolute heights, while an intra-orbit equality after powering would lower the degree.
Consequently all exponents separate the combined set.
Both minima have even degree, since inversion pairs their roots with none fixed.
The identity \(P_2(X^2)=P_1(X)P_1(-X)\) gives \(\bar P_2=\bar P_1\) in characteristic two.
For a reciprocal polynomial of degree \(2e\) in characteristic two, coefficient pairing gives \(P(1)=a_e\) and \(P'(1)=e a_e\).
Thus \(\bar P_1(1)=0\) would contradict squarefreeness.
Since \(n=2^k\), both \(X^n\pm1\) reduce to \((X-1)^n\), coprime to \(\bar F\).
Their multiplication maps are invertible on the finite free \(\mathbb Z_2\)-algebra \(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2\), by invertibility modulo two.

Set \(D=4d\), \(v=\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\), \(l=b-2v\).
There are \(2^{D(q+2k)}\) residue classes and strictly more than this many inputs because \((T+1)^{l+1}>2^{D(q+2k)}\).
Their nonzero difference has one-norm at most \((l+1)T\).
Inversion is defined in \(R\) by the unit constant coefficient and preserves its defining ideal by reciprocity.
Thus one can add or subtract the reversal at precisely width \(l\) and select a nonzero signed-reciprocal result.
Peeling off opposite zero coefficients amounts to division by a monomial with a two-sided formal width change, preserving signed reciprocity.
Dividing by the monic factors \(Y-1,Y+1\) to exhaustion also preserves signed reciprocity and the remainder ideal, by the units just checked.
The true-degree remainder \(H\) has nonzero endpoints and nonzero values at \(1,-1\).
Evaluating its reversal identity at \(1\) enforces the positive sign, including for a constant \(H\).

The divisor measure inequality bounds its one-norm by \(2^l\) times the one-norm before division.
The latter is at most \(2(l+1)T\).
Since \((l+1)/b\to1\), \(k=O(\log n)\), and \(b=o(n)\), the strict hypothesis gives \(\limsup n^{-1}\log\|H\|_1<h_*\).
The minima of the powered targets have measures \(nh_i\), ruling out a zero of \(H\) at any of them.
For \(q=o(nb)\) the same estimate gives \(n^{-1}\log\|H\|_1\to0\).

### Preparation of the exact comparison row

The even-order input has both prescribed orders \(2\) and the fixed sign at the width \(v\to\infty\).
Removing a common initial monomial \(Y^a\) from the row and its width-\(v\) reversal leaves a reversal pair of width \(v-2a\).
At least one formal endpoint is then nonzero.
Intersecting their common divisor with \(H^{v+1}\) removes their full shared multiplicities on the support of \(H\), since each original degree is at most \(v\).
Inversion pairs all roots and their common multiplicities on that support.
The resulting primitive divisor has nonzero constant coefficient and reverses at its true degree to itself up to sign, with the sign positive by its value at 1.
Exact division in \(\mathbb Z[Y]\) therefore keeps the same graph sign at the shortened formal width.
The divisor is nonzero at every powered target, so localization there preserves both contact orders and the nonzero numerator.
The nonzero endpoint survives division with its matching width change.
Multiplication by \((1+Y)^{b-w'}\), with \(w'\) the shortened width, returns the formal width to \(b\) without changing sign.
Neither any root of \(H\) nor a powered target is \(-1\).
The factors \(2^v\) and \(2^b\) in the norm estimates are \(\exp(o(n))\); the exact-row lower bound then gives slope \(S\).

### The pencil and the two endpoint assertions

For \(m=\deg H\) and \(t=\lfloor(b-m)/2\rfloor\), the reversal of \(J=Y^tH\) at width \(b\) is \(Y^{b-m-t}H\).
Both endpoint zero blocks have length at least \(v\).
The perturbation graph equals \(X^{nt}H(X^n)(1+\varepsilon X^{1+n(b-m-2t)})\); the last exponent is positive and the selected \(c\) are nontorsion.
Its remainder is divisible by \(2^{q+2k}\) in \(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2\).
The [lifting argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v274.md#lifting) uses the reversal at the actual \(b\), the bound \(k>D_0\) for its fixed local determinant constant, and \(b\ge2d-1\).
All apply eventually, giving a coefficient class in the reduction of the integral kernel.
This distinguishes the claimed lift from a graph equation only modulo \(2^q\).

The determinant \(AJ_b^*-A_b^*J\) cannot vanish identically.
At any powered target the exact pair has two nonzero entries and satisfies its graph equation, whereas the perturbation pair fails it.
The determinant has degree at most \(2b\).
At each of its roots where at least one perturbation coordinate is nonzero, at most one \(u\) makes the pair from \(A+uJ\) vanish.
At common perturbation zeros on \(H\), preparation of \(A\) prevents such vanishing; at zero the nonzero formal endpoint does the same.
There are no other common perturbation zeros.
At most \(2d\) more parameters could cancel a numerator at the selected powered nodes.
The stated positive range therefore leaves an admissible \(u\), whose logarithm is \(o(n)\).
The pencil is then coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), misses both original factors even to first order, and has the claimed coefficient class and slope.
These arguments concern reversal at the specified formal width even if the true degree of \(B\) is smaller.

In the central choice of \(J\) the coefficient matching holds as exact integer equality at both ends.
Because the endpoint pair is nonzero, the limiting paired envelope is at least zero at every positive log radius.
The bound on the one-norm lets one discard all \(j\) above a fixed cutoff at each such radius, uniformly in \(n\), while the shared endpoint blocks grow past that cutoff.
This proves equality along corresponding subsequences of both coordinate limits at the bounded indices determining the exterior faces.
The consecutive-index input says that at least one coordinate attains the whole interval for the genuine row.

When \(A(0)=0\) and \(q=o(nb)\), replacing \(J\) by \(H\) makes \(B(0)=uH(0)\ne0\).

The original opposite endpoint stays nonzero, and each modification has coefficient exponent at most zero.

### The converse and the height budget

An integral lift differs from \(B\) by \(2^q\) times an integral row, so \(G_b(B)(c)\in2^q\mathbb Z[c]\).
For a missing \(P_i\) that value is nonzero, and its norm is a nonzero integer divisible by \(2^{dq}\).
The upper estimate from its \(d\) conjugates is \((2\|B\|_1)^d e^{(nb+1)h_i}\), also valid when an endpoint vanishes.
Minimizing over the two missing factors proves the asserted \(h_*/d\) upper inequality.
Finally \(4\log(2nb+1)=o(n)\) and \(8\log\|B\|_1=(8S+o(1))n\); hence the strict budget follows when \(8S<\log2\).
The theorem gives an order of precision and leaves the interval between its constants open.
