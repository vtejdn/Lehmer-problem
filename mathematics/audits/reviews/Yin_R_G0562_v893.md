# Large prime divisors of the four-term exponent indices at a pole reduction

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:22 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the submitted [C345 v753](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v753.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer had no role in developing the argument and received its fixed text without its development discussion or a prior verdict.
The review covered the cover over the residue field, all three reductions, the relative norm calculation, the distributional restriction on the integer exponents and the examples.

Take an irreducible factor of the monic divisor of \(X^M-1\), of degree \(f\), multiplicity \(j\), and root order \(r\).
The separable part of \(X^M-1\) has exponent \(M'=M/p^{v_p(M)}\), so \(r\mid M'\).
Frobenius on the roots has orbit length \(f=\operatorname{ord}_r(q)\), also for \(r=1\) with the convention given.
The class of \(q^f\bmod M'\) maps to one modulo \(r\) and has order \(\operatorname{ord}_{M'}(q)/f\), because \(f\mid\operatorname{ord}_{M'}(q)\).
There are at most \(M'/r\) residue classes modulo \(M'\) congruent to one modulo \(r\).
This gives \(r\le\kappa_q(M) f\) without any cyclicity assumption on the entire group of units.
The full factor power divides \(X^n-1\) exactly when \(r\mid n\) and \(p^{v_p(n)}\ge j\).
Its minimum such exponent \(L=r p^{\lceil\log_p j\rceil}\) divides \(M\), and \(L\le p r j\le p\kappa_q(M)d\).
For \(W>1\), some prime part of \(M/L\) is at least \(W^{1/T}\) where \(T=\omega(M)\).
Removing from \(M\) the least sufficiently large power of that prime preserves divisibility by \(L\), and the resulting exponent is at most \(M W^{-1/T}\).
The same set of at most \(T\) candidate binomials works for every factor, with its full multiplicity, including inseparable factors.

For an odd \(\ell\ne p\), lifting \(q^{\operatorname{ord}_\ell(q)}\) along successive \(\ell\)-powers from its initial depth \(h_\ell\) gains exactly one valuation unit at each step.
Raising an element of \(1+\ell\mathbb Z_\ell\) to an exponent prime to \(\ell\) preserves the valuation of its difference from one.
This forces \(\ell^{(b-h_\ell)_+}\) to divide the order modulo \(\ell^b\).
For \(\ell=2\ne p\), starting with \(q^2\equiv1\bmod8\) gives the same conclusion for \(q^2\), whose order divides that of \(q\).
These single-prime lower divisors are mutually coprime and each divides the order at \(M'\).
Multiplying them proves the upper bound on \(\kappa_q(M)\) for support in a fixed finite list.

The [four-term limit](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement) gives \(v/(ud)\to h(B)/\log B>0\), and the [common exponent bound](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v747.md#statement) gives \(u\to\infty\).
Hence \(d=o(N)\) in every applicable row, and \(M h(\beta)\le 2C\) because the original width is \(a+b\).
The minimal polynomial of the Salem unit power \(\beta\) over \(E\) is monic integral with unit constant term, by taking all its relative conjugates and their inverses.
Monic division over the valuation ring therefore carries the original equation into a divisibility after any integral normalization.
At a positive pole valuation one obtains \(X^{u+v}-\epsilon\).
At negative valuation, multiplying by \(B^{-1}\) leaves \(-X^u(X^{v-u}-\epsilon)\), with \(X^u\) coprime to the reduced divisor.
Both signed binomials divide the stated binomial at double the exponent in every residue characteristic.
For \(B\equiv\delta\in\{1,-1\}\), expansion gives exactly \((X^u-\delta)(X^v+\epsilon\delta)\).
Dividing by the monic gcd with \(X^{2u}-1\) removes the full available multiplicity from that binomial and leaves no factor powers beyond those of \(X^{2v}-1\), even in characteristic two.
The extra exponent \(2u\) satisfies \(2u h(\beta)\to0\).

If \(\overline P\) divides a product of binomials, the coefficients of the remainder modulo \(P\) are in the base prime \(\mathfrak p\).
Evaluation at \(\beta\) stays within the integral local ring at every extension of \(\mathfrak p\).
Each such place therefore supplies at least \(\log(p)/e_{\mathfrak p}\) in negative log absolute value.
Their normalized weights sum to \([E_{\mathfrak p}:\mathbb Q_p]/[E:\mathbb Q]\) by multiplicativity of degrees and the sum over local extensions.
The resulting cost is exactly bounded below by \(\log(q)/[E:\mathbb Q]\) as claimed, using one actual prime of \(E\), regardless of other primes over \(p\).
All finite-place binomial costs are nonnegative since \(\beta\) is a unit, and their total equals the sum of the individual \(L_1\) values.
The [degree-independent height envelope](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) sends each value to zero when its argument has height tending to zero.
Thus a bounded number of short periods cannot cover the reduction.
With \(T\le t\), their heights are uniformly at most \(2C W^{-1/t}\) when \(W>1\).
After the single extra binomial is absorbed if needed, the fixed lower local cost gives an upper bound on \(W\) in terms of the indicated constants.
This proves (1) with prime divisors varying across indices.
An infinite subsequence of bounded greatest prime divisors would also bound \(\kappa_q(M)\) by the fixed-support estimate, contradicting \(M/d\to\infty\).

For rational \(B=m/n>1\) in lowest terms, primes of \(m,n,m+n\) respectively give positive valuation, negative valuation and residue \(-1\) when those primes occur.
In particular \(B=2\) gives the claimed simultaneous conclusions from \(2,3\).
For the cubic example the monic integer polynomial has values \(1,-1,-1\) at \(0,1,-1\), respectively, and has no rational root.
Its three changes of sign isolate three real roots, including a root greater than two.
The three norms at those integer arguments have absolute value one, verifying the exhibited case with no applicable prime.
