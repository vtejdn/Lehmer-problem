# Exterior and interior edge zeros carrying squared radial moments

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T06:07 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed [C345 v778](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v778.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of its complete argument.
The reviewer took no part in its development.

A shrinking total measure makes the primitive leading and constant coefficients have absolute value one and eventually falls below Smyth's gap for nonreciprocal algebraic integers.
Thus the conjugate moduli are closed under reciprocation with multiplicity and the positive and negative logarithms contribute equally to the second moment.
C345 v775 excludes bounded primitive directions regardless of the phase orders.
C345 v769 then gives \(Nh(z)\asymp_F1\) and \(d\asymp_F Nm\).
The C855 v2 bound applies with all \(d\) archimedean embeddings, since finite-place logarithms of a unit vanish.
It follows that \(N^2\mathbb E(t_\sigma)_+^2\gg_F m^{-2}\) and the same holds on the interior side.
A unique maximizing vertex at a limiting direction gives a gap at least a fixed positive multiple of \(N\) from the remaining exponents.
The top monomial has coefficient of fixed nonzero modulus after any conjugation of the phases.
This would bound every \(N(t_\sigma)_+\), contradicting the second-moment bound.
The minimizing face is also an edge by inversion.
Its parallel mate and it have one common primitive tangent up to sign.
The tangent exponent cannot vanish on infinitely many indices, since those primitive directions would be fixed up to sign.
A fixed unimodular completion then has \(k=o(N)\) and \(b'\asymp_F N\) after orientation.
The powered pairs are units and escape finite sets because a fixed nontorsion pair has at most one primitive relation direction modulo torsion, up to sign.
C345 v772 consequently makes each exposed face primitive.

For an exterior embedding put \(Y=b'\log|\sigma z|\) and \(s=b'/k\).
The lower powers of \(\sigma V\) in the fiber equation bound \(|B_J(\sigma U)|\) by \(C_F\exp(-Y+DY/s)\), hence by \(C_F e^{-Y/2}\) for large \(s\).
On fixed exterior-root disks, \(|\sigma U|\) stays above \(1+\delta\) and below a fixed constant, giving \(Y\asymp_F s\).
Factorization of \(B_J\) gives the required exponential approach, after allowing for all multiplicities.
Off those disks the exterior factors are bounded away from zero and every interior factor is bounded below on \(|\sigma U|\ge1\).
Thus large \(Y\) requires a circle root within \(Ce^{-cY}\).
Since \(|\sigma U|-1=e^{Y/s}-1\ge Y/s\), this already bounds \(Y\) by \(O_F(\log(2s))\) on that complement.
A constant face polynomial or a face without circle roots gives a uniform bound there directly.

The small point used in the disk discrepancy is \(U=\omega z^k\), whose height is \(kh(z)\ll_F s^{-1}\) because \(\omega\) is torsion at every place.
The cited degree-height bound gives \([\mathbb Q(U):\mathbb Q]\gg_F\sqrt{s}\), with bounded degrees absorbed in its absolute constant.
The embeddings of any common number field distribute the values of \(U\) with the same repetition at each conjugate, even when the degree of the phase field grows.
For each unit-circle zero the disk cutoff is globally Lipschitz, compactly supported and bounded by one.
Its arclength mean is \(O(\rho)\) and its Dirichlet energy is bounded, since the area supporting its \(O(1/\rho)\) gradient is \(O(\rho^2)\).
In the Pritsker estimate at \(\varepsilon=s^{-1/4}\) the modulus of continuity costs at most \(O(s^{-1/8})\), using \(\rho\ge s^{-1/8}\).
The squared energy-error factor is \(O_F(s^{-1}+s^{-1/2}\log s+s^{-1/4})\).
The discriminant of the primitive minimal polynomial is a nonzero integer, so its negative logarithmic contribution can be dropped.
Adding the circle mean and summing over the fixed roots proves the given probability bound for the exterior event with \(Y>T\) off the disks.
The integral of its \(s^{-1/9}\) error against \(2T\,dT\) is \(O_F(s^{-1/9}\log^2(2s))\).
The exponential part is integrable, and bounded \(T\) makes a bounded contribution without a discrepancy estimate.

It follows that the bounded complementary part is negligible against the divergent exterior squared moment.
The portion inside the disks has \(Y\asymp_F s\), yielding \(\mathbb P(E_+)\gg_F (sm)^{-2}\).
The positive contribution there to the height of \(U\) gives \(\mathbb P(E_+)\ll_F s^{-1}\), hence \(k\ll_F Nm^2\).
A nonempty disk event also supplies \(k\,t_\sigma\gg_F1\) while \(t_\sigma\le m\), giving \(k\gg_F m^{-1}\).
Inverting both torus coordinates and \(z\) inverts both phases but leaves the two exponents \(k,b'\) unchanged.
The reversed leading coefficient is a monomial times \(B_0(1/U)\), so its exterior zeros require nonzero interior roots of the original bottom face, with the same orientation used for the upper face.
Distance under inversion changes by bounded factors on these finitely many disks away from zero.
Both second-moment conclusions therefore hold with the prescribed multiplicities and directions.
Failure of a uniform positive gap would supply a sequence to which these necessary conditions apply after discarding finitely many initial indices and selecting an accumulation direction.
This proves the all-section consequence, also for two-endpoint faces with equal coefficient magnitudes.
