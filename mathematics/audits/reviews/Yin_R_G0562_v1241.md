## Rank two at the pole, the complementary trace factor and the endpoint

At 2026-11-25T10:48 UTC a reviewer outside proof development checked the complete statement and four arguments of C G0345 v895 for Yin.
The initial inspection used the submitted proof, its two mathematical inputs and the cited parameter proposal in L G0462 v324.
The reviewer received no development discussion, author explanation or previous finding for v895.

The strict sign conditions place \(w\) in \((0,1/M)\) and all other roots in \((-4,0)\).
The irreducibility result in v891 identifies their product \(\prod_i(1-Mw_i)=L^2\) as the field norm, so \(L^2/(1-Mw)\) is integral.
The criterion in v883 then applies with \(T<p/q\), where \(Y>0\).
It supplies the positive common unit and coprime exponents, and the lift has degree \(2n\) with inversion over the trace field.
Bézout puts \(\beta\) in the group generated by \(T,Y\), so it generates the same degree-\(2n\) field as \(T\).

I checked \(EE'=L\), \(E(Y+1)=T+1\) and \(T\iota(E)=EY\) directly from the graph.
The relative norm of \(E\) is \(1-Mw\), giving index \(L^2\) for the principal ideal in the order \(A\).
For odd \(v\) a common residue field for \(E=E'=0\) would have odd characteristic, \(T=-1\) and \(Y=1\), contrary to \(T^v=Y^u\).
The resulting two quotient algebras are complementary summands of the free \(R\)-module \(A/LA\).
For \(\ell^s\parallel L\), the \(\ell\)-primary component of the first summand has order \(\ell^{2s}\), the \(\ell\)-part of the index, so its free rank over \(\mathbb Z/\ell^s\mathbb Z\) is two.
On each summand the powers of the generator lift a basis from the residue field, making each monic relation divisible by its characteristic polynomial even at repeated residue roots.
On the first summand inversion pairs the eigenvalues of multiplication by \(\beta\).
The only possible self-inverse eigenvalue is \(-1\), so the determinant is one modulo \(\ell\).
Its square is one over the prime-power ring by conjugacy to the inverse, and its sum with one is invertible there, giving determinant one modulo \(\ell^s\).
This proves reciprocity and both claimed divisibilities of the quadratic polynomial.

On the complementary summand \(\beta^v=1\), so an inverse of \(u\) modulo \(v\) expresses \(\beta\) as a power of \(T\).
The complementary characteristic polynomial \(D\) therefore divides \(X^v-1\).
Cancelling \((X+1)^2\) from the trace lift after extracting \(W+4\) gives \(D=X^{n-1}J(X+X^{-1}-2)\).
Its value at \(-1\) is a unit because it divides \(-2\) at this point.
Hence \(H'(-4)=J(-4)\) is a unit in \(R\).
Since \(D\) is reciprocal with unit constant, its monic cofactor is anti-reciprocal of odd degree and vanishes at one.
Removing \(X-1\) and pairing reciprocal powers in the remaining factor proves the asserted divisibility in \(R[W]\), where substitution is injective by the leading Laurent coefficient.
Degree comparison gives \(2n-1\le v\).

For opposite parities the quotient by \(Y+1\) identifies both powers with \(-1\), so \(\beta^{uv}=(-1)^u=(-1)^v\) forces \(2=0\).
The coprime exponents then identify \(\beta\) with one, bounding the order of this quotient by two.
The norm of \(\beta^{2k}+1\) has square absolute value by extracting the unit \(\beta^k\) and taking the norm from the fixed field.
Since \(N(E)=L^2\), either even exponent excludes quotient size two and forces \(\lvert H(-4)\rvert=L^2\).
The signed value is \((-1)^nL^2\) by the positions of the roots.
In the Taylor expansion at \(-4\), the terms of order at least two are multiples of \(L^4\) after multiplication by \(M^n\).
When \(u\) is even the nonzero derivative term thus gives \((-M)^n\ne1\) modulo each \(\ell\mid L\), equivalently \(4^n\ne1\) there.

Finally \(b=N(Y+1)>0\) by the positive real places and complex conjugate pairs.
Modulo \(\ell\) the complementary summand contributes \(2^{2n-2}\) and the reciprocal pair contributes \((1+y)(1+y^{-1})\), including when it is a repeated root.
If \(2^a\parallel u\) with \(a\ge1\), then \(b=1\) and the order of \(y=z^v\) has 2-part \(2^{a+1}\) because \(z^u=-1\) and \(v\) is odd.
The displayed determinant identity gives \(x^2=y\) for \(x=2^{n-1}(1+y)\).
In the nonsplit case \(x^\ell=x/y=x^{-1}\), so \(x\) lies in the same order-\(\ell+1\) group as \(y\).
In the split case it lies in the order-\(\ell-1\) group.
Consequently \(2^{a+2}\) divides the relevant group order, excluding even \(u\) at \(q=2\) and in the cited \(L=3^{2k}\) proposal on this branch.
At \(3\) and \(5\), for odd \(v\) the remaining odd \(u\) forces \(z\) to have order two at \(3\), and order two or six at \(5\).
Order two gives the double root of \(X^u+1\) at \(-1\), forcing \(\ell\mid u\) by differentiation and \(\ell\mid b\) by the determinant.
Order six gives \(3\mid u\), and coprimality and oddness of \(v\) preserve order six for \(y\), yielding \(2+y+y^{-1}=3\).
When \(v\) is even, opposite parity already gives \(b=1\).
These checks establish every stated restriction conditional on integral realization.
