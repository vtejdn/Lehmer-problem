The estimates, inverse-power enumeration and lifting criterion of v708 were checked directly.

For coprime \(p,q\), integers \(u,v\) with \(uq+vp=1\) express \(\gamma\) as \((r^{1/p})^u(r^{1/q})^v\).
Since \(\gamma^{pq}=r\), it generates \(K\) and is a unit.
The odd power gives one positive exterior conjugate, one positive interior conjugate at \(\gamma^{-1}\), and \(d-2\) neutral conjugates, as follows by transporting the embeddings of \(r\).
In particular \(\gamma\) is Salem and its multiplicative measure is \(\gamma\).
The strict first-radical bound gives \(\gamma^q<p\).
The derivative of \(\log(t)/(t+2)\) has the sign of \(1+2/t-\log t<0\) for \(t\ge17\).
Thus the cases \(p\ge17\) use \(q\ge p+2\), and for \(p=11,13\) with \(q\ge17\) one can bound \(\gamma\) by \(13^{1/17}\).
I checked both comparisons with \(7/6\) by integer powering.

I checked the first sentence of Theorem 1 on page 3 of the retained author manuscript against the MRW08 source unit.
The strict interval \(1<M(S)<M(\text{Lehmer polynomial})\) gives \(d\ge56\), with degree 56 allowed and no primitivity hypothesis on this assertion.
The published exhaustive computations were used at statement scope.
The degree inequality gives \(n\ge55\) and \(p\ge59\), and \(59\cdot100^{61}<107^{61}\) checks the last strict estimate.

The factors at \(1,-1\) of \(G_a\) depend just on \(n,\epsilon\), as direct substitution also verifies.
Thus the degree and cofactor are known before selecting \(a\bmod p\), and all roots of its reduced Salem factor are nonzero since its constant coefficient is a unit.
Root powering commutes with reduction, for example by the monic resultant with \(Y-X^j\).
Frobenius permutes the roots of each irreducible reduced factor with the same multiplicity, giving \(\bar S^{[q]}=\bar P_a\).
Above a root of multiplicity \(k\), the \(q\) preimages have multiplicity \(k\) each in \(\bar P_a(X^q)\), as \(p\ne q\).
The multiplicities chosen by \(T\) sum to \(k\) across that fiber.
This proves both completeness of divisor enumeration and the need for the stated image test.
If \(\operatorname{ord}_q(p)>d\), any candidate root of degree \(f\le d\) has the same field as its image, since \(q\) is invertible modulo \(p^f-1\).
Hence no preimage of larger admissible degree can supplement the unique preimage in the image's own field.
Applying that inverse power separately on each Frobenius orbit, with its given multiplicity, proves existence as well as uniqueness for every seed residue.
Repeated modular powering gives \(59^{12},59^{20},59^{30}\equiv9,47,-1\bmod61\), proving order \(60>d\) at all the stated delays.

For \(j\le d/2\), \(\gamma^j<\sqrt p\), \(\gamma^{-j}<1\), and the remaining \(d-2\) conjugates have modulus one.

Given the earlier coefficients and traces, Newton's identity also fixes the trace modulo \(jp\).

Salem reciprocity determines the second half, giving at most six candidates per \(T\) and at most \(6p\) in the order case.
