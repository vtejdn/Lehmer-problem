### The cyclic class calculation

In the real cyclotomic Galois group the representative equal to one modulo three identifies Frobenius at two with \(-2\bmod q\).
The unique index-\(r\) subgroup contains the decomposition group of order \(f\), since \(rf\mid q-1\).
Thus two splits completely in \(K\) and each of its dyadic primes lies under \(h/r\) labels.
For an integral monic \(V\mid W\), its reciprocal lift and complement divide the parent and have disjoint binary reductions.
At the sampled primitive trace \(\tau=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), \(W(\tau)=-2(\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l})\), a dyadic uniformizer times a unit for every \(0<l<q\).
Indeed \(\zeta^{2l}\) has odd order greater than one, so \(1+\zeta^{2l}\) is an integral unit by the quotient of \(1-\zeta^{4l}\) by its equal-norm conjugate \(1-\zeta^{2l}\).
Consequently \(V(\tau)\) takes valuation one at its selected dyadic primes and zero at all other finite primes.
Taking the ideal norm to \(K\) contributes exponent \(f\) per selected label.
This yields \(A(\sigma)z=0\), while the product of all \(r\) distinct primes over two is principal and yields \(\Phi_r(\sigma)z=0\).

Projection onto an \(\ell\)-primary component by multiplication by \(\operatorname{ord}(z)/\ell^v\) gives an element \(x\) of order \(\ell^v\).
The order-\(\ell\) element \(\ell^{v-1}x\) has a proper annihilator in \(\mathbb F_\ell[X]\) containing the irreducible \(\Phi_r\), so this annihilator is exactly \((\Phi_r)\).
In degree at most \(r-1\) its only elements are scalar multiples of \(1+X+\cdots+X^{r-1}\).
Thus \(A=c\Phi_r+\ell A_1\) for an integer \(c\) and a polynomial \(A_1\) of degree at most \(r-1\).
Both \(A_1\) and \(\Phi_r\) annihilate \(\ell x\), so induction gives congruent coefficients in \(A_1\) modulo \(\ell^{v-1}\).
Combining all prime powers proves (1), also when \(B_r=1\).
If the common least residue is positive, all \(r\) counts contribute, and if it is zero, any nonzero count contributes at least \(B_r\).
When \(B_r>h/r\), the allowed interval contains at most one representative of a given residue.
Each primitive trace label accounts for \(2f\) roots in a reciprocal lift.
At \(B=3q\), the full binary period with one doubling therefore gives \(m(S)\ge(\deg S)\log 2/(6q)\ge fr\log 2/(3q)\) in the asserted case.

### The trace factor and the reciprocal lift

When \(h=r\) each prime has capacity one, so \(B_r>1\) makes the selected counts all zero or all one.
Since \(\bar P_l\) is squarefree and has only orders three and \(3q\), \(\bar W\) is squarefree with those primitive trace labels and one additional trace root \(1\).
Any repeated trace root would give repeated roots in the parent by the trace substitution.
Monicity preserves degrees and the distinct reductions, so exactly one rational irreducible factor \(V\) takes all the primitive labels.
Any further factor must be linear with odd integer root \(y\).

If \(|y|\ge3\), the exterior solution \(x\) of \(x+x^{-1}=y\) has \(|x|\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
For \(k=q-l\ge2\), its square already exceeds six, contradicting the leading-term estimate \(|x|^k\le6\).
When \(k=1\), \(q\) is odd and \(l\) even, making the trace expression negative for \(x<-1\), while for \(x\ge2\) the first pair of terms in \(P_l\) is nonnegative and \(x^q-2x+1>0\).
At the two remaining candidates \(C_q(-1)=-1\), \(C_q(1)=1\), and the order-three and order-six traces give \(W(-1)\in\{2,-4\}\) and \(W(1)=0\) exactly for \(l\equiv\pm1\bmod6\).
Thus \(W=(Y-1)^e V\), with simple factors.

For \(e=0\) the norm of \(\beta^2-4\) for a trace root \(\beta\) equals \(W(2)W(-2)\), which is \(5\) or \(-3\) according to the parity of \(l\).
Either value excludes a square.
For \(e=1\) and \(l>1\), the alternating strict signs at \(\theta=j\pi/l\) give \(l\) distinct interior roots under the injective map \(\theta\mapsto2\cos\theta\) on \([0,\pi]\).
At most one can be \(1\).
For \(l=1\), two consecutive integers in \([q/3,q/2]\) exist for \(q\ge29\), and the corresponding angles have opposite signs because the term \(2\cos(q\theta)\) alternates between \(\pm2\) while \(|1-4\cos\theta|\le1\).
The intervening root has \(\theta>\pi/3\), so its trace belongs to \(V\).
Each case with \(e=1\) thus supplies a real embedding at which \(\beta^2-4<0\).
The quadratic \(X^2-\beta X+1\) is irreducible over the trace field in all cases.
Since a lifted root recovers \(\beta\), its degree is \(2\deg V\), giving irreducibility of the full reciprocal lift of \(V\).
The lift of \(Y-1\) is \(X^2-X+1=\Phi_6\).
This argument also covers the conductors where inversion pairs two distinct ordinary Frobenius orbits.

### The four dyadic class orders

I independently tested primality by trial division, recomputed the modular powers and checked the integer norm identities.
In ascending conductor order the values \((-2)^f\bmod q\) are all one.
Trial division of \(f\) and testing \(f/p\) for every prime divisor \(p\) gave the residues \(105\), \(585\), \((1296,931)\), and \((2152,16)\), respectively.
Thus the four orders are exactly \(53,293,648,1076\), all of index two.
The radicands \(321,1761,1297,2153\) from the quadratic subfield formula are all squarefree and one modulo eight.
The displayed elements have norms \(-8,-128,-2048,-32\), and their coefficients in the integral basis \(1,(1+\sqrt D)/2\) at the second basis element are \(1,9,3,1\).
Those odd coefficients preclude divisibility by both dyadic primes, so a single prime class has order dividing \(3,7,11,5\), respectively.
Its conjugate has inverse class.

The four units have norms \(1,1,-1,-1\).
For a hypothetical norm-\(\pm2\) element one can bound its positive absolute value at the first embedding between \(\sqrt{2/U}\) and \(\sqrt{2U}\) by multiplying by a unit power.
The maximum of \(t+2/t\) on that interval is \(\sqrt{2U}+\sqrt{2/U}\), which bounds \(|y|\sqrt D\).
For the norm-one units the resulting bounds on \(y^2\) are \(864/321\) and \(4704/1761\), both below four.
For the norm-minus-one units one has \(U+U^{-1}=2b\sqrt D\) with \(b=1,5\), respectively, giving bounds below one in both rows.
The case \(y=0\) would have square norm, and \(y=\pm1\) in the first two rows would require a square among \(313,329,1753,1769\), all strictly between consecutive squares.
The absence of a generator of norm \(\pm2\) rules out class order one.
Each proposed order \(c\) is prime and coprime to \(f\), so \(\operatorname{ord}(z)=c\).
For \(r=2\) the monic linear \(\Phi_2\) is irreducible at every prime, giving \(B_2=c>1\) in each row.
