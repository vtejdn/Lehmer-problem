# Independent review of the finite-place bounds on vanishing initial Newton sums

## Local hypotheses and uniformity in the degree, pole and prime

At 2026-11-25T13:37 UTC a reviewer outside development examined the whole fixed [C0901 v174](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v174.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer received the statement, proof and scope without development dialogue, an author explanation or an earlier verdict on v174, and had separately reviewed the v172 examples cited for comparison.
All three bounds and their long-prefix consequences follow under the stated hypotheses.
I checked the statements and conventions of the three external theorems against the primary editions cited below and accepted them at that scope.
Their original proofs were outside this review.

Write \(F=\sum_{i=0}^{2D}a_iZ^i\).
Newton's identities in characteristic zero identify the zero prefix of power sums with \(a_{2D-j}=0\) for \(1\le j\le J\), and reciprocity gives \(a_j=0\) for the same indices.
If \(r\mid q\), all surviving terms except \(p^{2D}\) in the homogeneous evaluation carry \(q^{J+1}\) in the local ring.
If \(r\mid p\), all except \(q^{2D}\) carry \(p^{J+1}\) there.
These divisibilities use only integrality at the selected prime.
For \(x=-p\), the bases are \(-q,-(2q+1)\), and the even exponents give precisely the same pole value and constant-term contribution as the unsigned bases.
In both cases \(\beta=2\alpha-1\), so \(|\alpha|,|\beta|\) are coprime integers larger than one.
Taking valuations at prime divisors of each proves multiplicative independence, and both bases reduce to one at every \(r\mid x\).

If \(q\) is even the dyadic choice is \(x=q\), and if \(q\) is odd it is \(x=-(q+1)\).
In either case \(v_2(x)=v_2(q(q+1))\).
Yu's field condition (1.2) holds in \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), where \(d=2\), \(2\) is unramified and inert, \(e_{\mathfrak p}=1\), and \(f_{\mathfrak p}=2\).
The prime-ideal valuation agrees with \(v_2\) on rational numbers, and division by \(\beta^2\) leaves the valuation unchanged.
Theorem 1 accepts signed integer exponents \(2D,-2\), the nonzero difference \(\alpha^{2D}\beta^{-2}-1\), and \(B=3D\).
Its effective coefficient \(C(2,2,\mathfrak p)\) is fixed throughout.
Each modified height is \(\max(\log|\gamma|,\log 2)\le\log(2q+1)\) for \(\gamma=\alpha,\beta\).
This gives (1) uniformly over \(q,D\), including \(v_2(x)=1\). [Yu, pp. 337–339, equations (1.2), (1.5), (1.8)–(1.10)](https://bibliotekanauki.pl/articles/1390501.pdf)

The integral realization in C0345 v891 yields a noncyclotomic Salem minimum of degree \(d=2D\), whose log Mahler measure is \(\log\lambda<\log(1+1/q)<1/q\).
Thus Voutier's Corollary 2 implies \(q<(\log(6D))^3/2\), which bounds \(\log(2q+1)\) by an absolute multiple of \(\log\log(9D)\) for all \(D\ge2\).
Together with \(v_2(pq)\ge1\), this gives (2).
For \(J\ge cq\), rearranging (1) retains the full factor \(v_2(pq)\).
The comparison with the main theorem also uses the correct normalization \(d h=\log M\).
Writing \(y=\log(2D)\), its restriction is \(y/\log y>(q/4)^{1/3}\) for growing \(q\), whose inverse has order \(q^{1/3}\log q\).
The lower bound \(q/\log^2(2q+1)\) grows faster. [Voutier, pp. 83–84, Theorem and Corollary 2](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa74/aa7417.pdf)

For (3), at least one of \(q,-p\) is coprime to \(\ell\) and has absolute value greater than one.
Every prime divisor \(r\) of that choice is good, including each possible choice when \(\ell\nmid pq\).
The two rational bases give \(K_v=\mathbb Q_r\), residue degree one and Bugeaud–Laurent degree parameter one.
Their residues equal one, hence the joint order \(g\) equals one for odd \(r\) and for \(r=2\).
Theorem 3 applies to arbitrary positive exponents, including \(2D,2\) in residue characteristic two.
Writing \(L=\log(2q+1)>1\) gives admissible height parameters \(L_1=L_2=L\), since \(r\le q+1\) and the absolute logarithmic heights of the two bases are at most \(L\).
The source parameter is \(b'=(2D+2)/L\), so \(\log b'\le\log(3D)\) for \(D\ge2\).
The remaining maximum terms are controlled by \(\log r\le L\) and \(L>1\), and \(24r/((r-1)(\log r)^4)\) is bounded absolutely.
Combining this with \((J+1)v_r(x)\) proves (3) uniformly in the chosen prime and the exceptional \(\ell\). [Bugeaud and Laurent, author edition, pp. 2–5, Theorem 3 and Corollary 2](https://irma.math.unistra.fr/~bugeaud/travaux/logpadicdef.pdf)

Finally \(J\ge cq\) in (3) gives \(\log(3D)\ge c'\sqrt q/L-L\) with \(c'>0\) depending only on \(c\).
The second term is absorbed for sufficiently large \(q\), since \(L^2=o(\sqrt q)\).
The one-prime examples allow arbitrarily large degrees at their selected \(q\), and the necessity of these degree bounds leaves the integral existence question open.
