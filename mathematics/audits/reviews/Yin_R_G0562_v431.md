## Independent review of the good-prime theorem and both example families

At 2026-11-18T16:15 UTC an agent outside development of C389 independently reviewed the whole [submitted argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v389.md#decision-plane-powers-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The review began with the fixed claim and its stated inputs, without proof-development dialogue, author explanations or earlier findings on C389.
The reviewer also checked the final wording of the comparison with C388, which concerns small positive directions.
The three explicit inequalities, uniform good-prime dilation conclusion, reciprocal examples and prescribed bad-prime construction hold under the stated hypotheses.

The integral rank-two hypothesis gives positive degree in both variables and two support differences with nonzero integer determinant.
Away from the prime divisors of that determinant and the corresponding coefficients, the reduced support still spans both directions.
Ruppert's theorem on page 1 of the retained author preprint gives absolute irreducibility at every sufficiently large prime for precisely these positive partial degrees.
This verifies cofiniteness without an effective bound on the exceptional primes being needed.
The two sign substitutions are coordinate automorphisms, including in characteristic two, and preserve these conditions and the coefficient length.

At a good prime the reduced curve is irreducible in the torus, and its rank-two support excludes the binomial equation of a primitive section.
The parameter and its inverse on the section are defined by monomials with integer exponents, so the section is smooth also when one exponent is zero, negative or divisible by \(p\).
For any nonzero \((c_1,c_2)\in\mathbb F_p^2\), divisibility of \(c_1E_1f+c_2E_2f\) by \(f\) would force a scalar multiple by total degree.
Comparison of each nonzero coefficient would put every support difference in the kernel of the same nonzero form.
In particular this applies to \((a,b)\bmod p\).
At a section root of order \(m_x\), the restrictions of the two plane equations have orders \(m_x\) and at least \(m_x-1\), the second possibly infinite in characteristic \(p\).
The quotient of the local intersection algebra by the section equation therefore has length at least \(m_x-1\).
Distinct parameters give distinct points, so summing these local lower bounds and applying plane Bézout gives the budget \(D^2\).

The integral monic minimal polynomial divides the monomial-normalized specialization by polynomial division, even if its leading coefficient changes upon reduction.
Every root of the minimal polynomial has nonzero residue since it is a unit, and the reduced specialization is nonzero.
At each multiple residue at most \(m_x\le 2(m_x-1)\) conjugates occur.
For each remaining conjugate its simple residue singles it out among all its conjugates.
Thus inertia in the local splitting field fixes it, and the corresponding conjugate of its field-valued \(p\)-th root belongs to the same unramified subfield.
The discrete valuation of \(F(\gamma^a,\gamma^b)\) is at least one, so its \(p\)-th power divided by \(p\) has positive valuation even at \(p=2\).
This gives simultaneous vanishing of \(f,J\) at the indicated residue pair.

Differentiating the integral correction first and then reducing gives in the polynomial ring
\[
              E_i J=(E_i f)^p-f^{p-1}E_i f .
\]
On \(f=0\), divisibility \(J=fh\) would consequently give \(h=(E_i f)^{p-1}\) for both \(i\).
Both Euler derivatives are nonzero there by the support calculation, and the ratio would be a root of \(Z^{p-1}-1\) in \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).
This would force a forbidden constant linear relation even when \(p=2\).
The prime-to-characteristic portion of Voloch's published proof on pages 479–481 supplies the correction and derivative calculation.
C389 proves the support criterion and local lifting at nontorsion units directly, so the bound \(p>D^2+2\) in Voloch's torsion theorem is unnecessary here.
Bézout for \(f,J\) bounds the number of distinct intersection points by \(pD^2\).
The section inverse recovers the residue of \(\gamma\), and its \(p\)-th power recovers the original simple residue, giving \(d\le(p+2)D^2\).

For a unit the two sums of positive and absolute negative logarithmic moduli of its conjugates both equal \(m(P)\).
The sum of their squares is thus at most \(2m(P)^2\), with all embeddings counted.
The length bound gives \(m(P)\le\log L\) because the remaining integral factors have nonnegative measure.
The \(p\)-th root \(\gamma\) is a nontorsion unit of degree \(d\), and its conjugate logarithms are divided by \(p\).
Part A of C855 v2 gives the lower bound \(1/10\) for \(d\) times their squared sum, proving \(p^2\le20d(\log L)^2\).
Using \(p+2\le2p\) yields the prime bound and hence the stated degree threshold.
C253 applies in the Laurent ring since the absolutely irreducible \(F\) has no coordinate factor and retains its rank-two support there.
Its individual degree lower bound tends to infinity with \(\max(|a|,|b|)\), as required uniformly across factors.
For the dilation induction the norm of a root of the irreducible \(X^m-\alpha\) is \((-1)^{m+1}\alpha\).
Excluding the two signs at eligible primes prevents a \(p\)-th power at the next step, including at repeated dyadic steps, by the prime-binomial argument of C387.
Taking all \(q\)-th roots preserves the sum of positive logarithmic moduli and gives degree \(qd\).

For the reciprocal examples the support contains a unimodular triangle at every prime.
The identity \(t^s=t+1\) at a common zero gives \(t\ne0\), and \(B_s(t)=0\) gives \(t^{s-2}=-1\).
These imply \(t^2+t+1=0\) and \(t^3=1\), inconsistent with a power equal to \(-1\) in odd characteristic and in characteristic zero.
In characteristic two the two primitive cube roots satisfy both equations exactly when \(3\mid s-2\).
Primitivity and linearity in \(V\) establish absolute irreducibility in every remaining characteristic.
For positive directions the constant and leading coefficients of the specialized reciprocal polynomial are both \(-1\), including when interior terms coincide.
The citation of C388 for \(s=3\) agrees with its coprime positive-direction hypotheses.

For the bad-prime family, \(\mathbb C(z)/\mathbb C(z^\ell)\) has degree \(\ell\), and the \(\ell\) coefficients of \(z\) in the conjugates \((1-\zeta z)^\ell\) are distinct.
Their product expression for the monic resultant in \(V\) gives the minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb C(U)\).
The restrictions to the two coordinate axes give a nonzero constant and the pure powers \(U^\ell,V^\ell\), so the support differences have rank two.
Reducing the resultant in characteristic \(\ell\) gives \((V+U-1)^\ell\).
Direct expansion also confirmed both axial restrictions and this reduction for \(\ell=2,3,5\).
The positive root of \(X^n+X-1\) in \((0,1)\) exists uniquely by strict increase and increases strictly with \(n\).
It is a unit, and \(\gamma(1+\alpha^j)=1\) recovers it in the field of \(\alpha=\gamma^\ell\).
Its minimal polynomial of degree \(d\) is consequently a proper factor of \(P(X^\ell)\).
Each \(\gamma\) has logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(\log3\), so bounded-degree height finiteness implies that these degrees tend to infinity.
The directions \((1,1+\ell j)\) are distinct and primitive for each prescribed prime.
