### The scope of the arithmetic hypothesis

In (2) the explicit separation condition \(D\ge D_m>2\) is essential to the deduction from (1), since the numerator of \((K+\log(2D))/D\) can be negative for small \(D\).
Given any integer polynomial \(Q\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measure, take \(F(X,Y)=Y+Q(X)-X\) and \(n=1\).
The gcd is one, the diagonal is \(Q\), and choosing \(d\) large enough both contains all supported exponents and makes \(K+\log(2/d)<0\).
Omitting separation would thus include the unrestricted Lehmer gap already at \(m=1\).
The corrected (2) now uses exactly the regime in which the analytic comparison and the nonresonant arithmetic specialization have been proved.

### Relative clipping and choice of charts

The real and imaginary coordinates are trigonometric polynomials of degree at most \(d\).
A nonzero coordinate has only finitely many zeros and an integrable logarithm, so its absolute value bounds \(A\) below almost everywhere and \(\log A\) is integrable.
If \(n>2d\), the occupied frequencies \(k+nj\) from different rows are distinct, and the diagonal is a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
For fixed fast angle, tied coordinate maxima, clipped levels and critical points of the squared ratio are described by \(O_m(1)\) trigonometric polynomials of degrees \(O(d)\).
Identities can be omitted because the corresponding ties persist or the affected ratio is constant.
Removing the common zeros adds at most \(2d\) endpoints, and assigning clipped values there costs at most twice the bounded clipped range per point.
The shifted \(n\)-point mesh therefore has clipped error \(O_m(D^{-1}\log(C_m/u))\).
For each \(A>0\) the normalized fiber has a coefficient of modulus at least one and all coefficients have modulus at most \(\sqrt2\), which gives the independent \(O_m(u^{1/m})\) clipping loss by the finite-order test.

A maximizing chart with denominator \(v_\ell\) has a point where \(|v_\ell|=A>0\).
At that point the cleared level polynomial \(N_{sk}-\tau v_\ell^{k+1}\) can vanish for at most one of the two possible signed endpoints of a threshold range.
The other choice makes the level polynomial nonzero, even if \(|v_\ell|\) is arbitrarily small.
The quotient rule bounds its degree by \((k+1)d\), so all chart levels together have \(O_m(d)\) zeros.
Removing the common zeros as well prevents exiting a bounded chart through a denominator zero, since bounded ratios there force every numerator to vanish.
On each component where \(k\) is the first excessive derivative order of a chart ratio, the preceding normalized derivative has bounded range and its signed slope has magnitude at least \(\delta n\).
Such a component meets \(O_m(1)\) fast periods.
The additional periods touching partition endpoints or common zeros give a total of \(O_m(d)\) flags.

On all other periods one chart persists with \(A/|v_\ell|\le3\), a coefficient coordinate identically one and all scaled positive derivatives through order \(m\) bounded by \(2\delta\).
Freezing the coefficient vector changes the fast derivative of each order through \(m\) by \(O_m(\delta)\).
The Vandermonde matrix on the frequencies \(0,\dots,m\) keeps some real or imaginary part of some derivative of order at most \(m\) bounded away from zero on each of a fixed number of short intervals.
The divided-difference bound gives relative sublevel length \(O_m(w^{1/m})\), including the order-zero case by reducing the small threshold.
Choosing \(c_m>0\) so this is at most \(1/2\) gives the good subset with \(|G|\ge c_m A\) on each unflagged period.
These checks use the global chart count and relative sublevel estimates of [v327](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v327.md#charts).

### The stopped intervals and the two Turán–Nazarov estimates

For sufficiently large \(D\), fewer than a quarter of the \(n\) periods are flagged.
Every flagged singleton has a stopped ancestor or stops itself, and the terminal intervals are disjoint.
If an interval of length \(N\ge2\) periods splits, its smaller child has at least \(N/3\) periods, including for odd \(N\).
Thus a stopped child has flagged fraction at most \(3/4\) and greater than \(1/4\).
It contains at least a quarter good periods, and summing the lower bound on its flagged fraction gives total stopped length at most \(4B_0(2\pi/n)\).
The cut at the circle boundary causes no change in either proportion.

On the union \(S\) of the good subsets in a stopped interval \(J\), one has \(|S|\ge |J|/8\) and \(|G|\ge c_m A\).
Choose a coefficient \(a_j\) with \(\sup_J|a_j|\ge A_J\).
If it has \(k\le K\) terms, Turán–Nazarov gives \(\sup_S|a_j|\ge(8C)^{-(k-1)}A_J\), since every exponent is purely imaginary.
This includes a one-term coefficient, whose modulus is constant.
As \(A\ge|a_j|/\sqrt2\), taking the supremum of \(|G|\) over \(S\) gives (3).
Only the fraction of the interval occupied by \(S\) enters the constant.

Apply the inequality a second time to \(G\) on \(J\) and its measurable sublevel set, using disjoint frequencies and \(K\ge2\).
The relative length is bounded by \(\min(1,C e^{-s/(K-1)})\), whose integral over \(s\ge0\) is at most \((K-1)(1+\log C)\) for \(C\ge1\).
For almost every \(t\in J\) the clipping loss satisfies
\[
  (\log(uA(t)/|G(t)|))_+
  \le C_m K+\log(\sup_J|G|/|G(t)|).
\]
Integrating gives \(O_m(K|J|)\), and summing the disjoint stopped intervals costs \(O_m(K/D)\).
The remaining good periods cost \(O_m(u^{1/m})\).
Together with the mesh and fiber estimates at \(u=D^{-m}\) this proves (1), including the case with no flags.
For \(K=1\) the two measures coincide.
The use of Turán–Nazarov is at the recorded statement and applicability scope of [BS13-U001](../literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001).

The square-root convergence window follows from \(K\le(m+1)(2d+1)\).
Multiplying the Laurent form in v314 by the fast monomial \(Y\) leaves both measures unchanged and places it in the present class with \(m=2\).
At \(d=\lfloor5\sqrt n\rfloor\) v314 proves a strictly positive lower limit of the discrepancy, and at \(d=\lfloor n^{3/4}\rfloor\) it proves divergence.
The first conclusion suffices for order-sharpness and does not require convergence of the discrepancy.

### Arithmetic for separated bands

For two or more occupied rows, the positive partial-degree bound applies to a nontorsion \(Y\)-dependent component with width at most \(m\).
If all such components are torsion translates, the integral factorization has base gcd \(h\) and horizontal factors \(\Phi_q(X^aY^b)\) with \(b>0\).
Their lowest \(Y\)-coefficients are units, accounting for the whole gcd including integer content.
Additivity of \(X\)-width gives \(|a|\varphi(q)\le2d\), so \(n>2d\) guarantees \(a+nb\ne0\).
Every horizontal factor then has zero logarithmic measure on the diagonal, leaving precisely \(\log M(h)\).
For an irreducible diagonal the quotient by \(h\) still has positive \(Y\)-width and \(X\)-width at most \(2d\), hence at least two disjoint occupied specialized bands.
This quotient is a nonunit, forcing \(h\) to be a unit.
With a single occupied row the full diagonal and the gcd have equal measure.

Thus under \(D\ge D_m>2\), gcd measure one and \(\mu>0\) imply \(I\ge c_m'>0\).
Then (1) gives the desired lower bound \(\mu\ge c_m'/2\) whenever \(C_m(K+\log(2D))/D\le c_m'/2\).
The stated square-root corollary already has the necessary separation because \(d\le\epsilon_m\sqrt n\) and \(d\ge1\) imply \(D\ge d/\epsilon_m^2\ge1/\epsilon_m^2\).
One has \(K/D\le3(m+1)\epsilon_m^2\), and \(\log(2D)/D\) is uniformly small throughout that range for small \(\epsilon_m\).
