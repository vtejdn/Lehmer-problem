At finite places the cyclotomic polynomial has Gauss norm one and unit constant term, and its leading or constant term strictly dominates off the unit circle.

The product formula may be applied since \(z\) is nontorsion.
Summing with normalized local degrees gives (1), since the archimedean weights sum to one and the complete height contains the positive parts at every place.
The extension formula for normalized local degrees also gives field independence.

Writing \(\ell=\log(1/h)\), the choice \(n\sim\sqrt{3\ell/(2h)}\) has \(\log n\sim\ell/2\).

For \(z=\alpha^j\) with \(\alpha\) a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(z-1\) is nonzero and integral in the original field.

Computing there retains the exponent \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)]\) if powering lowers degree.

A nonzero residue of order \(t\) has \(p\nmid t\); the factorization of \(X^t-1\) remains separable in characteristic \(p\), so that residue belongs precisely to \(\Phi_t\) among its divisor factors.

The chosen lift \(x\) has \(x(z)=z^q\) by that uniqueness and its residue action, hence \((x^{-1}z)^q=z\).
Taking \(q\)-th powers of integral elements preserves a lower bound on the valuation of their difference, and \(x^{-1}\) preserves the valuation at \(P\).
It follows that \(c_0-(g^{-1}c_0)^q\) has valuation at least \(1/e_v\).
