## The curve cut gives the square-root bound for every fixed multiplicity

At 2026-11-24T18:31 an agent outside the development of the proof independently reviewed the five fixed sections for Yin.
The reviewer received the claim and scope without development discussion, author explanations or earlier findings on this proof.
The final sublinear-prime conclusion concerns the minimum number of terms required by a witness.

Write \(n=k-1\), with \(k=6\) for double contact and \(k=2r+2\) for multiplicity \(r\).
The mixed residue difference is one modulo \(p\), so its divisor \(g\) is coprime to \(p\), including composite \(p\).
After shifting by the residue-zero pivot, \(b_0=0\), the \(b_i\) are distinct with gcd one and \(|b_i|\le W\).
The two original graph values and their Euler derivatives through order \(r-1\) vanish at the inverse pair because the entire power of the reciprocal minimum divides the graph.
The monomial shift and the power substitution give the stated systems for \(y\), since the map \(X\mapsto X^g\) is unramified there.
The normalized exponents may have either sign.

The equations in the torus are linear in \(z_i\) or in \(z_i^{-1}\).
Multiplication by \(\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_i\) where needed gives ordinary degrees at most \(n\).
Successive projective intersections bound the sum of geometric component degrees by a function of \(r\) alone, including a component already contained in the next hypersurface.
Taking the components meeting the torus preserves this bound.
The fixed-node interpolation map on each moment kernel supplies \(Q_\pm\) of degrees at most \(r+1\).
Their product has degree at most \(k\), takes the prescribed nonzero values at all \(k\) nodes, and therefore lies in the pencil \(S_0+\lambda D\).
Its leading coefficient \(\lambda\) is regular on the torus since the interpolants depend linearly on the respective node values.
At each fixed \(\lambda\) there are finitely many monic divisors of the nonzero pencil member, including when it has repeated roots or lower degree.
Each permissible divisor for \(Q_+\) has nonzero value at zero, so \(Q_+(0)=c_0D'(0)\) fixes its scale, hence all the \(z_i\).
Thus the fibers are finite and each geometric component has dimension at most one.

An isolated point and its conjugates account for at most \(D_0\) components, where \(D_0\) bounds the sum of all component degrees.
For a curve component, its stabilizer in the absolute Galois group defines a field \(K\) of degree at most the number of components.
For example, the projective curve's Chow form, taken up to scalar, defines the same stabilizer, and the ratios of its coefficients give a field of definition of degree at most \(D_0\).
Every constant character value on the component belongs to \(K\) since its monomial has rational coefficients.
At \(y\) this value is \(\alpha^{g b\cdot v}\).
Any nonzero pairing therefore gives \(d_*\le D_0\), using the positive absolute exponent if necessary.
In the remaining case every constant character has value one, including all relations that could have placed the curve in a translated subtorus.

On the complete normalization, the character is constant precisely when its divisor is zero.
Each of the \(n+1\) coordinate hyperplane sections has degree at most \(D_0\) because the curve meets the torus.
Orders of the coordinate ratios have absolute value at most \(D_0\), and their divisors are supported on at most \((n+1)D_0\) geometric points.
The possible integer order matrices thus form a finite set, giving uniformly bounded bases of their kernels.
Each kernel \(\Lambda\) is saturated, so the quotient character lattice is free and describes the connected subtorus containing the curve.
Since \(g b\cdot v\equiv w(v)\pmod p\), each vector of such a basis has weight divisible by \(p\).
For \(p\) larger than all absolute basis weights the weight is zero on \(\Lambda\), and \(w\) descends surjectively to the quotient.
The character supports remain distinct because their \(b\)-values are distinct.

In coordinates adapted to that weight, let \(P+TQ\) be the first equation and let \(v_1\) have weight one.
Both summands are nonzero at the selected point by coprimality of the two original rows.
In the Laurent polynomial ring a common irreducible divisor through the point independent of \(T\) would divide both class polynomials and make both vanish there.
Over the fraction field in the other variables a common nonconstant factor in \(T\) must give \(T=-P/Q\).
Substitution in the derivative then yields \(\mathcal D(P/Q)=b(v_1)P/Q\) in the weight-zero variables.
Integration of this rational identity along the one-parameter diagonal action gives scaling by \(s^{b(v_1)}\), as follows by differentiating after dividing out this power of \(s\).
At a primitive \(p\)-th root \(s\), every weight-zero variable is fixed, whereas \(g b(v_1)\equiv1\bmod p\).
This contradicts \(P/Q\ne0\).
Thus there is no common divisor through the point in rank two, and in rank one the nonzero first equation already has finite zero set.
A curve through the point requires quotient rank at least three, equivalently \(\operatorname{rank}\Lambda\le n-3\).
This step uses the first two equations only and applies at every \(r\ge2\).

On the integer box of side \(T\) the total range of \(b\cdot u\) has length \(T\sum_i|b_i|\le nWT\).
A translate of \(\Lambda\) projects injectively onto at most \(n-3\) coordinate axes and meets this box in at most \((T+1)^{n-3}\) points.
The stated \(T\) in both versions satisfies \((T+1)^3>nWT+1\).
A larger fiber therefore gives a difference \(v\) with \(\|v\|_\infty\le T\), \(b\cdot v=0\) and \(v\notin\Lambda\).
The corresponding binomial of degree at most \(nT\) contains \(y\) but cuts the geometric curve properly.
Since the curve is defined over \(K\) and the binomial over \(\mathbb Q\), all \(K\)-conjugates of the point occur in the intersection.
The degree bound \([K(y):K]\le nTD_0\) gives \([\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\le nTD_0^2\), including when \(K\) is not contained in the point field.
The unit gcd of the \(b_i\) gives \(\mathbb Q(y)=\mathbb Q(\alpha^g)\).
All constants depend only on \(r\), and smaller supports satisfy the stated interpolation alternative.

In five coordinates the comparison can use the actual rank \(\ell\) in the count \((T+1)^\ell\).
With \(T=\lceil 10W^{1/(4-\ell)}\rceil\) one has \(T^{4-\ell}\ge10W\) for \(\ell=0,1,2\).
This gives \((T+1)^{5-\ell}>5WT+1\) and the asserted field-degree bound \(O(W^{1/(4-\ell)})\).
For a power-degree-preserving seed with \(p_0<p\le Kd\) and the stated row budget, \(W=O_K(d^2)\) and \([\mathbb Q(\alpha^g):\mathbb Q]=d\).
Ranks zero and one would give respectively \(d=O_K(d^{1/2})\) and \(d=O_K(d^{2/3})\), both impossible along an unbounded degree sequence.
For all sufficiently large degrees the two basis vectors of the resulting rank-two lattice are independent bounded relations on the normalized exponents and have weight zero.
The finite list of order matrices bounds the number of quotient lattices, while \(b\) may vary on each.

If every power preserves degree \(d\), then \(d_*=d\) and unbounded \(d\) excludes the bounded-degree alternative.
The row budget \(2d\) bounds the original graph degree by \(2pd+1\), which also bounds its span divided by \(g\).
Thus \(d^2\ll2pd+1\), giving \(d\ll p\).
On any such shrinking sequence one may take \(\theta\) to be the maximum of the prescribed logarithmic lower cutoff and \(\sqrt{h\log(2/h)}\).
Then \(\theta\to0\), \(h\log(2/h)=o(\theta)\) and \(d\theta\to\infty\).
Bertrand's postulate gives a prime in \([d\theta,2d\theta]\) at all sufficiently large degrees, so the original-seed estimate supplies witnesses there while \(p/d\to0\).
Six terms would contradict \(d\ll p\), and at most five would bound the degree by six independently of \(p\).
The conclusion is the lower bound of seven terms on these hypothetical sequences.
Whether the minimum support diverges is outside the proved bound.
