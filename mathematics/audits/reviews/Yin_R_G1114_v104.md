## Finding

The stated obstruction and all its degree and error bounds are established at their stated scopes.
The zero-rate conclusion for an arbitrary prescribed sequence holds for all sufficiently large prime delays, as the current statement explicitly says.

### The seed and its exterior factor

The local multinacci argument first bounds the modulus of every exterior root by the positive root \(\theta_r\).
Strict concavity of \(\log((2-x)x^r)\) on \([1,\theta_r]\) then excludes every intermediate modulus; equality at either endpoint forces the positive real root or the nonroot \(1\).
The resulting unique simple exterior root and absence of unit-circle roots imply irreducibility by the nonzero integer constant term of a hypothetical remaining monic factor.
This proves exactly the Pisot and minimality input needed here.
For \(r\ge3\), the leading and constant terms would force \(f_r^*=-f_r\) if they were proportional, while their interior coefficients contradict that equality.

The evaluations at \(3/2\) and \(2\) are exact.
Since \(3-A<0\), substituting \((3/2)^r\ge27/8\) gives the upper bound \(49/8-11A/8\le-107/128\), with the correct inequality direction.
The opposite sign at \(2\) supplies an exterior root for every \(N\ge4\).
On the circle, \(|f_r|=|f_r^*|>0\); hence the strict Rouché comparison for \(0<s<1\) counts \(N+r-1\) disk roots and one exterior root, including multiplicity.
Continuity of the roots as \(s\uparrow1\) gives at most one strictly exterior root, so the displayed positive root is simple.

Anti-reciprocity supplies its inverse as the only interior root.
The exterior irreducible factor has constant term of absolute value one and therefore must contain that inverse; otherwise the product of its root moduli exceeds one.
The common inverse pair forces reciprocity, and every remaining factor is cyclotomic.
A positive reciprocal quadratic unit has integral trace at least three, so its exterior root is at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2>2\).
Thus the factor here has degree at least four and is Salem, with logarithmic measure strictly exceeding \(\log(3/2)\).

### Distinct torsion roots and their multiplicities

The three conjugacy substitutions in L462v111 apply to all torsion orders, including orders one and two.
For order \(m\), their exponents \(2\), \(m/2+2\) and \(m/2+1\) are relatively prime to \(m\) in the respective odd, twice-odd and four-divisible cases.
Writing \(g=f^*\), the cleared equations for \(h=2\) have degree at most \(4r\), and that for \(h=1\) has degree at most \(2r\).
The pole of \(g/f\) at the Pisot root is genuine because the nonproportional irreducible polynomials \(f,g\) are coprime.
Its transformed arguments \(\theta^2,-\theta^2,-\theta\) are not roots of \(f\), so none of those three equations is identically zero.
Their combined root count is therefore at most \(10r\) for each delay; this does not use eventual simplicity.

Direct expansion verifies
\[
 zfH'-(Nf+zf')H=Nfg+z(f'g-fg').
\]
The derivative products cancel in degree \(2r\), whereas \(Nfg\) has nonzero leading coefficient because \(N>0\) and \(f(0)\ne0\).
Thus this polynomial has degree exactly \(2r\), and every common divisor of \(H,H'\) divides it.
In characteristic zero a root of multiplicity \(e\) contributes exactly \(e-1\) to that gcd.
The cyclotomic cofactor consequently has total degree at most \(10r+2r=12r\), including all repeated roots.
The exterior factor occurs only once because its exterior root is simple.
Subtracting the cofactor degree from \(N+r\) gives \(N-11r\le d\le N+r\), also when the lower bound is negative.

### The prime norm and the range of uniformity

At a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\), both seed values are one.
When \(p\nmid N\), the element \(\zeta^N\) is again primitive, so the absolute norm of \(\zeta^N-1\) is \(\Phi_p(1)=p\).
Multiplicativity of the integer resultant makes the nonzero resultant of the Salem factor a positive divisor of \(p\).
For that reciprocal factor, the trace extension has degree two, and the prefactor in \(\Phi_p(\alpha)=\alpha^{(p-1)/2}S_p(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) is a unit.
The absolute resultant is consequently \(B_p^2\).
The only square divisor of \(p\) is one.
The integral resultant identity makes each common integer-value gcd divide \(B_p\), proving \(G_p=B_p=1\) and both strict error bounds.

For prime delays and \(p_N=o(N)\), eventually \(3\le p_N<N\), so nondivisibility holds on that tail.
Dividing the degree bounds by \(N\) proves \(d/N\to1\), and then \(p_N/d\to0\).
For every finite cap \(3\le R<N\), any candidate odd prime \(q\le R\) fails on the included seed \(f_q\).
These quantifiers establish precisely the claimed failure of a prescribed common prime sequence.

The eventual qualification is necessary.
For example, at the excluded pair \(p=N=5\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 H_{5,5}=(z^2-1)\Phi_5(z)(z^4-2z^3+z^2-2z+1).
\]
The Salem factor has trace polynomial \(t^2-2t-1\), while \(S_5=t^2+t-1\), and their absolute resultant is \(9\).
Thus finite initial equalities \(p_N=N\), which are compatible with an eventual \(o(N)\) condition, cannot be included in an all-index assertion that both rates vanish.
The clarified claim makes no such assertion.
