## Splitting depths, mixed substitutions and the exact factor list

On 2026-11-18T16:25 a separately assigned agent for Yin reviewed the [submitted decision in C345 v390](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v390.md#decision-hereditary-exponent-submitted), its six mathematical sections and its inputs at their stated scopes.
The reviewer took no part in developing this proof and received its fixed text and review scope without the author's development discussion or an earlier finding about it.
The uniform exponent \(2R_L\), the gcd formula for every positive order, attainment of the minimum individual logarithmic measure and the refinement for the specified plane factors follow as stated.

For each root \(\eta\) encountered, some power of \(\eta\) is conjugate to the seed, so substituting that power of \(X\) in the original integer relation gives a nonzero relation of the same coefficient length.
The denominator theorem applies afresh, including for nonintegral seeds.
Along a single prime tower the total relative degree is the product of the step degrees.
Each irreducible step contributes exactly one to its \(p\)-adic valuation and each split contributes zero, since the degree of every chosen factor of a reducible prime binomial is strictly between zero and \(p\).
Thus the number of splits is bounded by \(v_p(R_L)\) on every chain of every finite length.

The product of the roots of a proper factor of \(X^p-z\) gives a \(p\)-th root of \(z^j\) in the field for some \(0<j<p\).
Bézout supplies a \(p\)-th root of \(z\) there.
For odd \(p\) the norm of a root of an irreducible \(X^{p^k}-z\) is \(z\), so the first irreducible step excludes every later split.
At two, consider any irreducible step \(y^2=z\) before a later split.
If \(y\) is a nonsquare, its conjugate \(-y\) is also a nonsquare in \(K(y)\), and the norm of a root of an irreducible \(X^{2^k}-y\) is \(-y\) for \(k\ge1\).
Induction would then exclude all later splits.
Hence the next step splits, and taking the norm of a square root of \(y\) shows that \(-z\) is a square in \(K\).
Dividing \(y\) by that square root of \(-z\) gives a square root of \(-1\) in \(K(y)\).
In all subsequent fields a nonsquare and its negative have the same square class, so a second irreducible step before a split is impossible.
Counting the single possible intervening step bounds the last-split depth by \(v_2(R_L)+1\), and the odd bound is \(v_p(R_L)\).

After processing an odd prime \(p\), a step of order \(\ell<p\) gives a relative degree \(e\le\ell\) prime to \(p\).
If \(w^\ell=z\) with \(w\) a \(p\)-th power upstairs, then \(z\) is one there too, and taking norms shows \(z^e\) is a \(p\)-th power downstairs.
Bézout gives the excluded power in the preceding field.
Decreasing order therefore preserves all processed odd exclusions, while taking two last preserves the full dyadic tower conclusion.
For primes outside \(2R_L\) the denominator theorem forces degree \(p\) for every \(p\)-th root at the terminal seed.
For arbitrary \(n\), first use the whole dyadic part of \(n\).
When the current binomial of degree \(m\) over the terminal field is irreducible, a \(p\)-th power at its root for odd \(p\) would make \((-1)^{m+1}z\), hence \(z\), a \(p\)-th power by the norm.
The field degrees therefore multiply to \(n\) even for repeated odd primes.
Over \(\mathbb Q\) the degree acquires the factor \([\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\), proving hereditary irreducibility.
The size estimate is exactly the bound for \(\log(2R_L)\).

For \(g=(A,N)\) the two quotients \(m=A/g\), \(n=N/g\) are coprime.
If \(\delta^m=\eta\), raising the roots of \(X^n-\delta\) to their \(m\)-th powers is bijective onto the roots of \(X^n-\eta\) by the permutation of \(\mu_n\).
This map commutes with the automorphisms fixing \(\delta\), so the image is already transitive under a subgroup of the automorphisms fixing \(\eta\).
The degree of a chosen root over \(\mathbb Q\) is consequently \(n[\mathbb Q(\eta):\mathbb Q]\).
The resulting substitutions of different factors have disjoint simple roots.
Primitivity and positive leading coefficients survive substitution, so Gauss's lemma gives the claimed factorization with its integer normalizations even for nonmonic \(P\).
The root formula for log measure leaves the leading coefficient term and the total contribution above each root unchanged.
Factoring a level-\(g\) factor after the further substitution \(A/g\) expresses its measure as a sum of positive terminal measures.
Every terminal factor has a conjugate root above the specified original \(\alpha\), by extension of embeddings, so the smallest terminal measure is attained among the requested radicals.

For \(X+4\) the four level-four roots are \(\pm1\pm i\), each with Gaussian valuation one at \(1+i\).
Their root fields are \(\mathbb Q(i)\), where their binomials are Eisenstein at that prime for every order.
The gcd formula thus gives relative-degree fraction \(1\) when \(4\nmid N\) and \(1/2\) for either factor when \(4\mid N\).
This proves that two is the exact single-seed denominator.
For a hereditary exponent prime to four or exactly divisible by two, the same formula at \(N=4\) would give a single factor because both \(X+4\) and \(X^2+4\) are irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The actual two quadratic factors force \(4\mid A\).

In the plane setting a nonzero monomial normalization of the primitive section supplies the required short relation.
The good-prime result applies to both signs of the nontorsion unit seed at its strict degree threshold.
For an \(\ell\)-th root the splitting field is contained in the tower adjoining \(\mu_\ell\) and then one root, of degrees dividing \(\ell-1\) and \(\ell\) respectively.
Thus every root field over the original seed along the selected towers has degree supported on the finite closure \(S\).
If \(p\notin S\), a \(p\)-th power at the terminal root would make its \(A_{F,L}\)-th power a \(p\)-th power upstairs, hence downstairs by coprime norm descent.
Every such \(p\) is good.
For \(p\in S\) with zero exponent in \(A_{F,L}\) the general denominator theorem gives the terminal exclusion directly.
Every nonempty \(S\) contains two, which is then processed last.
If \(S\) is empty, the good-prime theorem already treats every composite order with the required dyadic signs.
The reciprocal seeds have coefficient length six and the [computed good-prime set](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v389.md#reciprocal) leaves respectively \(S=\varnothing\) and \(S=\{2\}\).
The final examples apply to the individual nontorsion unit factor in formula (1).
For example a whole primitive section can have a cyclotomic factor \(X+1\) when \(s=3\), \(a\) is odd and \(b\) is even.
