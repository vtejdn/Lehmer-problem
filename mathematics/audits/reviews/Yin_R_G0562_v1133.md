## The prime is unrestricted and the support-length cost is explicit

At 2026-11-24T23:11 UTC an agent outside the development of this proof independently reviewed all sections of [C0901 v75](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v75.md#statement) for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and requested points of attention without its development discussion.
The universal inequality and the one-pair consequence hold with their printed assumptions and normalizations.
The final text resolves the reviewer's terminology question by requiring zero logarithmic measure for a discarded cofactor.

Write \(U=\mathbb Z[X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_h^{\pm1}]\) and work first over its fraction field.
Normalization in \(Z\) gives nonzero coefficients at zero and \(D\).
The second resultant input has degree exactly \(pD\), and substitution of \(X^p\) preserves the nonzero leading coefficient.
If the resultant is nonzero, \(B=(F(X^p,Z^p)-F(X,Z)^p)/p\) is a nonzero member of \(U[Z]\).
At every generic root of \(F\) the second input equals \(pB\).
The resultant root formula uses the leading coefficient \(A\) of \(F\) to power \(pD\), while the formula with \(B\) uses it to power \(\deg_Z B\).
Their ratio is exactly \(p^D A^{pD-\deg_Z B}\), with a nonnegative exponent, also for constant \(B\).
This proves integral divisibility for arbitrary leading coefficients.
For the opposite inequality the two leading-coefficient terms are \(pD\log|A(X)|\) and \(D\log|A(X^p)|\).
Combining each with the corresponding positive root logs and averaging gives \(pD\,m(F)\) and \(D\,m(F)\).
The \(D^2\) comparisons contribute at most \(D^2\log2\).
Jensen's formula and Haar invariance apply off the null sets of exceptional fibers and of zeros of the nonzero resultant.
Nonnegativity of the measure of \(R/p^D\) follows inductively from its integral coefficients.
The two inequalities yield the denominator \(p+1\), and additivity with nonnegative integral cofactor measure verifies inheritance by a nonzero multiple.

For nonvanishing, positive width and primitivity with irreducibility imply that the normalized \(F\) has no nonunit content over the base Laurent ring: such content would give a factorization.
A common factor over the fraction field therefore gives divisibility by \(F\) over the Laurent ring and forward invariance of the whole hypersurface in the torus.
The [primitive grids](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#grid) use rational primes different from \(p\) and pairwise distinct.
Their products form single rational Galois orbits, and a nonzero rational Laurent polynomial in the base misses every point on every sufficiently large such orbit: density supplies a point of nonvanishing and transitivity supplies the others.
In particular the end coefficients do not degenerate there.
Powering preserves the finite union of the nonempty fibers over each orbit.
Each fiber coordinate \(z\ne0\) has two equal iterated \(p\)-powers and hence is torsion.
If a rational Laurent polynomial not divisible by \(F\) vanished on all these fibers, its nonzero resultant with \(F\) would vanish on the same sufficiently large grids.
Monomial normalization in \(Z\) preserves the nonzero torus roots, including for a base-only test polynomial.
For zero base dimension the finite-orbit argument applies directly to the roots.

The full set of torsion points on the rational hypersurface is Galois stable, so its Zariski closure over the algebraic numbers descends to the rational base.
The rational density makes that closure the whole hypersurface, also after scalar extension.
The finitely many maximal torsion translates cover all torsion points by the [torsion-subvariety theorem](../literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001).
Thus each geometric irreducible component is a torus translate by torsion.
The [horizontal-factor argument](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) identifies the rational orbit of a codimension-one translate with a cyclotomic polynomial in its primitive character, up to a Laurent unit.
Positive width and rational irreducibility exclude an additional base factor.
The argument works in the torus without assuming properness of projection or absolute irreducibility over the rational base.

For a unit selected coefficient, the sign-normalized quadratic in \(Z\) is monic with constant coefficient one, which excludes nonunit base content.
The [trace classification](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#trace) supplies positivity except for the stated constants and signed single reciprocal sums.
A positive-measure factor then has width one or two.
At \(p=11\) the weaker of the two lower bounds is \(c=\log(11/4)/12\).
For \(|b|\ge2\), Jensen gives at least \(\log|b|>c\) with no restriction on the remainder.
For the exceptions with \(|b|=1\), a constant gives a quadratic with root-of-unity zeros, and multiplying a sum or difference of the two reciprocal pairs by \(X^a\) gives \((X^{a+j}\pm1)(X^{a-j}\pm1)\) with matching signs after normalizing the first pair's sign.
The absent-frequency assumption excludes \(j=a\).
With \(\delta=c/3\) the [finite moment comparison](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v61.md#transfer) applies to the full unfolded support, with \(k\ge2\), the printed \(\eta\) and the printed ceiling.
Trivial kernel there would yield \(m(P)\ge2c/3>c/2\), contradicting even equality at the stated threshold.
A nonzero kernel element necessarily has \(v\ne0\).
The two exponents in each of the \(N\) differences give \(|v|\le2N\) and total coefficient length at most \(2N\) in the remaining support.
This checks the term-count dependence, the degree-independent cutoff and the factor application under the stated logarithmic cofactor condition.
