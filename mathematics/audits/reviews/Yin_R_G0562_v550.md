### Signed norms and the two chords

Writing \(P(X)=X^{17}H(X+X^{-1})\) gives a monic integral \(H\) of degree 17, whose roots are the seventeen pair traces \(t_i=\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1}\), counted with multiplicity.
For irreducible \(P\), the equation \(X^2-t_iX+1=0\) gives \([\mathbb Q(t_i):\mathbb Q]\ge17\), so \(H\) is the minimal polynomial of \(t_i\).
Both \(t_i\) and \(-t_i\) have degree 17, whereas every auxiliary has degree at most five.
I also checked the argument specific to these auxiliaries by expanding \(Y^{\deg Q_j}Q_j(Y+Y^{-1})\).
The first ten expansions, in printed order, yield \(\Phi_4,\Phi_3,\Phi_2^2,\Phi_8,\Phi_5,\Phi_{12},\Phi_7,\Phi_9,\Phi_{30},\Phi_{11}\).
The last quintic reduces modulo two to \(Z^5+Z^4+Z^3+Z^2+1\), which is one at both residues and leaves remainder \(Z\) modulo \(Z^2+Z+1\).
This proves its irreducibility, and five does not divide 34.
For any monic reciprocal integer \(P\) of degree 34 with the stipulated coprimality, the products \(\prod_i Q_j(\pm t_i)\) are nonzero integers even when \(H\) factors.
Taking logarithms of their absolute values bounds both signs of \(s_1\) by \(\sum_i U(v_i)\), with \(v_i=\log|\alpha_i|\ge0\).

Hadamard's three-circles inequality applied to the exponential times the integral polynomial powers in the [ellipse argument](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/readme.md#ellipse) gives convexity in \(v\), including circles passing through an auxiliary zero.
Invariance under inversion makes the maximum even, so its chord on \([0,\log R]\) has nonnegative slope.
The pair representatives satisfy \(\sum_i v_i=\log M(P)\), including pairs on the unit circle.
At \(R=144/125\) I obtained \((R^2)^3-R^2-1=38903182631/3814697265625>0\).
Thus in the first range each \(v_i\le\log R\) and their sum is at most \(2\log R\).
In the second range their sum itself is at most \(\log(1177/1000)\).
Summing the respective chords gives \(15(0.2107)+2(0.7256)=4.6117\) and \(16(0.2107)+0.8225=4.1937\), and the first trace is integral.
For the comparison with Lehmer's measure, its degree-ten polynomial is \(X^5Q_{21}(X+X^{-1})\).
The signs of \(Q_{21}\) at \(-2,-3/2,-1,0,1,2,3\) are \(-,+,-,+,-,-,+\), locating four roots in \((-2,2)\) and one above two.
The degree-ten polynomial is negative at one and positive at \(1177/1000\) by rational substitution, so its unique exterior root lies between them.

I then used a separate calculation by isolation of all real stationary points.
For \(r=e^v\), put \(A=r+r^{-1}\), \(B=r-r^{-1}\) and \(z=Ax+iB\sqrt{1-x^2}\).
I computed the rational polynomials \(S_j(x)=|Q_j(z)|^2\) by Horner evaluation using \((iB\sqrt{1-x^2})^2=B^2(x^2-1)\).
