## Independent review of the bound and the zero-infimum equivalence

At 2026-11-20T09:05 UTC an agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the complete [v501 proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v501.md#statement).

The numerical bound, both limits and the threshold equivalence in v501 follow with the stated quantifiers.

For each qualifying choice, the tangency bound counts the two prime-factor incidences separately, even if they have the same minimum.
Thus \(0<h_1\le(1+q/p)\eta\le3\eta\) and \(0<h_2\le(1+p/q)\eta\le2\eta\), where \(h_i=\log M(P_i)\).
In particular \(\log H>0\), the degrees fit the width in v500, and \(M(P_i)<Q^3\).
The roots are nonzero nontorsion units of degree \(e_i\ge2\), since rational units are roots of unity.
Voutier defines absolute logarithmic height on p. 1 and states Corollary 2 on p. 4 of the retained author edition.
Its left side \(e_i h_{\mathrm{abs}}(\alpha_i)=h_i\) exceeds \(2/(\log(3e_i))^3\).
Writing \(f(x)=x\exp((2/x)^{1/3})\), this gives \(e_ih_i>f(h_i)/3\).
The derivative equals \(\exp((2/x)^{1/3})(1-(2/x)^{1/3}/3)\).
It is negative on \(0<x<2/27\), so \(3\eta<2/27\) gives \(e_i h_i>f(3\eta)/3=\eta\exp((2/(3\eta))^{1/3})\).

Rational coprimality gives \(R\ne0\).
At the eventual width \(b(p)\ge2\), the uniform estimate and divisibility in v500 give \(\log|R|\ge(1-1/b(p))\Gamma\) with one onset for every partner and qualifying choice.
For distinct minima the sum of logarithmic moduli in each root list is zero because its minimum is monic with unit constant term.
Holding one first-list root fixed in \(F\) and summing over the second list gives at least \(e_2\max(p\log|a_i|,0)\), by bounding the sum both by \(e_2p\log|a_i|\) and by zero.
Interchanging lists gives \(F\ge\max(e_2 p h_1,e_1 q h_2)\).
Consequently \(4F\ge4\sqrt{pq(e_1h_1)(e_2h_2)}\).
The derivative of \(\sqrt t/(1+t)\) is \((1-t)/(2\sqrt t(1+t)^2)\), so its minimum on \([1,2]\) is \(\sqrt2/3\).
Division by \(\log H=\eta(p+q)\) now yields the claimed coefficient \(4\sqrt2/3\).
For a repeated minimum \(F\ge e q h\), and subtracting \(2e(p+q)h\) from \([4eq-2(q-p)]h\) leaves \(2(e-1)(q-p)h\ge0\).
The resulting coefficient \(2\) exceeds \(4\sqrt2/3\).
Along any sequence in the statement put \(z=(2/(3\eta))^{1/3}\).
Then \(z\to\infty\), \(1-1/b(p)\to1\), and \(\eta e^z=2e^z/(3z^3)\to\infty\), which proves divergence with both normalizations.

The pointwise tangency inequality gives \(0\le\lambda\le\eta\), proving necessity of the zero infimum.
Conversely, fix one \(t_j>0\) below the stated three upper bounds.
When \(\lambda=0\), the v491 threshold theorem applies at \(Q_j=\exp(t_j)\in(1,6/5)\) with relative interval parameter \(2\) and the same supplied \(b(p)\to\infty\).
For this fixed \(j\) it gives a bound \(K_j\) on the number of rejected partners for every sufficiently large first prime, and a passing row has \(\eta<t_j<\log Q\).
Kedlaya's Theorem 4 on p. 3, with modulus one and \(A=2\), gives \(\pi(2p)-\pi(p)\sim p/\log p\).
After increasing the onset for this \(j\), the interval \((p,2p]\) therefore contains more than \(K_j\) partners at every such \(p\).
Choosing the first primes strictly increasingly produces a row below each prescribed \(\tau_j\).
The onsets and exclusion counts may depend on \(j\), while the resultant estimate uses each row's own cost and the single onset from v500.
This equivalence leaves the value of \(\lambda\) open.
