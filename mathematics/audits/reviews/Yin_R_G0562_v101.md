## Root radii, the exact square-root range and signed exponent mass

At 2026-11-17T00:42 UTC a separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin examined the complete fixed C345v101 argument submitted at 2026-11-17T00:39 UTC.
The reviewer had no role in the development of this cofactor argument and received its fixed text without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on it.
The reviewer had previously contributed to the C345v90 circle integral used as an input and checked its applicability here.
All sections of C345v101 pass at their stated scope.

For a monic cyclotomic product the sign in \(X^dA(1/X)=sA(X)\) is \(s=(-1)^{e_1}=A(0)\).
For nonzero common inverse roots of \(G\) the two evaluations give \(-sa\alpha^{k-d}+a\alpha^{-k}=0\), so \(\alpha^{d-2k}=s\).
A negative exponent or a cancellation changing the actual degree of \(G\) does not change this calculation.
Off center the root is torsion, while at \(2k=d,\ s=-1\) the equation is impossible.
A primitive reciprocal irreducible factor has inverse pairs, and the zero root only supplies \(X\).
Every positive-measure integer divisor therefore has content or primitive leading coefficient of absolute value at least two, or contains a monic nonreciprocal irreducible factor covered by Smyth's theorem.
All integer factors have multiplicative measure at least one, proving the asserted bound.
A constant \(G\) after cancellation causes no exception, an overall sign preserves measure, and at \(a=0\) every integer divisor of the monic product has unit content and measure one.

At the center \(d\) is positive and even, so the shifted term leaves both endpoint coefficients unchanged.
Each integral log-distance to a circle root has Poisson extension equal to its interior log-distance, by damping its Fourier coefficients at the interior radius.
Summing over roots gives the identity even with repeated boundary zeros.
The normalized mean of \(L\) is zero and \(\int L^-=u\), hence the Poisson maximum \((1+e^{-t})/(1-e^{-t})=1+2/(e^t-1)\le1+2/t\) gives the lower bound \(-(1+2/t)u\).
At an interior root of the central shift this implies
\[
              \tfrac d2 t^2\le(\log|a|+u)t+2u.
\]
Its positive quadratic bound is at most \(2(\log|a|+u)/d+2\sqrt{u/d}\) since \(\log|a|\ge0\).
For an exterior root the reciprocal number is an interior root of \(sG^*(X)=A(X)+saX^{d/2}\), with the same scalar modulus and the same \(L\).
This covers anti-reciprocal parents even though the shifted polynomial may lack any inverse pairs.
Every monic cofactor has constant of absolute value one.
Consequently the sum of its root logarithms is zero and their absolute sum equals \(2m(Q)\), counting multiplicities, with zero sum also for the empty cofactor.
This checks (1) including its factor \(1/2\) and the entire exterior-root cost.

Taking \(b=|a|\) in C345v90 gives \(Y=m(G)+\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\ge\max(u,\log|a|)\), since the integrated maximum dominates both \(L^+\) and the constant \(\log|a|\).
For \(e\le K\sqrt d,\ m(F)\le B\), (3) and \(d\ge64K^2\) give \(Y\le B+C_0+Y/4+K\sqrt Y\).
The inequality \(K\sqrt Y\le Y/4+K^2\) yields the stated bound on \(Y\).
If \(m(G)\to\infty\), the first error in (3) divided by \(m(G)\) is \(O_K(d^{-1/2})\) and the second is \(O_K(m(G)^{-1/2})\).
Taking \(K=1\) gives \(Y\le Y_B\), and for the stated \(c_B\) the second term of \(e\tau\) is at most \((\log2)/8\) while the first tends to zero uniformly.
Thus \(e\tau<(\log2)/4\) holds strictly for sufficiently large \(d\).
Any noncyclotomic monic irreducible divisor of \(Q\) would have degree \(f>1\) since the only linear monic unit factors are \(X\pm1\).
Dimitrov's recorded house lower bound forces \(f\tau\ge(\log2)/4\), contradicting \(f\le e\).
This proves exact equality in the stated window and under bounded \(m(F)\) with \(e=o(\sqrt d)\).
For a parent class with a gap, a positive shrinking divisor sequence of bounded degree is impossible by coefficient finiteness.
On its unbounded-degree subsequence the choice \(B=1\) forces eventual equality with a positive parent measure, giving the claimed gap.

In the signed binomial expansion, Möbius inversion of the Fourier coefficient at frequency \(v\) gives the bound \(2\|L\|_1\prod_{p\mid v}(1+1/p)\) without any bound on the number of occupied conductors.
Each large-prime factor is at most \(p^\eta\), while the finitely many remaining primes contribute a constant.
Thus summing at \(v\le T\) costs \(O_\eta(\|L\|_1 T^{1+\eta})\).
For the rest, \(v\mid n,\ v>T\) requires \(n>T\) and each conductor contributes at most \(2^{\omega(n)}\) distinct nonzero Möbius terms, weighted by \(e_n\).
The identity \(n 2^{\omega(n)}/\varphi(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}2p/(p-1)\) gives \(2^{\omega(n)}\ll_\eta n^{-1+\eta}\varphi(n)\).
For \(0<\eta<1\) summation uses \(\sum e_n\varphi(n)=d\) and costs \(O_\eta(dT^{-1+\eta})\).
Putting \(T=\sqrt d\) proves (4) for the requested \(\epsilon\), including the contribution from \(n=1\) in the low range.

For an interior root with \(t\ge1/d\), each numerator binomial has modulus at least \(1-e^{-t}\ge1/(2d)\) and each inverse binomial has modulus at least \(1/2\).
Their product is bounded below by \((4d)^{-S}\), and the possible overall sign has modulus one.
For \(t<1/d\), (5) is automatic because \(S\ge1\), while the exterior estimate uses the same reversal as above.
For fixed multiplicative-interval parameters, C345v98 bounds \(S\) whenever \(Y\) is bounded, with no condition on cancellation between positive and negative binomial exponents.
Thus \(e\tau_S=O(\log(4d)/\sqrt d)\) in the asserted bounded-measure range.
The house contradiction gives eventual equality, transferring the parent gap to every fixed \(K\).
If \(S\to\infty\), bounded degrees would admit only finitely many monic circle-root polynomials and hence finitely many exponent vectors.
C345v98 gives divergence of \(m(G)\), so the earlier ratio limit applies.

For at most \(H\) distinct factors, C345v94 gives \(S\le HE2^r\) and \(E\ll_H(1+r)\|L\|_1\), also for the conductor \(1\).
An occupied conductor with \(r\) primes has \(\varphi(n)\ge r!\), by ordering its prime divisors and multiplying the bounds \(p_j-1\ge j\).
Thus \((1+r)2^r\log(4d)=d^{o(1)}\) uniformly, either with bounded \(r\) or from \(\log(r!)\) for growing \(r\).
For \(e\le Kd^{1-\delta}\), (5) now gives \(e\tau_S/Y=O_{H,K}(d^{-\delta+o(1)})\).
A bounded remainder measure forces bounded \(Y\) by absorption, hence \(e\tau_S\to0\), exact cyclotomic removal and inheritance of the parent gap.
If the maximum of the prime count and multiplicity tends to infinity, the degree does also and C345v94 gives divergent whole measure.
Then \(m(Q)/m(G)\to0\) proves divergence of the divisor measure.
