### Local lifts, periods and positive density

In an arithmetic progression of positive period \(M\) with infinitely many admissible exponents, one can keep a single admissible residue modulo \(m=\operatorname{lcm}(M,30)\).
For odd exponents prime to three the cyclotomic contribution on that residue is \(X+1\) with or without \(\Phi_{10}\), so \(n_b=b/2+O(1)\).
The derivative modulo three of \(A_c\) is \(cX^{c-1}(X^{12}+X^9-1)\), coprime to \(A_c\) by the unit endpoint and the pole resultant.
Thus the old parent and its trace core are squarefree modulo three.
For an irreducible trace factor of degree \(u\), Frobenius to the \(u\)-th power fixes or exchanges the two lifts of a trace root.
The other trace roots are Frobenius translates, so all their lifts have orders dividing the same one of \(3^u-1,3^u+1\).
The torsion roots contribute a divisor of \(10\).
Taking the product upper bound on the least common multiple, then bounding the number of distinct degree-\(u\) factors by \(3^u/u\), gives \(t_c\le 10\cdot3^j\exp(\sum_{u=1}^j1/u)\le10\mathrm e j3^j\).

In the free monic quotient by \(A_c\), cubing \(1+3^h v\) puts it in \(1+3^{h+1}R\) for \(h\ge1\).
Consequently \(X^{b-c}=1\) in the quotient modulo \(3^k\) on the stated residue class, and the two parent sections coincide there.
Monicity permits cancellation of the same cyclotomic polynomial over \(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z\).
Writing the resulting divisibility in the Laurent ring, removing the monomial units and comparing coefficients in the basis \(1,X\) over the trace ring yields \(H_c\mid H_b\) modulo \(3^k\).
The complement is coprime to \(H_c\) modulo three by squarefreeness of the new trace, so Hensel uniqueness identifies these two monic reductions with the stated local subsets at precision \(k\).
Their signed norms are both \(1\) modulo \(3^k\), by the integral odd pole sign and multiplicativity.
For an independent arithmetic check, at \(c=19\) the parent factors modulo three into degrees \(1,3,3,8,16\), each once.
Starting at the least common multiple of \(3^v-1\) over these degrees and removing prime factors by modular powering gave \(t_c=3497546\).
Polynomial multiplication in the monic quotient verified \(X^{t_c3^{h-1}}=1\bmod 3^h\) for \(h=1,2,3,4\).

The exterior count of every nontorsion factor is at least one, so the degree comparison with \(a=3\) gives rational factor degree \(\gg b\) uniformly.
A rational degree-\(j\) trace divisor would give a degree-\(2j\) nontorsion divisor, and rationality of its local complement would give the same short rational divisor by monic division.
This proves falsity when \(j>0,\ j=o(b)\).
For fixed \(c,k\) the full resulting subprogression apart from finitely many exponents has relative density \(M/(mt_c3^{k-1})>0\) in the original progression.
This supplies the claimed lower density for each fixed precision.

### Growing precision and the overlap bound

For fixed \(0<\lambda<1\), \(j=n_c\to\infty\) on the chosen residue.
The spacing \(mt_c3^{k-1}\) divided by \(j^2 3^{j+k}\) is \(O_m(1/j)\), so the first eligible \(b\) at or above that target has \(\log_3 b=j+k+O(\log j)\).
Choosing \(k=\lceil\lambda j/(1-\lambda)\rceil\) yields both stated limits, while \(k=j^2\) gives the endpoint with \(j\to\infty\).
The two subsets are proper since \(j=o(b)\).
Passing the signed test at precision \(k\) implies passing at all smaller positive precisions, so the least recovering precision exceeds \(k\) on this subsequence wherever C398 gives eventual recovery.

For distinct exponents prime to three, subtraction of the two section equations forces \(x^{b-c}=1\) at a common root and hence \(x^N=1\) with \(N\) the prime-to-three part of \(|b-c|\).
The Frobenius identity \(x^c=f(x)^3\) and the invertibility of \(3\bmod N\) give \(f(x)=x^e\) for \(3e\equiv c\bmod N\), also when \(N=1\).
Among \(J+1\) multiples of \(e\) a shortest circular gap gives the required \(|u|\le N/(J+1)\) and \(1\le|v|\le J\), allowing \(u=0\) if residues coincide.
The rational function \(X^u f^v\) is nonconstant since the numerator of \(f\) has a nonzero zero outside its poles.
Indeed the difference of numerator and denominator is \(-X-X^3\), and at \(X^2=-1\) the numerator is \(-X\ne0\).
Clearing both signs of the exponents produces a nonzero equation of degree at most \(|u|+4|v|\), bounded by \(4\sqrt N+4\) at \(J=\lceil\sqrt N/2\rceil\).
Each common trace root contributes two different nonzero parent roots, since coalescence at \(X=\pm1\) would contradict squarefreeness of the parent.
Distinct trace roots give disjoint pairs.
This proves (4) with either parity and rules out a degree comparable to \(b\) copied from \(c=O(b)\).

### The hypothesis for a false divisor near the Salem degree

Set \(L=(2\cdot3^{k-1})d_{\max}\).
In a shortest product-one subsequence of the unit residues the prefix products up to the penultimate term are distinct, so the total degree of a removed subsequence is at most \(L\).
Iterating partitions all the factors because the remaining product stays one.
The first prefix of blocks to cross \(\alpha n_b\), followed by two successive extensions, has three distinct degrees, all below \(n_b\) by \(\alpha n_b+3L<n_b\).
The total remaining degree after the first crossing exceeds \(2L\), ensuring the extensions exist.
Each passes, whereas the at most two simple nontorsion factors permit at most two proper nonconstant rational trace divisors.
Therefore at least one of the three passes falsely.
At \(\alpha=1/3\), \(3^k d_{\max}=o(n_b)\) makes the entire interval \(n_b/3+o(n_b)\).
On an actual split subsequence, the Salem factor has log measure tending to \((\log\beta_4)/3\) and \(bh\to\log\beta_4\), so its polynomial degree is asymptotic to \(b/3\), its trace degree to \(b/6\), and its share of \(n_b\) to \(1/3\).
The precision construction alone supplies neither the local-degree hypothesis nor an actual split.
