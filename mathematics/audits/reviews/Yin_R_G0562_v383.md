# The cubic minimum at fixed Pisot norm and two limits of Pisot numbers

<a id="fixed-norm-review"></a>
## Independent finding

At 2026-11-18T12:33 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin checked the [fixed-norm calculation](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v159.md#minimum), including the square-constant consequence and both limiting families.
The reviewer had no role in developing this argument and received no development discussion, author explanation or earlier review of it.
The cubic gives the minimum in the two stated noninteger classes.
The positive-constant exclusion, the rescaled lower bound of two, and the two decreasing sequences with unit limit all hold at their stated scopes.

**The cubic and the norm conventions.**
For a nonlinear Pisot root the moduli of all other conjugates are strictly less than one, so their product gives \(\theta>|f(0)|=q\).
For \(p_q\) the discriminant of \((q-1)x^2-qx+q\) is \(q(4-3q)<0\), which excludes negative roots of the cubic.
It has exactly one positive root, larger than \(q\), and its complex pair has squared modulus \(q/\beta_q<1\).
Any proper monic integer factor omitting \(\beta_q\) would have nonzero constant of modulus less than one.
Thus the cubic is minimal and its field norm is \((-1)^3(-q)=q\).
Every number of field norm \(+q\) already has absolute constant \(q\), so the same example attains equality in both classes.
Uniqueness of the monic minimal polynomial makes the bound strict for \(f(0)=+q\).
The degree-one integer lies outside both nonlinear minima.

**The Schur estimates.**
Signing the numerator makes \(F(0)=q\) for either sign of \(f(0)\).
The first-coefficient identity gives \(j=\delta(q+t-H'(0))\) with \(|H'(0)|\le2\) and \(q+t-2>0\).
At \(\theta\le\beta_q\), the identity \((\beta_q-q)\beta_q(\beta_q+1)=q\) and \(t<1/q\) imply \(0<j<1+1/q\), forcing \(j=1\).
For \(A=\operatorname{sgn}(f(0))f\), the strict boundary inequality \(|qg|>|A|\) shows that \(A-qg\) has exactly the one interior zero already seen at the origin.
The reversed polynomial has its unique disk zero at \(t\), where \(A(t)\ne0\) by irreducibility and nonunit constant.
Consequently \(S\) is Schur and \(S(t)=qt\), including for quadratic candidates.

With \(u_1=q^2,\ u_2=q^3+v,\ a=1-q^{-2}\), direct expansion gives
\[
 S'(0)=q-(q^2-1)u_2/q^4,\qquad
 G(0)=S'(0)/(1-a^2)=\frac{q^3-(q^2-1)v}{2q^2-1}.
\]
At \(v=q-2\) the last numerator exceeds its denominator by \(q-1\).
It decreases with \(v\), so integrality forces \(v\ge q-1\) and \(b=G(0)\le b_*<1\).
The real Schwarz–Pick upper bound \((b+t)/(1+bt)\) increases with \(b\) and also holds at \(b=-1\) by constancy.
Clearing the positive denominators in the comparison with \(G(t)\) gives
\[
 (qt-a)(1+b_*t)-t(1-aqt)(b_*+t)
 =a\big(qt^3+qt^2+(q-1)t-1\big)\le0.
\]
Substitution of \(t=1/\theta\) yields \(p_q(\theta)\ge0\).
This rules out every candidate below its unique positive root.

The attribution agrees with the journal editions.
Boyd defines the associated quotient on printed p. 1246 and gives the cubic following (40) on p. 1256, with a reference to Dufresnoy and Pisot for the method.
His quadratic expression concerns a proposed least limit point of the associated-function class.
Dufresnoy and Pisot's normalization on p. 69 and their rational case in Theorems 1 and 2 on pp. 81–82 distinguish strict preterminal inequalities from endpoint cases.
The local Schur argument proves the minimum directly and handles terminal degrees without assuming strict preterminal estimates there.
The source check covered these statements and their use.

**Square constants.**
At \(q=d^2\) the cubic identity and \(q<\beta_q<q+1/(q+1)\) give \(q(\beta_q-q)\to1\).
For positive polynomial constant the cited first-branch exclusion supplies a fixed positive lower bound on \(\delta\) at \(j=1\), while \(1\ge\delta(q+t-2)\) would force \(\delta\to0\) as \(q\to\infty\).
Hence eventually \(j\ge2\) uniformly, and then \(\delta\ge2/(q+t+2)>2q/(q+1)^2\).
Taking the infimum within each prescribed even degree and multiplying by \(q=d^2\) gives the claimed lower limit of at least two.
The lower bound for the unscaled excess tends to zero.

**A fixed Salem grid.**
With the admissible auxiliary inverse held fixed, the two grid identities \(z^kC_k(z+z^{-1})=z^{2k}+1\) and \((z-1)z^{k-1}D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^{2k-1}-1\) give precisely \(f_k=z^{2k-1}A+B\), including \(k=1\).
The grid theorem supplies simple Pisot minimal polynomials of the asserted even degrees and a fixed lower bound \(\theta_k>q+\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2>\sigma\).
Since \(A\) is monic and \(A(1)=2P_0(1)<0\), it has a positive exterior zero.
Uniform convergence of \(A+z^{1-2k}B\) on small closed disks outside the unit circle gives its uniqueness and simplicity by counting zeros there, and also gives \(\theta_k\to\lambda\).
On the circle, multiplying the return identity by \((1+\bar z)\overline{P_0}\) gives twice the real part in the claimed squared-modulus difference.
A circle zero of \(A\) would force \(B=0\).
Unless \(z=-1\), the two equations imply \(f_0=P_0=0\), impossible for the Pisot polynomial on the circle.
At \(-1\), \(B(-1)=-A(-1)\) and \(f_k(-1)>0\) by the even degree and locations of the roots, so \(A(-1)\ne0\).
Thus its unique exterior root and unit constant make \(A\) an irreducible Pisot polynomial by the factor argument above.
The uniform lower bound puts \(\lambda>\sigma\), which gives \(P_0(\lambda)>0\) and \(B(\lambda)=-(1+\lambda^{-1})P_0(\lambda)<0\).
Therefore \(\theta_k>\lambda\) and \(A(\theta_k)>0\).
The successive polynomial values \(f_{k+1}(\theta_k)=(\theta_k^2-1)\theta_k^{2k-1}A(\theta_k)>0\) give strict descent to \(\lambda\).
The even-degree approximants have norm \(+q\), and the odd-degree limit of constant one has norm \(-1\).

**The explicit sequence at every \(q\ge2\).**
The quadratic input satisfies the return identity by coefficient comparison and gives \(B=q(1-z)\).
Here \(A(-1)=-2q,\ A(0)=1,\ A(1)=-2,\ A(q)=-q^2-2q+1\).
The cubic has two roots strictly inside the circle and its third root \(\lambda>q\).
The quadratic \(P_0\) has no circle root, so the circle modulus inequality is strict away from \(-1\).
For \(0\le s<1\), \(z^{2k-1}A+sB\) has \(2k+1\) interior roots counted with multiplicity.
At \(s=1\) its value \(4q\) at \(-1\) excludes the only possible boundary zero, preserving the count.
The monic degree-\(2k+2\) polynomial of constant \(q\) has its exterior root above \(\lambda\), since \(B(\lambda)<0\).
This proves irreducibility as before, and the same exterior convergence and sign test give strict descent to the norm-\(-1\) cubic unit for every fixed \(q\).
This construction provides a limit point and makes no minimality assertion for that limit.
