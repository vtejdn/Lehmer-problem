The reviewer took no part in the development and received the fixed claim and requested scope without proof-generation dialogue, author explanations or prior findings on v794.
The reviews of v789, v790 and v793 were consulted for their coverage of the inputs.
The earlier torsion certificate and the marked height theorem were used at their reviewed interfaces.

Expanding \(A\) gives \(U^4-7U^3+10U^2+7U+1\).
Thus \(F(U,1)=2U^4+21U^2+2\), and \(A(\pm1)=A^*(\pm1)=12\).
For all \(k,l>0\) the highest term of the section is \(X^{4k+2l}\) and its constant is one.
A monic rational factor therefore reduces to a monic polynomial of the same degree with nonzero constant at either prime.
An irreducible constituent of its reduction gives a nonzero root in \(\mathbb F_{p^j}\) for some \(1\le j\le d\), including when the reduction has repeated roots.
If \(d\le D\), then the first divisibility hypothesis forces \(x^{2k}=1\) in characteristic three, and evaluating at \(x^k=\pm1\) leaves \(x^l\ne0\).
In characteristic two the second hypothesis applies at this particular \(j\), so substitution of \(x^k=1\) or \(x^l=1\) gives \(x^l\ne0\) or \(x^{2k}\ne0\), respectively.
Both degree bounds hold even without coprimality of \(k,l\).

For the exterior counts, every factor on these positive sections is nontorsion by v793.
In v790 the marked height calculation at the two poles above a conjugate pair of roots of \(q_j\) gives \(s b_j(\log\beta_j+O(e^{-cs}))/d\to\frac12\log\beta_j\).
The unit and small-height conditions in this calculation hold for all the factors along escaping positive primitive directions with \(k\to\infty\) and \(s\to\infty\).
The interior conjugate cannot contribute a visit at a pole, since \(|z^k|<1\) implies \(|z^l|<1\).
Radial localization puts every exterior conjugate over one of the two disjoint disks.
Boundedness of \(b_j\) enters v790 only after the normalized limits, when deducing eventual equality of the integer counts.
Since \(\log\beta_j>0\), division gives \(s b_j/d\to1/2\) without a bound on \(b_j\).
Summing gives \(sH/d\to1\), and a sequence of factors violating uniformity would contradict that same limit.

For \(k=L_3(D)\), one has \(2\mid k\), \(3\nmid k\), and \(\gcd(k,rk+1)=1\).
For \(k=6L_2(D)+1\), one has \(k\equiv1\bmod 6\), \(\gcd(k,r)=1\), and \(L_2(D)\mid l=r(k-1)\).
In both cases \(k\to\infty\), \(s\sim r\asymp\sqrt D\) and every \(d>D\), so the uniform estimate forces the minimum exterior count to diverge.
This lower bound controls the absolute count and is consistent with a vanishing proportion among the \(2k\) exterior roots of the section.

At \((U,V)=(0,i)\), the nonzero partial derivative in \(V\) makes \(U\) a local parameter, so \(T^m-U\) is Eisenstein over the valuation ring of the parent field \(K\).
At \((U,1/V)=(\beta_3,0)\), the nonzero derivative \(A'(\beta_3)\) instead makes \(w=1/V\) a local parameter.
The image of \(U\) is a unit there and any \(m\)-th root of its nonzero constant coefficient lifts in \(\mathbb C[[w]]\).
Evaluation at that series gives an embedding of \(K(T)\) over \(K\), since \(T^m-U\) is irreducible.
The induced valuation has group \(\mathbb Z\) because its values are integral and it takes value one on \(w\).
Hence \(Y^m-w\) is Eisenstein again, and inversion of its root gives the full degree \(m^2\) for the two radicals.
The Laurent algebra obtained by adjoining the two radicals over the parent coordinate ring is free on the \(m^2\) indicated monomials.
Its generic fiber surjects onto the radical field of the same dimension.
Freeness then embeds the algebra into that field, so the power pullback generates a prime ideal.
Composing any nonsingular integral monomial substitution with its signed adjugate gives the positive scalar matrix \(|\det M|I\).
Injectivity on each support preserves every nonunit factor, hence irreducibility for the scalar matrices suffices for every such substitution.

I checked the two alternatives and the factor multiplicities in the published Conjecture 2.1 on pages 364–365 and the nonconstant-input hypothesis of Theorem 2.4 on pages 365–366.
The rectangle of exponents makes \(F\) noncyclotomic, and \(U^4V^2F(U^{-1},V^{-1})=F(U,V)\) makes it self-inversive.
Each exceptional integral linear condition in dimension two contains just two primitive lattice points of opposite sign.
On every remaining positive primitive direction, the conjecture supplies a pullback with a single irreducible rational factor of multiplicity one by the isogeny result.
Its specialization is the given section up to a Laurent unit.
The section has positive degree, constant one and no root of unity, so removing its cyclotomic part gives the same irreducibility conclusion in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
This verifies the implication from the rational conjecture for this fixed \(F\).
The proved non-self-inversive case excludes this \(F\), and as an element over \(\mathbb C(z)\) the polynomial is constant in \(z\).
