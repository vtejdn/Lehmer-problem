For the full filter I checked the cited uniform estimate at \(1\le j\le d\), including its use of the actual degree of a power and the multiplicities of that power's conjugates.
Reflection commutes with positive integer powers, giving \(|v_j|/d\ll\sqrt L\log^2(2/L)\) at small \(L\).

The two positive-part terms cost \(2(d-1)L\), smoothing costs \(d(d-1)\log(1/\rho)\), and the diagonal costs at most \(d\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\).
This gives \(H(2/d)\le dW\) with an absolute choice of the additive constant also ensuring \(W\ge1\), because \(H\ge0\).

The Fourier expansion of each positive Poisson kernel gives \(d^2+2S(t)\) after summation, including ordered off-diagonal pairs with nonzero phases.
Writing the two radii as \(r,hr\), the ratio of kernels is smallest at cosine one, with minimum \((1+hr)(1-r)/((1-hr)(1+r))\).
For \(x\le t\le1\), the bounds \(1-r\gg x\) and \(1-hr\le(1-r)+(t-x)\) give an absolute lower bound \(cx/t\).
Subtracting the constant term costs at most \(d^2/2\) in the resulting estimate on \(S(t)\).
Since \(-H'=S\), integration over \([x,Kx]\) leaves \(cx\log K\,S(x)\) and error at most \(Kxd^2/2\).
For \(x=2/d\), \(K=\max(2,W)\) is admissible at large \(d\), and both \(dW\) and \(Kxd^2/2\) are \(O(dW)\).
Moreover \(\log K\) is comparable with \(\log(2+W)\); \(e^{-xj}\ge e^{-2}\) through order \(d\).
These facts give (7) with the denominator shown, and bounded exceptional degrees are covered by \(|v_j|\le d\).
