## The two-sign gap and its delay-two consequences

For \(1<b<2\), an unbounded minimum term count among cyclotomic multiples would give an infinite sequence of distinct seeds whose minima tend to infinity.
Lazami's fixed-pair extraction applies to that sequence after choosing \(2-\lambda>b\).
The leading monic Pisot polynomial of the pair has no unit-circle zero, so damping the other term makes Rouché's inequality strict everywhere on the circle.
At the limit the actual seed accounts for the only possible exterior root, with multiplicity one.
Monicity and the nonzero constant then make the complementary factors cyclotomic.
The fixed pair bounds the term counts on the extracted sequence, which contradicts the choice of its minima.
Reversing the resulting \(A=Cf\) gives \(A^*=\tau C f^*\) for \(\tau=\pm1\).
Thus \(CH_{N,\sigma}\) is an integer polynomial with at most twice the bounded number of terms, and its leading term survives because \(N\ge1\).
The fixed-support gap in logarithmic Mahler measure gives a gap above multiplicative measure one, uniformly in both signs and the delay.
For \(b\le1\) the seed range is empty.

For \(q=1\) and \(\theta\ge19/10\), the two factors in the radial bound decrease in \(\theta\), since each derivative has the sign of \(1-r^2\).
At \(b=19/10\) the derivative in \(r\) of their product at one is \(760/261<3\).
This gives a single \(1<r_0<b\) at which every delay \(N\ge3\) forces a real root larger than \(r_0\).
The minimum of \(r_0-1\), the compact-seed gap at \(b\) and \(1/2\) is a positive absolute choice for \(c\).
All three estimates permit the degree and sign to vary.
Among measures strictly greater than one tending to one, the delay-two trace estimate bounds \(\theta\) above and the compact-seed gap rules out every unit-seed cluster value below two.
The nonunit estimate leaves the stated strict lower and asymptotic upper bounds on the norm-two subsequence.
The strict excess matters in these applications, since \(f=z-2\) at delay three gives a cyclotomic minus associate, and \(f=z^2-z-1\) at delay one gives \(zf-f^*=z^3-1\).

I checked the classification and cofactor interfaces against Lazami's final theorem and Boyd's 1996 Section 2, the logarithmic normalization and exponent-uniformity against Dobrowolski and Smyth's Theorem 2, and the delay-one representation against Smyth's Section 3.1.
Boyd's 1978 computation on p. 1255 covers \((1,1.3]\) at delays four through eight and \([1.1,1.3]\) at delay three using the sign normalized in his equation (28).
These historical search ranges supply context for the theorem.
The independent review covers the local deductions and the applicability of the cited statements.
The original classification and sparse-gap proofs, the proofs cited by the survey and the historical enumeration were outside its proof and computation scope.

## The radial bound, the integer exceptions and the limiting bands

At fixed conjugate modulus \(a=|\beta|\), the squared modulus of the interior factor decreases in \(\Re\beta\) and attains its maximum at \(\beta=-a\).
Clearing the positive denominators gives the stated nonnegative difference \(h(ab)-h(a)h(b)\).
The product estimate then uses exactly \(\prod a=q/\theta\), including the empty product for integer seeds.
Since \(f\) is monic and \(\theta\) is its only positive root above one, \(f(r)<0\) on \((1,\theta)\).
Strict radial dominance therefore fixes the sign of either associate at \(r\) and gives \(M_N>r\).
Expanding the trace comparison gives the factor \(r\theta(r^2-1)(r+r^{-1}-\theta+q/\theta)\).
If \(\theta-q/\theta>2\), its upper test radius \(R\) satisfies \(1<R<\theta\) by monotonicity of \(x+x^{-1}\) for \(x>1\).
Passage to \(R\) gives the weak trace inequality, also for \(N>2\).
For \(q=1,2\) the quadratic examples have negative interior root of modulus \(q/\theta\), are irreducible since \(q+1\) is nonsquare, and give the indicated cyclotomic quartic.

The sign normalization gives integer \(u_1\) and \(B(0)=q/\theta\) for either constant sign.
The only possible disk pole of the quotient is at \(1/\theta\) and is removed in \(B\).
For a nonlinear seed \(q/\theta<1\), so the Cayley transform and Schwarz's lemma give \(|H'(0)|\le2\).
Directly, \(B'(0)=u_1/\theta+q(\theta^{-2}-1)\), which gives
\[
 u_1-q^2+1=(\theta-q)(q+\theta^{-1}-H'(0)).
\]
This integer is positive for \(q\ge2\), giving the strict numerical cutoffs \(20/9\) and \(51/16\) for \(q=2,3\).
At \(r=3/2\), the coarse cubic comparison reduces to \(\theta>13/6\).
For the remaining integer seed two, the quartic comparison covers every \(N\ge4\).
At delay two the trace for a nonlinear norm-three seed exceeds \(51/16-16/17>13/6\), while every \(q\ge4\) gives trace at least three.
In the two exceptional integer cases the minus polynomials factor as stated, and each plus polynomial is negative at \(3/2\).

The resulting upper quadratic inequality at delay two gives the claimed upper bounds separately at \(q=1,2\).
The integer seed two occurs with just two fixed outputs, so it cannot recur on a subsequence with strict excess tending to zero.
At delay three, the derivative test at a limit point with \(q=1\) gives the necessary inequality \(3\le4\theta/(\theta^2-1)\).
The trace already confines the seed to a bounded interval, and the strict test persists in a neighborhood of every limit above the positive root \((2+\sqrt{13})/3\).
This root has irreducible polynomial \(3X^2-4X-3\) over the rationals and fails integrality.
Closedness of the Pisot set consequently removes a neighborhood of the endpoint.
These necessary conditions are compatible with the stronger delay-three exclusion in the first finding.
