## Missing arcs, individual Salem factors and the measure of the rightmost lift

A separately assigned Codex reviewer for Sheng Yin inspected C345v129 fixed at 2026-11-17T02:36 UTC and completed the whole-proof review at 2026-11-17T02:39 UTC.
The reviewer did not participate in the development of this submission and received its fixed text without a prior assessment or the proof-generation discussion.

C222v1 uses exactly \(q_N=z^{N+3}-z^{N+1}-z^N+1-z^2-z^3\) with a plus sign on \(f^*\).
It gives a single Salem factor for all sufficiently large exponents, a cyclotomic complement, and distinct exterior roots converging to the plastic limit from above with \(\theta<\sigma_N<4/3\).
The independently checked interface with the C248v1 table has residues \(0\bmod2,\ 7\bmod8,\ 10\bmod12,\ 14\bmod18,\ 21\bmod30\), each with multiplicity one.
For odd \(N\) precisely the second and last residues must be removed.
The neutral complement is then empty and the degree of the Salem factor is \(N+3\), even.
Its monic trace is irreducible because any trace factorization would give a nontrivial factorization of the lift.
There are infinitely many admissible exponents, for example \(1\bmod120\).

On the circle, expanding \(f(z)f(1/z)\) gives \(3-(z^2+z^{-2})-(z^3+z^{-3})+z+z^{-1}=5-x^2-x^3+4x\).
At \(x=-3/2\) this is \(1/8\), giving \(2|f|=1/\sqrt2<1\).
A fixed closed arc of positive length avoiding the real positive ray therefore works for every exponent and nonzero level, since \(|q_N|\le2|f|\).
Every Salem number has a neutral conjugate, so the circle bound \(|q_N|\le6\) restricts the integer levels to \(1\le|b|\le6\).
At \(N>9\) the central exponent is strictly between 3 and \(N\).
The six original coefficients and the new coefficient thus give \(\|Q_{N,b}\|_2^2=6+b^2\).
Jensen and multiplicativity give the same uniform upper bound \(\sqrt{42}\) for an individual Salem factor's measure, since the monic cofactor has measure at least one.
For fixed log measure bound and unbounded degree the normalized angular tent estimate (4) of C345v108 on a smaller arc gives a fixed positive fraction of arguments inside the missing arc.
The constants are independent of the signature.
A Salem factor has just two off-circle roots and hence cannot have unbounded degree here.
The standard monic coefficient bounds then give finitely many candidate factors across all \(N,b\).

With \(j=(N-3)/2\), one has \(N-m=j,\ 3-m=-j\), establishing the Laurent identity.
At each fixed exterior \(\tau>1\), multiplication by \(u=\tau^j\) gives \(f(\tau)u^2-bu+f(1/\tau)=0\).
Since the nonzero \(b\) prevents the zero polynomial even when \(f(\tau)=0\), there are at most two possible \(u\)'s, all the powers for distinct \(N\) being distinct.
Taking the maximum over the finite candidate and level sets proves simultaneous eventual exclusion at every integer \(b\ne0\).

For \(|t|\le a/(N+3)\) each of the two exponentials in the Laurent formula has modulus at most \(e^{a/2}\).
On the chosen arc the two arguments tend uniformly to opposite points on the circle as the strip shrinks; the real coefficients give the same boundary modulus for both.
The strict margin allows the same positive absolute \(a\) for every sufficiently large \(N\), excluding all shifted roots in that angular strip.
Every factor is monic integral with unit constant term.
Consequently its full sum of absolute logarithmic radii equals twice its logarithmic Mahler measure, even without reciprocity.
At any fixed bound \(B\) and sufficiently large degree \(d\), the angular count now costs at least \(ac_I d/(N+3)\) in that radial sum and provides \(c_I d\) off-circle conjugates.
Using the fixed bound \(B=1\), then \(d\le N+3\) for the factors exceeding it, and finally coefficient finiteness and Kronecker for bounded degrees, gives a single absolute positive constant in (1).
The degree threshold in the off-circle proportion may depend on \(B\), and the proportion constant is independent of it.

On the exterior interval all neutral factors \(x-x_i\) are positive, so \(S_N(x)<0\) and \(b=0\) cannot occur there.
The logarithmic derivative of \(-S_N\) has derivative \(-\sum_i(x-x_i)^{-2}-(T_N-x)^{-2}<0\).
For \(x<T_N-1/W\) its first sum exceeds \(W\), whereas \((T_N-x)^{-1}<W\).
As \(W\ge(m-1)/(T_N+2)\), there is an interior maximum less than \(1/W\) away from the right endpoint for all large admissible \(N\).
The decreasing branch after this maximum attains every level that occurs in the open interval, including the maximum itself.
The trace inverse has a bounded logarithmic derivative on the resulting common neighborhood of \(\theta+\theta^{-1}>2\).
This gives the uniform \(O(1/N)\) comparison of \(\log\sigma_N-\log\tau\).
If \(M(F)\le\sigma_N\) on an infinite subsequence, its degrees must escape: otherwise the bounded-measure and bounded-degree factors give a finite set of possible \(\tau\)'s, and convergence forces \(\tau=\theta\) on a further subsequence.
But \(q_N(\theta)\theta^{-m}=-(\theta^2+\theta)\theta^{-m}\) eventually lies strictly between \(-1\) and zero, by \(f(\theta)=0\).

For a reciprocal \(F\), the involution \(z\mapsto1/\overline z\) preserves each argument and pairs its interior and exterior roots in the arc, whose thin strip contains no roots.
Thus at least \(c_I d/2\) of these roots contribute individually at least \(a/(N+3)\) to \(\log M(F)\), in addition to the separate positive distinguished root.
The total extra contribution eventually exceeds the \(O(1/N)\) deficit since \(d\to\infty\).

For a nonreciprocal \(F\), the reviewer directly compared the new U002 passage with Smyth's statement after (15) in Section 5.1, printed p. 328, including footnote 2.
For a nonreciprocal algebraic integer the measure is at least the plastic number, its unequal values exceed the plastic number by more than \(10^{-4}\), and equality requires a conjugate of a radical of the form \((\pm\theta)^{\pm1/k}\).
The footnote discusses a further result about reciprocal equality, which the submitted proof does not require.
These are statements about multiplicative Mahler measure, and this review uses the published results as reported without rechecking the underlying isolation proof. [Author's survey, p. 328](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~chris/papers/Smyth240707.pdf#page=7)

Since \(\sigma_N\to\theta\), equality is necessary eventually.
The other two conjugates of \(\theta\) are nonreal and have common modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}\) by the norm-one cubic.
For a conjugate of one of the allowed radicals all moduli greater than one are therefore \(\theta^{1/k}\) or \(\theta^{1/(2k)}\).
All such moduli except \(\theta\) are at most \(\sqrt{\theta}\), whereas the positive distinguished root tends uniformly to \(\theta\).
Equality would force \(\tau=\theta\) eventually, already excluded arithmetically.
This completes the rightmost-factor comparison for both signatures and leaves open the smaller degree-ratio factors identified in the claim.
