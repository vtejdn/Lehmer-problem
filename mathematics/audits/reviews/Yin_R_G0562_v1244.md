## Residue transfer, degree counting and displacement bounds

At 2026-11-25T11:05 UTC a reviewer outside proof development checked the full statement and three arguments of G0345 v898 for Yin.
The reviewer used the submitted proof and its mathematical inputs, with no development discussion, author explanation or earlier finding on v898.
Both finiteness conclusions and both necessary displacement bounds follow under the stated hypotheses.

On the decreasing grid, \(Q_t'(t_j)\) has sign \((-1)^j\) and \(H(t_j)\) has sign \((-1)^{j+1}\).
Thus every \(r_j\) is positive, and \(H(h)/Q_t(h)>0\) gives \(\sum_j r_j/(h-t_j)<1\) and \(\sum_j r_j<4+h\).
For fixed \(i\), every subproduct of the factors with \(k\ne i\) in the interpolation formula has modulus at most
\[
 \prod_{k\ne i}\left(1+\frac{d_k}{|s_i-s_k|}\right)\le e^L .
\]
In the term \(Q_t/(x-t_j)\) with \(i\ne j\), division by \(Q_s'(s_i)\) leaves the additional ratio \((s_i-t_i)/(s_i-s_j)\).
The sum of its moduli over \(i\ne j\) is at most \(L\), now using the row indexed by \(j\) of the reference grid.
The term with \(i=j\) needs only the product estimate.
These evaluations use polynomial quotients even when a node coincides with one from the other grid.
Since \(d_0=0\) and \(|s_j-s_0|\le4\), the row at zero gives \(\sum_i d_i\le4L\), proving the stated uniform bound on \(V\).

Deleting \(s_e\) multiplies the proper residue at \(s_i\) by \(s_i-s_e\), giving a total absolute residue at most \(4V\).
After shifting by two, the denominator is monic integral with simple roots in \([-2,2]\), and monic division of the shifted numerator gives an integral linear quotient and proper integral remainder.
The signed-residue assertion of G0880 v1 therefore bounds the positive whole-lift measure away from zero for bounded \(L\).
The strict alternation and positive pole value place one trace root in \((0,h)\) and the other \(n-1\) in the disjoint negative grid intervals.
Only the positive root contributes to the logarithmic measure, which is strictly below \(\log(p/q)\) by \(p/q+q/p-2=h\).
Together the two bounds exclude all sufficiently large \(q\), uniformly over the allowed reference grids and their perturbations.

On the Chebyshev extrema one may delete zero from the integral node polynomial.
For \(N\ge2\), differentiation of the sine formula gives \(|Q_s'|=2N\) at interior nodes and \(4N\) at the two endpoints.
In the cyclic sample each interior trace occurs twice and each endpoint once, so the normalized sum of absolute evaluations is \(2V\) with either sign allowed at every reference node.
The node sum is \(-2n\) by reflection, and the coefficient of \(x^{-1}\) in \(H/Q_s\) is \(\sum_i u_i\).
The leading trace power in \(S\) contributes \(-2n\) to its coefficient at \(2n-1\), and reciprocity gives \([z]S=\sum_i u_i\).
For \(D=2N\) the quotient of \(S+zR\) by \(z^D-1\) has leading coefficient one, linear coefficient \([z]S\) and constant coefficient \(-1\), since \(\deg zR\le D\), \(S(0)=1\), and \(D\ge4\).
The excluded case \(N=1\) has \(Q_t(h)=H(h)\), incompatible with the strictly positive residues.

The integral coefficient function of \(R\) thus meets Sanders' norm hypothesis with a bound depending only on \(L\).
The signed cosets in v894 give the same cleared polynomial identity even when the sampled values change sign.
If there are \(l\) signed cosets, expansion before collecting gives at most \((6+l)2^l\) monomials, each with coefficient of absolute value at most \(\max(1,V)\).
After evaluating at \(p/q\), the sparse side has homogeneous degree \(2n+\deg W\) and the side arising from \(K\) has degree \(\deg W\) and nonzero value.
Some minimal vanishing subsum must meet both sides because a partition into sums confined to each side would force the latter value to vanish.
After division by a term from the lower-degree side, the possible coefficients for fixed \(q\) are signs or signed nonzero integers of size at most \(\max(1,V)\) divided by \(K\).
Their number, as well as the number \(k\) of remaining terms, is bounded in terms of \(L\).
Minimality gives nondegeneracy for the Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem on the group of rational tuples of rank \(2k\).
At least one bare ratio from each such mixed sum has the form \(p^Aq^B\) with \(A+B=2n\).
Coprimality and \(p,q>1\) uniquely determine both exponents from the ratio for fixed \(q\).
Counting all coordinates of all solutions and all allowed coefficient patterns therefore bounds the number of degrees uniformly in \(q\).
The external theorems enter at statement and application depth as in the [review of the exact-grid argument](Yin_R_G0562_v1240.md#finding).

For a displaced node \(j\) the smaller adjacent gap is at most \(2\sin(\theta_j)\sin(\pi/N)\), since the endpoints have zero displacement.
Concavity gives \(\sin\theta_j\le2\sin((\theta_i+\theta_j)/2)\), while the chord bound gives \(\sin(|\theta_i-\theta_j|/2)\ge |i-j|/N\).
In the exact cosine difference these inequalities bound the normalized displacement by \(\pi\varepsilon/|i-j|\).
Up to \(m\) indices on each side contribute at most a harmonic sum \(\mathcal H_m\).
The sine of the midpoint angle also dominates that of the half-difference, giving \(|s_i-s_j|\ge4|i-j|^2/N^2\).
The two sums of reciprocal squares yield the factor \(\pi^2/12\).
If either displacement expression had a bounded subsequence, these bounds and \(\mathcal H_m\le 1+\log m\) for \(m\ge1\) would bound \(L\) on that subsequence, contradicting distinctness of the realizable pairs.
When \(m=0\) one has \(L=0\) directly.
Rounding fixes both integer endpoints and gives \(N^2\max_i d_i\le1/(2c)\), so the finite-pair conclusion applies to every ordered grid surviving the distinctness condition.
