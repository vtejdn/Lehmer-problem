## Outcome of the estimates and uniformity checks

The three estimates for \(k/a\), the two pole moments, and the localization of every exterior root hold in their stated ranges.
The lower bound involving \(K\) uses the stipulated occupancy hypothesis at the poles.

**Radial width.**
The six ordered exponents start with \(0,a,au\) and end with \(N-au,N-a,N\), where \(au=\min(sa,b)\).
They are distinct since \(a,b\) are coprime, \(a\ge2\), and \(s,r\ge2\) with \(s\ge3\).
The leading binomial at an exterior root bounds \((e^{at}-1)e^{a(u-1)t}\) by four.
Setting \(z=a(u-1)t\) yields \(ze^z\le4(u-1)\), and \(e^z\le1+ze^z\) gives the stated \(T\).
Reversal preserves the entire parent, so this cap applies to the absolute logs of the interior roots of every factor as well.
Monic factorization and unit constant term give \(\sum|\log|x||=2m\) for each factor separately.

Part A of the all-root square bound counts a complex conjugate pair twice in \(d\sum\log^2|x|\).
Dobrowolski's divisor lemma counts six monomials in the parent and gives \(1+0.31d/(6!N)\) as a lower bound for \(e^m\).
Concavity on \([0,1]\) in the variable \(d/N\) supplies \(c_0=\log(1+0.31/6!)>0\).
It follows successively that \(1/10\le2dTm\le 2NTm^2/c_0\) and \(m\le kT\), proving (1) after using \(N=a(s+r)\).
If \(u\to\infty\) and \(k/a\to0\), squaring (1) forces \(v/(u/\log u)^3\to\infty\), since \(v\le s+r\le2v\).
With a fixed comparability bound on \(v/u\), a shrinking subsequence would contradict the two-cluster estimate for the third exponent, because \(au/N=u/(s+r)\) then stays positive uniformly.
Thus \(m\) is bounded below on that range, giving the stronger count there.
For \(d\ge16\) the derivative of \(\log t/t\) is negative at \(t=\log d>e\), justifying comparison with \(N\) in Voutier's bound.
The smaller degrees use its positive corollary, and a degree-one unit is torsion.

**Moments and localization.**
The pole formula gives \(t_s=s-1-2\lfloor(2s-1)/6\rfloor\asymp s\).
For \(sh\) sufficiently small, the modulus of \(1+e^{i\theta}\) is within \((s+1)h\) of one.
Its level-one set consists of two points with nonzero angular derivative, so compactness away from them gives the asserted uniform distance bound to a primitive cube root \(\omega\).
The three possible powers \(\omega^s\) all satisfy \(|\omega^s+\omega^2|\ge1\).
The derivative bound \(s+1\) on the joining segment now gives \(h\gg s^{-2}\), and the same conclusion holds directly when \(sh\) is bounded below.
In Jensen's formula the center value has modulus one and every root within \(\delta\) contributes at least \(\log2\) at radius \(2\delta\), including equality at the inner boundary.
The logarithm of the outer maximum is at most \(2(s+1)\delta\), since \((1+2\delta)^s+2\delta\le e^{2s\delta}(1+2\delta)\).
The resulting tail count for \(sL_j\ge w\) is \(O(se^{-w})\).
Integration against \(1\) and \(2w\), and division by \(s,s^2\) respectively, give the two moment bounds.

If \(|g_s'(\zeta)|<1/2\), then \(|\zeta|^{s-1}\le3/(2s)\), giving \(1-|\zeta|\gg(\log s)/s\) for large \(s\).
The seed equation instead gives \(1-|\zeta|\le|\zeta|^s\le3/(2s)\).
The finite exceptional range of \(s\) has a common positive derivative minimum since none of its roots is multiple.
Taylor expansion along a segment inside the unit disk shows that two distinct inner roots are separated by at least \(2\inf|g_s'(\zeta)|/s^2\).
One can therefore choose a single \(c_1>0\) for the disks of radius \(c_1s^{-4}\), using the radial bound also to put each entire disk in \(|z|\le e^{-c_2/s^2}\).
There is one seed zero in each disk, and the boundary error in linear Taylor approximation is at most \(s^2(c_1s^{-4})^2/2\), giving a uniform positive multiple of \(s^{-4}\) as the lower bound there.
All centers have modulus greater than \(1/2\), so the disks avoid the origin and each has exactly \(a\) separate holomorphic choices of \(w=z^{1/a}\).
For a sufficiently large absolute \(C\), \(3e^{-c_2r/s^2}\) is strictly below the boundary lower bound even at \(r=Cs^2\log(2s)\).
Rouché gives one simple zero on each branch, and different branches or disks give different \(w\).
Reciprocation and the global upper bound \(at_s\) exhaust the exterior roots.
The error in \(-\log|z|\) is \(O(c_1s^{-4})\), uniformly since the disks stay away from zero, and shrinking \(c_1\) makes it smaller than \(L_j\).

**Weighted occupancy and extreme parameters.**
The first moment and \(t_s\asymp s\) give \(m\ll Kk/(as)\), and the second gives exterior square sum \(\ll Kk/(a^2s^2)\).
Twice this is the total square sum for a reciprocal factor, and \(d\le Nm/c_0\) then gives \(k^2/a^2\gg s^3/(K^2(s+r))\).
Smyth's lower bound for a nonreciprocal factor gives \(k/a\gg s/K\), which is at least as strong throughout this range.
Applying Voutier to the first moment works in either case.
For \(s\le r<Cs^2\log(2s)\), (1) gives \(k/a\gg\sqrt{s}/(\log s)^2\) as \(s\to\infty\).
For larger \(r=O(s^3)\), (5) gives a positive lower bound when \(K\) is bounded.
In the primitive example \(r=s^3+1/a,\ a=2^{s^2}\), the right sides of (1) and (3) have orders \((\log s)^{-3/2}\) and \((\log s)^2/s^5\), while (5) gives \(k/a\gg1/K\).
The fixed-seed distribution result cited for comparison has constants depending on the seed and supplies no unconditional bounded \(K\) for this varying family.
