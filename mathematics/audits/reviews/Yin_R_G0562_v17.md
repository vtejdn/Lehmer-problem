## Finding on the fixed-frequency argument and the uniform conclusion

At 2026-11-16T16:04 UTC a separate reviewer for Yin inspected the full [uniform first-index theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v17.md#statement) and its fixed inputs.
The reviewer had independently reviewed [the complementary large-frequency result](Yin_R_G0562_v16.md#first-index-review) and had developed a separate gap argument for a factored subfamily at offsets \((d,2d)\).
The reviewer took no part in developing the first-index argument and received the fixed claim without its development dialogue, author explanations or a prior verdict on this version.
The uniform bound passes on the entire branch-two six-term family.

Failure would yield \(q_i\to\infty\) and \(t_i/e_i\to\infty\).
The constants in [C345v16](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v16.md#statement) exclude all sufficiently large reduced second offsets once \(q_i\) and \(t_i/e_i\) exceed the two other thresholds.
Consequently one can pass to a fixed coprime pair, although its common divisor \(g_i\) may grow or stay bounded.
The [six-term coefficient restrictions](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) give \(e_i=o(N_i)\), \(|b|=1,\ |a|\le3\) and a further subsequence with \(a,b\) fixed.
The [previous cutoff](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v16.md#middle) uses bounded coefficients only to bound the variation of the measure representing \(b_l\) through the interior interval.
It applies with these coefficients, giving \(t_i=o(N_i)\) by reciprocity and the [positive delay-one measure and fixed-support gap](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).

On the initial gap the coefficients of \(J_i\) agree exactly with \(1/F(z^{g_i})\).
Since \(t_i/g_i\to\infty\), the fixed positive moments of the reflected first coordinate of the limiting probability are half the positive Taylor coefficients of \(1/F\).
The [negative mass](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) changes each test moment by \(o(1)\), and marginal symmetry permits reflection.
This establishes the Herglotz representation of \(1/F\) in the open disk and in particular excludes interior zeros of \(F\).
In the [rational Herglotz decomposition](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#departure) the finitely many circle poles give atom kernels, leaving a continuous density after their subtraction.
Each marginal atom is therefore killed by multiplication by \(F\), whatever the conditional moments at that atom.
The remaining doubled density is \(\sigma=2\Re(1/F)\) at regular points and is positive there except at finitely many zeros of \(\Re F\).

The \(l\)-th Fourier coefficient after multiplication by \(F\) uses the three original indices \(kt_i+lg_i\), \(kt_i+(l-m)g_i\), \(kt_i+(l-n)g_i\).
All lie in the strictly positive interior moment window for fixed \(l\in\mathbb Z,k\ge1\), because \(e_i=o(t_i)\) and \(t_i=o(N_i)\).
This verifies integrality of the continuous rows and nonvanishing at the constant coefficient of \(H_1\).
The envelope is \(D=2\Re F/|F|\le2\), strictly below two on an open arc since \(\Im F\) is a nonzero trigonometric polynomial.
The strict squared mean bound and Parseval leave at most three unit terms.
For a trinomial the three distinct positive differences in the unequal-gap case give fourth moment \(15>12\).
With equal gaps the two signs, after a monomial and a rotation in the common phase, give a value of modulus three or \(\sqrt5\).
Every surviving binomial of exponent difference \(r\) has \(r\) distinct alignment points, each a zero of \(\Im F\) even if \(F\) itself vanishes there.
Multiplying \(2i\Im F\) by \(w^n\) gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(2n\), proving \(r\le2n\).
The shape list is finite for this fixed primitive pair, and \(\log D\) is integrable by its rational-modulus expression and finitely many finite-order zeros.
The strict inequality on an arc gives \(M(D)<2\).

The root moduli of \(F\) have product \(2/|b|=2\).
At an exterior root \(\alpha\), \(F(1/\alpha)\ne0\), and for every allowed signed monomial or binomial the quotient \(P(w)/P(1/w)\) is itself a signed monomial.
Thus the phase ratio \(R_P\) retains this exterior pole, including when \(F\) is reducible or has circle zeros.
An identity \(R_P(w^2)=R_P(w)^2\) would give poles at all \(\alpha^{2^l}\), so its difference is a nonzero rational function.
The finitely many shapes allow a single odd prime whose primitive nodes and their squares are regular and avoid those zeros and the exceptional points of the envelopes.
The cyclotomic squaring automorphism then excludes \(R_P(\xi)\in\mu_p\), making every nonzero specialized moment nonreal.
On the selected regular nodes all finite Toeplitz positivity conditions extend by continuity from the conditional probabilities.
The formulas for \(F,\sigma,H_k\) have rational coefficients, and conjugation commutes with all cyclotomic embeddings.
Consequently the Galois conjugates of the two-sided sequence are again probability-moment sequences at the conjugate nodes.
A fixed integer clears the denominator of \(c_k(\xi)\) for every \(k\), with the same conclusion at negative indices by conjugation.
Inverting the embedding matrix makes each integral coordinate a fixed linear combination of Fourier–Stieltjes transforms.
The [discrete integer-transform theorem](../literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) yields a common positive-tail period.
Its value at multiples of this period is the mass on the corresponding finite root group and must vanish because any nonzero specialized moment is nonreal.
The remaining residue-class means then vanish, so the late specialized moments are zero.
Each shape is nonzero at the chosen node, and hence every sufficiently late row vanishes identically.
At the last possible nonzero row positivity of the power image gives \(|c_r|\le1/2\), contradicting \(1\le M(H_r)\le M(D)/2<1\).
This contradiction is needed separately for each fixed pair in the bounded reduced-frequency range and suffices for the global existence of \(C,Q\).
The result bounds the first denominator index and gives neither numerical constants nor existence of six-term inputs at large \(q\).
