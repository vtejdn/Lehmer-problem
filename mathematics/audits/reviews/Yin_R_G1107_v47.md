## Minimum degree, the shifted roots and the exceptional cubic

I checked the normalization of the residue sign directly.
At a grid node, \(r=p-2s\) gives \(\sin(r\theta_j/2)=(-1)^{j+1}\sin(s\theta_j)\), and differentiation of \(B_n(2\cos\theta)\) gives \(\operatorname{sgn}B'_n(b_j)=(-1)^{j+1}\).
The neutral term therefore contributes exactly the phase \(W\) to the sign test, and \(B_k(-2)=(-1)^k\) gives the endpoint term \(-4s\).
The last-node value satisfies \(pW(b_n)\to4s^2\pi\).
For the lower bound, the terminal block has at most \(h+1\) nodes for degree bound \(h\), since each sign change of the perturbation costs a distinct zero.
Near a root of multiplicity \(m_x\) of \(t^2E^2\), \(h+1\) separated pairs in the initial block provide intermediate traces with \(|H|=O(p^{-m_x})\).
Interpolation after rescaling the mesh by \(p\) bounds the \(k\)-th derivative by \(O(p^{k-m_x})\), and evaluation at the last node bounds the maximum coefficient norm below by a positive constant times \(p^{-1}\).
Thus the normalized limit has multiplicity at least \(m_x-1\) at each root, giving degree at least \(s+1\) for even \(s\) and at least \(s\) for odd \(s\).

For odd \(s\) the \(s\) roots of \(J=tE\) are distinct.
At degree at most \(s\), the normalized nonzero limit must be \(cJ\), where \(|J'(0)|=1\) and \(|J(-2)|=2s\).
Along this subsequence the last-node inequality gives \(\liminf p|H'(0)|\ge2s\pi\).
In either odd residue class of \(p\) modulo four, the two candidate left indices differ by one, and the larger possible value of \(y^2+(y+1)^2\) is \(13/8\).
Since \(b_j>b_{j+1}\), choosing even \(j\) for increasing \(H\) and odd \(j\) for decreasing \(H\) gives the upper bound by the sum of the two phases for the absolute decrement.
The mesh in trace is asymptotic to \(4\pi/p\), and the two phases have sum asymptotic to \(16\sqrt{2}\pi^2(y^2+(y+1)^2)/p^2\) on each fixed offset.
The derivative at the mean-value point divided by \(H'(0)\) tends to one because the normalized derivatives converge uniformly to \(cJ'\) and \(cJ'(0)\ne0\).
The resulting upper bound \(13\sqrt{2}\pi/2<10\pi\) on \(\limsup p|H'(0)|\) excludes degree \(s\) for every odd \(s\ge5\).

For the construction, \(0<\eta_k<1/2\) and \(-1/8\le\lambda_k<0\).
At the two closest lattice offsets the inequality \(|y-\lambda_k|\le2y^2\) follows respectively from equality and \((1-2\eta_k)^2\ge0\).
Every other offset has \(|y|\ge1\), where \(|y|+1/8\le2y^2\).
Every grid trace is nonzero for odd \(p\), so the exact common factor \(b_j^2\) can be canceled in the magnitude comparison, including around the sine zero at trace zero when \(s\) is even.
The congruence \(p\equiv1\bmod 2s\) also keeps all \(E(b_j)\) nonzero.

To test uniformity, suppose a preceding node fails for each member of an unbounded sequence of conductors.
Its shifted product is nonzero and its quotient \(V_p\) is at most \(K\).
Select a limiting angle in \([0,\pi]\).
Away from \(\pi\) and the sine zeros, \(|G_p(b_j)|\) and \(|E(b_j)|\) approach the same positive value, so the quotient grows without bound.
At a sine zero, all factors except the one at that zero have ratio tending to one.
If the offsets are unbounded, select a subsequence with \(|y|\to\infty\), on which the remaining ratio tends to one and \(p\sqrt{2+b_j}|E(b_j)|\) grows as \(2\pi s|y|/\sin(\alpha_k/2)\).
Bounded offsets lie on the same discrete translate for the entire progression, so a subsequence has constant offset.
The ratio of the two vanishing factors tends to \((y-\lambda_k)/y\) by the nonzero trace derivative.
Equality \(y=\lambda_k\) would give a zero perturbation and a positive residue.
For the remaining offsets the lattice inequality gives a limiting quotient at least \(s\pi/\sin(\alpha_k/2)>K\).
At \(\pi\), the quotient is asymptotic to \(s(2(n-j)+1)\pi\), at least \(3s\pi\) in lower limit for preceding nodes.
Each possible limit contradicts the assumed failure.
At the last node the quotient tends to \(s\pi<K\) and the sign of the shifted product gives a negative perturbation term.
The specified interval for \(K\) is nonempty for each fixed \(s\), and \(H(-2)=O_s(p^{-1})\) gives the strict endpoint for large \(p\).
For each such \(p\) the residue and endpoint tests are finitely many strict affine inequalities on the coefficients.
Their intersection with the open condition on the nonzero leading coefficient contains a rational polynomial of degree \(s+1\).

For \(s=3\), the left straddling indices of the zeros at \(1,0,-1\) have respective parities even, odd, even when \(p=24a+13\).
On the left of each zero in angle the sign of \(J\) equals the sign of \(J'\) at that trace.
Both parity and the sign of \(J\) switch across the zero.
Thus both straddling nodes are favorable in each case.
The first possible adverse offsets have magnitudes at least \(7/6,5/4,4/3\).
Multiplication by the trace derivative and \(\sqrt{2+b_j}\) gives limiting lower bounds \(14\pi,5\sqrt{2}\pi,16\sqrt{3}\pi/3\), respectively, all greater than \(19\).
At an interior zero unbounded offsets give divergence by its simple derivative, and away from the zeros and \(\pi\) the quantity grows linearly in \(p\).
At the far endpoint it is asymptotic to \(6(2(n-j)+1)\pi\), which separates all earlier nodes from the last one since \(6\pi<19<18\pi\).
Here \(n\) is even, \(J(b_n)<0\) eventually, and the strict endpoint becomes \(-12+114/p<0\).
The proposed rational cubic therefore attains the degree-three bound.
For the additional \(s=2\) example in v20, the endpoint estimates \(10/p\) and \(14/p\) on the adverse negative traces both exceed \(7/p\), the adverse positive traces start at magnitude at least \(10/p\), and the last-node test is below \(2\pi/p\).
The strict endpoint \(-8+56/p<0\) holds throughout the stated progression.

I found the stated minima valid for arbitrarily large conductors with \(s\) fixed, both for real coefficients and for rational coefficients with strict endpoint.
The density argument permits denominators depending on \(p\).
The proof uses the cited phase and fixed-parent multiplicity argument and elementary interpolation, calculus and compactness.
