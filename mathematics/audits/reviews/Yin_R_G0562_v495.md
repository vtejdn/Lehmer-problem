## Verification of the prescribed offsets and unit endpoints

The fixed-seed assertions hold with the stated distinction between arbitrary integral jets and the two short witnesses available for \(e\ge4\).
The representative may vary with the offset.

**Prime selection.**
Kedlaya's Theorem 4 on printed page 3 gives an absolute prime-counting error \(O(X\log^{-8}X)\) uniform in the reduced class and modulus when its two parameters are fixed.
Here \(p\asymp(\log X)^4\), and subtraction at \(X,2X\) leaves a main term at least \(X/((p-1)\log(2X))\), which dominates both errors uniformly in \(v\).
Throughout the dyadic interval \(\log(3en)=0.9p^{1/4}+O(1)\), so \(y/p\to0.9^4\) with strict room at both window boundaries.
The chosen class gives \(r=(1+vn)/p<n\) even at \(v=p-1\), since \(n>1\).
The fixed seed has only finitely many exceptional substitution primes, while \(p>e(e-1)+1\) and \(n>p\) give the two degree-preservation hypotheses.
I checked the statement of Theorem 4 in the cited lecture and used it at statement scope. [Kedlaya, Theorem 4](../literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) · [Power substitution and fields](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers)

**Integral jets and divisibility.**
Centering the equation for the Euler derivative of \(b\) with \(g(z)=-\sigma\alpha^{-v}u\) adds \((2pr-2)u\) to \(2pn\ell\), giving exactly the \(vu/p\) term.
For either Laurent row the possible derivative increments at fixed value are \(zQ_0'(z)H(z)\).
The unit \(z\) gives the whole ideal \(Q_0'(z)\mathcal O\), and a zero increment forces \(Q_0\mid H\).
Thus the two image tests are independently sufficient for the two rows, with integral representatives obtained by reducing \(Y^{2e}f,Y^{2e}g\) modulo \(Q_0^2\).
Inversion fixes exactly \(\mathbb Z[s]\) in \(\mathcal O\), since an invariant element \(A+zB\) with \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[s]\) satisfies \((z-z^{-1})B=0\).
The same calculation at \(\alpha\) identifies the integral fixed suborder needed for \(p\mid u\).

The two coefficients of \(u\alpha^{-v}\) in the basis \(1,z\) are \(uW_{p+v}/W_p\) and \(-uW_v/W_p\).
Modulo \(W_k\) the \(k\)-th power of the recurrence matrix is scalar with determinant one, so \(W_{jk+i}\) is a unit multiple of \(W_i\) in that quotient.
The ordinary Euclidean algorithm on \(v,p\) therefore ends at \(W_1=1\), justifying cancellation of \(W_v\) modulo \(W_p\) even in the possibly nonmaximal order \(R\).
The derivative condition gives \(vu/p\in R\), hence \(p\mid u\).
For primes outside the divisors of \(N(q_0^2-4)\), multiplication by \(q_0^2-4\) is invertible on \(R/pR\), by its determinant in an integral basis of \(R\).
The congruence for \(W_p\) then gives comaximality and \(pW_p\mid u\).
The norm of \(p\) is \(p^{e/2}\), and taking half the logarithmic norm of \(W_p^2=(s^2-4)/(q_0^2-4)\) gives \(ph+O_{P_0}(\log(2p))\), including neutral conjugates by the cited logarithmic-form estimate. **Endpoints, size and residual.**
The two multipliers of \(Q_0^2\) in (5) correct the bottom and top coefficients separately because \(\deg Q_0^2=2e<4e\).
The centered value of \(A-A^*\) is \(-\sigma(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})u\ne0\), which excludes every root of the irreducible powered minimum from \(A-A^*\).

The final reciprocal increment therefore has nonzero slope at each relevant nonzero root and excludes at most \(Bn+B-1\) integers across the two gcds.
