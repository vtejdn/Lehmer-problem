# The odd-prime cyclotomic intersection bound and its normalization

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T02:20 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the complete fixed [C345 v722](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v722.md#statement) and recommends acceptance.
The reviewer took no part in developing v722 and received its fixed text, requested scope and necessary inputs without the author's development conversation, exploration notes or a previous verdict on this claim.
The review checked the field and orbit calculations, the packing inequality, the limits for variable primes, the effective constant and the passage from an arbitrary algebraic integer to a class product.

The nested intersections with the finite cyclotomic levels can change only by increasing degree, which is bounded by \(d\), so they attain \(F\).
Restriction from \(KE/K\) has image in \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\) with fixed field \(K\cap E=F\), giving \(H_0=\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\) and \([KE:\mathbb Q]=hd\).
This also applies to nonnormal \(K\).
At \(A>1\), the congruence kernel modulo \(p\) has size \(p^{A-1}\).
Indeed, \(v_p((1+p)^{p^j}-1)=j+1\) gives a generator for odd \(p\) by successive binomial expansion.
Every nontrivial subgroup of this kernel contains its unique subgroup of order \(p\), the kernel for descent from level \(A\) to \(A-1\).
Containment of that subgroup in \(H_0\) would put \(F=E^{H_0}\) inside level \(A-1\).
Minimality thus makes the reduction of \(H_0\) injective, also immediate for \(A=1\), and \(h\mid p-1\).

The exponent of every nonidentity element of \(H_0\) differs from one by a unit modulo \(p\), so it fixes no nonidentity \(p\)-power root.
For \(h>1\) all the nonidentity roots yield \((w-1)/h\) orbits, and for \(h=1\) all \(w\) roots can be used.
The primitive roots give \(\varphi(w)/h=s\) orbits.
At \(A>1\) the contribution beyond the primitive roots is positive in both cases, including when \(h>1\) because \(p^{A-1}-1>0\).
For \(\beta=\zeta\alpha^{p^j}\), raising to the order of \(\zeta\) recovers a generator of \(K\), after which division by \(\alpha^{p^j}\) recovers \(\zeta\).
The trivial stabilizer gives \(K(\zeta)=KE\) for every selected representative, including the identity in the case \(h=1\).
Conjugate twists at the same level give an isomorphism of these fields fixing \(\alpha^{wp^j}\) and therefore \(K\).
It sends their representatives to each other, proving the claimed distinction between different orbits.
The total logarithmic measure of each twist is \(hp^jm\), by extension of each embedding of \(K\) exactly \(h\) times and the absolute value one of every conjugate of \(\zeta\).
Positivity of \(m\) distinguishes the power levels.

In a common normal closure, every \(w\)-th root of unity reduces to one above \(p\).
The multiset of reductions of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) is invariant under Frobenius, including the multiplicity of each root.
The reduction of the polynomial of a twist is therefore \(P^h\bmod p\), where \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\).
All selected polynomials are integral monic, separable, pairwise coprime, and have the same degree \(hd\).
Since each twist generates \(L=KE\), its polynomial discriminant in absolute value is \(|D_L|\) times a positive square.
The relative discriminant formula bounds the root discriminant of \(L\) below by that of \(E\), equal to \(w/p^{1/(p-1)}\) at conductor \(p^A\).
With \(t=kJ\) and \(D=hd\), the logarithmic form of v707 divided by \(tD\) gives
\[
 (t-1)\log p+\frac{\sum_i\log|\operatorname{disc}P_i|}{tD}
       \le\log(tD)+(2-2/(tD))\sum_i\log M(P_i).
\]
The average on the left is at least \(\log(w/p^{1/(p-1)})\).
The sum of the measures on the right in logarithmic units is \(khm(p^J-1)/(p-1)\).
These yield the entire finite inequality, including the factors \(h\), \(1/w\) and the degree correction.
This reasoning uses no restriction on the numbers of conjugates outside the circle.

For \(Y=\log(1/(hm))\ge0\), the stated integer \(J\) gives \(p^{J-1}hm\le1\) and \(J\log p>Y\).
Thus the measure contribution is at most \(2kp/(p-1)\le3k\), and \((kJ-1)\log p\ge(k-1)Y\) since \(J\ge1\).
The factor \(kh/w\le1\) and \(p^{1/(p-1)}\le2\) give (5), uniformly in all odd primes.
If \(Y\to\infty\) and \(k\to\infty\), then \(J\le1+Y/\log3\), so division of (5) by \(kY\) gives \(\liminf\log d/(kY)\ge1\).
For bounded \(k\), the inequality \(m\le hm\) forces \(m\to0\).
Voutier's bound and the degree-one case imply \(d\to\infty\).
Here \(Y\le\log(1/m)<3\log\log(3d)-\log2\), which makes \(kY/\log d\to0\).
These two cases exhaust possible subsequences and prove (2).
It constrains \(hm\) even when \(p\) varies arbitrarily.
To obtain (3) in terms of \(m\) requires \(m\to0\) and \(\log h=o(\log(1/m))\).
These assumptions imply \(hm\to0\) and \(Y/\log(1/m)\to1\).
Together with \(s\le k\) they also imply \(s=o(\log d)\).
The extra assumption holds for fixed \(p\), and holds for moving primes in the specified range for \(\log p\).

For the effective implication assume \(s\ge\eta\log d\).
When \(k=1\) one has \(s=1\), so \(d\le\exp(1/\eta)\), and either \(m\ge\log2\) at degree one or \(m>2/(\log3+\eta^{-1})^3\) by Voutier.
When \(k\ge2\) use \(\log d\le k/\eta\) and \(2J\le2(1+Y)\le4e^{Y/2}\) for \(hm\le1\).
Rearranging (5) gives \((k-3/2)Y\le k(3+\eta^{-1})+\log4\).
On \(k\ge2\) the ratios \(k/(k-3/2)\) and \(1/(k-3/2)\) are at most four and two respectively, yielding \(hm\ge e^{-12-4/\eta}/16\).
The case \(hm>1\) is immediate for the displayed choice of \(c(\eta)\).
Under the same intersection hypothesis the uniform conclusion is \(hm\ge c(\eta)\).
Division gives \(m\ge c(\eta)/H\) when \(h\le H\), and in particular \(m\ge c(\eta)/(p-1)\) at each fixed odd prime.
The dependence on \(h\) matters when passing from \(hm\) to \(m\) across varying primes.

For an arbitrary nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer, its conjugates partition into equal-size classes of root-of-unity quotients by Galois transitivity.
Choose one exponent killing all the finitely many such quotients.
Raising each class product to this exponent gives the corresponding power of a member with exponent multiplied by the common class size.
Thus products of different classes have nontorsion quotient, all their powers continue to separate the classes, and any one product generates a field preserved by every positive power.
The moduli are constant within each class, so the sum of the positive logarithms over the distinct products is exactly the original total logarithmic Mahler measure.
The field, its degree and the odd-prime intersection parameters in this application are those of the chosen product.
The source units for the cyclotomic discriminant and Voutier's bound were checked at their established statement scopes.
