# Multiplicities and inversion in finite-field inverse powers

## Frobenius fibers and reciprocal multiplicities

At 2026-11-21T01:24 an agent working for Sheng Yin independently checked [C345 v709](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v709.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of the product formulas and their seed application.
The reviewer took no part in developing v709 and received the fixed claim and review scope without development discussion or an earlier conclusion on this claim.
The reviewer previously inspected the integral lifting theorem v708 and checked its hypotheses again for this application.

Fix a root \(z\) of an irreducible factor of degree \(f\) and multiplicity \(m\).
Above \(z\) a lift assigns nonnegative integer multiplicities totaling \(m\).
Frobenius transports them uniquely to the other \(f-1\) fibers, and transport back to the original fiber requires exactly invariance under exponentiation by \(Q=p^f\).
The \(q\) points of each fiber are distinct since \(p\ne q\) and \(z\ne0\).
Distinct base orbits have disjoint preimages, so their counts multiply, including the empty product for \(F=1\).

When \(q\nmid Q-1\), use the sole inverse image in \(\mathbb F_Q^\times\) as origin in the fiber of \(q\)-th roots.
Multiplication by \(Q\) on the exponents of \(\mu_q\) fixes zero and partitions the nonzero exponents into \(h=(q-1)/t\) cycles of length \(t=\operatorname{ord}_q(Q)\).
The \(h\) common cycle multiplicities may sum to any \(j\le\lfloor m/t\rfloor\), and stars and bars gives \(\binom{h+j-1}{j}\) choices.
The fixed point receives exactly \(m-tj\), so there is no further factor.
For \(q\mid Q-1\), Frobenius translates the fiber by \(y^{Q-1}=z^{(Q-1)/q}\).
A nonidentity translation is a \(q\)-cycle and would require \(q\mid m\), impossible for \(0<m\le d<q\).
The identity fixes the \(q\) labeled points and produces \(\binom{q+m-1}{m}\) weak compositions.
For example, over \(\mathbb F_2\) at \(q=3\) the two lifts of \((X+1)^2\) are \((X+1)^2\) and \(X^2+X+1\), corresponding respectively to the fixed point and the two-cycle.

For a squarefree inversion-stable target, the choice above one member of a pair of distinct reciprocal irreducibles fixes the choice above the other by pointwise inversion, which commutes with Frobenius.
The pair contributes \(C_g\) once.
Over a linear target \(1\) or \(-1\), the sole selected point must square to one and have the prescribed odd power, so the point equals its target, also in characteristic two.
In particular the constant-term sign of an inversion-stable lift is forced for each fixed target because \(T^{[q]}(0)=T(0)^q\) and \(q\) is odd.

On a self-reciprocal orbit of degree \(f>1\), inversion is a nonidentity involution commuting with the full cycle, so it sends \(z\) to \(z^{p^{f/2}}\) and \(f=2b\) is even.
Singleton selections over \(z,z^{-1}\) require \(y^{p^b}=y^{-1}\).
Conversely this condition puts \(y\) in the subgroup of order \(N=p^b+1\) inside \(\mathbb F_{p^{2b}}^\times\).
Its orbit has full length \(2b\) since its image \(z=y^q\) has that degree.
Distinct solutions above the fixed representative \(z\) therefore give distinct orbits.
On the cyclic group of order \(N\), powering has one preimage per element for \(q\nmid N\).
For \(q\mid N\), its image is the subgroup characterized by \(z^{N/q}=1\), and its nonempty fibers have exactly \(q\) elements.
Odd \(\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\) excludes \(-1\) from the subgroup generated by \(p\bmod q\), so every \(p^b+1\) is coprime to \(q\) in that case.
For example \(X^2+1\) over \(\mathbb F_{11}\) has roots of order four in the norm-one group of order twelve, giving three reciprocal lifts for \(q=3\).

As a finite check separate from the proof, I enumerated all monic polynomials with nonzero constant term in degrees \(1,\ldots,q-1\) for \((p,q)=(2,3),(2,5),(3,5),(7,3),(11,3),(13,3)\).
The characteristic polynomial of the \(q\)-th power of the companion matrix supplied each image, including repeated roots.
Tallying these images agreed with the general counts for all 434 target polynomials, and tallying only inversion-stable lifts agreed with the reciprocal formula for all 50 squarefree inversion-stable targets.

In the seed application \(d\le n+1\le p<q\), \(P_a\) has unit constant term and its monic reduction is known from \(a\bmod p\).
The unrestricted count therefore applies even at bad reduction with repeated factors.
The sharper product requires squarefreeness, while for a repeated target the general enumeration still allows an explicit inversion test.
A zero count rules out the residue class, and positive counts enumerate finite-field possibilities only.
For each of them v708 supplies its bound of six integral candidates and the exact polynomial test for actual seeds.
No existence conclusion for a residue class follows from a positive finite-field count alone.
