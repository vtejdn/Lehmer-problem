# Sharp Mahler bounds for factors of integer trinomials

<a id="trinomial-factor-gap"></a>
## Independent review of both sharp bounds

At 2026-11-17T07:47 UTC an agent outside proof development reviewed the complete [C345 v181 submission](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#decision-arbitrary-trinomial-factor-gap-submitted) for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed proof and its scope and checked Smyth's theorem against the cited source unit.
The review did not use proof-development discussion, author explanations or prior findings on v181.
Both bounds, their sharpness and the exclusion of Salem factors of degree at least four follow under the stated hypotheses.

After removal of a monomial, a constant has only constant divisors and a binomial with a root \(\alpha\) and its inverse forces \(\alpha^{2n}=1\).
For three terms, the two equations at the inverse roots give \(A^2=B^2+C^2+BC(\alpha^m+\alpha^{-m})\), including when any of the three coefficients is negative.
The rational trace is integral because \(\alpha\) and its inverse are algebraic integers.
At integer traces \(0,\pm1,\pm2\) the two quadratic roots have finite order, which would force \(\alpha\) to have finite order.
For all other traces, \(\alpha^m\) is a real quadratic unit with conjugate \(\alpha^{-m}\), since a rational algebraic unit has absolute value one.
The original equation puts \(\alpha^n\) in the same field, and Bézout applies to the two nonzero powers even when a coefficient in the Bézout identity is negative.

Each real embedding of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^m)\) extends to exactly \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):K]\) complex embeddings.
For an embedding extending \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R\), the modulus of the image of \(\alpha\) is \(\lvert\sigma(\alpha^m)\rvert^{1/m}\), including when \(\sigma(\alpha^m)<0\).
Thus exactly the \(e\) embeddings above the larger modulus contribute to the monic Mahler measure.
The product of their images of \(\alpha\) is the image of the relative norm at that real embedding, so its absolute value lies in \(K\) and equals the measure.
Inversion pairs all conjugates without fixed points, and hence their product is one.
For \(u=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/K}(\alpha)\), norm transitivity gives \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}(u)=1\).
Quadratic conjugation and a sign change both preserve this norm.
The resulting positive unit exceeds one, has degree two, and has integral trace at least three.
Its minimal possible value is therefore \((3+\sqrt5)/2\), attained by the larger root of \(X^2-3X+1\).
This polynomial is irreducible by its discriminant and has both its roots off the unit circle, as do all factors covered by the two-modulus conclusion.
A Salem minimal polynomial of degree at least four has unit-circle roots and is thereby excluded.

For a divisor of measure greater than one, multiplicativity gives either content of absolute value at least two or a primitive irreducible factor of measure greater than one.
Powers of \(X\) have measure one.
For the other factors the absolute leading and constant coefficients are each at most the measure, leaving only monic units up to sign when both coefficients have absolute value one.
The nonreciprocal units satisfy Smyth's hypotheses, including the exclusion of \(0\) and \(1\).
The checked source states the bound for the same multiplicative normalization and the same real root \(\theta\). [Smyth's statement and conventions](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions)
This review uses the theorem at this interface and the existing source inspection for the original proof.
Finally the discriminant \(-23\) and the rational root test give one real root and a nonreal conjugate pair for the irreducible \(X^3-X-1\).
Its real root lies in \((1,2)\), and the product of the nonreal pair is \(1/\theta\), giving equality for the general bound.
