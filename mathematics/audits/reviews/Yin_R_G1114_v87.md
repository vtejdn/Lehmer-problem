## The opposite-sign return and the gap for all eight-term sums of two offsets

The [coefficient classification](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v94.md#shapes) and the [strictly stable case](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#sum-offset) leave the opposite-sign reciprocal shape on an escaping sequence.
I checked that the [exponent reduction](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight) gives the two independent frequencies and \(e=o(h)\), and that \(N-D>h\) makes the lower polynomial the exact normalized prefix.
The [inverse-measure identities](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) give the signed moments and the strict limiting Mahler excess of the actual denominator on this branch.

For \(D=d+e\), the Blaschke product \(F_i\) has degree \(D\).
Its positive angular derivative implies that the \(D\) preimages of one are simple and that \(\lambda_\zeta=1/(\zeta F_i'(\zeta))\) is positive.
The expansion of \((1-F_i)^{-1}\) has no polynomial part, and evaluation at zero gives \(\sum_\zeta\lambda_\zeta=1\).
Taking residues of \(2/A(z^d,z^e)\) at the same poles gives \(\sum_\zeta|c_\zeta|\le2\).
I checked the local coefficient bound by expanding the geometric series in \(F_i\), whose \(k\)-th power starts in degree \(ke\).
Expansion of the same fractions at infinity gives coefficient \(-r_{-n}\) at \(z^{-n}\).
Reciprocity therefore makes the first \(D-1\) negative moments zero and the rest signed translates of the nonnegative moments.
This gives bounded total correction for every shift of size \(O(e)\), even though the specialized denominator has circle poles.

I checked the [ordered base and coset calculations](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#integer) with the four lower monomials.
The fixed-offset tests yield integral Fourier coefficients, the two half-integral terms exclude Haar measure, and the singular measure over \(A=0\) is killed in every projected row by the factor \(A\).
A grouped graph-slope density \(f\) satisfies \(Af=H\) with \(H\) integral Laurent, by the high-harmonic separation of the finitely many slopes.
Here \(2+s x\) and \(1+2s x\) have equal modulus on the circle, so the zero graph has one point on the second circle over every first coordinate.
If \(H\) were nonzero anywhere on this graph, a neighborhood would give a divergent transverse integral of \(|H/A|\) over an arc of first coordinates.
Thus \(H(x,y_0(x))\) vanishes identically as a rational function.
The two primitive coefficients of \(A\) in \(y\) have distinct roots in \(x\), giving a primitive linear divisor over the Laurent ring by Gauss's lemma.
The quotient \(f\) is integral Laurent, and its mean and strict Fourier coefficient bounds eliminate every nonzero graph density.
At the last possible full-dimensional harmonic the conditional power pushforward gives the factor \(1/2\), contradicting \(M(H_k)\ge1\) because \(M(A)=2\) and \(M(f_0)<1\).
Consequently only the fixed singular cosets of \(|A|^2\mu\) can carry a nonzero row, and their [annihilating characters](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#singular) specialize to nonzero binomials with shifts \(O(e)\).

For comparable offsets the [first-coefficient and block argument](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#comparable) uses the two signed halves before \(d+e\) and bounded partial multipliers at later offsets.
For \(d/e\to0\), the [sixth-root and transition estimates](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#separated) likewise use the same prefix below \(e\).
These bounds and the local absolute sum for \(R_i\) justify the moving shifts for both comparison series.
In particular \(Q_0R_i\) is a bounded absolute sum of shifted inverse series, and the factorization \((\Pi/2)R_i=1+z^{N-D}\) gives the stated polynomial identity without estimating \(1/A\) on the circle.
I checked the [two cutoffs](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) at degree \(O(h)\) against the prefix through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\), the [integer support estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) and the [fixed-support Mahler gap](../literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001).
The resulting contradiction proves the qualitative bounds for all branch-two eight-term lifts with third lower offset \(d+e\).

## Isolation above a marked vertex coefficient in bounded support

I checked the full statement and proof in C0900v1 and the endpoint and vertex arguments in L462v97.
In one variable reversal and monomial translation retain the Mahler measure and put the specified coefficient at the highest exponent.
The root product gives an everywhere lower bound \(m\), including equality for monomials.
The [sparse height inequality](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) makes the ordered coefficient tuples on a proposed sequence finite.
For the stabilized exponent relations, saturation follows from evaluation in \(\mathbb Z\), and finite generation makes each sufficiently late evaluation descend to the quotient.
Every bounded sequence of additional relations would repeat a fixed vector by extraction, contradicting the choice of the quotient.
The distinct exponents remain distinct there, so [Lawton's theorem](../literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) applies to the fixed nonzero polynomial \(F\), giving \(M(F)=m\).

For each fixed late evaluation, every translated polynomial has the same degree and leading modulus \(m\).
Its logarithmic measure is continuous in the torus phases by the root product and continuous dependence of the root multiset, including roots that pass through the circle.
Integrating the absolute logarithm first in the base phases gives the finite integral of \(\big|\log|F|\big|\) for every circle parameter by Haar translation.
Fubini gives mean \(\log m\) for a continuous function bounded below by \(\log m\), forcing equality at every phase and in particular for the original specialization.
The proof also checks for a fixed complex coefficient tuple or for a coefficient set finite in bounded disks, with the dependence stated in L462v97.

In dimension \(s\), strict separation gives an integral functional exposing the marked vertex.
The coefficient of \(t^s\) in the proposed specialization enforces that vertex as the unique highest exponent.
For any nonzero potential relation its lower \(s\) coefficients preclude an identically zero polynomial in \(t\), so collisions disappear and each finite set of bounded relations is eventually excluded.
Lawton convergence and closedness of the same univariate allowed set transfer exactly the same gap to that polynomial in every dimension.
The resulting \(\varepsilon(K,m)>0\) is qualitative.

## Applying the endpoint gap before cancelling the trace factor

I checked the product consequence for \(M(A_i)=|\operatorname{lc} A_i|=m\) and \(|\operatorname{lc}D_i|=1\).
The leading modulus of the product is \(m\), and multiplicativity gives an excess of at least \(\varepsilon(K,m)/m\) for \(D_i\) whenever its measure exceeds one.
In the trace application \(\Pi=A_i(1+z^{N-d-e})\) gives \(M(A_i)=2\).
The cyclotomic binomials preserve the leading modulus one and give \(M(D_i)=M(B^\sharp)\).
I verified the product identity by substituting \(R_i=2/A_i\).
The bounded multipliers, degree bound and bounded-variation comparison from [the reciprocal return](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v96.md#return) yield bounded circle \(L^1\) norm for \(A_iD_i\).
The bounded support of this integral product suffices to apply the endpoint gap at two and exclude a strictly positive excess tending to zero.
This use of the endpoint theorem imposes no term bound on either separate factor.
