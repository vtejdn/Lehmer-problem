## The universal divisor and its local loss

At 2026-11-19T08:13 UTC, an agent for Sheng Yin independently checked the complete [integral divisibility theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v496.md#statement).
The reviewer had no part in developing its proof and received the fixed claim and scope without development discussion or author explanations.
The [earlier review of v495](Yin_R_G0562_v628.md#finding) was also read, and both dependency interfaces used in v496 were rederived.
The divisibility holds with the ordinary row resultant to the first power at every rational prime.

For every conjugate \(a\), vanishing of \(V(a^p)\) would force \(U(a^p)=0\), contrary to rational coprimality.
Thus \(-U(a^p)/V(a^p)=a\) recovers the full field, and the same identity for \(b^q\) rules out a common powered conjugate since \(P,Q\) are distinct irreducibles.
In particular, the two orders for \(a\) have equal rank, and \(C\ne0\).
The substitution on the square-zero ideal generated by \(A_p\) acts by the inclusion of the smaller order followed by multiplication by \(H_p(a)\), where \(P(X)H_p(X)=A_p(X^p)\).
Differentiating this polynomial identity and taking the absolute norm gives \(p^e i_p^2\) for the norm of \(H_p(a)\), since \(a\) is a unit and the ratio of the absolute power-basis discriminants is \(i_p^2\).
The map on the ideal consequently has nonzero determinant of absolute value \(p^e i_p^3\), and the quotient map contributes the further factor \(i_p\).
This proves injectivity and the full index \(\Delta_p=p^e i_p^4\), with \(\Delta_q=q^f j_q^4\) by the same calculation for \(b\).

Since \(\Delta_p D_p\subseteq\iota_p(C_p)\), the map \(u\mapsto\iota_p^{-1}(\Delta_p u)\) is defined on all of \(D_p\) and commutes with the action of \(Y\).
Tensoring the two resulting module maps therefore gives a left composite equal to the scalar \(\Delta_p\Delta_q\) on \(M\), which annihilates the kernel of \(M\to T\).
In \(T\) the graph relations put \(x-z\) in the annihilator of both rows.
In \(T/(x-z)\) the Bézout expression for \(\rho\) belongs to the ideal generated by \(P(x)\) and \(Q(x)\).
The cube of this ideal vanishes, since both generators have square zero and the ring is commutative.
Hence \(\rho^3 J\) maps to zero in \(T\), and \(k=\Delta_p\Delta_q\rho^3\) annihilates \(J\) integrally, including at primes of inseparable reduction.

Over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), the determinant of multiplication by \(B_q^2\) modulo \(A_p^2\) is \(\operatorname{Res}(A_p^2,B_q^2)=C^4\), giving \(M_\ell\) length \(4v_\ell(C)\).
Monicity of both squared minima presents this module as a quotient of a free module of rank \(d=2\min(e,f)\).
The inverse image of \(J_\ell\) in that free module is free of rank at most \(d\), so \(J_\ell\) is a quotient of \((\mathbb Z_\ell/k\mathbb Z_\ell)^d\).
This accounts for at most \(d\,v_\ell(k)\) of the total length.

Division by the common integer content preserves the two graph conditions by monic division, so all preceding bounds apply to the normalized rows.
At least one normalized row survives reduction modulo \(\ell\).
Its actual degree bounds the degree of the common reduced gcd defining \((M_\ell/J_\ell)/\ell(M_\ell/J_\ell)\).
The ordinary Sylvester target of rank \(\deg U+\deg V\) contains the needed residue-field power basis even when a degree drops on reduction or one row is constant.
Nakayama's lemma gives a surjection onto \(M_\ell/J_\ell\), and every element of the Sylvester image maps to zero because it is a polynomial combination of the rows.
The Sylvester determinant is the nonzero ordinary resultant up to sign, so this quotient has length at most its valuation.
Restoring the common content \(c\) multiplies the normalized resultant by \(c^{\deg U+\deg V}\), preserving the bound.
Adding the two lengths proves (1), including the loss-free inequality whenever \(\ell\nmid pq\,i_pj_q\rho\).
