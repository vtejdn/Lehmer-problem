## The affine rank gives the exact scalar exponent

At 2026-11-20T21:57 UTC a reviewer outside proof development completed an independent review of the full fixed claim.
The substantive inputs were the submitted [statement and proof](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v661.md#statement), its selected dependencies and the scope of the review.
The rank, approximation exponent, density bounds, finite cancellation, degree compression and reciprocal conclusions all follow under the stated hypotheses.

Since the constant term of \(P\) is a unit, every integral Laurent numerator has an integral polynomial representative of degree less than \(d\), and division by \(P\) changes the quotient by an integral Laurent polynomial.
In the proper representative the pole at \(\rho\) has residue \(b_\rho\).
The positive and negative halves of the geometric expansion differ from the corresponding interior sums by zero or by the negative full trace.
The trace is integral for every exponent, including negative exponents, because \(\alpha^{-1}\in R\) and \(b\in R^\vee\).
The trace-dual calculation by interpolation in the cited proof applies to every monogenic order with separable monic defining polynomial.

After extension to \(L\), the coordinates of \(F(y)\) are the conjugate partial sums indexed by \(G\).
Quotienting by the constant diagonal subtracts the identity coordinate, leaving exactly the rows \(\mathbf 1_{gI}-\mathbf 1_I\).
Their rank is positive since the unit condition and hyperbolicity give both an interior and an exterior root, and the action is transitive.
Multiplication by \(b\alpha^n\) takes the power basis to any specified full block, up to its order.
For each prescribed cancellation the preimage of the rational kernel has codimension \(r\) because multiplication by the relevant power is invertible.
The simultaneous intersection therefore has dimension at least \(d-Nr\).
One positive integer clears the coordinates of a nonzero solution in the trace-dual lattice and all of its finitely many rational partial traces, yielding an integral class nonzero modulo \(P\).

For a rationally independent basis \(1,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\) of the span with constants, clearing the block's common denominator turns a simultaneous approximation at \(n\) into a full-block approximation at \(qn\).
The pigeonhole denominators are unbounded, giving exponent at least \(1/r\).
At each archimedean place represented by \(g\), the triangular system with coefficients \(g^{-1}\theta_i\) evaluates on rational coordinates with ordinary absolute values \(k,|p_1-k\theta_1|,\ldots,|p_r-k\theta_r|\).
The normalized exponents sum to one, the coordinate norm is at least \(k\) before normalization, and the rational projective height is at most \(\log k+O(1)\), including for vectors with common content.
Accuracy \(k^{-1/r-\epsilon}\) thus gives at least \((r+1+r\epsilon)\log k\) on the left of the Subspace Theorem.
Bounded-height rational points are finite, and on each exceptional hyperplane its rational vectors satisfy a nonzero rational equation.
Dividing that equation by \(k\) along an unbounded subsequence contradicts independence.
For density, each nonzero character of the chosen \(r\) coordinates has a vanishing geometric average.
Projection onto those coordinates bounds the two full-block frequencies by the measure of the centered box or ball in the torus, including the box endpoints \(\delta=0,1/2\).
The Gaussian bound for the ball at \(s=r/(2T)\) gives precisely \((2\pi\mathrm e T/r)^{r/2}\).

The transitive average of an orbit indicator is \((|I|/d)\mathbf1\), so the centered vectors span precisely the differences from any fixed orbit indicator.
Membership in a basis of \(r\) orbit subsets therefore determines membership in every translate of \(I\), giving at most \(2^r\) parts.
In fact the part products are pairwise distinct.
If two labels differ, some \(gI\) contains one part and excludes the other, so applying \(g^{-1}\) to the products places their moduli on opposite sides of one.
The products are transitively permuted and integral units, and the sum of the positive logarithms of their moduli counts each exterior root exactly once.
This confirms the submitted multiplicity argument and gives equality of the compressed and original Mahler measures.
Since the number of conjugates is at most the number of roots, strict minimality against smaller degrees forces \(D_0=d\) at a strict degree record.
Voutier's Corollary 2 gives \(\log 3+r\log 2>(2/m)^{1/3}\).
It also forces \(D_0\to\infty\) on a shrinking sequence.
Once \(\log D_0\ge\mathrm e\), the theorem and monotonicity of \(u\mapsto\log(u)/u\) give \(z>a\log z\) at \(z=r\log 2\), whence \(z>a\log a\) for large \(a\).

For a reciprocal input the interior choices represent exactly the \(h\) embeddings of \(K^+\), so \(K^+\subseteq W\).
Individual flips annihilate each pair difference, while double flips give \(z_i=-z_j\) for every pair and hence zero for all \(i\) when \(h\ge3\).
This identifies \(W=K^+\).
Two rational coefficients for the same residue would put their power ratio in \(K^+\), forcing a nonzero power of \(\alpha\) to equal one.
In a block of length \(N\le d\) the \(N\) basis powers are independent, and \(U=V\cap K^+\) has \(U\cap\alpha U=0\).
Because \(V+\alpha V\) has dimension at most \(N+1\), one obtains \(N-\dim U\ge\lfloor N/2\rfloor\).
With \(r=d/2\), substitution of \(T=C/m\) gives the claimed density bound, and \(md>2d/(\log(3d))^3\) tends to infinity when \(m\to0\).

An exact example checks both compression and finite cancellation.

The identities \(\alpha^2-2=\sqrt3\) and \(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}=\sqrt2\) give degree four, and the two parts are \(\{\alpha,-\alpha\}\) and \(\{\alpha^{-1},-\alpha^{-1}\}\).
Here \(r=1\), and the compressed unit \(-\alpha^2\) has degree two and the same Mahler measure.
For \(A=P'\), the four coefficients at \(-1,-2,-3,-4\) are \(2,0,4-2\sqrt3,0\), so exactly three of their centered values vanish.
