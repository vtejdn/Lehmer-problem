## The descent holds at the first power

At 2026-11-20 16:23 UTC a separate agent for Sheng Yin independently reviewed the full statement and proof of C0345v587.
The Salem or reciprocal quadratic conclusion, the degree and measure comparisons, and the strict positivity of the eventual radial spread all hold under the stated hypotheses.

Degree preservation excludes a torsion quotient of distinct conjugates because the images under all embeddings give the entire conjugate set of each power.
A scalar stabilizing either circle's finite set of conjugates has finite order, and its action on any one element then forces it to be one.
For each exterior conjugate \(u\), the identity \(u\bar u=b\) pairs the whole exterior set with itself.
If an image of \(b\) has outermost or innermost modulus, every transformed pair lies entirely on the corresponding circle.
Its product therefore gives an involution of that entire set, and comparison with the original product involution on the same circle forces the image to equal \(b\) or \(b^{-1}\), respectively.
The product formula ensures the latter occurs because \(b>1\) is a unit.
Every unit-circle conjugate is nonreal, since a rational conjugate at either sign of one would force \(b\) to equal that sign.
Thus the alternatives cover degree two and all possible higher degrees.

There are exactly \(d/2\) ordered representations of \(b\), one per exterior first factor, even when the two factors agree.
Automorphisms carry these representations bijectively to those of each conjugate of \(b\).
The \(D-2\) unit-circle values of \(b\) therefore use \((D-2)d/2\) of the \(d^2/2\) mixed ordered pairs, and the \(d\) ordered inverse pairs are disjoint from them.
This gives \(D\le d\), also when \(D=2\).
The sole exterior conjugate of \(b\) contributes \(2\log R\), while the exterior half of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) contribute \((d/2)\log R\).

For \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\), the relative norm \(u\) satisfies \(u^k=(\alpha^k)^{[\mathbb Q(\alpha):F]}\), giving a nontorsion unit in \(F\) of Mahler measure at most that of \(\alpha\) by the degree-height formula.
Strictness against smaller degrees therefore supplies degree preservation at each record.
When \(d>4\) the new unit improves the record even if its degree equals \(d\).
Finiteness in bounded degree and bounded measure forces degrees to tend to infinity along shrinking records, and zero spread would give exactly the excluded two reciprocal radii since the original measure is positive logarithmically.
This establishes the positivity hypothesis used in L0462v308 without giving a uniform lower bound on the spread.

Lemma 8.1 of Dubickas and Smyth, checked on printed pages 12–13, selects an exponent to isolate the outer modulus and permits a further doubling for real signs.
Its reciprocal degree estimate applies to the resulting powered radius.
The finite-set stabilizer argument above establishes the additional claim at exponent one.
