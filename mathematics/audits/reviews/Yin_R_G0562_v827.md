The largest power of \(\alpha=4+\sqrt{15}>3\) exceeds twice the sum of all smaller nonnegative powers.
Consequently evaluation at \(\alpha\) detects every nonzero polynomial of coefficient magnitude at most two, and the powers themselves are dissociated over the real numbers.
At the contracting embedding the constant term of \(F_r\) gives the lower bound \(1-1/(\alpha-1)\), while the full ternary geometric sum has upper bound \(1+1/(\alpha-1)\).
At the exterior embedding the leading coefficient gives the same ratio after dividing by \(\alpha^{r-1}\).
Thus for a nonzero ternary \(G\) in the stated block whose value lies in \(I_r\), both moduli of \(u=G(\alpha)/F_r(\alpha)\) are at most \(\alpha/(\alpha-2)<\sqrt2\).
The norm of \(u\) is a nonzero integer of absolute value less than two.
When its norm is one, its integral trace has absolute value two, and the equal-modulus condition gives \(u=\pm1\).
For norm minus one the two real images have opposite signs and reciprocal absolute values between \(1/\sqrt2\) and \(\sqrt2\), forcing trace zero.
That would give \(u^2=1\) and contradict the negative norm.
Polynomial dominance applied to the difference then proves the claimed uniqueness, including support minimality against arbitrary sign choices.

For \(r=2s+1\), reciprocity makes \(\alpha^{-s}F_r(\alpha)\) invariant under the quadratic conjugation and integral, hence a rational integer.
Its closed expression has growth comparable to \(\alpha^s\) and exceeds two already for \(s=1\) and thereafter.
Multiplication by the unit \(\alpha^s\) preserves the ideal.
The geometric identity yields \(\alpha^r=-1\) in the scalar quotient, where \(1\ne-1\) since \(R\) has integral basis \(1,\alpha\) and \(Q_r>2\).
The only possible proper orders divide \(2r\) and are at most \(r\).
Every \(j<r\) is excluded by applying block uniqueness to \(X^j-1\), and \(j=r\) is excluded by the sign.

The respective moduli were \(7,55,433,3409\), the first positive return exponents were \(6,10,14,18\), and for each modulus only the alternating vector and its negative vanished among the nonzero vectors tested.
