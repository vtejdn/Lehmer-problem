# Short-exponent pole radicals over a Salem compositum

<a id="finding"></a>
## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-21T04:17 UTC an agent working for Sheng Yin completed the independent review of [C345 v751](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v751.md#statement) and recommends acceptance of (1)–(3) and the stated example.
The reviewer took no part in developing this claim and checked its fixed argument, the applicable input statements and the field and local degree calculations.

The assumed degree equality gives linear disjointness of \(K,H\) over \(E\) because \(H=E(\lambda)\) is simple.
Restricting the tensor product to \(F\) gives the same disjointness there, so the composita \(D=KH,\ M=FH\) satisfy \([M:H]=[F:E]=n\) and \([D:M]=2\).
Inversion on \(K/F\) extends by the identity on \(H\).
All embeddings of \(M\) fixing the distinguished copy of \(H\) are real, including when \(H\) has complex embeddings elsewhere, as \(F\) is totally real.
Of those embeddings only the identity on \(M\) can restrict to the identity on \(F\).
At each of the others complex conjugation on an extension to \(D\) acts as inversion on the generator of \(K\) and as the identity on \(H\).
This verifies the relative signature input without imposing total reality on \(H\).

For a positive \(l\)-th root \(u\) of \(\lambda\) in \(D\), the two real images under inversion are among \(\pm u\), so \(u^2\in M\).
There are at most two real roots of \(T^l-\lambda^2\), which gives \([H(u^2):H]\le2\).
Because \(n>2\), a nonidentity embedding of \(M\) fixing \(H(u^2)\) exists.
Inversion cannot negate \(u\), as that would give a purely imaginary nonzero image whose square equals the positive \(u^2\).
This places \(u\) in \(M\), and over \(H\) every conjugate of \(u\) is now real, hence in \(\{u,-u\}\).
Thus \(u^2\) belongs to \(H\), and an odd radical lies in \(H\) itself.
It follows that the maximum radical exponent in \(D\) equals that in \(M\), is a multiple of \(e_H(\lambda)\), and divides twice that exponent.
The doubling criterion in (2) follows by testing the one indicated positive square root.

In v748 the monic polynomial \(J\) is irreducible over \(K\), with its coefficients generating \(E\), and all fields \(H\) and poles range over finite lists.
This supplies the degree hypothesis for the preceding argument.
The trace degrees grow, so eventually \(n>2\).
By (2) only finitely many primes can divide any \(e_{KH}(\lambda)\).
For each such \(p\) dividing the short exponent, v748 gives \([K(\lambda^{1/p}):K]=pj\) eventually.
This excludes the radical from \(KH\) of degree \(j\) over \(K\), and proves the simultaneous coprimality assertion on taking the maximum of finitely many thresholds.
For an arbitrary divisor \(k\), all roots over \(D\) of the minimal polynomial of \(c=\lambda^{1/k}\) have equal modulus \(c\).
Its real constant term therefore puts \(c^s\) in \(D\) for \(s=[D(c):D]\).
If \(s<k\), combining \(c^s,c^k\) would give a common factor \(k/\gcd(k,s)>1\) of \(a\) and \(e_D(\lambda)\).
This forces the full degree \(k\), and composition with \([KH:K]=j\) shows irreducibility of \(J(Z^k)\) over \(K\) and over \(E\).
Equality of these simple extension degrees gives linear disjointness.

An odd residue place where \(\mu\) has nonzero square image splits into two degree-one completions in \(H(\sqrt\mu)\) by simple-root lifting.
The field really is quadratic, since otherwise \(2e\) would be admissible already in \(H\).
When \(\lambda\equiv1\) and \(e\) is odd, the residue of \(\mu\) has odd multiplicative order and consequently is a square.
If the quadratic subfield lies in \(M\), the two collections of primes above these split places must each contribute \([M:H(\sqrt\mu)]=n/2\) to the total local degree.
This is impossible at odd \(n\), or when the gcd of the local degrees has the full \(2\)-adic valuation of the even integer \(n\).
For an unramified inert place of \(F/E\), base change to a completion of \(H\) of residue degree \(f\) splits the degree-\(n\) residue orbit into \(\gcd(n,f)\) orbits of size \(n/\gcd(n,f)\).
The residue generator lifts with separable minimal polynomial, so these are exactly the completion degrees even if that place of \(H/E\) itself ramifies.
Tensor-product identification with the completions of the single compositum uses global disjointness.
For odd \(f\) the common degree has the full \(2\)-part of \(n\), as required.

In the example \(X^3-6\) is Eisenstein over \(\mathbb Q_2\), giving a single totally ramified cubic completion at two with the positive cube root as a uniformizer.
Its integral valuation one excludes every higher radical in \(H\).
At five the residue \(1\) is a simple root, giving the stated degree-one completion.
The polynomial \(X^4-4X^2+2\) is Eisenstein at two.
Modulo five the quadratic for \(X^2\) has nonsquare discriminant three and root norm two from \(\mathbb F_{25}\).
That root is nonsquare since the norm of a square is square, proving irreducibility of the quartic modulo five and an unramified degree-four completion.
The four values \(1\pm\sqrt{2\pm\sqrt2}\) have exactly one entry above two and the other three strictly in \((-2,2)\).
The negative discriminant at any of these three embeddings gives the required irreducible reciprocal quadratic and its Salem root of degree eight.
The compositum of its field and the cubic \(H\) has degree \(24\), being a multiple of the coprime degrees eight and three and bounded above by their product.
The equal-local-degree obstruction at five excludes the extra square radical.
This verifies the example as a field calculation independently of the shrinking hypotheses needed for (1).
