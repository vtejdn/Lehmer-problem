### Local coordinates and the zero count

The Teichmüller lift exists over the finite unramified extension by simple-root lifting, and its \(p\)-th power lifts \(\xi^p\).
Each expansion coefficient of degree \(j\) has valuation at least \(j\).
If \(\bar U\) has order \(k\) at \(\xi^p\), the coefficient in position \(k\), divided by \(p^k\), has nonzero reduction by the substitution \(Y=\xi^p(1+Z)\).
This proves the upper bound on the minimum valuation \(s\), an integer since the expansion is taken over the unramified field.

Writing \(X/\omega=1+w\), one has \(Z=w+\cdots+w^p/p\).
All terms after the linear one have higher valuation when \(v(w)>1/(p-1)\).
The binomial coefficients of the proposed inverse are \(\prod_{r<j}(1-rp)/j!\), with the product starting at \(r=0\).
The factorial valuation bound proves convergence on the full closed disk and preservation of valuation of the nonconstant part.
Its formal \(p\)-th power is \(1+pZ\), and the distance from one of every nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity proves uniqueness in that disk.
This gives the claimed inverse even for ramified input roots.
The nonzero derivative of the coordinate change at each such root preserves its full order.

After normalization by \(p^s\), at least one of the reductions of \(L,M\) is nonzero, and terms of either polynomial beyond index \(s\) have positive valuation.
In degrees \(k=s+1,\ldots,2s+1<p\), multiplication by \(k!\) turns the conditions for the first \(2s+2\) coefficients to vanish on reduction into a polynomial of degree at most \(s\) vanishing at \(s+1\) distinct points.
The monic falling factorials are a triangular basis in those degrees, so \(\bar M=0\) would follow, and vanishing of the lower coefficients would then imply \(\bar L=0\).
This proves existence of the required unit coefficient without assuming that a particular endpoint coefficient is a unit.

For the scaled series on radius \(|p|^{1/e}\), a coefficient before \(p\) and after this unit index \(j_0\) has valuation at least \(j/e>j_0/e\).
For \(j\ge p\), all convolution terms in coefficient \(j\) have valuation at least \(-v_p(j!)\).
The scaled valuation is at least \((j-e(j-1)/(p-1))/e\), an increasing affine function of \(j\) with value \((p-e)/e\) at \(j=p\).
This is strictly greater than \(j_0/e\) by hypothesis and tends to infinity.
In the scaled series let \(l\) be the last maximal index.
Division at any root of absolute value at most one gives a convergent quotient of last maximal index \(l-1\), because its coefficient there is the old maximal coefficient plus a tail of strictly smaller norm.
A zero of higher multiplicity can be used again, and a series with last maximal index zero has no zeros on the disk.
This gives the bound \(l\le j_0\le2s+1\) counting multiplicities.

For a local check, take any prime \(p\ge5\), center one, \(U=-p-(p+1)(Y-1)/2\), and \(V=p+(p-1)(Y-1)/2\).
The expansions have \(s=1\), and the scaled graph in \(Z\) has zero coefficients in degrees \(0,1,2\) and coefficient \((p^2-1)/12\) in degree three.
Thus a triple contact at \(X=1\) attains the disk bound for \(e=1\).
This checks the local lemma at a torsion point, which is allowed in that lemma.

### Recovering all the original factors on reduction

Each distinct residue of a monic unit minimum separates by Hensel factorization, and its characteristic-zero factor has degree equal to the residue multiplicity \(E_i(\xi)\).
After shifting by \(\omega\) all lower coefficients lie in \(p\mathcal O\).
A root with positive valuation strictly less than \(1/E_i(\xi)\) would make the leading term uniquely smallest in valuation, so each root lies in the claimed closed disk.
Distinct input roots contribute additively there even when their reductions agree.
The exact order identity and nonvanishing of the original row at each powered node give \(\sum_i(m_i-1)d_i\le2b\).
Hence \(e=\max_i E_i(\xi)\le2b\), \(s\le b\), and \(p>4b+1\) implies \(2s+1<p-e\).
The disk lower contact count is \(\sum_i m_i E_i(\xi)\).
Taking integer parts in the resulting upper bound gives \(s\ge\lfloor\sum_i m_i E_i(\xi)/2\rfloor\ge\sum_i\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor E_i(\xi)\).
The inequality \(s\le\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^p}\bar A\) and the Frobenius invariance of the original multiplicities prove the full product divisibility and degree bound.

### Two different measures are impossible throughout the range

At a nonendpoint, inversion preserves the multiplicities of both reciprocal factors and gives a genuinely different residue.
Thus the maximum multiplicity in either factor is at most \(\lfloor e_0/2\rfloor\).
If the combined multiplicity \(E\) exceeded \(\lfloor b/2\rfloor\), both inverse disks would require \(s>\lfloor b/2\rfloor\), since otherwise the first finite margin and the local bound imply \(E\le s\).
The corresponding two different Frobenius images would exceed the degree of \(\bar A\).
Consequently \(E\le\lfloor b/2\rfloor\) in either disk, and both \(s\)-ranges now give \(E\le\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^p}\bar A\).

At an endpoint, whole division by \((Y-\xi)^k\) keeps integral quotient rows with nonzero reduction in the first row, because the original first row is primitive and the divisor monic.
Formal reversal preserves this endpoint order, and removal of its substitution leaves all the nontorsion contacts.
The first surviving coefficient of the integer Taylor expansion is nonzero by the choice of \(k\), with absolute value at most \(H_*\binom b k\).
It bounds the quotient weight \(s\) by \(S\) at the same Teichmüller center because \(p\) is odd.
The second finite margin thus allows the disk lemma with \(e\le e_0\), giving \(E\le\operatorname{ord}_\xi\bar A\) here as well.
Together these orders force \(\bar P_1\bar P_2\mid\bar A\), of impossible degree \(d_1+d_2\ge112>b\).
This excludes both cases left at 101, neither sign needs special treatment, and the degree argument alone stops before 223.
