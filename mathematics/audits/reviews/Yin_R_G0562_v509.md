## Independent finding on the local losses, the two signs and the integer exponents

At 2026-11-18T21:50 UTC an agent outside the development of [L462 v246](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v246.md#width) independently checked all three sections for Sheng Yin.
The reviewer received the frozen proof without its development dialogue and had previously reviewed the two-seed width classification and the separate finite census.
The fixed-seed lower bound \(\log H\ge2p\log\rho-o_P(p)\) holds uniformly over the rows at every sufficiently large positive integer \(p\).
The consequent fallback at every sufficiently large odd prime holds through width four.

### The two local comparisons and the norm

Formal reversal of \(U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\) about \(4p+1\) is \(V^*(x^p)+xU^*(x^p)\).
Taking the sum and the difference produces the specified signed eigenrows \(A=U+\varepsilon V^*\), each of norm at most \(2H\).
If either is zero, the reversal relation gives \(UU^\iota=VV^\iota\), contrary to hypothesis.
The reciprocal factor \(P^2\) divides both signed graphs.
This argument uses neither row coprimality nor primality of \(p\).

Fix a finite prime of the splitting field and write \(D=\varepsilon A^*\) as in the proof.
For either \(w=v^{\pm1}\) and \(x=\eta^{\pm1}\), one may replace \(w\) by \(y\) in every coefficient polynomial of the two root equations modulo the ideal power of exponent \(r_\pm\).
Subtracting the equations evaluated at \(x=\rho\) then gives
\[
 \nu\big((\rho-\eta^{\pm1})D(y)\big)\ge r_\pm,\qquad
 \nu\big((\rho-\eta^{\pm1})(pyD'(y)+D(y))\big)\ge r_\pm .
\]
The value equation gives the same lower bound \(r_\pm-c_\pm\) for \(A(y)\) as for \(D(y)\).
Subtracting \(D(y)\) from the derivative combination and using the derivative equation at \(\rho\) gives that bound for \(pD'(y)\) and \(pA'(y)\), since all relevant root values are units.
The inequalities remain valid when \(r_\pm<c_\pm\) or the residue characteristic divides \(p\).
For \(F=f-f^\iota=Y^{-2}(A-\varepsilon D)\), this controls \(pF(y)\) and \(p\mathcal E F(y)\) to valuation at least \(r_\pm-c_\pm\).
The identity \(\delta\mathcal E F-SF=\delta^3(a_4-a_0)\) follows by differentiating \(F=\delta(a_3-a_1+(a_4-a_0)S)\).
The multipliers \(\delta,S\) are integral at the chosen prime, so \(r_\pm\le \nu(p(a_4-a_0))+c_\pm+3t\).

Write \(R=r_++r_-\), and take \(a_4-a_0\ne0\).
At precision \(\min(r_+,r_-)\) the two factors \(v,v^{-1}\) both agree with \(y\), giving \(y^2\equiv1\) and \(\min(r_+,r_-)\le t\).
At precision \(\min(r_\pm,t)\) one has \(v^{\pm2}\equiv y^2\equiv1\), so \(\min(r_\pm,t)\le t'\).
Each statement uses units and remains valid above two.
Bounding the larger collision by the preceding derivative estimate and the smaller by \(t\) gives \(R\le C+4t\), with \(C=\nu(p(a_4-a_0))+c_++c_-\ge0\).
One also has \(R\le C+4R\), so \(R\le C+4\min(t,R)\).
Now \(\min(t,R)\le\min(t,r_+)+\min(t,r_-)\le2\min(t,t')\), proving (2) with its factor eight.
Finally \(S_1-S_2=(y-v)(y-v^{-1})/y\), so \(R\) is exactly the local valuation of this trace difference.

In the fixed field \(L\) the units \(\rho,\eta\) are multiplicatively independent since \(\rho>1,\ |\eta|=1\) and \(\eta\) is nontorsion.
The latter follows by conjugacy to the nontorsion \(\rho\).
The pairs \((\rho^{2p},\eta^{2p})\) remain independent, are pairwise distinct for positive integers \(p\), and have heights at most \(O_P(p)\).
I checked Proposition 2, equation (2.8), on printed page 7 of the cited author preprint against the [source unit](../literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd).
For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\) the independent pairs violate the \(\epsilon\)-height bound at most finitely often.
All finite-place summands in the positive common-divisor cost are nonnegative, and the ideal-valuation normalization is \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\).
For these ordinary units their finite contributions are precisely \(\min(t,t')\) times this weight because the denominators in \(y-y^{-1}\) and \(v-v^{-1}\) are units.
This gives \(o_P(p)\) along all positive integers, with the source theorem accepted at statement scope.

With the same weights the summed \(c_\pm\) are fixed finite constants, and the cost of the nonzero rational integer \(p(a_4-a_0)\) is \(\log|p(a_4-a_0)|\).
Every Galois automorphism permutes the two inverse pairs and hence sends \(S_1-S_2\) to itself or its negative.
The normalized logarithm of its absolute ideal norm therefore equals \(\log|S_1-S_2|=p\log\rho+O_P(1)\), since \(|S_2|\le2\).
Summing the local inequalities and absorbing \(\log p\) gives (3), with an error independent of the row and its sign.

### The simultaneous obstruction to equal endpoints

At either \(x_i\) the factors \(x^{2p},1+\varepsilon x,x-x^{-1},x^p-x^{-p}\) are nonzero for every \(p\ge1\).
The two traces \(S_i\) are distinct because one is greater than two and the other is in \((-2,2)\).
Dividing out the nonzero factors from the signed graph gives \(E-H_\varepsilon\delta O\), using \((1-\varepsilon x)/(1+\varepsilon x)=-(t-2\varepsilon)/(x-x^{-1})\).
Both \(t\) and \(S\) have nonzero local derivative, so double vanishing supplies equality of values and of first \(S\)-derivatives on the local branch at each point.
If the two endpoints coincide, \(O\) is constant.
If this constant were zero, \(E\), of degree at most two, would acquire two distinct double zeros, forcing \(E=0\) and \(A=0\).
For a nonzero eigenrow the constant can therefore be divided out in the quadratic identity \(E'(S_1)+E'(S_2)=2(E(S_1)-E(S_2))/(S_1-S_2)\).

The derivative of \(B_\varepsilon=H_\varepsilon(t)\delta\) in \(S\) is exactly \(H_\varepsilon S/\delta+(x-x^{-1})H_\varepsilon'(t)/p\).
In its second term the factor \(\delta\) cancels before any estimate, so the fixed coefficient of \(1/p\) stays bounded even at arbitrarily small \(|\delta_2|\).
The first-point derivative is \(H_\varepsilon(t_1)+O_P(1/p)\).
The secant \((H_\varepsilon(t_1)\delta_1-H_\varepsilon(t_2)\delta_2)/(S_1-S_2)\) is \(H_\varepsilon(t_1)+O_P(\rho^{-p})\), by boundedness of \(S_2,\delta_2\) and \(\delta_1-S_1=-2\rho^{-p}\).
Thus equal edges at sign \(\varepsilon\) imply \(H_\varepsilon(t_2)S_2/\delta_2=H_\varepsilon(t_1)+O_P(1/p)\).
If both signs had equal edges for arbitrarily large integers \(p\), multiplication of each equality by the other sign's fixed \(H(t_2)\) and subtraction would give
\[
        H_+(t_1)H_-(t_2)-H_-(t_1)H_+(t_2)=O_P(1/p).
\]
The left side is the fixed nonzero \(4(t_1-t_2)/(d_1d_2)\).
This contradiction proves eventual nonvanishing of the edge difference for at least one of the two eigenrows at each exponent.
No bound on \(S_2/\delta_2\) or Diophantine condition on the exponent enters this elimination.

### Exterior evaluation and the finite minimum

At \(\rho\) the value equation divided by \(y^4\) has leading contribution \(a_4+\varepsilon\rho a_0\) and remaining absolute sum \(O_P(\|A\|_1/y)\).
In the Euler derivative equation subtract \(4p\) times the value equation.
The term with index \(i\) after division by \(y^4\) is
\[
 \big[p(i-4)(a_i+\varepsilon\rho a_{4-i})+\varepsilon\rho a_{4-i}\big]y^{i-4}.
\]
Only \(\varepsilon\rho a_0\) remains at \(i=4\), hence \(|a_0|\ll_P p\|A\|_1/y\) and \(|a_4-a_0|\ll_P pH/y\).
For the sign with nonzero difference, (3) thus gives \(\log H\ge2p\log\rho-o_P(p)\).
The constants, the two-sign threshold and the common-divisor error depend only on the seed, and the last extra \(\log p\) is sublinear.

The two quartic traces in the width classification each exceed \(3/\sqrt2\), the trace of \(\sqrt2\).
Monotonicity of \(u+u^{-1}\) for \(u>1\) yields \(2\log\rho>\log2\) with a fixed positive margin for both seeds.
One common sufficiently late threshold therefore excludes both seeds at height \(2^p\), including equality, and the substitution \(x\mapsto-x\) gives the same costs for their negatives at odd \(p\).
The previous degree bound handles widths below four.
The independent finite census through 1009 and the qualitative eventual result do not identify a common numerical boundary.
At width five a polynomial of degree at most five with double zeros at two distinct inverse residues can have a linear quotient by their squared quadratic, leaving the quartic edge identity unavailable.
No width-five asymptotic or matching quartic upper bound follows from this proof.
