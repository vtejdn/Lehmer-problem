### Parity and the inverse

The inverse Schur formula gives (5) upon inserting \(B^*(z)=D^*(z^2)+zE^*(z^2)\).
The reversal of \(wE+qE^*\) in formal degree \(\ell\) is \(E^*+qwE\), even when either endpoint of \(E\) vanishes.
Eliminating \(D\) gives (7), and eliminating the reversed coefficients gives \(KE=q\Delta-\Delta^*\) and \(LD=qV-V^*+NE^*\).
These identities work also at \(\ell=1\).
The constant and degree-\((\ell+1)\) coefficients of \(\mathcal F(V)\) are \(q(qS-V(0))=qL\) and \(qS-V(0)=L\).
Thus (8) gives precisely the asserted integral \(U\), while (6) gives the leading coefficient one for \(D\).
Substitution recovers (5) in both directions, including \(E=0\) exactly when \(\Delta=0\).
For stable \(B\), the strict inequality \(L|zB|>N|B^*|\) on the unit circle makes \(A\) strictly stable and \(A(1)=jB(1)>0\).
The integral inverse \(f\) has one exterior root, which is real and exceeds one since \(f(1)<0\).
Any monic integral factor excluding that root would have a nonzero integer constant of modulus less than one.
This verifies the exact-degree irreducible Pisot conclusion.
For the displayed counterexample the values of the even part at \(-2\) and \(-1\) are \(17/16\) and \(-7/16\).

### Coefficients and the two endpoints

At one, equation (7) reads \(\Delta(1)=NV(1)-LU(1)\), so the unit sign makes \(N\) invertible modulo \(L\).
Reversing (7) in degree \(\ell+1\) sends \(w\Delta\) to \(w\Delta^*\).
Eliminating \(V^*\) then yields (11), with coefficient \(-N^2w\) before division by \(-w\).
For \(0<r<\ell-1\) its leading residue vanishes, contradicting \(0<S<L\) from the trace estimate.
For the two distinct endpoints the respective leading residues are \(-\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon q\).
The two explicit odd parts each supply a unit numerator over \(K\) as well as a nonzero degree-\((2\ell-1)\) coefficient of the monic \(B\).
The denominator divisibility from the Schur step is therefore sharp, and these consecutive support exponents have difference one.
The formulas \(S=(L/N)(q+t)-v\), \(|t|<1\), and \(|v|\le q/K\) put \(q-1<S<q+2\) when \(1\le j\le q-2\), since \(K\ge2q^2-1\).
The endpoint restriction requires \(d\ge6\), whereas the later comparisons of the next coefficients use \(d\ge8\).

### Uniform growth and exclusion

The product \(q=\theta\prod_{\beta\ne\theta}|\beta|\) gives \(\delta=\theta-q>0\).
The derivative of \(F=f/f^*\) at zero is \(L\), so its \(j=L-N\) is the present branch parameter even when \(j\) varies.
The identity \(j=\delta(q+\theta^{-1}-H_q'(0))\) and \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\) show that \(\delta\to0\) forces \(q\to\infty\) and \(j/q\to0\).
For the positive delay-one polynomial \(zf+f^*\), the sign at one gives a real exterior root \(\sigma>1\), and continuity from strict Rouché comparison bounds its exterior root count by one.
Reciprocity and the unit constant force the same irreducible factor to contain \(\sigma^{-1}\).
Boyd's trace bound gives \((\log\sigma)^2\ll j/q\), which rules out a reciprocal quadratic eventually.
Thus the exterior Salem factor has degree \(r_{\rm out}\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness and has total logarithmic measure \(\log\sigma\).
Dobrowolski's inequality at that degree gives \(\log r_{\rm out}/\log\log r_{\rm out}\gg X=(q/j)^{1/6}\).
It follows first that \(\log r_{\rm out}\gg X\), then that \(\log r_{\rm out}\gg X\log X\).
All constants are uniform and \(r_{\rm out}\le d+1\), so any subsequence with \(j\le Md\) contradicts this estimate at \(q=d^2\).

From \(\sum\beta_i^2=v^2-2y\) and the elementary symmetric expression for \(x\) one gets \(|y|<(s+v^2)/2\) and \(|x|<\binom{s}{2}<q/2\).
The absent coefficient \(E_{\ell-2}\) in both endpoints for \(\ell\ge3\) then gives \(V_{\ell-1}=(L/N)(qy+x)-qu=O(qd)\).
For the constant endpoint its residue is \(-q^2S\equiv(j-1)S\pmod L\).
Both sides divided by \(L\) tend to zero, so equality forces \(j=O(d)\), contradicting the just-proved \(j/d\to\infty\).

For the top endpoint the two leading residues give \(V_{\ell-1}=-S\) by the same size estimate.
The degree-one residue is \(-\varepsilon\), giving \(qV_1+S\equiv0\pmod L\).
Since \([w]E^*=0\), equation (9) gives an integer \(x=(qV_1+S)/L\) with \(x(j-1)\equiv S\pmod q\).
For the first strict Schur transform the leading, constant, linear and penultimate coefficients are \(a,c,x-ty,e\).
Dividing by \(z\) in the two successive transforms removes one zero each time, including when a constant vanishes.
The second transform has leading coefficient \(a^2-c^2>0\), and the modulus of its constant \(a(x-ty)-ce\) is strictly smaller.
This verifies (17) under full strict stability.

Inserting \(u=-\varepsilon/K\) and \(v=\varepsilon q/K\) gives (21) directly from \(S=(L/N)(q+t)-v\) and \(V_{\ell-1}=-S\), with \(R\to1\).
If \(S=q+1\), then \(t\to1\), \(a\asymp j/q\), and \(|ce|/a=O(1/(qj^3))\).
The coefficient \(1+t/q\) tends to one in (22), so (17) first bounds the integer \(x\) and then forces \(x=-1\).
The congruence modulo \(q\) would force \(q\mid j\), although \(1\le j<q-1\).
If \(S=q\), then \(t<0\), \(|t|\asymp j/q\) and \(|ce|/a=o(|t|)\).
Inequality (17) restricts \(x\) to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
At \(x=-1\) one has \(y\to-1\), so \(ty\ge|t|/2\) eventually and the modulus on the left exceeds one.
At \(x=1\) the congruence modulo \(q\) forces \(j=1\).
At \(x=0\) one has \(V_1=-1\), hence \(j^2\equiv N^2\equiv\varepsilon\pmod L\).
The bounds \(0\le j^2-1<L\) and \(0<j^2+1<L\) force \(\varepsilon=j=1\).
Both occurrences of \(j=1\) contradict \(j/d\to\infty\).
Every shrinking sequence has a subsequence in one of the excluded cases, proving the uniform existence assertion.
