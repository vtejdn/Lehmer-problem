## Uniform prime averages, residual integrability and the exact rate

At 2026-11-24T20:55 UTC an agent outside the proof development independently reviewed for Yin the [statement](../../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v29.md#statement) and all three proof sections of C0901 v29.
The reviewer received the fixed argument and its mathematical inputs, without development discussion, author explanations or previous findings about v29.
The argument proves the classification of even feasible probabilities for every finite \(B\) and the exact rate (1) for every nonzero real polynomial, including polynomials with zeros on a forbidden orbit.
The prime distribution and exponential-sum inputs were checked at theorem-statement and application scope.

Siegel–Walfisz at modulus one gives \(S_N\sim N\), as the proper prime powers contribute at most \(O(\sqrt N\log^2(2N))\) up to \(2N\).
Thus the maximum single weight is \(O(L/N)\).
The truncation \(h\) is circle-Lipschitz with constant \(L^3\), and the Fejér kernel's first distance moment is \(O(\log(2T)/T)\).
Consequently the uniform approximation error tends to zero at \(T=\lceil L^{12}\rceil\).
Each of its nonconstant coefficients is \(O(1+\log L)\), and its constant coefficient is bounded by \(\int H\,d\eta\).

For every frequency up to \(T\), reduction of the Dirichlet approximation gives a coprime numerator and denominator with error at most \(1/(d_k Q)\le d_k^{-2}\).
When all denominators are at least \(L^{100}\), both denominator terms of Vaughan's estimate, including its seventh power of the logarithm, are \(O(N L^{-43})\) at \(X=N,2N\), since \(d_k\le N/L^{100}\).
The remaining \(X^{4/5}L^7\) and the proper-prime-power error are \(O(N L^{-40})\).
Taking the endpoint difference, dividing by \(S_N\), and summing over \(2T\) modes therefore costs \(o(1)\). [Vaughan, Theorem 1 and (2), p. 136](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/on_the_distribution_of_p_modulo_1.pdf)

If one denominator is smaller, division by its frequency and reduction give \(r\le2L^{112}\) and \(N|\beta|\le2L^{100}\).
Eventually \(r<N\), so all primes in the sample lie in reduced classes.
Theorem 4 in Kedlaya's notes states the absolute \(O_A(x(\log x)^{-A})\) error for \(\psi(x;r,u)\) uniformly in the reduced class and modulus, with the auxiliary parameter fixed.
For \(N\le x\le2N\) and \(A=400\), removal of proper prime powers leaves error \(O(NL^{-400})\).
The supremum of the test on one class is \(O(1+\log L)\) and its total variation is at most \(L^3N|\beta|=O(L^{103})\).
Partial summation over at most \(2L^{112}\) classes gives a normalized error \(O(L^{-185})\), proving (3) without a numerical onset requirement. [Kedlaya, Theorem 4, p. 3](https://kskedlaya.org/18.785/errorbounds2.pdf)

For \(|v|\ge1/8\), integration over whole periods and at most one extra period bounds the interval mean of the nonnegative \(H\) by an absolute constant.
For \(|v|<1/8\) and \(\|y\|>4|v|\), the shifted distance stays at least \(\|y\|/2\).
In the other short-interval case, the level-set integral gives \(O(1)+\log(1/|v|)\le O(1)+H(y)\).
For \(r>1\), summing this bound over the unit classes and using \(\prod_{(u,r)=1}|1-e(bu/r)|=|\Phi_r(1)|\ge1\) gives a bound uniform also for small \(r\).
For \(r=1\), \(N|\beta|\ge N\|\theta\|\ge1/8\) handles the singular starting value.
To reach the larger truncation, every prime with \(\|p\theta\|<y\le L^{-3}\) supplies a reduced fraction \(a_p/p\), since a numerator divisible by \(p\) would contradict \(\|\theta\|\ge1/(8N)\).
Their interval length \(2y/N\) and spacing at least \(1/(4N^2)\) give at most \(1+8Ny\) such primes.
Layer integration with the maximum prime weight costs \(O(L^2/N+L^{-2})\), completing (2).

For distinct \(p,q\), the total joint weight is normalized by \(1-\sum_p w_p^2\ge1-\max_p w_p\), so each marginal is eventually at most twice the original weight.
At \(0<t=\|\theta\|<1/(8N)\) the sine bound on the two denominator factors gives \(16pqt^2\), while the numerator is at most \(4\pi^2 pqt^2\) even if \(pqt\) is large.
At \(t=0\) the cyclotomic value is one.
For \(t\ge1/(8N)\), two distances below \(1/(8N)\) would give \(|qa-pb|<1\) for their nearest numerators, hence \(p\mid a\) and an impossible smaller \(t\).
If exactly one denominator is small, its cancellation with the numerator is bounded by the geometric sum, including at exact zeros, at cost at most \(2N\) in modulus.
In the other case both distances are at least the truncation threshold.
This proves the indicated pointwise majorant, and applying (2) to its nonnegative truncated terms with the marginal bounds proves (5).

For every fixed \(k\ge1\), a root of \(\Phi_{pq}(X^k)\) has order \(c\) with \(c/\gcd(c,k)=pq\).
All its cyclotomic factors are consequently allowed as soon as \(p,q\) exceed the largest forbidden order, even when \(k\) shares prime factors with \(pq\).
The dual integral of each such logarithm is finite and nonnegative.
The bound (5) controls the removed atoms from above by an absolute constant and controls the residual integral of \(\log|1-e(kx)|\) outside \(\|kx\|<1/(8N)\) from below uniformly in \(k,N\).
The residual charges none of the finitely many omitted limiting points.
Its bounded positive part and monotone convergence of the restricted negative parts give a finite common lower bound on the full integrals.
The inequality \(H(kx)\le\log(2\pi)-\log|1-e(kx)|\) now gives the exact uniform-return hypothesis needed for the rotation lemma.

For positive residual mass, that lemma yields Cesàro mean absolute value zero, also for \(A_{dn}\) by comparison with the first \(dN\) indices at fixed \(d\).
All divisors in the power factorization have integrable logarithms because their sum is integrable and each is bounded above.
Finite divisor inversion transfers the Cesàro estimate to each \(I_s(kn)\).
At every step of the two- and three-difference argument the previous subproducts are fixed, so finitely many density-zero exceptional sets can be discarded in the selected positive-density progression.
The two opposite inequalities yield \(I_s(1)=0\) for every \(s\), and the full moment theorem identifies the symmetrized residual as its mass times Haar.
The zero residual needs no normalization.
For even probabilities the finite-orbit theorem gives uniform masses on each forbidden primitive orbit.
Conversely these orbit measures pass all allowed tests by nonzero integer resultants, and Haar passes with equality.

The dual rate at fixed \(P,B\) is therefore the maximum in (1), with the same result for \(W(a)/a\to\infty\).
Any positive mass on an orbit containing a zero of \(P\) gives objective \(-\infty\), while Haar always supplies a finite value.
For \(B=\varnothing\) the only even feasible probability is Haar.
Real-polynomial integrands depend only on conjugation symmetrization.
In the nontorsion unit example every relevant absolute resultant is a positive integer, so any nonunit resultant costs at least \((\log2)/\max_{b\in B}\varphi(b)\).
This proves the stated dichotomy under its strict small-measure hypothesis and leaves the unrestricted exceptional-unit existence question open.
