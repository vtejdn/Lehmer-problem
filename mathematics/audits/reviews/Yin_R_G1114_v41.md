## Exact inverse, volume and limiting condition

The criterion recovers precisely the positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomials with the prescribed numerator, in both degree parities.
The determinant, finiteness for a fixed numerator and equivalence for a sequence of varying numerators follow under the stated hypotheses.

### Endpoint test

Since \(p=C/j\) is monic with every zero in \((-2,2)\), \(C(2)>0\), so each positive divisor \(b\) gives an integer \(q\ge2\).
Division by \(p\) gives \(B/p=x-a-\sum_i w_i/(x-y_i)\), with the stated \(w_i=-B(y_i)/p'(y_i)\).
The difference of the kernel values at \(2,-2\) is \(4/(4-y_i^2)\), giving
\[
 F(-2)=-j/(q-1)-4+4E.
\]
In particular \(E<E_*\) is equivalent to \(F(-2)<-j/(q+1)\), and equality corresponds to equality.
Positive weights give one simple zero of \(F\) between consecutive poles and one in each exterior interval, by its strictly positive derivative and its one-sided limits.
Both endpoint values are negative in either successful case.
The leftmost zero is then above \(-2\), the rightmost is above \(2\), and \(j/F\) has residue \(j/F'(x)>0\) at every zero.
For \(h=0\), \(E=0<E_*\), \(B=x-2-b\) and \(j=(q-1)b\), giving \(f=z^2-(q+b+1)z+q\).

### Polynomial reconstruction and converse

Writing \(D=z^m(z+1)^eB(z+z^{-1})\) gives a reciprocal monic polynomial of degree \(2m+e\) with constant one.
The numerator in (1) has constant \(q\), degree \(2m+e+1\) and leading coefficient \(-1\).
It vanishes at \(1\) by \(C(2)=(1-q)B(2)\).
At \(-1\) the explicit factor \(z+1\) or the equality \(j/F(-2)=-(q+1)\) supplies the second zero.
Division by \(1-z^2\) is consequently integral, and the quotient \(f\) is monic of degree \(r=2m+e-1\).
The identity \(Y(1/z)=z-z^2Y(z)\), multiplied by \(z^r D(1/z)=D(z)/z\), gives \(f^*=D-zf\).
Thus \(f(1)=D(1)/2<0\).

At every nonreal unit root of \(D\), the numerator \(C\) is nonzero at the trace, and the derivative of \(z+z^{-1}\) is nonzero.
There \(Y=f/D\) has a simple pole with positive real residue \(j/(4\sin^2(u)F'(2\cos u))\) for \(z=e^{iu}\).
For \(e=1\), \(D\) has a simple root at \(-1\) and the residue there is \((q+1+j/F(-2))/2>0\) by the strict inequality and \(F(-2)<0\).
For \(e=0\), \(r\) is odd and \(D(-1)=-2f(-1)\ne0\).
Thus \(f\) is nonzero at every unit root of \(D\), and any unit root of \(f\) would also annul \(f^*\) and \(D\).
For \(W=zf/f^*\) each preimage of \(-1\) on the circle therefore corresponds to a simple pole of \(Y\) with residue \(-1/(\zeta W'(\zeta))\), the reciprocal of the angular derivative of \(\arg W\).
There are \(2m+e-2=r-1\) upward crossings because \(D\) has a single off-circle reciprocal pair.
If \(f\) has \(N\) roots in the disk, the winding number is also \(1+N-(r-N)\), including any common off-circle zeros with their multiplicities in the difference.
Hence \(N=r-1\), and \(f(1)<0\) and monicity force the unique remaining root to be real and greater than one.
A proper monic factor excluding that root would have nonzero integer constant of absolute value less than one.
This proves irreducibility.
The identity \((1-z^2)Y-(q-z)=C/B\) gives back the reduced numerator, and its \(jz\) term at zero gives the claimed index.

Conversely the [forward trace construction](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) fixes the monic \(B\), its degree and the presence of \(z+1\) in \(D\).
It gives \(C(2)=(1-q)B(2)\) and \(B(2)<0\), and the reciprocal transform gives the [positive weights](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#strip).
At \(-2\), \(F=B/p<0\) by the locations and degrees of the two root sets.
When \(e=0\), regularity of \(Y\) forces the endpoint equality, while for \(e=1\) the [upward crossing at \(-1\)](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v34.md#crossings) forces the strict sign.
The two cases yield disjoint tests and recover the original \(f\) by (1).

### Coefficient volume and examples

Taking \(B_0=x^{h+1}-2^{h+1}-b\), every monic integral choice with the given endpoint is uniquely \(B_0+(x-2)H\) for \(H\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree less than \(h\).
In these coordinates the \(i\)-th row of the weight derivative is \((2-y_i)(1,y_i,\ldots,y_i^{h-1})/p'(y_i)\).
Taking absolute determinants gives \(\prod_i(2-y_i)/\Delta\), since the product of the derivative magnitudes counts every pairwise difference twice.
The diagonal scaling from the unit simplex to the weight simplex contributes \(E_*^h\prod_i(4-y_i^2)\).
Dividing the weight volume by the derivative determinant gives \(E_*^h\Delta\prod_i(2+y_i)/h!\) in coefficient coordinates.
The extra point \(-2\) supplies precisely the factors \(2+y_i\) in the enlarged Vandermonde.
The monic trace basis preserves this determinant, and its column norms are at most \(\sqrt{h+1}\) and \(2\sqrt{h+1}\) for the constant and nonconstant columns respectively.
Hadamard's inequality gives (2), and the closure of the bounded simplex contains both the strict inputs and the allowed equality face.
This proves finiteness for each \(b\) and hence for fixed \(C\).

For the strict example \(C=2x-1\), \(b=1\), the test reduces to \(0<19+6u<16\), with no integer equality solution.
Its three integral choices are \(u=-3,-2,-1\), and substituting \(u=-2\) in (1) yields the displayed quartic.
As a check of the equality face, \(f=z^3-4z^2+2\) has roots in \((-1,0),(0,1),(1,4)\) by its endpoint signs.
Its associate gives \(B=x^2-2x-2\) and \(C=5x-8\), with \(y=8/5\), \(q=b=2\).
The weight is \(66/25\), so \(E=(66/25)/(36/25)=11/6=E_*\), as required for this odd-degree input.

### Returning to Salem numbers

Uniformly in \(j\), the [derivative identity](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(j=(\theta-q)(q+O(1))\) with absolute error in the parenthesis.
Either \(\theta-q\to0\) or \(j/(q-1)\to0\) forces \(q\to\infty\) by \(j\ge1\).
Then \(j/(q-1)\sim\theta-q\), and \(E_*\to1\).
Stirling's formula gives the asserted logarithm of the volume upper bound when \(h\to\infty\).
For Salem numbers tending to one, the [prescribed-constant inverse](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#statement) gives \(e=1\) and permits choosing each \(q\) sufficiently large for its individual polynomial to make the excess tend to zero.
Conversely, a sequence passing the test with \(jb/C(2)\to0\) gives Pisot inputs of vanishing excess and hence Salem numbers tending to one by the same equivalence.
This establishes a criterion for such sequences and leaves their existence open.
