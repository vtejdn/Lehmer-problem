## Independent finding on the uniform lift and the finite exclusions

At 2026-11-18T20:56 UTC an agent outside the development of [C345 v406](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#decision-single-prime-integer-test-submitted) independently checked its full statement and both proof sections for Sheng Yin.

The lower bound for every admissible row, the simultaneous upper bound for all eligible bounded-measure seeds, and both directions of the finite-exclusion equivalence hold as stated.

### One substitution prime works for all the specified seeds

Write \(p=p_e\).
Since \(p^{1/4}>2e\), the term \(\log(3e)\) is \(o(p^{1/4})\), so \(\log X\sim0.9p^{1/4}\).
For the reduced class \(-1\bmod p\), subtracting the two prime counts at \(2X\) and \(X\) yields a main term at least \(X/((p-1)\log(2X))\).
The ratio of the absolute error with exponent eight to this lower bound is \(O(p/(\log X)^7)=O((\log X)^{-3})\).
This proves existence of the least prime in the specified interval without requiring an effective threshold.
For any such \(n\), \(\log(3en)=0.9p^{1/4}+\log(n/X)\) with \(0<\log(n/X)\le\log2\).
This puts \(e\le\log(3en)\), gives \(n>p\) eventually, and makes both strict margins around \(\tfrac12<0.9^4<1\) survive rounding the fourth power up to an integer.
The congruence makes \(r=(1+n)/p\) an integer between one and \(n-1\), and hence gives the required offset \(v=1\).
All these choices depend only on \(e\).

Suppose \(\alpha=\beta^n\) in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Then \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and both \(\beta\) and its inverse are integral by monic equations over the algebraic integers.
It follows that \(\beta\) is a nontorsion unit of degree \(e\), and the equal degrees convert the power law for absolute height into \(h=n m(\beta)>2n/(\log(3e))^3\).
This exceeds one uniformly in the chosen seed for large \(e\).
For prime \(n\), a proper factor of \(X^n-\alpha\) over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) would give a product \(b\) of \(j\) roots in that field, with \(0<j<n,\ b^n=\alpha^j\).
Bézout would then make \(\alpha\) an \(n\)-th power there.
The resulting irreducible substitution has degree \(d=ne\) and preserves measure.
For the prime \(p>e(e-1)+1\), a collision of two powered conjugates of \(\alpha\) would give a primitive \(p\)-th root among the at most \(e(e-1)\) ordered quotients, too few to contain its Galois orbit.
Thus \(z=\alpha^p\) generates the seed field, even in the small degrees covered only by the lower bound.
For a lifted root \(\gamma\), \(\gamma^p\) generates a field containing \(z\), hence \(\alpha=\gamma^n\), and then \(\gamma\) by \((n,p)=1\).
This verifies both powered degrees and the identity \(Q(Y)=Q_0(Y^n)\) used in the endpoint argument.

### Simultaneously preserving the two rows

For each sign the fixed four-class construction supplies two rows with independent value and derivative vectors, first values \(u_i\ne0\) and irrational ratios \(w_i/u_i\).
Write \(f=Y^{-2e}a,\ g=Y^{-2e}b\).
The centered \(a\)-equation gives \(w=2pn\ell-u\), where \(\ell=(\mathcal E f)(z)\).
Using \(g(z)=-\sigma\alpha^{-1}u\) and \(pr-1=n\) in the centered \(b\)-equation gives \((\mathcal E g)(z)=\sigma\alpha^{-1}(\ell+u/p)\).
Both \(u\) and \(\ell=(w+u)/(2pn)\) are fixed by inversion.
The affine change of ratio preserves irrationality, and its determinant multiplier \(2pn\) preserves nonvanishing of the two-column determinant.
In particular the plus-sign pair satisfies the displayed integer test, and the construction with prescribed minus sign has its corresponding signed equation and diagonal.

Multiplying either term of the first endpoint correction by \(Q_0^2\) leaves its value and first derivative zero at \(z\).
In degree \(B=4e\) the constant and leading endpoint changes are independent because \(Q_0^2\) is monic of degree \(2e\) and has constant coefficient one.
After this correction the difference \(E=A-A^*\) is nonzero at \(\beta=\gamma^p\), with centered value \(-\sigma(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})u\).
Thus \(E\) has no root in common with the irreducible \(Q_0(X^n)\).
Also \(b(z)\ne0\) excludes every root of \(Q_0\) from the zero set of \(b^\flat\) by Galois conjugacy and reciprocity.
The interior adjustment \(\nu Y^e Q_0^2\) is reciprocal about \(B\), fixes both endpoints and leaves \(E\) unchanged.
At each nonzero root of \(E\) or \(b^\flat\), the respective evaluation of \(A\) or \(a\) has nonzero slope in \(\nu\).
There are at most \(Bn+B-1\) forbidden scalars, so both gcds can be enforced in each row with \(0\le\nu\le Bn+B\).
No division by content is necessary since the endpoints are already one.
These operations keep the joint determinant and all required jets intact.
The original cancellations give \(P_0(x^n)^2\mid F\), and taking the component of residue zero modulo \(n\) gives \(P_0^2\mid G\), separately for the two rows.

The two seed coefficient vectors occupy disjoint classes in \(A\), so their initial one-norms are at most \(\|A\|_1\).
The total additional one-norm is at most \((2+2\|a\|_1+Bn+B)\|Q_0\|_1^2\).
Here \(\log\|Q_0\|_1\le ph+e\log2\) and \(\log(Bn+B)=O(\log(2dp))\).
One can see the uniform absorption directly from \(e\le p^{1/4}/2\), \(\log(2dp)=O(p^{1/4}+\log p)\), and \(h>2/(\log(3e))^3\).
Combined with the absolute four-class bound for the initial rows, this proves \(\log H(P_0)\le Cph\) with one absolute \(C\) for all the specified seeds and large \(e\).

### Lower bound, exact decisions and the quantifiers of the equivalence

For any admissible row with maximum coefficient magnitude \(H_*\), take \(U=a,\ V=Y^B b(1/Y),\ r=-1\).
These Laurent data give its displayed seed graph and have modulus difference \(aa^\iota-bb^\iota\).
After shifting by \(Y^B\), both extreme coefficients are one, so the polynomial is nonzero.
Reflection of \(P_0^2\mid G\) and unramified powering give the integral factor \(Q_0^2\), with logarithmic measure \(2ph\).
The integral quotient has nonnegative measure.
On the circle one has \(\big||a|^2-|b|^2\big|\le\max(|a|,|b|)^2\), and \(\max(|a|,|b|)\le(B+1)H_*\).
Integration gives \(2ph\le2\log((B+1)H_*)\), also for small \(e\), with no height restriction.
Taking the maximum over a pair and then the least over pairs yields (1), including the convention for the empty set.

The coefficient box, supports and prescribed prime are finite or computable for each \(e,k\).
The two summands of each \(G_i\) occupy distinct classes modulo \(p\), with constant and leading coefficient one, so each \(G_i\) is a nonzero monic polynomial.
Every possible seed appears among the monic irreducible factors of \(\gcd(G_1,G'_1)\), by separability and double divisibility.
The stated degree, reciprocal symmetry, nontorsion and both square divisibilities are exact tests.
For example, torsion can be tested against \(\Phi_m\) for \(m\le2e^2\), by \(\varphi(m)\ge\sqrt{m/2}\).
In the exact field \(\mathbb Q[X]/(P_0)\), inversion is the nontrivial involution fixing \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), since \(\alpha\) obeys its quadratic over that fixed generator and \(\alpha\ne\alpha^{-1}\).
Thus membership in \(K^+\), irrationality of the ratios and the common determinant condition are all decidable by rational arithmetic.
The enumeration needs neither an initial list of seeds nor a substitution prime.

Under a positive gap \(\delta\) choose an integer \(k\) with \((\log2)/k<\delta\).
For all sufficiently large degrees the strict margin absorbs \(\log(4e+1)/p_e\), so every individual row already exceeds the box.
If the gap fails, factorization and the nonreciprocal bound give nontorsion reciprocal unit seeds with \(h\to0\).
Their degrees can be taken to tend to infinity since bounded degree and bounded measure bound all integer coefficients.
These degrees are even since an odd-degree reciprocal irreducible polynomial would have a root at \(-1\).
For any fixed \(k\), one eventually has \(h\le1,\ Ch<(\log2)/(2k)\) and \(p_e\ge2k\).
Then \(Cp_eh\le\lfloor p_e/k\rfloor\log2\), so an actual pair passes in arbitrarily large degrees.
This negates the exclusion for every proposed \(k,e_0\).
The proof establishes a finite decision in each degree and leaves the uniform exclusion across all large degrees open.
