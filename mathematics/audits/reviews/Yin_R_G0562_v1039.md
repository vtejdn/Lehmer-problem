## The three strict bounds hold for every unit signature

At 2026-11-23T09:00 an agent outside the development of the [height-five claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v886.md#statement) independently reviewed its complete argument for Yin.
The reviewer received the fixed claim and assignment, without development dialogue, author explanations or previous findings on this claim.

Taking a positive power preserves both the unit and the nontorsion conditions, so the proof can work over \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) for every dilation.
Reciprocity up to sign puts every root of \(P\), including every conjugate of \(\beta\), in \([1/R,R]\) in modulus.
At finite places \(\lvert\beta\rvert_w=1\).
The \(D\) complex embeddings, counting both members of every nonreal pair, give \(2D h(\beta)=\sum_\sigma\lvert\log\lvert\sigma\beta\rvert\rvert\le D\log R\) by the unit norm.
The denominator in the coefficient ratio can vanish only if \(\beta^{2(u+v)}=1\).
The associated projective coordinates therefore represent \([a:b]\) of height \(\log p\), and the triangle and ultrametric inequalities give \(h([a:b])\le\log2+(u+v)h(\beta)\).
This estimate also covers \(p=6,7\), since those local bounds use no lower threshold on \(p\).

Put \(B=p/q\), \(r=R^u\), and \(s=R^v\).
Kronecker's theorem gives \(R>1\), so \(s>r>1\).
For either sign of \(b/a\) the difference of squares for the graph quotient gives \(s\lvert r-B\rvert\le Br-1\), as the denominator has modulus at least \(\lvert r-B\rvert\).
Thus \((r-1)(s-1)/(r+s)\le B-1\).
For \(r>B\), substitution of \(s>r\) yields \(r^2-2Br+1<0\), while \(r\le B\) directly gives the same upper bound \(r<B+\sqrt{B^2-1}\).
Since \(B\le6/5\), this upper bound is at most \((6+\sqrt{11})/5<2\).
The two height estimates give \(rs\ge C=(p/2)^2\ge9\).
The radial fraction increases with \(s\), and substituting \(C/r\) gives \((C+1)/(r+C/r)-1\).
For fixed \(C\) the denominator decreases on \(1<r<\sqrt C\), and for fixed \(r>1\) the original fraction increases with \(C/r\).
At \(r=13/10,\ C=9\) the value is \(231/1069>1/5\ge1/q\), proving the strict radius bound.

On the circle the graph quotient has one interior zero and an exterior pole for either coefficient sign, and its constant sign factor leaves its degree unchanged.
Consequently the level set for the quotient of \(x^v\) by this graph evaluated at \(x^u\) contains at least \(v-u\) distinct points, by its degree \(v-u\).
The remaining roots of the degree-\(u+v\) parent include equally many exterior and interior roots with multiplicity, by reciprocity.
This gives at most \(u\) exterior roots and bounds their product by \(R^u\).
The monic integral minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) divides \(P\), so its exterior product gives \(M(\beta)\) and satisfies the same bound.

The reciprocal of that monic noncyclotomic minimal polynomial has constant coefficient one and degree \(D>1\).
Dimitrov's theorem supplies a root of the reciprocal of modulus at most \(2^{-1/(4D)}\), hence a conjugate of \(\beta\) of modulus at least \(2^{1/(4D)}\). [Primary statement](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/22975)
Combining \(\log R\ge(\log2)/(4D)\) with \(R^u<13/10\) and \((13/10)^2<2\) proves \(u<2D\).
The review uses Dimitrov's theorem at statement scope.
