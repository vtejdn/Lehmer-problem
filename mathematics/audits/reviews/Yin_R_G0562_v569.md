# Review of prime-width divisibility at simultaneous residue roots

## Independent whole-proof review

At 2026-11-19T02:45 UTC a reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the [fixed prime-width theorem](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v437.md#statement), both argument sections and their declared inputs.
The reviewer had no part in its development and received the fixed text without development dialogue, author explanations or previous review findings for this version.
The simultaneous divisibility and degree bounds hold under the two numerical hypotheses and also under the stated separate disk hypothesis in place of the first numerical one.
Both applications for \(p\ge257\) hold with the specified selection of at least two distinct minima.

### Degree, size and residue disks

For \(U=A,\ V=\varepsilon A_b^*\), the genuine numerator condition gives a defined nonzero value for \(-U/V\) at each powered root and recovers its original root.
The [derivative polynomial](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) is nonzero, has degree at most \(2b\), and contains the distinct powered minima to order at least \(a-1\).
The recovered fields have their original degrees and powered logarithmic measures \(p\,m(P_i)\).
Thus \((a-1)D\le2b\), and the logarithmic budget gives \((a-1)p\,m(F)<(a-1)p\log(2)/4\).
Taking logarithms of the original strict inequality also yields \(\log H<(a-1)p\log(2)/8\).
These three bounds hold independently of either extra hypothesis in (1).

Odd reciprocal degree forces a root at \(-1\), and irreducibility excludes that case for a nontorsion minimum.
A quadratic reciprocal unit minimum has the form \(X^2-sX+1\).
For \(|s|\le2\) its roots are torsion, while \(|s|\ge3\) gives Mahler measure at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2>2^{1/4}\).
Thus all selected degrees are at least four.
With at least two distinct factors each degree is at most \(D-4\), so the first numerical condition gives \(e_\xi\le p-3<p-1\).
The alternative hypothesis supplies this last strict disk bound directly.

I checked the [powered resultant estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#energy) with the common prescribed orders \(m_i=a\).
The hypotheses include exactly the nonzero original row values at the powered nodes, the field recovery and \(e_\xi<p-1\) at each occupied disk.
The two signed rows have the same one-norm \(H\).
The local derivative estimate counts \((a-1)E(\xi)\) zeros reduced to the center.
Evaluation of the two nonzero powered resultants charges that weight at each of the \(E(\xi)\) nodes there.
The archimedean upper bounds for the two resultants therefore give
\[
 (a-1)(\log p)\sum_\xi E(\xi)^2\le 2D\log H+2pb\,m(F).
\]
The degree bound \((a-1)D\le2b\) and the strict bound on \(\log H\) make the first summand \(<pb\log(2)/2\), while the strict bound on \(m(F)\) gives the same estimate for the second.
Neither step uses \(D\le p+1\), so the alternative hypothesis supports the entire argument.

### Multiplicity and Frobenius

The second numerical condition yields
\[
 E(\xi)^2<\frac{pb}{(a-1)\log_2 p}\le\frac{p^2}{(a+1)^2}.
\]
For \(k_\xi=\lfloor aE(\xi)/2\rfloor\) we have \(2k_\xi-1+e_\xi\le(a+1)E(\xi)-1<p\).
The [local Taylor divisibilities](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#digits) apply with the two required strict disk inequalities and at least \(a\) contacts for each original root.
For \(j<k_\xi\) the scaled \(j\)-th Taylor coefficient at the unit center lifting \(\xi^p\) is divisible by \(p^{k_\xi-j}\).
Reduction thus gives a zero of \(\bar A\) of multiplicity at least \(k_\xi\) at \(\xi^p\).
Because \(F\) has rational integer coefficients, Frobenius permutes its distinct reduced roots and preserves their multiplicities, hence also the exponents \(k_\xi\).
The asserted monic divisor descends to \(\mathbb F_p[Y]\).
Primitivity gives \(\bar A\ne0\), and its degree is at most the formal width.
Finally \(\sum_\xi E(\xi)=D\) by monicity and \(\lfloor aE/2\rfloor\ge E\lfloor a/2\rfloor\).
These steps keep both repeated roots of an individual reduction and collisions between different minima.

### The two prime tests

At \(a=2\), \(b\le(p+1)/2\) gives \(2b\le p+1\) and \(9b\le9(p+1)/2<8p\le p\log_2 p\) for \(p\ge257\).
Thus the local exponents are the full \(E(\xi)\), giving \(\bar F\mid\bar A\).
For the original varying-order test, \(p>2^8\) implies \(a=\lfloor p^{1/8}\rfloor\ge2\) and \(p^{7/8}<p/2\).
Oddness of \(p\) then gives \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\le(p+1)/2\).
Also \(ab\le p+a\) and \(a/p\le p^{-7/8}<1/4\).
For \(a\ge2\), \((a+1)^2/[a(a-1)]\le9/2\) follows from \(9a(a-1)-2(a+1)^2=(a-2)(7a+1)\).
Therefore the ratio in the second condition is at most \(45/8<8\le\log_2 p\), and the first follows from \(a-1\ge1\).
Primitive scalar normalization reduces the row norm and preserves the orders and genuine numerator values.
The original strict small-measure reduction makes every qualifying primitive minimum a reciprocal unit minimum after choosing its sign.
For the all-power unit-square pair the degrees coincide, and its two distinct logarithmic measures sum to \(3m_c\).
This gives \(2D_c\le b\) and \(3m_c<(\log2)/4\) in the fixed-double application, as claimed.
