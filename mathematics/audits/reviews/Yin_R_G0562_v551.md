### Enumeration and integer bounds

For \(D=2h\) the first \(h\) Newton sums determine the coefficients through the middle, and \(c_{D-i}=c_i\) then fixes the entire polynomial.
The [native recursion](../../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v3/Reciprocal.cs) takes every permissible next sum in the residue class which makes the next Newton coefficient integral.
It discards a sum when its absolute value strictly exceeds the applicable integer bound.
For the prefix sieve its integer score differs from the exact scaled score by at most \(\sum_{j\le k}|s_j|\), and comparisons in the overlap use the original common denominator.
Reflection respects divisibility of the exponents by each \(q\mid D\).
Changing the sign of \(X\) changes the signs of exactly the odd-indexed sums, and the five tested orders are closed under this operation.
If every odd-indexed sum through \(h\) vanishes, the odd-indexed coefficients through \(h\) vanish successively and reflection gives a polynomial in \(X^2\).
Taking the first nonzero odd sum positive thus gives exactly one member of each remaining orbit.

The first reflected sum uses \(c_{h+1}=c_{h-1}\).
Every subsequent Newton identity has the term \(k c_k\) through \(k=D\) and has no derivative term for \(k>D\).
If the exterior root moduli have logarithms \(u_i\), then the excess \(2\cosh(ku_i)-2\) above the unit-circle pair bound is superadditive on the nonnegative half-line.
Their sum of logarithms is at most \(\log T\), giving \(|s_k|\le D-2+T^k+T^{-k}\) with \(T=1089/1000\).

### Final classifications

For each order \(n\le2D\) with \(\varphi(n)\le D\), I obtained \(\Phi_n\) from SymPy and computed the remainder matrix of the basis \(X^i+X^{D-i}\) for \(i<h\), together with \(X^h\).
Multiplication by the half-coefficient vector tests all coefficients of the remainder exactly.
For these orders and degrees, the individual remainders of \(1,X,\ldots,X^D\) have coefficients of absolute value at most two, and I checked the signed 64-bit dot-product bounds.
In degrees 56 and 58 this removes 531,016 and 960,820 polynomials, respectively.
Degree 56 includes the entire polynomial \(\Phi_{87}\), which the test correctly excludes by equality.

For the rest I split \(P(X)=A(X^2)+XB(X^2)\) and used ordinary integer convolution to form \(A(Y)^2-YB(Y)^2\).
The squared roots give \(M(P_j)=M(P)^{2^j}\), so a strict excess over \(\binom Di T^{2^j}\) in any coefficient implies \(M(P)>T\).
I compared with the exact integer floor and used arbitrary-precision convolution whenever the absolute-term bound exceeded the native arithmetic range.
The first excess occurs at \(j=5,6,7,8\) with counts \(31,52216,7294,4\) in degree 56 and \(81,103616,12188,10\) in degree 58.

### Degree and prime consequences

For a nonzero integer polynomial with \(1<M(P)\le T<2\), multiplicativity gives an irreducible factor \(F\) in the same measure interval.
Its leading and constant coefficients are units because their absolute values are bounded by \(M(F)\), and \(X\) has measure one.
After normalizing the leading coefficient, [Smyth's bound](../literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) forces reciprocity since \(T^3-T-1<0\).
An antireciprocal irreducible polynomial or a reciprocal one of odd degree can only contribute the linear endpoint exception, of measure one.
Lehmer's degree-ten polynomial has negative value at \(T>1\) and its unique exterior root is Lehmer's measure \(\rho\), giving \(T<\rho\).
The [Mossinghoff–Rhin–Wu theorem](../literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) bounds \(\deg F\) below by 56.
If \(F=S(X^q)\) at degree 56 or 58 with \(q\ge2\), the root product gives \(M(S)=M(F)\) at degree at most 29, excluded by the same theorem.
The census therefore exhausts the two possible degrees below 60 and proves the [lower bound of 60](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#degree) for arbitrary nonzero integer polynomials in this measure interval.
At the [prime-223 frontier](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#frontier) either allowable pair requires the smaller measure to occur in degree 56 or 58.
I checked the strict comparison \((223\cdot114+1)1091659^2<T^{446}\) in the [smaller-factor estimate](../../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v266.md#target).
Thus neither sign admits a qualifying two-value row at 223.
I used the published degree theorem and the [previously reviewed prime-223 reduction](Yin_R_G0562_v545.md#finding) at their stated scopes.
