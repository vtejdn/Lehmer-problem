The balanced inertia holds at every sufficiently large separating exponent for the fixed nodes and half-orders.
The stated lower bounds on interior zeros, interior poles, reduced degree and formal width follow for both signs.

### Leading columns and the assignment by radii

On compact subdisks the differentiated geometric series converges absolutely, since each fixed derivative order introduces only a polynomial in the exponent.
Euler jets on the left and conjugated Euler jets on the right take the monomial kernel \(x^s\bar y^s\) to \(v_s v_s^*\).
Expressing the Euler jets in terms of ordinary jets gives a triangular matrix with nonzero diagonal \(c_i^j\), preserving inertia by congruence.
The successive signs are \(+,-\) at \(qn,qn+1\) and the first \(U\) pairs give a \(2U\)-by-\(2U\) matrix.

At a node with half-order \(v\) the Chinese remainder map at 0 and 1 identifies the polynomials of degree below \(2v\) with two truncated Taylor polynomials of degree below \(v\).
Its inverse images \(Q_{a,l}\) of the coordinate monomials therefore give an invertible change of derivative rows, also after scaling by \(n^{-l}\).
For columns of type \(e=a\), substitution of \(e+qX\) and division by \(X^l\) gives \(q^l\) plus terms divisible by \(X^{v-l}\).
For \(e\ne a\) every term after division is divisible by \(X^{v-l}\).
This proves polynomial regularity in both variables and the specialization, including \(q=l=0\) with the constant monomial interpreted as one.
The factorization \(c_i^{qn+e}=r_i^{nq}(c_i/r_i)^{nq}c_i^e\) leaves a unit-modulus phase and a bounded factor in each entry.

For \(r_j<r_k\) and pair indices \(s>t\) assigned respectively to these rows, interchanging the indices increases the rate by \((s-t)(\log r_k-\log r_j)>0\).
The multiplicity of a radius is \(2m_r\), so every radius boundary in the sorted column list lies strictly between two pair indices.
Consequently every maximizing term assigns exactly the same set of columns to each radius and may permute them freely within each block.
The sum of those terms factors into the product of the block determinants after removing the positive factor \(e^{nE}\) and the fixed permutation sign.
If a nonmaximal assignment exists, the finite list of permutation weights gives a strictly positive gap.
Its polynomial prefactors are absorbed by reducing the gap, as required for the exponential error in the claim.
For a single radius the block product already gives the entire determinant.

### Nonvanishing on all separating classes

At a positive separating representative \(a_0\), the phases \((c_i/r)^{a_0}\) at a fixed radius are nonzero and distinct.
At \(X=0\) the two column types split, and within each type the factors \(c_i^e\) are nonzero.
The resulting matrix tests the first \(u_i\) Euler derivatives of \(Y^{q_0}B(Y)\), where \(\deg B<m_r\).
Euler derivatives there impose ordinary zero multiplicity \(u_i\), and the monomial is invertible at each phase.
Thus the degree-\(m_r\) product of the required node factors divides \(B\), making the specialized block nonsingular even when \(q_0>0\).

The radii and phases are algebraic in the fixed embedding, since complex conjugation preserves algebraicity and \(r_i\) is the positive square root of \(c_i\bar c_i\).
Expanding a block at variable \(X\) and phase exponent \(n\) therefore gives a finite sum of \(\beta^n p_\beta(X)\) with algebraic coefficients and \(|\beta|=1\).
Taking \(Q\) divisible by all torsion quotient orders of the bases makes the distinct surviving \(\beta^Q\) have pairwise nontorsion ratios.
Including the original collision orders makes separation constant on each class, because equality of two nonzero \(n\)-th powers means that their quotient has torsion order dividing \(n\).
One can use \(a_0\in\{1,\ldots,Q\}\), including \(a_0=1\), without requiring a jet matrix at that exponent to be invertible.
The required nonsingularity is at the formal value \(X=0\) with phases specialized at \(a_0\).
The sum of the grouped coefficients at \(X=0\) is this nonzero determinant, so at least one coefficient polynomial survives.
The affine substitution \(X=a_0+Qt\) keeps each nonzero coefficient polynomial nonzero.

Lemma 4.1 in the retained edition applies to a nondegenerate algebraic linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients at a fixed absolute value for which at least one base has absolute value at least one.
For every positive \(\epsilon<1\) it bounds \(-\log|F(t)|\) by \(\epsilon t\) outside a finite set of integers.
All remaining bases here have modulus one, so the triangle inequality gives a polynomial upper bound in \(t\).
Together these give eventual nonvanishing and \(\log|D_r|=o(t)=o(n)\).
This use is at the statement scope of the published result. [Xiao, pp. 23–24](../../literature/pdf/Xia24--arxiv-v3.pdf#page=23)

Only finitely many radii and separating residue classes are involved.
Their block products thus have modulus \(\exp(o(n))\) along the entire separating set.
The ratio of the discarded permutations to \(e^{nE}\) times that product tends to zero exponentially, proving \(\log|\det W_n|=nE+o(n)\).
This argument permits arbitrarily close distinct phases along the chosen set.

### A summable bound for every tail column

Removing \(q_*\) and inserting \(q\ge U\) shifts each affected order statistic upwards.
The sum of the increases is \(q-q_*\).
Since each \(\log r_j\le\log\rho<0\), rearrangement bounds the new exponent by \(E+(q-q_*)\log\rho\le E+(q-U+1)\log\rho\).
Every permutation has one replacement entry of polynomial growth in \(q,n\) and finitely many original entries of polynomial growth in \(n\), all after extracting their assigned exponential factors.
The constants and degrees can be chosen simultaneously over the finite choices of replaced column, row and type.
Cramer's common denominator is independent of \(q\), giving a bound of the form
\[
 \|W_n^{-1}w_{qn+e}\|\le L_n (1+q)^C \rho^{n(q-U+1)},
 \qquad \log L_n=o(n).
\]
To sum its square put \(k=q-U\).
For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{k\ge0}(1+U+k)^{2C}\rho^{2nk}
 \ \le\ \sum_{k\ge0}(1+U+k)^{2C}\rho^{2k}<\infty .
\]
The entire signed tail after congruence therefore has operator norm bounded by a fixed constant times \(L_n^2\rho^{2n}\), since each transformed rank-one term has norm equal to the square of its vector norm.
Both \(W_n\) and \(w_s\) use the same invertible row transformation of \(P_n\) and \(v_s\), giving identical Cramer coordinates.
The leading transformed matrix has eigenvalues \(+1,-1\), each with multiplicity \(U\).
Once the tail norm is below one, the min–max principle preserves both indices and leaves no null eigenvalues.
Every constant or exceptional exponent here depends only on the fixed nodes and orders.

As a separate check of derivative order and sign, I formed two exact Hermitian matrices over the Gaussian rationals.
With Euler derivatives the \((j,k)\) entry at \(x,y\) is \((z\,d/dz)^{j+k}[(1-z)/(1-z^n)]\) evaluated at \(z=x\bar y\).
I obtained the derivatives recursively from the quotient identity and used exact Hermitian elimination.
For \(n=9\), nodes \(1/3,1/2,(3+4i)/10\) and half-orders \(1,1,2\) gave inertia \((4,4)\).
For \(n=13\), nodes \(1/3,-1/3,1/2\) and half-orders \(1,2,1\) again gave \((4,4)\).
The second set has a genuine power collision at every even exponent.

### Genuine contacts and the degree of the reduced map

At a contact, \(A(c_i^n)\ne0\), \(c_i\ne0\) and the graph equation force \(A_b^*(c_i^n)\ne0\).
Then \(f(c_i^n)=c_i\) so the powered nodes are distinct.
Dividing the graph by the analytic unit \(-\varepsilon A_b^*(x^n)\) shows that \(f(x^n)-x\) vanishes through order \(2u_i-1\).
In a pair of local jet coordinates at \(c_i,c_j\), substituting \(f(x^n)\) for \(x\) and \(\overline{f(y^n)}\) for \(\bar y\) leaves the truncated numerator unchanged.
The denominator \(1-(x\bar y)^n\) is an analytic unit there, including for distinct nodes.
The local pullback has triangular jet matrix with nonzero diagonal powers of \(nc_i^{n-1}\), so inertia transfers to the usual disk kernel of \(f\) on the powered nodes.

I checked the disk divisor argument for real coefficients, formal width, cancellation and possible factors at zero.
On the circle \(A\) and its formal reversal have equal moduli and matching orders at common zeros by conjugation.
After reducing, \(f\) is a unimodular constant times the quotient of finite Blaschke products with disjoint interior zero sets.
The kernel of a product with \(s\) factors is a sum of \(s\) positive rank-one kernels, also after taking jets.
Multiplication by the denominator Blaschke product and its conjugate gives an invertible congruence at the genuine powered nodes.
The resulting kernel is the numerator kernel minus the denominator kernel, so its positive index is bounded by the interior zero count and its negative index by the interior pole count.
In the reduced function every exterior zero corresponds under \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\) to an interior pole, with multiplicities, including the pair infinity and zero.
Its degree is therefore the sum of the two interior counts and is at most the formal width.
This gives at least \(U\) of each kind and width at least \(2U\).

Distinct irreducible reciprocal minima have disjoint simple root sets in characteristic zero.
For each such polynomial inversion gives one distinct interior node per exterior root, including both members of each nonreal exterior conjugate pair.
The half-order at each node is \(v_j\), giving \(U=\sum_jv_j e_j\).
When there are no unit-circle roots, \(\deg P_j=2e_j\) and the asserted specialization follows.
The threshold throughout concerns a fixed list of nodes and orders.
