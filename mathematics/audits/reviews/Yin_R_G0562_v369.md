# Review of the power-index divisor of a two-row resultant

## Independent finding on the complete divisibility and sharpness argument

At 2026-11-18T11:17 UTC an independently assigned reviewer for Sheng Yin checked the entire [fixed claim](../../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#statement), including the local inequality (2), the global exponent in (1), the example realizing it, and the measure implication (3).
The reviewer received the fixed text and review scope without its development dialogue and did not participate in developing the argument.
All three inequalities follow with the stated normalizations.
The local length factor \(e^2\), the possible presence of ramification in the splitting field, and the scalar content of either row are accounted for.

### Branches and repeated original residues

All \(\alpha_i,\beta_i\) are local units, and degree preservation gives distinct \(\beta_i\) in characteristic zero.
For a fixed residue \(b\) of the \(\beta_i\) and center \(\gamma\) above it, each original residue \(u\) with \(u^p=b\) is simple because \(\ell\ne p\).
Hensel lifting produces the \(p\)-th root of \(\gamma\) in that residue, and the unit derivative determines the coefficients of its branch \(a(T)^p=\gamma+T\) recursively.
For an original root with residue \(u\), the geometric sum dividing \(a^p-\alpha_i^p\) by \(a-\alpha_i\) has reduction \(p u^{p-1}\ne0\) at \(T=0\).
Thus \(a-\alpha_i\) is a unit multiple of \(T+\gamma-\beta_i\).
For every other original residue \(a-\alpha_i\) is already a unit.
This proves that \(P(a(T))^e\) generates \((g_A)\), even with several indices in one group or with \(\ell\mid\operatorname{disc}P\).

Substituting the branch in the divisibility \(P^e\mid F\) and dividing by \(a^r\) gives the stated congruence of \(U+a^{s-r}V\).
For two groups the coefficient difference of their \(V\)-terms reduces to a nonzero residue, since the quotient of the two original residues has order \(p\) and \(p\nmid s-r\).
The resulting two-by-two matrix is invertible over \(\mathcal O[[T]]\).
Both \(U\) and \(V\) belong to \((g_A,g_B)\) in that ring.

### Local intersection lengths and scalar content

Multiplication by \(g_B\) on \(\mathcal O[X]/g_A\) has determinant \(\prod_{i\in A,j\in B}(\beta_i-\beta_j)^{e^2}\) up to sign.
The determinant is nonzero, and the cokernel is precisely \(C_b\).
The length over the splitting ring is therefore \(q e^2 w(A,B)\) for \(q=v_\pi(\ell)\), while the weights use \(v(\ell)=1\).
Modulo \(\pi\), \(C_b\) is the residue polynomial ring modulo \((X-b)^{m_b}\).
In particular \(T^{m_b}\in\pi C_b\).
Finite length now makes \(T\) nilpotent, so evaluating formal power series in \(C_b\) is legitimate.
The branch argument annihilates both rows there, and the nonzero residue of \(X\) makes stripping their respective monomial factors harmless.

After division by the common integer content of \(F\), at least one of the two stripped rows remains a nonzero polynomial modulo \(\pi\), since its coefficients are rational integers and both rows together have content one.
That same reduction is divisible by every \((X-b)^{m_b}\) for the selected distinct \(b\), giving the required total degree.
Selecting a monic polynomial of degree \(m_b\) among \(g_A,g_B\) for each such \(b\) gives pairwise comaximal ideals by their coprime reductions and unit resultants.
Monic division by their product furnishes representatives of degree less than \(\sum_b m_b\) for the entire direct sum of the \(C_b\).
Thus the actual degree-truncated Sylvester target surjects onto this sum, and both Sylvester summands map to zero there.
The determinant of the square Sylvester map is the nonzero resultant up to sign, including when one row is constant.
Its cokernel length \(qv_\ell(H)\) bounds the sum of the selected lengths.
Cancelling \(q\) proves (2).

Monicity of \(P^e\) ensures it still divides the primitive normalization of \(F\) integrally.
If the removed common content is \(c>0\), restoring it multiplies the absolute resultant by \(c^{\deg U_0+\deg V_0}\) by homogeneity in the two rows.
This accounts for common content while allowing the two individual row contents to differ.

### The index and the extremal example

In the [powered order-index formula](../../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index), the determinant is taken between two power bases of the same rational vector space.
Distinct powered residues give valuation zero to numerator and denominator of each pair quotient.
If the original residues coincide, the geometric sum is an \(\ell\)-adic unit and the valuations cancel, regardless of the original difference valuation.
Precisely the pairs above the same \(b\) with different original residues remain.
Their contribution is \(\sum_{A<B} w(A,B)\).
There are at most \(\binom{\min(p,d)}2\) pairs of groups for each \(b\), so (2) gives the exponent in (1) prime by prime.

For \(a=\ell^t\equiv1\bmod p\), the reduction of \(x^p-ax-1\) modulo \(p\) has roots \(z\) satisfying \(z^{p^j}=z+j\), of exact Frobenius orbit length \(p\).
The monic polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), has unit constant term and has a real root greater than one by its sign at \(1\).
Since \(\beta=1+a\alpha\), the two fields agree.
In the original power basis the columns \(1,\beta,\ldots,\beta^{p-1}\) give a triangular matrix with diagonal \(1,a,\ldots,a^{p-1}\), so the index is exactly \(a^{\binom p2}\).
The rows \(X-1,-a\) have resultant of absolute value \(a\), attaining (1) at \(\ell\).
Equivalently, reduction of \(P\) at \(\ell\) has \(p\) simple roots of \(x^p=1\) over the residue splitting field, all with powered residue one.
Every cross-group difference of powered roots has valuation \(t\), so the selected local weight also attains (2).
This checks the sharp exponent for \(d=p,e=1\), including \(p=2\).

### The measure bound

If both rows became constants after removal of their monomials, a nonzero exponent difference would put a positive power of \(\alpha\) in \(\mathbb Q\).
Being a rational algebraic unit it would be \(1\) or \(-1\), contrary to nontorsion, so \(N>0\).
The Sylvester rows have Euclidean norms \(\|U_0\|_2,\|V_0\|_2\), repeated \(\deg V_0,\deg U_0\) times respectively.
The [sparse coefficient estimate](../literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) bounds each norm by \(\sqrt{k}\,2^{k-2}M(F)\), since coefficients of the two disjoint classes are actual coefficients of \(F\).
Combining Hadamard's inequality with the sum of (2) over \(\ell\ne p\), and using \(m(F)=e\,m(P)+m(R)\) after content normalization, gives (3) with its displayed constants.
The source estimate is used at its recorded theorem-statement scope.
The review checks the resultant lower bound and its example. An input-independent lower bound for the resulting weight sum remains open.
